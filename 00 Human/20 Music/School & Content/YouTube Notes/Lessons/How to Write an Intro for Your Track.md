# How to Write an Intro for Your Track (You Suck at Producing #39)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uJ01aoWadLE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uJ01aoWadLE)

## Synopsis

A focused intro-writing lesson: reserve roughly 16 bars for the calmest, least-intense elements of the track's overall palette, treat the choice experimentally (test an element, swap it if it doesn't actually fit rather than forcing it to stay), ease elements in gradually rather than all at once, build intensity steadily toward the drop, and drop one unexpected, unfamiliar texture right before the payoff for a last beat of intrigue. Complements the more detailed tiered-energy-clip material already in the vault with a sharper focus specifically on how to choose and sequence intro material.

## Techniques

- **Write the intro from the calmest, least-intense elements first, test-and-swap what doesn't fit** — reserve ~16 bars for low-intensity "getting to know you" material built from the mellowest parts of the track's palette (not the heaviest ones, which belong later); try an element, listen honestly, and swap it out if it doesn't actually work rather than keeping it because it's already placed. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Ease elements in gradually and build intensity steadily toward the drop, then add one unfamiliar texture right before the payoff** — an element introduced too abruptly should be pulled back and faded in more casually instead; increase intensity/processing steadily as the intro progresses; right before the drop, a single unexpected texture that appears nowhere else in the track adds a last beat of intrigue immediately before the release. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

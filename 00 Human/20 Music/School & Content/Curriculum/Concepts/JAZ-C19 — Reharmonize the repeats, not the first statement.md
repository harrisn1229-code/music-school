# JAZ-C19 — Reharmonize the repeats, not the first statement

**Q.** When does reharmonization make a song worse, and what is the rule of thumb that prevents it?

> [!question]- Answer
> It competes with the melody for attention. **If the tune is the point — a strong vocal hook, a memorable lead line — dense reharmonization pulls focus and the song gets worse.**
>
> The rule: **reharmonize the repeats, not the first statement.** Let the listener learn the plain version, then change what is underneath it. It then reads as development rather than as confusion, and it hands you a second section for free.

## Why it matters

It is the discipline that keeps every technique in this lesson from becoming the failure mode Lesson 3 already warned about — a substitution reads as sophisticated against plain harmony and as directionless wall to wall, and the same is true of reharmonization across a song.

For Nate's own arrangements it is a scheduling rule as much as a harmonic one: verse 1 plain, verse 2 reharmonised, and the change lands as an arrangement event rather than as harmonic noise.

## Drill

Take a song of yours with a repeated section. Leave the first statement exactly as written and reharmonise only the repeat. Then listen once through and check the change registers as an event rather than as a mistake.

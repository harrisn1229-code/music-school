# Psych-Pop (Tame Impala)

**What makes it the genre:** everything is *slightly wrong on purpose* — pumping over-compression, tape wobble, effects in the "wrong" order, brutal instruments under a soft, buried voice. Kevin Parker's formula: 60s psych sonics + modern pop/hip-hop rhythm section + one-man-band layering. The dreaminess is engineered, not vibes.

## The pillars

### 1. Drums — pumping, crushed, played soft
- Parker: *"I spend 90% of my time on drums."* Bass first, drums second, everything else follows.
- The sound: **play/program hits SOFT, then compress violently** — compression creates the energy, not the performance. His hardware move is a DBX 165A pumping; yours: [[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]] or [[Pro-C 2]] slammed (8+ dB GR, fast release so it audibly breathes), or [[Parallel Comp Send]] returned hot enough to *hear* the pump.
- Lo-fi capture aesthetic: he tracked whole kits with 3 mics (condenser overhead + 57s on kick/snare). In the box: your drum packs → [[RC-20 Retro Color]]/[[Kramer Tape]] driven hard, or a distorted duplicate tucked under the clean kit ([[Saturation Send]]).
- Fills: tom/snare rolls in **triplets**, played live and quantized loose ("Let It Happen" era).

### 2. Bass — the lead instrument
- Melodic, rubbery, upfront — the hook is often the bassline ("The Less I Know the Better" — which is actually *guitar through a synth pedal*).
- Chain: DI → compressor → **saturator → filter** (boost mids for the rubber, LPF the top). Your rig: [[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Electric Bass DI Track]] with Tupe 3 pushed harder + Pro-Q LPF ~3–5 kHz.
- Fuzz bass ("Elephant"): layer the clean DI with 1–2 distorted duplicates (different drive flavors — [[Decapitator]] E + [[Corrosion]]), **pan the dirty layers hard L/R, clean up the middle**, light chorus on the dirt.

### 3. Guitar — effects in the "wrong" order
- The signature: **drive/compression at the END of the chain, after reverb/delay/modulation** — reverb *into* distortion smears into a thick, synthy wall instead of a defined amp tone. In Live: reverb → [[Pedal]]-style drive/comp as *inserts on the track*, not sends.
- Reference chain (Reverb Machine): Strat middle pickup → AC30-ish jangle amp → compressor → vintage reverb → short delay → **phaser last** ([[PhaserFlanger]]/Small Stone flavor).
- Power chords for the aggressive sections; double-track panned L/R; mid-forward EQ.

### 4. Synths — lush = layers + slow phase
- Prophet-5-style polys ([[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]], [[Drift]], [[Omnisphere]]): same chords in 2–3 layers at different octaves, slightly different patches.
- The dream-motion: slow **phaser** on the pad layer, LFO pitch wobble, sidechain the pad from the kick ([[Kickstart 2]]) for the pulse ("Borderline").
- Pitch-fall accents ("Let It Happen"): ride the pitch wheel down and let it drift back, or **automate a delay's time knob** — hardware-style detune-fall as the repeats re-clock.

### 5. Vocals — soft voice, brutal track, buried on purpose
- *"The instruments are brutal, the voice is really soft"* — the tension IS the sound. Falsetto-leaning, simple melodies, no runs.
- **Layer until you don't recognize your own voice** (Parker's actual rule) — 4+ unison takes, tight tune, blended equal so no take is "the lead."
- Sits 2–4 dB lower in the mix than a pop vocal — another instrument, not the focus (also why he's endlessly sampleable).
- Space: slap delay ([[Delay Send Slap]] with wobble up), long modulated verb ([[ValhallaVintageVerb]] 70s mode), flanger/phaser automated onto individual lines and words ("Mind Mischief"), delay throws on line-ends.

### 6. Mix moves
- **Hard panning** like it's 1968 — bass layers, guitars, keys thrown wide L/R; the courage to be lopsided is period-correct.
- Tape/space-echo on everything that matters ([[EchoBoy]] Space Echo style, [[Little PrimalTap]], [[ValhallaDelay]] tape modes — automate their time knobs for the psych warble).
- One flanger/phaser pass over a whole section as a transition ([[ValhallaSpaceModulator]] on the master, automated in for 2 bars).
- Whole-mix filter sweeps into section changes (Auto Filter on the master bus, automated).

## Arrangement

**Section map** — *"Let It Happen"*, *"The Less I Know the Better"*, *"Borderline"*

| Section | Bars | Slots filled | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 8–16 | BASS · BED · TEXTURE | 3 |
| Verse | 16 | SUB · BASS · BED · LEAD · TRANSIENT · TEXTURE | 6 |
| Chorus | 16 | + MIDS · AIR (pad layer doubles) | 8 |
| Breakdown | 8–16 | BED · TEXTURE · LEAD (drums out) | 3 |
| Outro | open-ended | drifts — parts drop one at a time, effects stay | ↓ |

**Entrances & exits**
- **Bass is in from bar 1 and almost never leaves** — Parker writes it first, and it's the through-line the sections hang on.
- Drums enter on a crash, full kit, no gradual build. When they leave (the breakdown), they leave all at once.
- The synth pad *layer count* is the section marker — one layer in the verse, two or three stacked at different octaves in the chorus.

**The contrast move — timbre, not subtraction.** The same parts play in the verse and the chorus; what changes is a **global effect pass** — a whole-mix filter sweep, a phaser dragged across two bars, a tape-speed wobble into the change. This is the genre's defining structural habit and the reason its sections feel different without new material being written.

**Density rule: high and roughly constant, 5–8 slots.** Psych-pop is a *dense* genre — the opposite of trap. Contrast comes from processing, not from emptying slots. If your verse and chorus differ mainly by element count, you're arranging modern pop with psych sounds.

## Session recipe (start here)
~100 BPM (pop-leaning) or ~80 half-time · write the **bassline first**, drums second, chords third · commit early — Parker: the magic is in the first hours; print effects, don't defer decisions.

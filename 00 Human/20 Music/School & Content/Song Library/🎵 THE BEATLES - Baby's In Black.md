# Baby's In Black — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Formally **verbose** by their standards — refrain, verse, bridge and a solo section — a shape Pollack notes turns up almost exclusively in John's songs. Stylistically it's a mishmash: bluesy tune and chords over a near-hillbilly vocal arrangement, with drone open fifths under the final verse. John called it a waltz, but the tempo and agitation don't fit the dance.

**Form.** Very few sections are square ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]). The 4-bar intro is engineered so you feel you walked in mid-song, coming out of a refrain. The refrain is **12 bars** in three equal phrases, melodically an inverted arch and harmonically closed. The verse is **14 bars** phrased asymmetrically **4 + 4 + 6**. The bridge is nominally 8 but runs into the downbeat of bar 9 and **elides directly into the next refrain — the words elide too**, one section's syllable landing on the other's downbeat.

**Harmony.** Almost nothing but I, IV and V, firmly in A, with the bridge adding vi and V-of-V ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). The trademark is that **V-of-V resolves to IV rather than V** — the same move as "She Loves You" and "Eight Days A Week". The refrain uses blues chords in a decidedly non-blues order, and in the verse the chords deliberately don't change on the phrase boundaries, which loosens an otherwise closed section ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]).

**Melody.** Range is managed across the whole song rather than inside a section ([[MEL-C05 — Register and intensity]]): the recurring refrain holds the lowest note and hammers a constricted ceiling, the verse opens the top a little, and **the true peak is withheld until the downbeat of the bridge**.

**Arrangement.** An unbroken end-to-end vocal duet, John below and Paul above — the absence of textural relief is what keeps the tension up. The band texture is likewise constant, with one calculated exception: a radical change of backing for the final verse only, then straight back for the closing refrain.

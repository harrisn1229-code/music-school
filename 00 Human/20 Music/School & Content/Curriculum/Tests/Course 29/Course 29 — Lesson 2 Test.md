> **Q1.** **Major or minor triad × ♭7 or natural 7 — maj7, dominant 7, minor 7, minor-major 7**
> One more third on the triad, and the two choices underneath it give four chords.
>
> **Q2.** **C E G B♭ · C E G B · C E♭ G B♭ · C E♭ G B**
> Dominant 7, major 7, minor 7, minor-major 7, in that order.
>
> **Q3.** **Half-diminished (m7♭5) and diminished 7 — they differ only in the 7th, ♭7 against ♭♭7**
> *Also accepted:* m7♭5 and °7 · Cø and C°7
> The triad underneath is the same in both; only the top note moves a half step.
>
> **Q4.** **V and vii — either chord spotted out of context names its parent key**
> A bare maj7 could be I or IV, and a bare m7 could be ii, iii or vi. These two are unambiguous.
>
> **Q5.** **A complete lower structure — a full triad for the 9 and 11, a full seventh chord for the 13**
> Missing the 3rd, the added 2 stays a 2. Missing the 7th, the added 6 stays a 6.
>
> **Q6.** **It makes the name about octave placement, when the real test is what is present underneath**
> An extension can be voiced right next to the root and is still called a 9.
>
> **Q7.** **The 3rd — present, the D reads as the 9; absent, it reads as the 2**
> Cadd9 keeps the E and adds the D. Csus2 replaces the E with it.
>
> **Q8.** **9th drop the 5th · 11th drop the 3rd · 13th drop the 5th, 9th and especially the 11th**
> The 3rd goes on an 11th chord because the two are a half step apart and clash.
>
> **Q9.** **A chord over a foreign bass note — a chord tone in the bass gives an inversion, anything else a new chord**
> The bass note does not have to belong to the upper chord at all, which is what separates this from a plain inversion.
>
> **Q10.** **One triad over six different bass notes reads as six different chords**
> *Rubric (5):* defines the upper structure as a chord over a bass note that need not be one of its own tones (1) · gives the ♭6 of a major key, or the natural 7 of a minor key, as the home placement (2) · names at least two of the chords it can read as (1) · says the real skill is the bass motion and voice leading between two readings, not the names (1)
> Put an E minor triad on the ♭6 of E major and the bass note alone decides what you have: a ♭6 major 7♯11 with the root underneath, a ♭7 dominant 13 a fourth below, a iv minor 6/9 a major third below, an altered dominant, and two rarer suspended-dominant colours. Nothing in the hand changes. That is why one memorised shape becomes a vocabulary rather than one chord. The difficulty is not the arithmetic — it is chaining two or more readings inside a real progression so the bass walks rather than jumps, because an upper structure that arrives with no line into it just sounds like a wrong bass note.

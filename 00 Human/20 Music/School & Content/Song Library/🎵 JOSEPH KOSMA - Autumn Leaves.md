# Autumn Leaves — Joseph Kosma

> A G-minor standard whose first 8 bars are the canonical teaching progression for jazz-guitar comping — one pass through Cm7–F7–B♭maj7–E♭maj7–Aø7–D7–Gm(i)–G7 touches all four common seventh-chord qualities (minor 7, dominant 7, major 7, half-diminished), which is why it shows up repeatedly across the vault as the worked example for upper-structure triads, extension substitution, and reharmonization. Full extension-by-extension breakdown: [[Autumn Leaves Chords - 5 Useful ways to unlock Extensions]]. The same 7 chords, derived from the opposite direction (root-position 7th chords → their upper-structure triads), are worked in [[Upper structure triads for comping and soloing]] and documented on [[Upper Structure Triads from Seventh Chords]].

## Chords (first 8 bars, basic Drop 2 — no extensions)

`Cm7 – F7 – B♭maj7 – E♭maj7 – Aø7 – D7 – Gm6(i) – G7`

- All bare Drop 2, top string set (keeps room to add extensions later without muddying the lower "basic power" of the chord).
- **The tonic minor chord (Gm, bar 7) is voiced as a minor 6, not minor-major** — a deliberate resolution-vs-color choice: minor 6 reads more consonant/resolved, minor-major reads more like unresolved color. Worth choosing per context (a slow bossa nova version calls for less color here than a burning swing version).

## The extension-substitution system

Same skeleton, five increasingly colored passes — the reusable mechanism, not just this tune's specific voicings:

1. **Bare Drop 2** (above) — the reference set everything else is built from.
2. **Root → 9th** on every chord (one substitution each): Cm9, F9, B♭maj9, E♭maj9 (= a rootless Gm7 shape — same notes), Aø11 (3rd→11th instead, to dodge a clashing diatonic 9), D7♭9♭13, Gm6/9, G7♭9♭13.
3. **Root AND 5th substituted** (keep 3rd + 7th fixed as the anchor): Cm11, B♭maj7(9,13), E♭maj9, Aø(nat9, from C melodic minor), D7♭9♭5 (altered/diminished-scale color), Gm(maj6/13), G7♯9♭5.
4. **Drop 2 shapes re-derived from upper-structure triads**: Cm9(11) via a B♭-major-triad upper structure over the 3rd, F7♭13♭9, B♭maj7♯11 (Lydian color), E♭maj7(9,13), Aø(nat9,11), D7alt(♭5,♯9), Gm(maj7) with no extension (deliberate close-voiced dissonance instead), G7♭9♭5 (≈ D♭7 tritone sub).
5. **Modal/color-first pass** (chords treated as isolated sounds, not functional steps — the late-Miles-Davis-Quintet treatment): Cm(11,13), F7alt (3rd omitted to delay the ii→V function signal), incomplete B♭maj7♯11(9) resolving to a C7(13)/B♭ slash chord, plain E♭maj9, an **inserted extra ii-V** (B♭m7(11)→E♭7(9)) that reharmonizes the cadence toward Aø7, **Am9 substituted outright for Aø7** ("doesn't belong in G minor" — kept on purpose), D7♭13♯9, Gm6/9♯11 (from D harmonic minor — an unusual tonic-minor color), G7♯9(nat5) via a B♭-triad-over-B-bass upper structure (diminished-family color, not altered).

## Steal this

- **The root→9th / 5th→11th-13th substitution system, generalizable to any tune's changes, not just this one** — always keep the 3rd and 7th (the guide tones) fixed, and treat the root and 5th as the two "free" slots for color. → [[Extended Chords]]
- **Which extension is "correct" isn't fixed by the chord symbol — check it against function.** The Aø7 in bars 5/6 gets an 11th instead of the diatonic 9 specifically because that 9 (B♭) clashes with the cadence into G minor. → [[Chord Substitution]]
- **A chord-tone can be deliberately omitted to delay a functional signal, not just added to for color** — dropping F7's own 3rd (A natural) in the modal pass keeps the ear from immediately hearing "we've arrived at the V" while the alterations still carry forward motion. → [[Chord Substitution]]
- **A tune survives heavy reharmonization when its bones are strong enough** — Kosma's chord progression stays recognizable even under the video's most heavily altered pass (inserted extra ii-V, substituted Am9, upper-structure G7), which is explicitly why it was picked as the demo vehicle. Useful test for choosing which of Nate's own tunes tolerate deep reharmonization practice.

# Most Effective Shred Exercise Ever? - Sweeping + Alternate Picking + Music Theory

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hrwvfHC1x-4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hrwvfHC1x-4)

## Synopsis

A theory-first approach to combining scale runs with arpeggios: rather than treating them as two separate vocabularies (alternate-picked scale runs vs. swept arpeggio shapes), find every arpeggio already hiding inside a scale box you already know, and use that to build a combined workout in an exotic mode (C Lydian in the worked example).

## Techniques

- **The core idea:** any diatonic scale box already contains every arpeggio built on its own degrees — the 1st, 3rd and 5th notes of a scale degree, visualized inside the same box shape, *are* that degree's arpeggio. Worked on A minor: A-C-E (5th fret low E, 8th fret low E, 7th fret A string) is the A minor arpeggio, visualized directly inside the A minor scale box — and the same box also contains D minor (D-F-A) and B diminished (B-D-F) arpeggios, and so on through all seven scale degrees.
- **Diagnostic exercise:** ask yourself (or a student) to play a given mode's arpeggio cold — most players who can play a scale's fragment on the fretboard can't yet produce its arpeggio on demand. If you can visualize the scale box, you can derive a custom arpeggio shape for any mode from it rather than needing to memorize arpeggio shapes as a separate system.
- **Worked exotic-mode example: C Lydian** — chosen for its raised 4th degree (added dissonance/color relative to a plain major scale) — building the C Lydian arpeggio directly from the C Lydian scale box using the same 1-3-5-derivation method.
- **Combined workout:** the resulting exercise mixes alternate-picked scale runs and swept arpeggio shapes derived from the same scale box, rather than practicing scale technique and arpeggio technique as unrelated skills.

# how to make your own 90's rnb samples

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PQjIBDgJehQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PQjIBDgJehQ)

## Synopsis

An early (2020) loner breakdown — a much more compact, from-scratch version of the "make a period-styled R&B sample entirely with VSTs and self-recorded vocals" recipe this channel returns to for years afterward. In one uninterrupted 9-minute build he: writes a 4-chord, all-ninths progression and runs it through a short color chain; records three raw, unprocessed vocal takes (main/high/low) purely to prove "you don't have to be able to sing to do this"; gives each take a genuinely different [[Little AlterBoy]] pitch/formant setting; bounces the three-take stack down to one stem and deliberately filters it thin, reasoning on-screen that three full vocal layers would leave "no room for the artist to hop on it"; layers a second, more-processed keys part over the same chords; adds a pitched-down, filtered Battery 4 drum-kit pass purely as background texture ("nostalgic feeling"); and finishes by pitching the entire finished sample down 7 semitones, reversing a section, and cleaning up the resulting edit points with a crossfade de-click setting. Most of the individual moves (per-take AlterBoy settings, EQ-both-ends-then-reverb, a Cassette "fuzzy box" stage, whole-sample pitch-down as the last step) reappear — often word-for-word in reasoning — across dozens of this channel's later, more elaborate breakdowns already logged in this vault; this video is valuable mainly as the compact, earliest-documented version of the pattern, and as the first-recorded instance of the "bounce the vocal stack thin so a flip has room to sing on top of it" reasoning. Auto-captioned; music-heavy sections (the many "and that sounds like this" playback moments) carry no useful transcript text, so timestamps below are approximate/sequential rather than clock-accurate.

## Techniques

- **Stack ninths across every chord for an instant R&B read.** Four chords, "lots of ninths... they're all like ninth chords, so lots of notes in them — that's how you get like an R&B sound." A blunt, direct rule of thumb rather than a voicing recipe: extend a plain progression's chords to 9ths across the board when the genre target is R&B. → [[Chord Progressions]]
- **Piano/chord-bed chain (4 stages).** Dry chords → EQ (cut lows + some mids) → a plate reverb ([[Little Plate]]) → a "clean chorus" preset (Arturia-sourced on-screen; owned-lane stand-in [[Chorus-Ensemble]]) → a separately-layered "warm keys" preset (Arturia Analog Lab — unowned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) stacked on top "just to give it a bit more depth." → [[EQ]], [[Little Plate]], [[Chorus-Ensemble]]
- **Record vocals completely raw first, with zero effects, before any processing decision is made.** "I'll play this raw with no effects on it just to show you that you don't have to be able to sing to do this." Three takes recorded: a main melody, low harmonies, and high notes. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **Main vocal chain.** EQ (cut lows) → [[Little AlterBoy]] at the SAME pitch, Formant brought down 5 → reverb + a very slight/light delay (owned-lane [[Delay]]).
- **High vocal chain.** EQ (cut lows) → AlterBoy pitched ALL THE WAY UP, Formant brought down only a little → noticeably more reverb than the main take — "I want this to be more in the [mix]," i.e. deliberately more submerged/atmospheric.
- **Low vocal chain.** EQ (cut some of the lows, not too aggressively — "don't want it to be too...") → AlterBoy also pitched all the way up, Formant brought down "quite a lot" (more than the high take) → an average amount of reverb → a Fruity Delay (owned-lane [[Delay]]).
- **Bounce the 3-take vocal stack to one stem, then filter it thin — deliberately, to leave room for a future artist.** Mute the piano, arm disc-recording on the master channel, Alt+R to render — "if you were having that in a sample normally, like it'd be hard for the artist to find room to hop on it." → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Bounced vocal stem chain.** EQ (cut some lows and some highs) → reverb → an Arturia high-pass sweep filter (owned-lane [[Auto Filter]]).
- **Second, layered keys part.** Copy the same chord progression, add a few extra notes. Chain: EQ (cut lows) → reverb → a Fruity Delay ([[Delay]]) → [[Cassette]]'s "fuzzy box"/degradation stack, added specifically to detune the part and "give it like a very old-school feeling."
- **Battery 4 texture pass, used purely as background nostalgia, not a real drum part.** A very basic pattern in Battery 4 (unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]), bounced to audio, pitched down, filtered (highs and lows cut), and brought well down in the mix — "just so you can have this extra level of texture and nostalgic feeling." → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Restraint once the core layers are locked.** "Honestly this sample is kind of done, like I'm not gonna go crazy, I'm not gonna add loads of [stuff] — I'm just gonna bounce it all into one and then [mess] with the pitch and maybe reverse a part of it." A deliberate stopping point rather than continuing to layer.
- **Final polish: pitch the whole finished sample down 7 semitones, cut the now-muddier lows, reverse a section.** Applied to the fully bounced/combined sample as the literal last processing pass, matching the master-pitch-down-as-final-step move this channel repeats in later videos. → [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Crossfade de-click at reverse/edit transition points.** "One thing to stop the clicking that I do to stop the clicking when it transitions from parts to parts, I go to this de-clicking mode and I click crossfade — you can see it kind of fades in and out so you don't get the little pops." Owned-lane equivalent: a short fade/crossfade at the clip boundary in Ableton's clip editor. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

## Synopsis

A beginner sweep-picking exercise on a single worked Cmaj7 arpeggio shape, paired with the core mental-model shift BERNTH considers the real obstacle: reusing the "strumming a chord" motor pattern instead of the "picking individual notes" one.

## Techniques

- **Worked shape: Cmaj7 arpeggio** — root C (10th fret, D string), major 3rd E (9th fret, G string), perfect 5th G (8th fret, B string), major 7th B (7th fret, high E string).
- **The core mental-model reframe: sweep picking borrows the chord-strum motor pattern, not the individual-note-picking pattern.** Most players' hands already have two separate motor connections — "arpeggiate/solo mode" (pick each note individually, e.g. alternate picking) and "chord/strum mode" (one continuous motion across strings) — and default to the first one for an arpeggio, which caps out at a wall as tempo rises. Sweep picking requires deliberately tearing apart that default association and re-routing arpeggio-playing onto the strum-mode motion instead. Framed as the single biggest mental obstacle for most beginner sweepers, more than any physical difficulty.
- Practiced very slowly first, watching for zero individual string attacks — one continuous motion across the strings, banked into muscle memory before any speed work.

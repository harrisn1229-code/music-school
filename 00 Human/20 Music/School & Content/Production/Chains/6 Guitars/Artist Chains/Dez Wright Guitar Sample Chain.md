# Dez Wright Guitar Sample Chain

**Reference/vibe:** a processed, textured guitar sample built for a trap/R&B flip — clicked in blind first, matched to a real guitar recording second, then run through a full color/movement/space chain rather than left dry. Reverse-engineered from a loner (YouTube) breakdown of Dez Wright's production on A Boogie's "Numbers," [dez wright's synth secrets will elevate your melodies!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_8vOz54DTiY).

## Chain overview

Write the harmony blind → identify + look up the chords → record a real guitar playing them → preamp → amp → tape saturation → flanger → spring reverb → EQ, with a separate perc loop + texture layer mixed in underneath for ambience. Every stage after the amp is a color/movement/space device, stacked in that specific order — saturation first (grit), then movement (flanger), then space (reverb), then a final tonal cleanup (EQ).

## Step 1 — Write and match the chords

1. Click in a simple chord progression using whatever placeholder sound is handy — described as "a random sound," not a considered patch choice. The point is capturing the harmony, not the tone.
2. Once a progression sounds right, identify the actual chords and look them up on a guitar chord/tab site (name not given in the source video — an unidentified tool, not a plugin).
3. Record a real guitar playing those chords (the source used a **C minor** and a **G minor** voicing) directly into the DAW.
- **Why:** writing blind first, then matching to guitar-friendly voicings, is faster than composing directly on guitar and sidesteps needing strong guitar-specific chord knowledge up front. Called out in the source as "the biggest cheat code for making guitar samples."

## Step 3 — Color, movement, space

### 3. [[Cassette]] — tape saturation
- Adds grit/warmth ahead of the movement stage. *(general starting point — no exact settings given on screen)*

### 4. [[Phaser-Flanger]] — movement
- Sits between the saturation and reverb stages — color/movement added to an already-gritty signal, before space is layered on top.

### 5. [[Ghz DC19 3]] — spring reverb
- Use the built-in Spring section for the reverb character rather than a plate/hall/room reverb. Positioned near the end of the chain, adding space to an already fully-colored signal.

### 6. [[EQ Eight]] — finishing EQ
- Closing stage on the chain — tonal cleanup after all the color/movement/space processing, not before it.

## Step 4 — Ambience layer (parallel, not inline)

- A separate **percussion loop** and a **texture layer** are mixed in underneath the processed guitar, not inserted inline in the guitar's own chain — a companion ambience bed rather than another guitar-chain stage. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]

## Arrangement

**Where it sits:** **MIDS**, with the parallel ambience layer (Step 4) reaching into **BED** and **TEXTURE**.

**When it plays:** built as a loop for a trap/R&B flip, so the sectional work happens downstream in the chop — see [[Hip Hop - Trap]] § Arrangement. The sample's job is to be four or eight bars that survive being cut up.

**The move — parallel, not inline.** Keeping the ambience on its own layer means it can be muted per section without touching the dry guitar. An inline reverb would weld the two together and cost you that.

## Notes

- **Order matters:** saturation → movement → space → EQ is the load-bearing sequence here — each stage builds on what the previous one added rather than processing the dry signal independently.
- No exact plugin settings (gain, flanger rate/depth, reverb decay, EQ bands) were shown on screen in the source video — treat every device's setting as a starting point to dial in by ear, not a quoted spec.

# i combined r&b melodies with uk garage drums to make an insane beat (crazy bounce!?)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4-jcoCVdC0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4-jcoCVdC0)

## Synopsis

A from-scratch UK garage beat built specifically to fuse garage's swung, bouncy drums with R&B-style chord voicings and melodic layering — the explicit premise being "songs recently with garage drums but more R&B chords." loner starts with a four-chord R&B progression (Emaj7 → C#m → D#m7add9 → D#m/A#, i.e. the same triad voiced two ways at the end) played on airy one-shots from Kill September's *Mercury 2* kit, layers a grittier counter-melody and a second ambient melodic element (panned hard right, heavy delay) from the same kit, then builds a simple root-following bass from Oliver's Power Tools "super deep bass," beefed up with OTT (multiband), MicroShift (chorus-like widening) and a 30 Hz EQ cut. A Splice vocal chop and a phone-recorded field texture ("a recording from a shopping center") round out the melodic/textural layer. The back half of the video is the more instructive part for the vault: a genuinely detailed UK garage drum build — normal-grid snares plus deliberately off-grid "bounce" snares nudged 4 ticks right, claps hitting on/just-before the snare, a click-like first hi-hat, a second hi-hat tightened and shifted off-grid for the real bounce, and a simple, spaced-out kick that deliberately stays out of the percussion's way. Live-recorded hand claps (chopped to stay in time, compressed, reverbed with a "cool room" preset, band-passed) add texture and human bounce. Structuring touches: a chopped/velocity-ramped/left-to-right-panned snare roll as a glitchy transition, and a chopped ambient tape-style sound as a non-riser intro→drop transition. Video also has a DistroKid sponsor segment (not enriched — no production content).

## Techniques

**Chords / melody**
- Chord progression: Emaj7 → C#m → D#m7 (add E, extra melody note) → D#m/A# (same D#m triad, inverted so D# sits at the bottom "like it normally is").
- Chord one-shot: Kill September *Mercury 2* one-shot kit, "Unstable Sign" — EQ cutting the low end, medium-size reverb, going for "very airy ambient."
- Counter-melody: two notes only, from the same *Mercury 2* kit — deliberately "grittier and distorted" to contrast the first sound and blend "ambient" with "more industrial traditional garage."
- Third melodic element: another *Mercury 2* one-shot, panned hard right, heavy delay (wet all the way up), some low end cut.
- Vocal chop: found on Splice, chopped down to a liked section — called out as one of the "little textures and little melodic elements that are going to make these types of beats stand out."
- Field-recording texture: "a recording from a shopping center" layered underneath for ambience and added high end — subtle.

**Bass ([[BASS Slot]] / [[SUB Slot]])**
- Source: Oliver's Power Tools kit, "super deep bass" one-shot; simple line just following the chord roots.
- **[[OTT]] as a bass-beefing multiband compressor** — depth brought down (i.e. reduced from OTT's default extreme), low end boosted, high end taken out, some mids taken out — "so it's really hitting in the low end." → [[Multiband Compression]], [[Bass Design]]
- **A little [[MicroShift]] for a chorus-like thickening effect** — distinct from MicroShift's usual full-stereo-width role; used here lightly, on a bass, for character rather than width. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **[[EQ Eight]] cutting at 30 Hz** — "you don't want those really really low frequencies" underneath the bass. → [[SUB Slot]]

**Drums — the UK garage bounce (the video's most concrete section)**
- Snare: normal-grid hits on the standard garage backbeat positions, PLUS extra "bounce" snares placed in between — two mid-pattern plus one at the end — nudged **4 ticks to the right** (Select None → select just those snares → nudge-right ×4) so they land slightly off-grid. This off-grid placement is credited as the source of garage's bounce and the natural feel, especially once other elements layer in. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- Clap: some hits land on the snare, some just before it — adds extra bounce on top of the snare pattern.
- Percussion: a "click" sound layered in for extra percussion.
- Hi-hat 1: closer to a click than a hat — subtle, adds bounce without being a real hi-hat pattern.
- Hi-hat 2: "where the bounce really comes in" — shifted back off the grid, and tightened via the device's **Out** knob (shortens the tail) before being placed.
- Kick: deliberately minimal and spaced out — "there's a lot going on with the percussion" already, so the kick pattern stays simple and repetitive rather than competing for attention.
- Recommended source: Splice's Oliver's Power Tools kits for "unique sounding" garage drums; the snare and some percussion in this build came from a second, unnamed kit.
- Live-recorded hand claps: clapped into a mic along to the beat, chopped to stay in time, then run through a compressor + an "Intensity" reverb (Cool Room preset) + a bandpass EQ cutting highs and lows — adds texture and extra human bounce once blended under the programmed drums. → [[Reverb]]

**Structuring / transitions**
- Glitchy snare roll: the snare pattern chopped up into a unique roll, extra snares added with velocity gradually brought down, and panned left→right across the roll — used as a transition into the next section.
- Intro→drop transition: not a riser or an impact — a chopped ambient tape-style sound, deliberately contrasted against the airy ambient melodic layer for an "industrial vs. airy" textural juxtaposition.

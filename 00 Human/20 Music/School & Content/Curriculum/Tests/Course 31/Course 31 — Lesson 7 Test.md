> **Q1.** **One chromatic passing tone added to a seven-note scale, making eight notes**
>
> **Q2.** **It keeps chord tones landing on the beat instead of drifting onto offbeats**
> Seven notes in straight eighths shift by one position each bar; eight do not.
>
> **Q3.** **G A B C D E F F♯ G — the F♯ is the addition**
> Run it down from the root and G, F, D and B all land on downbeats — the four chord tones of G7.
>
> **Q4.** **Notes surrounding the target before you play it — D♭ – B – C**
> *Also accepted:* D – B – C · B – D♭ – C · D – D♭ – B – C
>
> **Q5.** **One note rather than two or three — and a half step below is strongest**
> Chromatic from below borrows the leading-tone pull; from above is softer, and a scale step is the most neutral.
>
> **Q6.** **On the note you want heard, usually a chord tone on a downbeat — and not on every note**
> Enclosures are punctuation; wall to wall, a line stops having a shape.
>
> **Q7.** **Chromatic embellishment of static harmony — and it names the chromatic line, not the chord**
> The harmony can be genuinely static or just slower-moving than the line riding against it.
>
> **Q8.** **It is a sustained line across several beats or bars, not a single arrival on one chord tone**
> Closer to a bass-line device borrowed into the melody than to a one-note ornament.
>
> **Q9.** **It generates motion without changing the chord, so the decision to hold still survives**
> *Rubric (5):* says CESH creates forward motion without changing the harmony (2) · says adding chords would undo the deliberate choice to keep the harmony static (2) · names a note worth walking — a chord root or a guide tone (1)
> A static vamp is a decision, not an accident: the interest is supposed to come from melody, voicing and texture rather than from changes. Adding chords does not fix the vamp, it replaces it with a different piece of music. CESH supplies the missing motion inside the constraint — pick a note that matters, a chord root or a guide tone, and walk it by half step across the bars while everything else stays where it is. The ear hears movement and the harmony never moved.
>
> **Q10.** **Learn by ear · chunk · analyse each chunk · name one concept · improvise**
> Analyse each chunk against the chord underneath it, then improvise from the concepts rather than the licks. Ruch's method for turning a transcription into vocabulary rather than a memorised part.
>
> **Q11.** **Naming the concept — skip it and you have a memorised part rather than usable vocabulary**
> Learning a solo by ear builds ear and vocabulary, but on its own it is not enough.
>
> **Q12.** **Enclosures, chromatic approaches and bebop passing tones transfer; the note rate does not**
> *Rubric (5):* names at least two transferable devices — an enclosure on a lead, a chromatic approach into a section, bebop passing tones in a bassline (2) · says density does not transfer (2) · says the note rate is a genre convention rather than a principle (1)
> Enclosing the second note of a two-note synth hook costs two 16ths and stops the line sounding quantised from scratch. A single chromatic approach into the downbeat of a chorus is the cheapest lift in the arrangement. The bebop scale's added eighth is exactly what makes a walking-ish bassline land its chord tones on the beat. What does not come along is the note rate — that was a genre convention answering the tempos and the instrumentation bebop had, not a claim about how melody works, and importing it wholesale into a trap beat just sounds busy.

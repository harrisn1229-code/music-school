# How to Make Any Kick Drum on a Minimoog | 10cc "I'm Not in Love"

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OhuCwhM7DzY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OhuCwhM7DzY)

## Synopsis

Recreating the Minimoog kick from 10cc's "I'm Not in Love" — genuinely useful because the Minimoog has no direct way to route an envelope to oscillator pitch (unlike the ARP 2600), so the workaround (a maxed-resonance self-oscillating filter standing in for a pitch-drop oscillator) generalizes to any synth with the same limitation.

## Techniques

- **Push filter resonance to maximum so the filter itself self-oscillates as a pure sine tone**, then route the filter's own decay-time envelope to cutoff — that decay curve becomes the kick's pitch-drop speed, and the envelope's contour amount sets how deep the pitch drop goes. This stands in for a dedicated pitch envelope the instrument doesn't otherwise have.
- **A separate tone oscillator (triangle for softer, square for bassier/harmonically richer) mixed in underneath the pitch-drop layer** for the kick's sustained body/tone, independent of the self-oscillating filter's transient.
- **A little noise blended in for "air"/cushion** — described as giving the kick a more natural, less purely-synthetic feel.
- **VCA decay time controls ring/sustain length** (more decay = more 808-like ring); **VCA attack time controls roundness** (a touch of attack softens the transient into "rounder," zero keeps it hard and percussive) — the two independent dials for tail length and transient hardness.
- **The whole patch is 3 knobs' worth of character control once built**: pitch-sweep depth (contour amount), sweep speed (filter decay), and ring length (VCA decay) — demonstrated dialing the same patch from a hard "jazz" kick to a longer, more melodic "house" kick using just those three.

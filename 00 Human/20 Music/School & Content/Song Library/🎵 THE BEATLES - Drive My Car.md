# Drive My Car — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

The key of this song is **decided by the melody, not the chords** — a point Pollack revised his own designation over. Chords rooted on only five notes, an opening D chord, and a repeated D → G that on paper is an open-and-shut I → IV. But the vocal hammers G so insistently that the same two chords read equally well as V → I in G ([[EAR-C01 — Tonic recognition, and the three levels of practising it]]).

**Form.** A flat folk-ballad shape: four verse/refrain pairs in a row with one verse handed to a guitar solo as the only relief. Rhythmic placement is deliberate and consistent — the verse enters late, after the downbeat; the refrain sits squarely on it; the coda winds up well ahead of it. The **2-bar intro** is among the most disorienting they recorded: an eighth-note pickup, a syncopated guitar contour and an off-beat bass entrance make the meter genuinely hard to find ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]), and by the time the drum fill lands on the beat you've lost the trail.

**Harmony.** The verse's last chord sets up a pivot, but the target is deceptively deferred to bar 2 of the refrain, and before the new key can settle the music swings home and parks on a fat V that motivates the next verse — tonal mobility of a kind usually reserved for a bridge ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]). Only in the outro does the home key finally sound settled. The vi chord in the verse carries such a prominent ♭7 in the vocal that whether the chord's own third is in the mix at all is an open question, and that note is held through into the next chord for a straight major/minor clash.

**Melody.** Rap-like declamation rather than tune, shot through with ♭7s on top and 4–3 appoggiaturas underneath. The guitar solo is the one place D gets melodic emphasis — and for that stretch alone the progression finally sounds like I → IV.

**Arrangement.** The bass is unusually motivic, ornamenting roots with a triadic figure that free-associates with the refrain's top line. The electric piano's slow triplets run against a **4/4 backbeat that keeps going underneath them**, which both cushions the rhythmic dissonance and rubs your nose in it ([[RHY-C04 — Polyrhythm and hemiola]]).

## Production & Arrangement
Recorded on a **fairly basic 4-track**, in this order: **Track 1** — Paul (rhythm guitar) + Ringo (drums) + John (tambourine), with George's live guide lead-guitar intro on the same track. **Track 2** — George's *re-recorded* lead guitar overdub, done in the same pass as Ringo's cowbell. **Track 3** — lead + harmony vocals. **Track 4** — bass (Paul) + doubled backing vocals, recorded **last**, deliberately, to make the bass as loud as possible in the final mono mix — a conscious attempt to compete with the "Motown bass sound" coming out of America; bass-tracked-last is genuinely rare studio practice.

The 2026 stereo remix (Giles Martin) centers drums and vocals — respecting how the original mono mix would have carried them — and spreads the guitar tracks (George's overdub lead, Paul's rhythm guitar) across the stereo field, rather than hard-panning instruments to one side the way the original Capitol-style stereo pressing did.

## Steal this
- **Track the bass last, on purpose,** if you want it to dominate the final balance — most sessions build bass-up; doing the opposite forces every other part to make room for it rather than the reverse.

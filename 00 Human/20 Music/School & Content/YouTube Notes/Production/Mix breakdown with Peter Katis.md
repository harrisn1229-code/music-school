## Synopsis

A short, dense **Mix with the Masters** breakdown — Peter Katis (The National, Interpol, Frightened Rabbit) walking through his mix of a song against its demo, playing short before/after clips and narrating the decisions in between. There are no numbered plugin settings here — it's a compressed tour through five distinct, genuinely reusable moves, each stated as a small, general-purpose principle rather than a one-off for this song: (1) a **first-chorus breakdown** — deliberately holding the first chorus back from the demo's full-energy version so the arrangement has "somewhere to go after that," an explicit go-to trick he names as not his own invention but a favorite regardless; (2) **staging the bass in two separate, deliberately withheld entrances** — no bass at all until the second chorus (an electric bass lick, capped in low end by routing it into the guitar bus rather than the bass bus), then a second, genuinely low sub-bass layer held back even further until the vocal returns after the bridge — both tremolo'd so neither "takes over" the mix or reads as "a wash"; (3) **automating EchoBoy on hi-hat overdubs** to fake a drum roll/fill through the bridge's scene-change transition — a fix for a structural problem (the section had zero percussion and "fell completely flat" without it) rather than a part anyone especially loves, deliberately mixed low so it reads as connective tissue; (4) the **guitar-bus-as-low-end-cap routing move** — putting a bass part through the guitar bus specifically because that bus doesn't carry much low end, instead of routing it through the bass bus; and (5) **muting the guitars for a couple of measures right after the bridge ends**, on a suggestion from someone else in the room during the final mix pass, which Katis generalizes into a blanket principle: "anytime you can mute something and bring it back in, it's just a great move, I think, without exception." The whole song was reportedly tracked and mixed in about two days, with one later recall pass — worth noting as context for how fast these decisions were actually made, not painstakingly planned in advance.

## Techniques

- **First-chorus breakdown, to give the song "somewhere to go."** Where the demo's chorus "just keeps on cruising" at full arrangement the first time it appears, the mix version deliberately strips it back — banking the full-energy chorus version for a later hit rather than spending it on the first pass. Named as a favorite recurring trick, not this song's own invention. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Tremolo on TWO separate bass parts, both times explicitly to stop the bass "taking over" the low end or reading as "just a wash."** In his own words: "I still want it to hold back the low end in the song, and so I put the tremolo on it. The tremolo just kind of breaks it up... I'm a big fan of tremolo, and anything that modulates the sound in and out, up and down, so it isn't just a wash." Owned lane: [[Auto Pan]] with Phase kept at 0° (amplitude tremolo, both channels chopping together) rather than the mirrored-phase auto-pan setting. → [[Auto Pan]], [[Modulation]], [[Bass Design]], [[BASS Slot]], [[SUB Slot]]
- **Withhold real bass entirely until the second chorus.** The demo has no real bass at all before then; the mix's first bass entrance is an electric bass guitar "lick," deliberately delayed as a structural landmark rather than present from the top. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Bass Design]]
- **Route that bass into the GUITAR bus, not the bass bus, specifically to cap its low end.** "That's just the regular electric bass guitar that comes in there. But I guess I still want it to hold back the low end in the song... it's a bass guitar that I stuck in the guitar bus, because I did not want it to have a lot of low end." A deliberate cross-bus routing choice — borrowing a bus's tonal ceiling rather than doing the same job with EQ on a bass-bus-routed track. → [[3 Guitar Bus|Guitar Bus]], [[Bass Design]]
- **A second, genuinely low sub-bass layer joins ONLY once the vocal returns after the bridge — a separate, later withholding decision from the second-chorus bass entrance above.** "It's joined by an actual bass — sub bass. It doesn't come in until the vocal starts again." Also tremolo'd, "so it doesn't completely take over the track." Two staggered low-end reveals stacked on one song, each timed to a different structural landmark (chorus #2 vs. post-bridge vocal return). → [[Bass Design]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[SUB Slot]]
- **Automate EchoBoy on stacked hi-hat overdubs to fake a drum roll/fill through the bridge's scene-change transition, kept low in the mix and explicitly a functional fix rather than a favorite part.** Going into the bridge ("definitely sort of a scene change"), "there's some funny little trickery here with some EchoBoy automation... it almost creates like a little drum roll. And that's something I made just with some hi-hat overdubs." He and the band didn't love the part on its own merits — "I don't love it either, but something has to happen" — because the section had zero percussive elements otherwise and "the song just fell completely flat" without it. It's mixed low deliberately, carrying the song until the vocal re-enters rather than drawing attention to itself. *(qualitative only — no specific EchoBoy parameter values given)* → [[EchoBoy]], [[Automation]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Mute the guitars for a couple of measures right after the bridge ends, then bring them back — a suggestion from someone else in the room during the final mix pass, generalized into a blanket mixing principle.** "Someone said, why don't you mute the guitars when the bridge ends — it's actually a great call, because it actually opens the song up even more. It's just for a couple of measures, not a big deal, but again, anytime you can mute something and bring it back in, it's just a great move, I think, without exception." → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Automation]]
- **Two-day turnaround: tracked and mixed in roughly two days, with one recall pass after the band left.** Framed by Katis as fast even though "in some worlds that's a lot of time" — offered as context for the pace at which these decisions were actually made, not evidence they were slow, deliberated choices. *(general context, not a standalone technique — logged here only)*

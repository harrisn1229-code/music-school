# B.B. King Guitar Lesson - 5 Licks To Get Started!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5PpQeBEVu_s)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5PpQeBEVu_s)

## Synopsis
Jack Ruch's beginner-focused companion to his more advanced B.B./Bloomfield breakdown ([[From BB king to Mike Bloomfield - A Classic Slow Blues]]) — this one builds the **B.B. box from the ground up**, in the key of G, starting from a single root note and working out to a full triad, three foundational licks, and two arpeggio shapes for outlining the IV and V chords. Where the companion video assumes the box and focuses on phrasing/touch across a worked solo, this one is the "how the position is built and why" primer: it derives the B.B. box from a D-major-shape triad stacked inside a C-major-shape barre, shows how G major, G major pentatonic, G minor pentatonic and the G minor blues scale (♭5) all live inside that one hand position, and states plainly that B.B. King's actual vocabulary was a *blend* of major and minor pentatonic rather than one scale. The three licks build in difficulty (sixth-to-root-to-bend; the iconic 5th-to-♭7 whole-and-a-half bend; a minor-blues-scale hammer-on/pull-off trill), then the lesson layers in C7 and D7 arpeggio shapes inside the same position so a beginner can start targeting chord tones over the IV and V changes instead of just running one scale over the whole 12 bars. Two full 12-bar solo examples close the video, one basic and one more "note-y," showing the same small vocabulary recombined. No gear, amp, or production content — pure fretboard/note-choice technique, and a near-ideal companion/prerequisite to the existing [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]] note.

## What's taught
- **Deriving the B.B. box from a triad, not just naming a fret position.** Root on the 8th fret of the B string (G, in the key of G). That root is the top note of a **D-major-shape triad** slid up to the 8th fret (G major). That triad in turn sits inside a **C-major-shape barre chord** slid up and barred behind it — also a G major voicing. The whole B.B. box is built by stacking these two chord shapes on the same root, not memorized as an abstract scale diagram. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]], [[Triads as a Fretboard Map]]
- **From that one hand position, four different scales are all available without shifting**: G major scale, G major pentatonic, G minor pentatonic, and G minor blues scale (minor pentatonic + ♭5) — stated explicitly as the reason this position is so productive: one physical shape, four melodic options layered on top. → [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- **B.B. King's actual vocabulary was a blend, not a single scale** — explicitly: combining the **major 6th from the major pentatonic** with the **minor 3rd from the minor pentatonic** in the same lines, rather than committing to one scale for a whole phrase. Directly reinforces [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]'s "the blend is what most players actually do." → [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- **Lick 1 (major pentatonic based): 6th → root → double bend → back to 6th.** Play the major 6th (E), up to the root (G), then bend from the 9th degree (A) up a whole step to the 3rd (B) — described as "a double little pump bend" — then resolve back down to the 6th. *(key of G)*
- **Raking as an articulation, not just a picking shortcut.** On lick 1, rake the pick across the muted E note along with the fretted G to add "a little bit of attitude" to the note — palm-mute the unused strings in between so the rake reads as one aggressive attack rather than noise. A touch device, same family as [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]'s pick-position tone control. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Bend the target interval to match the underlying chord tone, not a fixed scale degree.** Over the I (G) chord, lick 1's bend goes a full whole step (A → B). Over the IV chord (C7, which contains B♭), the *same* lick is played with the bend shortened to a **half step (A → B♭)** instead — because a full bend to the natural B would clash with C7's ♭7. General lesson: the bend target should track the chord tone available under the current chord, not stay fixed to what worked over the previous chord. *(general starting point — adjust bend distance by ear against the underlying chord tone)* → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]], [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- **Lick 2 — the iconic B.B. King whole-and-a-half bend.** Grab the 5th of the scale (D) and bend it up a minor 3rd to F (the ♭7) — described as a big bend that "takes a little bit of strength to get." Release back down to D, then a smaller first-finger half-step bend on C. Resolve down through the minor 3rd → 9th → 6th, landing the phrase on the same major-6th/minor-3rd tritone relationship that defines the style. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Lick 3 (minor blues scale, ♭5): a half-step hammer-on/pull-off trill between the 4th and ♭5.** Run up the top of the minor pentatonic (♭3-4-5), then trill between the 4th and ♭5 with a hammer-on/pull-off rather than a straight bend — described as "a really great little technique," expandable/repeatable and playable as a straight pull-off (stays fully minor) or resolved up to the major 6th (sits in the major/minor "in-between zone"). Phrasing is explicitly flexible — the same lick reads differently over a medium shuffle vs. a slower/funkier groove. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]], [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- **Two arpeggio shapes added inside the same B.B.-box hand position, for outlining the IV and V chords**: a **C7 arpeggio** (root–3rd–5th–♭7 of C7) and a **D7 arpeggio** (same shape) — both fingered without leaving the box position, so a soloist can stay in one hand shape and just shift which notes they target as the chord changes underneath. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Targeting the C major triad specifically inside the C7 arpeggio** when the IV chord arrives, rather than continuing the I-chord scale material — demonstrated live over a 12-bar: same G blues vocabulary over the I chord, then a hard pivot to C-major-triad notes the instant C7 hits. Framed as "all you need to do" to start outlining a chord change: hit its keynotes. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]], [[Blues Form and Harmony]]
- **D7 targeting phrase: 3rd → 5th → root → ♭7 of D7**, then the identical shape moved up/transposed back onto the IV chord when the form returns to it — reusing one arpeggio motif across two different chords by relocating it rather than inventing a new lick per chord. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Fusing a scale-based lick with an arpeggio by entering the lick from the top of the arpeggio.** In the second 12-bar example, the C7 arpeggio is run root-up (3rd-5th-♭7-root-3rd-5th-♭7) and then flows directly into one of the earlier scale-based licks from that same landing point — explicitly called out as "a really cool way to fuse these two things together": arpeggio vocabulary and scale-lick vocabulary treated as one continuous well to draw from, not separate toolkits. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Beginner practice framing, stated directly**: learn the three basic licks first inside the B.B. box, then add the two arpeggio shapes on top to start outlining chord changes, then recombine/vary/expand the licks freely rather than playing them verbatim — explicitly encouraged to "take some of these basic concepts... and create something new out of it."

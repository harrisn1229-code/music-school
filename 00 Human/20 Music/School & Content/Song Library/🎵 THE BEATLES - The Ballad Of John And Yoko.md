# The Ballad Of John And Yoko — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (2 bars vamping on I — the extreme brevity sets a hurried tone for everything after) · Verse/Refrain ×3 · Bridge (8 bars **plus two extra beats**) · Verse/Refrain ×2 · Outro (the three-times-out gambit on the refrain's second half). Verse and refrain are best parsed as one unit; neither is self-sufficient.

Cut on impulse in a single April 1969 session with only John and Paul present, the two of them covering every instrument by overdub — and the record gives no hint of it. A stylised blues that keeps the flavour of the idiom while **carefully avoiding any suggestion of twelve-bar form**.

Chords are rooted only on I, IV and V. The verse vamps four bars on I and then flattens its seventh, so the second half retroactively becomes a four-bar **dominant preparation** for the refrain's opening IV ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). The V chord is repeatedly coloured by a simultaneous major/minor third cross-relation — the blues rub applied to the dominant rather than to the tonic — and the tune leans hard on ♭7 with lighter use of ♭3 ([[BLU-C06 — Blue notes and the blues scale]]).

The vocal arrangement escalates in **three** steps rather than two: John alone for three passes, Paul entering tentatively on the last word of each verse line in the fourth, then singing the fifth all the way through. Three closing signatures — slow triplets in the lead guitar, an added-sixth final chord, and a trailing drum fill that lands after you have already assumed the track is over.

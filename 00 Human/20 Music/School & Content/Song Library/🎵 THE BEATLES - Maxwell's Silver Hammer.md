# Maxwell's Silver Hammer — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Verse (20) · Refrain (8) · Interlude (4), three times over, with the interlude phrase set to words for the only time as the outro. There is **no intro at all** — the first verse simply starts, even though the interlude would have made an ideal one.

Music-hall Paul with straight-faced serial-killer comedy over the top. What is technically worth the visit is a song that **never modulates once** yet spends an unusually dense budget of applied dominants — V-of-ii, V-of-V and V-of-vi do all the travelling ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). The refrain comes in two versions of the same eight bars: one closed, ending on I, which appears three times (sung once, instrumental twice), and one open, ending on V, which heads the later refrains.

The bass is the real feature. It descends stepwise from the verse's first bar, fills bar 4 with a descending scale and bar 8 with a rising one, and answers the tune's rising scale in the connector. The interlude sounds like it roams and is in fact fully closed — the line just walks D down to G and back, with the intervening chords in inversion to keep it smooth ([[POP-C04 — Four chords with a moving bass]]).

The melodic ceiling is **staged in three steps** ([[MEL-C01 — Melodic contour]]): the verse arches to C♯, the connector nudges it to D, and only the refrain reaches the high E. Best small detail in the essay — the anvil hits beats 1 and 2 all song, then moves to beats 2 and 3 in the final bar, and that shift is what seals the ending.

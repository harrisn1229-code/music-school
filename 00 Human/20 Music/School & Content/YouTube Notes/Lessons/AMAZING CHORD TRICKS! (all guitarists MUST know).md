## Synopsis

BERNTH addresses rock/metal players' bias against "boring" major-key writing with Lydian ♯11 chord voicings, then reveals the compositional device behind a companion "10 levels of modern guitar riffs" video: treating each chord of a cadence as the tonic of its own Lydian key rather than staying in one parent scale.

## What's taught

Full extraction routed directly — see [[Chord Voicings]] and [[Diatonic Modes]] for the complete breakdown: Lydian (major scale with a raised 4th, producing a tritone against the root) as a way to add real dissonance/tension to major-mode writing without leaving major entirely; four ♯11 voicings (Emaj9♯11, Amaj7♯11, A(6,♯11), and a movable easy-to-remember maj7♯11 shape) as direct substitutes for plain major chords; and the compositional payoff — reharmonizing each chord change in a progression as the start of a fresh Lydian key (E Lydian → A Lydian → B Lydian → back to E Lydian, over an E-A-B cadence) rather than treating the whole progression as diatonic to one scale.

# What is the Harmonic Minor scale?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-amnre-0I3w)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-amnre-0I3w)

## Synopsis

A short, focused explainer on what harmonic minor is, why it exists (specifically to unlock functional harmony — a real perfect cadence — in the minor key), and its audible "exotic/Arabic" byproduct. Companion piece to [[Songs that use the Melodic Minor scale]], which covers the scale melodic minor further refines out of harmonic minor's own melodic awkwardness.

## Techniques

- **What changes vs. natural minor**: only the 7th degree (leading tone) is raised a half step closer to the octave (e.g. G natural minor's F natural → G harmonic minor's F♯).
- **Why it exists — functional harmony**: natural minor's 5th chord (v) is minor, so it can't produce a real perfect cadence (V→i) — a minor v just doesn't have the same strong pull. Raising the 7th makes the 5th chord MAJOR (V), restoring the strong dominant-to-tonic pull. This is the entire reason it's called "harmonic" minor — it exists to make functional harmony/cadences work in a minor key.
- **The audible byproduct**: raising the 7th widens the gap between the 6th and 7th degrees to a minor 3rd (an augmented 2nd) — unusually wide for a Western scale — giving the upper half of the scale a distinctly "exotic, Arabic-like" sound.
- **Functional harmony recap (tonic/subdominant/dominant), worked on "bury a friend" (Billy Eilish, G harmonic minor)**: i (Gm) = tonic function, fully at rest, no tension. iv (Cm) = subdominant function, some tension, wants to resolve onto the dominant. V (D major, only possible because of the raised 7th) = dominant function, maximum tension, wants to resolve back to tonic. The song's iv→V→i motion is a textbook functional-harmony cadence made possible specifically by the harmonic-minor-raised 7th.
- **Billy Eilish reuses the identical device**: "idontwannabeyouanymore," "when the party's over," "my future," "bad guy" (the exact same chord progression as "bury a friend") all use harmonic minor the same way.
- **Blending harmonic and natural minor within one song, rather than committing to one throughout**: "Girl" (Beatles) alternates harmonic minor (complete with its own perfect cadence) and natural minor across the song.
- **Historical association**: harmonic minor commonly reads as "classical/Baroque" to a modern ear — it was a common default in pre-Romantic classical music, e.g. Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D minor.

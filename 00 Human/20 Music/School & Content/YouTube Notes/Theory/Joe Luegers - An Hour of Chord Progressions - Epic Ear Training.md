## The framing that matters

> *"People have a hard time learning chord progressions by ear because this skill is really a combination of other skills. If you're not solid on your music theory, intervals, cadences, scale degrees, and melodic dictation, you're already working at a major disadvantage."*

This reframes progression-hearing as a **composite** skill rather than a single one — which is why it feels impossible when attacked head-on. It decomposes into five sub-skills the school already teaches separately.

## The five techniques

Full treatment with worked examples in [[Identifying Chord Progressions by Ear]]. In brief:

1. **Dictate the bassline first** — ignore everything else, get the bass scale degrees, and you have the chords. He's blunt that this alone is enough: *"it's really not a bad idea for this to be the only technique you use."* Fails on inversions, which pop uses sparingly.
2. **Major vs. minor** — only three of each exist diatonically, so hearing quality halves the search space immediately.
3. **Listen for the cadence at the end** — identify the V–I and reverse-engineer backwards from it.
4. **Listen for specific scale degrees in the texture** — degree 1 lives only in I, IV and vi; degree 7 only in V and iii. Each degree you can hear eliminates chords.
5. **Build harmonic vocabulary** — know the chords in every key and have progressions memorised. He apologises for this one being slow: *"that's the kind of advice people don't like to get because it's not a fast solution."*

He also tosses vii° out of consideration entirely — *"you're more likely to see fancy things like modal mixture than you are to ever see a diminished triad"* — which is a defensible simplification for pop ear training and worth knowing is a simplification.

## The exercise material

36 progressions — three in each major key, working through the circle of fifths three times. Structure of each:

- A scale and a perfect cadence to establish the key
- A four-chord progression, repeated four times
- **Bass boosted on the last two repeats** — a built-in scaffold for technique 1
- Roman numerals and letter names spoken afterward

His grading advice: **get the Roman numerals right and treat letter names as a bonus.** That matches how [[Course 09 — Ear Training Method]] already scores.

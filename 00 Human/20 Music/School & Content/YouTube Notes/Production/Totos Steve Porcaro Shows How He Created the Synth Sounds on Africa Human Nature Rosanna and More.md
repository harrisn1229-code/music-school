# Toto's Steve Porcaro Shows How He Created the Synth Sounds on Africa, Human Nature, Rosanna, & More!

## Synopsis

A long-form (2+ hour) studio session with Toto's Steve Porcaro, built around his CS-80 (Toto's "Africa," "Rosanna," Michael Jackson's "Human Nature") and running through his early Minimoog/Oberheim rig, the DSX/OB8 sequencer, and a Prophet-5. Roughly the back third of this video re-covers the same Michael Jackson/"Human Nature" studio material already captured on [[Steve Porcaro In-Studio - Writing Human Nature and Unreleased MJ Songs Part 1]] — not re-routed here to avoid duplicating that page's entries. The genuinely new material is the CS-80 deep dive and the DSX/OB8 sequencer segment.

## Techniques

- **CS-80 poly aftertouch is the instrument's defining trait: every held note responds independently to touch pressure, "like individual players," not uniformly** — a musicality Porcaro says later poly-aftertouch-capable MIDI synths still don't fully recreate. The Yamaha GS1 (the CS-80's non-programmable digital successor) carried the same poly-aftertouch trait and the same "breathing" quality as a result.
- **The CS-80's glide pedal has TWO distinct modes, both different from ordinary portamento**: "glissando" mode moves the pitch in true half-steps (not a continuous slide) and can move different held notes in two different directions AT ONCE — release the pedal and the pitch jumps immediately to its destination note. The second mode does the opposite: releasing the pedal STOPS the glide wherever it currently is, letting a player freeze a glissando mid-slide rather than always resolving to the target. Together these give real-time control comparable to writing a glissando part for a string section, but playable live — used on the intros to "After the Love Is Gone" and "Hollow Notes," and throughout "Serious Music" (John Oates).
- **Two go-to CS-80 preset builds, reused across a career of sessions**: (1) a **brass preset**, structured around initial level feeding into the filter (a specific gain-into-filter staging Porcaro still calls "hard to wrap my head around") — used on "Africa"'s verses; (2) a custom **panel** patch (built from scratch on the front panel rather than a preset) — used on "Rosanna"'s solo and Toto's "Mama," sometimes layered with extra aftertouch. Standard chain for both: a Roland Space Echo for slap delay, and a real studio live chamber (Sunset Sound, Capitol) for reverb — no plate/spring substitutes mentioned.
- **Without a CS-80 available, the nearest substitute for its clav-adjacent tone is a Prophet-5 or Jupiter-8** — but Porcaro is explicit that neither "breathes" the same way, since the touch-response/poly-aftertouch behavior is what the substitute patches can't reproduce, not the raw timbre.
- **DSX/OB8 (Oberheim) polyphonic step-sequencer: individual sequences can be triggered live, on the fly, mid-performance** — described as something "not easy to do with a computer, or really most other sequencers." Porcaro's first-ever synth rig (a Minimoog through a monophonic Oberheim DS2 sequencer) had the same live-triggerable-sequence behavior; the later polyphonic DSX (designed by Marcus Ryle, alongside the OB-8) kept it.
- **Record a sequence into the DSX at a very slow tempo, then speed the whole thing up on playback with no pitch change** — because the sequencer is storing trigger/CV events rather than audio, changing playback speed changes timing only, not pitch, unlike a tape-speed trick. A genuinely useful way to program a fast, intricate pattern by hand at a tempo you can actually play accurately.

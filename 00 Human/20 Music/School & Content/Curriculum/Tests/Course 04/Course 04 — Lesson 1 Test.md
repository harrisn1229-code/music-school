> [!danger]- 🔒 Answer key — do not open until you have answered every question
> **Q1.** **For the rest of that measure only, and only on that exact line or space**
> Both halves matter: the duration and the octave restriction.
>
> **Q2.** **No — an accidental applies only to the exact line or space it sits on**
> The same letter in another octave is unaffected. This is the half people miss.
>
> **Q3.** **The bar line**
> *Also accepted:* the end of the measure · a natural sign within the bar
>
> **Q4.** **Same pitch is a tie · different pitches is a slur**
>
> **Q5.** **Tie: attack only the first and hold through. Slur: play legato**
> On guitar a slur means a hammer-on or a pull-off — an instruction about technique, not only phrasing.
>
> **Q6.** **3 and 3½**
> A dot adds half the note's own value, so 2 + 1 = 3. The second dot adds half of what the first added, so + ½.
>
> **Q7.** **The second dot adds half of what the first dot added — the wrong answer is 4**
> Answering 4 assumes the second dot repeats the first rather than halving it again.
>
> **Q8.** **1½ and 1¾**
> A quarter is 1 beat, the dot adds ½, and the second dot adds half of that ½.
>
> **Q9.** **Play an octave higher than written — cancelled by *loco***
> *Also accepted:* cancelled by the end of the dotted bracket
>
> **Q10.** **`8vb` is the octave-lower version — both exist to keep ledger lines off the page**
> Without them a high or low passage becomes unreadable.
>
> **Q11.** **Back to the sign (𝄋), play from there to the *To Coda* mark, then jump to the Coda**
> Three moves, in that order.
>
> **Q12.** **It skips the first two moves, and navigation errors lose your place rather than costing one note**
> *Rubric (5):* says the answer omits returning to the sign and playing up to the To Coda mark (2) · says the jump is where readers get lost (1) · says a navigation mistake breaks the whole performance, unlike a single wrong note (2)
> "Go to the coda" names only the third move and drops the return to the sign and the stretch you play before jumping. That matters more than most notation details because of what failure looks like. Misread a dot and you play one note for the wrong length; misread the roadmap and you're in the wrong part of the chart with the band somewhere else, and there's no recovering mid-phrase. That's exactly why the 30-second scan in Lesson 3 checks the roadmap before anything except the key and time signature.

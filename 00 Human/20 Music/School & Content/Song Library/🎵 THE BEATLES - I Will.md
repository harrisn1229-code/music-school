## Production & Arrangement
McCartney's warm little love song (White Album) — famously he **sang the bass line vocally** (a mouth-bass 'boom') instead of playing bass, over gentle acoustic and light percussion. Intimate, simple, perfectly voiced.

## Steal this
- **Vocal/mouth bass:** sing the bass line for a soft, round low end when a real bass would be too heavy.
- **Small and warm:** acoustic + light perc + close vocal; simplicity as the craft.
- Sweet [[Secondary Dominants|secondary-dominant]] color in a simple frame.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — I Will]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #144.*

**Form.** A tiny song on one of pop's most worn progressions whose interest is concentrated almost entirely in the **outro**, which he rates the most sophisticated thing in it. Three structural oddities for something this short: **no intro at all**, an outro long in proportion to the whole, and different lyrics in every verse, so a two-minute song still tells a story. Verse 1 is **9 bars** — a square eight elided with a two-bar interlude that separates it from verse 2; verse 2 is exactly 8, with the tonic sustained through the whole eighth bar; bridge 8; the outro is grown out of a morphed final verse and is itself a morph of the bridge.

**Harmony.** `Verse (two chords per bar): I – vi · ii7 – V · I – vi · iii – I7 · IV – V · vi – I · IV – V · I – vi · [interlude] ii – V`. Stock material, deliberately. The move that matters is the **added seventh on the tonic**, which converts a resting I into **V-of-IV** and pulls the phrase forward instead of letting it settle ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]) — mid-verse, and again on the held chord ending verse 2. `Bridge: IV – iii · vi · ii – V · I (as V-of-IV) · IV – iii · vi · V-of-V · V`: the verses are harmonically closed, the bridge the opposite in both directions, starting away from I and ending wide open on V via its own V-of-V. `Outro: IV – V · vi – vii° – I (×2) · IV – V · vi · ii – V · ♭VI (≈3 bars) · I · IV – iii · vi · ii – V · I` — the last verse deforms at bar 6 into a three-strikes rhetorical loop, and the third statement stretches into a long phrase arriving on **♭VI**, the harmonic climax and the only genuinely foreign chord in the song ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]).

**Notable.** The verse tune is disjunct with clean arches and upward octave leaps; the bridge answers with far more stepwise motion, which is most of why it reads as contrast despite sharing the chord vocabulary. **There is no bass guitar — Paul scat-sings the bassline**, recorded very close, and the vocal staging runs single → parallel thirds at the bridge → unison → thirds again in the outro.

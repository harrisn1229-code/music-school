# Course 11 — Bass Writing & Design

How a bassline relates to everything else, and how to write a good one. The line first, then what it does against the drums, the chords and the vocal, then the sound it comes out as — in that order, because the relationships are the subject and the tone is the smallest part of it.

**What it assumes**

- [[Course 10 — Drum Programming & Beat Construction]] — step 1 of the method is *finish the drum groove, then write the bass around it*. It's a real dependency, not an ordering preference.

- [[Course 06 — Loop Harmony & Modal Colour]] — a moving bass under static harmony is [[POP-C04 — Four chords with a moving bass]], approached from underneath.

> [!info] Composition, not technique
> This is a writing and arrangement course. It covers **how bass lines relate to everything else and how to write a good one** — no fretboard drills, no scale positions, no repertoire. [[Bass Design]] covers the sound in Lesson 3; everywhere else the notes are the subject.

> [!info] Taught from zero
> No prior knowledge assumed and nothing skipped — Lesson 1 starts at one root note per bar, which is genuinely the beginner rung. Some of it will be revision, and that's the cheaper error. Run `/school test 11` early if a lesson turns out to be known.

## The lessons

### Lesson 1 — Writing the Line

**Read**
- [ ] [[Bassline Writing]] → *The method*, steps 1–6 and 11

**The material**
- Two steps open the method, and almost every bad bassline is one of them skipped.
- **[[BAS-C01 — Groove first, bass second — roots first, by ear|Groove first, bass second.]]** Finish the drum groove, then write around it. The trap inside that is assuming the bass follows the kick map — it doesn't, it locks to the **pocket**. Rhythmic choices made with no drums playing are provisional, and a real loop will overrule them.
- **Roots first, by ear.** Land the roots before any decoration. The beginner rung is literally one root per bar on each chord change, and chord quality doesn't matter yet, because only the root is sounding.
- That step is diagnostic: not hearing the roots is an ear gap rather than a writing problem, which is what [[Course 09 — Ear Training Method]] is for. Register is the trap in the same breath — the identical line an octave too low goes from fine to undefined mud, and the fix is moving it up, not processing it.
- Then motion. **[[BAS-C02 — Octaves are the default vocabulary|Octaves are the default vocabulary]]** — up and back, occasionally through a passing tone, the 5th above being the classic. Fills come from **stealing the melody's rhythm**, so the busyness hides inside phrasing that's already there.
- **Record busy and mute down:** play a too-busy line through the whole loop, then keep only the killer moments, never repeating a fill in consecutive phrases.
- Two constraints close the lesson. **[[BAS-C03 — The band test, and never two basslines at once|The band test]]** — imagine a bassist physically playing it, and rewrite it if no player would phrase it that way. And the hard rule: **never two genuinely different basslines at once.** Two distinct low melodic lines read as mud, not richness. Doubling the *same* line is a different thing and is fine.

**Concepts**
- [[BAS-C01 — Groove first, bass second — roots first, by ear]]
- [[BAS-C02 — Octaves are the default vocabulary]]
- [[BAS-C03 — The band test, and never two basslines at once]]

**Homework**
- [ ] **Review** — name the first two steps of the method and the trap inside each. Then say what the band test is and what it rejects.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[One-Finger-Per-Fret 5-Level Daily Drill]] · bass · 100→200 bpm. Level 4's root-fifth-octave riff puts this lesson's roots-first-then-octave-motion method physically under the hand.
- [ ] **Apply** (~15 min) — write over C–Am–F–G as one root per bar. Add subdivision. Add the octave. Stop there. Then solo the low end of a current project and count the distinct melodic lines in it.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 11 — Lesson 1 Test]]

### Lesson 2 — Bass Against Harmony, Melody and the Kick

**Read**
- [ ] [[Bassline Writing]] → *The method* steps 7–10, and the funk/disco and section-energy bullets
- [ ] [[Chromatic Bass Lines]]

**The material**
- "How busy should the bass be" isn't a taste question — it's a question about what else is moving. **[[BAS-C04 — Busy basslines only work under static harmony|Showcase basslines only work over static held chords.]]** When the harmony above is busy, only root notes work; with a vocal or a synth hook on top, the bass supports.
- The corollary is the useful half: **two chords means the riffs ARE the line.** Static harmony moves all the interest into the fills, and one perfectly placed fill can be the entire event.
- **[[BAS-C05 — Bass and keys as a conversation|Bass and keys are a conversation]]**, which is this course's central question in one line: what story is the bass telling, what story are the keys telling, and do they talk to each other? Keep the harmony simple and let the exchange carry it.
- The polyphonic version is a bass patch playing its own upper voice a 3rd or 5th above the root, in **contrary motion** — genuine two-voice writing rather than a melodic line with a thicker patch.
- For groove, one move does most of the work: **[[BAS-C06 — Syncopate off the kick to make it funky|switch to a 16th grid and move some notes off the kick's strong beats]]** onto the weak spots in between. Layer octave uppers and downers for bounce. Between phrases, a **chromatic walk-up or walk-down** connects regardless of scale.
- **[[BAS-C10 — Every section gets a new line, all of them share a root|Across sections]]**, write a genuinely new rhythm, register and contour for each — rising, peak, B section, wind-down — while every line keeps returning to the **same root as home.** That's what keeps four different basslines reading as one song.

**Concepts**
- [[BAS-C04 — Busy basslines only work under static harmony]]
- [[BAS-C05 — Bass and keys as a conversation]]
- [[BAS-C06 — Syncopate off the kick to make it funky]]
- [[BAS-C10 — Every section gets a new line, all of them share a root]]

**Homework**
- [ ] **Review** — say when the bass gets to show off and when it can only play roots. Then name the single move that makes a line funky.
- [ ] **Apply** (~15 min) — take a line locked to the kick, move a third of its notes onto 16th offbeats, and connect two phrases with a chromatic walk-up. Then write a keys part that *answers* it rather than moving with it.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 11 — Lesson 2 Test]]

### Lesson 3 — The Low End as a Shared Resource

**Read**
- [ ] [[Bassline Writing]] → *808-specific* and step 10
- [ ] [[Bass Design]], then [[Kick Design]] → the kick/bass clash bullets
- [ ] [[Sidechaining]] and [[Masking and Carving]]

**The material**
- Kick and bass fight because they're both trying to own the same twenty hertz. **[[BAS-C07 — Decide who owns the sub — don't EQ both|The fix isn't EQ on both — it's deciding who owns the sub.]]**
- Put an analyser on each, play them together, find where each fundamental actually peaks, pick one to hold the low end, and high-pass the other until the clash stops. If high-passing the kick guts it, swap the kick and cut the new one instead. The same decision made in *time* rather than frequency is sidechaining, or automating kick length by section.
- **[[BAS-C08 — Tune the 808 up high, then drop it back|Tuning an 808: pitch it up an octave or more, tune it there, then drop it back.]]** Never guess at pitch down at 40 Hz — the ear genuinely can't do that job in place.
- Slides on an audio 808: pitch it with warp **off**, then set warp mode to **Tones**, then draw the slides in the clip's transposition envelope.
- When the part is right but the tone is wrong, **[[BAS-C09 — The sound ladder, and limiter-not-compressor|walk the ladder]]** rather than processing your way out: synth patch → different synth → real or sampled bass guitar → DI plus amp sim.
- Then control it with a **limiter, not a compressor.** Bass wants a ceiling with its dynamics intact, not gain-riding that pumps. Saturate for body, and sidechain last.

**Concepts**
- [[BAS-C07 — Decide who owns the sub — don't EQ both]]
- [[BAS-C08 — Tune the 808 up high, then drop it back]]
- [[BAS-C09 — The sound ladder, and limiter-not-compressor]]

**Homework**
- [ ] **Review** — kick and bass both peak at 55 Hz. Say what you do, in order. Then describe how to tune an 808 and why you can't do it in place.
- [ ] **Apply** (~15 min) — analyse the kick and bass fundamentals on a current track, decide ownership in writing, and high-pass the loser. Then detune an 808, try to tune it at sub, fail, and tune it an octave up instead.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 11 — Lesson 3 Test]]

# Understanding Kaytranada's vision with Neal Pogue

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a_aoMLjyp40)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a_aoMLjyp40)

## Synopsis

A short, mix-philosophy-heavy breakdown from Neal Pogue (mix engineer for Kaytranada, OutKast, TLC, and others) walking through his mix of a Kaytranada/Kali Uchis track. Unusually light on plugin-chain detail compared to most Mix with the Masters entries — at 9:39 and ~1,000 words, it's really three ideas stretched across the runtime: (1) respect what an artist has already printed into a demo rather than reverse-engineering or replacing it, (2) an ears-first, reactive workflow — never reach for EQ or a compressor before actually listening to decide whether it's needed, and (3) a concrete, deliberately minimal 2-bus/Stereo Bus routine when he does reach for something: an unowned Waves L2 Ultramaximizer followed by Ozone 9's EQ and Maximizer, both barely engaged. What's most valuable here isn't a chain to copy move-for-move — it's the restraint discipline itself, backed by two named, concrete data points (the L2 threshold, the Ozone EQ gains) that make "light touch" measurable rather than just a vibe.

## Techniques

- **Session organization: color-code a track/session visually by artist** (Kali Uchis's track colored pink) — a minor but real visual-workflow habit for keeping a multi-artist or multi-vocalist session legible at a glance. *(0:00)*
- **Vocal mic choice matters more than processing on a strong-fit voice.** Kali Uchis's "smooth, appealing pop vocal" is credited to "the right microphone" being used at the tracking stage — no specific mic or setting named, just a reminder that a good vocal choice up front can mean minimal mix intervention later. *(0:20)*
- **Respect what the artist already printed — don't rebuild or reproduce a demo's baked-in delay/reverb.** Kaytranada had already printed a delay and "a distinct reverb" onto the vocals before Neal got the stems. His stated rule, given to artists generally: *"leave on the track what you're married to"* — if you've been listening to a demo for months and love a specific sound/trick, keep it printed rather than stripping it, because the mix engineer will otherwise waste time chasing a reference that's already sitting right there, and a rebuilt version will always read as "different" even if technically similar. The mixer's own creative contribution ("I have a lot of room to do me") layers ON TOP of what's kept, rather than starting from a blank slate. → [[Mix Handoff]]
- **Restraint as the default: if a synth already sounds good, don't touch it.** "I really didn't add that much EQ to any of the synths. They already sounded good... I have no ego at all. If it already sounds good, I don't have to touch it." Framed explicitly as "sometimes with mixing, it's not what you do, it's what you don't do."
- **Ears-first workflow: never reach for EQ or a compressor before listening first to decide whether it's actually needed.** *"I don't reach for the EQ or a compressor until I listen first... you have to see with your ears."* Self-described as coming from the pre-DAW "VU meter age" rather than the "monitor age" — waveforms are useful only for "fixing little things," never for judging whether a sound is active or good; that judgment call is by ear alone. → [[Critical Listening for Mastering]]
- **Genre-reference framing, not a technique:** a string part on the track reminded him of the Philly soul sound / Barry White / disco — a personal-taste observation about how the arrangement lands, not a processing move; logged here only, not routed further since it's song-specific.
- **The Stereo Bus (2-bus) is treated as reactive, not automatic.** *"When it comes to compression, only if I need it. It's a reactive thing. I know some people, they like to go just straight to the compressor without really listening first. Me, I love to listen to music first and see if something needs something."* On this particular song he didn't use a dedicated stereo bus compressor (an unnamed hardware "board" he sometimes routes through, sometimes doesn't) — but he did engage his usual two go-to plugins inside Pro Tools, lightly. → [[Master Chain]], [[Critical Listening for Mastering]]
- **Named 2-bus go-to chain, order matters: an (unowned) Waves L2 Ultramaximizer FIRST, then Ozone 9's EQ + Maximizer.** *"My two go-to Stereo Bus compressors are the L2 and the Ozone 9... for the L2 which I usually put before the Ozone, I just like how that sounds."* Concrete settings, both deliberately light:
  - **L2 (Waves Ultramaximizer):** Threshold **−2.6**. A/B'd bypassed vs. engaged — "very light, but it opens it up a little bit more." Unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]].
  - **Ozone 9 Equalizer:** two moves total, both tiny — **+0.3 dB ~80 Hz**, **+0.8 dB ~8 kHz**. *"I'm never a heavy EQer unless I know that it needs it... just light little touches."* → [[Ozone 9 Equalizer]]
  - **Ozone 9 Maximizer:** used for "a little more beef, a little more heaviness," run light on this song rather than pushed for loudness — "just give it a little bit more 'umphhh.'" → [[Ozone 9 Maximizer]]
  - **Stated overall stance:** "I'm not heavy on the compression, I'm just not. A lot of guys like to compress heavy. It's not my thing." A/B demo confirms the whole chain reads as extra clarity and a slightly harder hit, not raw loudness. → [[Master Chain]]

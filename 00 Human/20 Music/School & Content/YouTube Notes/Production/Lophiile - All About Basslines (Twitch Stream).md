# Lophiile — All About Basslines (Twitch Stream)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tnruvmw_OWc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tnruvmw_OWc)

## Synopsis

A 3h20m focus stream where lophiile builds beats over Mat Zara guitar loops specifically to teach how he writes basslines: groove first, root notes by ear, then fills built from octaves and the melody's own rhythm, then ruthless muting of everything that distracts. Along the way he gives his full drum-shaping playbook (the "car EQ" kick curve, mono snares, saturate-don't-limit drums), his limit-not-compress hot take, the UAD Ampex → Pro-L "FL-bump" master trick, 808 slide workflow with warp-mode details, and LFO Tool / Max-for-Live-LFO movement tricks. The single richest bassline-writing resource in the vault — it seeded the new [[Bassline Writing]] technique note.

## Techniques

### Bassline writing (the core lesson)
- **Groove first, bass second** — he always finishes the drum groove, then writes the bassline *around* it ("I make the groove first and then make the bass line around that"). Bass notes do NOT need to start on the kicks. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Find the root notes by ear first** — before any fills, land the roots under the chords; this is where ear training pays. Once the roots work, "you can start to play with what goes in between them." → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Song Practice]]
- **Octaves are the default fill vocabulary** — jump up the octave, drop back down, occasionally hit a passing note (e.g. a 5th) on the way; a whole usable bassline can be "mainly octaves with the occasional tiny fill." → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Mirror the melody/guitar rhythm** — for fills, copy the *rhythm* of what the guitar/melody just played (not necessarily the notes): "you can still have it pretty busy, but it's hiding in" the existing phrasing, which keeps it cohesive. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Record busy, then mute down to the best parts** — play a too-busy bassline through the whole loop, then go back and delete everything except the killer moments; "anything that's distracting, you've got to get rid of." Also: never repeat the exact same fill twice in a row — vary the second pass. → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Imagine a bassist in the band playing it** — his reality check for whether a line is physically/musically plausible ("kind of stupid but it helps me").
- **Don't get in the way** — if the song will carry vocals, the bass supports; busy showcase basslines (Chris Brown "Fine China") only work because the harmony above them is static held chords. If there's a riff/synth hook, simplify the bass. → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Masking and Carving]]
- **When there are only two chords, the riffs ARE the bassline** — static harmony means the interest lives entirely in the fills between roots.
- **One-fill placement** — sometimes the best bassline has a single fill "in the perfect place" (echoed in the Maria Isabel breakdown).

### 808s and bass sounds
- **808 slide workflow (audio 808s):** pitch the sample to the right note FIRST with warp *off* (clean stretch), then enable warp in **Tones** mode ("best for bass"), open the clip **transposition envelope**, and draw slides directly — way easier than MIDI slides. → [[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]], [[SUB Slot]]
- **Tune 808s up high, then bring them back down** — pitch the 808 up an octave+ to actually *hear* the note center, tune it there, then drop it back; never guess at the note while it's sub-low. → [[Kick Design]], [[SUB Slot]]
- **Real-bass VSTs he reaches for:** NI **Scarbee Rickenbacker (Pure Neck DI)** — "one of the best bass VSTs" (in Komplete → [[Kontakt 7]]); NI Motown bass; **Trilian** is his main now; Diva is good-sounding but pricey/CPU-heavy for what it is. Chain on his preset: Ableton [[Saturator]] "A Bit Warmer" preset → compressor (old preset, barely doing anything) → **Ampeg amp sim** for realism. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[BASS Slot]]
- **Sidechain the bass last** — after the line and sound are right, duck it to the kick and call it done. → [[Sidechaining]]

### Drum shaping (repeated all stream)
- **Soft snare rescue: saturate it** — "right there it already sounds amazing" — then EQ out the "papery" high end; you want it to slap in the mids, not hiss. → [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Mono the snare** ([[Utility]]) — "snares are dope mono" (stereo-offset snares only if you can really make it work); also fix samples that are panned slightly off-center. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **The "car EQ" kick curve:** kill the very top with a **shelf**, not just a filter — a full low-pass "sounds like a kick under a pillow" and loses the attack; shelve the lower-highs down, filter only the extreme highs. Plus his signature **~1 kHz dip** for boxy/distorted thumpy kicks. → [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]], [[Kick Design]], [[EQ Eight]]
- **Narrow a too-stereo kick** — mono/narrow it before anything else. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Shorten + warp-tighten hits** — trim length for punch, and *speed a sample up slightly with warp on* (maintains pitch) to snap it tighter into the groove.
- **Don't limit drums — saturate/distort them.** He limits the master and limits bass/keys, but drums get saturation for control instead. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Compression Staging]]
- **Place the shaker where there's room** — noticed the lo-fi hi-hat had no "high highs," so he boosted the shaker exactly there to poke through: "you don't want things fighting — pick where everything sits." → [[Masking and Carving]], [[AIR Slot]]
- **Flams: use a real one** — his programmed flam sounded fake, so he searched "flam" in the browser and used a sampled one: "you can't fake everything."
- **LFO Tool on loops/shakers for groove dynamics** — half-note duck shape to make static loops breathe; it also fakes a snare-side duck. Alternative he explored live: **Max for Live LFO mapped to Auto Filter frequency**, shape tweaked, kept *really subtle*. He prefers Auto Pan's fixed note values over pure random ("random sounds off on some bars"). → [[LFOTool]], [[LFO]], [[Sidechaining]]

### Limiting / master (the hot takes)
- **Limit, don't compress** — "I use limiting 90% of the time on bass, keys, anything that needs control… compression is hard to control and you can always hear it." Waves **L1** all the time on sources. (His own caveat: "I'm a weirdo, don't take it for bible.") → [[Limiting]], [[Compression]]
- **The FL-bump trick: UAD Ampex ATR-102 (default setting) → [[Pro-L 2]] on the master.** "That's how I get my shit to bump and distort cleanly like an FL beat" — kicks distort "so nice," low end noticeably thicker. Chat suggested the free **Kilohearts limiter** as a stand-in — Nate owns [[kHs Limiter]], so the owned chain is [[Kramer Tape]]/[[Cassette]] → [[kHs Limiter]] or [[Pro-L 2]]. → [[Master Chain]], [[Clipping and Loudness]]
- **Pro-L latency caveat** — Pro-L 2 adds noticeable latency (worse than L1); he keeps it on the master anyway while producing but won't stack limiters on individual tracks. *(Check lookahead/true-peak settings to reduce it — general starting point.)* → [[Latency-Free Tracking]]
- **Legacy Ableton compressor to stop kick clicks** — the old "Compressor (Live 8)" doesn't click on fast attack the way the current one can. *(niche)*
- **Waves SoundShifter on the master for vibe** — small pitch shifts of the whole beat to audition keys/feel; "I like the sound of the SoundShifter on the master." → [[Pitch and Formant]]

### Workflow / misc
- **Resample the whole beat to apply an effect section-wide** — instead of automating a radio/speaker EQ on every track, bounce the beat, drop the "speaker" effect on the resampled clip for that section (his 90s Timbaland radio moment). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Automation]]
- **J-37 tape on chimes/textures** — saturation + pitch wobble + can double as tape delay. Owned equivalents: [[Kramer Tape]], [[Cassette]]. → [[TEXTURE Slot]]
- **Audition the beat in different keys** — pitch the whole thing around "just to see how I feel" (via SoundShifter on the master).
- **Sample sources named:** Maxwell pack kicks (his always-default — "it doesn't matter that much and I hate looking for kicks"), Monty Booker pack snares/hats, Boi-1da pack, Fred pack (sticks loop), 112 acapellas for vibe-testing, **Pro Tools stock loops** ("don't sleep on them"), his own Splice pack. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Groove obsession pays** — "see what happens when you spend time on the groove"; if you can't stop looping it, it might be a placement; if you're fighting to save a beat, it usually isn't.

# Ian Kirkpatrick — Bad Liar (Selena Gomez) Breakdown + Q&A

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WrJWYpehZLY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WrJWYpehZLY)

## Synopsis

A full teardown of Selena Gomez's "Bad Liar" (opened for the first time since 2018) plus a long, unusually candid Q&A. The song is Ian's **lesson in restraint** — built on the Talking Heads "Psycho Killer" bassline (recreated in Trilian), a drone/noise bed that "sets the scene," all-Melodyne harmonies (Selena was unavailable), and a brick-wall-EQ harmonic kick. The Q&A is career gold: don't be precious, advice changes with career stage, the Mike Caren acapella-reproduction training, and "starts are the shit you flush to reach the diamonds."

## Techniques — production

- **Bassline = "Psycho Killer" (Talking Heads), recreated in Trilian, super-chopped, pitched down→up** — Julia Michaels played the Psycho Killer bass; they wrote to it, he **re-recorded it in Trilian** (not the sample) and chopped/pitched it. (David Byrne graciously took a 33% split.) → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Two-layer bass: Trilian (low end removed) + a sub in the *same octave* that doesn't overlap** — same principle as Don't Start Now; he shows a channel-compare confirming they don't occupy the same space, and notes he'd **sidechain the sub to the pulsing bass** today to avoid interference (2018 Ian didn't; the mixer likely did). → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Bassline Writing]], [[SUB Slot]], [[Sidechaining]]
- **A drone/noise bed rides the whole song and drops out right before the hook** — a tone + noise printed so that **taking it out is noticed** ("something happened"). → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Brick-wall-EQ harmonic kick (split kick)** — two copies of the same kick; one gets a **brick-wall EQ at ~41 Hz**, which produces a weird added harmonic. "First time I realized you can get a harmonic effect from a brick-wall cutoff" (uses Q3 for it now). → [[Kick Design]], [[EQ]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **All harmonies + doubles built in Melodyne from the lead** — Selena couldn't track harmonies, so he **generated every harmony and double in Melodyne** off her lead, and chopped the "ninnin" background rhythmically for intensity. → [[Melodyne]], [[Pitch and Formant]], [[2 Backup Vocals Group]]
- **Process doubles: de-ess them HARD (super dark), keep the lead bright** — de-ess the doubles heavily so you can push them louder **without their S's competing** with the lead's sibilance. → [[De-essing]], [[2 Backup Vocals Group]], [[AIR Slot]]
- **Manual vocal alignment, not VocAlign** — "leave some room for nature" (Tom Higginson's advice); time-stretch algorithms do unknown things to the vocal. → [[Melodyne]], [[Phase Alignment]]
- **Dr. Rex / ReCycle for transient-sliced found percussion** — a decade-old "box tap" file (tapping a cardboard box) sliced in Dr. Rex is the source of many little noises and the claps (claps also = Julia/Justin/Ian clapping in Oregon). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Reason for texture: Combinator presets (Malström/Thor) + Pulverizer + Scream + imager-to-mono** — a chord pad from the **Reason vocoder's crackle on a sine wave** (a harmonic-EQ side effect he loves). → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Ben Rice's printed vocal FX** — the vocal producer sent effects that **used Selena's breath for rhythm** + printed throws. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[De-essing]]

## Philosophy — "the producer sets the scene"

- **Establish the listener's reference, then exceed it** — inspired by the Neptunes (No Doubt): they hid **white noise** in a track and only revealed it by filtering it. "The producer establishes what's loud, what's bright, what's wide — the listener has no reference, you do — so show them 'bright,' then go brighter; 'wide,' then go wider." The root of his set-the-scene / responsible-panning thinking. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Depth and Space]], [[Ian Kirkpatrick - SLDMML pt 1 (Tuning and Timing a Vocal)|responsible panning]]
- **Restraint is the production** — "Bad Liar is a lesson in restraint"; the bass is "one bit," the drums fit on one screen. Aaron Bayshuck (Interscope) helped him **not add more** — the song had legs. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Ian Kirkpatrick - The Production Secret Behind Huge Pop Hits]]

## Philosophy — career (Q&A)

- **Don't be precious; don't tie self-worth to the work** — rejection is constant; compartmentalize or it's unsustainable. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - On Loops, Artist Ego and Originality]]
- **Advice changes with career stage** — a 10-years-deep answer ("I don't care if it gets cut") is *wrong* for a beginner; he now checks how he'd have answered years ago. 
- **"Starts are the shit you flush to reach the diamonds"** — reframe your pile of unfinished ideas: they're what has to come out to get to the good ones, and they seed your subconscious. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Ian Kirkpatrick - On Loops, Artist Ego and Originality]]
- **Reproduce-an-acapella as training (Mike Caren)** — Caren sent him Jason Derulo acapellas to reproduce for years; a great way to learn (and how he trains/scouts producers). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **"Banger vs hit = the top line"** — a track is a track; the crossover to a *hit* is the songwriting and how it interacts with the production. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Ratios are real** — ~23 songs done on the Lizzo project, ~3 will make the record; throw a lot at the wall. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - On Loops, Artist Ego and Originality]]

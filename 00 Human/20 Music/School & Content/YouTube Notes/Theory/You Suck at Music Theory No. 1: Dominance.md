# You Suck at Music Theory No. 1: Dominance

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uaivGz5sryQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uaivGz5sryQ)

## What's taught
The series-opener: the authentic cadence (V→I) as the strongest resolution in tonal music, how adding a ♭7 to the V chord turns it into a dominant 7th that pulls even harder toward the tonic, and a short compositional payoff — turning any diatonic chord into a dominant 7th to briefly point at (and modulate toward) a new key a 5th below it.

## Techniques
- **The authentic cadence (V → I) is the strongest resolution move in a key** — builds triads on every degree of C major, isolates the V (G major) resolving to I (C major), and names it the source of "resolution, warmth, and security." → [[Chord Progressions]]
- **Add the ♭7 to the V chord to build the dominant 7th** — take the V chord (G major) and add a flatted 7th on top (F natural, NOT the F♯ that the G major scale would normally supply) to get G7; the "flat" is relative to the diatonic 7th of that scale-degree's own major scale, not an absolute flat. That dominant 7th pulls toward the I even more strongly than the plain triad did — described as "always wanting to follow the alpha," i.e. resolve down to the tonic. → [[Seventh Chords]]
- **Invert the destination chord for a smoother landing** — resolving V7 → I with the I chord voiced in an inversion (5th on the bottom instead of the root) rather than root position, so the top voice moves less. → [[Chord Inversions]]
- **Turn any diatonic chord into a dominant 7th to modulate toward a new key** — inside an existing progression, take a non-tonic chord (e.g. the IV, F major, in the key of C) and add a dominant ♭7 on top to make it F7; that F7 now behaves like a V7 and pulls down a 5th to a NEW key (B♭ major) rather than back to the original tonic — an instant, smooth modulation with chromatic top-voice motion built in. → [[Secondary Dominants]], [[Modulation (Key Change)]]

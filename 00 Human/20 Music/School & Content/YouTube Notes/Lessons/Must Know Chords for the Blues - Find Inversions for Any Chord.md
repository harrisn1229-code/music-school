# Must Know Chords for the Blues - Find Inversions for Any Chord

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRz8dACIpjI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRz8dACIpjI)

## Synopsis
Jack Ruch's short rhythm-guitar lesson on dominant 7th chords — the chord quality that dominates blues harmony — teaching not just a set of shapes to memorize but a **reusable method for deriving the inversions of any chord type, on any string set, from a single starting voicing.** Worked entirely in E7: four inversions (root position + 3 inversions) on each of three adjacent four-string sets (top four, middle four, bottom four), for 12 shapes total. The method itself — cycle every chord tone up to the next role in the chord's formula (root→3rd→5th→♭7→root) — is the most reusable thing in the video; the 12 E7 shapes are the worked example. Ruch then applies the shapes to an 8-bar blues based on "Key to the Highway" (E7-A7-B7), demonstrating a three-stage practice progression from "one voicing per chord" up to "freely mixing all 12 shapes across the whole neck." Short, dense, and squarely aimed at rhythm/comping vocabulary rather than soloing.

## What's taught

- **Why dominant 7th specifically:** a dominant 7th chord = root, major 3rd, 5th, ♭7th, and in a blues, often **all three chords of a I-IV-V progression are dominant** (not just the V, as in a major key) — so a rhythm player needs this shape all over the neck, not just in one spot. *(~0:20)* → [[Blues Form and Harmony]] § *Why every chord is a dominant 7th*, [[Seventh Chords]]
- **The universal inversion-finding method: cycle each chord tone up to the next role in the chord's formula.** For a dominant 7th (R-3-5-♭7), take any 4-note voicing and move each note to the next slot in the cycle — root moves to the 3rd's position, 3rd moves to the 5th's, 5th moves to the ♭7's, and the ♭7 loops back around to the root. Repeating this cycle three more times from any starting voicing walks through root position and all three inversions in order, and generates a genuinely new fingering each time rather than just relabeling the same notes. Explicitly framed as generalizable to **any chord type on any string set** — swap in that chord's own formula (e.g. major triad R-3-5, minor 7 R-♭3-5-♭7) and the same "advance every note one slot" move produces its inversions too. → [[Chord Inversions]]
- **Top four strings (D-G-B-e), E7 root position:** root on the D string 2nd fret, voiced bottom-to-top **root-5th-♭7th-3rd** (E-B-D-G♯). *(~0:40)*
- **Top four strings, 1st inversion:** applying the cycle once — root(E)→3rd's slot, 5th(B)→♭7's slot, ♭7(D)→root's slot, 3rd(G♯)→5th's slot — produces **3rd-♭7th-root-5th** (G♯-D-E-B) bottom-to-top. *(~1:15)*
- **Top four strings, 2nd inversion:** cycling again produces **5th-root-3rd-♭7th** (B-E-G♯-D) bottom-to-top. *(~1:45)*
- **Top four strings, 3rd (final) inversion:** cycling a third time produces **♭7th-3rd-5th-root** (D-G♯-B-E) bottom-to-top, completing the loop back toward root position an octave up. *(~2:00)*
- **Middle four strings (A-D-G-B), same recipe relocated:** move the same root position voicing down a string set by playing the root (E) on the A string 7th fret instead of the D string — still **root-5th-♭7th-3rd** bottom-to-top, but a different physical fingering than the top-string-set root position (same "chord recipe," new shape). *(~2:45)*
- **Middle four strings, 1st inversion: 3rd-♭7th-root-5th**, same cycle-order relationship as the top-string-set inversions. *(~3:10)*
- **Middle four strings, 2nd inversion — transcript slightly garbled here:** Ruch names this shape moving "up again" to a **root-3rd-♭7th** voicing; by the established cycle pattern (5th→root, root→3rd, 3rd→5th, ♭7th→root) the expected 4-note result is **root-3rd-5th-♭7th**, so the auto-transcript most likely dropped the "5th" from the spoken list — treat the note order as a best-effort reconstruction rather than a verified quote. *(~3:20)*
- **Middle four strings, 3rd (final) inversion: ♭7th-3rd-5th-root.** *(~3:35)*
- **Bottom four strings (E-A-D-G), root position: root-5th-♭7th-3rd**, again the same recipe on a third physical shape. *(~4:00)*
- **Bottom four strings, 1st inversion: 3rd-♭7th-root-5th.** *(~4:15)*
- **Bottom four strings, 2nd inversion: 5th-root-3rd-♭7th, with the 5th in the bass** — Ruch calls this one out specifically as **"a really great E7 voicing that you almost never see anyone play"**, i.e. a genuinely underused shape worth deliberately adding to vocabulary, not just a theoretical waypoint. *(~4:30)*
- **Bottom four strings, 3rd (final) inversion: ♭7th-3rd-5th-root, root on top.** *(~4:45)*
- **Transposing to the other two blues chords:** the video only works out E7's shapes, but the same shapes transpose directly — move the whole root-position voicing so the root note is A (for A7) or B (for B7), and every inversion falls into the identical bottom-to-top order relative to the new root, since the cycle-method relationship between the shapes doesn't change with the key. *(~5:15)*
- **Applied to an 8-bar blues based on "Key to the Highway"** (a real, distinct 8-bar form, not a truncated 12-bar — see [[Blues Form and Harmony]] § *8-bar blues*), a 3-chord blues in E7-A7-B7. → [[Blues Form and Harmony]]
- **Practice progression, stage 1 — "simple":** stay on the top four strings only, play just **one voicing per chord**, and for each chord change move to whichever inversion is physically closest to where the hand already is (E→A, then E→B), minimizing hand movement rather than picking shapes for their sound. *(~5:45)*
- **Practice progression, stage 2 — "mixing it up":** still confined to the top four strings, but now cycling through **multiple different inversions per chord** instead of landing on just one, so the comping has more internal movement while staying in one physical zone. *(~6:15)*
- **Practice progression, stage 3 — "the sky's the limit":** drop the top-four-strings restriction entirely and use **any and all 12 shapes freely across the whole neck**, mixing string sets and inversions at will — stated explicitly as "the point you want to get to" once the shapes are actually under the fingers, i.e. the earlier two stages are scaffolding, not the end goal. *(~6:45)*

> [!danger]- 🔒 Answer key — do not open until you have answered every question
> **Q1.** **3 — sweetness, warmth, glow. ♭3 — dark, serious, the thing that defines minorness**
> *Also accepted:* any wording carrying warm/bright against dark/sad · the exact words matter less than the contrast
> The 3 has a dimension the 5 doesn't; the ♭3 is what "minor" actually sounds like.
>
> **Q2.** **Because hearing a 3rd without hearing which 3rd is the standard starting gap**
> Number tells you how far, quality tells you which colour, and quality is the half doing the emotional work. Starting anywhere else trains around the gap.
>
> **Q3.** **5 world: 1, 4, 5, 2 — clean, neutral, pure. 3 world: 3, 6, 7 — warm, glowing, sweet**
> *Also accepted:* 1-4-5-2 and 3-6-7
>
> **Q4.** **That most confusions will happen within a family, not across one**
> It turns a vague sense of "I got it wrong" into a prediction you can check, which is what makes error-logging useful.
>
> **Q5.** **3 for 6 is normal — same world. 3 for 4 is not — that's a cross-world error**
> A cross-world mistake suggests the tonic reference has slipped rather than that two similar colours got swapped.
>
> **Q6.** **Close in pitch is not close in feeling. 5 against ♯4 is the example.**
> They sit one fret apart and are about as opposite as the system gets: 5 is pure, stable and expansive; ♯4 is bright, strange and spiky. A half step melodically, maximally distant by feeling.
>
> **Q7.** **3/6, 3/7 and ♭3/♭7**
> Adjacent on the feeling circle, which is the same statement as "in the same world".
>
> **Q8.** **They're hearing size and not quality, and quality is the half that carries the emotion**
> *Rubric (5):* says the number is being heard but not the quality (2) · says quality is what carries the emotional content (2) · notes that a number-only answer is incomplete in a way paper theory never forces you to notice (1)
> Hearing "a 3rd" locates the note but says nothing about what it does. Major and minor 3rds are one semitone apart and produce opposite emotional readings, so the half that's missing is the half a listener actually responds to. On paper the gap stays hidden, because C→E and C→E♭ are visibly different and nobody has to hear anything. By ear it's the whole question.
>
> **Q9.** **It holds the reference; you stop needing it once the mind holds a virtual tonic.**
> Every feeling state is measured against the tonic, so the tonic has to stay put. In real listening the music establishes it for you. While learning, the drone is what stops the reference drifting.
>
> **Q10.** **Because the words are scaffolding for the recognition, and eventually they slow it down**
> *Rubric (5):* says the words are a route to the recognition rather than the thing itself (2) · says your own words work better than someone else's because the association is yours (2) · says the end state is direct recognition without the verbal step (1)
> The point of a feeling word is to give a nameless sensation a handle so you can hold it and check it. Someone else's word only works if their association happens to match yours, which is why you replace it. Once the recognition is solid the word becomes an extra step between hearing and knowing — you want to hear "♭3", not to hear a feeling, retrieve the word "gloomy", and then map the word to a degree.

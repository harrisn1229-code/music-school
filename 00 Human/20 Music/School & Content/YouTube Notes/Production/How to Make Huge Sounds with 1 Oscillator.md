# How to Make Huge Sounds with 1 Oscillator

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rUTd7Ikg_8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_rUTd7Ikg_8)

## Synopsis

A deep, worked demonstration of audio-rate oscillator modulation as an alternative to stacking more oscillators for a bigger bass sound — more oscillators can add phasing and pitch drift without adding real character, while a single oscillator modulated by a second oscillator running in the audio-frequency range (rather than a slow LFO) adds complex, shifting texture while keeping one clean pitch center. Companion deep-dive to [[I Can Finally Tell You What Ive Been Working On]]'s "audio-rate modulation" configuration, from the same Bass DNA course preview.

## Techniques

- **More oscillators isn't automatically a bigger or more interesting bass** — stacking oscillators for size risks phasing and audible pitch drift between them; a single audio oscillator can carry MORE character than two stacked oscillators when paired with audio-rate modulation instead, while keeping one unambiguous pitch center (multiple full oscillators can "stray into different pitches," reading as generic thickness rather than character).
- **Audio-rate modulation of oscillator PITCH**: route a third oscillator — running in the audio-frequency range, not LFO range — into oscillator 1's frequency-modulation input (instead of sending it to the mixer/filter as an audible layer). Produces a "ripping" texture, subtle at low amounts but described as what makes a sound "shake" or "cut through" in a track; the source explicitly frames this as a way to make an analog patch read a little more digital.
- **Audio-rate modulation of the FILTER, as a distinct alternative to modulating pitch**: route the same audio-rate oscillator into the filter's modulation input instead. A different result from pitch modulation — pull resonance down "so it's not so over-enhanced" when using this route, and the modulator oscillator's own tuning and WAVEFORM (triangle vs. square demonstrated) both meaningfully change the character of the effect.
- **Detune the modulator oscillator's frequency to control the character of the resulting movement** — small frequency changes on the audio-rate modulator produce an "odd kind of chorus" effect; dropping it an octave changes the flavor again. This is tunable in real time as an expressive/character control, not just a fixed setting.
- **Modulate BOTH oscillator pitch AND the filter simultaneously with the same audio-rate oscillator** for a more extreme "shred," almost guitar-like result — audio-rate pitch modulation can shift the perceived pitch center enough that the patch's master tune needs re-checking/compensating afterward.
- **Fix the oscillator stage before reaching for the filter.** Most people reach for the filter first when a bass sound needs to be bigger or more powerful, but oscillators come BEFORE the filter in the signal chain — getting the oscillator content right first gives the filter more to work with, and makes everything downstream easier and better-sounding. A generalizable ordering principle, not specific to bass.
- **Vary the modulation amount/routing by arrangement context, not just per-patch** — the source describes deliberately bringing audio-rate modulation in or out depending on where a bass line sits in a song (a standout moment vs. a supporting line), rather than treating it as a fixed always-on setting for a given patch.

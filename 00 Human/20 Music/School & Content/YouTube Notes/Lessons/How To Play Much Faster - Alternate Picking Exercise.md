# How To Play Much Faster | Alternate Picking Exercise

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jo1NMo-4M-o)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jo1NMo-4M-o)

## Synopsis

The "walk before you run" case for speed-burst training: introduce a short burst of 16th-note-triplets inside a comfortable-tempo run of straight 16th notes (ascending A minor on one string), rather than pushing the metronome linearly higher every session — framed with a walking-vs-running analogy and a direct reference to Martin Miller teaching the same principle at a workshop.

## What's taught

- **Why linear tempo increases don't work: slow-tempo and fast-tempo picking are different techniques, not the same technique played faster.** Directly analogous to walking vs. running — you don't learn to run by walking progressively faster; at some point a different motion is required. Turning the metronome up 1 BPM at a time every day doesn't bridge that gap.
- **The exercise: a comfortable mid-tempo group of straight 16th notes with a short burst of 16th-note triplets inserted, then back to the comfortable tempo.** Establishes a "home base" feel (the mid-tempo 16ths) the player already trusts, then briefly steps outside it (the triplet burst) and returns — repeatedly touching the fast feel without staying in it long enough to break down. Works well as a warm-up specifically because it exercises both the mid-tempo motion and the speed-picking motion in the same pass.
- **Prerequisite gate, stated explicitly:** don't start this exercise below roughly 80-90 BPM continuous 16th-note alternate picking — it assumes the base motion is already comfortable and is a speed-extension drill, not a beginner picking exercise.
- **General technique reminders reinforced through this exercise (not new, but repeated as load-bearing):** stay relaxed rather than picking with aggressive attack (both for tone reasons — usable on clean/acoustic tone — and injury prevention, given high daily playing volume); fretting-hand angle should let the pinky reach comfortably, avoiding a flat/collapsed wrist angle that limits stretch; thumb position is a personal/ergonomic choice (low slung vs. high) rather than a single correct answer, citing players who succeed with either; the chromatic "spider" exercise (fretting all four fingers across two strings, pressing down until each finger is actually needed) recommended as a complementary left-hand-only companion drill, with the same never-flatten-the-fingers / never-block-the-string rule as its usual form.

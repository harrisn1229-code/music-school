## What's taught

- **Everything repeats at the 12th fret.** Only 12 notes exist in music, so any note's fingering pattern repeats exactly one octave higher starting the 12th fret (and repeats again past the 24th fret on longer necks) — reduces the perceived size of the fretboard to memorize, since only frets 0–11 are genuinely new information.
- **The low E and high E strings share the identical note layout** (both tuned to E, standard tuning) — memorizing the low E string's notes gives the high E string for free, one fewer string to learn independently. (Distinct from, but complements, the already-logged low-E/A-string parallel-layout fact.)
- **Natural notes first, sharps/flats filled in after — the core method, restated from [[The 5 MOST Important Music Theory TRICKS! (Finally Sound Like A PRO)]]'s octave triangle.** Learn the "white key" natural notes (C D E F G A B, the C major scale) on one string, saying each note aloud while playing (explicitly not a technique/speed exercise), then fill the gaps between naturals by thinking of each in-between note as the lower natural raised a half-step or the higher natural lowered a half-step. Recommends working one string at a time rather than the whole neck at once, and extending past the 12th fret once a string is solid.
- **"Find one note everywhere" drill — broader than the octave triangle.** Pick a single note (worked example: E) and locate every instance of it across the entire fretboard up to the 12th fret, on every string — distinct from the octave-triangle's string-to-string relocation shape, this is an exhaustive single-note fretboard map. Recommended to alternate this drill with the natural-notes-per-string approach on different practice days to keep the routine varied.
- **Payoff framing (leads into the paid course's Chapter 2, not detailed here):** once notes are visualized reliably, the next step is applying interval structure (scale/arpeggio formulas) directly on top of that note knowledge to escape "box and shape" thinking entirely — matches the interval-based visualization approach already logged from [[The BEST System For Amazing Guitar SOLOS! (finally sound like a PRO)]].

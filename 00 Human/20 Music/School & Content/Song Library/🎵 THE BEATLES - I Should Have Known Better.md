# I Should Have Known Better — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Looks like the plain one on the album and isn't. Two things carry it: **the verse exists in two versions** — closed and 10 bars when another verse follows, open and 8 bars when a bridge follows — and a syncopated accent on the last eighth of the bar runs through the whole song, always foiled by a backing track that never syncopates at all ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]).

**Form.** A strange take on the two-bridge model: **three verses in a row between the bridges** (the middle one instrumental), and the second bridge running straight into the outro with no verse between. The bridge is an unusually long and developed **16 bars in four distinct phrases**.

**Harmony.** Few chords, all ordinary; the only real event is the trip to the relative minor for the bridges ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]), pivoting back on the chord that is VI in the minor and IV at home. Harmonic rhythm does structural work in the verse: it slows midway to add ballast, then the I–V oscillation returns for symmetry ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). One bar is a rarity for the genre — a chord held in **inversion** while the bass walks down to its root. The bridge teases once more by dipping back to the minor just as you think the corner has been turned.

**Melody.** The verse covers a sixth, mostly noodling on its top four notes, but still reads as an ascent; its signature is a long sustained open vowel followed immediately by fast syllabic setting. The bridge answers with a clean arch across a tenth, climaxing on a falsetto flip. At the end of the guitar solo the leading tone refuses to resolve upward and an added-sixth chord arrives instead.

**Arrangement.** The harmonica feels omnipresent but isn't — outside the solo verse its main job is announcing each verse, two bars at a time, and it makes a piquant 9th and a flat cross-relation against the chord it enters on. Scarcity is what makes its one continuous pass land. The electric guitar enters **one bar before** the section it belongs to, to hide the seam. John is double-tracked everywhere except the second bridge, which drops to a single track — with the double restored for exactly the falsetto flip. Reserving one production element for one moment is a bigger event than any layer you could add.

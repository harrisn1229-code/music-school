# 12-Level Chromatic Alternate-Picking Speed Workout

A 12-tempo-level daily picking play-along, chromatic-scale-based (all four fretting fingers, one note per fret) so there's no scale/theory content to learn — the drill is pure right-hand speed and clean string transitions. Starts at 50 BPM (Level 1) and climbs through all 12 levels in one sitting.

## How to run it

- Play through all 12 levels in one sitting, back to back — the workout is the ladder, not one isolated tempo.
- **Goal, every note, at every tempo: one continuous unbroken stream** — no hesitation or unwanted noise at string transitions. A clean lower tempo beats a sloppy higher one.
- Log the date and the BPM level you got stuck at, then return the next day and try to beat it — the drill is explicitly designed as a day-over-day progression tracker, not a one-and-done exercise.
- If a specific tempo step feels technically wrong (not just "hard," but genuinely uncontrolled), that's a signal to drop back and drill string-transition mechanics specifically rather than muscling through at that tempo.

## Variation — asymmetric 4-notes-per-string chromatic run in groups of 10

A more advanced, musically-shaped chromatic exercise (distinct from this drill's plain linear ladder): a 4-notes-per-string chromatic pattern across a chosen fretboard section, **not symmetrical** because of the B string's different tuning interval from its neighbors — you have to actually track the fingering rather than just repeat one shape across every string. Phrased in **groups of 10** (a group of 6 combined with a group of 4, repeating) instead of the usual groups of 2/3/4 — the odd grouping puts accents in more interesting places than a standard chromatic warmup. Picked with continuous alternate picking throughout (no exceptions), which also makes it a solid wrist-loosening warmup on top of the note-spacing/hand-synchronization work. Practiced at 3 tempos in the source video.

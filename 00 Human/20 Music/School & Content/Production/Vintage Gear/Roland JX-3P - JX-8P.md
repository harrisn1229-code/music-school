# Roland JX-3P / JX-8P

## What it is
Roland's "other" 80s polys — two-DCO analog machines hidden behind membrane buttons and (on the 8P) a menu system that scared players away for decades. Under the hood: some of the lushest analog pad architecture Roland ever shipped, now thoroughly rehabilitated as sleeper classics.

## What makes the sound
- **Two DCOs per voice** (where the Junos had one) with cross-mod on the 3P and a sweetened chorus — denser, glassier ensembles than a Juno, less flagship-shiny than a [[Roland Jupiter-8|Jupiter]].
- **The JX-8P adds velocity/aftertouch and darker voicing** — expressive swelling pads and the famous factory patches; its "Vibe/String" register is the mid-80s ballad bed.
- Both hide their editing behind buttons/sliders sold separately (PG-200/PG-800 programmers) — presets got used *as-is*, which is why a handful of JX patches are recognizable across dozens of records.
- Slightly grainy DCO top end + chorus = "expensive VHS" texture, a synthwave favorite.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]] + a second detuned layer approximates the two-DCO density; [[DUNE 3]]/[[Serum 2]] two-osc pad recipes with chorus; [[Omnisphere]] includes JX-sourced material; [[TAL-Chorus-LX]] for the ensemble glaze.
- **Paid:** Roland Cloud JX-3P; Cherry Audio and others cover the JX-8P lane.
- **Free:** two-DCO-plus-chorus recipes in any owned synth.

## Used on
- Genre/era: mid-80s pop ballad pads, city-pop and AOR beds, late-80s R&B — reliably present in that era's session-gear lore but rarely pinned to specific records; genre-level per the rule, with the [[Synthwave]] revival mining the 8P register hard.

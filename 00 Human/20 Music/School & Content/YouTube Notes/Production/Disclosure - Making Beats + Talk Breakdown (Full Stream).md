## Techniques

### Songwriting & harmony
- **Latch is "a sick, flavorsome F minor" (per Jacob Collier) — and the bass avoids the root** — minimal F in the bassline, dark ♭s and "naughty voicings," F-minor pentatonic covering the melodic real estate. Guy: "I was just doing what I thought sounded dope." Root-avoidant basslines = instant sophistication. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], 🎵 DISCLOSURE - Latch if/when a song page exists
- **Howard: ninth chords "in nearly every single circumstance"** — the default color extension. → chord craft
- **Division of labor:** Guy writes drums while Howard writes chords simultaneously; vocals last, written **in the room** with the singer 99% of the time ("it's always about getting in the room"). Howard's studio = laptop + monitors + MIDI keyboard + piano — writing rig, not production rig. → workflow
- **"Do enough takes, you can make it sound good"** — Howard taught SG Lewis vocal confidence this way: not singing lessons, comping confidence. → [[1 Main Vocal Track|Main Vocal Track]] Step 1.5 spirit

### Session prep & sound choice
- **Pre-highlight your favorite patches before the session** — they went through the [[Roland D-50]] beforehand and starred the keepers "so there's less messing about mid-session." Same move as palette slots: choose sounds when you're browsing, not when you're writing. → [[Sound Palette]], [[Instrument Preset Map]]
- **D-50 = big pads with twinkles on top** ("flute vibes"); match the era end-to-end: "it's got to be 80s for all the reverbs, no doubt — it's definitely an 80s Juno." Era-consistent processing sells the sound. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Roland D-50]], [[Vintage Gear Index]]
- **Record synths in as audio (not balance-in-place)** — commit the patch to audio while writing; the MIDI isn't the sound. (The FX Chain System bounce-in-place convention, from the source.) → [[FX Chain System]] step 2

### Routing & mix
- **Dedicated sidechain trigger track** — BigKick multi-output split: the steady sub/kick feeds the sidechain buses on its own channel, so when extra syncopated kicks get added the pump "keeps going straight down the middle" instead of stuttering. Trigger ≠ audible kick. → [[Sidechaining]]
- **Sidechain-filter the compressor** so lows don't trigger it (watch the GR return as you dial the HPF). → [[Sidechaining]], [[Compression Staging]]
- **LinnDrum top on a dead kick** — "I love a LinnDrum top end of a kick" — click layer, quiet. → [[Kick Design]], [[LinnDrum]]
- **Bass chain minimalism:** Logic overdrive + EQ bump at 1 kHz + sidechain — "not a lot needs to happen there" when the sound is right. → [[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]]
- **"Sneaky stuff" under loud drums** — the beat reads as huge kick/snare, but underneath: heavily distorted, very quiet accent layers (Massive Attack-ish) that the sidechain pumps so hard "you don't hear them — all they do is bring out the hi-hat." Texture you feel, not hear. → [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[1 Drums FX Track|Drums FX Track]]
- **Manual swing** — pushed hits nudged by hand, unevenly ("you could go full Dilla"); adjust the percussion to match once the swing moves. → [[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]]
- **Minimal instruments, everything upfront = the good-session tell** — on Talk: "not many instruments, loads of space… if you're willing to show off almost all of them right up front, that's a sign of a good session." → arrangement philosophy, [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

# You Don't Know Me — Ray Charles

> A classic Eddy Arnold/Cindy Walker song, taught here in Ray Charles's arrangement (key of E♭ major) by Jack Ruch as a real-tune vehicle for **functioning dominant chords** — the primary V7 (B♭7) and three secondary dominants (E♭7 → A♭, C7 → Fm, G7 → Cm) — plus a diminished-passing-chord move (A♭ → A°). Full breakdown of the lick/soloing method → [[A Classic Tune for Learning About Secondary Dominants]].

## Chords
**Key:** E♭ major. The portion of the tune covered here is a **chain of secondary dominants**, not a stock progression: **E♭ (I) → B♭7 (V7) → E♭ (I) → E♭7 (V7/IV) → A♭ (IV) → C7 (V7/ii) → Fm (ii) → G7 (V7/vi) → Cm (vi) → A♭ (IV) → A° (passing diminished)**. Every secondary dominant resolves down a fifth into its target exactly as expected (E♭7→A♭, C7→Fm, G7→Cm), which is what makes it a clean teaching vehicle for [[Secondary Dominants]] — but because the ii chord itself never gets its own dominant (no true ii-V-I pair), and the sequence isn't a stock turnaround/axis/1-6-2-5 shape, it doesn't map onto any single entry in this vault's `progressions:` controlled vocabulary. Tagged `progressions: []` rather than forced onto the nearest-sounding label — only the primary V7-I at the very top (B♭7→E♭) is a "real" dominant-tonic cadence in the classic sense.

Progression (as walked through in the source video, not the full lead sheet — this is what's covered):
- **E♭ → B♭7 → E♭ → E♭7** — opens on the tonic, moves to the primary dominant (B♭7 = V7) and back, then to **E♭7**, a **secondary dominant (V7/IV)** that resolves to the next chord, A♭ major.
- **… → A♭** — target of the E♭7 secondary dominant; adding the ♭7 (D♭) to the E♭ chord voice-leads down and resolves to G, the 3rd of A♭.
- **… → C7 → F minor** — **C7 is V7/ii** (F minor is the ii of E♭ major); still functions as a dominant even though it resolves to a *minor* chord.
- **… → G7 → C minor** — **G7 is V7/vi** (C minor is the vi of E♭ major); same minor-target dominant logic.
- **… → A♭ → A° (diminished passing chord)** — not a functioning dominant, but a diminished sound with enough space in the form to address it with a version of the same diminished lick.

## Soloing devices worked over this tune
- **Movable diminished-7 arpeggio lick off the chord's own 3rd** — start on the 3rd of whichever dominant is sounding, ascend a dim7 arpeggio (3rd–5th–♭7–upper tone), then resolve down into the 3rd of the target chord. Same shape relocated to all five spots above (B♭7→E♭, E♭7→A♭, C7→Fm, G7→Cm, A♭→A°). → [[Diminished Chords]], [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]]
- **E♭ major pentatonic** as the base solo template through the whole form, into which the diminished lick gets substituted one spot at a time, then blended with "minor-y bluesy" phrasing for a full solo. → [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]

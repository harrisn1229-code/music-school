# Boss DS-2

**Category:** Pedal (distortion)
**Vendor:** Boss
**Ballpark value (used, 2026):** $60–90

## What it is

Boss's Turbo Distortion — the DS-1 lineage with a second "Turbo" mode adding mid-boosted, thicker gain; remote-switchable between modes.

## What it's used for

Tighter, mid-forward distortion that cuts where the [[Big Muff Pi]] scoops; grunge/garage rhythm and solo boost (Turbo II).

## Signal chain notes

After [[Wah Pedal]]; Turbo I = DS-1-ish rhythm, Turbo II = fat lead. Famously a Kurt Cobain staple (In Utero era) and John Frusciante's long-time drive.

🎬 *From video:* a boost-into-overdrive stacking order worth trying ahead of this pedal — a low-gain clean-boost stage (Roger Mayer Voodoo 1: "I set it kind of like a boost. It adds a little bit of hair... it honestly in a track still kind of reads as clean") feeding a more obvious overdrive: "you get more of that low-end body from the [boost] and... a little bit of extra push of that boost going into the [overdrive]" — extra push and low-end body, not just more gain. Maps onto this pedal's own "more obvious"/mid-forward dirt role, same [[Big Muff Pi]]-adjacent "cuts where the Muff scoops" lane. — Jack Ruch, [My Home Recording Setup for Guitar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KYTGFneEJ2A)

🎬 *From video:* a concrete volume-dial-in method for any dirt pedal with its own volume/level control, including this one — set gain to taste, but push the pedal's own **volume higher than feels necessary solo**, to compensate for the *perceived* volume loss caused by the pedal's own compression. Demoed on a two-in-one overdrive+clean-boost pedal (Analog Man King of Tone / an Apollo Approved "Vertical"-style pedal): "it might sound loud when you're playing by yourself, but as soon as a band comes in and drums and cymbals, you're going to notice that perceived volume loss with the compression of an overdrive." Also: overdrive's main job isn't just grit — it adds compression (sustain, "squishier" feel) and typically cuts some high end, taming a bright clean guitar/amp combo, which is worth remembering when A/B'ing this pedal against a cleaner rhythm tone. Set the pedal's own volume slightly louder than clean rhythm volume, then re-check in a full-band context, not alone. → [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track|Electric Guitar Amp Track]] — Jack Ruch, [Dial In Your Amps and Pedals for Great Clean and Overdriven TONES!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=45s77xBLZBU)

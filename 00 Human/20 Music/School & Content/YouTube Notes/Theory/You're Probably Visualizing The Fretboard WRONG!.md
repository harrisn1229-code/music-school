# You're Probably Visualizing The Fretboard WRONG!

## Synopsis

BERNTH's 5-step fretboard-mastery system, worked entirely in C major: notes repeat identically on the low E and A strings (just shifted), then build UP from a bare triad (root-3-5) to a 7th arpeggio (add the 7th) to a full mode/scale shape (add 2-4-6) — so every scale position is understood as "an arpeggio with 3 extra notes," not memorized as a disconnected shape. Closely related to this batch's other BERNTH arpeggio/CAGED videos, but the specific incremental triad→7th-arpeggio→mode construction method (rather than CAGED positions or 3nps) is this video's distinct contribution.

## What's taught

- **The E-string/A-string parallel-layout trick.** The natural-note sequence (C D E F G A B) is laid out identically on the low E string and the A string — same order, same intervals — just starting from a different fret (C on the 8th fret of low E = C on the 3rd fret of A). Once the low E string's notes are memorized, the A string's notes are "free" — same shape, shifted position, not a new sequence to learn.
- **Step 1 — build a triad off every scale degree (root-3-5), three fingering options per triad.** For each scale degree, count up 3 and 5 notes within the parent scale to get that degree's 3rd and 5th (e.g. C major's first-degree triad = C-E-G). For any given triad there are three usable fingerings that each START on the root with a different finger (index, middle, or pinky) — deliberately learning all three (not just the most obvious one) means never having to jump position with the same finger every time when connecting arpeggios or sweep-picking, since whichever finger is free at the position change can lead in.
- **Step 2 — add the 7th to get a 7th-arpeggio (4 notes).** Count up 7 notes from the root within the scale to find the major or minor 7th, add it to the triad. Repeated across every scale degree gives the complete set of diatonic 7th arpeggios for the key, each with 3 root-first fingering options — a full-neck arpeggio vocabulary built entirely from counted scale degrees, not memorized diagrams.
- **Step 3 — add the 2nd, 4th, and 6th to get the full 7-note scale/mode shape.** Since a 7th arpeggio already has 4 of a heptatonic scale's 7 notes, adding the remaining 3 (the 2nd, 4th, 6th of that position) completes the full scale shape at that position — meaning each scale-form position is understood as "the 7th arpeggio I already know, plus 3 notes," not a separate thing to memorize.
- **Step 4 — the mode-per-degree mapping (standard, restated for this system):** playing C major starting from each of its own degrees gives the seven modes in order — D Dorian, E Phrygian, F Lydian, G Mixolydian, A Aeolian, B Locrian — so the 7 positions built in Step 3 are simultaneously the 7 modes, each now understood via its underlying arpeggio rather than as an abstract mode name.
- **The stated payoff — train yourself to keep "seeing" the arpeggio inside a scale shape while playing it,** so switching between running a scale and dropping into an arpeggio/sweep-picked line mid-solo becomes fluid rather than requiring a mental context-switch. Cites his own song "The Kindling" (opening track, album *Elevation*) as built largely from improvising with this exact scale/arpeggio-blending system.
- **Step 5 — octave extension:** repeat the same 7-note shape's formula up/down an octave to extend it across the full neck, rather than memorizing a separate two-octave fingering — matches the octave-extension trick already logged from [[The BEST Arpeggio Trick!

# Fix Your Plucking in 5 Minutes

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PWatGRe1iHI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PWatGRe1iHI)

## Synopsis

A short live-coaching format from BassBuzz: a bassist who self-diagnoses "sloppy finger speed and accuracy" — specifically hitting the wrong string when improvising fast 16th-note lines — plays a before riff, gets coached through a five-minute fix, then replays the same riff after. The coach's diagnosis: the stumbling isn't a raw-speed problem, it's that improvised fast lines pull the plucking hand outside patterns it has actually rehearsed, so the fingers hesitate mid-line. The fix is a small, drillable set of string-crossing exercises — a fixed number of plucks per string walked up and down the strings (2, 3, 4, then 1 per string, since most real playing needs two, three, or four notes per string before moving on) — plus, critically, deciding in advance whether a direction change **rakes** (repeats the same finger across the string change) or **alternates** (keeps strictly alternating fingers through it), since indecision at that exact fork is what causes the flub. A metronome-driven accuracy threshold rounds it out: find the tempo you nail 100% clean, then creep it up until you're missing roughly 10% of the time, and drill there rather than either the too-easy tempo or full speed. Short video, but genuinely concrete and immediately practice-able — no filler.

## What's taught

- **Diagnosis: stumbling = hand pulled outside a rehearsed pattern, not a speed ceiling.** Fast improvised 16th notes expose whichever string-crossing shapes haven't been drilled in isolation — the fix is pre-rehearsing enough patterns that improvising draws on rehearsed material more of the time.
- **Break the complex skill into simple, verifiable pieces, then recombine.** Isolate a piece, confirm it works on its own, move to the next piece, then chain them back into the full improvised skill — rather than drilling the whole complex thing at once.
- **Drill: N plucks per string, walking up and down the string set.** Pick a fixed count and cross strings with it — can be on open strings or any fretted note, since the drill targets the plucking-hand pattern, not the pitches. Progression demonstrated live: **2 plucks/string** (easy starting point) → **3 plucks/string** (noticeably harder) → **4 plucks/string** (named, with 2 and 3, as covering most of what real playing actually needs before a crossing) → **1 pluck/string** (every string is a crossing — the purest test of the finger-choice decision below).
- **Metronome threshold method: find 100% clean, then creep to a 10% miss rate.** Don't just chase max speed — find the tempo where the pattern is nailed every time, then nudge tempo up until you're missing it about 1 in 10 reps, and drill at that edge.
- **The direction-change fork: rake vs. alternate — and you must pre-decide which.** Crossing strings in one direction, the hand naturally alternates fingers the whole way (no decision needed). Coming back the **other** direction there's a real fork: keep alternating straight through, or **rake** — repeat the landing finger across the direction change instead of alternating (worked example on 1-pluck-per-string: alternate = index-middle-index-middle-index-middle the whole way; rake = index-middle, index-middle, then middle-middle-middle-middle-middle on the way back). Also drill the **flipped starting finger** (start on middle instead of index) for each version — four distinct rehearsed patterns from one drill.
- **Root cause of stumbling: indecision at the fork, not lack of raw reps.** "The stumbling happens when you're not deciding" — the fix is explicitly deciding rake-or-alternate ahead of time and drilling that specific choice slowly, one pattern at a time, until it's automatic — not just doing more reps at speed.
- **Applying it back to a real riff: slow down slightly and shift attention to the plucking hand.** When bringing the fix into an actual line (not an isolated drill), play it a notch slower than performance tempo and consciously feel the fingertips on the strings, de-emphasizing the fretting hand so the plucking-hand decision-making gets full attention while still being consolidated.
- **Failure mode to watch for: one finger jumping to play multiple notes in a row.** Named as the concrete symptom of an undecided rake/alternate choice colliding with a fast line — the tell that a pattern needs to be pulled out and drilled in isolation rather than pushed through at speed.
- **Format note (not a technique, but worth flagging):** this is a real before/after coaching session — the player replays essentially the same riff at the end, applying the slow-down + plucking-focus + pre-decided fingering fix live, which is the practical payoff of the whole video.

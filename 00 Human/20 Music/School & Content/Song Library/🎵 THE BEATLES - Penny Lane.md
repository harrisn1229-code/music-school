# Penny Lane — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Verse (8, AA′B as 2+2+4) · Verse · Refrain (8, AA′) · Verse · Verse (solo) · Refrain · Verse · Verse · Refrain · Refrain/Outro with a **written ending instead of a fade**. Completely cyclical — no bridge, and the solo section is just another verse. There is no real intro: the vocal enters with about an eighth note of backing ahead of it.

The single most unusual thing in the song is that **the refrain is in the key of ♭VII** — verses in B, refrain in A ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]) — which Pollack flags as something he cannot find another example of, and which is also why the final refrain's move up reads as earned rather than as the fifties last-chorus key bump. The pivot leans on F♯ **major**, not diatonic to A, so the raised third does the work: a real change of key, not a tonicisation ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]).

The verse opens with a textbook descending walking bass under I, then puts a minor seventh on the tonic in bar 4 and slides into jazzy, impressionist territory before closing with a classical 4–3 over V. Its skeleton is just I – ♭VI – V, with ♭VI borrowed from the parallel minor ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]), blurred because the voices move at different rates. That parallel-minor shadow is the ominous undertow under a sunny lyric.

A thick wall-of-sound mix closer to *Pet Sounds* than to Spector. The **piccolo trumpet arrives in four graded doses** — solo section, one isolated note, obbligato, closing lick — the same staging used for the horn in "For No One".

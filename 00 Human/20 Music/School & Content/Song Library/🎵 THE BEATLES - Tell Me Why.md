# Tell Me Why — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4) · **Refrain** (12 — two parallel 6-bar phrases, each a 4-bar main phrase plus a 2-bar connector) · Verse (8) · Refrain · Verse · Refrain · Bridge (10) · Refrain · Outro (4). Odd twice over: the song opens with a refrain, and the lone bridge is held back until very near the end.

Almost the whole song is one cliché — I–vi–ii–V — used for both refrain and verse, so the interest is in **how much differentiation you can get without changing the chords** ([[POP-C02 — Why four chords is satisfying]]). Most of the progression lies along the circle of fifths, and it is tonally open: every phrase of every section ends on V. The refrain's connector restates the whole four-bar progression in **harmonic double time** ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]); the bridge does the opposite and drags each chord across two bars.

The differentiation comes from melody and bass. The refrain tune is pentatonic-major and rhythmically stretched with a walking bass under it faking faster harmonic motion; the verse leans on the **bluesy minor third**, is chattier and jumpier, and sits over a stable bass. The bridge is set up by simply sustaining I at the end of the preceding refrain until it ripens into **V-of-IV** ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]).

Because everything circles back to V forever, stopping the song takes a genuinely foreign progression: the outro is entered as a [[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]] to vi and then goes to ♭VI, a 4–3 over V, and finally I.

# Sexy Sadie — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (a single **6-bar phrase open at both ends**, and note for note the verse's third phrase) · Verse (**14 bars**, 4+4+6) · Verse · Bridge (12, AAB) · Verse · Verse · Bridge · Outro (fade). The longer two-bridge model, but with two sung verses in the middle rather than one.

The analytic hook is a single two-chord gesture — **I moving to V-of-iii** — used as the song's signature and given three different continuations, so the same progression means something different every time it appears. Resolving to iii it behaves functionally; moving to ♭VII it produces a **tritone root motion** the ear excuses only because the phrases either side are rhetorically parallel; elsewhere it stops being a dominant at all and becomes one step in a chromatic descent ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]).

I, IV and V hold the key, but iii, V-of-iii and ♭VII do real work rather than decorate ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The **♭VII → V** move is a textbook cross-relation ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]), the same trick as "I'm A Loser" and in the same key. In the bridge a rising diatonic stream I–ii–iii–IV is answered by four chromatic chords descending, with a C/C♯ cross-relation at the hinge — ascent and collapse balancing exactly, which is Pollack's reading of the whole song: an uphill struggle against something that keeps shoving you back down.

Style is deliberately mismatched — cutting lyrics over doo-wop backing vocals and a near-Classical piano part drowned in unnaturally heavy reverb.

# Fox Stevenson "Why I NEVER use OTT"

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)

## Synopsis
Fox Stevenson lays out, once and for all, why he's stopped reaching for OTT: it splits a sound into low/mid/high bands and rides each one's gain independently, which drags the high-frequency partials out of phase alignment with the low end's zero crossings — a real problem the moment you need to master or limit around a bass-heavy low end, not just an aesthetic quibble. He demonstrates the effect on a plain square wave and then A/Bs a bass sound processed with OTT against the same sound run through Saturn 2 and Radiator instead, getting a near-identical tone with far more control over the high end. From there he widens the argument into a broader point about compression itself: OTT is not a compressor in the way most people mean the word, and compression doesn't have to mean crushing dynamics away — it can be used to build dynamic contrast on purpose, which he shows on a snare by isolating its snap frequency, using a slow attack and mid-length release to crush just the sustain, then bringing the level back up with makeup gain. He closes by scoping OTT down to sound design specifically, warning that leaning on it for every mix decision trains you to make "OTT sounds" instead of building broader sound-design range.

## Techniques
- **Multiband OTT-style processing offsets high-frequency partials from the fundamental's zero crossings — a real phase problem, not just a sound-design quirk.** OTT independently gain-rides low/mid/high bands; because each band's dynamic movement happens on its own timeline, a sound's high-end partials drift out of phase alignment with the low end's zero crossings, which is audible as high end that no longer sits "with" the low end and becomes a genuine problem the moment you need to master (limit) around a bass-heavy low end. → [[OTT]], [[Phase Alignment]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Fox Stevenson "Why I NEVER use OTT"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)
- **Demoed the phase-offset problem on a plain square wave** — a square wave stands in for "perfect distortion" (a maximally simple, harmonic-rich signal); running it through OTT visibly/audibly shows how far the high-end partials shift once each band is compressed independently of the others. → [[OTT]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Fox Stevenson "Why I NEVER use OTT"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)
- **A/B: swap OTT for Saturn 2 + Radiator on a bass sound and land on a near-identical tone with far more control.** Matching the low end and taming the highs on a Saturn 2 + Radiator saturation chain gets close to the same tonal result as OTT would, but without OTT's uncontrolled, phase-offset high end — his go-to alternative when a sound needs harmonic excitement rather than multiband up/down squashing. → [[OTT]], [[Saturn 2]], [[Radiator]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Fox Stevenson "Why I NEVER use OTT"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)
- **"OTT is not a compressor" — reframes compression as something that can build dynamics, not just remove them.** Pushes back on the common assumption that compression always means crushing/limiting dynamics away; used with intent (slow attack, controlled release), compression can instead sculpt a sound's dynamic contour on purpose — demonstrated directly on the snare technique below. → [[Compression]], [[Compressor]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Fox Stevenson "Why I NEVER use OTT"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)
- **Reshape a snare's snap-vs-crack balance: isolate the snap frequency, slow attack + mid release, crush hard, then makeup gain.** Leave the fundamental alone and target just the snare's snap frequency band; a slow attack lets the initial transient through uncompressed, a mid-length release and hard gain reduction crush the sustain/body, and makeup gain brings the level back up — the result trades body/crack for a louder, sharper snap, entirely without OTT's broadband multiband squashing. → [[Compression Staging]], [[Transient Shaper]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Fox Stevenson "Why I NEVER use OTT"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)
- **Light, even multiband compression across an already-uniform source, instead of OTT's aggressive per-band up/down movement.** When a source is already fairly consistent in level (not very dynamic), a small, even multiband compression pass across every frequency area gets the "glue everything down" result more predictably than OTT's default aggressive squashing. → [[Multiband Compression]], [[Multiband Dynamics]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Fox Stevenson "Why I NEVER use OTT"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)
- **Reserve OTT for sound design specifically — leaning on it for everything trains you to make "OTT sounds."** Not an outright ban: OTT still has a place in dedicated sound design, but habitual use across every mix element becomes a crutch that narrows your sound-design range rather than expanding it. → [[OTT]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Fox Stevenson "Why I NEVER use OTT"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)

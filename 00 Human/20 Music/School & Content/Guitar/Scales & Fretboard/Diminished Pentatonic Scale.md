# Diminished Pentatonic Scale

**A five-note alternate to the minor pentatonic scale, made by one edit: flatten the perfect 5th to a ♭5, everywhere it occurs in the shape.** The diminished-flavor sibling to [[Minor 6 Pentatonic Scale]]'s major-6 swap — both take the standard minor pentatonic box and replace exactly one scale degree in every octave to shift its harmonic color, rather than introducing a new shape to memorize.

## The core edit

C minor pentatonic: **C (root) – E♭ (♭3) – F (4) – G (5) – B♭ (♭7)**. Diminished pentatonic: **C – E♭ – F – G♭ – B♭** — the perfect 5th (G) becomes a ♭5 (G♭) everywhere it appears across the neck; every other note and the overall box shape stays identical. One note changed, in every octave, no new fingering to learn from scratch.

## Underlying theory — where the diminished sound comes from

A diminished triad = a minor triad with the 5th flattened (root-♭3-♭5; e.g. C-E♭-G♭ vs. the C minor triad C-E♭-G). Diatonically, a diminished triad occurs naturally on the **vii° of a major key** and the **ii° of a natural minor key** — cited as the most common places to encounter it in real music (e.g. the ii°→i motion, B°→Cm in C minor).

## When to use it

Framed specifically as an alternative to always defaulting to symmetrical diminished-arpeggio shapes (moving the same shape in minor 3rds) when soloing/writing over a diminished chord in a rock/metal context — gives a genuinely different-sounding option built on the already-familiar pentatonic box rather than arpeggio sweeping.

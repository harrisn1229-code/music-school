# Disclosure - 'F For You' from 'Settle' Twitch Breakdown

Nostalgic teardown of "F For You" — **their first-ever house tune (124 BPM, first 4-on-floor kick) and the first song Howard sang on**, built entirely on stock Logic plugins. Guy frames it as "a confession" ("we knew nothing"), so much of the value is *what-not-to-do* self-critique — but the flags are genuinely instructive (comp attack at 0, everything limited to 0.1, over-compressing the vocal instead of adding air). The keepers: a **MacBook demo vocal became the record**, **group vocals stack low-mids fast**, **bass delay only on the top end**, and the origin of the signature **Juno square-wave beeps**. Whisper transcript (no captions) — high confidence.

## Techniques
- **A demo/guide vocal can be the keeper — commit to the take.** Howard recorded the "infected with restless whispers" hook on his **MacBook mic while ill with a cold**, meant only as a guide for a future singer. Guy mixed it, management loved it, and it became the record — Howard's first vocal, unplanned. Lesson: don't assume the scratch take is disposable; the character (even a cold) can *be* the sound. → workflow.
- **Group vocals pile up low-mids *fast* — cut them early.** On a 4-part stack he pulled ~70 dB(!) of lows/low-mids out with a stock EQ (says he'd go gentler now, but the instinct is right): one vocal's low-mids sound fine, four of them build a huge muddy boost. Earliest instance of the [[Masking and Carving]] low-mid rule (see [Birthday](https://youtu.be/b-6x_4XRwOQ)). → [[EQ]], [[LEAD Slot]].
- ⚠️ **Never set a compressor's attack to 0** — it produces horrible distortion. (Self-flagged mistake on the vocal comp.) → [[Compression]].
- **Air + de-ess brings a vocal forward better than more compression.** The over-compressed vocal "loses all its life"; a touch of [[Gem Dopamine]] air + a de-esser would have lifted it to the front without the squash. → [[De-essing]], [[Compression Staging]].
- **Sidechain the chords/synths off the *vocal*** — the lead was sent to a bus keying a compressor on the chord synths so they duck when the vocal enters (done here because the vocal lacked presence and everything was already slamming 0 dB). The right fix is presence/air on the vocal; the duck is the crutch. Reinforces the empty-bus vocal-duck in [[Sidechaining]]. → [[BED Slot]] ducks [[LEAD Slot]].
- **Bass delay is fine — but only on the top end.** Delay the upper harmonics of a bass, never the lows/sub ("delaying the mud sounds mental"). → [[Delay]], [[BASS Slot]].
- **The signature Juno square-wave "beeps," velocity-controlled** — a rising/falling Juno square wave, volume driven by velocity; "one of the first times we did the beeping thing, then it ended up on every song." Simple, sits great with drums. → [[Sound Selection Guide]].
- **Sylenth1 square-wave bass through a phaser, bounced to audio** — the movement is the phaser setting, printed so it's locked; then light compression → top-only delay → EQ (cut super-highs) → light limiting → sidechained to kick. → [[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]].
- **Rhythmic vinyl crackle on each clap = the Burial move** — a crackle hit gated to every clap (Burial's signature); reinforces the same trick in [Birthday](https://youtu.be/b-6x_4XRwOQ). → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]].
- **Drums = one Ultrabeat kit, multi-output routed** — kick (layered with "Old Kick 26") to main out, claps to 3/4, hats/cabasa/tambourine above, like Battery's per-pad outputs; two chords only (G♯m7, pitched around) on EXS24-as-synth + ESP an octave up. → [[2 Drums Group|Drums]].
- **Bounce every bass hit to an SPD for live** — Guy printed each phased bass note to a Roland SPD pad and played the bassline live with sticks, so the phaser gave each hit variation on stage. → live-rig note.
- *What-not-to-do (era artifact):* limiters on **everything**, drum bus + master pinned to 0.1 dB, Ozone 5 across the drums — naive loudness-by-limiting; the modern approach is saturation-into-a-clipper (see [[Master Chain]]).

# Another Girl — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Pollack treats this less as a career highlight than as a catalogue of every Beatles device in circulation by early 1965 — and as evidence of John's influence running back onto Paul, since the opening gambit, the refrain habit and the modulation scheme all come from "You're Going To Lose That Girl".

**Form.** A standard two-verse / two-bridge layout that *sounds* stranger than it is, because the verse's last phrase behaves like a refrain and doubles as the intro. Parse that phrase as part of the verse and the shape snaps into place. The intro runs closer to five bars than four — it lands on the downbeat of bar 5 — so every appearance elides into whatever follows. The verse is 16 bars phrased AABC, close to a non-traditional 12-bar plus a short refrain.

**Harmony.** Verses stay inside I, IV, V and ♭VII, with ♭VII used only as a neighbour between two statements of I ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). The IV is then held for four bars that deliberately **don't line up with the phrase divisions** — harmonic rhythm working subliminally ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). The bridge is a genuine modulation to C major, the relative major of the parallel minor ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]], [[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]). Two pivots earn attention: going in, the verse's final D chord puns as IV at home and V-of-V in C, unresolved until two bars into the bridge; coming out, C is re-read as III in A minor and cadences V–i, except the "i" arrives major.

**Melody.** Descending chromatic fragments circling a narrow range, leaning on ♭3 and ♭7 hard enough to read bluesy over harmony that is nothing like a blues — and hard enough to leave the mode of the home key genuinely ambiguous ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]).

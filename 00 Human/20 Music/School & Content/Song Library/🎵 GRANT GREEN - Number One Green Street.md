# Number One Green Street — Grant Green

> A swing blues in B♭, the second of two Grant Green solos Jack Ruch transcribes to build jazz-blues vocabulary — see [[How Grant Green Played Jazz Blues]] for the source video, and [[🎵 GRANT GREEN - Miss Ann's Tempo]] for the companion excerpt over the same key/form, several devices shared between the two. A second Jack Ruch video, [[Transcribing Licks And Making Them Your Own]], transcribes an overlapping I→IV→VI→ii passage from this same solo with a methodology focus (how to rework a transcribed line into your own), adding the minor-third-transposition device and a diminished-7-arpeggio reading of the VI7 color not covered in the first video.

## Chords

**Key:** B♭ · **Form:** 12-bar jazz blues, swing feel (from *Green Street*, 1961 — track listed as "No. 1 Green Street"). Bars 1–4: **B♭7 (I)**. Bars 5–8: a **ii–V–I into IV** — **Fm7 – B♭7 – E♭** → **ii7 – V7 – I** (of E♭) — before working back toward the top. Bars 9–12: the jazz-blues turnaround — **G7 – Cm7 – F7 – B♭7** → **VI7 – ii7 – V7 – I**, the same "6-2-5-1" as [[🎵 GRANT GREEN - Miss Ann's Tempo]]. → [[Blues Form and Harmony]], [[Turnarounds]] § *VI7*, [[The ii-V-I]]

## Solo breakdown (Jack Ruch's transcription, first chorus)

- **Pickup into bar 1:** a rhythmic pickup phrase plays *into* the top of the form rather than starting cold on the downbeat. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Bars 1–4 (I, B♭):** plain blues vocabulary — **root, major 3rd, 4th, 5th, root, ♭7** — called out as "pretty standard blues stuff," the floor beneath the denser material elsewhere in the chorus. → [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- **Bars 5–6 (2-5-1 into IV):** a lick built directly out of the ii chord of the target — **F minor 7 – B♭7 – E♭**, played "right out of an F minor seven" arpeggio rather than a scale run across the two bars. → [[Chord Substitution]] § *4. Interpolating a ii in front of any dominant*, [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]]
- **Landing on IV (E♭):** the same I→IV chromatic pivot as [[🎵 GRANT GREEN - Miss Ann's Tempo]] — hitting the **♭7 of E♭7 (D♭)**, confirming it's genuine recurring Green vocabulary rather than a one-off. → [[Chord-Tone Soloing]]
- **Back to I:** more minor-pentatonic blues vocabulary bridging back to the top of the form. → [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- **Bars 9–12 (6-2-5-1 turnaround):** the same signature move as the companion tune — target the **♭9 of G7 (A♭)** for the altered/bebop color — followed by a **C minor arpeggio/triad** over Cm7, then F7, back to B♭7. Closes the turnaround with a **chromatic walk up to the 3rd of the I chord (B♭7)**, a guide-tone-targeting resolution mirroring the ♭9-then-chromatic-walk device used on the VI7. → [[Turnarounds]] § *VI7*, [[Bebop Vocabulary]] § *Target the guide tones* / *Approach notes*
- **[[Transcribing Licks And Making Them Your Own]]'s reading of the same I→IV→VI→ii stretch:** the I→IV move is described as the **B♭ altered scale over B♭7, shifted down a half step over E♭7** to outline an **E♭♯11 (Lydian dominant)** color — a slightly different framing of the same chromatic pivot documented above. Going into the VI chord (G7), the ♭9 color is attributed to a **diminished-7th arpeggio** device (stated rule: dim7 built a half step above the dominant's root — standard half-whole-diminished; the specific worked instance named in that video is ambiguous between that and a same-root G-dim7-over-G7 reading — confirm by ear). Over the ii chord (Cm7), Green plays a short phrase and then **immediately transposes it up a minor third and repeats it** — called out as "a really common theme in a lot of Grant's phrasing," and not documented from the first video. → [[Diminished Chords]], [[Connecting Licks Into Longer Lines]] § *Transposition device*

## Steal this

- **Approach a IV chord with a 2-5-1 built from its own ii-chord arpeggio** (F minor 7 into B♭7 into E♭) rather than running a scale across the transition — a small, self-contained harmonic unit worth having ready any time a IV chord is a bar or two away.
- **Chromatic walk-up to the tonic's 3rd** as a turnaround-closing device — the mirror image of the ♭9-then-chromatic-walk approach used on the VI7 earlier in the same chorus, now landing the whole form instead of coloring one chord.
- **"Learn the arpeggios to the underlying chords first"** — Ruch's own stated on-ramp for finding lines like these, aimed at players newer to this vocabulary. → [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]]
- **Transpose a short phrase up a minor third and replay it immediately** — a genuine recurring Grant Green habit (confirmed here over the ii chord), and a free way to generate a second phrase from one idea without composing anything new. → [[Connecting Licks Into Longer Lines]]

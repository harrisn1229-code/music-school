# Ian Kirkpatrick — Charli XCX "Move Me" Synths

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RXm50CdQ9-o)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RXm50CdQ9-o)

## Synopsis

Ian walks the synth layers of Charli XCX's "Move Me" — a **70/140 BPM half-time** track where the challenge is keeping a slow tempo from feeling "sleepy." His solutions are all about **using synths as rhythm/tempo agitators** and burying candy low in the mix with OTT. Names specific sources (Dramatica "Concerto," Nexus, Kontakt "ready or not"), the behind-the-beat pluck trick, and a textbook hidden-synth-in-vocal.

## Techniques

- **Verse-defining synth from "Concerto" (Dramatica)** — "this guy makes the whole vibe of the verse — without it it's scary/mysterious, with it it's the song." Build the section around one identity synth. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[MIDS Slot]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Fight "sleepy" half-time with rhythmic synths, not more drums** — at ~70 BPM (half-time 140) it's easy to feel sleepy; use busy/agitating synth rhythms "to get tempo without using drum elements." A tempo agitator. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Bassline Writing]]
- **A PadShop tempo-agitator from a vocal source** — one rhythmic layer is PadShop fed by a vocal/something; adds rhythm and motion. → [[PadShop]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Ascending synth = Nexus + OTT on full max** — "OTT is good for getting information across at low volume: it expands and compresses whatever you put it on, so you can bury something very low and still get a lot of it." Great for atmospherics. → [[Nexus]], [[OTT]], [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[Multiband Compression]]
- **"Ready or Not" synth (Kontakt) as a vibe reference** — a favorite background synth named because it evokes the Fugees "Ready or Not." Uses Kontakt with a "weird cue," kept very low. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Behind-the-beat plucks so they read past the drums** — deliberately pushing pluck hits *late* (behind the beat) so they're heard even though they share the beat with the drums — "you get a little more of it because it's behind the drum by a little." → [[Automation]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Hidden synth under the double-time vocal** — "primo example of hiding synths in vocals": a synth sits under the double-time vocal, printed at 0 dB then left low (negative) in the mix. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Ian Kirkpatrick - Hiding Synths In Vocals]]
- **Section-as-theme: a sound that appears once and returns at the end** — a first-verse pitch-mapped sound is withheld for the rest of the song then brought back at the end "like a theme" for an emotional payoff. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Pitch Map for pre-chorus vocal FX** — a lot of the pre uses pitch-mapping on the vocal (gated reverb + pitched vocal). → [[Pitch and Formant]]

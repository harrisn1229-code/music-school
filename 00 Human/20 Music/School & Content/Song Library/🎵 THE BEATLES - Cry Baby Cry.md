# Cry Baby Cry — The Beatles

> Lennon's fairy-tale verse structure inspired by nursery rhymes. George Martin plays harmonium. Eerie, minor-key atmosphere with each verse presenting a different royal character.

## Chords
**Key:** G major / E minor

**Verse ("The king of Marigold…"):** Em – Emmaj7 – Em7 – Em6 – C – C7 – G
→ **i – i(maj7) – i7 – i6 – VI – VI7 – III** (the Em→Emmaj7→Em7→Em6 is a chromatic inner descent E–D♯–D–C♯)

**Chorus ("Cry baby cry"):** G – Am – F – G – Em7 – A7 – F
→ **I – ii – ♭VII – I – vi7 – V7/V – ♭VII** (the F is a borrowed ♭VII, A7 a secondary dominant)

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Cry Baby Cry]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #154.*

**Form.** A standard folk-song shape opened by a refrain, then four verse/refrain cycles, then an outro — note that he starts the form on the **refrain**, not the verse. Refrain 8 bars in two loosely parallel phrases; verse 12, better heard as a 4-bar sung phrase plus a 2-bar instrumental flourish, stated twice; outro the refrain three times over. The structural interest is **elision**: the opening refrain ends on E minor and runs into verse 1, bar 12 of every verse elides into the following refrain, and the first two outro refrains overlap the next. Without that the folk frame would sit square and dead.

**Harmony.** `Refrain (8): I – ii – ♭VII – I · vi – V-of-V – ♭VII – I` · `Verse (12): vi held 4 bars over a descending bass E – E♭ – D – D♭ · C7 (IV) – I, ×2`. **There is no V chord anywhere in the song** — ♭VII carries the whole cadential load ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]), which produces an implied cross-relation against the key signature's F♯, made explicit by the A major chord in the refrain and again by the C7's blue B♭ against the G chord.

**Notable.** The verse's first four bars are his object lesson in **when not to use Roman numerals**: E minor, E♭ augmented, G in 6/4 and a half-diminished seventh on D♭ are not a progression, they are a line ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]), and the only reason they read as chords is the time scale — one bar each. The tune is mostly **pentatonic on G–A–B–D–E** ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]), and the track ends on E minor, not G, with an unresolved 4th hanging over it ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]).

**Against this page.** The frontmatter `key:` here reads A major; **Pollack reads G major / E minor, deliberately unresolved between the two** — which is what this page's own Chords section says as well. Nothing above has been changed.

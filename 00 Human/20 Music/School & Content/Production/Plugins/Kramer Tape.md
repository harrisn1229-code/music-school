# Kramer Tape

![[Images/Kramer Tape.png]]
*Waves' current UI (Kramer Master Tape shown — Kramer Tape's interface is nearly identical).*

**Category:** Drive/Color
**Vendor:** Waves

## What it is

Waves' tape saturation plugin modeled on engineer Eddie Kramer's tape machine settings, capturing analog tape warmth and compression.

## What it's used for

Analog tape warmth, glue, and gentle compression character on tracks or busses.

## FX chain notes

A coloration stage, often used per-track and again lightly on the mix bus.

## Techniques

- **The engineer this plugin is named for, describing the exact real-world move it exists to model: an overdriven console mic pre stacked with hot tape level, for a "slight crunch" that reads almost like amp distortion with no amp in the path.** Eddie Kramer, on the 12-string/6-string guitar blend that opens "All Along The Watchtower": "the Helios mic pre would break up quite quickly, and I'm obviously overdriving it... I'm just pushing the envelope and driving it really hard so you get that slight crunch on the acoustic, and then, of course, the tape is being hit really hard too — I can imagine the needles are banging off the end stops." Two saturation stages stacked in series, not one device doing all the work: preamp drive first, then hot tape level on top. His own stated modern recreation path (no HLS available): use a normal mic pre, "gradually distort the input, just gradually, just enough," then reach for a plugin's EQ stage for the rest — maps to driving [[UAD Neve 1073]]'s Unison gain for harmonic push, this device for the tape-print half. → [[UAD Neve 1073]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[1 Acoustic Guitar Track|Acoustic Guitar Track]] — Mix with the Masters (Eddie Kramer), [[Eddie Kramer's story behind "All Along The Watchtower" by Jimi Hendrix]]
- **Best-match owned equivalent for a Studer A800 tape machine used as a combined de-esser AND deliberate voice-thinning stage, via a named preset, on a background-role lead vocal.** "A little de-essing, again I'm using the Studer A800 and trying to get that analog sound. In this case, I'm using the Pop setting because I wanted him to be a little thinner." The de-essing here is a side effect of the tape stage's own saturation/EQ character (the "Pop" formula), not a threshold-based de-esser — worth remembering tape emulation is a legitimate de-ess lane when the goal is character + gentle taming together rather than surgical sibilance control. This plugin has no "Pop" preset equivalent, but the mechanism transfers: dial EQ/saturation toward thin/bright rather than this note's usual warm/full settings. Studer A800 itself unowned → [[Plugin Wishlist]]. *(qualitative — no exact settings given)* → [[De-essing]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Tony Maserati Vocal Chain]] — Tony Maserati (Mix with the Masters), [[Vocal chain with Tony Maserati]]

- **Closest owned stand-in for a tape stage that needs an ENVELOPE-MODE noise knob — a real gap worth knowing about.** Recreating a Mk.gee CZ-5000-style synth chain, the source specifically reaches for the "Reels" tape plugin because its noise knobs have an **envelope mode** — hiss/noise only sounds *while a note is playing*, instead of a constant fuzzy noise bed sitting under the whole part. This plugin doesn't document that same envelope-linked noise behavior, so treat it as the nearest owned tape-coloration substitute rather than a like-for-like match; if the noise-under-every-note artifact is audible and unwanted, that's the gap. Chain position in the same recipe: flanger → chorus → **tape (this device)** → plate reverb → distortion → heavy end-of-chain compression. → [[Phaser-Flanger]], [[Chorus-Ensemble]], [[Little Plate]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — loner, [the secrets behind Mk.gee's HYPNOTIC production!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=969p5Q4JSZU)
- **The genre-right call for a gentle mastering pass (jazz named specifically) — and a genuine alternative to limiting, not just tonal color.** Peaks attenuate gradually, shaped in a manner indicative of the genre's era; this device's own settings can even be tuned toward the specific machine that era of jazz would have run through. A Clipper ([[kHs Clipper]]) would be the wrong choice here — no reason to impart aggressive transient distortion, no reason to push toward a loudness that would necessitate clipping. The opposite call (aggressive genres like EDM, where a clipper is right and this device would fight the intended aggression) is logged on [[kHs Clipper]]. → [[Critical Listening for Mastering]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Clipping and Loudness]] — Sage Audio, [Before You Master Music, Watch This](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tEqcGkgHgqc)
- **The Lowpass control's job maps directly onto tape's real top-end rolloff — a plugin-only recreation confirmed the mechanism** — an in-the-box attempt to fake 1970s tape character explicitly targeted "subtle saturation, subtle compression, and rolling off a lot of that top end" as tape's three ingredients; instead of a separate channel EQ, a high shelf pulled down (or this plugin's own Lowpass) does that rolloff from inside the tape stage itself. → [[Saturation and Distortion]] — Zach Scholz, [I Tried Recreating 1970s Tape Sound… Without Tape](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ma276-aF5lg)
- **Flux is doing the same job real tape's "natural tape compression" does when engineers describe driving a tape plugin for glue** — a plugin-only 70s-recreation session ran acoustic guitar through a wow/flutter-plus-saturation stage into a UAD Oxide plugin specifically "for some of that natural tape compression" — push Flux for that squash, keep Playback near unity so it reads as glue rather than a level change, exactly this note's existing Flux guidance. — Zach Scholz, [I Tried Recreating 1970s Tape Sound… Without Tape](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ma276-aF5lg)
- **Direct hardware validation of this plugin's own "Tape finish" [[Master Chain]] stage** — bussing several tracks together and sending them out to a physical tape deck at once, consolidating to a stereo pass, produces glue credited specifically to "tape's compression and saturation" — the same principle this plugin already runs at 15 ips / very low flux / unity playback on the master, just done on hardware and applied earlier, at a guitar-group bus rather than the final 2-bus. → [[Sony TC-630]] — Zach Scholz, [Using Analog Tape to Glue a Mix Together](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xk0LYE7HGAI)
- **Tape speed choice validated from the buying-guide side: 15 ips is the fidelity speed, slower speeds are the character speeds** — 15 ips gives excellent clarity/low noise (why this plugin's Master Chain and Guitar Bus stages both default to it); 7½ ips reads warmer with more personality; 3¾ ips is hissier and darker — confirms this plugin's own Tape Speed guidance (15 ips for warmth/glue as the *cleaner* end of the character range, not the darkest one). — Zach Scholz, [Don't Buy a Tape Machine Before Watching This](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXiwDEBE9VM)
- **Drum-bus tape option in the group-saturation move** — one of his named tape choices (with UAD Ampex and J37) for saturating a whole drum group rather than single hits; J37-style tape also earns a spot on chimes/textures for pitch wobble + tape-delay duty — Lophiile, [Make Your Drums Slap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v4ZhwlhF5yE) & [All About Basslines stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tnruvmw_OWc)

## Used in my chains

My **analog-glue tape stage** — one shared tape texture across the mix is the cheapest cohesion there is. Usually **low drive at 15 ips**, backed off to unity; driven hard only when the squash *is* the effect (drum bus). **Flux is the real drive knob**:

- **[[Master Chain]]** — *slot 4, tape finish*: 15 ips, very low drive, unity out — final top-end sweetening + gentle transient softening that reads as "expensive."
- **[[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]]** — *slot 2, tape stage*: driven **hard** (GP9-style, lows rolled off) so it squashes the hats/percussion every time the kick hits; back off if it loses overall dynamic range.
- **[[3 Guitar Bus|Guitar Bus]]** — *slot 2, tape glue*: low drive, 15 ips, Flux to taste, output unity — "guitars glued to the record."
- **[[3 Inst-Synth Bus|Inst-Synth Bus]]** — *slot 2, analog unify*: low drive — one tape texture over every digital instrument.
- **[[1 Acoustic Piano Track|Acoustic Piano Track]]** — *slot 3, sweeten*: low drive — tape wobble/harmonics give a too-perfect VST piano the "recorded in a room" quality.

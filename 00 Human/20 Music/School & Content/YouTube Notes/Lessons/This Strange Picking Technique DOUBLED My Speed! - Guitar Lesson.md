## Synopsis

A compressed sweep-picking variant that trades the usual single-note-at-a-time fretting for holding a small chord shape down and letting the picking hand articulate it — closely related to sweep picking but built to remove the left-hand bottleneck that normally caps sweep speed.

## Techniques

- **The core problem with traditional sweep picking:** the fretting hand is still working almost like a solo/scale line — mostly single notes, occasional hammer-ons/pull-offs, carefully avoiding letting notes overlap. That left-hand precision requirement is what caps how fast a traditional sweep can go.
- **The fix:** reduce the arpeggio to a small shape — worked example on the top three strings (G, B, E) — and **keep the fretting fingers pressed down together like a chord grip** instead of thinking note-by-note. The left hand does much less work per note this way.
- **Right-hand role:** with the left hand essentially just holding a chord shape, the picking hand does the real articulating — light palm muting from the picking hand keeps the notes from blurring into an undifferentiated strum while still letting them separate rhythmically.
- **Explicitly not "just strumming":** the technique only works, and only sounds different from plain strumming, when played tight against a metronome — described as playing 16th or 32nd notes (or triplets) with real rhythmic precision, not loose chord scraping.
- **Two stated goals:** (1) push sweep-picking speed further than the traditional left-hand-limited approach allows, and (2) open up more unique/less-generic-sounding sweep sections by not being locked into single-note arpeggio shapes.

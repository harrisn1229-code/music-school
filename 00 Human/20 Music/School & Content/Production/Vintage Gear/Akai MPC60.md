# Akai MPC60

## What it is
The MIDI Production Center — Roger Linn's post-Linn-Electronics design for Akai, fusing a 12-bit sampler, a 99-track MIDI sequencer, and the now-universal **4×4 velocity pad grid** into one box. The MPC turned sampling into an instrument you *play*, and its sequencer timing became the feel of hip hop. Every pad controller and every DAW drum grid descends from this machine.

## What makes the sound
- **12-bit / 40 kHz sampling** with Akai's converters + filtering — grittier than modern digital but brighter and cleaner than the [[E-mu SP-1200]]; "punchy" is the usual word.
- **The swing and timing are the real instrument.** Linn's quantize/swing (54–58% is the classic pocket) plus the machine's slightly loose internal timing produce the head-nod feel; MPC swing is arguably the most copied *groove* in music software → Ableton's groove pool, [[Group Humanizer]].
- **Pads + note-repeat** made programming physical — velocity-layered hats, drum rolls, and chopped-sample performances that sound played, not drawn.
- 16 levels / tuning per pad and quick chop-assign workflow → the chop-a-loop-across-pads vocabulary of hip hop production → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] + [[Simpler]]/[[Sampler]] replicate the chop workflow (slice-to-drum-rack is the MPC move in Ableton); Ableton's MPC-style groove files for the swing; [[Decimort 2]] for the 12-bit converter character; [[Group Humanizer]] for pocket.
- **Paid:** Akai MPC Software / MPC hardware line (direct descendants), XLN XO for a modern take on the workflow.
- **Free:** Akai MPC Beats (free version of the modern MPC software); MPC60-processed drum kits circulate widely.

## Used on
- DJ Shadow — *Endtroducing.....* (1996): built almost entirely on an MPC60 + records, documented and celebrated.
- DJ Premier — Gang Starr-era productions on MPC60: the boom-bap swing standard.
- Genre/era: late-80s→90s hip hop production wholesale; the MPC3000 successor carried it on (J Dilla's machine — his note will cover the drunk-swing evolution).

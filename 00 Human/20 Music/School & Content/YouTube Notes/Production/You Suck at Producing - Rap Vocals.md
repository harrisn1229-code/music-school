# You Suck At Producing: Rap Vocals

📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DIuHKtdDK8Y)

## Synopsis

A comedy-sketch wrapper (a satirical "rapper friend" recording session) around a genuinely solid beginner rap-vocal-mixing walkthrough: manual timing correction, a low-cut for plosive pops, a fast-attack/fast-release compressor with makeup gain to tame loud words without losing overall power, and a dedicated compressor-as-de-esser for harsh S sounds.

## Techniques

- **Manually nudge an early/late vocal clip into pocket rather than re-recording.** A take that's rushing or dragging relative to the beat gets dragged slightly left (earlier) or right (later) in the timeline until it locks into the pocket — the most basic fix for a performance that's close but not quite on the beat.
- **Low-cut around 100 Hz to remove a plosive/mic-pop, not a broad EQ sweep.** A pop from a spittle hit or plosive consonant shows up as an ugly low-frequency spike on an EQ analyzer; raising the low cutoff to roughly 100 Hz surgically removes it without touching the rest of the vocal's tone. → [[EQ]]
- **Fast attack + fast release compressor to tame specific loud words, then makeup gain to restore the lost level.** Set the threshold just under the loud outlier words, drop attack and release both low so the compressor can react and recover fast enough to keep up with rapid-fire rap delivery, then add makeup gain afterward — the net result reads as *tighter and more powerful*, not just quieter, because the loud spikes are gone but the overall level is restored. → [[Compression]]
- **A dedicated second compressor, reacting only to sibilant highs, IS a de-esser** — rather than reaching for a labeled "de-esser" plugin, insert a second compressor and set it up to react specifically to the vocal's high-frequency S/T energy; it ducks only during those sounds and leaves the rest of the performance untouched. The video's own beginner-level version of the more surgical Multiband-Dynamics de-esser build already documented on [[De-essing]]. → [[De-essing]]

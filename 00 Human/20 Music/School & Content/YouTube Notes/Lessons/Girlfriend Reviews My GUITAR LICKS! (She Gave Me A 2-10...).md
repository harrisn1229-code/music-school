# Girlfriend Reviews My GUITAR LICKS! (She Gave Me A 2/10...)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IT9PFISF2Fw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IT9PFISF2Fw)

## Synopsis

An informal listener-perception experiment: BERNTH plays 7 shred licks for his non-musician girlfriend and has her rate each on sound (1-10) and perceived difficulty (1-10), without technical framing. The gap between her ratings and the licks' actual technical difficulty is the real content — a useful reality check on which techniques *read* as impressive to a non-player ear versus which ones actually are hard.

## What's taught

- **Perception-vs-reality gaps, the video's core finding, tabulated:**
  - Harmonic minor lick with alternate picking across strings — actually one of the harder items technically (constant string switching); rated only moderate difficulty (5/10) by ear, but sound scored highest (9/10) — an "acquired taste" dissonant interval read as exciting rather than off.
  - Descending chromatic scale (tremolo/sweep on fixed shapes) — rated the LOWEST sound score (2/10, "sounds like an exercise") specifically because of *note choice* (chromatic = no clear tonality), not technique. Correctly identified as harder to listen to than to play by ear alone once she could hear it wasn't from a scale.
  - Sweep-picked arpeggio (single repeated minor shape moved chromatically) — rated as VISUALLY very hard (8-9/10) but is one of the mechanically *easier* items for BERNTH specifically because it's one shape repeated, not multiple different shapes — a clear case of visual complexity reading as harder than it mechanically is.
  - Legato-heavy line (pull-offs generating most of the notes, minimal right-hand work) — rated genuinely difficult (7-9/10) specifically because the **left hand visibly does almost all the work while the right hand looks passive** — correctly intuited as hard despite looking "easy" on the picking side, a rare case where the non-player's read matched the technical reality closely.
  - Odd note-grouping lick (pentatonic scale, notes grouped 3+5+3+5+3 rather than an even count) — rated "sounds odd/weird" and NOT correctly attributed to note choice; she guessed it wasn't the notes (right) but couldn't identify the grouping/rhythm as the actual source of the odd sound.
  - Tapping + slide combination — rated as the hardest-looking (9/10) and also highly rated for sound (8/10) — one case where visual difficulty, ear rating, and actual technical difficulty roughly agreed.
- **Stated creator takeaway:** useful information for **which techniques to lean on for audience impact vs. which ones are just for personal challenge** — a chromatic scale run is genuinely difficult to execute cleanly but reads as "just an exercise" to a general listener, while a single repeated sweep shape looks impressive despite being comparatively easy once the shape is learned.

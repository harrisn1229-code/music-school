# I recreated "I Am The Walrus" from scratch

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4RM80hbrcc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4RM80hbrcc)

## Synopsis

A production vlog (sponsored by computer builder Scan) documenting a from-scratch, copyright-clearance-motivated recreation of the Beatles' "I Am The Walrus," instrument by instrument — useful mainly as a virtual-instrument matching reference and a workflow example for transcribing an orchestral arrangement into MIDI.

## Techniques

- **Pianet**: matched with a Keyscape pianet patch, approximating the original's Hohner electric piano.
- **Horns**: BBC Symphony Orchestra doubled with Session Horns Pro for extra bite.
- **Violin**: no dedicated violin instrument owned — BBC Symphony Orchestra doubled with Spitfire Solo Cello for texture (a cello isn't a violin, but layering it in thickened the sound usefully).
- **Cello** (the arrangement's key harmonic-space instrument, playing almost throughout): BBC Symphony Orchestra layered under Spitfire Solo Cello as the primary voice, prioritized for realism since it's so exposed.
- **Workflow**: strings/horns transcribed by ear into MuseScore, exported as MIDI, then imported and assigned virtual instruments — rather than programmed directly in the DAW.
- **Drums**: Abbey Road 60s Drummer, a period-correct kit/mic emulation.
- **The King Lear radio broadcast**: recreated literally by tuning an actual secondhand AM radio live and capturing whatever was broadcasting, matching the original's aleatoric method rather than simulating old BBC audio.
- **Backing vocals**: a single performance pitch-shifted into a mock-choir, standing in for the original's 16-voice arrangement.

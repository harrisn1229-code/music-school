# You Suck at Producing - Using Foley to bring Basic Drum Loops to Life

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s_tr6C6R7nU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s_tr6C6R7nU)

## Synopsis
Made around Underbelly's collab track "Reverse" with Stasis, this is a genuinely dense sound-design tutorial on turning ordinary field-recorded/foley material and even a single drum one-shot into percussion using nothing but Ableton's warping tools. Two techniques carry the whole video: using transient/warp markers plus the preserve-segment slider to chop a slow field recording (wind chimes) into a rhythmic percussion pattern purely by controlling how much of each segment survives, and building an entire varied percussion loop out of ONE snare sample using transpose, note length, and a randomized Auto Pan. Also touches an LFO-multi-parameter randomization trick for hi-hats. Well worth a full watch for anyone building texture-based drum layers rather than reaching for another sample pack.

## Techniques
- **Warp markers turn transient detection into a percussion-chopping tool** — Ableton auto-places a warp marker at every detected transient in a clip (visible as thin lines across the waveform); double-click one to create a new draggable warp point, then drag it left/right to stretch that segment of audio. Setting loop mode to a single forward-play arrow (rather than the default back-and-forth looped-fill) makes Ableton leave any gap as silence instead of covering it with looped filler audio.
- **The preserve-segment slider is the real percussion-design control** — this slider governs how long each transient-to-transient chunk of audio plays before cutting to the next chunk; turning it DOWN makes each segment much shorter, which turns a slow, sustained field recording (wind chimes in the source example) into a rhythmic, percussive pattern purely by how much survives of each segment. Concrete settings used: **preserve-eighths** for an eighth-note pulse against kick/snare, then splitting off a section and dropping to **preserve-sixteenths** for denser 16th-note rolls, and even **preserve-32nds** for an extreme glitchy blur at the very end of a phrase. A limiter (ceiling lowered) helps even out the volume once a source is chopped this aggressively.
- **Turn a single snare one-shot into a full percussion loop** — drag both the fade-in and the sustain/loop point toward the transient to shorten the sample drastically into a short, percussive click; place it densely (every 16th note, or 32nd notes for rolls) across a loop, then vary EACH repeat's **octave transpose** (Shift + Up/Down arrow — a fast, keyboard-only way to shift a note an octave) for pitched variation and per-hit **volume** for dynamic variation, so the exact same sample reads as multiple different hits.
- **Auto Pan's Sample & Hold waveform, maxed, is the finishing touch on a single-sample percussion loop** — switch Auto Pan's waveform from the default smooth L/R sweep to **Sample & Hold** (a fully random value chosen fresh every cycle), push **Shape** all the way up, set **Rate** to match the pattern's hit rate (16th notes, matching the snare pattern above), and push **Width** up — the result is a random stereo bounce on every single hit, which reads as dramatically more dynamic/stereo than the plain mono repeat it started as.
- **An LFO's Random/Sample & Hold waveform mapped to several hi-hat parameters at once** — turn on the device's LFO, switch its waveform to Random, turn OFF Retrigger so it runs continuously rather than resetting per note, and map it to volume, pitch, panning, and filter cutoff simultaneously — every hit lands with a different combination of all four, which reads as much more alive than a single-parameter variation. → [[Rhythm and Groove]] (already routed, shared bullet with [[You Suck at Drums No. 4: Adding Swing & Groove to Hip Hop Drums|Adding Swing & Groove to Hip Hop Drums]])

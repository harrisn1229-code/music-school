# How to hear the key of a song INSTANTLY

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OIPDxGHKa3s)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OIPDxGHKa3s)

## Synopsis

A short, practical method for finding a song's tonic by ear on guitar: physically search chromatically up one string until a note sounds like "home," then confirm major/minor by feel or by testing whether the minor pentatonic scale off that root fits. Genuinely additive to the vault's existing, deeper [[Transcribing and Playing by Ear]] workflow — a physical brute-force search technique that page doesn't cover, alongside its sing/imagine-based methods.

## Techniques

- **Chromatic single-string search for the tonic.** Play chromatically up one string (the low E, in the demo) against the track until a note sounds like "home" — a direct physical search rather than singing or imagining the target note first. Framed as the practical fallback for anyone who can't yet reliably sing/imagine the tonic in their head.
- **Major/minor-by-feel, plus a minor-pentatonic playability test to confirm.** First pass: judge the found tonic's overall color by ear (happy/bright → major, sad/dark → minor) — with the explicit caveat that minor keys don't always read as sad (can read as funky or other moods depending on genre/context). Confirmation test: play the minor pentatonic scale rooted on the candidate tonic against the track — if it fits cleanly, the key is very likely minor.
- **Probability framing for why this method works reasonably well even with imperfect ears**: any given key uses 7 of the 12 available notes, so a random guess is already more likely to land on an in-key note than an out-of-key one — searching chromatically converges faster than the odds might suggest.

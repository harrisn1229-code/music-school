## Synopsis

BERNTH teaches a scale-degree counting shortcut for harmonizing a single-note melody or solo (octaves, thirds, fifths, sixths) without needing to consciously track interval qualities note by note, worked over the intro melody to his own song "Dopamine" in F Dorian. ## What's taught

- **Octaves first, as the safest/easiest harmonization** — double the melody an octave up and/or down; zero harmonic risk since it's the same notes, and the fretboard's identical repeat after the 12th fret makes it an easy fingering transfer.
- **The core shortcut for any diatonic interval: count that many scale degrees up from the melody note, staying inside the scale, rather than tracking major/minor quality by ear.** Thirds = count up 3 degrees (skip one); fifths = count up 5 (skip three); sixths = count up 6 (skip four). The resulting interval's quality (major/minor/perfect) falls out automatically from the scale's own formula at that degree — the shortcut removes the need to consciously decide major-3rd-vs-minor-3rd note by note.
- **Each interval's character:** thirds are the pleasant default but risk sounding stale/cheesy if used exclusively; fifths are more open/harder-edged (visualize the melody as power-chord roots, keep only the top note); sixths are more "experimental," recommended specifically as the fix when an all-thirds harmonization gets too sweet.
- **Caveat: the scale-degree shortcut is safe ~90% of the time, not always** — it doesn't account for what the underlying chords/rhythm section are doing, so an in-scale harmony note can still occasionally clash against a specific chord voicing; treat it as a fast first pass, verify problem spots by ear.
- **Writing method: harmonize the full melody separately in each interval first (octaves, then thirds, then fifths, then sixths), THEN mix and match** the best-sounding sections from each pass into a final combined take, trusting the ear over a fixed rule at the combining stage.

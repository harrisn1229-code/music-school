# Line 6 DL4

**Category:** Pedal (delay/looper)
**Vendor:** Line 6
**Ballpark value (used, 2026):** $150–250 (original; MkII differs)

## What it is

The green delay modeler — 16 delay models (tape, analog, reverse, sweep…) plus the looper that launched a thousand loop artists. (If yours is the MkII, note it — different manual.)

## What it's used for

Everything from slapback to ambient reverse washes; the looper doubles as a writing scratchpad.

## Signal chain notes

Last in the pedal chain, after dirt ([[Big Muff Pi]]/[[Boss DS-2]]) — delay after distortion stays intelligible. Tap tempo to the song BPM before tracking.

## Techniques

- **Boss Delay Machine recipe, mapping onto this pedal's last-in-chain slot** — three, maybe four repeats that "die out and get a little bit dark as they go" (a slight low-pass darkening on the tail), with a slight bit of modulation layered on the repeats; tap tempo set to the song for a faster slapback or a longer ambient wash depending on mood. Same "delay after distortion stays intelligible" ordering this pedal's own signal-chain note already runs — pedals stack Voodoo-1-boost → King-of-Tone-overdrive (occasionally fuzz-before-overdrive) ahead of it. → [[Delay]], [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track|Electric Guitar Amp Track]] — Jack Ruch, [My Home Recording Setup for Guitar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KYTGFneEJ2A)
- **Looped-chunk woodshedding of chord changes** — record a short 2–4 bar chord chunk into the looper, work out arpeggio/chord-tone ideas against silence first, then loop the chunk and test the phrasing against it before combining chunks into a full progression; a competing looper pedal's dedicated, no-latency record button and built-in drum-machine count-in are called out as the features that make this workflow frictionless — worth checking the DL4's own record-button behavior/latency against that bar, and pairing loop layers with a metronome or drum-machine app for the same rhythmic anchor if the DL4 doesn't have one built in. Full routine → [[Woodshedding Changes with a Looper]]. — Jack Ruch, [[How using the TELEPORT can IMPROVE your PRACTICE routine!]]

# how to make melodic r&b beats for tory lanez & chris brown (underwater synth/vocal sample)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JCup6FVW5o)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JCup6FVW5o)

## Synopsis

loner breaks down a melodic R&B beat built in the style of Tory Lanez's "Take Care" (name-checking Chris Brown/Drake as the same lane), reverse-engineering the reference's central idea — a "watered down," underwater-sounding synth pad plus vocal chops — into a from-scratch build. The core of the video is a single reusable trick: take a plain one-shot, reverse it, trim its start/length by ear until the reversed tail creates a natural pulsing swell, then play a simple four-chord progression through it and process it heavily (low cut → long reverb → a low-pass-leaning EQ → a mix-reduced multi-effects "shaper" stage) until it reads as submerged/underwater rather than as a clean synth pad. The vocal-chop half runs a similar heavy-processing philosophy (bandpass EQ → an automated half-time rhythmic effect → reverb → heavy delay) so the two elements — synth and vocal — are genuinely the only two melodic sources in the whole beat, which the video explicitly holds up as proof that a catchy chord progression, strong sound selection, and a bouncy drum pattern are enough on their own; you don't need to keep stacking extra elements. The second half of the video is a compact drum-programming breakdown (layered hi-hats at different "eras" of processing/timing feel, velocity/pan-varied kicks for bounce, a long subby 808) plus a short arrangement pass using selective muting (drums in/out, vocals half-time on/off) to signal intro/pre-hook/hook/verse sections without writing new material for each.

## Techniques

**Ambient underwater synth pad (the video's headline trick):**
- **Source:** an ambient/synthy one-shot found in the producer's own ("Nami's") one-shot kit — described as sounding like "the watered-down synth" in the Tory Lanez reference.
- **Reverse + trim:** reverse the one-shot, then adjust its **start point and length by ear** until the reversed tail's natural swell creates the desired "pulsing in and out" motion — this manual trim is what actually produces the pulse, not a separate LFO/modulation stage.
- **Chords (four, one per bar, D minor tonal center):** Dm7 → D♯maj7 → Gm7 (voiced with the **top two notes dropped down** an octave) → Fmaj (voiced with the **C dropped down** an octave). See [[Chord Progressions]] for the theory writeup.
- **Rhythmic hit pattern:** each chord is played **four times per bar** (not held as a single sustained hit) — this repetition is what generates the "pulsing effect," working together with the reversed-tail trim above.
- **Processing chain (in order):** low-end cut (EQ) → long reverb, "infinity" preset (auto-captioned "intensity reverb… infinity preset," exact plugin unconfirmed) → **[[Ghz Loh1 3]]** ("good hurts low high," HI-cut side used to tame excess brightness) → **[[Shaper]]** (ShaperBox-style device) with **Mix pulled to ~50%**, used purely to filter out some high-end frequencies for a "choppy," sample-like, underwater/low character — an 11th confirmed reuse of the owned ShaperBox lane already tracked on [[Plugin Wishlist]] and [[Shaper]].
- 🎬 general lesson: reversing a plain one-shot and hand-trimming the start/length is a cheap, no-extra-plugin way to generate rhythmic pulse before reaching for any dedicated modulation device. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

**Texture layers under the pad:**
- **Perc loop:** a percussion loop pulled from XO ("this perc loop from x y" — a near-certain auto-caption garble of the owned [[XO]] drum-sample browser/loop source), dropped in unprocessed because it already sounded right against the pad.
- **Rain noise:** a rain/ambience one-shot, again from the producer's own one-shot kit, layered in purely for texture/atmosphere. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]

**Vocal chop:**
- **Source:** a vocal chop found on Splice, hand-chopped down to just the parts wanted.
- **Processing chain (in order):** bandpass EQ (cuts both high and low end) → a **half-time rhythmic effect on its "quarter" preset** (auto-captioned "half time on the quarter preset," exact plugin unconfirmed) — **automated to drop out partially at certain points** for movement across the arrangement → a medium-sized reverb → a **heavy [[ValhallaDelay]]**, used specifically to fill out space since the synth pad and this vocal chop are the ONLY two melodic elements in the entire beat.
- **Automation as arrangement:** the half-time effect is fully engaged for the first four bars (used as a de-energized pre-hook section), then partially disengaged for the hook, audibly adding energy the moment it lifts. → [[Automation]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

**Drums — layered hi-hats at different "feels":**
- **Snare:** a single snare sample, no elaborated processing mentioned.
- **Hi-hat 1:** an "old-school, more acoustic" sounding hat, deliberately mixing old/vintage character against the newer elements — "when you mix older new sounds it can sound really interesting."
- **Hi-hat 2:** shifted far off the timing grid (dragged noticeably late/early) specifically for a "natural, live-feeling" swing — a concrete example for [[Pocket and Microtiming]].
- **Hi-hat 3:** a more modern hat with rolls, layered against the two above for a boom-bap-meets-modern hybrid feel (the Tory Lanez reference's own bounce is described the same way).
- **Kick:** velocities varied per hit + several hits panned, purely to add bounce/movement rather than keeping every kick identical in level and position. → [[Kick Design]]
- **808:** a long, very subby 808 sustaining under the pattern.
- 🎬 general lesson: three differently-processed/timed hi-hat layers (vintage-toned, off-grid-swung, modern-with-rolls) stacked together is a repeatable way to fake a "hybrid era" drum feel without programming three full patterns. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]]

**Hook-strengthening pass (added after the base beat was built):**
- A **flute** one-shot (from the producer's one-shot kit), run through a **massive reverb** and a **really big delay**, added specifically because "the hook just needs something else."
- A **counter-melody**, added on top for the same reason.

**Philosophy stated directly in the video:** you don't need to keep adding elements if the chord progression is catchy, the sound selection is strong, and the drums have a good bounce — with only two melodic sources (synth pad + vocal chop) in the entire beat, the artist can still hear whether they want to jump on it. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

**Arrangement/structure (selective mute, not new material per section):**
1. **4-bar intro:** just the synth pad + texture layers (perc loop, rain), no drums.
2. **Pre-hook:** full drums enter; vocal chop's half-time effect is kept fully **on** (lower energy, sets up the hook).
3. **Hook:** half-time effect **lifted** on the vocals (brighter, more energy).
4. **Verse:** vocals removed entirely — muting the vocal chop is used as the explicit signal to the artist "this is the verse, write here."
5. **Second pre-hook variation:** hi-hats and snare pulled out, kick + 808 kept — a lighter version of the same section for contrast on repeat.
- 🎬 general lesson: use selective muting (drums in/out, one FX toggle on/off) rather than new musical material to differentiate intro/pre-hook/hook/verse — the whole song used only two melodic elements. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

# How to Supercharge Your Tracks with Filter Pedals

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umFdDKUv1X0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umFdDKUv1X0)

## Synopsis

A full compositional demonstration built entirely around the idea of pairing one synth with one filter pedal as a "new instrument" — walking through chord, melody, bass, and countermelody parts of a piece, each built from a different synth+pedal pairing, closing with a philosophy of using self-imposed limitations (one pedal per synth, an entire piece in minor 7th chords) as a compositional tool rather than a constraint.

## Techniques

- **Pair a synth with a filter pedal and treat the combination as a distinct "instrument"** — deliberately limiting a part to one synth + one pedal (or a plugin + a pedal, no hardware required) produces different musical results than calling up a preset and turning a filter knob; the constraint itself becomes a source of inspiration.
- **OB-X press-and-hold poly-chord voicing**: build a chord from a bottom triad plus separate color notes layered above (e.g. a minor 11th built as a C-G-E♭ triad + 5th/m7/9th/11th color tones), so one key triggers the full pre-designed voicing — freeing both hands for expressive filter/pedal control instead of holding a chord shape.
- **Split a mono source hard left/right, dry on one side and through a resonant filter on the other**, for phantom-panning movement that comes free from the frequency-dependent phase/volume differences between the two paths — no pan automation needed, and it can reduce how much reverb is needed for a sense of depth.
- **A clock-derived envelope patched into a filter's cutoff CV** gives rhythmic filter modulation that's adjustable live (double/triple/quadruple time) without touching DAW automation or an LFO sync setting — same trick reused to drive an arpeggiator's rate directly, changing sweep range and rhythmic density live mid-performance.
- **Run a filter pedal in band-pass mode on a synth that already has its own filter**, specifically for a two-filter (MS-20-style) character the synth's single low-pass/high-pass section can't produce alone — resonant interest in the middle of the range rather than only top or bottom.
- **Voicing discipline**: plain triads/roots in the low register for a strong foundation, 7th/9th/11th color extensions reserved for the upper register only.
- **A dual-voice source (a clavinet with a built-in secondary Rhodes-like piano voice) split in stereo through a stereo filter**, each side independently processed, for an orchestral-style pizzicato/celeste ostinato.
- **Deliberate "dirt" as organic character**: a small amount of distortion/anomaly on a synth part, drawn from the observation that close-mic'd acoustic instruments reveal natural imperfections that synths otherwise lack — a little grit reads as more alive/organic, not as a flaw.
- **Compositional constraints as a creative tool**: writing an entire piece using only minor 7th chords, and committing to one pedal per synth throughout, as deliberate self-imposed limitations that shape (rather than restrict) the outcome — sketch first, then let individual sounds expand into more bars if they need more time to express themselves.

# JAZ-C22 — Quartal voicings state a mode without naming a chord

**Q.** What is the *So What* voicing, and why fourths rather than thirds?

> [!question]- Answer
> Three perfect fourths with a major third on top — from the bottom, **E A D G B**.
>
> A stack of thirds names a chord, and naming a chord implies a function — which is exactly what modal music is trying to avoid. **Fourths sound open and unassigned**, so they state the mode without committing to a chord. That is where the hollow, floating sound comes from.

## Why it matters

It transfers straight into production: quartal voicings sit beautifully under a vocal because they are sparse and have no strong 3rd fighting the topline. This is the same problem [[JAZ-C06 — Shell voicings]] solves, answered a different way — the shell keeps the 3rd and drops the 5th, the quartal voicing avoids the 3rd altogether.

Over sixteen bars of one chord it also removes the harmonic commitment that gets tiring, which is why it is the default answer to a long static section.

## Drill

Voice a static Dm7 vamp as a tertian chord and then as the *So What* stack, and A/B them under a melody. Then move the same quartal shape in parallel across a two-chord vamp and hear it hold the two chords together.

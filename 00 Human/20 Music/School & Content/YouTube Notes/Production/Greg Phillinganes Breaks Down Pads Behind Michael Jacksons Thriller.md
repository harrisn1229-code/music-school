# Greg Phillinganes Breaks Down Pads Behind Michael Jackson's Thriller

## Synopsis

A long, full-take walkthrough of every pad layer in Thriller (Prophet-5, Jupiter-8 doubling, piano, string-like sweeps, the intro's one-off sound) with Greg Phillinganes performing each part start to finish — the richest content is a real unison-mode voice-allocation gotcha discovered live during the session, plus arrangement philosophy.

## Techniques

- **Unison mode + limited voice count means connecting/sustaining between chords drops voices — every chord has to be re-articulated separately.** A synth's voice count splits across unison layers per note (e.g. 16 voices ÷ 4-note chord = 4 voices/note); holding a note over into the next chord via legato or sustain pedal doesn't carry that voice cleanly, silently thinning the sound. Fix: play without the sustain pedal, re-striking every chord distinctly. Chord density interacts with the same allocation — a 5-note chord (vs. the "sweet spot" 4) measurably weakens the sound on this patch by unbalancing per-note voice count.
- **Piano (percussive, decays naturally) and pad synths (sustained, orchestral) are deliberately layered for their DIFFERENT envelope characters, not redundancy** — the piano provides an attack "bloom" that disappears, while the Prophet/Jupiter pads hold underneath, doubling for stereo width and richness while each contributes a distinct envelope shape to the combined texture.
- **Selective silence as orchestration**: the piano is deliberately absent from the outro specifically to leave room for other pad layers (pipe organ) coming in — reconfirms the "container" arrangement principle from other videos in this series, demonstrated here at the performance level (a player choosing NOT to play a passage, not just a mix decision).
- **A "who/what/when/where/why" framework for arrangement/orchestration taste decisions**: who's the right performer for this part, when in the song does it enter, where does it sit stylistically relative to the genre/era, and why — what emotion is it actually serving. Offered as the underlying discipline behind "leave space" and "make it subliminal" advice given elsewhere.
- **A richer opening chord (C minor 9, not a plain triad) as a deliberate "exotic" touch heard exactly once** — the intro's very first chord adds the 9th (D♯) on top of the tonic C minor triad that otherwise governs the whole song, giving the opening moment a jazzier, more anticipatory color; that specific voicing/pad sound appears nowhere else in the track.

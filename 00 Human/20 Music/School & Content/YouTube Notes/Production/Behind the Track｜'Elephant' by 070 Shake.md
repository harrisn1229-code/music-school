# Behind the Track｜'Elephant' by 070 Shake

📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OKBT8-0l9AI)

## Synopsis

A short **Mix with the Masters** "Behind the Track" piece — 070 Shake and her producer/collaborator Dave talking through how "Elephant" came together. It's not a plugin-by-plugin breakdown; it's a process/philosophy piece, and everything in it is a workflow or mindset choice rather than a setting. Their default process: start from a bare-bones instrumental (a simple baseline + rudimentary drums) and let a freestyle vocal take spark the direction, then build the rest of the production around whichever vocal idea lands. On this song specifically, Shake describes the take as very in-the-moment — something she was actually going through, delivered in a talking rather than singing/rapping register because that read as more direct and assertive. Dave picked an "analog, dark, and grungy" overall synth palette to match the vocal's mood, centered on a vintage hardware synth he bought secondhand from Billy Corgan (Smashing Pumpkins) via a Reverb listing — Corgan doesn't know who bought it. On the vocal chain itself, the pair is deliberately minimal: almost no processing ("it's really not that much stuff going on, it's just really how she sounds"), autotune pulled back further than they ever have before to lean into a raw, natural tone, and — notably — **no vocal comping at all**: Shake records full continuous takes and redoes the entire take rather than punching in fixes, a philosophy they frame on camera with a clip from David Cronenberg's *Naked Lunch* about whether rewriting/editing betrays the honesty of the original expression. The most memorable production anecdote is a genuine accident: a chord that was supposed to stay major flipped to minor because of a wrong note, and they kept it because it "sounded perfect" — framed directly as their broader philosophy that the best moments in their process are the mistakes, not the planned choices. A smaller anecdote closes the video: Dave is the reason the song is on the project at all — Shake wanted to scrap it at one point and he pushed to keep it.

## Techniques

- **Vocal-first workflow.** Default process: start with a very simple baseline + rudimentary drums (deliberately minimal, just enough to react to) → the vocalist freestyles over it, either the whole song or part of one, often in a single pass → IF that vocal idea inspires them, build the rest of the production outward from it. The instrumental exists to give the vocal something to spark off, not the other way around. *(~0:20–0:45)*
- **"Nourish the part that speaks to us."** Whatever element in a song — a lyric, a melody, a piece of production — carries the real feeling gets protected and built around; everything else is in service of that one thing. Stated as a general creative principle, not song-specific. *(~1:35)*
- **In-the-moment, talking-register vocal delivery as a deliberate stylistic choice.** The "Elephant" vocal idea was something Shake was actually going through in real time; she liked that the delivery came out sounding like she was *talking to* someone rather than singing or rapping at them — more direct/assertive than a sung melody would read. *(~0:50–1:20)*
- **Near-zero vocal processing philosophy.** When asked what effects shape the lead vocal's sound, the direct answer is "it's really not that much stuff going on — it's just really how she sounds," attributed partly to the literal texture of her voice (smoking) and partly to a deliberate choice to lean further into that natural character than they have before. → [[1 Main Vocal Track]], [[Vocal Tuning]]
- **Autotune pulled back further than ever, as an ongoing direction, not a one-off.** "We really kicked back off the autotune more than we ever have in the past" — framed as something they want to keep pushing further (more raw vocal, less correction) in future work, paired with the no-comping philosophy below. *(no specific Retune/plugin settings given — qualitative only)* → [[Vocal Tuning]]
- **No vocal comping — full continuous takes, redo instead of punch.** Shake records an entire verse or an entire song in one uninterrupted pass rather than assembling a comp from multiple takes; if a line doesn't land, she'll redo the *whole* take rather than punch in a fix, explicitly to preserve "one expression and one flow" — likened to ordinary speech, where you can't go back and re-say a sentence differently mid-conversation. → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]]
- **The *Naked Lunch* framing for why they don't over-edit a take.** They illustrate the philosophy with a clip from David Cronenberg's *Naked Lunch* — Burroughs/Kerouac/Ginsberg-styled characters arguing over whether rewriting is "deceit," a betrayal of the original tumbling-out of thought, versus a moral duty to consider every angle. Used here specifically to frame vocal takes: reworking/comping a performance risks losing the honesty of the original delivery. → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]]
- **"Analog, dark, and grungy" synth coloring chosen to match the vocal's mood**, rather than a generic patch choice — the overall sonic direction on the instrumental side is picked in response to what the vocal is saying and how it feels. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A specific vintage hardware synth, bought secondhand from Billy Corgan (Smashing Pumpkins) via a Reverb listing, without Corgan knowing who the buyer was.** Named on camera as "a Code 8" — this is very likely a mis-hearing/garble in the auto-generated captions of a real vintage-synth product name (the actual unit is not confidently identifiable from the audio/transcript alone). - **Mistakes in the room treated as a primary creative source, not something to correct.** The song's most distinctive chord moment — a note that was supposed to keep a progression major instead flipped it to minor — happened by accident (a wrong note), and was kept because it "sounded perfect" even though it wasn't what the player was reaching for.

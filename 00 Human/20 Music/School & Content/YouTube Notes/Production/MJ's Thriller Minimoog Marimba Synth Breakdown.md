# MJ's Thriller Minimoog Marimba (Synth Breakdown)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XZroZKokkpI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XZroZKokkpI)

## Synopsis

A one-part-only synth patch from Thriller's outro — a two-oscillator plucked "marimba" sound whose main lesson is split evenly between synthesis (a dual-decay-envelope pluck) and performance (how the part was physically played changes its feel more than the patch does).

## Techniques

- **Two decay-only envelopes working independently, not one shared shape.** Filter envelope: no attack, no sustain, decay-only — controls how fast the brief bright "attack" transient closes down. VCA envelope: also decay-only, independently tuned to stop the note ringing rather than assuming the filter decay value also covers amplitude. Workflow order: dial in the filter decay's closing speed first, then bring the VCA decay down separately until the note stops ringing at the right length.
- **Two oscillators, mid pulse wave an octave below plus a skinny pulse wave an octave above at lower mixer volume** — filter closed at the base, opened only by that decay envelope's contour; resonance added specifically for a thinning, highpass-like "plunk" that clears low-mid space in an already-dense track (same resonance-as-mud-clearing logic as the growl bass patch) rather than just adding emphasis.
- **Keyboard tracking needed even on a short plucked patch** — without it, notes played higher up didn't "speak" clearly; tracking (or a compensating higher initial cutoff) restores consistent articulation across the keyboard.
- **A performance-technique demonstration, not just a settings one**: Greg Phillinganes deliberately played the part crossing one hand over the other, mimicking a mallet-percussion player's physical motion (steel drum/marimba technique) rather than standard keyboard fingering — described as giving "a different feel" and more "authentic" articulation than playing it "like a keyboard player." The same notes, played with different physical technique, read differently — a reminder that a synth performance inherits technique from the instrument it's imitating, not just from the patch.

# Songs that use the Mixolydian ♭6 scale

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GTRDQeiLNck)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GTRDQeiLNck)

## Synopsis

Most of this video's worked examples (Game of Thrones' Valar Morghulis theme, "Twilight" by ELO, "Single Ladies" by Beyoncé) are already documented on [[Scale Modes Reference]] from an earlier enrichment pass — not repeated in full here. Two things ARE genuinely new: a fast 2-chord vamp for establishing the scale's color, and a critique of its alternate name.

## Techniques

- **The "Mixolydian ♭6 vamp": I → iv (minor) establishes 6 of the scale's 7 notes in just two chords, including both of its defining "character notes."** In C: C major → F minor. The major I chord supplies the scale's major 3rd (the "bright" half of the sound); the MINOR iv chord (borrowed, not the diatonic major IV) supplies the ♭6 (the "dark" half) — together the fastest possible way to state the scale's signature bright-yet-dark quality without needing all 7 notes present. → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Modal Interchange]]
- **A new song example not previously logged**: "The Fragile" (Nine Inch Nails), in G Mixolydian ♭6.
- **The "Aeolian dominant" alternate name, and why it's a confusing label worth knowing but not preferring.** Same scale, reached from the opposite direction: take Aeolian (natural minor) and raise the 3rd to major so a dominant-7 chord can be built on the tonic ("dominant" in scale-naming means "yields a I7 chord"). The video's own take: calling a scale "Aeolian" while removing minor's single defining feature (the minor 3rd) is conceptually awkward, even though the name correctly signals the scale's core identity as "a minor scale with a major 3rd."

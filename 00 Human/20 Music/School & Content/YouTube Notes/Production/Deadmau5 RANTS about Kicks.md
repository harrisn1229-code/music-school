# Deadmau5 RANTS about Kicks

## Synopsis
A short reaction clip where deadmau5 tears into a viewer's track (and a piece of common EDM-forum advice) over kick design, then pivots into an unrelated aside on why Ozone's genre mastering presets clip. This is a DIFFERENT Sol State clip than "How Duda Changed Deadmau5 Kicks FOREVER" already documented at length in [[Kick Design]] — that video covered the room-reverberation origin story, the big-kick-OR-big-bass genre rule, and the psychoacoustic reverb-priming trick. This one covers different ground: deadmau5 specifically ranting against the "tune your kick to the bass" trope that circulates in EDM production circles, arguing a kick is a transient element and bass is a bass element, with a brief practical fix (shorten the decay, carve some low end) for a track that's reading as an undifferentiated 30 Hz wall on a club system — which echoes the same club-translation concern from the Duda video but for a different underlying reason (the kick's own low-end content overwhelming the mix, not room reverberation extending its perceived length). It closes with a short, kick-unrelated mastering aside about Ozone preset gain staging.

## Techniques
- **Kicks make kicks, bass makes bass — don't tune the kick to the bassline.** deadmau5 pushes back hard on the "tune your kick to the bass" EDM pro-tip: a kick is fundamentally a transient/punchy element, not a bass instrument — reach for a sub oscillator or a dedicated bass patch for low-end duty instead of trying to make the kick carry it. A kick CAN function as the track's fundamental low frequency that the arrangement "spins around," but that "very very seldomly works" because it forces writing the whole track's melody/harmony around that one fixed root note — a constraint most tracks can't accept. Practical fix for a kick swallowing a mix (the reviewed track read as pure 30 Hz mud with nothing else audible on a real club system): shorten the decay to roughly an eighth note and optionally carve some of the low end down, then handle the actual bass separately with a low synth plus a small EQ lift around 30–80 Hz. This sits in direct tension with Disclosure's "always tune the kick to the key" habit already documented in [[Kick Design]] — a real philosophical split between artists on how much low-end/pitch duty a kick should carry. → [[Kick Design]], [[Bass Design]] — deadmau5 (Sol State), [Deadmau5 RANTS about Kicks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Bzu8xNp5X4)
- **Ozone's genre mastering presets are too hot on input gain, not output gain — pull the INPUT down, leave the output alone.** deadmau5 found Ozone's stock genre-specific mastering presets needed roughly 8 dB of headroom (6 dB "was still too much") to sound right and avoid instantly clipping the moment a preset loads — the fix every time was pulling the preset's INPUT slider down an extra 2–4 dB before the signal even reaches the chain, never touching the final OUTPUT slider (that one just pins the ceiling and should stay put). His read: iZotope's presets ship gain-staged for a hotter incoming mix than most people's masters actually run, so the preset clips until you compensate on the way in. → [[Ozone 9 Maximizer]], [[Gain Staging]] — deadmau5 (Sol State), [Deadmau5 RANTS about Kicks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Bzu8xNp5X4)

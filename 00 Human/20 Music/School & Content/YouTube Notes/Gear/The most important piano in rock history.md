# The most important piano in rock history

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tesy80wwR0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tesy80wwR0)

## Synopsis

Gear history, not theory: the story of a single handmade C. Bechstein grand piano that lived at Trident Studios (London) from 1968 until the studio's 1981 sale, and appears on a remarkable run of classic-rock-era recordings. Case study in why a century-old, individually hand-built instrument can sound meaningfully different from even another unit of the exact same make/model — the opposite of a mass-produced modern instrument.

## Techniques

- **What made it distinctive**: reportedly fitted with harder-than-usual hammers (bright, crisp tone when struck) and a notably stiff/heavy key action (had to be played harder, which also pushed the resulting tone louder) — combining for a bright, present tone specifically good at cutting through a dense rock mix. Demonstrated on "Life on Mars?" (Bowie) — the piano cuts through clearly even when mixed well back from the front.
- **Recordings it's confirmed on**: The Beatles (1968) — "Hey Jude," "Honey Pie," "Martha My Dear." Elton John — self-titled debut album incl. "Your Song" (1970); *Madman Across the Water* incl. "Tiny Dancer" (1972). David Bowie — *Hunky Dory*, *The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust*, *Aladdin Sane* (session pianist reportedly Rick Wakeman on at least *Hunky Dory*). T. Rex — "Get It On" (1972, also Rick Wakeman). Queen — first two albums + part of the third, most notably "Seven Seas of Rhye" — explicitly NOT "Bohemian Rhapsody" despite a commonly repeated claim, since Queen had stopped recording at Trident by 1975. Harry Nilsson — "Without You" (1971). Lou Reed — *Transformer* incl. "Perfect Day" (1972). Carly Simon — "You're So Vain" (1972). The Boomtown Rats — "I Don't Like Mondays" (1979). Also named more loosely: Manfred Mann, Genesis, Supertramp, Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds.
- **General instrument-character lesson**: a mass-produced modern instrument (the video's own example: a Nord Piano 3) sounds essentially identical unit-to-unit; a century-old handmade piano, by contrast, develops genuinely one-of-a-kind character from its specific construction and maintenance history — part of why a "the studio's own piano" story like this one becomes a lasting part of a recording's identity.
- **What happened to it**: Trident Studios sold in 1981; the piano's post-studio history is murky and only sourced to unverified secondhand studio-worker accounts — reportedly damaged when a crane cable snapped during the move out, possibly repaired and resold, possibly restrung at some point and "never sounded the same" afterward. Reportedly sold at auction in 2008 for £200,000; current location/owner unknown.

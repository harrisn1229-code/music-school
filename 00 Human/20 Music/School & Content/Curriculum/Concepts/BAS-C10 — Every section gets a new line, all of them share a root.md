# BAS-C10 — Every section gets a new line, all of them share a root

**Q.** Writing bass across a whole song: what changes section to section, and what stays the same?

> [!question]- Answer
> **A genuinely new rhythm, register and contour for each section — while every line keeps returning to the same root as home.** Rising section, peak, B section, wind-down: four different lines, matched to each section's energy rather than the same pattern played louder or busier. The shared tonal center is what keeps all four reading as one song instead of four sketches.

## Why it matters

It's a different scale of decision from the groove-level moves. Syncopating off the kick makes one bar funky; this is what stops a four-minute record sounding like one loop with the faders moved. It also gives you a concrete test for a section that isn't working: if the line is only louder or busier than the last one, it hasn't changed, and if it has wandered off the root, the song has come apart.

## Drill

Name the four sections of a track you're on and say what rhythm, register and contour the bass takes in each. Then check they land on the same root.

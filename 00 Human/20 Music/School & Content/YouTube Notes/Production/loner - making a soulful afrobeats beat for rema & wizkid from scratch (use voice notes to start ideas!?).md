# making a soulful afrobeats beat for rema & wizkid from scratch (use voice notes to start ideas!?)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e-KvnLXHAy4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e-KvnLXHAy4)

## Synopsis

loner builds a soulful afrobeats instrumental from scratch, aimed at the R&B-inflected sound of Rema's and Wizkid's recent albums, starting from a chord progression that was hummed/played into a phone voice memo before ever touching the DAW — the whole video is effectively a case study in "capture an idea the second it happens, refine it later." From there the build moves outward in layers: electric piano chords (processed with a tape stage, a subtle reverb, a delay, and a low-cut EQ) → a simple electric-guitar counter-melody (amp-simmed, compressed, cassette-crunched, then consolidated, reversed, chopped, and filtered into a separate ambient texture layer that "almost sounds like a vocal") → a Splice-sourced melodic vocal chop (pitched down, edited for space, drowned in filter/reverb/mono/pan) → an ethnic-winds flute layer (articulation choice used as a variation tool, not just melody) → a Kontakt Rickenbacker-style bass (following the chord roots plus added licks, pitched down after tracking too high) → a soft Analog Lab pad with a rhythmic shaper pulse → an afrobeat-specific Splice drum kit with a layered fill and extra percussion. Most valuable here: the voice-memo-to-DAW workflow itself, the reverse-and-filter trick that turns a fully-processed guitar part into a completely different ambient texture layer, the vocal-chop editing-for-space technique (muting/trimming a Splice chop down to just the usable phrase), and the reminder that a good drum fill — not just a good kit — is what actually elevates an afrobeats groove.

## Techniques

- **Capture the chord progression the moment it happens, on whatever's at hand — a phone voice memo, not the DAW.** The whole beat's chord progression came from noodling on a keyboard away from the studio; it was voice-memo'd on the phone specifically "so I could remember it," then played back and rebuilt properly once back at the DAW. *(~0:10)*
- **Arturia Wurli V ("Worley V2"/"Wurly V2" as auto-captioned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]], third confirmed use on this vault), "Plain Roads" preset as the default go-to electric-piano sound.** Explicitly named as "my go-to preset" for chords with an R&B/soulful character — a big part of the Rema/Wizkid sound identity. Chords played in freely first (matching the voice-memo idea), then quantized. Owned equivalent: [[Keyscape]] Wurlitzer 140B/200A patches on [[1 Electric Piano Track|Electric Piano Track]]. *(~0:20)*
- **Tape stage (J37) on the chord bed — owned equivalent [[Kramer Tape]], best-match per the existing vault pattern.** First processing move on the fresh chords: a tape saturation stage "I like doing this a lot... it's just super clean." *(~0:35)*
- **"Infinity" reverb, deliberately turned way down for subtlety — second confirmed sighting of this named reverb/preset (see [[Plugin Wishlist]]).** Added right after the tape stage; size, decay, AND wet all pulled down together "I just want to make it super subtle" — a restrained, barely-there reverb rather than the long/always-on version of the same-named tool logged elsewhere on this vault. *(~0:38)*
- **Dotted delay preset on the chords, with wow/flutter-style character turned down for a cleaner bounce.** Tries the dotted-time preset on the delay first ("let's try the dotted presets, see what that sounds like"), then dials down what the auto-captions render as "the wire and the flutter" (near-certain garble of wow/flutter-style tape-delay modulation) — keeps the delay's rhythmic bounce without the tape-warble character. Kept the first preset tried rather than auditioning further: "that actually sounds really good as it is." Owned best-match: [[Delay]] (Ableton) for the dotted-time move; [[EchoBoy]] for the wow/flutter-style tape character if that specific modulation is wanted. *(~0:45)*
- **EQ cut on the low end of the chord bed, as the final chords-processing move** before moving to the next instrument — owned: [[EQ Eight]]. *(~0:55)*
- **A simple electric-guitar counter-melody, deliberately kept as "just a very simple counter" rather than a competing lead** — several takes recorded before landing on a phrase that "kind of" hit the target feel, then chopped/time-aligned to the chord grid. *(~1:05)*
- **AmpliTube amp sim, browsed by CHARACTER TAG rather than by amp model** — the plugin's preset browser lets you filter by character (clean, dirty, etc.) and instrument type (electric guitar); picked "clean" character specifically to suit this vibe before auditioning individual presets within that filtered set. No owned amp-sim equivalent confirmed — see [[Plugin Wishlist]] (multiple prior AmpliTube sightings already logged); owned best-match lane: [[Morgan Amps Suite]] (clean/boutique voicings) or [[Archetype- John Mayer X]] (owned, not yet installed) on [[1 Electric Guitar DI Track|Electric Guitar DI Track]]. *(~1:20)*
- **Valve compressor to tighten up an uneven guitar take** — "there's some notes that are hanging way louder than others" — a straightforward level-evening pass, owned best-match [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII]] (opto/tube compressor). *(~1:35)*
- **Sketch Cassette added for extra crunch and vintage dirt on the guitar** — owned equivalent already tracked and used repeatedly: [[Cassette]] (Wavesfactory). *(~1:40)*
- **Switch the amp preset a SECOND time, after the compressor/cassette stages, specifically to push more presence** — a reminder that amp-sim character choice isn't locked to the first pick; revisit it once the rest of the chain reveals what the part actually needs. *(~1:45)*
- **Consolidate (print/bounce) the fully-processed guitar part to audio, THEN reverse it and chop it up on a brand-new track — turning one finished part into a second, completely different texture layer rather than reusing the same audio.** The reversed/chopped chunks get treated as a fresh sound source, not a variation of the original guitar track. *(~1:50)*
- **Filter the reversed guitar chunks super ambient: a low+high cut (bandpass-style EQ) plus a heavy vintage-verb pass (mix pushed heavy, decay turned down, size pulled a little smaller than default), then a half-time device.** The combined result "almost sounds like a vocal with all the reverb and stuff" — a fully instrumental, heavily-processed guitar layer reading as a vocal-adjacent texture. Owned: [[EQ Eight]] (filter), [[Reverb]] (vintage verb — closest owned analog for "vintage verb," settings as described), [[Automation]] (half-time). → [[1 Electric Guitar DI Track|Electric Guitar DI Track]] "Ambient/washy" variation. *(~2:05)*
- **Splice search for finishing elements — chops, brass sounds, a vocal chop — AFTER the core instrumental layers are built, not before.** Sound selection for the "extra ear candy" layers is deliberately done later in the build, once the harmonic/rhythmic skeleton (chords, guitar) already exists to judge candidates against. *(~2:30)*
- **A melodic Splice vocal chop — time-stretched and pitched into key first, then edited down to just the usable phrase by ear.** After finding a vocal chop that was "pretty melodic" and suited the vibe, it's time-stretched + pitched to the song key, auditioned as-is, then pulled down an octave; a specific short gap/section inside the phrase is identified as the good part and everything else is muted/trimmed away — deliberate excerpting rather than using the whole found phrase, to make the part read "spaced out." → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]. *(~2:45)*
- **Filter the vocal chop super ambient, heavy reverb to "drown it out," forced to mono, then panned right — with the earlier reversed-guitar texture layered alongside it in the same stereo position for the whole loop.** A deliberate "far away," non-lead placement for a chopped vocal — the mono+pan move (rather than leaving it centered/stereo) is what pushes it out of the foreground, with the guitar texture doubling up in the same spot for thickness. → [[Depth and Space]], [[Reverb]] (effect), [[EQ Eight]]. *(~3:05)*
- **Ventus Ethnic Winds flute chops, using PITCH/OCTAVE placement as an articulation-variation tool, not just a melodic choice.** This Kontakt-hosted ethnic-winds library changes its articulation character depending on which note is played — different notes within the same octave (e.g. D♯3 vs. others in the C3 range) trigger audibly different flute articulations, so the same phrase read at a different pitch/octave sounds like a different performance rather than just a different note. Used more as a textural/rhythmic bounce element than a lead melody. Unowned — no ethnic-winds/world-flute library currently tracked; see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Woodwinds). *(~3:25)*
- **Flute chain: Cassette "Crushed" preset → plate reverb → clean chorus.** Owned equivalents throughout: [[Cassette]] ("Crushed" preset — matches the "you guys know how much I love this one" callout, a repeat favorite on this vault), [[UAD Pure Plate Reverb]] (plate character, "something super clean"), [[Chorus-Ensemble]] (clean chorus stage). *(~3:40)*
- **Bass: Kontakt Scarbee Rickenbacker ("scary rickenbacker" as auto-captioned — see [[Kontakt 7]], already the owned pick for this exact library elsewhere on this vault), paired with an AmpliTube amp preset layered on top.** Written by literally following the chord progression's root notes, plus additional licks/melodic fills layered in beyond the plain root line. The tracked part read too high in register, so the WHOLE sample was pitch-shifted down afterward rather than re-recorded/re-triggered at a lower octave. → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Bass). *(~4:15)*
- **Analog Lab soft pad ("Layers" bank) as a simple sustained bed under everything, finished with a rhythmic shaper stage.** A deliberately "very soft, simple" pad patch (not a featured element), then a shaper device added specifically so it "pulses a little bit" instead of sitting static — owned best-match: [[Shaper]] (the vault's established owned-lane stand-in for ShaperBox-style pulsing, already logged repeatedly — see [[Plugin Wishlist]] item 4). → [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Synths/Keys). *(~4:55)*
- **Half-time the flute layer late in the build because it read "a little bit too present"** — a mix-balance correction made by ear after hearing the full arrangement, not a decision baked in when the flute was first written. → [[Automation]]. *(~5:05)*
- **Splice "Afrodancer" Afro drum kit as the genre-specific drum foundation** — reached for a purpose-built afrobeat kit rather than a generic trap/hip-hop kit, naming genre-match as the deciding factor (consistent with the "match the kit to the genre" pattern already logged elsewhere on this vault). → [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Drums). *(~5:15)*
- **A drum loop layered in on top of the programmed kit specifically to push the groove further** — "that bounce is really nice" — a loop used as a groove-reinforcement layer alongside programmed hits, not a replacement for them.
- **A strong, hard-to-replicate drum FILL called out as a genuine level-up for afrobeats specifically** — "if you can find good drum fills and stuff, it's going to help take your afrobeats to a different level" — explicit advice that fill quality, not just kit/loop quality, is a differentiator for the genre. → [[Rhythm and Groove]].
- **A layered rim shot for percussion texture, kept even though it wasn't cutting through the mix** — added for the textural contribution rather than audible presence; a "it's there for feel, not for being heard" percussion layering call. → [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Drums).

# Sidechaining

**What it is:** any processor whose *detector* listens to a different signal than the one it processes. The classic use: a compressor on the bass keyed from the kick — every kick hit ducks the bass, so both feel loud in the same octave.

## Why it exists
Kick and bass occupy the same frequencies. Played together they mask each other, eat headroom, and mud the low end. Ducking one when the other hits makes space *in time* instead of in frequency — and the pumping itself became a compositional device (modern dance basslines are written *into* the duck).

## The three flavors
1. **Compressor sidechain** ([[Pro-C 2]] external SC, [[Glue Compressor]], [[Duck_Buddy]]): reactive — louder trigger = more duck. Organic, but limited curve control and can click on subby material.
2. **Volume-shaper LFO** ([[Kickstart 2]]): draws the duck curve directly; consistent every hit, can reach silence, anti-click smoothing built in. Trigger it from the kick (audio/MIDI) when patterns vary. Curve *position* is the groove: early ramp-up = urgent, late = lazy. Mix knob = duck depth — back it off until the pump stops being audible.
3. **Frequency-domain sidechain** ([[Soothe2]] external SC, [[Pro-Q 4]] dynamic node keyed externally): ducks only the *colliding frequencies*, not the level — invisible unmasking. The modern default for vocal-vs-instruments (see [[Masking and Carving]]).

## Recipes
- **Kick vs 808:** [[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]] stage 4. Trap counter-school (Mixed by Ali): don't duck at all — EQ pockets + gate the kick tail; sidechain "pulls the punch out."
- **Stealth duck:** multiband — duck only <150 Hz of the bass. Full mix benefit, zero audible pump.
- **Ghost trigger:** a muted click track feeds the compressor SC — duck patterns independent of the actual kick (works through kickless breakdowns).
- **Vocal priority ducks:** backups −1–2 dB under the lead ([[2 Backup Vocals Group|Backup Vocals Group]]), FX bus under the vocal ([[3 FX Bus|FX Bus]]), delay/reverb tails under the dry vocal ([[Delay Send Other]], [[Reverb Send Long]]), pads under everything ([[1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]]).
- **Click fixes:** raise the synth's own amp release slightly, use anti-click smoothing, or soften the curve corner.

**Rule of thumb:** duck the *lower-priority* element, keyed from the higher-priority one — see the masking hierarchy in [[FX Chain System]].

- **"Bow tying" — a whole-signal amplitude sidechain (LFO Tool-style) on a bass/sub carves a pinched, bow-tie-shaped envelope into the low end: the sub gets ducked out right at the kick hit, exactly when it's needed.** Fix: sidechain only the top end of the bass (just above the sub), not the whole signal — you rarely hear sub, you feel it, so ducking it costs low-end energy for zero perceived-pump benefit. This is the inverse of the sub-only multiband duck documented above from Disclosure (duck only the sub, protect the top) — here the strategy flips: duck the top, protect the sub. Defaults to multiband sidechaining on the kick as a habit. → [[LFO Tool]], [[Multiband Compression]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [New Sidechain Trick (Bow Tie, Pump & Subs)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJwF61mjWuQ)
- **Advanced alternative to sidechaining entirely: bus the kick and sub together and clip them, forcing their transients to lock together.** Route both to a shared group and drive a clipper across it — clipping forces them to share headroom and align tightly, getting closer to a "perfect" kick/sub relationship than sidechain compression alone; clean up the resulting kick distortion with a fast, soft-knee multiband move sidechained to the kick's own low end (only active while the kick and sub overlap), then blend dry kick back in to restore its level. Use an oscilloscope to see exactly which harmonics got clipped away and re-EQ them back into the dry kick — a supplement to your ears, not a substitute. → [[ClipGain]], [[jo.Oscilloscope]], [[Clipping and Loudness]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [New Sidechain Trick (Bow Tie, Pump & Subs)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJwF61mjWuQ)
- **Low end is binary, high end is to taste — a philosophy for splitting the multiband sidechain.** Below the low-end crossover, duck essentially all-or-nothing (fully ducked out or full volume) so only one element ever occupies that register at a time — mute everything else on the low band and the kick alone fills the gap on its own; above the crossover, dial duck depth to taste since more of the mix can coexist there without masking. "You don't really want layers in your low end, you want just one thing." → [[Multiband Compression]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Advanced Sidechain for MORE BASS (Rare Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkTUVnJKsB0)
- **A spectrum of high-end pump styles, not just one setting.** On the top end: a genuinely subtle duck-plus-tiny-pump that clears space exactly where needed and leaves a little room for the hi-hat transient on the next eighth, a more aggressive and audibly pumped version, and the fully obvious "Daft Punk"-style pump that a lot of producers still reach for — not a personal favorite, but it can work for the right aesthetic/genre. → [[Kickstart 2]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Advanced Sidechain for MORE BASS (Rare Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkTUVnJKsB0)
- **Give the snare its own, lighter low-end sidechain than the kick's.** A snare is shorter and pitched higher than a kick, so it doesn't need nearly as much low end taken out — run a separate, gentler duck on the snare's low end rather than reusing the kick's aggressive binary settings. — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Advanced Sidechain for MORE BASS (Rare Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkTUVnJKsB0)
- **"Adaptive sidechaining" — at mixdown/mastering, consolidate every instrument's low-end duck onto one shared trigger instead of a bespoke sidechain per instrument.** Once flipping the master, you don't want to be managing a different low-end sidechain per element — keep top-end ducks separate/per-instrument (subtle and to-taste anyway), but swap all low-end ducking onto one shared low-end trigger. The payoff isn't just simpler routing — it recovers low-end sustain: swapping the snare off its own dedicated low-end duck and onto the shared trigger let several more oscillations of low end survive under the snare's tail instead of getting cut off. → [[Multiband Compression]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Advanced Sidechain for MORE BASS (Rare Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkTUVnJKsB0)

## From videos

- **"We sidechain almost every sub" — sidechaining a sustained sub-bass note against the kick even in a straight four-on-the-floor track, purely for felt-not-heard movement.** Myles Smith / Peter Fenn, on *Stargazing*: a sustained quarter-note [[Serum 2]] sub-bass patch is pulsed via [[Kickstart 2]] against the kick specifically so a static-sounding sub gains physical, danceable movement — described as something you feel more than consciously hear, and a stated default across nearly all their tracks, not a special case. → [[Serum 2]], [[Kickstart 2]] — Myles Smith / Peter Fenn (Tape Notes), [[TN 184 Myles Smith & Peter Fenn - Full Video Episode]]
- **How to duck ANY reverb or delay plugin (not just ones with built-in ducking) with a sidechain compressor on the return.** Built-in ducking (some reverb/delay plugins include it) attenuates the start of the effect's tail so a vocal's transient punches through first — but relying on it locks a chain to whichever plugins happen to have the feature. Universal build: put the temporal effect on an aux/return track, dial in settings, then insert a COMPRESSOR on that same return and sidechain it to the DRY vocal track — every time the dry vocal hits, the compressor attenuates the wet return for the vocal's attack and decay, then the effect blooms back in right after. Owned build already runs exactly this shape on [[Reverb Send Long]] and [[Delay Send Other]] (Pro-C 2, sidechained from the lead); this generalizes it as a technique usable on ANY send, including ones without a dedicated sidechain stage yet — e.g. [[Reverb Send Room]]. Pair with a post-effect EQ dip around the vocal's masking range (200–400 Hz — see [[Masking and Carving]]) on the return for the second half of the fix. → [[Reverb Send Room]], [[Reverb Send Long]], [[Delay Send Slap]], [[Delay Send Other]], [[Masking and Carving]] — Sage Audio, [The 5 MOST IMPORTANT Vocal Mixing Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rXk1lvT6AhU)
- **A fourth genre data point for skipping the kick/bass sidechain by choice: a reggaeton sine-wave sub bass, left unducked against the kick, with the mixer unable to recall a specific reason beyond "I felt that I didn't need it."** Extends the trap counter-school entry above (Mixed by Ali/Kenny — "sidechain as a band-aid") to reggaeton: a simple-arrangement philosophy that skips the duck rather than reaching for it by default, with heavy compression on the bass itself doing the definition/consistency work instead. → [[Bass Design]], [[4 Bass/1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]] — Mosty (Mix with the Masters), [[Mosty mixing a reggaeton track]]
- **Kick-triggered sidechain compression applied across three different roles in the same mix — a lead vocal, a bass on the drop, and a piano's mono-narrowing move — showing how many different jobs "duck it off the kick" can do beyond the classic bass/kick duel.** On a lead vocal: a C2-style compressor sidechained to the kick, kept in the mix even though its origin was unclear ("not sure if Mark had it... but it's on there now"). On bass, specifically for the drop section: **2.5 dB GR**, keyed off the kick — "the only thing I really did... getting hit with the side-chain on the kick... hitting it 2.5 dB." On piano: a light kick-sidechain used not for level ducking but for **stereo narrowing** — "hitting it with a little bit of side chain off the kick, making it a little more mono in certain frequencies" — a less common use of sidechain compression to pull width in rather than duck volume. → [[Compression]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Electric Bass DI Track]], [[1 Electric Piano Track]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]] — Mix with the Masters (Josh Gudwin), [[Mixing Dua Lipa's vocals with Josh Gudwin]]
- **A 32-band dynamic-EQ sidechain duck between two co-lead VOCALISTS (not the usual instrument-vs-vocal case), restricted to the Mid channel only so a printed reverb riding on the same bus doesn't get ducked along with it.** On a combined vocal Group carrying James Blake's lead plus backing vocals, a 32-band frequency-domain ducker ("Trackspacer") is sidechained to a SECOND singer's vocal ("Slowthai"), so James's vocal ducks in the **1–6 kHz** zone specifically at the moments the two sing together — "James's vocal will duck down in the area which allows Slowthai to cut through the most." Set to **M/S mode, Mid-channel only**: because the Group also carries a printed reverb tail from a wet vocal delivery, restricting the trigger to the dead-center Mid image ducks only the dry vocal, leaving the (wider) reverb alone. Without it: "you get a bit of like... mess in the middle" at overlap points (example given: the word "champagne"). Owned-equivalent build: [[Pro-Q 4]] Dynamic EQ bands spanning the collision range, each sidechained to the other vocalist, Stereo Placement set to Mid per band — see that plugin's note for the full mapping; [[Soothe2]]'s external-sidechain mode is a rougher broadband fallback. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Soothe2]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[James Blake Vocal Chain]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — Mix with the Masters, [[MSM Mixing James Blake's Vocals]]
- **A hybrid manual-duck + frequency-domain-catch-all recipe for the kick/bass duel, run on "pretty much every mix" regardless of genre.** Duplicate the kick track complete with every plugin already on it (the exact post-processing signal, not a dry copy, so the trigger timing is correct), bring the duplicate down to sit right under the bass, hand-draw a manual volume "dip" on the bass around each kick hit (not a live compressor reacting in real time), then layer [[Soothe2]] on top, triggered by the duplicated kick, to catch any transient/resonant energy the manual dip missed. Stated as genre-agnostic — "sidechain compression works for every genre out there" — with only the *amount* varying (EDM can push it into an obvious pump; most genres want it fully invisible). Net result: no audible bass-dip, no added voltage even once the bass is back in, and 4 dB of headroom recovered versus skipping the move. → [[Soothe2]], [[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters), [Mixing Summits with Jaycen Joshua](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L6xx7DWKzng)
- **Side-chain a newly-added horn stab under a looped chunk of the ORIGINAL vintage multitrack backing track, so the archival lead vocal (also pulled from the same old tape) still reads clearly on top.** Scoring the "Love & Mercy" bed-montage sequence from Brian Wilson's own master tapes (Wilson sent the composer his complete multitrack archive, including 68 versions of "Good Vibrations" alone), the "Till I Die" section (1970, 8-track) is built by looping a piece of the original backing track, adding a new horn hit, and sidechaining it — described only qualitatively on camera ("I think we were sidechaining," flagged as "a bit corny these days") — so a decades-old vocal take still sits above the new instrumental layer without a fight. *(general starting point, no numbers given)*: key a compressor on the new/added element off the vocal (or the looped backing track), moderate ratio, release matched to the vocal's phrasing, rather than ducking the vocal itself. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Atticus Ross (Mix with the Masters), [Behind the Track｜ 'Love & Mercy' – Brian Wilson Tribute](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jTwSMtPakok)
- **A stock one-bar ShaperBox sidechain preset, used as-is with no custom curve drawing, to duck a dark textural synth bass under a piano breakdown.** Fourth-plus tracked reuse of Cableguys ShaperBox on this channel (still unowned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) — this time the default "one bar" preset was judged good enough without any manual curve editing, a reminder that a factory sidechain shape can be the fast, correct call rather than always reaching for a custom-drawn curve. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Kickstart 2]], [[5 Inst & Synth/1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]] — loner, [making a billboard number 1 pop beat for post malone's next album from scratch!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WX_IB6K-xIo)
- **Side-chain a dedicated noise/texture layer to the main melodic sound for instant character, as an instrument-design pattern, not a mix-stage fix** — "I've got a noise layer, and I've side-chained it to the piano" — pairing every patch with a subsidiary noise section keyed off the main voice was generalized into a full plugin feature (Luma) precisely because it made any sound feel more textured/alive with no extra manual work. Reusable without owning the plugin: build a two-chain Instrument Rack (main voice + a noise/texture chain), put a Compressor on the noise chain with external sidechain keyed from the main voice, so the noise layer pulses in time with every note. → [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — loner, [I Created The 'Perfect' Plugin For Producers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kI2f9JNImGE)
- **A concrete worked recipe for flavor 3 (frequency-domain sidechain) above: dynamic EQ bands keyed off a vocal, to duck an instrumental's specific frequencies only while the vocal sings.** On the instrumental, build multiple bands centered on the exact frequencies that should dip whenever the vocal is present, set each band **Dynamic**, then point each band's **external sidechain** at the vocal — the band's threshold now measures the vocal's level instead of the instrumental's own, so only those targeted frequencies duck, and only while the vocal is actually singing. Taken further: assign **Stereo Placement per band** (one dynamic band ducking the mid image, another the side image) to combine the frequency-specific duck with a stereo-imaging decision in the same move. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[2 Inst-Synth Group]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Width and Stereo]] — Sage Audio, [EQ Masterclass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPBnL6mx9c0)
- **Six ways to resolve kick/808 clash, A/B-tested against each other on the same pair: do nothing (clips), manual volume automation on the 808, subtractive EQ on the 808's low end (sounds bad — kills its fundamental), phase alignment (cleanest technically, only works if the 808 never changes note), standard sidechain triggered off a click track (raise Release until the post-attack spike disappears), and multiband sidechain (a free M4L frequency splitter ducks only the low band, for when the bass sound has mids/highs worth keeping).** His actual go-to once all six were compared: nothing on the 808 at all except a stock Ableton Limiter on the print — the limiter alone absorbs the clash. Verdict: no universally correct method, it depends on the sound and the genre. → [[Kick Design]], [[Limiter]] — Oliver (Sol State), [Oliver ENDS The "Sidechain Your 808" Debate (6 Popular Techniques Compared)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ORENdwJV15Y)
- **Self-sidechain by filtering the compressor's own detector input, not an external track — the way to stop a kick-heavy master bus comp from being dominated by the kick.** Distinct from a normal sidechain: instead of keying the compressor from another instrument, enable its **sidechain EQ/filter on its own input signal** and highpass the detector (Max highpasses at **~70 Hz**) so the kick's dominant low-frequency energy stops reaching the detector while everything above it still does — the compressor still reacts to the kick's higher-frequency content and every other element, it just stops being single-handedly triggered by the kick's sub. Reusable anywhere a bus compressor's gain reduction is being hijacked by one low-frequency-heavy source it shouldn't be reacting to alone. → [[Master Chain]], [[Compression]] — Max Konyi, [Final Mix & Master | Studio One LoFi Tutorial | Part 9](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OKUCuyLBSg)
- **"Preader"/pre-fader external sidechain mode keeps the duck amount independent of the trigger's own fader level.** When routing a sidechain send, switching the destination compressor's input to **external + pre-fader** ("preader" in Studio One's terminology; the pre-fader-send equivalent in any DAW) means the duck strength stays exactly where it was dialed in even if the triggering track's own volume gets pulled down later during mixing — because the sidechain signal is tapped before that fader, not after it. Worth defaulting to for any sidechain send that's expected to survive later level rides on the trigger. → [[Master Chain]], [[2 Inst-Synth Group]] — Max Konyi, [Final Mix & Master | Studio One LoFi Tutorial | Part 9](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OKUCuyLBSg)
- **"Sidechain" isn't just compressors — the same trigger routing drives an Auto Filter or a Gate too.** Point Auto Filter's sidechain at the kick and use the Envelope amount to sweep the cutoff on every hit (negative = sweeps down for a "whoosh," positive = sweeps up); point a Gate's sidechain at the kick with Floor pushed to −∞ and a sustained synth is only audible during kick hits, chopping a held pad into the kick's rhythm. Any pad in a Drum Rack can be the sidechain source, not just the kick — the source dropdown lets you pick a specific pad (e.g. the hi-hat) with pre/post/post-mixer signal-point options. → [[Auto Filter]], [[Gate]] — You Suck at Producing, [How to Sidechain Everything in Ableton Live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gej-sIZDTVU)
- **Isolate the kick from the snare by filtering the sidechain INPUT, not the target** — when a shared drum track triggers the duck on both kick and snare hits, enable the compressor's sidechain EQ and pass only low frequencies through it — only the kick's low end then reaches the detector, so the snare stops triggering the compressor while the kick keeps working exactly as before. — You Suck at Producing, [Sidechain Compression](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zeR2vPvrjU4)
- **White-noise ghost-trigger sidechain on everything except the drums, kept short** — rather than keying off the actual kick audio, feed the sidechain input from a muted white-noise "ghost" track so the trigger timing is fully independent of the kick sample's own transient; apply that sidechain to a Compressor across everything that isn't drums (grouped), with Release kept short — just enough for the kick to poke through, not so much that the pumping itself becomes audible. Paired with a deliberately minimal element count (a drop with only drums, bass, and chords) so what few elements there are duck cleanly out of the kick's way. → [[Compressor]] — You Suck at Producing, [How to get INSANELY loud mixes (ASMR Tutorial)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lV_lpR2Wgv8)
- **A dedicated fast "ghost" MIDI transient as the sidechain source, not the kick sample itself, when the duck needs to be tighter than the kick allows** — a kick's own transient/sample length sets a hard floor on how tight the ducking can get (a ~128 ms kick means the duck is *at least* 128 ms plus whatever release is dialed in, no matter how the compressor's Release knob is set); instead, create a new MIDI track with a very short, fast transient sound (a glitch pluck) programmed to hit everywhere the kick hits, route the target's sidechain input to that track instead of the kick, and now Release alone fully controls the duck length. → [[Kick Design]] — You Suck at Producing, [Making Kicks Hit Hard + Getting Paid with DistroKid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BKyhiAniCxs)
- **Sidechain a melodic part to the kick for movement-based warmth, not just tone-based warmth** — a compressor on a keys/pad track, sidechained from the kick drum, makes that part duck slightly on every kick hit; the part audibly "ducks out of the way" of the kick, and that rhythmic interaction is framed as adding warmth/glue on top of separate EQ/saturation moves — sidechain as a "make it feel like one thing" tool, not only a masking fix. → [[BED Slot]] — You Suck at Producing, [Warmth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yv3v9SsoDMw)
- **Group everything except the kick and sidechain the whole group to the kick for a beat-wide "swell," not just one element.** Rather than sidechaining a single melodic part, bus every non-kick element (percussion, samples, synths, everything but the FX) into one Group and put a heavy sidechain on the Group keyed from the kick, so the whole mix breathes with the kick hit — framed explicitly as the source of a beat's "vibe," not a masking fix. A lighter version: sidechain just the hats/perc to the kick so it stays present without burying the low end. → [[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]] — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [No More BORING Drums](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAUfaggMYzM)
- **Group-wide sidechain: fold everything except drums into one Group track, then key a single Compressor on that Group off the kick** — rather than sidechaining bass/synths/vocal-chops individually, select every non-drum track, group them (Cmd/Ctrl+G), add a [[Compressor]] to the Group with External input keyed from the kick, so the whole non-drum submix ducks together as one on every kick hit. → [[Compressor]], [[BASS Slot]] — You Suck at Producing, [The Most Slept On Ableton Synth for UK Dubstep](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vLsEQAxir1o)
- **Any source can trigger any target — and manual volume automation is a valid alternative to a live sidechain plugin** — sidechaining isn't limited to kick-into-bass; a vocal ducking a background pad works the same way. - **The DELAYED sidechain trigger** — UAD/DSP plugins and latent processors ([[Soothe2]], [[Oxford Inflator]]) make a straight sidechain land out of sync; Guy keeps three lanes — kick / straight sidechain / **delay-compensated sidechain** — and feeds latent chains from the delayed one. If the pump feels late, fix the trigger, not the compressor — Disclosure, [Prince Edit + Talk VIP stream](https://youtu.be/hOvLf9nX500) → [[Latency-Free Tracking]]
- **One MIDI-triggered sidechain bus that almost the whole project routes through, then sound-designed as its own element.** Nearly every element in a project (not just bass) feeds a single sidechain bus driven by a volume-shaper triggered by a dedicated MIDI signal rather than an audio detector; the bus itself then gets bitcrushed and band-filtered so mostly drums come through unaffected, turning the duck bus into a textured, rhythmic element rather than a transparent utility. → [[Kick Design]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Dedicated sidechain trigger track** — split the kick instrument to multi-output so a steady, inaudible trigger feeds the sidechain buses on its own channel; added syncopated kicks then don't stutter the pump ("I want it to keep going straight down the middle") — Disclosure, [Making Beats + Talk stream](https://youtu.be/1GhqAf9lc3A)
- **Fix feel with the pump, not the grid** — a messy shaker/late cowbell left off-grid on purpose: "the sidechain will bring it into rhythm" — Disclosure, [Remix Stems Stream](https://youtu.be/IOSPhXcXT_8)
- **Distortion buys masking forgiveness** — an overdriven bass clashing with the kick "doesn't matter because it's so dirty"; sidechain it anyway — Disclosure, [Remix Stems Stream](https://youtu.be/IOSPhXcXT_8)
- **Sidechain is a production technique, not a band-aid** — duck the 808 to the kick when you *like the sound/feel* it creates; reaching for it because "they can't both hit" papers over bad sample pairing, timing offset, or uncut masking. Fix those; sidechain by choice — Kenny Beats, [808 & Kick + Subtractive EQ](https://youtu.be/4iAKvWWlCvY)
General tips pulled from production breakdowns (via `/music-enrich`), newest-first.

- **Manual noise-into-compressor sidechain instead of a dedicated sidechain plugin** — rather than a built-in sidechain-ducker plugin, route a plain 4-on-the-floor noise/click trigger to a bus and key a regular compressor off it, hand-tuning attack/release on the compressor itself to shape the pump — more manual than a one-knob ducker, but full control over the compressor's actual gain-reduction curve rather than a canned duck shape — Disclosure, [First Twitch Stream Ever (Production Tutorial)](https://youtu.be/IObzp3OugtY)
- **Trigger sidechains from a *metronome beep*, not the kick** — since kick *length* is heavily automated (short vs long), keying off the kick makes the pump inconsistent; a separate constant beep on a muted track gives full, independent control over the pump depth. → [[Automation]] — Disclosure, [Douha (Mali Mali)](https://youtu.be/gCfU5Cq9WU4)
- **Beat UAD latency with delayed trigger copies** — UAD plugins run off the interface (~50 ms round-trip), so to sidechain a bus sitting behind several UAD plugins, duplicate the trigger beep and delay copies (sometimes 5) until the key lines up on the beat. — Disclosure, [Douha (Mali Mali)](https://youtu.be/gCfU5Cq9WU4)
- **Sub-only multiband duck is the recurring Disclosure bass move** — multiband-sidechain only ~100 Hz-and-down to the kick and leave the mids/highs unducked, so kick and sub never clash while the note keeps its life. (Stock-Logic caveat: needs an MB comp *with* a sidechain — Multipressor doesn't have one.) → [[Multiband Compression]], [[SUB Slot]] — Disclosure, [Birthday](https://youtu.be/b-6x_4XRwOQ) & [Never Enough](https://youtu.be/lGopQwQo9Ag)
- **Duck a synth off the *vocal* via an empty bus** — send the lead to a muted empty bus and key the chord-bus compressor off it so the vocal makes room for itself over a slamming bassline; **slow release** so it flows (a fast release = "radio compression, always full"). Trackspacer does the frequency-domain version. → [[BED Slot]] ducks for [[LEAD Slot]] — Disclosure, [Help Me Lose My Mind](https://youtu.be/FPgLRkgKoMU)
- **Double-sidechain a bass** — key *two* compressors off the kick: one on the **whole volume** (gentle, ~3–4 dB) and a second, aggressive one on **only the lows** ([[Pro-MB]], ~12 dB — up to ~70 dB on a subtle-kick track), so the sub ducks hard while the mid detail keeps pumping less. The philosophy: **"pick one sub"** — punchy kick *or* subby bass, sidechain the other's sub out; a clashing kick-sub + bass-sub is "wasted energy you can't perceive." The sidechain also **glues** (drums pull the bass, squash the pads — "everything holding hands"). → [[BASS Slot]] / [[SUB Slot]] — Disclosure, [Deeper (with Leon Thomas)](https://youtu.be/SM0FZ58ckVw) & [Blue World](https://youtu.be/lFTEptvdBg8)
- **Sidechain instead of automating levels** — key a synth-bus comp off the *vocal* so synths dip ~2 dB when it sings; fills gaps, stays thick and consistent — Disclosure, [Looking For Love](https://youtu.be/NlJt0pCptGk)
- **Frequency-conscious "make space"** — feed [[Soothe2]]/[[Pro-MB]]'s sidechain with the vocal so instruments give up exactly the frequencies it occupies (sharper than a wideband ducker like Trackspacer); send the lead vocal to the sidechain of pads/sustained parts — Disclosure, [Looking For Love](https://youtu.be/NlJt0pCptGk) & [Higher Than Ever Before](https://youtu.be/9XkHSronZck)
- **Duck only the sub** — a [[Pro-MB]] frequency-specific sidechain ducks a bassline's sub off the kick, leaving the detail above untouched — Disclosure, [Higher Than Ever Before](https://youtu.be/9XkHSronZck)
- **One-instance groove ducking** — [[Kickstart 2]] sidechains synths/reverbs to lock the groove without routing a kick trigger — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8)

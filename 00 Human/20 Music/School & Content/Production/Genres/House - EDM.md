# House / EDM

**What makes it the genre:** the groove engine — four-on-floor kick as the clock, everything breathing around it via sidechain, arrangement as energy management (build → drop → breakdown), and sound design carrying the identity a lyric would carry elsewhere.

## The pillars

### 1. The pump (the genre's heartbeat)
- Kick 4-on-floor → **everything melodic ducks from it** ([[Kickstart 2]] on bass hard, pads/keys gentler, even reverb returns). Curve shape = the groove: deep+fast = aggressive, shallow+lazy = deep house sway ([[Sidechaining]]).
- Offbeat bass (notes between kicks) + duck together = the house bounce.

### 2. Low end — surgical
- One sub at a time, mono <120, kick owns its 5 ms — the pocket is *engineered*, not hoped for.
- Bass sound design: clean sub layer + character mid layer processed separately ([[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]]; Disclosure-style rubber = filtered analog patch, [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]]/[[Drift]] + [[Ghz Vulf Compressor 3]] pump).
- **Two bassline archetypes:** "stupidly simple"/near-childish — the bass IS the melody, reads aggressive and heavy — vs. syncopated/funky (16th-note patterns, swing, more harmonic movement). Pick the archetype the track's energy calls for before writing. **Chord extensions on top of an aggressive bass soften it; straight triads keep it hard** — a real tradeoff, not just taste. → [[Bassline Writing]] — Max Konyi, [House Music Tutorial in Studio One in 15 Minutes | Weekly Challenge #04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CIO3Z_a0gaA)

### 3. Drums — texture over power
- The kick is the power; hats/shakers/percs are *texture loops* layered thin (2–4 lanes, each quiet, panned around — [[2 Drums Hats Group|Drums Hats Group]] wide).
- Claps big and roomy ([[Reverb Send Room]] high send); ride the open-hat offbeat for drive.
- Groove: swing/shuffle on the hat lanes (16th swing 55–62%), velocity humanized.

### 4. Vocals — chopped, thrown, sampled
- The Disclosure vocal: bright (+air at tracking), clean, riding above a dense mix, treble emphasized because hats/shakers crowd the top ([[1 Main Vocal Track|Main Vocal Track]] + air).
- Vocal *as instrument*: chops pitched/formant-shifted ([[Little AlterBoy]]), throws everywhere ([[Delay Send Other]] hot), reversed tails into downbeats.
- Mix into an SSL-style glue comp from day one ([[Glue Compressor]] on the master while producing — the Disclosure habit, peaks ~−8 dB into it).

### 5. Arrangement = energy management
- 8/16-bar grammar strictly · build = subtract low end + riser + tighten the filter · **drop = silence beat then everything** · breakdown = the emotional section that earns drop #2.
- **Delayed drop — bring the kick in on beat 3, not beat 1** (Disclosure signature): hold the kick out until the vocal hook lands so the drop makes room for the singer *and* hits harder; sync it to the chord rhythm. Great live (crowd counts 1-2-3-4 then it drops). *Source: [Holding On](https://youtu.be/8H8U-WjvdVc); see also the "never on beat 1" fills in [Never Enough](https://youtu.be/lGopQwQo9Ag).*
- **Odd-bar-count drops** — hold the drop a couple of bars *past* where the ear expects it (past bar 9, then longer still) to build tension and "make them wait." *Source: [Douha (Mali Mali)](https://youtu.be/gCfU5Cq9WU4).*
- Automate one macro (filter cutoff, verb size, width) across whole sections — motion beats new parts.

### 6. UK garage / bootleg aesthetic — imperfection *is* the genre
- **Don't polish out era-correct flaws.** Hats mixed too loud and "bad"-sounding, vocals left slightly out of tune — leave them when they read as *old-school garage* rather than as mistakes ("they'd be that loud in the original tune"). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].
- **Bedroom MPC/Akai bus saturation** over the whole flipped loop ([[Decimort 2]]-style sampler grunge) so it sounds "made through a crappy MPC in someone's bedroom." → [[Saturation and Distortion]].
- **Flip a recognizable soul EP + vocal** into a bootleg — [[Keyscape]] "Greg Phillinganes" EP for the chords; aim "cheesy but hard enough to not be cheesy," a club tune that bridges eras.
- *Source: Disclosure, [Writing U.K. Garage in the Studio!](https://youtu.be/OJ9d8AR8bBg)*

## Arrangement

Pillar 5 already says arrangement *is* energy management. This is the shape it takes.

| Section | Bars | Slots filled | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 16 | TRANSIENT only — **loopable drums for the DJ** | 1 |
| Build | 16 | + BED · MIDS, low end thinning, filter tightening | 4 |
| Drop | 16 | everything, **after a beat of silence** | 6–7 |
| Breakdown | 16–32 | BED · LEAD, drums gone — the emotional section | 2–3 |
| Drop 2 | 16 | as drop 1 + one held-back element | 7 |
| Outro | 16 | back to loopable drums | 1 |

**Entrances & exits**
- **16 bars, near-universally** — verified across Valentino Khan, AC Slater, Shiba San in the sources below. A fill lands at bar 8 of each section and another right before the drop. A section that's *almost* 16 bars is much harder to beatmatch, which is the actual reason.
- With section length effectively fixed, **the only creative decision per section is whether the next one steps energy up or down.**
- Intro and outro exist for the DJ, not the listener. Keep them loopable and drum-only.
- The build *removes* low end while it adds tension — sub out, filter tightening, riser up.

**The contrast move — silence, then everything.** The beat of silence before the drop is the whole mechanism. Everything upstream is setup for it.

**Density rule: 1 → 7 → 2 → 7.** Wide swings on a fixed grid. Where trap subtracts inside a static loop, house cycles the whole arrangement through an energy curve every 64 bars.

## Session recipe
120–126 BPM (house) · start with kick+bass+one loop until it grooves on its own for 8 bars — if that's not danceable, nothing added will fix it · design the drop's signature sound before writing anything else.

*Vintage gear behind the sound: [[Roland TR-909]] (kick/hats/clap) · [[Roland TB-303]] (acid itself) · [[Roland Juno-106]] (chord stabs) · [[Roland SH-101]] (the 90s bassline) · [[Roland JD-800]] (the rave piano) · [[Roland TR-808]] · [[Roland TR-707]] + [[Roland TR-606]] (the acid rig) — full library: [[Vintage Gear Index]].*

## From videos

- **A full house-beat rebuild tracing Drake's *Honestly, Nevermind* back to its genuine 80s Chicago-house lineage, not a surface impression.** Drums: thumpy kick + real acoustic hi-hat samples (feel of the [[Roland TR-909]] without pulling literal 909 samples), four-on-the-floor kick with extra low-velocity ghost notes for swing, and a delay on the main hi-hat (learned by ear from "Texts Go Green") with a THIRD hi-hat part used purely as an end-of-loop fill. Melodic percussion: a repeating soft synth-bell one-shot, inspired by "Massive." Vocal chops: a small chopped fragment (not a full phrase) run through reverb → a delay set to a DIFFERENT time value than the hi-hat's delay — the mismatch is named explicitly as "a massive key to creating swing" — → Pan effect; traced to house's own origin story, Loleatta Holloway's 1980 "Love Sensation" acapella, sampled/remixed 300+ times by Chicago DJs (heard on Black Box's "Ride on Time," another flip of the same acapella). Chords: a deliberately "cheap"/non-vintage piano sound (not a realistic vintage patch) → reverb → EQ (heavily filtered) → delay → heavy compression — bouncy house piano again traced to the same Black Box/Holloway lineage. Bass: the album varies its bass *source* track to track — a guitar bass on one, an 80s-style DX7 synth-bass patch (Arturia DX7 V, "Rebound Bass" preset + micro pitch-shift) on another — rather than one bass formula throughout.

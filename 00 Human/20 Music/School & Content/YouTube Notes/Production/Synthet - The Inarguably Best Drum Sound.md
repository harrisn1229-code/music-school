# Synthet - The Inarguably Best Drum Sound

## Synopsis

A hi-hat-programming checklist: placement variation (beyond the plain every-4th pattern, adding off-beat hats), strict velocity variation instead of uniform hits, swapping a hat for a ride to change the color of a section, layering shakers/percussion loops and panning them out for width, and choosing tail length (semi-open vs. short vs. mixed) per hit deliberately rather than by default. Closes with FL's note-repeat function for generating fast/triplet/trap-style repeated hat runs without hand-drawing every note.

## Techniques

- **Vary hat placement beyond the default pattern** — a plain "every 4th beat" hat pattern is a start; adding off-beat hits is what actually drives the groove forward. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Velocity variation is mandatory, not optional** — hats at uniform strength read as harsh/mechanical; a real drummer's velocity varies hit to hit. Layer in hand-percussion-style accents on top for extra realism.
- **Swap the hat for a ride (or combine) to change section color** — an easy way to create a new feel for a part without touching the rest of the pattern.
- **Layer shaker/percussion loops and pan them out** — combined with the hat pattern, panned loops create movement and width; "not afraid to almost excessively add" groove loops for this.
- **Tail length is a per-hit sound-choice decision** — for a back-beat hat, a semi-open tail is often preferred; for a higher-density roll, a short tail; mixing tail lengths across hits is deliberate, not accidental.
- **FL's note-repeat function** generates repeated hits (8ths, triplets, fast trap-style rolls) on a hi-hat without manually drawing every note — drag to increase repeat density.

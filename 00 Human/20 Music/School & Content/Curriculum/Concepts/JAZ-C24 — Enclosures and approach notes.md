# JAZ-C24 — Enclosures and approach notes

**Q.** What is an enclosure, where does it belong, and how does an approach note differ?

> [!question]- Answer
> An enclosure **surrounds a target note before playing it.** For a target of C: **D♭ – B – C** (chromatic both sides, classic bebop), **D – B – C** (diatonic above, chromatic below, smoother), **B – D♭ – C** (more angular), **D – D♭ – B – C** (longest and most obviously jazz).
>
> An **approach note** is the one-note cousin: precede the chord tone with the note a half step below (the default and the strongest, because it borrows the leading-tone pull), a half step above (softer), or a scale step away (most neutral, most usable outside jazz).
>
> Both belong on the note you actually want heard — usually a chord tone on a downbeat. **Not on every note.** Enclosures are punctuation; wall to wall, a line stops having a shape.

## Why it matters

The most transferable device in the whole vocabulary, and it works in any genre. Take a two-note synth hook and enclose the second note: it costs two 16ths and stops the line sounding quantised from scratch.

A single chromatic approach into the downbeat of a chorus is the cheapest lift available anywhere in an arrangement.

## Drill

Take a melody you have already written, pick the three notes that matter most, and enclose only those. Leave everything else alone and check the line still has a shape.

## What's taught

- **Ignoring music theory, told as a failure story.** Years of treating "playing by ear/from the heart" as a creative-freedom stance collapsed at a conservatory jazz-guitar entrance exam: asked to improvise over a plain F blues, it "sounded absolutely terrible" despite years of advanced metal technique. Learning basic theory (intervals, cadences, chord construction) afterward didn't constrain his playing — it unlocked it, while he kept playing the same music he always liked.
- **Not fixing clean/efficient technique fundamentals early.** Distinguishes "technique" as named tricks (tapping, sweeping) from technique as *how* you physically play: wrist angle, whether picking motion comes from the wrist or the whole arm, thumb position. His own uncorrected picking angle and "flying pinky" became a ceiling on advanced material years later, forcing a painful technique rebuild. Acknowledges the counter-argument (some great players' quirky technique IS their sound) but recommends clean fundamentals as the safer default.
- **No specific goals.** A student who set a concrete goal (learn a specific song by a target "no-pressure deadline") progressed faster than the author's own goal-less beginner years — even after missing the deadline. Goals also help filter which technique/theory topics are worth drilling versus skipping for the music you actually want to play.
- **No practice plan** — overlaps prior videos already routed (1-hour routine, four 15-minute blocks, daily consistency over occasional long sessions); not re-logged.
- **Not finding a mentor sooner, framed as a genuine regret.** Years of self-teaching advanced shred technique alone (no local teacher covered sweep picking/tapping) before finally getting structured conservatory feedback — named directly as "probably one of the absolute biggest mistakes" costing multiple years of progress.

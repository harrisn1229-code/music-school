## Synopsis

A product-demo video, not a technique tutorial in the usual sense: dnksaus walks through two of his own paid Gumroad products built entirely from stock Ableton devices macro-mapped into racks. First half: his brand-new **Instrument Rack Pack** (kicks, basses, keys, texture layers, drum one-shots, trap leads) — every single preset deliberately built fully randomizable from the ground up, framed as a design philosophy rather than a gimmick. Second half: two free DLC updates to the existing **Dank Sauces** rack pack (19 new racks total), covering effects racks (EQ Multiplier, Phase Out, Stereo Green, a "messed up" Vocoder) and drum/texture racks (Little/Big Texturizer, Color Washer, Color Wind Generator, Dynamic Noise, Drum Buddy, Lo-Fi Drum Bus). Most of the individual rack names are pure product name-drops with no settings behind them — this note and the routing below only pull out the handful that carry an actual reusable technique (a setting, a build order, a stated caution, or a concrete artist reference). Neither pack is owned, so both get [[Plugin Wishlist]] entries rather than plugin notes; the underlying devices are all owned Ableton stock. ## Techniques

- **Design philosophy: build every preset randomizable from the start** — across the entire Instrument Rack Pack, every kick/bass/keys/texture preset ships with its own randomize control by design — the pitch is explicitly "load it, hit randomize a bunch, then layer effects on top" rather than treating any preset as a finished sound. → [[Randomized Rack Design]]
- **Sustain-bass build order: saturate/waveshape → protect the low end → Hybrid Reverb for space** — the "sustain bass" starter kit's documented recipe: start with a Saturator or wave-shaper stage, add a [[Hybrid Reverb]] for space, and explicitly control where the high-end distortion sits and how much low end gets isolated from mud (or just kept dry) so the low end stays clean through the rest of the processing. → [[Hybrid Reverb]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **128-preset bass browsable via one knob, chained into his own Bass Enhancer** — "Baba Buoy" exposes all 128 of its bass presets through a single preset-select knob rather than a dropdown; he routes his own (also-wishlisted) Bass Enhancer device directly after it as the standard next stage. → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **A texture rack voiced to sound different per octave** — the "Hot Iron Sizzle" background-texture rack is deliberately designed so its character changes meaningfully depending on which octave it's triggered in, getting multiple distinct textures out of one patch; a "Water Starter" rack applies the same starter-point philosophy to water/liquid ambience. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Cascading multiplied EQ boosts = a "magnifying glass" effect — needs a limiter downstream** — the Dank Sauces "EQ Multiplier" rack stacks/multiplies the same EQ boost across several bands at once for a "magnifying glass"/Roddy-Ricch-style mix-lightning effect; explicitly flagged as dangerous to run without a limiter, compressor, or clipper after it since the boosts compound fast. → [[EQ]], [[Clipping and Loudness]]
- **One-knob phase-out, referenced against Billie Eilish's "Bad Guy"** — the "Phase Out" rack collapses the classic phasing-into-silence transition effect into a single knob, with "Bad Guy" cited as the reference sound. → [[Modulation]]
- **One-knob Haas-style stereo riser** — "Stereo Green" gets an easy Haas-type stereo riser from a single control, faster than hand-building an unlinked-delay Haas pair. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Turn noisy/non-tonal sources into selectable tonal chords** — the "Color Washer" rack picks a root note and chord quality (major/minor) for a noisy source, with further control over triad-vs-seventh voicing, which chord tones play (e.g. root + fifth only), and a Decay-style knob shaping timbre — a rack-based cousin of the Vinyl-Distortion-into-Auto-Shift generative-tone trick documented elsewhere in this vault. → [[Randomized Rack Design]]
- **Reinvent any drum hit as a new snare: kill Warp, triple the pitch** — the "Drum Buddy" rack's underlying trick: take any one-shot, turn off Warp entirely, and multiply its playback pitch by roughly 3x to turn it into a usable brand-new snare. → [[Snare Design]]
- **Glue drums less aggressively on non-drop sections** — the "Lo-Fi Drum Bus" glue rack is deliberately dialed back on non-drop sections (verses, breakdowns) so the drums don't read as too intense where the arrangement wants restraint — vary the glue amount by section rather than leaving one setting on throughout. → [[Drum Bus]]
- **A deliberately "unrefined" Vocoder rack** — described as "not really a macro kind of rack, more of a put-it-on-and-let-it-do-messed-up-stuff-for-you" rack; full Vocoder coverage deferred to its own dedicated video later in this batch, so not expanded here.
- **Name-dropped without enough technique to route anywhere on their own** — Spectral Icing, Video Game Keys, RC-23, Aggro Face Bass, Tank Reese, FM Bass Factory Builder, FOB Bass, Scary Sprite, hybrid trap leads/Quacky Butler (Instrument Pack); Gazam, Factory Mod, Retro Telephone, Shifter Multiplier, Base Garbage, Little/Big Texturizer, Color Wind Generator, Dynamic Noise (Dank Sauces) — captured in the wishlist entries below for completeness, but none carry a setting/reason beyond "here's a cool preset."

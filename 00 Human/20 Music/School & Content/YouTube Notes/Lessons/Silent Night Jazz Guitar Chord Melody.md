## Synopsis

A solo-guitar **chord-melody arrangement** of "Silent Night" walked through measure-group by measure-group, with the harmonic reasoning behind every reharmonization spelled out rather than just shown. Nathan Borton takes a plain I–V–I carol progression and dresses nearly every phrase with a jazz device: inserted ii chords ahead of the V, walkdowns connecting IV back to ii, dominant chords approached chromatically from a half step above, diminished chords substituted in for dominant color, a delayed-resolution ♭II major 7 voicing, diminished-arpeggio-derived melody notes harmonized with matching diminished (and "major-diminished") voicings, a quick inserted ii–V, a tritone sub, and a closing ♯IV chromatic triad walkdown into a big open-string B♭maj9♯11 final chord. What makes this valuable beyond "here's a cool voicing" is the *why*: almost every substitution is justified by naming which melody note is sounding and which chord tone of the new harmony it becomes (drop-2 harmonization philosophy — see [[Chord Melody Arranging]]), and the diminished-substitution logic is stated as a fully generalizable rule (any diminished chord built off the 3rd, 5th, ♭7, or 𝄫7 of a dominant chord gives that dominant a richer "dominant 7♭9" sound), not a one-off trick for this tune. Full PDF/tab and chord analysis are gated behind the creator's Patreon; this transcript captures the spoken theory only, no notation.

## What's taught

**Measures 1–3 (opening).**
- Opens on a **major 6/9 chord** (extra color over a plain major triad) as the tonic voicing.
- Melody harmonized with **drop 2 voicings, roots on the E and A strings** (the two most common drop-2 root-string pairs — see [[Chord Voicings]] § *Drop 2*).
- The plain tune is just I–V–I here; Borton **inserts the ii chord right before the V**, then builds a **IV → iii → ii walkdown** leading into it — "a walkdown progression" is his own term for the descending stepwise chord motion, framed as a general tool for making a simple progression more interesting.
- Extends the V chord by an extra measure before resolving.

**Measures 4–6.**
- Similar idea, different target: instead of walking to the ii, this phrase aims at a **G dominant 7**, approached from the IV chord down a half step via an **A♭7** — stated as a general rule: *"you can approach any dominant chord from a half step above."*
- The melody's 13th over the A♭7 is called out as a particularly rich color note.
- Right after the G7, Borton inserts a **B diminished chord** — explained via the general substitution rule: *any diminished chord built off the 3rd, 5th, ♭7, or 𝄫7 of a dominant chord gives you a "dominant 7♭9" sound*, making the dominant richer/fuller. Confirms the melody note (D) is a chord tone of both the G7 and the B°7, restated as "the key to good harmonization: always keep the melody in mind."

**Measures 7–10.**
- Standard guitar voicings for **Cm7 and F7**, but deliberately **broken up rather than played as full block chords** on every beat, to better fit the melody line (see the "don't harmonize every note" physical-execution principle on [[Chord Melody Arranging]]).
- Last beat of measure 8: a **slightly dissonant dominant voicing** justified by a chromatic melody line (C moving to B natural) underneath it.
- Measure 9 — a **delayed-resolution trick**: whenever the root of the key is sitting in the melody (here, B♭), harmonize it with a **♭II major 7 chord** instead of resolving straight to I — called one of Borton's "go-to voicings" for this exact situation.
- Resolves to I with the same major 6/9 voicing from the opening, then sneaks in an **extra dominant chord acting as the V7/IV** (the "5-7 of four") heading into the next section.

**Measures 11–14.**
- Mostly the tune's original changes with extra color layered in.
- Measure 12: melody notes drawn from the **diminished arpeggio** (root, ♭3, ♭5, 𝄫7) — harmonized either with **traditional diminished voicings** or, as chosen here, **"major-diminished" voicings** (a spicier alternative using the same functional slot).
- Measures 13–14: a **quick inserted ii–V** to add motion, plus a **tritone sub on beat 3 of measure 13**.

**Measures 15–18.**
- More harmonic expansion over the same original changes: a diminished chord approaching the F7 **from a half step below**, then that same **F7♯11 voicing** from earlier reused, paired with a **tritone sub** and a **♯5 harmonized in the melody** to lead back to I.
- Ends the phrase with **another walkdown into a G7** functioning as the V7/ii of the next phrase (C minor).

**Measures 19–22.**
- Revisits the **major-diminished** concept to harmonize an **F♯ diminished chord** moving to G.
- Measure 21: the **G minor voicing broken up** (same "don't play every chord solid" principle), then a **chromatic line walking down to E natural** under a C7 voicing in measure 22.

**Measures 23–24.**
- The **half-step-above dominant approach** device reused twice back to back: **A♭7 → G7**, then **G♭7 → F7**.

**Ending.**
- A classic **♯IV walkdown**: normally a ♯IV walkdown starts on the sharp-4 of the key and descends chromatically back to I; here the arrangement keeps **B♭ (the melody note) held on top** while **triads** underneath outline the walkdown.
- Adds a **D♭6 passing chord** for a smoother transition into the following **C7**.
- Final chord: a big **B♭ major 9♯11**, voiced using the guitar's **open strings** — chosen specifically because it rings out and resonates well on the instrument.

# Someday My Prince Will Come — Frank Churchill

> A 3/4 jazz waltz in B♭ major — one of the few standards in a waltz feel, built around a signature dominant 7♯5 as its 2nd chord.

## Chords (as taught for guitar)
Key: B♭ major, 3/4 time. B♭6/maj7 tonic; E♭maj7 (IV) carries an available ♯11 (true of any major IV chord — the 4th mode of a major scale is Lydian); the tune's signature 2nd chord is a **dominant 7♯5**, voiced in practice as a flat-13 shape (from C harmonic minor). Slash chords create deliberate chromatic bass motion; a C♯dim7 with the melody note A functions in practice as a rootless A7♭9.

## Steal this
- **A go-to jazz-waltz comping backdrop: bass note on beat 1, chord stabs on "and" of 2 and on 3** — a reliable fallback pattern for 3/4, which most jazz vocabulary (built mostly in 4/4) doesn't cover by default. → [[Jazz Comping]]
- **Any inversion of a half-diminished shape on a dominant's ♭7 also voices that dominant's ♭9/♭13** — one fretboard-study target (half-diminished inversions), two usable voicing vocabularies. → [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Whole-tone vs. altered scale over a dominant 7♯5** — both contain the chord's tones; altered adds ♭9/♯9 for more tension. Pick one by ear, not by rule.
- **Practice arpeggios starting from every note in the shape, not just the root** — closes the gap between "knowing the chord" and finding it live mid-improvisation. → [[Chord-Tone Soloing]]

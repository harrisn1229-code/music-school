# POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII

**Q.** In `I–♭VII–IV` (C–B♭–F in C), where does the ♭VII come from — and what Beatles device does this describe?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Mixolydian** (major with a ♭7), or equivalently **borrowed from the parallel minor** — both readings are valid. It has **no leading tone**, so it lifts and drifts rather than pulling home. It's a **Beatles signature**, alongside descending chromatic bass under a held chord ("Michelle", "Something").

## Why it matters

POP-03 blank, and POP-18 — the Beatles question — blank in your core lane. ♭VII is the single most common non-diatonic chord in rock and pop, and the missing-leading-tone point is why it sounds modal rather than functional.

## Drill

Play C–B♭–F and C–G–F back to back. Name why one pulls home and the other doesn't.

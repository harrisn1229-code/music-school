# Transcribing Complex Music by Ear in Real-Time | Ep.02

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v0zTEeU0wfs)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v0zTEeU0wfs)

## Synopsis
Episode 2 of Max Konyi's real-time transcription series steps up the harmonic difficulty from Ep.01's pop/country material into jazz-fusion, video-game orchestral, and K-pop territory — songs with fast chord changes, extended/altered chords, and genuinely ambiguous major/minor resolutions. Three tracks: "Yellow Jacket" by Sean Martin, the Genshin Impact soundtrack, and "Star" by Heize (K-pop). The through-line is that the same core toolkit from Ep.01 (bass-motion tracking, the no-perfect-pitch tonic trick, chord-position feeling recognition) scales up to handle real harmonic complexity — it doesn't need new tools, just faster/deeper application of the same ones plus a bigger trained vocabulary.

## Techniques
- **"Yellow Jacket" (Sean Martin, jazz fusion) — same no-perfect-pitch key-finding trick, applied to a track with fast chord changes right at the top.** Sang the felt tonic, played a random note, heard it as scale-degree 4 relative to the sung tonic, and back-calculated the key as D from that single relationship.
- **A ii–V–i is recognized as one fused "chunk," not two chords added together.** Explicitly: "I don't even hear it individually as a two and a five — to me it sounds like a chunk," and that chunk itself carries its own scale-degree feeling relative to the main key (in this case, a minor ii–V–i landing a whole progression that itself functions as scale-degree 6 of the overarching key) — matches [[Scale Degree Ear Training]]'s point that trained listeners recognize chord-progression fragments as holistic units, not reconstructed live from parts.
- **Bass notes narrow chord identity fastest because their scale-degree feeling is heard directly**, same principle as melody-reading but applied to the lowest voice — used repeatedly across all three songs as the first thing checked before quality/voicing.
- **Genshin Impact soundtrack — a specific "new vocabulary item" being actively acquired on-camera:** a chord Konyi didn't have trained until recent years, which he identifies mid-video as a **ii minor 9 voiced without its normal minor 3rd but WITH the major 3rd of the overall key** — meaning the chord's own quality reads ambiguously (no clear 3rd of its own) while the *key's* major 3rd sitting inside it gives it a distinctive bright/major-tinged color despite functioning as a minor ii. Demonstrates the "expanding vocabulary from real music" process described in [[Transcribing and Playing by Ear]] in real time — he'd heard the sound repeatedly across other songs, worked it out, and it had become recognizable enough to name on the spot here.
- **Sharp-4 in the bass reliably signals one of exactly two chord types**, confirmed again on the Genshin material: either a secondary dominant of V (a "two-chord"/V-of-V, a move Konyi notes has been common since the Baroque era) or a minor 7 flat 5 (half-diminished ii). Hearing ♯4 in the bass narrows the guess to those two before any testing.
- **"Star" (Heize, K-pop) — a minor ii–V that resolves to MAJOR instead of the implied minor**, confirmed live: the voicings leading in (minor 7 flat 5 on the ii, tension on the V) strongly imply landing on a minor vi, but the track resolves to major instead — flagged as common in both K-pop and traditional jazz repertoire, and as a trap for assuming a set-up always pays off as implied.
- **Jazzy extended/altered chords are read by rough family (dominant, altered-dominant, sus, half-diminished) rather than forced to one exact voicing on a first pass** — several chords across "Star" are left as "roughly this, I might be wrong about the exact extension" rather than stalled on, consistent with [[Transcribing and Playing by Ear]]'s first-pass-triage principle (lead-sheet-level accuracy first, exact voicings on a dedicated re-listen).
- **A whole ii–V is heard first as its bass-note contour, then confirmed by chord quality on top** — repeated pattern across both jazz-adjacent tracks: bass motion identifies the ii–V shape before the individual chord qualities are even confirmed, with quality-checking functioning as a confirmation pass rather than the primary detection method.

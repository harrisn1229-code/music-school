# Parliament "Flashlight" | Synth Bass Tutorial

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M8s8sgOU9dY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M8s8sgOU9dY)

## Synopsis

A Minimoog recreation of the Parliament "Flashlight" bass growl, built on the same saw+square octave-apart recipe seen across this channel, but with the real content in the performance-specific settings: single-trigger monophonic behavior, a deliberately mismatched glide/keyboard-tracking setup, and a clipped, sustain-free envelope that leaves room for the drum hit.

## Techniques

- **Combined Decay/Release on one knob, with sustain intentionally at zero for a "clipped" bass hit that leaves room for the kick/snare to speak** — filter cutoff closed to zero so the envelope-to-filter amount ("Contour") does all the tonal shaping; raising that amount brightens the sound. A deliberately percussive, un-sustained bass character rather than a held-note growl.
- **Monophonic single-trigger behavior used as a performance feature, not a limitation**: because the Minimoog doesn't retrigger its envelope on every new note in a legato phrase, fast note runs read as one continuous "growl rip"/moan rather than a series of separate attacks — deliberately "throwing away" some note attacks is part of the instrument's expressive character, not a mistake to fix.
- **Glide (keyboard-CV lag) turned ON specifically for an expressive downward pitch "rip" after a high note**, contrasted directly against the same phrase played with glide off (straight to pitch, no slide) — a clear A/B example of when to reach for glide as a deliberate ornament rather than leaving it off by default.
- **LFO (vibrato) keyboard-tracking turned OFF so vibrato rate stays constant across the whole keyboard range**, rather than speeding up at higher pitches the way it would with tracking on — the inverse choice from the filter key-tracking discussed elsewhere in this channel's patches (see [[Filters and Resonance]]), applied here to a modulation source instead of the filter.
- **The pitch knob's non-quantized (continuous, not stepped) response is called out as essential to the part's vocal/expressive character** — a fully continuous pitch control, not a semitone-quantized bend range, is what allows the smooth "growl rip"/moan quality described above.

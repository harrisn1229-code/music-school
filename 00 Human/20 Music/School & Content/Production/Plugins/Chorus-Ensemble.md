# Chorus-Ensemble

![[Images/Chorus-Ensemble.jpg]]

**Category:** Modulation
**Vendor:** Ableton

## What it is

Ableton's modulation effect combining classic chorus and multi-voice ensemble modes for thickening and widening a signal.

## What it's used for

Widening mono synths, guitars, and vocals, and adding classic chorus movement to pads and leads.

## FX chain notes

Placed after EQ/compression on an instrument chain, often as a subtle send-based width effect rather than a heavy insert.

## Techniques

- **"Clean chorus" as stage 3 of a 4-device chain on the very first chords of a from-scratch build, sandwiched between a plate reverb and a separately-layered "warm keys" pad.** A 9th-chord progression runs EQ → [[Little Plate]] → this device's clean chorus preset (Arturia-sourced on-screen; owned-lane stand-in) → a second "warm keys" layer stacked on top for extra depth — chorus used purely for width/glue here, not obvious modulation character. → [[Little Plate]], [[EQ]], [[90s R&B Sample Chain]] — loner, [how to make your own 90's rnb samples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PQjIBDgJehQ)
- **Faster Rate + higher Amount as a "faux Leslie" rotating-speaker effect, on a pedal's second chorus voice** — the source pedal's rarely-used second voice is described as sounding like a rotating speaker when set faster than its default slow chorus sweep; the owned-plugin equivalent is pushing this device's Rate and Amount well above the usual gentle-drift settings (② 0.3–0.5 Hz / ③ 15–25%) documented in Key Parameters below, rather than the slow/subtle width role this device normally runs. Worth trying on a guitar or keys part that wants a Leslie-adjacent swirl without a dedicated rotary emulation. → [[Modulation]] — Jack Ruch, [My Home Recording Setup for Guitar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KYTGFneEJ2A)
- **Clean chorus as the closing stage of a flute chop's color chain.** On a Ventus Ethnic Winds flute layer (see [[Plugin Wishlist]]), this device runs last after a Cassette "Crushed" preset and a plate reverb — a plain clean chorus, not pushed for obvious width/character, simply finishing the tone. → [[Cassette]], [[UAD Pure Plate Reverb]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — loner, [making a soulful afrobeats beat for rema & wizkid from scratch (use voice notes to start ideas!?)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e-KvnLXHAy4)
- **Two separate chorus stages used purely for width, not obvious modulation character, across a keys-and-guitar sample.** Pad layer: chorus added mid-chain (after a reverb + EQ, before [[ValhallaDelay]]) "to make it a bit wider" ahead of a bounce/chop pass — see [[ValhallaDelay]]'s entry on the same build. Guitar layer: BOTH of an amp plugin's chorus presets stacked together specifically "to make it super wide," between an EQ stage and a ping-pong delay (also reached for as a width tool, not an echo). Two independent confirmations that chorus is a first-reach width tool on this channel, not just a movement/character effect. → [[ValhallaDelay]], [[Delay]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Afro Sample Chain]] — loner, [how i made the sample that got a 1 million stream placement (it's easier than you think!?)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=atjGOpxBzag)
- **Chorus as a general tone/movement stage on two Mk.gee-inspired chains — a baritone guitar bass tone and a Casio CZ-5000-style synth build.** Guitar chain: preamp/comp/amp → **chorus → flanger → tape emulation**, ahead of the actual secret-sauce stage (a noise-gated distorted reverb send). CZ-synth chain: two layered CZ-emulation VSTs → **flanger → chorus → tape (envelope-mode noise) → plate reverb → distortion → heavy compression**. → [[Phaser-Flanger]], [[Kramer Tape]], [[Little Plate]], [[Mk.gee Baritone Guitar Chain]], [[Modulation]] — loner, [the secrets behind Mk.gee's HYPNOTIC production!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=969p5Q4JSZU)
- **Two stacked instances, each doing a different job — pitch drift on one, maxed width/feedback on the other — for an Arca-style constantly-moving piano.** Instance 1: a pitch-only-flavored mode (the source's transcript says **"VA mode"** — likely a garbled auto-caption of **Vibrato mode**, the closest match on this device's own mode list, since the described behavior is pure pitch movement rather than delay-time chorusing), slow LFO **Rate**, kept subtle — **Amount** mapped to a macro for to-taste pitch drift. Instance 2, placed after it: **Classic** mode, **Dry/Wet 100% (fully wet)**, **high-pass filter engaged** on the wet path (keeps the low end tight/undisturbed by the chorusing), slow LFO **Rate** again but **Chorusing Amount maximized**, with **Feedback** mapped to its own macro to push intensity further. Meticulous, stacked chorus like this — two instances, two distinct roles — is called out as an Arca sound-design staple, part of a broader habit of layering several independent LFO/envelope-driven movements at once (pitch drift + chorus feedback + a velocity-linked filter + a brightness stage) rather than reaching for one dramatic effect. Full chain: [[Arca Piano]]. → [[Arca Piano]], [[Modulation]] — asd, [ableton sound design | DECONSTRUCTION STUDY [ver. 1] Arca Inspired Piano](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=equNWJvGVio)
- **Chorus on a strummed guitar sample's body layer, ahead of room reverb and EQ, for a fuller "R&B sample" width** — on a from-scratch guitar loop built to sound like a decades-old soul sample (not a real vintage recording), chorus is the second stage after a preamp/distortion pass — chorus → room reverb → EQ → a shaper stage — thickening the strummed layer before space and tone shaping are added, rather than chorus being the last effect applied. → [[ValhallaRoom]], [[Bryson Tiller Vocal Chain]] — loner, [the secrets behind bryson tiller's 'trapsoul' sound!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVJ5fOlLy4w)
- **Ensemble mode as a go-to width pass on a dense sound-design layer** — switching Chorus-Ensemble to its **Ensemble** mode (rather than Chorus or Vibrato) was called out as a favorite specifically for how naturally it widens a sound without it losing definition — used as one stage in a long processing chain on a "squishy," heavily-layered bass/lead texture. → [[Width and Stereo]] — Eli derp, [How To Make Insanely Phat Basses with @eliderp_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8a84OhMSfzM)
- **Two stacked Vibrato-mode instances as a free tape-wobble alternative** — when a paid tape-emulation plugin's pitch-wobble/dropout character isn't available, duplicate Chorus-Ensemble in **Vibrato** mode: run one instance subtle and fast, a second slow and a bit more pronounced, and turn up **Warmth** on both for extra saturation/analog color — reads as a lo-fi wobble stack built entirely from stock. → [[Modulation]] — You Suck at Producing, [The best FX for creating LUSH Soundscapes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wysKsyJf7dg)

## Used in my chains

Subtle motion/width — kept low-mix so it never smears the source:

- **[[1 Electric Piano Track|Electric Piano Track]]** — *slot 4, Motion*: Mode Ensemble, Rate 0.4 Hz, Amount 20%, Width 100%, Dry/Wet 30% — the Dyno-My-Piano shimmer.
- **[[1 Acoustic Guitar Track|Acoustic Guitar Track]]** — *last-resort width* on a single take that should've been doubled: Amount 15%, Rate 0.3 Hz, Dry/Wet 20% (a real double always beats this).
- **[[1 Electric Guitar DI Track|Electric Guitar DI Track]]** — low-mix on clean funk/city-pop parts (the Nile Rodgers lane, after Pro-Q → [[Pro-C 2]]).
- **[[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]]** — only **above 200 Hz** (synthwave/pop) so the sub stays mono-clean.
- **[[Width Send]]** — *subtle tape-chorus flavor* on the return instead of surgical width — more vintage motion.

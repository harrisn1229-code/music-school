# Zach Scholz - Using Analog Tape to Glue a Mix Together

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xk0LYE7HGAI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xk0LYE7HGAI)

## Synopsis
Zach frames mix glue as fundamentally a busing problem: group related tracks and run shared effects across all of them at once (his example is a drum bus — kick, snare, overheads consolidated to one stereo out with shared EQ/compression) rather than treating each track in isolation; today's twist is doing that busing step on physical analog tape instead of digitally, by tracking a full arrangement (acoustic guitars, bass, vocal, slide guitar) and sending several tracks out to a hardware tape deck at once, consolidating them down to a stereo left/right pass — he credits tape's inherent compression and saturation with doing the actual gluing, calling the result "absolutely beautiful," and most of the runtime is him tracking and auditioning individual sounds (mic position, DI blend, mixer color) rather than detailing the tape pass itself, which happens at the very end of the video.

## Techniques
- **Busing = shared effects on grouped tracks, at the same time** — his working definition of glue: send two or more channels to a bus (e.g. a drum bus — kick/snare/overheads consolidated to one stereo out) so EQ/compression applied there hits everything simultaneously, which is what makes a mix read as "in unison" rather than as separate parts.
- **Buses to physical tape instead of doing the glue step digitally** — sends several tracks from the session out to a hardware tape deck at once and consolidates ("busses"/"consolidates") them to a stereo left/right pass, rather than summing them in the box.
- **What tape actually contributes to the bus** — he attributes the glue specifically to tape's own compression and saturation plus general added character ("absolutely beautiful… one of my favorite things to do"); no tape speed, flux, or level is stated for this bus pass in the transcript. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Direct match for the Master Chain's tape-finish stage** — this is a hardware validation of the same tool and principle the vault already runs at the back end of the master chain ([[Kramer Tape]] at 15 ips / very low flux / unity playback, for "final rounding"); Zach applies the same tape-glue idea earlier, at a group/bus stage on guitars, rather than on the finished 2-bus. → [[Kramer Tape]]
- **Owned hardware equivalent for this exact move** — Nate's [[Sony TC-630]] is documented for the same "run it through tape, print it back" role (currently used per-source); routing a small submix into it instead of a single source would replicate Zach's bus-to-tape technique. → [[Sony TC-630]]
- **Mixer-stage color stacks before the tape stage** — recording direct into his hardware mixer, strumming hard visibly drives clipping/breakup through the mixer itself — an extra, pre-tape saturation layer on top of whatever the tape adds later.
- **Pick + EQ "scoop" for a 60s-style guitar tone** — chooses a pick and deliberately goes for a "scoop" (mids pulled back) character he associates with 60s guitar sounds, instead of a fuller/brighter tone.
- **Mic placement over double-micing** — instead of blending two mics on the guitar, moves a single mic's position/angle between takes to change the captured tone, and notes the shift is audible.
- **Bass = body mic + DI blended together** — hollow-body ("Höfner"-style) bass captured as a blend of a [[Shure SM57]] (on the body, for the acoustic resonance) and the DI signal at the same time, not either alone. Notably close to Nate's own [[Hofner B-Bass H-Series]] and its chain's documented "Beatles/Sgt Pepper lane: DI + amp blended" variation.
- **Chorus printed on the vocal for an "indie" color** — a chorus effect is deliberately printed on his voice while tracking, not left clean, for that specific character.
- **What actually goes into this tape-bus pass** — three guitar parts played together (the main acoustic lead, a "reciprocal" tremolo guitar doubling the same line, and a third harmonizing part), then bused to tape to hear how the combination glues; a separate stereo acoustic pair and additional slide guitar were tracked in the same session but outside this specific bused group.

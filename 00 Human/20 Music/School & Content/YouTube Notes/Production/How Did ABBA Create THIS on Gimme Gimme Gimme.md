# How Did ABBA Create THIS on "Gimme Gimme Gimme"

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vyhnf370lKY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vyhnf370lKY)

## Synopsis

A front-panel-first patch recreation on an ARP Odyssey of the synth texture doubling the real strings on ABBA's "Gimme Gimme Gimme," plus a genuinely useful walkthrough method for reading an unfamiliar modular-style front panel by tracing signal flow left to right, and a keyboard-tracking amount that's a deliberate middle ground rather than off or full.

## Techniques

- **A repeatable method for reading an unfamiliar semi-modular front panel: scan left to right in signal-flow order (oscillators → LFO → mixer → filter → envelopes), and use the panel's own printed section labels to confirm what's patched to what** rather than guessing from the sound alone. Explicitly framed as a transferable first step for any new instrument, not Odyssey-specific.
- **Keyboard control voltage (key tracking) on the filter set to roughly HALF, not off and not full — a deliberate middle setting, chosen for a specific reason.** Zero tracking makes upper-register notes lose definition/"dull out"; full tracking makes them "too bright." Half was chosen so the patch "speaks" evenly across the range it's actually played in — a reminder that key tracking isn't a binary on/off choice, and the right amount depends on the register the part will occupy (contrast with [[Bass Design]]'s existing advice to turn tracking OFF for bass, where the goal is different: real bass gets duller not brighter going up the neck).
- **Two-finger simultaneous vibrato, moving both oscillators' modulation attenuators together by hand**, rather than routing a single LFO destination — a manual-performance alternative on synths (like later ARP Odyssey revisions) with dedicated vibrato buttons, when a part calls for hand-controlled expressive depth rather than a fixed LFO rate/amount.
- **A synth patch deliberately kept subordinate in texture to a doubling real-instrument part (here, real strings) rather than competing for the same sonic space** — described as "just adding a little bit of different texture" alongside prominent real strings, valued as a hybrid organic+synthetic color rather than a replacement for either. Worth remembering when layering a synth pad/lead under a live string or brass part: the synth's job can be to add character, not to be heard as its own distinct line.
- **Naming a synth's tonal character in personal, consistent adjectives (a "glossy" Minimoog vs. a "non-glossy" Odyssey) as a deliberate workflow habit** — used to decide which instrument to reach for and why, before even touching a knob.

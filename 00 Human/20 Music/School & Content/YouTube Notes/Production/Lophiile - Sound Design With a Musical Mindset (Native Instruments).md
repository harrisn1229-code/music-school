# Lophiile — Sound Design With a Musical Mindset (Native Instruments)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=spPaJzYPgzk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=spPaJzYPgzk)

## Synopsis

Lophiile (Tyler Acord — her "Avenue," BTS "Paradise," Alina Baraz) lays out his whole sound-design philosophy for Native Instruments: keep synthesis dead simple (one oscillator, one filter) because the complexity should come from the *chords*, treat processing of real instruments as sound design in its own right, and lean on resampling — speed/pitch round-trips through audio — as the main way to get textures no plugin can make. Ends with concrete resources for building the musical side (theory channels, MIDI packs, learning the major scale in all 12 keys). This is the densest of the lophiile batch and pairs directly with his resampling talk on the Help Me Devvon podcast.

## Techniques

- **One oscillator, one filter — complexity comes from the chords.** His base philosophy: don't stack modulation; get your mind used to a single osc + single filter as the starting point and let seventh chords / voicings do the interesting work. → [[Massive]], [[Serum 2]], [[BED Slot]]
- **[[Massive]] old-school saw:** take the *smooth square* wavetable and set bend +/- (wavetable position) to get a rawer, more analog-feeling saw than the stock saw. *(exact bend amount not stated — set by ear)*
- **[[Massive]] patch recipe (his go-to bed sound):** smooth-square-bent saw → low-pass filter with an envelope opening/closing it → stack unison in the **Voicing tab** with **Restart via Gate** on for thickness → add **white noise** low in the mix for analog hiss → copy the *same envelope* from the filter onto the **amp** so loudness moves with brightness → subtle **LFO on pitch** for drift. "This patch already sounds amazing — I'd use this on so many records." → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[BED Slot]]
- **Processing IS sound design.** A real instrument or sampled EP is raw material: his Scarbee A-200 electric piano (Kontakt) gets a tape emulation ([[RC-20 Retro Color]]; also Waves J37, free Reaktor tapes), then **MVibrato (Melda)** for movement — the RC-20 + vibrato together "create a crazy little wave, super beautiful." → [[Kontakt 7]], [[Modulation]], [[BED Slot]]
- **Chorus picks — his two favorites are free:** [[TAL-Chorus-LX]] (Juno-style, "awesome on this sound") and **Blue Cat Chorus** ("great on everything from guitars to vocals"). → [[Modulation]], [[Roland Juno-6 - Juno-60]]
- **Formant shift for lo-fi:** free **MAutoPitch** (Melda) — turn the auto-tune section *off*, use only the **formant shift** knob to make keys/samples "a little more lo-fi." Owned equivalent: [[Little AlterBoy]] formant knob / [[kHs Formant Filter]]. → [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **The flagship resample trick (speed round-trip):** write your MIDI part → **double the project/clip speed and play it up an octave** → bounce that fast-high version to audio → stretch the audio back down in **Re-Pitch** warp mode → it lands at the original key/tempo but the top end is muted "in a really nice way" and it sounds sampled/real, especially with tape on it. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Simpler]]
- **Resample an existing element up an octave in AUDIO** — freeze/flatten the synth, new audio track, **Complex Pro** warp, +12 st: the artifacts make it a *different* layer that can come in and out against the original — layering without adding a new sound source. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Reverse layers, but MIX them with the forward original** — print a part (e.g. a Juno arp), reverse the audio, and blend reversed + original together rather than replacing; "really cool stuff" lives in the overlap. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[TEXTURE Slot]]
- **Resample drums at a higher tempo, then Re-Pitch down** — instead of pitching the drum rack, crank the tempo, record the drums fast, then drop the audio back in Re-Pitch mode: instant "broken-down"/halftime section with thicker, darker drums. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]]
- **Sample your ENTIRE beat and mess with it** — bounce the whole thing, stretch/reverse/pitch it; "there's really no rules — you might come up with ideas you never would have thought of."
- **Musicality resources:** Jeff Schneider's YouTube channel (applicable theory), Ian Kirkpatrick's IG (creative-process stories), graves' **Prism** MIDI pack (chord progressions to start from), and his #1 assignment for students: **learn the major scale in all 12 keys** — every scale is derived from it. → [[Song Practice]]

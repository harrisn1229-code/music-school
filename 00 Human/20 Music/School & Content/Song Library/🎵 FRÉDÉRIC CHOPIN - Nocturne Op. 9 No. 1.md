# Nocturne Op. 9 No. 1 — Frédéric Chopin

**Performer:** Arthur Rubinstein
**Reference recording:** [YouTube — Rubinstein](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ygc5nXlgLkQ)

Key of B♭ minor. One of Chopin's most lyrical nocturnes — slow, expressive, wide left-hand arpeggios under a singing right-hand melody.

## Chords
Classical piano, not a jazz/pop chord-changes tune — no `progressions:` tag applies here (tagged `progressions: []` deliberately), and this is a harmonic outline rather than a chord-symbol chart.

**Ternary (ABA) form**, 85 measures in 6/4: the primary theme opens and closes the piece, flanking a contrasting secondary theme starting at measure 19, with a varied, more ornamented reprise of the primary theme from measure 70. The opening measures **oscillate between the tonic (i, B♭ minor) and the submediant (VI, C♭ major)** — a chromatic-mediant-adjacent color relationship (B♭ minor and C♭ major share no diatonic function but sit a half step apart at the root) that gives the opening its wistful, harmonically "floating" character before the phrase resolves back to B♭ minor. The piece is a well-known example of Chopin's habit of stretching functional harmony with chromatic passing motion under a long-breathed melodic line — treat any full Roman-numeral reduction as secondary to just hearing where the tonic pulls resolve.

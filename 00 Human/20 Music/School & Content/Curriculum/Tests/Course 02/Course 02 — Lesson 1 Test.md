> **Q1.** **Tonic, subdominant, dominant**
> *Also accepted:* T · SD · D
>
> **Q2.** **Tonic: I, vi, iii · subdominant: IV, ii · dominant: V, vii°**
> vi and iii are tonic substitutes because they share notes with I.
>
> **Q3.** **T → SD → D → T**
> Most of Western popular music in four symbols.
>
> **Q4.** **Tonic is home · subdominant is departure · dominant is tension wanting to resolve**
> *Also accepted:* rest / motion away / pull back
>
> **Q5.** **Because vi shares the tonic function with I, and V doesn't**
> Chords sharing a function can stand in for each other. Swapping within a function is a colour change; swapping across one changes what the bar is doing.
>
> **Q6.** **T – T – SD – D**
> C is I, Am is vi — both tonic. F is IV, subdominant. G is V, dominant.
>
> **Q7.** **Because a function describes a role, and the role survives a change of key**
> *Rubric (5):* says "vi" names a role while "Am" only names a chord in one key (2) · says the role behaves the same way in every key (1) · names a practical payoff — transposing, analysing by ear, or reusing a progression (2)
> "Am" tells you which key you happen to be in and nothing about what the chord is doing. "vi" tells you it's a tonic substitute, which is true in every key at once. Practically that means a progression you learn in C is a progression you know in all twelve, an analysis you make by ear doesn't depend on identifying absolute pitches, and a shape you like becomes reusable rather than tied to the session it came from.
>
> **Q8.** **No. The cycle is the default, not a rule — analyse the outlier by what it does instead**
> *Rubric (5):* says T→SD→D→T is a norm rather than a law (2) · says the useful question is what function the outlier serves (2) · gives a concrete example of a legitimate departure (1)
> The cycle describes what most progressions do, which is exactly what makes departures audible. A progression that breaks it isn't wrong; it's doing something the ear notices. The move is to label what each chord is actually doing and see which expectation got skipped — a I going straight to V drops the departure and sounds abrupt or urgent, while a loop that never leaves tonic function feels static on purpose. The cycle is a reference to measure against, not a target to hit.

# 101 music theory terms you should know

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZjKyQ7cUlO8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZjKyQ7cUlO8)

## Synopsis

A rapid-fire vocabulary glossary — 101 music theory terms defined in sequence, each briefly illustrated with a song example, framed as expanding a musician's shared "language" for describing what they hear. Broad terminology coverage, largely already documented (often in more depth, with more worked examples) across the vault's `Theory/` folder.

## What's taught

A representative sample of the terms covered (definitions largely match, and are less detailed than, existing vault notes): conjunct/disjunct melody, scale, semitone/tone, interval, consonance/dissonance, tritone, chord/triad, tertiary vs. quartal harmony, arpeggio, riff vs. ostinato vs. motif, sequence, diminution/augmentation (as applied to melodies, intervals, *and* chords — the video flags "diminished"/"augmented" as genuinely overloaded terms with distinct meanings in each context), melodic and chordal inversion, modulation, cadence (authentic/perfect cadence specifically), tonicization vs. full modulation, secondary dominant, transposition — plus many further terms (dynamics, articulation, form labels, notation vocabulary, etc.) covered later in the video but not itemized here.

One point worth flagging for future reference: the video explicitly calls out **"diminished"** and **"augmented"** as terms with three distinct musical meanings depending on context — a melody/rhythm (halving/doubling note values), an interval (shrinking/widening by a semitone), or a chord (built from a diminished/augmented interval) — worth remembering as a source of confusion when reading theory discussion elsewhere.

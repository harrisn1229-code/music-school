# All The Things You Are — Jerome Kern

> A 36-bar AABA-shaped jazz standard that modulates through **four key centers** (A♭ → C → E♭/G → E → back to A♭) — more modulation than almost any other tune in the standard repertoire, which makes it the canonical teaching vehicle for pivot-chord analysis and for the "modulate up a major 3rd" device. Full worked breakdown: [[Jens Larsen - All The Things You Are - Harmonic Analysis]].

## Form and key map

| Section | Bars | Key center | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 8 | A♭ major → C major | 1-6-2-5-style turnaround, then a G7 pivot resolves UP to C major instead of down to the expected Cm |
| A2 | 8 | E♭ major → G major | Same shape as A1, transposed up a step; C7 pivots up to G major |
| Bridge | 8 | G major → E major | 2-5-1 in G, then a deceptive ii-V that resolves to E major instead of the expected vi (E minor) |
| A3 | 12 | E major → A♭ major (stays) | One-bar pivot (E major → C7alt) drops back into F minor/A♭ major; extended +4 bars into a tag/coda ending, no further modulation |

## Chords

*As analyzed, not the literal real-book voicings — see note below on triads.*

**A2** — `A♭m7 – D♭m7 – G♭7 – B♭maj7 – C7(pivot) – E♭maj7 (→ hints Cm)`
**Bridge** — `Am7 – D7 – Gmaj7 – F♯ø7 – E major (deceptive resolution) – [Emaj7 → C7alt, one-bar pivot]`
**A3 (coda, extended)** — `Fm7 – B♭m7 – E♭7 – A♭maj7 – D♭maj7(IV) – D♭m6/G♭7(iv-minor) – Cm7(iii) – ♭3°/♯4° dim – B♭m7♭5(ii) – E♭7alt(V) – A♭maj7(I)`

## Chord-scale map for soloing (from the video, A♭-major reading)

| Chord | Scale |
|---|---|
| A♭ tonic material (Fm7-B♭m7-E♭7-A♭maj7) | A♭ major throughout |
| G7 / C7 (pivot chords) | C / G harmonic minor (♭9/♭13, "smooth" reading) — or Lydian-then-half-step-down as an alternate color on the landing major chord |
| Bridge (G major, then F♯ø7-E) | G major scale; F♯ half-diminished as a straight minor ii-V |
| C7alt → Fm (bridge's one-bar pivot) | F harmonic minor |
| D♭m6/G♭7 (iv-minor in the coda) | D♭ melodic minor |
| ♭3°/♯4° diminished (coda) | C harmonic minor, derived by ear from A♭ major (swap B♭→B, D♭→D) — enharmonically ≈ A♭ harmonic major |

## Steal this

- **Confirm the key from the ENDING (final cadence + final melody note), never from the opening chord** — the tune opens on what sounds like a tonic-minor (Fm7) but the real key is A♭ major, confirmed only by where it actually resolves. → [[Keys]], [[Cadences]]
- **"Modulate up a major 3rd" is a reusable, recognizable device** — worth having in the toolkit for both analysis (spot it fast in other tunes) and composition/reharmonization. → [[Modulation (Key Change)]]
- **When a pivot chord is genuinely ambiguous, pick the reading that puts the surprise at the resolution, not at the dominant** — an altered dominant already signals "modulating," so save the "aha" for the landing chord. → [[Modulation (Key Change)]]
- **A single altered chord tone can flip a stable tonic triad into a functioning altered dominant for a fast one-bar key exit** (E major → lower the 5th → functions as C7alt → resolves to F minor). → [[Chord Substitution]] § *Tritone substitution*
- **The ♭3°/♯4° diminished before a final ii-V is a recurring, teachable device, not a one-off** — same chord/melody-note relationship also appears in "Stella by Starlight" and "I Remember You." → [[Diminished Chords]]
- **Bare triad voicings (not extended chords) can be a deliberate ambiguity tool** — Kern's own coda opens in plain triads specifically so the F-minor/A♭-major ambiguity survives a couple of bars before resolving; voicing the tonic minor as a m6 chord (the "normal" jazz habit) would kill that ambiguity outright. → [[Chord Voicings]]

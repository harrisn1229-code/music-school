# the secrets behind sza's 'sos' production!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qbkFwpExtVQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qbkFwpExtVQ)

## Synopsis

loner reverse-engineers SZA's *SOS* by building three original beats, each one inspired by a different track on the album rather than a literal chain reveal of SZA's actual sessions. **Beat 1 ("Blind"-inspired R&B guitar beat)** builds a two-layer clean-guitar chain — a dry signal chain (EQ → compressor → spring reverb → slap delay → flanger → shaper box) plus a second reamped pass through a Montreal Assembly Count to 5 pedal (shaper box → EQ → reverb → pan) layered on top as an ambient texture — then borrows *Blind*'s hook-vocal formant-shift character for a vocal-chop treatment (Little AlterBoy with the formant knob automated, finished with a sample-rate drop + duplicate + time-stretch), a real bass guitar, a seaside ambient texture + chopped perc loop, and boom-bap drums with a "shift your drum sounds" swing tip citing *Snooze* and *Open Arms*. **Beat 2 ("Seek and Destroy"-inspired, darker)** is built from a mono synth-chord part panned right against a piano panned left, glitchy Arturia Analog Lab "Technicolor" synth textures lifted from a separate Dahi livestream breakdown of *Used* (chopped and pitched down an octave — see [[Dahi - SZA Used Beat Breakdown]]), and a hybrid bassline that mixes real bass-guitar parts with an added synth-bass layer for a darker vibe. **Beat 3 (a *Used*/"Smoking on My Ex"-adjacent flip)** recreates the Webster Lewis "Open Up Your Eyes" sample that underlies *SOS*'s rap-heavy tracks — a Rhodes-style element with a matched ascending chord progression and bounce, bongos/tambourine/light rim-shot percussion, an electric guitar hit run through a Kontakt "Evolution Strawberry" bank and an AmpliTube amp sim for a washed-out texture, and vintage-leaning vocal processing (AlterBoy → reverb → Waves King's Microphones → EQ), finished with a Jay Versace–style time-stretch chop and 808-first drum programming (the chord bounce dictates the 808 pattern) plus a kick borrowed from *Forgiveness*. Most valuable overall: the two-layer clean/reamped guitar chain as a reusable ambient-texture recipe, and the repeated "borrow the processing/percussion character of the actual sample, not just the melody" discipline across all three beats.

## Techniques

**Beat 1 — "Blind"-inspired R&B guitar beat:**
- **Two-layer guitar chain: a dry processed signal + a separately reamped ambient-texture layer, blended together.** Layer 1 (dry): clean guitar → EQ → compressor → **spring reverb** → short **slap delay** → flanger → shaper box. Layer 2 (texture): the *same guitar part* re-run through a **Montreal Assembly Count to 5** hardware pedal set to delay/detune the signal out of time, then shaper box → EQ → reverb → pan (**PanMan**), and the result is layered on top of Layer 1 as an ambient wash rather than replacing it. *(general starting point — no exact EQ/comp/reverb/delay settings given)* → [[EQ Eight]], [[Compressor]], [[Reverb]] (effect), [[Delay]] (effect), [[Modulation]], [[PanMan]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], new chain: [[SZA R&B Guitar Chain]]
- **Automate the AlterBoy Formant knob up/down (pitch untouched) to recreate a "woozy" hook-vocal character.** Borrowed from *Blind*'s hook vocals, which sound like an automated formant shifter — not a static setting. Record vocal chops, run through Little AlterBoy, and automate the Formant control moving over time while leaving Pitch alone, so the voice wobbles in timbre without going out of tune. → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Finish a vocal chop with sample-rate reduction, then duplicate + time-stretch for a faster, lo-fi-thickened result.** After the AlterBoy formant-automation pass: lower the sample rate (bitcrush-style), duplicate the chop, and time-stretch the duplicate to make it play faster — layering the stretched dupe back under/against the original for density. *(general starting point — no exact sample-rate/stretch amount given)* → [[kHs Bitcrush]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **A real bass guitar under the guitar chain**, rather than a synth or sampled bass — keeps the R&B beat's low end organic to match the clean-guitar character above it. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Two textural layers finishing the sample: a "seaside" ambient texture + a chopped percussion loop.** A seaside/ocean-adjacent ambient texture sits under the beat for place/mood, alongside a separately chopped perc loop — two different textural ingredients doing different jobs (mood vs. rhythmic ear candy). → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **"Shift your drum sounds" for natural boom-bap swing** — cited directly against *Snooze* and *Open Arms* as the reference feel: nudge individual drum-hit timing/placement off the strict grid rather than leaving everything quantized, for the same natural, not-quite-mechanical swing SZA's own boom-bap tracks have. *(general starting point — no exact ms/swing-% given)* → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]]
- **Two kicks layered, plus hi-hats, to finish the drum kit.** → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[2 Drums Group]]

**Beat 2 — "Seek and Destroy"-inspired (darker beat):**
- **Mono synth chords panned hard right, piano panned hard left, as the whole stereo picture for a chord/melody pairing.** Rather than keeping both harmonic elements centered or both spread, one part is deliberately mono and thrown to one side while the second part occupies the other — a cheap way to fit two harmonic layers without them fighting for the same center-stage space. *(general starting point — no exact pan values given)* → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Glitchy Analog Lab "Technicolor" synth textures, lifted from a separate producer's livestream breakdown of a different song on the same album ("Used"), then chopped and pitched down an octave.** loner names the source directly: Dahi's own livestream breakdown of *Used* (already logged on [[Dahi - SZA Used Beat Breakdown]]) showed "reflection"-style glitchy synth hits layered over the initial sound — recreated here by opening **Arturia Analog Lab**, browsing into the **Technicolor** preset bank, picking a preset, laying down the melody, then chopping it and pitching the result down an octave for a darker, less immediately-recognizable texture. → [[Dahi - SZA Used Beat Breakdown]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A hybrid bassline: write the whole part on real bass guitar first, then swap select sections to synth bass for a darker vibe, keeping some of the original bass-guitar parts.** Not a full replacement — a composite built by A/B'ing which sections read better as synth vs. real bass and mixing the two sources within one part. → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Bass Design]]
- **Jumpy percussion built from a simple delay-fed hi-hat pattern + separate percs, then a snare/tom layer, then a kick + extra snare.** A layered build order (hats+delay → percs → snare/tom → kick+snare) rather than one full kit dropped in at once. → [[Delay]] (effect), [[Layering and Stacking]]

**Beat 3 — *Used* / "Smoking on My Ex"-adjacent flip of Webster Lewis's "Open Up Your Eyes":**
- **Match the sampled record's own chord bounce, not just its notes, when rebuilding a flip from scratch.** The main element of the Webster Lewis sample is a Rhodes-style electric piano; loner used a similar **ascending chord progression** and specifically matched the **same bounce/rhythmic feel** of the original chords — called out as important because that same chord bounce later dictates the 808 pattern's groove (see drums, below). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Replicate the sample's actual percussion character (bongos, tambourine, a very light rim shot) rather than defaulting to a generic kit.** Listening back to what's actually audible in the sampled percussion and rebuilding those specific instruments/hit character, instead of substituting a standard drum-kit percussion layer. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **An electric guitar hit sourced from a Kontakt "Evolution Strawberry" bank, then processed through an amp sim (AmpliTube) for a washed-out texture.** The guitar hit is called out as "a very important element of the sample" — sourced from a specific Kontakt library bank rather than a live guitar take, then run through an amp simulator specifically for the washed-out/degraded coloring, not for a clean amp tone. No AmpliTube owned — closest tracked equivalent for the same amp-sim role: [[Guitar Rig 7 FX]] (also unowned, demo-only on this machine). → [[Kontakt 7]], [[Guitar Rig 7 FX]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Vintage vocal chain: AlterBoy → reverb → Waves King's Microphones → EQ.** A four-stage vocal chain aimed at a vintage/period-accurate character to match the sampled source — Little AlterBoy first, then reverb, then a mic-character emulation plugin (captioned "King's Microphone," near-certainly **Waves King's Microphones** — already tracked twice on [[Plugin Wishlist]] for the same near-identical caption), then a final EQ pass. → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Reverb]] (effect), [[EQ]] (effect), [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **A second, heavier time-stretch chop pass on the finished sample, referencing Jay Versace's chop style on "Smoking on My Ex."** After the chain above already sounds finished, loner deliberately stretches the sample further using a stretch/warp control for an additional slowed-down, warped character — named as a specific stylistic reference to how Jay Versace chopped the same sample/song. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **808 programmed first, because the chord bounce (matched above) dictates the 808's rhythmic pattern.** Direct payoff of matching the sample's chord bounce earlier: since the bounce is already locked in, the 808 pattern follows from it rather than being written independently. → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **A kick borrowed from a different SOS track ("Forgiveness," the album's outro) for this beat**, paired with a heavy boom-bap-style snare and a finishing hi-hat. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Kick Design]]

# Stevie Wonder's REAL Superstition Bass | Minimoog vs Moog IIIc

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lEktjvpNfac)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lEktjvpNfac)

## Synopsis

A side-by-side patch recreation of "Superstition" on both the Moog 3C (Moog Modular / TONTO) and the Minimoog — with an important correction from TONTO's own builders (Malcolm Cecil and Robert Margouleff) that no Minimoog was ever used on Stevie Wonder's classic-period albums, only the 3C. Also covers the story behind TONTO's custom pitch/mod joystick controller.

## Techniques

- **Historical correction: only the Moog 3C, never a Minimoog, appears on Stevie Wonder's classic four albums** (*Talking Book*, *Innervisions*, *Fulfillingness' First Finale*, *Songs in the Key of Life*) — per TONTO's own builders. The 3C's harmonic character is described as genuinely different from a Minimoog's.
- **Deliberately drive EVERY gain stage past unity for saturation, not just a dedicated distortion module** — on modular hardware, the mixer stage saturates heavily once pushed past roughly 70% of its range, and driving the following output/master gain stage further compounds it; demonstrated stacking saturation stage by stage rather than relying on one drive knob.
- **Blend in a sine wave from the SAME oscillator generating the main waveform** (the CS-80/Wyoming-style trick already documented elsewhere) for extra low-end weight with no separate tuning risk.
- **Modular "normals" are worth knowing about when patching**: some connections (here, envelope generator → VCA) work with NO patch cord because the instrument's internal wiring defaults to that connection — patching it explicitly just makes the routing visible/teachable, it doesn't change the sound.
- **Keyboard control voltage dialed via an attenuator, not just on/off**, to get brightness increasing only in the upper register while low notes stay consistently bass-y — a continuously adjustable middle ground between full tracking and none.
- **The TONTO joystick**: Malcolm Cecil and Robert Margouleff built Stevie a custom expressive pitch/mod controller from a repurposed model-airplane RC joystick, wiring voltage controllers inside and adding rubber bands so it self-centers/snaps back to zero on release — one axis for pitch bend, the other for filter cutoff — specifically so Stevie could bend notes the way blues/R&B phrasing requires ("you can't play the blues if you can't bend the notes"). Audible on "Boogie On Reggae Woman." A DIY precursor to a modern expressive controller, built from a hobbyist RC part decades before MPE existed.
- On the Minimoog side, the same patch simplifies to the now-familiar square+sawtooth-in-unison recipe (see [[Bass Design]]), closed filter with contour+decay shaping, and oscillator-3 vibrato with tracking off — confirming the formula translates across instruments even though the 3C's saturation character doesn't.

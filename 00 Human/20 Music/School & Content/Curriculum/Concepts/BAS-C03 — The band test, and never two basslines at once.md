# BAS-C03 — The band test, and never two basslines at once

**Q.** Give the sanity check on a written line, and the one hard rule about layering bass.

> [!question]- Answer
> **The band test** — imagine a bassist physically playing it; if no player would phrase it that way, rewrite it (unless the unplayable line *is* the point, e.g. reversed audio). **The hard rule:** never run two genuinely different basslines simultaneously. Chords and melody tolerate multiple simultaneous parts; two distinct low-end melodic lines clash and read as mud rather than richness. **Doubling the same line** on two instruments is a different thing entirely and is fine.

## Why it matters

The band test is the same four-limb constraint that governs realistic drum programming, applied to one player instead of a kit. The layering rule is where most muddy low ends actually come from — not EQ, arrangement.

## Drill

Play your busiest bassline and try to physically play it, or air-play it. Then solo your low end and count how many distinct melodic lines are running.

# The Most Misunderstood Knob On Your Synth

## Synopsis

A focused demonstration of the resonance knob's real behavior on a subtractive synth — not just "make it sweep sing," but the specific ways resonance can be used deliberately: as a self-oscillating pitch source, as a percussive transient generator when paired with a fast envelope, and as a way to build a fixed vocal-like formant on a bass patch without any filter-envelope modulation at all. Anthony Marinelli's own "Think Like a Synth" course material, condensed to one control.

## Techniques

- **Resonance thins the bass as it rises — a byproduct, not a bug.** Boosting resonance emphasizes the cutoff frequency at the direct expense of the fundamental underneath it; sweeping the filter with high resonance makes the moving cutoff peak louder than the main signal, audibly thinning the tone as resonance climbs.
- **Pushed far enough, the filter self-oscillates into a pure sine tone with no oscillator input at all** — tune that self-oscillating pitch to unison with the main oscillator for a resonant "whistle" layered on top of the patched sound, or remove the main oscillator entirely to hear the filter as its own bare pitched source.
- **Self-oscillation + a very short envelope-generator decay on the filter = a Hammond-style percussive "pluck" on every note** — a fast decay time with the EG routed to the filter gives each note a sharp, tuned transient; softening the EG's attack time (instead of leaving it instant) turns that same pluck into a softer, more "talking" character rather than a hard hit.
- **Resonance can build a fixed FORMANT with zero filter-envelope modulation** — set cutoff and resonance manually to park the resonant peak at a vowel-like frequency and leave it there (no EG amount into the filter at all); this produces a static, vocal-quality tone rather than the more common swept-filter "wah" sound, and it can be moved live by hand for an expressive, human-timed "talking" effect that an automated envelope can't replicate exactly.
- **Bass-formant technique, keeping tone consistent note-to-note**: rather than shaping tone with VCA envelope sustain, set the VCA sustain to a constant level and instead park the filter cutoff (small or no EG amount) at a fixed formant frequency — the bass then keeps the same tonal "vowel" regardless of how long a note is held.
- **Adding a small amount of "bite" without breaking the fixed formant**: let the envelope generator control ONLY the attack portion of the filter (a brief upward blip at the start of the note), while the sustain stage stays parked at the same manually-set formant frequency — this avoids the "robotic" sameness of a bass patch where every note follows an identical full envelope shape, while still keeping a stable, recognizable tonal core.

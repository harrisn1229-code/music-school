# Blues lick you've heard and might LOVE playing

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q0VJux8IEus)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q0VJux8IEus)

## Synopsis

A short, practical lick lesson (the "Brown Eyed Girl" 6ths-interval lick) that's really a worked application of the diatonic-6th interval mapped onto three CAGED-family chord shapes (G, C, D), each shown with its underlying chord-tone/passing-tone structure so the lick can be transposed and targeted deliberately rather than memorized as one fixed pattern. A direct real-world companion to the deeper theoretical drill in [[Mapping 6ths Across the Five CAGED Positions]] — same interval, same author, opposite pedagogical direction (here: start from a recognizable lick and reverse-engineer the theory; there: build the theory first, then improvise).

## What's taught

- **The lick's harmonic skeleton: two notes a 6th apart, alternating between two adjacent chord tones with a chromatic passing tone in between.** Worked concretely on a **G major** bar-chord shape (E-shape barred, 3rd position): top string frets 3-5-7 are scale degrees 1-2-3 of the key, with fret 5 (the "2") functioning as a **passing tone**, not a chord tone — landing on frets 3 and 7 (root and 3rd) gives the lick's stable, "holdable" notes. Simultaneously, on the 3rd string, frets 4 and 7 are the chord's 3rd and 5th, with fret 5 as the passing tone between them — the two voices move in parallel 6ths throughout.
- **The same lick reproduced on C (A-shape, 3rd position) and D (A-shape moved up), via identical chord-tone/passing-tone logic** — demonstrating the lick is a movable interval-relationship, not a fixed set of frets: locate the 5th and 3rd of the target chord a 6th apart, fill the gap between them with the chromatic passing tone, and the lick transfers to any CAGED-family shape once you can find those two chord tones by ear/fretboard knowledge.
- **A second full derivation on the same three chords using 5-3 (rather than 3-1) as the chord-tone pair** — worked with scale-degree counting (whole step down from 5 is 4, half step from 4 is 3; whole step down from 3 is 2, half step from 2 is 1) to show the passing-tone insertion rule applies uniformly regardless of which two chord tones the lick targets.
- **I-IV-V application:** demonstrated as a usable phrase over a G-C-D (I-IV-V) vamp for jamming/composing, plus an explicit note that the same 6th-interval shape recurs an octave apart in two separate physical positions for G specifically — worth learning both.
- **Practical fingering note:** each of the three chord positions requires a slightly different physical fingering for the identical interval relationship because of the guitar's non-uniform (mostly-4ths, one-3rd) string tuning — the *sound* and interval math stay constant even though the *shape* changes chord to chord, the same shape-vs-function distinction documented on [[Chord Inversions]].

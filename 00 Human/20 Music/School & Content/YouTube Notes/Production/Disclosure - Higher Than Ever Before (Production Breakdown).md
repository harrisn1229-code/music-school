# Disclosure - Higher Than Ever Before (Production Breakdown)

Guy breaks down his first jungle attempt, made in LA with writer Simon "Circuit" Aldred. The standout content is a deep, practical passage on **vocal air vs de-essing** — the single best de-essing tutorial across these six videos — plus a clear explanation of **frequency-specific sidechain with Pro-MB and Soothe** (duck only the sub; make space by ducking exactly where the vocal sits), and the famous **George Michael inverted-de-esser-to-reverb** trick. Also strong on chopping breaks and choosing synths whose harmonics fit the key. High caption confidence.

## Techniques
- **Air vs de-essing is a balance you ride, not one move.** Record with too much treble (S's hurt) — it's easier to take away than to add, and boosting absent highs boosts mic noise + phasing. Chain (all working the [[AIR Slot]] of the [[LEAD Slot]] vocal): **[[Gem Dopamine]]** (boost highs) → immediately de-ess → **[[Soothe2]]** (harshness) → boost again (Chandler). Like mastering, it's the stages *leading up*, not one hard de-ess. *(De-ess range 3–6 dB centered ~6–9 kHz; Soothe focus 5–10 kHz; keep net air by re-lifting after taming.)* → [[De-essing]].
- **Manual de-essing beats any de-esser** — a de-esser always adds a lisp when pushed. Chop and lower each loud S by hand (−a couple dB). Only bother once committed (album stage). Ed Sheeran records word-by-word and turns every S down manually. Take Six singers de-ess *themselves* live.
- **The whisper trick:** record a subtle whisper layer, line it up so it doesn't read as a double, then de-ess the main vocal hard and let the whisper carry the S's/air — great for airy voices.
- **Make room in the arrangement:** busy tambourines/shakers/hats crowd the [[AIR Slot]] the vocal needs — roll off some drum highs (gentle shelf above ~8–10 kHz on the hats/drum bus) to make space. Air vs body is the trade. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[AIR Slot]].
- **Dry saturation on the high end** ([[Saturn 2]]) adds air with fewer phasing issues than an EQ boost.
- **Record airy through a 500-series "fake Neve"/Maag Air Band** with treble/AIR up, then control the S's after.
- **Frequency-specific sidechain ([[Pro-MB]]):** duck *only the [[SUB Slot]] band* of a bassline off the kick and leave the [[BASS Slot]] detail above; on the drum bus, just dip the low-mids. He's since moved to **[[Soothe2]] (dynamic)** for precision. *(Pro-MB: one band < ~120 Hz, key = kick, 3–4:1, fast attack, 3–6 dB GR.)* → [[Multiband Compression]], [[Sidechaining]].
- **Make space by ducking where the vocal sits:** on the synth bus, [[Soothe2]] + a compressor keyed off the vocal so the [[BED Slot]] synths move out of the [[LEAD Slot]] vocal's way. Send the lead vocal to the sidechain of pads/sustained harmonic stuff — a pop staple. → [[Sidechaining]].
- **George Michael reverb trick:** *invert* a de-esser and use it to drive a reverb *send* — the S's trigger the reverb like a crash cymbal (the "Older"/"Jesus to a Child" sound).
- **Synth/sound design:** the choppy "choir" is the writer singing the whole chromatic scale so his voice plays as a synth, then **LFO Tool** drawing a volume-envelope chop per note ("make it do what I sing" — hum the rhythm, draw the graph). **Ring Shifter** (Logic frequency shifter) for character. **Trilian** bass for the best front-end transient (moog VSTs miss the first transient; Monark/Arturia close). **Logic FM synth** for the lead because it gives *major* harmonics that fit — pick oscillators whose harmonic content matches the key. **ShaperBox** distortion automation to open the cutoff into noise for excitement.
- **Vocal panning:** 5 tracks — 1 mono center, 2 slightly L/R (~±30), 2 hard L/R; mute the first four bars so it widens/pops as it progresses. → [[Stereo Field]], [[LEAD Slot]].
- **Bounce vocals into Serato Sample and chop** to build a fresher post-chorus.
- **Recording philosophy:** record through great gear (500-series Neve/LA-2A copies, Maag Air Band, SSL 6 mixer), then keep it in the box; run a Juno through something lush on the way in for character.
- **Tremolo trend:** 16th-note vocal tremolo is everywhere; he's moved to 8th notes.

# I Want You (She's So Heavy) — The Beatles

> Lennon's obsessive, repetitive refrain over a crushing groove. Second section builds with white noise generated by the Moog synthesizer. Ends with an abrupt mid-phrase tape cut — one of the heaviest Beatles recordings.

## Chords
**Key:** A minor (D minor in the heavy riff sections)

**Verse ("I want you so bad"):** Am7 – C – D – F – G – E7/G♯ – Am7
→ **i7 – ♭III – IV – ♭VI – ♭VII – V7 – i** (a climbing line capped by the **E7** dominant)

**Riff / "It's driving me mad":** Dm – Dm/F – E7♭9 – C5 – B5 – B♭7 – Aaug
→ a chromatic descent (D–C–B–B♭–A) over the famous heavy guitar figure, landing on the **E7♭9** dominant.

## Production & Arrangement
Hypnotic and minimal — 14 words of lyric over nearly 8 minutes, built entirely on **repetition + dynamics**. A massive layered **white-noise/wind crescendo** (multiple Moog + tape noise passes) builds for minutes until the track **cuts off dead mid-note** — Lennon's famous "cut it there" edit, one of the most abrupt endings in pop.

- Heavy, bluesy, Leslie/tremolo-drenched guitars; the arrangement swells and contracts rather than "developing."
- The **abrupt tape cut** replaces a fade — silence as a shock.

## Steal this
- **Tension through repetition:** ride one riff/loop and build with *layers + dynamics*, not new parts.
- **Growing noise bed:** stack white-noise/riser passes ([[ValhallaSupermassive]] / noise + automation) into a wall, then...
- **Hard cut ending:** end dead on a downbeat, no fade — the silence is the hook.
- **Minimal lyric as mantra** — a few repeated words can carry a long track.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — I Want You (She's So Heavy)]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #182.*

**Form.** Not a medley — **two separate songs cross-cut cinematically**. One supplies the verses; the other supplies the intro, the refrains and the enormous outro. The separation is engineered on three independent axes: **meter** (squared-off 4/4 with a jazzy backbeat in the verses, slow cranking 6/8 everywhere else), **texture** (full ensemble behind a lead vocal versus a bass ostinato with slow guitar arpeggios), and **lyric perspective** (the verses address her directly, the refrains talk about her in the third person). The intro is **five bars** and every non-repeated appearance ends with a grand pause, so it plays closer to six; the refrains are AA doublings of it, ten bars; the verse reads as ABA′B′ but parses into deliberately unequal phrases of **8½ + 2 + 8½ + 6**, the limping half-bar a Lennon signature. The outro runs just under fourteen consecutive iterations of the intro phrase.

**Harmony.** `Intro (5): i (2) – V/V (E9) – VI (B♭7) – V+ (A augmented)` · `Verse, first half [in A minor]: i (6) · i – III – VII6/4 – IV – V (half bar) · i – I (= V of D)` · `Verse, second half [in D minor]: i (6) · i – III – VII – VI – chromatic climb · V/V (E9, held 6)`. The intro lands on **another augmented V**, echoing "Oh! Darling" two tracks earlier, and its five-bar length plus a **V-of-V whose resolution is deferred** ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]) is exactly what lets the phrase loop fourteen times without closing. An **F natural sustained as a common tone** runs straight through the chord changes, and he hears the bass figure as a ground bass in the passacaglia sense rather than a rock riff.

**Notable.** The tune is **minor pentatonic** ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]) with the alternate phrases leaning on **flatted fifth and seventh** ([[BLU-C06 — Blue notes and the blues scale]]). He sets the track against "Hey Jude": both split in golden-section proportion, but here the coda material is exposed from bar 1 and already heard twice as a refrain, and the weight sits on the *first* half — which is the only thing keeping the riff from overexposure.

**Against this page.** A real disagreement. **Pollack reads the home key as D minor**, with the verses *starting* in A minor (v) and converging home from there. This page reads A minor with D minor in the heavy sections — the inverse. Nothing above has been changed.

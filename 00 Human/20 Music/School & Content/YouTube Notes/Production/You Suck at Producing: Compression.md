# You Suck at Producing: Compression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bUyiL3KmZDI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bUyiL3KmZDI)

## Synopsis
A beginner-friendly walkthrough of Ableton's stock Compressor on a drum loop, covering all four core controls (threshold, attack, release, ratio) with audible before/after demos for each, plus makeup gain and the compressor's own Dry/Wet knob as a built-in parallel-compression control.

## Techniques
- **Lower the threshold until the gain-reduction meter actually moves** — a compressor sitting above a source's peak level does nothing at all; the threshold has to be pulled down into the signal's actual level range before the gain-reduction indicator (the needle/meter showing how much the compressor is turning things down) starts responding.
- **Attack = how long the compressor waits after crossing threshold before it starts clamping** — demoed at 3 ms vs. 0.01 ms: the near-zero attack setting reacted to the drum hits far more aggressively and sounded noticeably more extreme/compressed.
- **Release = how long the compressor waits after dropping back below threshold before it lets go** — demoed slow vs. fast: a slower release meant the gain-reduction meter took much longer to recover after each hit. For a rhythmically fast source like a drum loop, a faster, more responsive release keeps the compressor from still clamping down when the next hit arrives.
- **Ratio sets how much of the signal above threshold gets let through** — at a ratio of 2:1, for every 2 dB the signal goes over threshold, only 1 dB comes out; cranked up toward 10:1, it gets extreme and heavily squashed.
- **The stock Compressor's Dry/Wet knob is a built-in parallel-compression control** — full compression can sound like too much; pulling Dry/Wet back to around 50% blends the compressed and uncompressed signal together for a punchier-but-not-squashed middle ground, without needing a separate parallel bus/return track. → [[Parallel Processing]]
- **Makeup gain restores the level lost to compression** — after turning down the loud parts, add makeup gain back in so the overall part isn't quieter than before, just more even.

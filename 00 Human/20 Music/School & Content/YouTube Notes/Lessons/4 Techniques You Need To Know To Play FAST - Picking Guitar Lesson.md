# 4 Techniques You Need To Know To Play FAST | Picking Guitar Lesson

## Synopsis

A head-to-head comparison of four ways to play the identical lick — alternate picking, economy picking, hybrid picking, and legato-style hammer-ons — recorded back to back on the same notes/phrasing so the tonal and mechanical differences are directly audible, with the advantages/disadvantages and getting-started drill for each named in turn.

## What's taught

- **Alternate picking, demonstrated failure-first.** A badly-synchronized fast take (right hand not locked to left-hand notes, no subdivision awareness) shown as a cautionary example before the fix: small, basic 3-note patterns to a metronome, focused on individual-note accuracy, gradually sped up without sacrificing cleanliness. Named as the foundational technique everything else compares against.
- **String-transition picking-angle detail, worked on a real lick.** For a specific E-A-D string-crossing lick: the E-to-A transition is comfortable (ends on a downstroke, naturally positions for the upstroke); the A-to-D transition is the hard one (ends on an upstroke on the A string, forcing the pick back above the A string before the D-string downstroke) — the single hardest moment in the phrase, and the reason picking-angle mastery (switching between two different angles depending on which stroke ends which string) is the real skill beneath "alternate picking."
- **Economy picking — see full mechanic on the dedicated [[Economy Picking]] note.** This video's specific framing: picked up only recently at the time of filming, still not fully mastered, but already noticeably smoother on the video's demonstrated lick than alternate picking's angle-switching problem.
- **Hybrid picking (pick + middle finger).** Downstroke with the pick on one string, then pluck the next string with the middle finger, repeated per string — learned from a Tom Quayle workshop. Both motions must carry equal dynamics/attack (no louder pick strike vs. quieter finger pluck) for the technique to sound smooth rather than uneven.
- **Legato (continuous downstrokes + hammer-ons, no finger-picking).** Same picking-and-fretting shape as the hybrid-picking example but with the second note of each string hammered on instead of finger-plucked — a smoother, softer variant good for fast, fluid-sounding lines; common in fast shred lines specifically for its legato smoothness.
- **Cross-technique quality bar, stated once and applying to all four:** every note must be clearly audible and equally articulated regardless of which technique produced it — the listener should not be able to hear where string transitions happen or which notes were picked vs. fretted-only.

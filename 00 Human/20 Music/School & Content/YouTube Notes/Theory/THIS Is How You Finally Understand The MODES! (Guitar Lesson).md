# THIS Is How You Finally Understand The MODES! (Guitar Lesson)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fBSiTdQB80)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fBSiTdQB80)

## Synopsis

A deliberately narrowed modes lesson — only Aeolian (natural minor) vs. Phrygian, a single one-note comparison — building to the video's real payoff: a practical, ear-based method (with an audible "wrong scale over wrong riff" demonstration) for deciding which mode a riff actually calls for, plus the caution that even a scale-correct note can sound bad if used carelessly.

## What's taught

- **One-note relationship, restated (see [[Harmonic Minor Modes]] and other batch entries for the same "change one note" pattern applied elsewhere).** E Aeolian/natural minor vs. E Phrygian differ in exactly one note: Aeolian's major 2nd (F♯) becomes Phrygian's minor 2nd (F) — everything else identical. Recommends learning the fundamental Ionian/Aeolian scale in one octave first (not a scale box), memorizing the actual notes and whole-step/half-step structure, then reaching every other mode (except Locrian, which changes more than one note from either fundamental) via single-note edits.
- **Identify which mode a riff calls for by finding its characteristic note in the backing chords/riff, not by ear-preference for a scale's general sound.** Worked with two E-rooted metal power-chord riffs: riff 1 built from E-F♯-G power chords (F♯ = Aeolian's major 2nd) calls for natural minor; riff 2 built from E-F-G power chords (F = Phrygian's minor 2nd) calls for Phrygian. Playing the "wrong" scale audibly clashes — demonstrated directly: Phrygian's F over the F♯-containing riff, and natural minor's F♯ over the F-containing riff, both sound "terrible"/dissonant in an unintended way. **Key takeaway stated directly: the chords/riff in the background dictate the correct scale choice — you can't pick a mode just because you personally like its sound**, and neither scale is a universal "safe" default.
- **Being theoretically correct doesn't guarantee sounding good — a caution about dissonant scale degrees.** Even when playing the objectively "correct" scale (Phrygian over the Phrygian-flavored riff), constantly landing/accenting the scale's own most dissonant note (the ♭2, plus a mention of the ♭6) still sounds bad if overused without care — these notes are legitimate tension/color tools, not notes to lean on by default just because they're technically in-key.

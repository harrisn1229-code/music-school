# What's the Difference Between Distortion and Saturation?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JbkPe6mXhts)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JbkPe6mXhts)

## Synopsis

A terms-clarification video: saturation = a nonlinear (soft-knee compression + harmonic distortion) response that occurs when an electrical component is overloaded past what it can reproduce linearly; distortion is the broader umbrella (any alteration of a waveform's shape), of which harmonic distortion from saturation is just the most commonly-meant form. The most reusable content is a direct character comparison of tube, transistor, and tape saturation.

## Techniques

- **A precise definition: saturation = soft-knee nonlinear compression + harmonic distortion, both caused by the same overload mechanism.** When an electrical component (transistor, tube) receives more signal than it can reproduce linearly, the input/output relationship becomes nonlinear — functionally identical to a compressor's ratio (1:1 while linear, shifting toward e.g. 2:1 or 4:1 as overload increases), and specifically SOFT knee (the ratio gradually intensifies with more overload rather than snapping to a fixed ratio at a threshold). The same overload that causes this compression also distorts the waveform's shape, generating harmonics (multiples of the original/fundamental frequency, e.g. a 200 Hz tone saturating a tube generating harmonics at 400 Hz and 600 Hz — 2nd and 3rd order). → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Compression]]
- **Distortion is the broader category — harmonic distortion (from saturation) is just the most commonly-meant form; other named forms: noise, intermodulation distortion, phase distortion/cancellation, and bit-depth distortion.** Technically almost any audio processing alters a waveform's shape in some way and could be called "distortion" in the broadest sense — even the cleanest digital processing introduces some form of it — but in everyday mixing language "distortion" nearly always means harmonic distortion specifically. Useful as vocabulary when a problem doesn't fit the harmonic-distortion mental model (e.g. intermodulation or phase-cancellation artifacts need different fixes than a harmonic-distortion problem would). → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **A direct character comparison: tube = strong, full, lower-pitched 2nd-order-dominant; transistor = mid-to-high-order, brighter, cuts through a busy mix; tape = magnetic-particle-based (not electrical-component overload) and often compounds with tube/transistor saturation from its own recording-chain amplifiers.** Tube saturation's dominant 2nd-order harmonic reads as an almost-doubling of the original signal, at a lower pitch than higher-order harmonics would produce — the source of tube's "full" character. Transistor saturation leans mid-to-high-order, reading brighter/more defined and more apparent in a busy mix — a useful pick when something needs to cut through rather than sit warm and glued. Tape saturation works via a different physical mechanism entirely (magnetic particles reorienting once fully saturated, not an electrical component overloading), but because real tape recording chains also run signal through tube/transistor amplifier stages to imprint it, tape saturation in practice often layers WITH tube or transistor character rather than being a single pure mechanism — worth remembering when troubleshooting why a tape emulation sounds more complex than "just tape." *(All three character descriptions explicitly flagged as generalizations — actual harmonic order/amount varies by specific unit and how hard it's driven.)* → [[Saturation and Distortion]]

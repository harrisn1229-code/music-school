# Explained! "What the Modes Really Sound Like"

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fIQ9ykjV0Qg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fIQ9ykjV0Qg)

## Synopsis
A behind-the-scenes breakdown of Max's companion orchestral "modes comparison" video: he wrote one 8-bar melody + harmonization, then transposed the exact same arrangement through every parallel mode of the major scale, keeping instrumentation/rhythm/arrangement identical so the *only* variable is which notes the mode allows. He explains why he excluded Locrian, what he calls "magic mode" (all modes' notes combined), and — most useful for the vault — the "relative pairs" grouping he uses to organize/compare modes, which reframes the standard Ionian-Dorian-Phrygian-Lydian-Mixolydian-Aeolian-Locrian ordering into major/minor pairs matched by brightness.

## What's taught
- **Composing IN a mode (not just soloing over it) means holding the arrangement fixed and only changing the note choices.** Practical workflow: write one melody + simple triadic piano harmonization that touches every note of the starting mode, orchestrate it, then for each new mode keep the same rhythm/instrument entry points/arrangement shape and only alter which specific notes are played (and lightly adjust the harmony/voice-leading where the mode forces it) — isolates the mode's emotional color as the only thing that changes between versions. Directly useful as a modal-composition (not modal-improvisation) method, which Max notes is underdocumented online compared to modal soloing content. → [[Diatonic Modes]], [[Modal Pop]]
- **Keeping the key center audible is what makes a mode's character actually land** — you can't demonstrate a mode's mood without simultaneously reinforcing where "home" is, because it's the tension between the fixed tonic and the mode's characteristic notes that produces the mode's specific feeling; lose the sense of tonic and the mode's color reads as random rather than as "this specific flavor of major/minor." → [[Keys]], [[Modal Pop]]
- **Locrian doesn't function as a stable tonic and was deliberately excluded** — because Locrian's tonic triad is diminished (no perfect 5th above the root), it can't create the anchored "home" feeling a key center needs; when Max tried composing a Locrian version anyway, it didn't read as "we're home in Locrian," it read as hearing the SAME material in a relative Aeolian or Phrygian instead. Reinforces (with a first-hand compositional test, not just theory) why [[Diatonic Modes]] already flags Locrian as "rarely used as a true home key."
- **"Magic mode" (aka mixed mode) = borrowing from ALL parallel modes at once rather than picking one** — instead of thinking "I'm in Lydian, occasionally I borrow from elsewhere," Magic Mode treats every note from every parallel mode on the same tonic as simultaneously available, to be combined freely. This is the plain-language ceiling case of [[Borrowed Chords]]/modal interchange — not "borrow occasionally," but "everything is always available." Term originates from composer/theorist W. Matthew, credited by Max as a major influence. → [[Borrowed Chords]], [[Scale Modes Reference]]
- **The "relative pairs" way of grouping modes — a genuinely new organizing frame not yet in the vault.** Rather than the standard brightness ladder (Lydian→Ionian→Mixolydian→Dorian→Aeolian→Phrygian→Locrian) or alphabetical-by-scale-degree order, Max pairs each major mode with its relative minor counterpart (same key signature, different tonic — same relationship as [[Keys]]'s relative major/minor, generalized to all 7 modes): **Ionian ↔ Aeolian** (the "middle" pair — plain major/plain minor), **Lydian ↔ Dorian** (the *brightest* major paired with the *brightest* minor), **Mixolydian ↔ Phrygian** (the *darkest* major paired with the *darkest* minor). Ordering a mode study/demo in these pairs (rather than straight through the brightness ladder) is presented as more ear-friendly because it repeatedly contrasts major against minor rather than drifting slowly from bright to dark. → [[Diatonic Modes]], [[Scale Modes Reference]]
- **Cross-reference**: this pairing and the underlying brightness ladder is the same information [[Diatonic Modes]]'s existing color-tone table encodes (Lydian/Mixolydian as the two brightest majors relative to Ionian, Dorian/Phrygian as the two brightest/darkest minors relative to Aeolian) — this video gives it a second, complementary organizing lens (paired comparison rather than a single ladder).

# The Billie Jean Chord Stack - It's 4 Sounds!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_mIYTG2hlT8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_mIYTG2hlT8)

## Synopsis

The chord counterpart to [[MJ's Billie Jean Bass - It's 4 Instruments!]] — the "ooh ooh" chord stab is also a 4-layer stack: a stringy CS-80 patch (doubled, 2 tracks), a rounder/shorter CS-80 patch (single track, built-in chorus/tremolo), a sine-based CS-80 patch with slight octave-beating and ring-mod tremolo, and Michael Jackson's own voice singing 2 of the 3 chord notes. Includes a full Prophet-5 recreation of the lead patch for anyone without a CS-80.

## Techniques

- **CS-80's velocity + pressure-sensitive keybed as a deliberate source of performance variation**: the CS-80 responds to both how hard a key is struck AND how much it's pressed into after the initial hit — every attack comes out slightly different, which is framed as part of why the chord stack reads as organic/"real" rather than mechanically identical. A Prophet-5 recreation of the same patch, by contrast, is purely a gate (on/off), so its release/attack are perfectly consistent note to note — close in tone but missing that natural irregularity.
- **Sound 1 (doubled, 2 tracks)**: 2-oscillator CS-80 patch — oscillator 1 = pulse wave with pulse-width modulation (reads almost like vibrato, but it's the waveform shape changing, not pitch); oscillator 2 = sawtooth, filter lightly opened and envelope-modulated. Both oscillators' outputs combine into the layered "stringy" tone. **Prophet-5 recreation**: oscillator 1 pulse, oscillator 2 sawtooth, balanced via the oscillator mix knob, through the Prophet's single shared filter (vs. the CS-80's genuinely separate dual signal paths) — filter cutoff ~50%, no resonance, ~50% envelope amount, light filter attack/mid decay/light sustain/light release, VCA envelope slightly slower attack, similar decay, full sustain, moderate release. **Two settings that are easy to miss and silently kill the patch**: the Prophet's RELEASE button must be engaged or there's no release time at all, and the mod WHEEL must be up or there's no pulse-width modulation at all — without it, the sound loses its "chewy," rich character entirely.
- **Sound 2 (single track, not doubled)**: shorter, rounder, more articulate than Sound 1, with the CS-80's built-in chorus+tremolo circuit engaged (the source of the instrument's characteristic stereo-spread "cheesy richness"). Oscillator 1: sawtooth + pulse wave mixed, filter with light initial cutoff, slow-ish attack envelope, near-max decay, mid release. Oscillator 2 (the "lower" voice): pulse + sawtooth again, plus a small amount of white noise mixed in pre-filter, filter opened just under halfway, mid-length attack/near-max decay/mid release. **Getting the release time right is called out as especially important on the CS-80** for how the part feels rhythmically.
- **Sound 3 (sine-based, subtle octave-beating)**: two sine waves patched DIRECTLY into the VCA (no filter stage at all) — oscillator 1 at the fundamental (slow attack, mid decay, full sustain, generous release), oscillator 2 the same shape but tuned an octave up and mixed in at low level, purely to add a touch of upper-harmonic brightness — described explicitly as "organ thinking," the way drawbar organs build brightness from harmonic mixes rather than filtering. **Tremolo via the ring modulator used as a slow-speed VCA**: engaging the CS-80's ring modulator at a slow sine rate with a moderate depth setting produces a gentle tremolo (a ring modulator is functionally a VCA with sine-wave modulation — sped up, the same circuit produces ring-mod-style tone-mangling instead). This sound is structurally used AS A DOUBLE of Michael's own vocal line, and — notably — only plays in specific song sections (A and B sections, plus only the first half of the second half of the bridge, never resolving to the V chord there), rather than throughout.
- **Sound 4 is Michael Jackson's own voice**, singing 2 of the chord's 3 notes as a wordless "ooh" — doubling Sound 3's tone closely enough to blend as one layer. Anecdote (via co-host Stephen Ray): Michael reportedly beatboxed/vocalized the "Billie Jean" parts in collaborators' ears before recording, consistent with wanting a genuinely vocal quality baked into the final synth-heavy arrangement.
- **Arrangement discipline**: not every layer plays throughout — Sound 2 and Sound 3 in particular are held back from certain sections specifically so their entrance highlights a structural moment (a section building, a bridge half) rather than adding static thickness for the whole song.

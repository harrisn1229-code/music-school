# How Guitarists Should Learn Music Theory (so many people don't do this!)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2d32vYc706c)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2d32vYc706c)

## Synopsis

A methodology video, not a specific-topic lesson: Jared's core teaching philosophy for how theory should actually be studied — always translated onto the instrument as a self-designed fretboard exercise, never left as abstract paper/staff knowledge — worked through four concrete examples (modes on one string, the circle of fourths as a physical shape, live interval-finding, and playing the diatonic chords of a key through). ## What's taught

Full extraction routed to a new dedicated methodology note — see [[Learning Theory on the Fretboard, Not on Paper]] for the complete writeup (the core claim, the four worked translation examples, the compounding-across-songs argument, and the "scratching your head is real practice time" principle). Summary of what's there:

- **Theory studied abstractly (books, charts, the staff) doesn't transfer to playing until re-derived specifically on the fretboard** — recognizing a rule and being able to produce it under your fingers are treated as separate skills.
- **The actual practice task, given any theory concept: design your own fretboard exercise for it**, rather than looking one up — four worked templates (modes on one string, circle of fourths as a physical shape, live interval-finding by ear, diatonic chords of a key played straight through with qualities named aloud).
- **One well-internalized fretboard structure generalizes across every song that uses it** — the stated payoff for doing the translation work instead of memorizing progressions per-song.
- **"Scratching your head" — pausing to work out how a concept fits under your hands — is named directly as good practice time for theory specifically**, the inverse of how technique practice should be evaluated.

# 5 TIPS TO IMPROVE YOUR SOLO IMPROV TODAY

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rYilSAi1AC4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rYilSAi1AC4)

## Synopsis

Five decoration techniques for solos that sound "too scalar": repetition, dynamic accents, silence, enclosures, and passing tones, plus a two-pass (write-then-improvise) practice routine over a backing track.

## Techniques

- **Repetition legitimizes.** A repeated phrase (up to ~3 times before it reads as cheesy) sounds intentional — a useful side effect is that repeating a small mistake can make it read as deliberate rather than accidental. Repetitions don't need to be back-to-back; other lines can sit between them.
- **Dynamic accents.** Not every note needs equal volume/length — vary between staccato (short) and legato (long), and accent selectively, so notes carry differentiated weight instead of all reading as equally important.
- **Silence.** Let solos breathe with deliberate space rather than filling every beat.
- **Enclosures.** Approach a target note from a semitone above and below (either order) before landing on it — "a sandwich" around the note. Self-test by counting enclosures in a short solo by ear.
- **Passing tones.** Notes played briefly between two target notes; their exact pitch matters less since they're neither targeted nor sustained — distinct from enclosures' semitone-above/below rule.
- **Practice routine:** over a C minor blues backing track, write and perform a 2-chorus solo applying these techniques, then improvise a second solo on the same track, get feedback, and repeat until hitting diminishing returns — writing first is meant to force deliberate use before the techniques become reflexive.

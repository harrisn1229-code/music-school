## Synopsis

Part 2 of Max Konyi's 3-part "Essentials of Rhythm" series (← Part 1: [[Essentials of Rhythm - What Gives Rhythms Their Meaning (Important)]], → Part 3: [[Essentials of Rhythm - Organizing Beats and Bars (Meter)]]). A short, purely definitional video: how long a note lasts and what each duration is called — whole, half, quarter, eighth, sixteenth notes — measured in "how many beats does it take up," with everything demonstrated directly on a Studio One piano roll grid against a click-track kick pattern.

## What's taught

- **Note values are measured by how many beats they last, not by wall-clock time.** A whole note = 4 beats, half note = 2 beats, quarter note = 1 beat, eighth note = 1/2 beat, sixteenth note = 1/4 beat. Each name tells you its length *relative to a whole note* (a half note is literally half a whole note's length, an eighth note an eighth, etc.).
- **A quarter note doesn't have to start ON the beat to still be a quarter note.** Note *value* (duration) and note *placement* (on-beat vs. offbeat) are independent — a quarter-note-length note played starting halfway between two beats is still a quarter note; it's just offbeat. Length and start-position are two separate decisions.
- **Eighth notes fit two per beat, sixteenth notes fit four per beat** — visualized on the piano-roll grid as perfectly even subdivisions ("like a clock") once the grid resolution matches the note value being placed.
- **Match your DAW's grid/quantize resolution to the note value you're actually working in.** Setting the piano-roll grid to sixteenths shows sixteenth-note gridlines; switching to eighths collapses that back to two-per-beat gridlines. Practical implication for quantizing: if a part only ever uses quarter notes, quantizing to a quarter-note grid gives a more accurate result than defaulting to a finer grid that doesn't match the part's actual rhythmic content.
- **Beyond the basic five (whole/half/quarter/eighth/sixteenth) there's 32nd, 64th, 128th, plus triplets and dotted notes** — flagged as real but out of scope for this beginner-tier video; the goal here is just fluency with the pure power-of-two divisions before adding those complications.
- **Ear-training tip: learn to hear a note value by counting how many hits land per beat**, then name it from that count (two per beat → eighth notes) — framed as something that becomes intuitive with repetition, useful both for quantizing your own work and for communicating with other musicians.

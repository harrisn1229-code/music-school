# Secondary Subdominants

Part of [[Music Theory]] → [[Secondary Dominants]]. Extends the secondary-dominant logic one layer further: instead of borrowing a *dominant*-function chord from a related key, borrow a **subdominant/predominant**-function chord from one.

## The idea
- Notated the same slash way as secondary dominants: a subdominant-function chord "of" a related key's tonic, e.g. **ii∅7/V** = the ii∅7 chord as if V were the tonic.
- Subdominant-function chords available above the slash: **ii, IV, ii7, IV7, ii°, ii∅7, iv** (the same roster used for plain diatonic and [[Borrowed Chords|borrowed]] subdominants).
- The most common form by far is the **half-diminished supertonic 7th** (ii∅7) as the secondary subdominant.
- Usually resolves straight into the **matching secondary dominant**: ii∅7/V → V7/V.

## Caveats (more restricted than secondary dominants)
- Their existence is **more debatable** among theorists than secondary dominants — some just call the same progression a short modulation instead.
- Found almost exclusively in **Romantic-era** music.
- Restricted to only **"of IV"** and **"of V"** (the closest related keys) — composers didn't reach further afield than that.

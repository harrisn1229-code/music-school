# Course 22 — Hanon IV

The rest of Hanon's Part II — turning the thumb under, then the scales, chromatic scales and arpeggios in every key. From exercise 32 on, every exercise prints its own metronome mark.

**What it assumes**

- [[Course 21 — Hanon III]] — Hanon is cumulative and these exercises assume the ones before them are still in the warm-up.
- A piano or keyboard, [[The Virtuoso Pianist - Hanon.pdf]] open on a stand, and a metronome.
- No theory. This course teaches no ideas and tests none — it is the place you play the exercise.

**What this unlocks**

- [[Course 23 — Hanon V]] — the next block of exercises, which assumes these are in the hand.
- Scales, chromatic scales and arpeggios in every key, under the hand rather than on paper.
- The thumb-under motion, which is the single mechanic every scale and arpeggio in the book rests on.

## How to run it

These are Hanon's own instructions, printed in the book, and they are the method rather than preamble. They apply to every lesson below, so each lesson states only what is particular to its exercise.

- **The metronome mark is a range, not a target.** Start at the low number, get it clean and even, then move up. Arriving at the top number with an uneven hand is not a pass.
- ***"Lift the fingers high and with precision, playing each note very distinctly."***
- **Both hands carry the same difficulty throughout** — the figure the left hand plays ascending is copied by the same fingers of the right descending. That refusal to let the weak hand off is the whole design of the book.
- **Hanon is cumulative.** *"As soon as Ex. 1 is mastered"* means move on, not stop playing it. Finished exercises stay in the warm-up.
- Page numbers below are the PDF's own count, 1–116, matching the `pages` parameter — not the printed book-page numbers visible in the scan.

## Course final

A **performance check**, not a test page. There are no `type: school-test` pages in this course and nothing here enters the spaced-repetition drip — finger facility is a motor skill, and the drip is for facts.

The check is a rep: play exercises 32 through 43 back to back, in order, at the tempo each one is marked for, fingers high and precise, both hands equal. Pass is your own honest read of whether it was clean and even. Nothing in the system can hear the piano, so nothing here pretends to grade it.

# Mix with the Masters 2012 - Manny Marroquin

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aiaxq9B7eFQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aiaxq9B7eFQ)

## Synopsis

Manny Marroquin's opening address to his 2012 Mix with the Masters seminar group — almost entirely a philosophy/mindset piece rather than a technical breakdown, so it sits apart from most videos on this channel. His core thesis: a mix succeeds or fails on honesty and emotional connection, not sonic perfection — "you cannot bullshit your way through a mix... whatever comes out of those speakers is true." He explicitly frames the week ahead as not-a-technique-class ("I'm not here to teach you how to mix, 'cause you guys know how to do that") and instead pitches creativity as a *tastemaker* instinct (recognizing "that just got me" moments and reacting to them, not throwing effects around) over technical correctness. The one concrete, repeatable workflow habit in the video: he always starts a mix with the drums, even on a song that has none — a personal anchor point rooted in his own background as a drummer.

## Techniques

- **Always start a mix with the drums, regardless of genre or whether the song has drums at all.** "Now when I start a mix, being a drummer, I always start with the drums. It could be a ballad, it could be a song without drums, and I'll still start in the drums." A personal, stated-as-fixed habit — not "audition where to start," but always the same entry point. → [[Gain Staging]], [[Bass Design]]
- **Pick "marker" elements from the rough mix before starting execution.** "I listen to the rough, I pick whatever things that I like as a marker" — the rough isn't just a reference to beat, it's where he flags the specific things worth preserving/building toward before touching the session. *(qualitative only — no specific workflow steps given beyond this)*
- **Mix philosophy (not a technique, captured for completeness): honesty over sonic polish, creativity as tastemaking rather than effect-stacking.** "You cannot bullshit your way through a mix... if you're honest with that mix, the client will appreciate that honesty more than you trying to make the best mix ever." Creative doesn't mean "throwing a reverb everywhere" — it means reacting to what a specific move does to the track ("oh shit, that just got me") and trusting that reaction. Kept on this page only — a mindset framing, not an actionable setting or signal-chain move.

# Why Don't We Do It In The Road? — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (**8 bars** — three percussion layers stacking across the first six, the stuttered title question filling the last two) plus the same 24-bar chorus **three times**, with a complete ending. Two passes would be thin, four would outstay it.

One of Paul's **"bonsai tree" songs** in Pollack's taxonomy: minimal material, stick-figure form, and no interest whatsoever in disguising either. The instructive comparison is with "Rocky Raccoon" two tracks earlier, which builds a convincing song form out of one repeated frame; this one makes no such attempt and is the better for it.

The chorus is a **12-bar blues with every unit doubled** ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]) — eight bars of I, four of IV, four of I, then two/two/four. The tempo is fast and the harmonic rhythm is not, one change every two to eight bars, and the gap between the two is most of what gives the track its lumbering weight ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]).

The ending is a loud ringing cymbal crash on a downbeat, with the final word of the lyric placed on the beat **just before** it, syncopated so it lands weakly — the crash takes the emphasis the word was supposed to have.

Almost entirely self-played. Two elements are staged rather than constant: the guitar is withheld until bar 13 of the second chorus and then stays, and the bass thumps unvaried roots until switching to jumpy melodic riffing for the final chorus.

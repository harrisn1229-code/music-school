# Songs that use the Purple Rain chord progression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEZzB2PpIQ8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YEZzB2PpIQ8)

## Synopsis

Analyzes Prince's "Purple Rain" chord progression (I–vi–V–IV, all with add9 extensions), then generalizes to the much more common "abstract" bare-triad version of the same four-chord relationship across a wide range of other songs, including harmonic-rhythm and tuning variations.

## Techniques

- **Prince's specific voicing**: B♭add9–Gm7add11–Fadd9–E♭add9 — every chord carries a 9th- or 11th-family extension, creating a dense, "sticky," cluster-like harmonic texture that Bennett describes as tense but consonant (not dissonant), with a perceptible "thickness" from how closely the extension tones sit to their chord tones.
- **The abstract shape underneath, I–vi–V–IV, used far more widely in plain-triad form**: "Two Princes" (Spin Doctors) — same shape, key of D (a nod noted for the shared "Princes"/"Prince" naming coincidence, not a claimed influence). "Take Me Home, Country Roads" (John Denver) — same shape, but with an uneven harmonic rhythm (the final IV chord gets only half a bar instead of a full bar like the other three). "Big Me" (Foo Fighters) — key of C: C–Am–G–F.
- **"Not a Lot, Just Forever" (Adrianne Lenker)** — key of D♭, played on a guitar tuned to "double drop D♭" (all six strings down a further semitone from standard double-drop-D) — the top three open strings ring G♭–B♭–D♭ as a continuous drone/pedal layered over the moving chord progression underneath, functioning as an ever-present set of upper chord extensions regardless of which of the four chords is currently sounding.
- **"Photograph" (Ed Sheeran)** — verse only, key of E: I–vi–V–IV under the lyric "Loving can hurt."
- **"Don't Let Me Get Me" (P!nk)** — key of E♭, uses the shape for BOTH verse and chorus (not just one section); the pre-chorus deliberately breaks away to a different two-chord pattern (♭VI–♭VII-adjacent) specifically to create contrast, so the main progression's return for the chorus reads as fresh rather than as "the same thing we've had the whole song" — an applied instance of the same develop-beyond-the-loop thinking covered in the dedicated "How To Write More Than A Chord Loop" video elsewhere in this batch.
- **Time-signature variant**: "Suspended in Gaffa" (Kate Bush) — same I-vi-V-IV shape, but set in **6/8** rather than the 4/4 used by every other example in the video, giving the identical harmony a distinctly different lilt/feel purely from the meter change.
- **Overall contour, framed explicitly**: unlike some progressions whose "shape" depends heavily on voicing choices, this one has a genuinely consistent *descending* character across virtually every example — I down through vi, V, to IV — cited as the throughline that makes all these otherwise-different songs feel like variations on one underlying gesture rather than coincidentally-identical chord lists.

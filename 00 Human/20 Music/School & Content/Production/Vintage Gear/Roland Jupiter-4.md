# Roland Jupiter-4

## What it is
Roland's first polyphonic synth — four voices, one VCO each, "Compuphonic" patch memory, and a personality much stranger than its flagship successor. The JP-4 is the cult Jupiter: wobbly, characterful, and beloved precisely for its rough edges.

## What makes the sound
- **One VCO per voice with famously drifty tuning** — the four voices beat against each other like a small choir that's had a drink; modern stability this is not, and that's the charm.
- **A huge-sounding ensemble/chorus button** that instantly turns thin single-osc patches into wide vintage beds — the proto-Juno move, wilder here.
- **An extremely fast LFO** (into audio rate) for sci-fi warbles and ray-gun effects no later Jupiter could match.
- Slow attack strings/brass through that ensemble = the melancholy late-70s/early-80s European synth sound.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drift]] (single-osc + character + chorus is exactly this recipe), [[Analog]] with heavy detune drift, [[TAL-Chorus-LX]] as the ensemble layer; [[Omnisphere]] carries JP-4-style sampled sources.
- **Paid:** Roland Cloud Jupiter-4, Arturia's Jup-family coverage; hardware-wise the JP-4 has no reissue — originals are collector bait.
- **Free:** the Drift + TAL-Chorus recipe above.

## Used on
- Genre/era: 1979–82 European new wave and early synthpop (it appears constantly in that scene's studio photos and gear lists — Duran Duran's earliest records, the Numan/Visage orbit), before the Jupiter-8/Juno wave replaced it; specific record-level credits are muddier than its successors', so most associations stay era-level.

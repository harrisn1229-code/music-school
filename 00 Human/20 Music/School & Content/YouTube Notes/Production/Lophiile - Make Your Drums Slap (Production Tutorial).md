# Lophiile — Make Your Drums Slap (Production Tutorial)

## Synopsis

Lophiile's first tutorial video: the quick moves he actually uses to make drums knock inside a busy loop. Nothing exotic — sample choice first, shorten hits for punch, a touch of saturation, surgical EQ on whistle frequencies, a limiter on the master *while producing*, transient shaping, group saturation so the drums "stick together," and the one everyone misses: turn everything that isn't drums DOWN. Short, dense, and maps almost 1:1 onto the vault's drum chains.

## Techniques

- **Pick good samples first** — "a huge part of it"; a great kick sample (Maxwell pack kick, a Jake One snare here) makes every downstream move easier. If it doesn't slap out of the box, keep digging. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Shorten hits for instant punch** — the snare's baked-in reverb shouldn't fire on *every* hit: clip it short on hit 1, leave the verby tail on hit 2 for contrast. Same on the kick: trim the low-frequency tail until it's punchy but stop before you lose the low end. → [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]], [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Consolidate edited samples** — once a fade/trim is right, consolidate the clip so it moves as one clean unit instead of dragging fades around.
- **A touch of saturation on individual hits** — "it just has some magic to it": louder, more harmonics, sometimes punchier. Small amounts. → [[Saturator]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **EQ out the junk** — found a "whistling" resonance on the snare ("I hate that frequency") and notched it; also cut "papery" top. Sweep and cut what annoys you before boosting anything. → [[EQ Eight]], [[EQ]]
- **Limiter on the master WHILE producing** — keeps him honest about how the drums actually smack; uses [[Pro-L 2]] with the **Dubstep style** ("pretty punchy"). → [[Limiting]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Transient designer when it needs it** — [[Transient Master]] (NI): attack up, sustain down a bit, until the hit "pokes" — then back off if it pokes too hard. → [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]]
- **Group the drums and saturate the GROUP** — his key glue move: kick + snare + hats into one group with tape-style saturation so "when your kick hits, your hi-hat ducks a bit — there's a bit more distortion going through everything." Tools he names: [[Radiator]] (Soundtoys — his go-to; **turn its noise off** for control, pull input/mix down if too much), UAD **Ampex ATR-102**, Waves [[Kramer Tape]] + J37, and Ableton's Drum Buss ("a bit heavy-handed"). → [[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Turn everything ELSE down.** "The number one thing" wrong with student mixes where drums don't come through: pads/keys/808s are all too loud. Drums should be the loudest thing hitting the limiter — everything else sits secondary. → [[Gain Staging]], [[Masking and Carving]]

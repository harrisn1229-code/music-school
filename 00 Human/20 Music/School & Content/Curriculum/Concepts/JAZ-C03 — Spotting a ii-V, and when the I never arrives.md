# JAZ-C03 — Spotting a ii-V, and when the I never arrives

**Q.** What is the scan that finds a ii–V on an unfamiliar chart, and what are the two common cases where the promised I never turns up?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Scan for any m7 chord followed by a dominant 7th a fourth above it.** That is a ii–V, and whatever it points at is the intended I — even when the I never actually sounds.
>
> The two cases: the **deceptive** move, where the ii–V lands on vi or ♭VI instead (`Dm7 G7 Am7`); and the **unresolved** ii–V, which moves straight to the next ii–V without resolving at all.

## Why it matters

This is the reading tip that pays for itself. It is also the fix for the commonest misreading of a bebop head — an unresolved chain of ii–Vs makes a chart look like it changes key every two bars when it does not.

The chaining device behind it is worth having as a writing tool, not just an analysis one: make the I of one unit into the ii of the next, or simply drop a ii–V in front of any chord you want to arrive at.

## Drill

Take a standard's chart and bracket every m7 followed by a dominant a fourth up. Write in the I each one implies, then mark which of those I chords actually arrive, which are deceptive, and which never resolve at all.

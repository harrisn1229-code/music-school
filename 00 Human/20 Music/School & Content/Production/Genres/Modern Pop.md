# Modern Pop

**What makes it the genre:** the lead vocal is the product — everything else is furniture arranged around it. Immaculate vocal chain, stacked hooks vs intimate verses, contrast as the master arrangement tool (every 8 bars something changes), and a polished loudness that still breathes.

## The pillars

### 1. The vocal hierarchy (where the budget goes)
- Lead: the full [[1 Main Vocal Track|Main Vocal Track]] → [[3 Vocal Bus|Vocal Bus]] treatment, tuned invisible (retune 35–50, Humanize 100), compressed in stages, always the loudest thing.
- **Verse = intimate, chorus = enormous:** verses mono-ish and dry, choruses bloom — doubles hard-panned in fours, harmonies inside them, [[Width Send]] automated up, long verb ducked under the dry voice. The chorus lift IS this contrast, not level.
- Stacks tuned/timed tighter than the lead ([[1 Double Vocal Track|Double Vocal Track]] editing rules); ad-lib answers panned wide.

### 2. Drums — punchy, tight, small-count
- Few elements, each perfect: one kick, one snare/clap stack, one hat lane, maybe percs. Pop kicks are tight (sustain down), snares are layered (body + clap slap).
- Four-on-floor for dance-pop (kick ducks everything — [[Kickstart 2]] gentle); halftime borrowed from trap for urban-pop.
- The 80s lane (Weeknd): gated-verb snare ([[Reverb Send Room]] send high + gate), analog synth bass instead of 808.

### 3. Bass — supportive, never competing
- Synth bass or Hofner-clean: locked to the kick pocket, mids controlled, sub mono. Melodic fills only in vocal gaps (the McCartney rule).

### 4. Music — hooks in every register
- Chords: diatonic + one borrowed chord for the "lift" · keys/synth beds M/S-carved out of the vocal's lane ([[1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]]) · one countermelody max at a time — pop is disciplined about who speaks when ([[Masking and Carving]] hierarchy strictly enforced).
- Ear candy budget: risers/impacts/sweeps into every section ([[3 FX Bus|FX Bus]], ducked under the vocal), one "signature sound" per song that recurs.

### 5. Mix presentation
- Clean top (vocal air wins over hats), controlled low mids, wide sides/solid center ([[Width and Stereo]]).
- Master −9 to −10 LUFS, limiter transparent, dynamics preserved *between* sections even when loud within them ([[Master Chain]]).

## Arrangement

**Section map** — *"Levitating"*, *"Blinding Lights"*, *"good 4 u"*

| Section | Bars | Slots filled | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 4–8 | the signature sound + one more | 2 |
| Verse | 16 | BASS · MIDS · LEAD (mono, dry) · TRANSIENT | 4 |
| Pre-chorus | 8 | + BED · AIR — filter opening, drums thinning | 5 |
| Chorus | 16 | everything: SUB · BASS · BED · MIDS · LEAD stacked · AIR · TRANSIENT | 8 |
| Post-chorus | 8 | instrumental hook, vocal out | 5 |
| Bridge | 8 | strip to 2–3, usually LEAD + BED | 3 |
| Final chorus | 16 | chorus + one thing held back all song | 8 |

**Entrances & exits**
- **Build the chorus first, then delete things to make the verse.** Stated in the session recipe; it's the whole arrangement method.
- **The pre-chorus is engineered, not written** — filter opens, drums thin or drop, the stack widens. Its only job is to make the chorus land.
- Something changes every 8 bars. A pop section that repeats identically twice is a bug.
- Hold one element in reserve for the final chorus — a countermelody, an octave-up vocal, a new percussion lane.

**The contrast move — density swing.** Verse 4 slots, chorus 8. This is the widest verse-to-chorus density swing of any genre on this shelf, and it *is* the genre. The lift is contrast, not level: see pillar 1.

**Density rule: 4 → 8 and back, every cycle.** Everything is furniture arranged around the vocal, so the question per section is only ever "how much furniture."

## Session recipe
Write the chorus first · track the lead early over a rough bed (the vocal dictates the arrangement, not vice versa) · build verse by *removing* chorus elements · A/B against a reference at matched loudness from the first hour.

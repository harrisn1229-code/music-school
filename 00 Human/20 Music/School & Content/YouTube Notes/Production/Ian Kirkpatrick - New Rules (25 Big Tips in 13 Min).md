# Ian Kirkpatrick — "New Rules" (25 Big Tips in 13 Min)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iHSmH57_wY8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iHSmH57_wY8)

## Synopsis

The single densest New Rules session breakdown — Ian opens the actual Cubase project and walks every stem: the half-speed vocal trick, the layered percussion built from tapping a guitar and desk with the room-talk left in, the 8-layer main synth that starts life as a pitched 808 tom, the two-layer snare, the vocal chain (Auto-Tune-to-track then Melodyne everything), reverse-reverb hits, the pitched drumline fill, and the mix moves that went to the mixer. Threaded through it is his real thesis: **pay attention to the *reason* behind each decision, not the settings**, and manage the **"rate of information"** so you never over-feed the listener. This page is the master record; the individual moves fan out to the plugin/effect/technique/chain notes below.

## Techniques

- **Half-speed vocal tracking** — kept a copy of the whole track at half speed and had Dua sing to it; recording it felt "so slow and dumb," but sped back up the vibratos come out fast and tight. Figure the part out, halve the session, record, speed up. → [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **Percussion from found sound** — tapped the back of a guitar and tapped the desk for the rhythmic layer; **left Emily and Caroline's background talking in** on purpose ("the more talking the better, it sounds natural"), then **gated the whole thing**. Pitch it down an octave for weight, or up an octave to expose the room-talk texture. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Found-sound processing chain** — pitch down an octave → **BigCrusher (Reason) used *as a gate*** → **SoundToys Crystallizer to blur/smear the sound** → **stereo image knocked fully to mono** → **UAD Cambridge EQ ("Camera GQ")** last. → [[Crystallizer]], [[Stereo Field]], [[EQ]]
- **The main synth is 8 layers, and layer 1 is a pitched 808 tom** — "I thought this was Reason, surprise surprise it's an 808 tom with a ton of OTT on it" (808 from the "808 Warfare" pack). Clean sine-wave 808 underneath for body; **Sausage Fattener** doing the "awesomeness" (light compression/color). Each layer has a job — pull the bottom layer and you lose sustain. → [[OTT]], [[Roland TR-808]], [[BASS Slot]], [[SUB Slot]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Two-layer snare** — layer 1 the famous **"bamboo" snare** with **heavy upward OTT** ("upwards all the way"); layer 2 a **tonal/transient snare**. Together the listener just hears "a snare" — they don't know it's two. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[OTT]]
- **Chorus synth = many layers combined into one stem** — stacked several sounds, printed as one; **LFO Tool ~65%** for the pumping rhythmic gate; OTT with **zero upward** ("just for purpose"). → [[LFO Tool]], [[Sidechaining]], [[BED Slot]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Vocal tracking chain (tracking day)** — **Auto-Tune on while tracking** just to make it sound good fast, **then Melodyne everything afterward** for the real tuning. Only a compressor on the lead ("is it even doing anything"). → [[Auto-Tune Pro]], [[Melodyne]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]]
- **Background-vocal doubles group** — **60% wet/mix**, heavy gain reduction (want them "far away"), EQ **boosting highs at 16 kHz, dipping 700 Hz, attenuating 100 Hz**, then **Antares Avox Sybil** de-esser. → [[De-essing]], [[EQ]], [[2 Backup Vocals Group]], [[AIR Slot]]
- **"Little pieces of the vocal on its own track" echo** — one of his favorite echoes ever: chop tiny vocal fragments to a dedicated track for scoop/echo candy. → [[Delay]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Manual sidechaining of the 808/bass** — instead of a compressor, **select all the notes and draw your own volume envelope, cutting at every note**. More control, more time, "sometimes it's fun." → [[Sidechaining]], [[Automation]], [[BASS Slot]]
- **Kick strategy** — two kicks in the hook (a big one + a smaller one), one smaller kick in the verses; the **chorus kick is the layers plus a "roomie"** (room-mic'd kick) for a nice combo. → [[Kick Design]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[1 Drums Kick Track|2 Drums Kick Track]]
- **Reverse-reverb into the "one"** — a reversed-reverb swell resolves onto the downbeat; also a **snare roll with tons of layers**. → [[Reverb]], [[3 FX Bus]]
- **Pitched vocal excitement** — a printed pitched vocal (likely from **Output Exhale**) as a "weird source of excitement" before the drop; plus an **altar-boy-pitched-up vocal** as candy. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Exhale]]
- **The mix / "violating a sense of space"** — mix chain referenced **Radiator**, **Pultec Pro**, and a **reverb that disappears** so the vocal jumps "from spacey to close." He loves suddenly changing the sense of space to grab attention because the listener isn't expecting it. → [[Little Radiator]], [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]], [[Reverb]], [[Depth and Space]]
- **The chorus fill (the "so smooth" transition)** — a **drumline sample from a Kontakt library**, chopped, with **volume automation** and **individual hits transposed up 3 and up 4 semitones** so the fill rises in pitch (zeroed out it sounds flat/wrong). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Automation]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **"Just at the point of perception" texture** — a low chorus texture kept barely audible on purpose — present but sub-conscious. → [[Depth and Space]], [[TEXTURE Slot]]

## Philosophy (the real lesson)

- **Chase the *reason*, not the recipe.** "The most important thing is to pay attention to the reason behind their decisions — not 'I always compress at this ratio.'" The New Rules stop-everything moment came from an *inspiration* ("it'd be cool if everything just stopped"), and the production served that.
- **Rate of information** — his answer to "how do you know when it's overproduced": don't give the listener too much information. If something distracts from the vocal, or there's too much rhythmic information, it's overproduced. Serve the song; resist "flexing on production."
- **The listener doesn't hear the layers** — 8-layer synths, 2-layer snares: the audience just hears "a synth," "a snare." Complexity is invisible and in service of one impression.
- **Timeline:** written 2016, out 2017, ~6 months of on-and-off production. Little Mix's label passed (said it had no hook); it was re-pitched to Dua months later. "Dare to suck."

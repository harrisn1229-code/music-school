# Ian Kirkpatrick — Ear Candy Adventures

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZLgI56ZONJo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZLgI56ZONJo)

## Synopsis

A 2.5-hour ear-candy stream: he builds fills/knickknacks on a loop, tours the "ear candy city" of Look At Her Now, and reveals the **PadShop "explosion" cymbal** (his favorite effect he's ever made). Threaded through it are two of his deepest lessons — **"use all your tools to create contrast/dynamic"** and the **vision-vs-habit rabbit hole** ("am I doing this because the song needs it, or because I always do it?"). Plus concrete craft on snare-vs-kick timing, compression-as-depth, and printing effects.

## Techniques

- **Ear candy = fills/knickknacks that hold attention or distract for a beat — but it's a slippery slope** to over-producing and distracting from the top line. Add candy only where the song has space. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **The PadShop "explosion" cymbal** — his favorite effect ever, a crash/cymbal replacement made in **PadShop** (granular): it grabs a ~**32 ms** grain; **0% random** = it loops that spot; **duration** stacks 2/3/4 loops in sequence; turn **random up** and after each loop it jumps to a new position — so it fans out into a **stereo cloud / "explosion of all the vocals in the song."** Turn it up loud as an impact. → [[PadShop]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Stereo Field]]
- **Cut a cymbal off abruptly ("violate it")** — chop the cymbal tail instead of letting it ring, "like opening a hi-hat really fast" (inspired by a Skrillex cymbal cutoff). Sudden absence as an accent. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **"Use chaos to find order"** — randomly scroll/slide through a sample's waveform (Cubase's slide, now in Ableton too) until your ear catches a rhythm, then build a phrase from that fragment. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Imager automation to MONO right before the chorus** — Look At Her Now's ear-candy group snaps to mono just before the hook so the stereo chorus "explodes" open by contrast. Width as a dynamic. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Stereo Field]], [[Automation]], [[Ian Kirkpatrick - Look At Her Now Breakdown]]
- **Print effects and move on** — his whole vocal-FX method: make an echo-with-reverb, **print it**, done. Also "print with the verb and cut it off on purpose." → [[Reverb]], [[Delay]], [[Depth and Space]]
- **Snare-vs-kick timing is all feel** — a snare *behind* the kick gets its "clack" drowned by the kick; nudge it *ahead* so the crack leads. He demos looping both. → [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[Phase Alignment]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Compression as a *depth* weapon, not just level** — slower attack lets the transient through → snare "in your face"; faster attack tames the transient → snare "far away." Use the comp to place a sound front-to-back instead of only riding volume. → [[Compression]], [[Depth and Space]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Don't compress delicate vocals on the way in** — very light comp tracking at most; he does the heavy vocal compression **post** ("I'll mess up a vocal so fast with external stuff on the way in"). → [[Compression]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **Layered snare roll from two blended samples** — a roll built from bits of two different loop samples blended, plus a tiny clap that "does nothing out of context but was doing something in the mix." → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **"The knocks" — a hand-tapped rhythmic element, panned** — a light percussive layer that's literally him tapping something, panned off-center. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[TEXTURE Slot]]
- **Found texture: a high-passed Central Park NYC noise sample** in a pre-chorus for a vibe shift (Lossy does that job for him now). → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Pro-Q 4]]
- **Use a collaborator's better FX** — Ben Rice printed vocal effects that were "sicker," so he used those. Don't be precious about whose FX it is. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - On Loops, Artist Ego and Originality]]

## Philosophy

- **Your job is to create contrast/dynamic with *every* tool** — volume, note pitch (verse lower than chorus), stereo width, effects — always contrasting a section against what came before. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Depth and Space]], [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Vision beats habit** — producing is easier with a vision/theme; without one you fall back on autopilot habits. The rabbit hole: "am I doubling the vocal because the song needs it, or because I *always* double the hook?" Question every habit against the song. Don't be a one-trick pony (a generic reverb on every song) — but don't dogmatically refuse a habit either. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - New Rules (25 Big Tips in 13 Min)|serve the song]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Watch other producers for *how they think*, not their presets** — "when I watch Disclosure stream I pay attention to the reasons they do things, so I learn to think like them." Understanding a compressor deeply gives you "another weapon for dynamic." → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - The Production Secret Behind Huge Pop Hits]]
- **Get in the zone on demand** — after years it's a learnable skill, but it still needs a **vision/theme** first; he now "pretends people are around" to get going (opposite of where he started). 
- **Business: "don't fight over crumbs"** — take the deal, get your foot in the door, even splits in the room, trust your team, think long-term. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - On Loops, Artist Ego and Originality]]

## Techniques

### Starting a track / workflow
- **Start the idea from a big distorted synth bass, not a beat or a chord progression** — Mike Dean's own habit (seen in his session clips), used here as the very first sound in the recreation, similar in character to "The Ends" on *Birds in the Trap Sing McKnight* — a deliberate departure from starting with drums or a chord bed. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[BASS Slot]]

### Sub 37 + vocoder lead (spoken-word vocal as modulator)
- **A Moog Sub 37 run through a vocoder, modulated by a recorded spoken-word vocal (not a sung take)** — seen in a Mike Dean session clip: the vocal is spoken, not sung, into the vocoder as the modulator signal, with the Sub 37 as the carrier/tone source, for a robotic-but-musical lead texture. loner's own version uses a preset built for his upcoming Layers Volume 2 bank. No exact vocoder settings given (band count, formant, etc.) in the transcript — described qualitatively only. → [[Vocoder]], [[Pitch and Formant]]

### CS-80 + Jupiter-8 layered pad (the core of "the Mike Dean sound")
- **Mike Dean's pads (heard on "Pick Up the Phone" and the "Goosebumps" remix) are most often a CS-80 + Jupiter-8 combination.** Recreated here with the **Arturia Jup-8 V** (heard/captioned as "the jupiter 8 from Arturia") left on its **default patch** deliberately, so the sound gets built by hand rather than preset-browsed:
  - Base oscillators: **sawtooth + rectangle (pulse) wave**.
  - **Fine Tune pulled up** to detune the two oscillators against each other for a wider stereo-ish spread *(classic analog-poly detune-for-width move, not vocoder/chorus width)*.
  - Effects added: **chorus, reverb, overdrive**.
  - **Modulated the chorus amount and the filter cutoff** together for continuous movement rather than a static patch.
  - Layered underneath/on top: the **"Classic Strings" factory preset from Arturia CS-80 V4**.
  - **Mike Dean deliberately avoids heavy effects processing on his pads/leads — "he often lets the synths speak for themselves"** — the character comes from the analog-modeled oscillators and playing, not a big FX chain; the video frames his keyboard-playing skill as the actual "secret," more than any specific plugin. *(general framing, not a specific technique with settings)*

### Matriarch lead via one-shots (no VST workaround)
- **Moog Matriarch lead lines, sourced as one-shot samples (not a VST — none exists) from Kill September's sample kit, played back completely unquantized** — loner's stated read on why: "that's how Mike Dean does it" — deliberately loose, human timing rather than grid-locked MIDI, even though the source is a sampled one-shot rather than a live take. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[LEAD Slot]]

### The Astroworld transition (Stargazing-style song blend)
- **Keep the bass sound AND the bassline consistent across a song-to-song transition, then vary it lightly** — Mike Dean's *Astroworld* trick that makes two otherwise-separate songs "glue together seamlessly" (named example: the Stargazing transition): reuse the same bass patch and mostly the same bassline through the transition, adding a few extra notes and running it filtered/low-passed rather than swapping to a new bass sound. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[BASS Slot]]
- **Build the transition's texture layer from a single one-shot kit** (Kill September's kit again) — an arp run from the Matriarch, a glitchy chopped-chord sound, and a synth arp run all pulled from the same kit rather than sourced separately, for a texturally-cohesive transition section. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[TEXTURE Slot]]
- **Reuse a previous section's lead, pitched down a full octave, to emulate "keeping the original sample" through a bridge** — Mike Dean kept *Stargazing*'s original sample audible into the bridge section rather than dropping it; loner emulated this not by resampling the original but by taking the **lead synth part from the earlier section of his own recreation and pitching it down one octave (12 semitones)** for the bridge, giving continuity without a literal repeat. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **A synth "key bell" lead with a fast up/down melodic contour to evoke a specific visual theme** — a bell-toned synth part whose melody moves up and down quickly, chosen specifically because the rapid contour "feels like a roller coaster" — matching *Astroworld*'s theme park subject matter; a reminder that a melodic *shape* choice, not just a timbre choice, can carry thematic meaning. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **Build a dedicated short transition beat from the same one-shot kit, Sicko Mode-style** — modeled on how "Sicko Mode" transitions from Drake's verse into Travis's: Kill September supplied a purpose-built transition beat made entirely from his own kit's one-shots, used here as connective tissue between sections rather than reusing the main beat. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Automate a synth's delay send AND its delay feedback level together across the transition** — on the synth key-bell sound specifically: rather than a static delay throw, both the delay amount and the feedback amount are automated to rise/change through the transition, building the echo trail's density as a compositional move. No exact automation curve/timing given — described qualitatively. → [[Delay]], [[Automation]]

- **Rhodes-preset electric piano through reverb, heavy compression, and tremolo, modeled on the "Stop Trying to Be God" outro** — opened "Whirly V2" from Arturia (auto-captions almost certainly garbled **Wurli V**, Arturia's Wurlitzer-family electric-piano instrument, which ships multiple EP models including Rhodes-style patches) and chose its **Rhodes preset**, then added **reverb**, **noticeably heavy compression**, and **tremolo** — the tremolo specifically called out as clearly audible in the reference track. No exact compression ratio/reverb type given in the transcript. → [[Modulation]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A "woozy" synth-organ preset stands in for a scarcely-used real organ layer** — *Stop Trying to Be God* has an organ part used sparingly; rather than sourcing a literal organ patch, loner used a **woozy synth organ preset from his own Layers Volume 2 bank** for a similarly unusual, semi-detuned organ character. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Two texture layers from the same one-shot kit as a closing pass** — finished the beat by adding two more textures from Kill September's kit on top of everything else, a final layering pass rather than new sound design. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[TEXTURE Slot]]

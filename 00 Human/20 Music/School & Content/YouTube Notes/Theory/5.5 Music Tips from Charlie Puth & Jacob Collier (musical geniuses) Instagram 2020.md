## Techniques
- **Word-painting: make the melody's rhythm and pitch physically embody what the lyric means.** Charlie Puth's core habit: when a lyric describes being "held back," the note itself gets held — the melodic/rhythmic choice acts out the word's meaning rather than just carrying it. Taken to its extreme, he says the vocal line should communicate the song's meaning even with the words stripped away: mute the vocal on one of his tracks and the chords/melody alone should still tell you what the song is about, because the music is "speaking it for you." → [[Writing for Voice]] — Charlie Puth (Sol State), [5.5 Music Tips from Charlie Puth & Jacob Collier (musical geniuses) Instagram 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PjQr58ob5pY)
- **Write the melody first as mumbled, wordless "gibberish," then reverse-engineer the lyric to fit it.** Jacob Collier describes Charlie Puth's process: he'll hum/mumble a vocal melody with no real words attached — pure rhythm and pitch — and only afterward figure out what it's actually "saying," discovering the verse's melodic shape before a single lyric exists. What sounds like nonsense in the moment becomes the finished verse melody once real words are fit to the same contour. → [[Melody Writing]] — Charlie Puth / Jacob Collier (Sol State), [5.5 Music Tips from Charlie Puth & Jacob Collier (musical geniuses) Instagram 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PjQr58ob5pY)
- **Use your full "bag of tricks" sparingly, not all at once.** Jacob Collier traces this restraint back to Stevie Wonder and Quincy Jones, whose jazz backgrounds gave them a deep well of harmonic/melodic vocabulary alongside genuine pop sensibility — the "textbook" move, per Collier, isn't deploying every advanced idea in one song, it's holding most of it back, because not every listener (or collaborator) is going to track everything two virtuosos are capable of doing at once. → [[Committing and Printing]] — Jacob Collier (Sol State), [5.5 Music Tips from Charlie Puth & Jacob Collier (musical geniuses) Instagram 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PjQr58ob5pY)
- **Mono-check on a single cheap monitor is a legitimate mix-validation step, not a shortcut to feel guilty about.** Charlie Puth mixed/checked a track in mono through one KRK monitor — Jacob Collier's response (relief at "being forgiven" for similarly minimal setups, like referencing off a single SM58 or a basic 4" speaker) frames this as a real technique: if a mix holds up collapsed to mono on one modest speaker, it holds up anywhere, and expensive multi-monitor referencing setups aren't a prerequisite for a mix that translates. → [[Referencing and Translation]] — Charlie Puth / Jacob Collier (Sol State), [5.5 Music Tips from Charlie Puth & Jacob Collier (musical geniuses) Instagram 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PjQr58ob5pY)
- **The "perfect studio" is a myth — mucking around on a phone often beats a pristine setup, and technical mistakes rarely sink a song.** Both push back on the idea that hit records come from a flawless, fully-optimized rig: the reality is producers muck around and record ideas however they can, including a guitar demo tracked straight into a phone. Charlie Puth admits he mixed an entire album with his monitor speakers wired backward (left signal coming out the right speaker) the whole time — out of phase throughout — and it barely mattered because the album didn't lean heavily on panning; Jacob Collier adds that a phone-through-an-amp demo often has a "handmade" quality that gets lost the moment the same part is re-recorded "properly" in a nice studio with pristine plugins (his own example: singing a bassline into a voicemail versus having a bass player re-track it cleanly with Neve plugins — he actually wanted the rough phone version's character back). Mindset point, not a repeatable procedure — no note routing. — Charlie Puth / Jacob Collier (Sol State), [5.5 Music Tips from Charlie Puth & Jacob Collier (musical geniuses) Instagram 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PjQr58ob5pY)
- **"The human chord" — the half-tip: chasing the feeling of a great chord change over anything else.** Jacob Collier closes on a half-joking, non-technical aside — he says he's had trouble dating because nobody gives him the feeling that chords do, hoping he'll eventually meet "the human chord." Pure sentiment, not a technique, which is presumably why the video calls itself "5.5" tips rather than 6 — no note routing. — Jacob Collier (Sol State), [5.5 Music Tips from Charlie Puth & Jacob Collier (musical geniuses) Instagram 2020](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PjQr58ob5pY)

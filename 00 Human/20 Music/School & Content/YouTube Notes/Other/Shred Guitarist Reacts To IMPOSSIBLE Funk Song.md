## What's taught

- **Cross-genre transcription as a deliberate creativity tool, stated as the core lesson.** His own frustration-message anecdote (a student stuck playing "the same stuff") is answered with: listen to and transcribe great players in styles outside your own — the inspiration and vocabulary transfers directly into your own playing and writing, even without consciously trying to imitate it.
- **Strict alternate-picking discipline through ties and rests.** When a phrase has a tied note (no new pick stroke, but the "beat" still passes), the down/up alternation must still continue mentally through it — skipping the phantom stroke breaks the down-up-down-up grid and causes inconsistent pick placement later in the phrase, even though the tie itself sounds fine in isolation. Chose alternate picking over economy/sweep/legato specifically to match the recording's "aggressive, on-point" articulation.
- **Uneven note-per-string groupings read as more organic than a fixed pattern.** Because the source line wasn't written for guitar, it naturally mixes 2-note-per-string and 3-note-per-string groupings within the same phrase rather than sticking to one system — cited as part of why it sounds less mechanical than typical guitar-idiomatic licks.
- **A chromatic 16th-note run arranged inside a 7/8 measure**, deliberately avoiding overlapping/ringing notes at speed — a concrete chromatic-in-odd-meter exercise.
- **Syncopated phrasing (fusion feel) defined concretely:** heavy melodic accents deliberately land off the quarter-note pulse rather than on it — practiced against a metronome or a backing track with programmed drums specifically to nail the off-beat accents without the safety net of a live band's visual/aural cues.

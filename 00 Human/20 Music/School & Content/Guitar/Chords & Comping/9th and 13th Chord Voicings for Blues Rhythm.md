# 9th and 13th Chord Voicings for Blues Rhythm

Part of [[Guitar Positions]]. A small vocabulary of **rootless 9th- and 13th-chord grips for blues rhythm guitar** — four-note shapes on the middle four strings, each movable to any I-IV-V key by sliding whole steps — plus two concrete rhythmic **fills** for turning plain chord jabs into a fuller-sounding comping part. Where [[L-Shape Root Map for Blues Chords]] solves "where does my hand go from I to IV to V" for shell voicings, this page is the "what do I actually grab once I'm there" layer, one step up in color from a shell (root-3rd-♭7) toward the 9th/13th extensions [[Chord Voicings]] names as the next thing to add after guide tones. Different content from [[Layering Rhythm Guitar Parts with Triads]] (same channel) — that page stacks three separate triad-based *parts*; this one is a single comping part built from bigger extended-chord grips, with fills woven into the same part rather than layered as a second part.

## The two chord families

Worked in the key of **G** (G9-C9-D9 / G13-C13-D13, a I-IV-V blues), all four-note, all **rootless** (no root in the shape — the bass supplies it):

### 9th chords — position 1
- **G9 (rootless):** notes **B F A D** (3rd-♭7-9-5th). Only sounds like a G9 once a bass player puts a G underneath — this shape alone is ambiguous.
- **C9:** **C E B♭ D G** (root-3rd-♭7-9-5th on top).
- **D9 (the V chord):** slide the C9 shape **up one whole step** — same grip, new root: **D F♯ C E A**.
- **Chromatic connector:** between C9 and G9 there's a half-step passing chord that walks the two together — treat it as connective tissue, not a chord to dwell on.

### 13th chords — position 1
- **G13:** **F B E G** (♭7-3rd-13-root on top).
- **C13:** **B♭ E A C** (♭7-3rd-13-root on top).
- **D13 (the V chord):** slide the C13 shape **up one whole step**: **C F♯ B D**.
- Stated character difference: the 13th-chord family reads **brighter** than the 9th-chord family — same function (I-IV-V), different color, "different tools in your toolbox" for the same progression.

### Moving both families to position 2 (up the neck)
Both shapes are movable — reuse the **C9/D9 shape** (transposed) to cover **G9** up at the 10th fret, then pair it with the **rootless-G9 shape** (transposed) to cover **C9**, then slide that up a whole step again for **D9**. Same relay for the 13th-chord family: the down-low **G13** shape reappears as the up-the-neck **G13** grip in a second position, paired with the C13/D13 shapes relocated the same way. **The generalizable move:** once a shape is learned for one chord, the *next* chord in the I-IV-V cycle is often just that same shape slid a whole step, or a neighboring shape from the same family — you're not learning six unrelated grips, you're learning two movable grips and where the next chord's version of each one lives.

## The rhythm pattern and fills

**Base rhythm template:** accent on **beat 1** and the **"and" of beat 2** — this is the plain comping pattern every fill below gets layered onto. Play this alone first, on just the chords, before adding anything.

**Fill 1 — end-of-measure fill (turns the part into a 2-bar phrase):** on **beat 4** of the first measure, insert a **D major triad** (G-C-E) resolving down to the **top part of a G7 chord** (F-B-D), with a **hammer-on from B♭ to B (half step)** leading into that landing. Over the IV chord (C9), play the same fill shape but **leave the ♭7 (B♭) in** rather than hammering it up to B — because B♭ is already a chord tone of C9, not an outside note there. *(Concrete transcript-grounded takeaway: the same fill lick doesn't transpose blindly across chords — check whether the target note is diatonic to the chord under it before reusing a lick verbatim.)*

**Fill 2 — beginning-of-measure pickup fill (stacks with Fill 1 for a fuller part):** a rhythmic pickup starting on **beat 3 of the previous measure**, alternating **E and G** (two notes, simple back-and-forth) leading into the top of the 2-bar phrase. Over the IV and V chords, the same fill is modified: **E-G-E**, then instead of resolving back up to G, walk **down to the root of the current chord** (root of C for the IV chord, root of D for the V chord). Stack this pickup fill with Fill 1 together for the fullest version of the part.

## Drill — build the part in layers, then relocate it

The video's own build order, worth practicing exactly this way rather than jumping straight to the finished part:

1. **Comp the bare chords** (9th-chord family, position 1) with just the base rhythm (beat 1 + "and" of 2) against a I-IV-V backing track. Get this solid before adding anything.
2. **Add Fill 1 only** (the beat-4 D-triad-to-G7-top fill, with the B♭-stays-in adjustment on the IV chord). Play the 2-bar phrase against the backing track until the hammer-on lands cleanly.
3. **Add Fill 2 on top of Fill 1** (the beat-3 pickup, E/G alternating, with the E-G-E-then-walk-to-root variation on IV/V). This is the fullest version of the 9th-chord part.
4. **Relocate the whole part to position 2** (10th-fret-area shapes) and repeat steps 1-3 there — same rhythm, same fills, different fretboard location.
5. **Combine positions live:** comp one pass through the form in position 1, then jump to position 2 for a second pass, mixing in the fills — the goal stated directly is fluency moving between positions mid-tune, not just knowing each position in isolation.
6. **Repeat the entire 1-5 sequence with the 13th-chord family** (both positions) as a second full pass — same rhythm template, same two fills, brighter-sounding grips.
7. **Free-play stage:** once both families and both fills are solid in both positions, comp freely over the backing track mixing families, positions, and fills at will — the stated end goal, modeling how the shapes actually get used in real playing rather than as a fixed routine.

*(General starting-point structuring of steps 4-7 — the video demonstrates each layer and both positions but the explicit "drill it in this order" framing is the standard way to internalize a build-up part like this.)*

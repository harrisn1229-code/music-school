# The 5 MOST Important Guitar Practice TRICKS! (Make Faster Daily Progress)

## Synopsis

Five metronome-specific practice techniques, none of them "just turn it on": tracking reference notes instead of every note in a fast passage, reprogramming the click to a backbeat feel for groove development, using a MIDI/virtual-instrument click in a DAW instead of the stock click sound, subdividing an odd-meter click into custom groupings, and a specific practice-time-tracking tool recommendation.

## What's taught

- **Reference-note tracking for staying in time on fast passages.** The brain can't consciously track every individual note in a fast run (analogy: focusing on every letter of a word instead of the word itself) — instead, pick out the notes that land ON the metronome's click (or on a drum accent, in a band context) as reference points, and listen specifically for whether THOSE notes are ahead of or behind the click. If the reference points line up, the whole fast passage is very likely in time; drifting reference points signal the actual timing problem without needing to consciously track every note.
- **Backbeat-only click for groove development.** Instead of a metronome counting straight quarters (1-2-3-4), set it (or program a DAW click) to sound ONLY beats 2 and 4 — matching where a drummer's snare typically falls and where an audience claps — and practice making that sparse "2 - 4 - 2 - 4" click feel like it's grooving, rather than counting a stiff 1-2-3-4 in your head. Framed as a practical substitute for playing with a live drummer/band when developing an internal sense of groove and backbeat feel.
- **Use a MIDI track + virtual instrument as your DAW click instead of the stock metronome sound**, specifically for long recording days — a more musical click sound is less fatiguing over many hours than a stock beep, and a MIDI click can be customized per song or per song-section (accented vs. unaccented, different sounds) far more flexibly than most DAWs' built-in metronome settings.
- **Program a custom subdivided click for odd meters, matching the riff's actual internal grouping** — rather than a plain count of "1-2-3-4-5-6-7" for a bar of 7/8, program the click (via the same MIDI-click workflow) to accent the riff's real sub-groups (worked example: a 7/8 arpeggiated sequence felt as 4+3) so the click itself reinforces the riff's actual phrasing rather than a generic beat count. Explicitly recommends listening for the riff's natural grouping (2+2+3, 3+4, etc.) and building the click to match it.
- **Practice-time tracking tool: metronomeonline.com** (no affiliation stated) — recommended specifically for two features: an unaccented, less-fatiguing click sound for general daily practice, and a built-in practice-time tracker in the browser window, which replaced manually timing sessions with a stopwatch/phone app and is credited with materially improving his own routine structure this year.

# Jazzy LoFi Hip-Hop - Beat Breakdown

## Synopsis

Max Konyi's first-ever attempt at writing a lofi hip-hop beat (made in **Studio One**; all techniques below are DAW-agnostic and map onto Ableton), narrated as a genuine experiment rather than a formula — he'd never made one before, likes the genre as a listener, and wanted to see if he could land the feel from scratch. The result is one of the most concrete, plugin-specific breakdowns in this batch: a full RC-20 lofi-preset build module by module, a half-diminished-centered jazzy chord loop, a "fake harp" ornament technique (play a random gesture, conform the notes afterward), a Lydian-dominant decorative run, mod-wheel-driven clarinet realism, and a genuinely clever chord-tail crossfade trick for hiding a mid-ring chord change behind a kick hit. Strongly complementary to this vault's existing [[Neo-Soul - Lo-fi]] genre page — same pillars (loose drums, half-diminished/extended jazz harmony, RC-20 texture, sidechain-everything), extended here with concrete preset-level detail.

## Techniques

**Drums (~1:30–4:30)**
- **Drum sample selection deserves real time — up to an hour on a serious track.** Not every track gets that budget, but the discipline of auditioning kick/snare/hat choices carefully (rather than grabbing the first thing) is treated as one of the most important steps, "possibly the most important."
- **Choke groups on BOTH kicks and hats** — group the two kick samples together (so a fast kick pattern can't have overlapping tails ringing over each other) and do the same for the hats (a closed-hat choke shuts off the open hat) — prevents unnatural overlapping ring, especially important for fast/rolled hits. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **All drum editing done in the piano roll**, by preference, even when other grid/pattern views are available in the DAW — a workflow habit, not a technical requirement.
- **"Slugging" the hi-hats: play every offbeat 8th note slightly BEHIND the beat, deliberately.** A named lofi-specific term for a narrow, consistent late-timing push applied only to the off-8ths (not the whole pattern) — distinct from a swing quantize preset since it's played/felt rather than quantized. Paired with an explicit "aversion to anything landing early/ahead of the beat" — loose and late reads as the style; loose and early reads as a mistake. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Leave the groove almost entirely unquantized** — drums are deliberately loose, not locked to the grid; occasional manual nudges only to fix notes that read as rushed.

**Chords and harmony (~4:30–10:00)**
- **A half-diminished-centered jazzy vamp: F♯ø7 → Fm6 → C9/E, looped**, ambiguous between C major and C minor. Keyscape's "Creative" preset category (a deep sub-library beyond the standard piano/EP patches) supplied the chord-instrument sound. → [[Keyscape]], [[Borrowed Chords|Borrowed Chords]]
- **A secondary arpeggio layer plays the same chord tones in the lower octave, underneath the main chord voicing** — a distinct "Arps" track, not just a doubled part; adds rhythmic movement to a static chord loop without changing the harmony.
- **Full RC-20 lofi-preset build, module by module** (XLN Audio, owned): **Noise** — pink noise preferred over white this time (vinyl skipped deliberately), kept quiet; **Wobble/Flux** — turned up to full to hear the wobble character, then pulled back to a subtler amount, with the wobble set to follow the notes slightly, and switched from RC-20's stereo wobble default to MONO; **Distort** — subtle tube saturation for grit, deliberately skipping the Digital (bitcrush) module this pass even though "it is dope"; **Space** — wants "some big space" from this module; **Magnetic** — demoed cranked all the way for the volume-fluctuation ("magnitude") and flutter (tremolo-like) effect, then brought back to a musical amount. → [[RC-20 Retro Color]]
- **Sidechain everything, as a baseline habit — "especially in lofi."** Applied broadly across the chord/arp/pad layers against the kick, not treated as optional. Values the resulting "spacious," open kick pattern specifically because it gives the kicks meaning when they land. → [[Sidechaining]]
- **The "fake harp" ornament trick: play a random gesture first, conform the pitches to the chord afterward.** Not skilled enough to play an idiomatic harp glissando live, so: sweep a hand across the keys (white keys read better than black for the desired gesture) with NO regard for which notes land — just capturing the timing/gesture feel — then go back and manually correct every note to fit the actual underlying chord. Timing is kept as-played; only pitch gets fixed in the edit pass. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **A Lydian-dominant decorative run over a dominant 13♯11 chord** — a pad voicing colors the underlying C9 chord into a C13♯11 (adding the ♯4/♯11 and a natural 13); over that color, played the full Lydian-dominant scale (C major scale with a ♭7 and a ♯4/♯11 — i.e. the 4th mode of G melodic minor) straight up and down as a decorative gesture rather than picking individual "correct" notes deliberately. → [[Scale Modes Reference]]
- **Full-octave pitch-bend sweep on a pad, tested both directions** — auditioned a pad note bent a full octave down then rising to pitch (vs. the reverse) specifically to make the pad "ramp back into the beginning of the loop" — used as a transitional/anacrusis device rather than an expressive one-note bend.
- **Realistic solo instrument via mod-wheel dynamics + hand-corrected fingerings, aiming for "sample or real player" quality** — a Kontakt clarinet ("Herring Clarinet") melody played live using the mod wheel for dynamic/expression control, then the "fumbled" fingerings from the live take were manually edited afterward — the goal explicitly stated as making a virtual instrument read as authentically played, not just technically correct. → [[Kontakt 7]]
- **Chord analysis of the finished melody, mapped scale-degree by scale-degree against each chord** — worked example: over F♯ø7 the melody sits on the ♭5 resolving down to the ♭3; the same shape recurs over Fm6 (♭5→♭3 relative to that chord); over C9/E the melody moves 5th→♭3→major 3rd as the chord turns dominant (natural 4→♯4/♯11→5); over the return to Fm6 it's the natural 7th falling to the 5th, landing near (not exactly on) the tonic — a concrete demonstration of writing a melody by tracking exactly which chord tone/tension it's landing on at each moment, not picking pitches by feel. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **Accent hit deliberately timed to bleed into the next chord, then physically edited/hidden via a chord-tail crossfade.** A short Serum cluster stab (voiced as a C minor cluster) is smacked on a snare hit right after the melodic phrase resolves — but its tail is long enough to ring over the NEXT chord change and clash. Fix: bounce the exact same hit twice — once voiced as the "before" (minor) chord, once as the "after" (target) chord — then in the audio editor, swap/overlap the two bounces' tails and crossfade between them at the seam, so the ringing content itself morphs from one chord's harmonic color into the other's rather than clashing or cutting. Timed to land exactly on a kick hit so the sidechain ducking further masks the edit — "you're not even going to hear the change happen." → [[Committing and Printing]]

**Bass (~10:30–13:00)**
- **Deliberately remove a bass patch's front-end punch/attack for lofi** — normally chases punchy bass attack, but here actively worked to soften/remove it, since punch reads as too clean/aggressive for the genre; also shelved off the extreme sub-lows and addressed one resonant "throaty" frequency by ear rather than by a fixed EQ curve. A genre-specific reversal of a usual mixing instinct. → [[Bass Design]]

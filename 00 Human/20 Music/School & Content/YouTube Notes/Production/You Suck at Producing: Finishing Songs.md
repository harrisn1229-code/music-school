# You Suck at Producing: Finishing Songs

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XXKTJsmTMTQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XXKTJsmTMTQ)

## Synopsis

A comedic "parenting" framing (beats as babies you raise or abandon) around the real problem of unfinished tracks — what to do when a loose song structure stalls out and you can't tell what it needs next. Underbelly's answer isn't one trick but a small set of session-discipline habits: don't relisten to a work-in-progress unless you're actually about to work on it (repeated passive listening cements you to a stuck version instead of moving it forward), always leave a session with the next section already started so momentum survives the break, and — when a song is genuinely stuck — go digging through the graveyard of abandoned old projects for a usable part rather than starting from nothing. He demonstrates the last one live: auditioning a dead, unfinished beat, finding one small moment worth keeping, dropping it into a new track literally named "stem cells" in the stuck song, and finishing the track with it. Closes with a plug for his paid Zoom lessons (not a technique, not routed).

## Techniques

- **Never relisten to a work-in-progress unless you're about to actively work on it** — passive relistening cements the current, unfinished version in your head as "the" version, making you attached to a past state of the beat instead of pushing it forward. Only hit play on a WIP when you're sitting down to actually change something.
- **End every session with the next section already started, not just the current one finished** — if you're wrapping up near the end of a session, begin the next section before you stop rather than leaving a clean-but-dead-ended stopping point. Having *some* idea already down for where you were headed makes it far easier to pick the thread back up next time versus starting cold.
- **Mine your graveyard of abandoned projects for a part that unblocks a stuck song** — when a track has stalled and you can't tell what it needs, open old unfinished/abandoned beats ("dead baby beats") and audition them specifically looking for one usable piece — a texture, a sound, a small musical idea — rather than a whole part. Demonstrated live: he searches an old shelved idea, finds a short vocal-ish "wower" texture moment that doesn't work as its own song but has "a place," creates a new track named **"Stem Cells"** in the stuck song, and drops just that harvested piece in — enough to unstick the arrangement and finish the track.

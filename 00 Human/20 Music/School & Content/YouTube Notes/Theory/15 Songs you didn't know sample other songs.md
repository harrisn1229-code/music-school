# 15 Songs you didn't know sample other songs

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EUb7vTDN3JI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EUb7vTDN3JI)

## Synopsis

A "hidden samples" catalog — hit songs whose sampled origins are non-obvious because the sampling song became far more famous than its source, with several worked production/legal techniques for how the sample was extracted or cleared.

## Techniques

- **Pitch/tempo-shifted sample for a mood transformation**: Gnarls Barkley's "Crazy" is built from the first 8 bars of "Last Man Standing" (a 1968 spaghetti-western score cue), raised in both pitch and tempo.
- **Reconstructing an instrumental loop by exploiting silent gaps in a vocal-heavy source (pre-AI stem separation)**: Portishead's "Glory Box" samples Isaac Hayes' "Ike's Rap II," which has Hayes speaking over the sampled section — Portishead stitched an instrumental loop together by using the small gaps BETWEEN his spoken phrases across multiple loop rotations, since those gaps happened to line up just enough to reconstruct a clean instrumental.
- **Interpolation vs. sampling, a legal-risk-driven choice**: Oasis's "Go Let It Out" drum groove is lifted from Johnny Jenkins' 1970 cover, but by ear it sounds re-performed rather than lifted audio — likely a deliberate choice to interpolate rather than sample, specifically to reduce legal exposure.
- **A sample-lawsuit-to-re-record case, with the replacement singer's own career payoff**: Black Box's "Ride On Time" sampled Loleatta Holloway's "Love Sensation" vocal without clearing it; songwriter Dan Hartman sued, and Black Box reissued the track with the sampled vocal re-sung by session singer Heather Small — who later became M People's lead singer.
- **A multi-hop sample chain inside a single song's own intro**: House of Pain's "Jump Around" stacks THREE separate samples — a horn intro from Bob & Earl's "Harlem Shuffle," a slowed main groove loop from Chubby Checker's "Popeye the Hitchhiker," and a screech sound that isn't sampled directly from its ultimate source (a Motown track, "Shoot Your Shot") but via an intermediate 1989 track ("Ain't Saying Nothing" by Divine Styler) that had already sampled it first.

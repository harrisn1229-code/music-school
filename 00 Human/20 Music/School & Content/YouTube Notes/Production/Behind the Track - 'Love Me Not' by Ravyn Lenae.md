## Synopsis

Producer(s) walk through building "Love Me Not" (Ravyn Lenae) from a sample pack through to a co-write with Anderson .Paak, then a later "make it hit harder" enhancement pass. Mostly songwriting/creative-process narrative, with a few concrete production/arrangement techniques.

## What's taught

- **Vintage-tone layering built from a sample-pack seed, not from scratch.** The song originated from a friend's sample pack containing a guitar tone built from **~10 layered guitar takes**; a drum break was pulled from a personal drum-break archive and layered over that sample as the foundation.
- **Vintage-modern hybrid instrumentation stack.** Bass, acoustic + electric guitar layered together, and a **Farfisa** (vintage combo organ) added specifically to push the track toward a 50s/60s vintage character while keeping the arrangement contemporary enough to not read as a period piece.
- **Co-write vocal-tone reference, adapted rather than copied.** Anderson .Paak wrote and sang the original hook as a reference/demo vocal with a distinctive "megaphone"-style thin, compressed tone; the final artist's vocal deliberately echoes that same tonal character on the verses (intentionally thinner/more intimate, like "talking through an intercom") while opening up bigger and fuller on the hook — a tonal contrast used as a structural device between verse and chorus.
- **Deliberate lyrical/musical juxtaposition** — an uplifting, joyful-sounding arrangement paired with lyrics that are more ambivalent/bittersweet underneath (the "love me, love me not" flower-picking metaphor) — the contrast is the intended effect, not an error to reconcile.
- **A later "make it hit harder" pass, framed as a genre-lean move: push a track from a "rock" feel toward hip-hop by adding low end and overall fullness**, while explicitly preserving the existing groove rather than changing it — the enhancement targeted low-frequency weight and density, not rhythm or arrangement.

# Secondary Dominant Insertion at a Minor Blues Chord Change

**Stay in strict minor pentatonic/blues phrasing through a minor blues, then substitute a real secondary dominant chord — not just an implied one — into the single bar right before a chord change, and layer three escalating devices onto that one bar: a plain dominant-7 arpeggio, a diminished-7 substitute for it, then altered voicings/scale.** Worked over an **A minor blues (Am / Dm / Em)** at the **Am → Dm transition**: insert **A7** (V7 of D minor — a secondary dominant, V7/iv) in the bar immediately before Dm lands. Sits next to [[Think Different with Chord Substitutions]] (same channel, same core move — insert the target chord's own V7 as a transition chord in a minor i–iv vamp — but that lesson works a **static two-chord vamp** with a relative-major-arpeggio layer instead of this page's diminished/altered layers) and [[Dominant-Altered Superimposition Over a Minor Chord]] (the scale-superimposition sibling — that page **implies** a V7-altered sound over a chord that never actually changes; this page inserts a **real chord change** at one specific spot in a moving progression). Also a minor-blues-scoped sibling to [[Dominant 7 Arpeggios Over Blues Pentatonics]] (that page targets IV7/V7 arpeggios on a **major** blues; this page targets one specific **secondary**-dominant transition inside a minor one).

## The setup: one real chord substitution, not a superimposition

Over an A minor blues, the diatonic move would be **Am → Dm** straight. Instead, substitute **A7 in the bar right before Dm** — A7 is a major triad (A C♯ E) plus a ♭7 (G), i.e. **A, C♯, E, G**, the V7 of D minor. Works two ways: if the band actually voices the A7 in the rhythm, or if the band just sits on Am the whole time and the substitution is purely melodic/soloed — "if it rubs a little bit against that A minor chord that's perfectly okay," since the tension is the point and it resolves the instant Dm arrives. **Everything else in the solo stays strict minor-pentatonic/blues phrasing** — this is a targeted one-bar insert, not a wholesale scale swap for the form. → [[Secondary Dominants]], [[Blues Form and Harmony]] § *Minor blues*.

## Device 1 — the A7 arpeggio

Notes: **A, C♯, E, G** (root–3rd–5th–♭7). Play in one position, starting from different chord tones for different melodic shapes, and resolve by landing on a chord tone of Dm when the change arrives. Once solid in one spot, **relocate the same shape to different positions and octaves** across the neck — it's a movable line, not fixed to one box.

## Device 2 — C♯ diminished 7th substitute (one-note edit of Device 1)

**Derivation:** take the A7 arpeggio (A, C♯, E, G), **drop the root (A)** — the remaining C♯, E, G is a **C♯ diminished triad** — then **add B♭** on top to complete a full **C♯ diminished 7th** (C♯, E, G, B♭). **The practical payoff: every A7 arpeggio line already under the fingers is reusable — just swap the A for a B♭.** No new shape to learn from scratch, one note changed. Sound: more dissonant/tense against the Am chord than the plain arpeggio, resolving "really nicely" — arguably more colorful — into Dm. Same relocate-across-the-neck practice once comfortable in one spot. This is the same "diminished 7th built off a dominant's 3rd" substitution mechanic documented generally on [[Diminished Chords]], applied here to a specific secondary-dominant transition rather than a primary V7.

## Device 3 — altered voicings and the A altered scale

**Chord voicings:** start from a basic **shell voicing** of A7 — root, ♭7, major 3rd, no 5th — then add color tones on top, one at a time or combined: **♭5**, **♯5**, **♭9**, **♯9**. Named favorite stack: **♯5 + ♯9**, then dropping to **♭9** before resolving to Dm.

**Full scale:** the **A altered scale** — **A (root) – B♭ (♭9) – C (♯9) – C♯ (major 3rd) – E♭ (♭5) – F (♯5) – G (♭7) – A** — i.e. the same basic dominant shell (root–3rd–♭7) plus the four alteration tones layered on top, all sitting in one neck position. Scalable from a short line (♯9 → ♭9 → root → ♭7 → resolve down into Dm's minor 3rd) up to fully fleshing out the whole scale in a longer phrase — "up to you to decide what sound you're going for." → [[Altered Chords]].

## Practice / drilling

1. **Nail the harmony first, then the shapes.** Confirm by ear/on paper why A7 works as V7/iv before drilling any lines — the whole technique depends on hearing the dominant pull, not just memorizing a shape over a chord symbol.
2. **Drill Device 1 (plain arpeggio) alone until it's automatic**, starting from every one of its four chord tones (A, C♯, E, G) in one position, always resolving onto a Dm chord tone. Only then move to Device 2.
3. **Drill the one-note edit explicitly** — play an A7 arpeggio line, then replay the *identical* fingering with B♭ substituted for A, back to back, so the ear locks onto how small the mechanical change is versus how big the sound change is.
4. **Layer in Device 3 last** — start from the bare shell (root-♭7-3rd, no 5th), add exactly one alteration at a time (♭5, then ♯5, then ♭9, then ♯9) before combining them, rather than jumping straight to the full altered scale.
5. **Relocate every device across the neck/octaves** once solid in one position — same shapes, different starting frets, so the technique isn't tied to one box.
6. **Practice the pentatonic-default discipline directly**: play a full 12-bar chorus using nothing but minor pentatonic, and consciously "break out" only in the one transition bar, then drop back to pentatonic immediately after landing on Dm — the skill being drilled is *restraint everywhere except the one spot*, not constant outside playing.
7. **Transpose the whole system to other keys/transitions** once comfortable in A — any i→iv (or i→any diatonic target) move in a minor blues takes the identical recipe: insert the target's own V7, then optionally its diminished substitute, then its altered scale, all confined to the one bar before the change.

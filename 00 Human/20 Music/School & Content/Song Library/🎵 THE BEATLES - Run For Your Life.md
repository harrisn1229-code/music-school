# Run For Your Life — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (6 bars on one chord) · Verse/Refrain (a fused 16-bar compound section) · Verse/Refrain · Break (**a real 12-bar blues**) · Verse/Refrain · Verse/Refrain · Outro (fade). The acoustic guitar enters just before the downbeat and is accented so that **the beat is genuinely unlocatable until the rest of the band arrives**.

The nominal key is D major but the song's centre of gravity is B minor — every verse veers off to cadence there, and the tune stripped of its chords points at B minor far more than D. The **I–vi cliché** was early-Beatles staple vocabulary and had all but vanished by this period; here it does structural work rather than colour, tilting the whole song toward the relative minor ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]).

The refrain is a ready/set/**surprise**: vi goes to V-of-V twice and then, on the third pass, to ii instead — in first inversion, carrying a G♯/G♮ cross-relation against the two E major chords before it ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). Repeating V-of-V while withholding V for most of the song is a standing Beatles device; the break is the **only place V appears at all**, and it is a textbook [[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]] except that V repeats in bars 9–10 instead of moving to IV. A rising chromatic bass lick hauls B minor back to D — and is pointedly absent after the break, which ended in D.

Vocals are fussily staged: single-tracked and exposed in the verses, apparently double-tracked with **each track panned to its own channel** in the refrains.

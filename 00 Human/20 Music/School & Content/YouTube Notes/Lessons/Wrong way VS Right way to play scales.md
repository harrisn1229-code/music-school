# Wrong way VS Right way to play scales

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bw8oUp9geuY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bw8oUp9geuY)

## Synopsis

A defense of a viral Instagram clip: linear (straight up/down) scale practice isn't wrong, it's the correct way to first memorize a scale shape in every key — but continuing to drill it after memorization stops being productive. Seven follow-on practice methods are demonstrated, ordered easiest to hardest, from soloing over the scale's own chords through to a full-fretboard diatonic-triad climb.

## Techniques

Full seven-round progression extracted and organized in [[Beyond-Linear Scale Practice Progression]]:

1. Solo the scale over its own diatonic chords (looped).
2. Interpolate the root between every scale tone (Joe Satriani-credited) to break pure sequential muscle memory.
3. Practice in skipped intervals (thirds, fourths, fifths, sixths) — see existing [[Scale Sequences in Thirds]].
4. Say each note's letter name aloud while playing — a fretboard-memorization drill, harder in accidental-heavy keys.
5. "Chord scales" — harmonize the scale into moving triad and inverted-7th-chord voicings climbing the neck.
6. Sweep-pick the scale's diatonic 7th chords as a chain of arpeggios rather than picking single notes.
7. Climb the full fretboard playing every diatonic triad of the key — already documented via [[Triads as a Fretboard Map]] and [[Hard, Yet Effective Way To Learn The fretboard]]; ranked hardest here for time-to-mastery, not raw technical difficulty (that's round 6).

# Reharmonizing The Slow Blues

## Synopsis

## What's taught

### Baseline — the plain 1-4-5 in G
`G7 | C7 | G7 | G7 / C7 | C7 | G7 | G7 / D7 | C7 | G7 | D7`. Soloing baseline: G major/minor pentatonic for general vocabulary, then the individual chord-tone arpeggios (G7, C7, D7) once pentatonic vocabulary is comfortable — the stated on-ramp before any reharmonization is worth attempting.

### Variation 1 — chord-substitution pass (still reads as a blues, not full jazz)

**1. Bar 3 — one chromatic passing chord, five valid readings.** Take the **rootless G9 voicing** (3rd–♭7–9th–5th, no root) and slide the whole shape up a half step. Because the four notes don't change, only the implied root does, the same shape now reads as any of:
- **A♭ dominant 9** → play A♭ mixolydian.
- **C half-diminished / Cm7♭5** → play C locrian.
- **E♭ minor 6** (an E♭ minor triad with the major 6th in the bass) → E♭ dorian-family color (Ruch's own phrasing cuts off mid-sentence in the transcript — he names the chord but the scale word is lost to a caption gap; E♭ dorian is the standard chord-scale for a minor-6 sound and is flagged here as *(general starting point, not a direct quote)*).
- **D altered dominant** — Ruch's own favorite reading of the four. The same four notes spell the ♭7, major 3rd, ♭13 and ♭9 of D7, so he plays **D altered** over it. Resolves back to a G chord tone.

This is the same "one grip, several harmonic identities" device the vault already tracks on [[Multifunctional Voicings]] — see **Routed to** below for the new entry it seeds there.

**2. Bars 5-6 — after I→IV, a chain of minor 7th chords: ii → iii → ♭iii → ii.** In G: **Am7 → Bm7 → B♭m7 → Am7.** Two layers of vocabulary given for it:
- **Plain chord tones** of each minor 7 chord in turn (know the arpeggio for Am7, Bm7, B♭m7 cold and target those notes).
- **Relative-major-arpeggio substitution** — play the **major 7 arpeggio a minor 3rd above** each minor chord's root instead of the minor chord's own scale: **C major7 over Am7** (giving the ♭3-5-♭7-9 of an Am9-no-root sound), **D major7 over Bm7**, **D♭ major7 over B♭m7**. Same mechanism already logged on [[Chord Substitution]] from a different Jack Ruch video ("Think Different with Chord Substitutions," over a static i–iv vamp) — this is a second, independent worked instance, this time over a **chromatically descending chain of four minor 7ths inside a blues** rather than a static two-chord vamp. See **Routed to** below.

**3. The V-chord approach — a ♭VI7 lydian-dominant color chord before landing on V.** Instead of moving straight to the V chord (D7), go up a half step first to the **♭VI dominant** (A♭7 in G) and resolve from there into D7. Over the A♭7, Ruch's go-to color is the **lydian dominant scale** (A♭ lydian dominant: A♭-B♭-C-D-E♭-F-G♭) — stated directly to resolve especially smoothly into the following V chord.

### Variation 2 — full jazz-harmony pass (classic ii-V/turnaround vocabulary layered on top)

**4. Bar 1 — a 2-5-1 into the IV chord.** Rather than landing on the IV chord (C7) directly, treat C7 as a momentary "I" and precede it with its own ii-V: **Dm7 (ii of C) → G7 (V of C) → C7 (IV of the tune).** Outline the chord tones of Dm7 and G7 to build a line that moves through the substitution smoothly. Same device the vault already has many worked instances of on [[The ii-V-I]] and [[Blues Form and Harmony]] — see **Routed to**.

**5. Bars 3-4 — elaborated into a full 3-6-2-5-1 landing on the same IV chord.** Extend #4 backward: **E half-diminished (Em7♭5, the iii/vi-of-C slot) → A7 altered → Dm7 → G7(altered) → C7.** Two ways to hear the opening Em7♭5→A7alt→Dm7 stretch, both usable for lines:
- As a **minor ii–V–i** into D minor (Em7♭5 = ii°, A7alt = V, Dm7 = i) — vocabulary: a **B♭ diminished-7 arpeggio** resolving into D minor.
- As **G minor 6 / G Dorian reinterpreted** — read the Em7♭5 shape as a Gm6 chord instead and solo G Dorian over it, then move into the A7-altered color, resolving to a chord tone.

**6. Bar 6 — a diminished passing chord bridging the IV chord back to I.** Standard device: from the IV chord (C7), slide up a half step to a **diminished 7th passing chord**, then resolve into I (G). Play straight out of that diminished 7th arpeggio. Same "sharp-4 diminished, IV back to I" device the vault already documents at length on [[Diminished Chords]] and [[Blues Form and Harmony]] (bar 5-6's most common option) — this is a fresh, independently-sourced confirming instance in G rather than the usual worked keys (F/C).

**7. Bars 9-11 (the turnaround section) — a 3-6-2-5 in the tonic key, then a compressed 1-6-2-5.** Now back in G as "1": **Bm7 (iii) → E7 (VI7/secondary dominant) → Am7 (ii) → D7 (V, or an altered-dominant variation of it) → G (I)**, then, in the actual final turnaround bar, a quick compressed **1-6-2-5** (G → E7 → Am7 → D7). Same VI7/III7–VI7 turnaround family already extensively documented on [[Turnarounds]] and [[The ii-V-I]] — a further confirming instance, again notable mainly for reusing the identical shape at two different structural moments in the same chorus (bars 3-4's C-targeted version and bars 9-11's G-targeted version), which is a good demonstration that "3-6-2-5" is one movable shape, not a key-specific formula.

### Stated practice philosophy
Ruch's closing framing, stated directly and matching the vault's existing "chord tones first" progression ladder ([[Chord-Tone Soloing]]): **know your triads and 7th-chord arpeggios cold first** — that's the foundation everything above is built from — and only stretch into the altered-dominant / lydian-dominant colors once the plain chord tones are comfortable. He describes this as something he "practices all the time," navigating the same substitutions around different parts of the fretboard rather than in one fixed position — i.e. drill each device (the chromatic-slide reharmonized chord, the minor-7 chain, the inserted ii-V's, the diminished passing chord, the turnaround) as a **movable shape**, and specifically practice landing a chord tone exactly on the beat as each new chord arrives, the same "nail the transition first" principle already logged from this channel's other blues-reharm videos.

## Concrete drills to lift and practice

1. **The five-way chromatic-slide chord drill.** Grab the rootless G9 shape (3rd-♭7-9th-5th on the middle strings), slide it up a half step, and practice naming/hearing all four readings in sequence over a static droned root: A♭9 (A♭ mixolydian) → Cm7♭5 (C locrian) → E♭m6 (E♭ dorian) → D7alt (D altered) → resolve back to a G7 chord tone. Once each is comfortable in isolation, pick just one (start with D altered, Ruch's own favorite) and drop it into an actual 12-bar blues chorus at bar 3.
2. **The minor-7-chain relative-major drill.** Loop Am7-Bm7-B♭m7-Am7 (bars 5-6 after I→IV) and play it two ways back to back: first straight chord tones of each minor 7, then the relative-major-a-minor-3rd-up substitution (Cmaj7/Dmaj7/D♭maj7/Cmaj7) over the identical four bars. Practice switching between the two approaches mid-phrase once each is solid alone.
3. **The ♭VI7-lydian-dominant approach-to-V drill.** Over just the V chord's approach (the bar before D7 arrives), slide up a half step to A♭7, run the A♭ lydian dominant scale (A♭-B♭-C-D-E♭-F-G♭), then resolve into D7 (plain or altered) and on to G. Loop this two-chord approach on its own before dropping it into the full form.
4. **The 2-5-1-into-IV plus 3-6-2-5-1 elaboration drill.** Bar 1: play Dm7-G7 landing on C7 (the IV). Once solid, extend it backward per bars 3-4: Em7♭5-A7alt-Dm7-G7-C7, alternating between the two available readings of the opening two chords (minor-ii-V-into-Dm vocabulary vs. Gm6/G-Dorian vocabulary) so both feel available under the fingers.
5. **The diminished-passing-chord bar-6 drill.** From C7 (IV, bar 6), slide up a half step to the diminished 7th chord and resolve to G (I) — practice arpeggiating straight out of the diminished 7th shape as the connective material, matching the vault's existing sharp-4-diminished vocabulary on [[Diminished Chords]].
6. **The double 3-6-2-5 drill.** Play the same Xm7-VII... shape (major-3rd-based iii-VI-ii-V) twice in one chorus at two different structural spots — once targeting C (bars 3-4) and once targeting G (bars 9-11) — to internalize it as one movable shape usable anywhere a ii-V-I would resolve, not a fixed lick tied to one key center.
7. **Full-chorus assembly + transposition.** Once devices 1-6 are individually solid, run them together over one full 12-bar chorus in G exactly as laid out above, then transpose the whole reharmonized chorus to at least one other key (following the video's own stated 1-4-5 number-system logic) so the substitutions transfer by interval relationship rather than by memorized chord names.

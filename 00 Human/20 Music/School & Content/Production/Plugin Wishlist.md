# Plugin Wishlist

Gaps identified while building the [[FX Chain System]] — the collection (302 plugins) covers nearly everything; these are the few holes where a purchase would actually change results, ranked by impact. (Rule of thumb before buying anything: the [[Production System Report]] first-call table probably already covers the job.)

## High impact (real workflow gaps)

1. **Melodyne (Studio or Editor)** — the one genuine hole. Manual, note-level pitch/timing editing is the documented method for the natural lanes ([[Frank Ocean Vocal Chain]], Finneas comping) and for fixing stack notes Auto-Tune can't isolate. Auto-Tune Pro is a *style*; Melodyne is *surgery*. → Used in: every vocal chain's prep step. Reinforced by an instrumental use-case, not just vocals: Melodyning a bounced-and-pitched-down sample to extract its own bass notes (found B♭–C–B♭–C–A♭) before writing a new bass part against it, pitching the whole sample up 2–3 semitones first when the extracted notes read too low — see [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[PARTYNEXTDOOR Jazz Sample Chain]] — loner, [how to make moody vintage r&b samples for partynextdoor (sounds like a real jazz sample)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wD4RzEKo7vU)
2. **VocAlign (Synchro Arts, Standard is enough)** — automatic time (+pitch in Pro) alignment of doubles/stacks to the lead. The research was unanimous that alignment beats all stack processing ([[1 Double Vocal Track|Double Vocal Track]]); doing it by hand works but costs 20 min/song. → Used in: every hook stack.
3. **Youlean Loudness Meter 2 (FREE)** — proper LUFS-I/short-term + true peak on the master. The Ozone Maximizer shows some of this; a dedicated meter is clearer. Zero-cost fix for [[Clipping and Loudness]] targets — just install it.

6. **Room correction (Sonarworks SoundID or ARC)** — not a plugin for the music, a plugin for the *room*. If mixes translate inconsistently ([[Referencing and Translation]]) despite good referencing, this is the actual fix for a bedroom control room.

## Low priority (covered, but nice someday)

- **Serum 2 expansions / Omnisphere Keyscape integration** — content, not capability (Omnisphere already reads Keyscape patches as a satellite — verify it's enabled; that combo is a hidden superpower you may already own).
- **A dedicated vintage sampler emu (RX950/S950 style)** — the [[Neo-Soul - Lo-fi]] crunch; [[RC-20 Retro Color]] + [[Decimort 2]] get ~90% there.

- **Eventide Omnipressor and Tokyo Dawn Labs Molot (free) — compressed at 1:1/no measured gain reduction purely for the harmonic/timbral shift, an analog-mastering-era habit most digital compressors can't replicate.** Omnipressor at 1:1 with no measured compression or expansion still generates a strong third-order harmonic and noticeably maximizes the signal via its input/output stages — usable purely to prepare a track's character/level for the next processor with zero dynamics change. Molot at no measured attenuation introduces something like a 6 dB/octave high-pass subtle enough for mastering (~0.5 dB attenuated at 50 Hz), boosts everything above 200 Hz by ~0.2 dB and the air region by ~0.3 dB, while generating aggressive even+odd harmonics at high amplitude. No owned digital compressor currently supports this "great-sounding compressor engaged with zero GR" use case. → [[Compression]], [[Compression Staging]] — Sage Audio, [5 Advanced Compression Techniques (Simple to Complex)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V1lZaf8NWd0)
- **MSED (free) — a dedicated mid/side mute utility used specifically to preview how a stereo vocal effect (reverb, delay, micro-pitch, chorus) will translate once a phone/portable speaker folds it down to mono.** Send the stereo effect via an aux, insert MSED on the return, mute the side image, and listen to what survives — a fast mono-compatibility check distinct from a generic mono-sum utility since it isolates just the side content on a send rather than the whole mix. Applies to any stereo instrument, not only vocals. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Referencing and Translation]] — Sage Audio, [Perfecting Vocals on Modern Playback Systems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pzEG3HxI3WE)
- **Melda Audio Saturate — confirmed ASYMMETRICAL saturation (even + odd harmonics), a useful contrast case against owned [[Saturn 2]] (confirmed SYMMETRICAL, odd-only).** Used in Sage Audio's distortion breakdown purely as a comparison example, not a settings walkthrough — the real content is the confirmed harmonic-behavior fact itself: two general-purpose "saturator" plugins with the same broad label can land in structurally different distortion buckets. Worth vetting specifically when a part needs even-harmonic warmth that Saturn 2's symmetrical algorithm can't supply without switching its own internal saturation style. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Saturn 2]] — Sage Audio, [Distortion: The Definitive Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lnoMqI8Hodo)
- **Air Windows plugins (free) — use BIT SHIFTING instead of word-length extension for gain/pan changes, in fixed 6 dB increments with zero rounding-error risk.** Normal gain/pan changes extend a signal's word length to represent fine amplitude changes, which can introduce quantization/rounding error on lower-bit-depth material (mostly inaudible on modern 32/64-bit DAW processing, but the mechanism is real). Bit shifting instead moves the entire bit series left (+6 dB) or right (−6 dB) — word length never changes, so no rounding error, but every change locks to 6 dB steps; the same mechanic applies to panning. Worth trying specifically when "closer to the original recorded signal" character matters more than fine gain resolution. → [[Saturation and Distortion]] — Sage Audio, [3 Advanced Audio Concepts You've (Probably) Never Heard Of](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cjwBvTtE5-I)

# DOMINANT 7 Sus4 Arpeggio Guitar Shapes for Soloing with Chordal Tones

## Synopsis

Ninth entry in Jared Borkowski's 12-chord-type melodic-arpeggio series (same series already in the vault via [[AUGMENTED Triad Arpeggio Guitar Shapes for Soloing with Chordal Tones]] and [[HALF DIMINISHED Arpeggio Guitar Shapes for Soloing with Chordal Tones]]) — this installment covers **dominant 7 sus4** (1-4-5-♭7), worked from a C root across all five CAGED-derived positions. The vault already has the series' reusable five-step practice ladder fully documented on [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]] § *Single-chord-type ladder*, so this page routes only what's genuinely new to this chord type: the specific fingerings for the five sus4 positions, and — the most useful transcript-grounded insight — an explicit warning about the chord's biggest trap: because a dominant 7sus4 arpeggio has no 3rd, adding the "obvious"-sounding minor 3rd turns it into C minor pentatonic by ear, which is NOT the implied harmony of a sus4 chord (no 3rd at all, major or minor) and can mislead a soloist who isn't paying attention. Jared's recommended step-5 add-note scale is Mixolydian (the ♭7 already in the chord makes this the natural fit, not major scale).

## What's taught

- **Series/resource context** — 9th video in the 12-chord-type series; free "chord tone vocabulary pack" PDF (all 12 types × 5 positions) offered via soundguitarlessons.com. Next entry in the series (confirmed in-video): augmented triad → already in the vault as [[AUGMENTED Triad Arpeggio Guitar Shapes for Soloing with Chordal Tones]].
- **The five-step ladder, run on all five C dominant-7sus4 positions** — same method as the rest of the series (map root-to-root → melodic pattern off each chord tone → constant chord-tone-only improv "fitness test" → musical phrasing → add non-chord tones). Full method already on [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]] § *Single-chord-type ladder* — not re-documented here.
- **Chord-specific trap (new, transcript-grounded): dominant 7sus4 has no 3rd, so it's harmonically ambiguous — and the ear reflexively wants to fill in a minor 3rd, turning the line into C minor pentatonic, which is the WRONG implied sound.** Jared calls this out explicitly mid-lesson: because there's no 3rd (major or minor) in the chord itself, the arpeggio "sounds very pentatonic" if you're not careful, and it's "important that we don't just mindlessly fill in the note that would make it C minor pentatonic... that minor third is not really part of the harmony." He notes it CAN work once you're used to treating the chord as harmonically nebulous (it starts to imply a minor 11 sound), but flags it as a real trap in a piece of music where the harmony genuinely is a plain dominant 7sus4 — the minor-3rd color isn't the implied sound and can read as a mistake.
- **Recommended step-5 scale: Mixolydian (i.e. C Mixolydian / F major), not the major scale of the chord's own root** — the extra color note he demonstrates most is the major 3rd of the IV (a 4th-scale-degree-relative color), explicitly distinguished from the "wrong" minor 3rd trap above. Chromatic connecting between established chord/scale tones is fine as always.
- **Position-specific technique notes (five CAGED-derived shapes, all off C):**
  - Position 1: significant pinky-hopping/rolling required due to the guitar's fourths tuning colliding with the sus4 chord's own stacked-4th-like structure — flagged as one of the more physically awkward shapes in the series so far.
  - Position 2: a fingering choice between reaching back for the next chord tone vs. an alternate 1-3-1 / 3-4-3 fingering if the reach is uncomfortable.
  - Position 5 (last shape, near top of neck): "inchworm technique" — contract the hand into a cramped position, then expand out the other side; recommended fingering **3rd finger → pinky → 1st finger → pinky → shift to 1st finger for the top notes → pinky, 3rd again**.
- **General practice-note callouts (consistent with the rest of the series, not sus4-specific): deliberately no metronome for vocabulary-mapping work (reserved for dedicated time-work), and the "fitness test" (step 3) is explicitly about proving to yourself you know the shape, not about musicality — musicality is steps 4-5.**

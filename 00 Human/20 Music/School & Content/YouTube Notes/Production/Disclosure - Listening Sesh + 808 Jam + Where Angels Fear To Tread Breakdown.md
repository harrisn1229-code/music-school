# Disclosure - Listening Sesh + 808 Jam + Where Angels Fear To Tread Breakdown

A 3.5-hr multi-part stream: a hands-on **TR-8 (808/909) jam**, a long **songwriting Q&A with Howard** (their chord/melody process — genuinely valuable), and the main event, a full breakdown of **"Where Angels Fear To Tread"** (a **Four Freshmen "Fools Rush In"** flip, the same 40s jazz-acapella source as Blue World). Best material: the **whisper-track-for-clarity** trick, **velocity→sample-*pitch* to humanize claps**, **Logic tremolo at 180° = an auto-panner**, the historical **"one EQ per chord" resonance carve** (why Pro-Q 3 / Soothe were a revolution), and Howard's **chord-and-melody writing method** (+ the "don't mute the chords while writing the bass" cautionary tale). ## Techniques

### Where Angels Fear To Tread (Four Freshmen flip)
- **Flip a vintage acapella into modern club production** — sampled the **Four Freshmen** (40s/50s jazz-acapella, Brian Wilson's #1 influence) and rebuilt it to "stand up to modern production." Warp in **Ableton (Pro mode)** — 84 → 108 BPM needs clean algorithms; "Logic warping I can hear working, Ableton's cleaner." → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].
- **Whisper-track for *clarity*, not just air** — Guy and Howard whisper the lyrics along with the sample (high-passed to the air + [[Gem Dopamine]]), blended under it to make an old crackly recording intelligible. They do this on *most* songs, even getting big singers (Mary J. Blige) to record a whisper take. → [[De-essing]], [[AIR Slot]].
- **The "one EQ per chord" resonance carve (pre-Soothe)** — to notch a left-panned string part's resonances across a 4-chord section, he ran **four EQs, each notching one chord's resonances, on the left channel, automated in turn**. It's insane and obsolete — "this is *why* Pro-Q 3 and Soothe were a revolution" — but the lesson is that **note-/chord-following dynamic resonance control now does this in one move**. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[Soothe2]].
- **Sample-vocal chain:** soft-tube saturation (sparkle) → de-ess → **[[Gem Dopamine]]** (highs — *keep* the hiss/crackle, it's the vibe) → heavy compression (**reduce the dynamic range so the music between vocal phrases sits at the vocal's level**) → [[Oxford Inflator]] (boost the sides) → EQ (lift high-mids, notch resonances, cut low-mids) → [[Soothe2]] → **tremolo** → H-delay + plate → sidechain → chorus → multiband (control the music's lows vs the voices' highs independently). → [[2 Main Vocal Group|Main Vocal Group]].
- **Logic tremolo at 180° phase = an auto-panner** (like SoundToys PanMan); at non-180° it's a volume tremolo. Automate it for movement, then bus it through delay + plate. → [[Modulation]].
- **Name a sample flip differently from the original** — "Fools Rush In" is a jazz standard with countless covers, so they renamed it to avoid getting lost among them (and out of respect). → workflow.

### Drums
- **Velocity → sample *pitch* (not volume) to humanize repeated one-shots** — vary the pitch of a clap/hit per hit (+ off-grid placement) so a repeated sample reads like a real hand rather than the same sample every time. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[2 Drums Group|Drums]].
- **Nile Rodgers's clap secret = "big hands"** — asked how Chic/Sister Sledge got the dopest claps, Nile just clapped *harder/bigger*; performance beats processing. Track your own claps (Laurent-tan hands) → overdrive → EQ → Transient Master to cut the in-betweens. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].
- **Groove built on the TR-8** (Roland's modern 808/909) hands-on for the initial feel, then into Battery; the 808's programmed rhythm happened to match Howard's chord rhythm — chase those serendipitous locks. → [[Roland TR-808]], [[2 Drums Group|Drums]].

### Songwriting (the Howard Q&A)
- **Chords first → loop 8 bars → hum gibberish until a melody sticks.** Also, playing chords on piano naturally puts a **top-note melody** in your hand — use it. **Whistle constantly and record ideas on your phone**; mine them for a session when a chord loop needs a top-line. → [[Song Practice]].
- **Don't mute the chords while writing the bassline/drums** — a bassline written under muted chords can imply a *different key* (the "Jaded" story: bass written as if minor, then major chords returned → "wrong" notes baked in). Keep the harmony audible while writing parts. → songwriting.
- **Shift the whole key ~3 semitones (varispeed) to hear wrong notes with fresh ears** — after a chord loop has been going for ages you stop hearing an out-of-key note; transposing the whole thing re-exposes it. → songwriting.
- **A catchy pop melody re-harmonized under "crazy" chords will fight it** — the technically-correct fix (shifting the melody to fit) "satisfies in a math way, not a human way" and can ruin the hook; sometimes you ditch the clever chords instead. Melodies over *simple* chords are catchier because the ear predicts them (thirds/fifths/sevenths, pentatonic-friendly). → songwriting.
- **Jimmy Napes moves:** **move the bass up a semitone** for tension (Motown/jazz); and **title-first writing** — start from the song title (bonus for double meanings), build outward. → songwriting.
- **If it comes together quickly, keep it; if not, move on** — for this kind of music the best ones fall out fast; simple meaning, beautifully put, beats over-grafting. → workflow.

# You Won't See Me — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** two-bridge model, one verse between the bridges, no solo. Verse is an unusual **18 bars** — four 4-bar phrases in AABA′ plus a 2-bar *petit reprise* — and **every phrase is harmonically closed**, starting and ending on I. The bridge is a true middle eight, **open at both ends**, which is the relief after four claustrophobically closed verse phrases. The outro plays the verse without the lead vocal and fades fast.

The unifying device is invisible: **four-note descending chromatic fragments tucked into an inner voice** in every section — piano at first, then the backing vocals. Chromatically altered chords, above all the secondary dominant, are what let a chromatic line be knitted into an otherwise diatonic fabric ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]), and one phrase borrows **iv from the parallel minor** to supply its own ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The bridge carries a six-note descent that lands its resolution on the next verse's first downbeat, and its chord changes fall in bars 1, 2 and 4 rather than 1, 3 and 4 — an ultra-slow syncopation. The proof it is all deliberate: at the end of the verse the backing voices invert the fragment and sing it **upward**.

Paul's double-tracked lead is almost entirely anticipatory syncopation ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]); the single place he sings on the downbeat is the one place the backing track itself syncopates. And **the instrumental texture never changes at all** — the entire energy curve is built from vocal layers alone ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]).

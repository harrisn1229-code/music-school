# loner - making a billboard number 1 pop beat for post malone's next album from scratch!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WX_IB6K-xIo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WX_IB6K-xIo)

## Synopsis

A speculative from-scratch build imagining Post Malone's next album, reverse-engineered from a few unreleased previews loner describes as "very ambient," synth-heavy, and built on "very emotional yet melodic chords." The whole beat is built in the order a mix engineer would think about it — synth pad first, then vocals, then drums, then melody, then bass, then a piano breakdown — with almost every stage getting the same two-step treatment: pick a preset, then immediately revoice/reprocess it into something more personal (change a chord voicing after the fact, swap a preset mid-build, pitch a doubling vocal layer instead of recording a real double). The synth pad opens on Arturia Analog Lab's "Layers Volume 2" bank (an unreleased preset pack), an airy "Burning Desire" preset in B major with hand-revoiced chord tops, swapped mid-build to "Night Sky" for a more Post Malone-ish character, then EQ'd (low cut), heavily reverbed, and run through a cassette-tape plugin for vintage warmth. Two vocal lines get near-identical AlterBoy-based chains with one deliberate difference each — a pitch-up/format-down/50%-mix "ghost double" on the main vocal, and a formant-down/full-wet chorus-preset treatment plus a dark-room Vintage Verb on the harmony — a clean real-world example of "same base chain, one distinguishing move per layer," which this channel repeats constantly across its vocal-stack videos. Drums come from a disco-poppy Splice kit (Oliver Power Tools Vol. 3): hats programmed in layers, a stepped kick fill, and manual swing for a natural feel. The melody uses Kill September's one-shot kit with a stretch-mode tip credited to another creator ("Sikki"), then heavy reverb, an octave-down pitch move, and a tight-but-heavy delay. Bass is a two-part system — a Layers Volume 2 plucked bass filling the gaps between chords, and a MODO-Bass-style "Moto Bass" (misheard/auto-captioned) finger-bass carrying the main line, with a six-semitone pitched-down run at the end and Decapitator drive to make it stand out. The back half of the video builds a piano breakdown/bridge (Arturia Piano V2, chords and vocals copied across, manually "strummed" for a human feel, run through a J37 tape emulation + an Abbey Road Chambers reverb) contrasted against a dark, deep sidechained synth bass ("Pray for Love," Layers Volume 1, ducked with a ShaperBox one-bar preset), then closes by rebuilding the drum kit's snare layer (a lighter snare hitting every few bars, plus an open hat) and creating a stripped-down "verse" variant by muting the high pluck and vocals. Short, fast-paced, and light on exact numbers (auto-captioned spoken narration), but dense with real production sequencing and several "layer two versions of a sound, keep the different one" moves worth generalizing beyond this specific beat.

## Techniques

**Synth pad (opening layer)**
- **Pick a preset for genre-matching "feel" first, then hand-revoice the chord tops after the fact rather than programming a new voicing from scratch.** Loaded Analog Lab's unreleased "Layers Volume 2" bank, chose the "Burning Desire" preset specifically for a "super melodic" airy character, wrote a B major chord progression, then went back and nudged individual chord-top notes up/around so the tops sit in a similar register across the progression — described as "changing the voicings of your chords after you've played them" for a different feeling without touching the underlying harmony. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Swap the preset mid-build once the arrangement reveals the pad isn't quite matching the target artist, without changing the MIDI.** After the first pass, switched the same "Layers Volume 2" instrument from "Burning Desire" to "Night Sky," judged "a bit more Post Malone-ish" — same chords, same voicing work, different underlying patch. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **EQ low cut → heavy reverb → tape/cassette vintage stage, in that order, for an airy ambient pad.** Cut the low end with EQ first (keeps the pad out of the bass/kick's lane before anything else touches it), then a deliberately heavy reverb for the "super ambient" target sound, then a cassette-tape plugin (clean-tape preset) purely for vintage color. → [[EQ]], [[Reverb]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Cassette]], [[BED Slot]]

**Vocals (two-line stack: main + harmony)**
- **Same base move (pitch-shift plugin + EQ), one parameter flipped per layer, to build a "ghost double" instead of recording a real second take.** Main vocal: boost the high end with EQ for presence, then Little AlterBoy (auto-captioned "ultraboy" — the same recurring homophone this channel's other videos get right) pitched all the way up, Formant brought down, Mix pulled back to ~50% — the pitched-up layer sits *behind* the untouched lead rather than replacing it, reading as two vocals from one performance. → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]]
- **Harmony vocal: AlterBoy's built-in "vocal chorus" preset, then EQ, then AlterBoy again with Formant shifted down (same direction as the main vocal, but no pitch shift this time) at full 100% mix and boosted Drive, finished with Valhalla Vintage Verb's dark vocal room preset (size pulled down, mix ~90).** The distinguishing moves against the main vocal aren't the formant direction (both go down) — it's the absence of a pitch shift, the full 100% mix instead of ~50%, the extra Drive, and the added reverb tail, so the two layers still read as distinct despite sharing a formant direction. → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]], [[Reverb]], [[Pitch and Formant]], [[1 Harmony Vocal Track]]

**Drums (foundation pass)**
- **Splice "Oliver Power Tools Vol. 3" kit for a disco-poppy drum character** — named explicitly as the reason for choosing this kit over a trap/hip-hop bank; genre-appropriate kit selection is treated as most of the work before any processing. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Layer multiple hi-hat patterns rather than programming one hat part** — a simple bouncing hat pattern first, then a second layered hi-hat pattern copy-pasted across the arrangement for density, plus a third 4-step hi-hat shifted slightly back in time from the kick's own 4-step fill (so the two interlock instead of doubling exactly). → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Kick programmed as a 4-step fill rather than a simple downbeat pattern**, paired with the offset 4-step hi-hat above for a moving, non-static groove foundation. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Manually raise the global swing amount as the very last step of the drum pass, specifically for "a very natural feel."** Applied after all the hat/kick layers were placed — swing is treated as a finishing humanization move, not a starting parameter. *(no exact swing percentage given — general starting point: try 8–16% and listen against the groove)* → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[1 Drums Hat Track|2 Drums Hat Track]]

**Melody (one-shot layer)**
- **Set one-shots to Stretch mode so a sample plays back at a FIXED length regardless of the pitch it's triggered at — a technique explicitly credited to another creator's video ("Sikki"), not loner's own discovery.** Without Stretch, a one-shot triggered at a higher pitch naturally plays shorter (faster pitch = faster playback in a non-time-stretched sampler); Stretch mode decouples the two so every note, high or low, sustains for the same length. Same lesson already documented on this channel from a different video, mapped to Ableton's Simpler Complex/Complex Pro Warp mode. → [[Simpler]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Sound source: Kill September's one-shot kit** (arp runs, chopped chords, textures — already a recurring go-to source on this channel for melody layers). → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Heavy reverb applied BEFORE the melody is even written**, then a simple melodic idea pitched down an octave and nudged slightly in time, finished with a tight-timing but heavily-wet delay. Reverb-before-writing is a deliberate order: the wash is baked into how the melody is heard and shaped while composing, not bolted on afterward. → [[Reverb]], [[Delay]]

**Bass (two-part system)**
- **A plucked synth bass (Layers Volume 2) used ONLY in the gaps between chords, not as the main bassline — a distinct, secondary bass layer rather than a doubled part.** Explicitly placed to fill the space between chord hits, leaving the real bass line to a separate instrument entirely. → [[Bass Design]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A finger-picked "Moto Bass" (near-certain auto-caption garble of IK Multimedia MODO Bass, already tracked on this channel from a different video's Höfner recreation) carries the main bass line**, preset switched from an initial pick to "New Bass" mid-build. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A pitched-down bass run (six semitones) as a dedicated ending flourish, distinct from the main bass pattern**, followed by Decapitator (light drive) + EQ specifically to make that run "stand out a little bit more" from the rest of the bassline. → [[Decapitator]], [[EQ]], [[Bass Design]], [[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]]

**Piano breakdown / bridge section**
- **Build a bridge by literally copying the existing chord MIDI and vocal audio into a new section, then reworking the arrangement around them, instead of writing a new bridge from scratch.** Piano V2 (Arturia) loaded with a preset, the same chord progression copied in from the pad, the vocals copied in too — establishing the bridge is "the same song, thinner," not new material. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Manually "strum" a copied block chord part on piano — select the whole region and offset/roll the notes by hand** for a played, human-strum feel instead of the pad's stacked-hit MIDI. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **J37 tape emulation → Abbey Road Chambers reverb, specifically "to make it sound like it's in a room."** Owned equivalents: [[Kramer Tape]] for the J37 tape stage (already the tape-sweetening stage on the piano chain), [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] (1970s Color, chamber-family mode) for the room-chamber reverb character — this exact Abbey Road Chambers substitution is already tracked from a separate Beatles-recreation video, now confirmed reused on an unrelated pop breakdown. → [[Kramer Tape]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]], [[Reverb]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[1 Acoustic Piano Track]]
- **A dark, deep synth bass ("Pray for Love" preset, Layers Volume 1) as textural contrast under the piano, sidechained with a ShaperBox one-bar preset** rather than a manually-drawn duck — the stock one-bar preset was judged "good enough" without custom curve editing. → [[Sidechaining]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]]

**Arrangement — building a distinct verse section from the bridge's elements**
- **Rework the drum kit's snare layer for the transition back into the hook**: a lighter snare added underneath the existing snare pattern, deliberately only hitting every few snares (not doubling every hit) for extra texture without changing the pattern's density; plus an added open hat. → [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Build a "unique" stripped-down verse section by SUBTRACTING elements from the fullest section rather than writing new parts** — muted the high-end pluck/melody layer and the vocals, leaving pad + drums + bass, explicitly framed as a verse being "a bit more stripped down." A concrete example of arranging by subtraction instead of addition. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

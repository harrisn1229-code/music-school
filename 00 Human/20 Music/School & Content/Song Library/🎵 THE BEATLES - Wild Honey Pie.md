# Wild Honey Pie — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Break (**7 bars**) + Refrain (4 bars), three times through, then an outro with a complete ending. A miniature rather than a fragment — it has a complete form on a minimal definition, plus a real written ending. The **seven-bar break** is an odd length nobody had to bother with on a throwaway, and it is the reason the section registers at all. It also works as an entr'acte between the two songs around it, which is a use of the album as a medium rather than filler; the first of Paul's one-man-band tracks.

The vocabulary is essentially a **semi-chromatic stream of dominant sevenths** with just enough root motion to establish G as home ([[BLU-C02 — Why every chord is a dominant 7th]]). The mechanism is audible in the harpsichord's right hand, which walks a **descending parallel set of tritones**. Bluesiness comes from the tonic carrying its own flat seventh and from an implied **major-against-minor clash on the tonic**, sharpest right at the close ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]).

The final cadence uses an altered V that resists naming: the bass moves G–F–G, which tempts a ♭VII reading, but D persists as a melodic pedal, so the chord is better spelled from D with both third and fifth flattened, in first inversion.

There is also a cross-track effect worth hearing: B natural sits on top of the opening chord, and B was the tonic of the song you just heard — same pitch, new function.

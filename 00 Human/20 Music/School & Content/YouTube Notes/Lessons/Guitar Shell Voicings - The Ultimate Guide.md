## What's taught

**The core claim:** any of 60+ jazz chord-quality symbols reduces to one of **8 shapes** (9 counting the reused minor-triad case), played as **16 total shapes** (each doubled: one rooted off the 6th string, one off the 5th string). Memorize the 6th-string set first, then the 5th-string set.

- **What a shell voicing is:** root + 3rd + 7th only (sometimes a 6th in place of the 7th) — the "guide tones" (3rd = major/minor, 7th = dominant/major-7) are what actually define a chord's quality; the 5th is always dropped ("it's implied, we're just not playing it"), matching [[Chord Voicings]]'s own "the 5th is the first thing to go" rule almost word for word — third independent source confirming that principle after Jared Borkowski/[[Rootless Voicings - WOW!]] and Daniel Seriff.
- **The 8 shapes (spelling given root-relative; shape number is Jared's own numbering):**
  1. **Major 7 (maj7 / M7 / △7)** — spelled 1-3-5-7, shell = **root-7-3**. Two root-string positions (6th-string and 5th-string), no 5th played.
  2. **Major 6 (6)** — same family as maj7 but swap the 7th for the plain 6th: **root-3-6**. New chord type vs. Seriff's video (Seriff's series doesn't cover 6 chords at all).
  3. **Dominant 7** — **root-♭7-3** (6th-string) / **root-3-♭7** (5th-string). Lower the maj7 shape's 7th a half step, same move Seriff's video teaches.
  4. **Minor 7** — **root-♭7-♭3** (6th-string) / **root-♭3-♭7** (5th-string) — flagged explicitly that the top two notes *flip order* when you cross from the 6th-string shape to the 5th-string shape (6th-string: root then ♭7 then ♭3, top to bottom reversed on the 5th-string shape: root then ♭3 then ♭7).
  5. **Minor 7♭5 (half-diminished, ø7)** — **the same shape as minor 7** — a "freebie": since the shell never voices a 5th, there's nothing to flat between m7 and m7♭5, so one shape covers both. Same identity Seriff's video demonstrates with exact fret numbers on [[Chord Voicings]] and [[Seventh Chords]].
  6. **Dominant 7sus4** — **root-♭7-4** (the 4 replaces the 3). New chord type vs. Seriff's video. Often resolves back to the plain dominant 7 shape, but stands on its own too.
  7. **Minor 6** — **root-♭3-6** ("the mysterious spy soundtrack chord"). New chord type vs. Seriff's video.
  8. **Diminished 7** — **the same shape as minor 6**, another freebie: dim7 (1-♭3-♭5-𝄫7) and m6 (1-♭3-6-nothing-flat) differ only in the 5th, and the shell doesn't voice a 5th, so the 𝄫7 (enharmonically the same pitch as a 6) and the plain 6 land on the identical fret — Jared explicitly names this "harmonic equivalent" reasoning. **This is a different mechanism than Seriff's dim7 shape** (Seriff's series derives dim7 by flatting minor 7's 7th a second half-step, giving a shape that is NOT the m6 shape) — worth flagging as two different, non-interchangeable systems that both arrive at a valid dim7 voicing; don't mix the two mid-progression.
  9. **Minor(major7)** — 1-♭3-5-7 (minor triad + major 7th), shell = **root-7-♭3** (or **root-♭3-7** on the 5th string). Described as "very crunchy," rarely stands alone, most commonly heard in the classic **chromatic descending inner-voice line**: I → I(minor/major7) → i7 → i6 (the "Stairway to Heaven" line). New chord type vs. Seriff's video.
  10. **Major triad** — **root-5-3** (6th-string, skips the 4th string) / **5-3 with the 5th on the bottom** (5th-string voicing) — deliberately voiced with the 5th under the 3rd rather than root-under-everything because a plain root-position pop triad "just doesn't fit" next to a run of shell voicings; this spaced-apart triad blends better. Flagged as rare in traditional jazz standards/the Real Book, more common in modern jazz compositions (Pat Metheny cited as an example of a composer who leans on triads).
  11. **Minor triad** — not a new shape: **reuse the minor 7 shape** (shape 4) for any bare minor triad you see on a chart — "pretty safe" in virtually all cases, since a minor triad almost always implies the minor-7 sound of the underlying scale.
- **The symbol-reduction rulebook (the sight-reading skill, not just the shapes):**
  - **Always ignore the 5** — it's baked into every shell voicing already.
  - **Always ignore slash-chord bass notes** (e.g. `G7/B`) — read only the chord above the slash; the bass player (or your own bass note if solo) covers the slash note. You're not playing anything "wrong" by skipping it, you're just not adding the extra melodic bass motion the slash implies.
  - **Ignore any extension number when it follows a 7** — `G7♭13`, `Cmaj7♯11`, `Dm7♭9` → play the plain shape before the extension (G7, Cmaj7, Dm7) and drop everything after it.
  - **When an extension number appears ALONE with no 7 written, mentally insert a 7 and treat it as that 7-chord** — `G9` → read as G7; `G13` → G7; `Dm11` → Dm7; `Dmaj13` → Dmaj7. This is the one rule that trips people up (an extension number alone does NOT mean "ignore," it means "this is secretly a 7-chord, use the matching shell shape").
- **Practice workflow demonstrated live:** pull a real lead sheet (Google image search or the **iReal Pro** app's "Jazz 1350 Standards" playlist, ~1300+ tunes) and read straight through applying the reduction rules in real time. Demoed cold on three unfamiliar/semi-familiar tunes (including "Dancing on the Ceiling" and an unnamed ballad) scanning specifically for chords likely to "throw you off" (slash chords, bare extension numbers, sus-with-alteration like `C7sus♭9`) to stress-test the system — every chord resolved cleanly to one of the 8 shapes.
- **Full musical demo:** plays the complete chord changes to **"Manhã de Carnaval" (Black Orpheus)** using only these shell shapes, with the melody layered on top, to show the system in actual musical context rather than isolated shapes.
- **Recommended tunes to drill the system on** (all findable in iReal Pro): **Fly Me to the Moon, The Girl from Ipanema, Autumn Leaves, Dream a Little Dream of Me, My Funny Valentine.**
- **Series context:** episode 15 of a "How to Learn Guitar Chords" series; companion free PDF booklet "Any Jazz Chord" (soundguitarlessons.com) contains the same diagrams and a fretboard note-name reference chart for the 6th/5th strings.

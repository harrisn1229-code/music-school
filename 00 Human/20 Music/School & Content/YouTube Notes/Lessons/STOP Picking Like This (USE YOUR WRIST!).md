# STOP Picking Like This (USE YOUR WRIST!)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6DmcHPxKPjw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6DmcHPxKPjw)

## Synopsis

Names and debunks a specific misconception — that one fixed picking motion should work at every tempo — then gives a three-tempo-zone framework and four progressively harder string-jumping/wrist exercises built around it.

## Techniques

- **The misconception, stated directly:** players expect a single perfect picking motion (grip, hand shape) to carry them from slow to fast seamlessly. Grip and hand-closure genuinely do stay constant — but the underlying wrist *motion* has to change subtly as tempo rises, and not knowing this reads as "I suddenly got worse" when really the player has crossed into a new tempo zone that needs a slightly different motion.
- **Three tempo zones, named thresholds:** slow (below ~90 BPM), mid (roughly 90–160 BPM), fast (above ~160 BPM). The exact numbers are personal/approximate, not a hard rule — the point is that a technique that feels perfect and controlled at 90 BPM will often suddenly feel "wrong" again around the 100 BPM mark, and again near 160 BPM, purely from crossing a zone boundary, not from doing anything actually incorrect.
- **Exercise 1 — classical-style string-jumping exercise around a repeating "dominant string" theme.** Forces constant jumps between non-adjacent strings while always returning to one recurring note/phrase — targets wrist unlocking and jump agility specifically, distinct from single-string tremolo speed.
- **Exercise 2 — alternate one measure of single-string tremolo picking with one measure of a harder 2-string phrase**, deliberately aiming to make both measures *feel* identical in picking-hand motion — the point is building the harder skill directly on top of an already-mastered foundation (single-string speed) rather than practicing it in isolation.
- **Exercise 3 ("Königsklasse" — "highest class," direct callout as the hardest drill here) — switch strings after nearly every single stroke.** Brute force fails here; unnecessary or clunky motion produces noise and forearm burn. Framed as the wrist-independence ceiling exercise of the set.
- **Exercise 4 — combine a fast-tempo burst and a mid-tempo phrase in one continuous line with no rest between the two.** Requires the switch between motions to be instant/automatic (muscle memory), since there's no pause to consciously reset between the two speeds.
- **Closing concept — build custom mixed-note-grouping workouts.** Pick a scale (worked example: A minor, high E string to low E string) and assign an irregular, non-repeating note count per string (worked example: 3-2-1-5-2-3) rather than a uniform grouping — deliberately breaks predictable, memorized picking patterns and is framed as a combined technique **and** creative-composition exercise, not just a physical drill.

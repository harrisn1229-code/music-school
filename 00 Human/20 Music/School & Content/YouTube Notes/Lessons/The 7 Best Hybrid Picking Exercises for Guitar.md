# The 7 Best Hybrid Picking Exercises for Guitar

## Synopsis

A long, exercise-dense lesson from Jared (Sound Guitar Lessons) on **hybrid picking** — holding the pick while the fingers (middle/ring/pinky) pluck alongside it. He frames three separate payoffs up front: seamlessly switching between pick-style and finger-style playing, playing chords without always strumming (useful for a pick player who wants a fingerstyle-adjacent comping option), and speeding up single-note lead/solo lines at string crossings that pure alternate picking handles less cleanly. Before the seven numbered exercises, he spends the first several minutes on a single foundational idea borrowed from classical guitar pedagogy — **planting** (a deliberate moment of preparing/settling a finger on the string before plucking it, for consistent tone/attack) — arguing it's the difference between a sound that's controlled every time versus one that's swooped-at-and-hoped-for. The seven exercises then split into two groups: **five chordal/right-hand-coordination exercises** (ascending finger order, descending finger order, a combined ascending/descending pattern with a "full plant" twist, alternating pick and full block chord, and a simultaneous full-block pluck focused on note-to-note evenness), each demoed on open strings first, then a scale, then real chords (C, G, Am, and later Dm7/Ddim/Cmaj7) — and **two lead-guitar exercises** (a melodic-thirds/pentatonic string-crossing pattern where the crossing note gets hybrid-picked instead of pick-alternated, and a harmonic/double-stop technique covering both interval-pair picking through a pentatonic scale and a Stevie Ray Vaughan-style drone-note-plus-moving-line device). This is a longer, more thorough companion to Jared's other picking-system video, [[Directional Alternate Picking System|Alternate Picking Guitar Exercises (that will fix your picking technique forever!)]] — that one drills the default picking-direction rule for pure pick playing; this one is the technique you reach for when pure alternate picking hits a wall (chords, wide string crossings, double-stops).

## What's taught

The full extraction — foundation plus all seven exercises with concrete finger orders, chord examples, and the two lead-technique variants — lives on the new technique note **[[Hybrid Picking Exercises]]** (created from this video); summary:

- **Foundation — planting.** A deliberate prep-moment before each pluck, borrowed from classical guitar right-hand pedagogy: the finger settles on the string ("am I where I need to be") before plucking, rather than swooping in blind. Named as the single biggest lever for consistent tone/volume/attack. Foundation drill: pick one string, then plant-and-pluck one finger (start with middle) moving string to string on open strings, listening for identical tone every time.
- **Exercise 1 — ascending: pick → middle → ring → pinky**, on open strings, then an E natural minor scale on one string, then chords (C, first-inversion G, Am).
- **Exercise 2 — descending: pick → pinky → ring → middle**, same progression (open strings → scale → chords).
- **Exercise 3 — combined ascending/descending with a "full plant."** Plant middle+ring+pinky simultaneously right after the pick stroke, release them individually going up; revert to sequential (one-at-a-time) planting coming back down. Classical-guitar-derived prep for fast arpeggiation.
- **Exercise 4 — alternate pick and full block chord.** Pick, then all three fingers plucked together as a chord, alternating pick–chord–pick–chord (e.g. over Dm7 → Ddim → Cmaj7).
- **Exercise 5 — simultaneous full block pluck (pick + 3 fingers at once).** Focus is evenness — no string popping out louder than the others — flagged as harder than it looks despite being conceptually the simplest.
- **Exercise 6 — melodic-thirds pattern for lead lines**, using a C major/A natural minor scale (5th position): pick notes that stay on the same string, hybrid-pick (finger, usually middle) the note that crosses to a new string. Same crossing-note logic applied to a pentatonic scale with a plain skip-one-note-up pattern (not true melodic thirds, since pentatonic structure doesn't support it).
- **Exercise 7 — harmonic/double-stop hybrid picking**, pentatonic scale: (a) pick+finger together on two notes moving through the scale, alternating a 4th and a 3rd interval as you move (Stevie Ray Vaughan-style); (b) a drone note held (pinky, or any finger) while picking a moving line against it — demoed with an open-string drone (D, E) under a blues-scale lick, the exact device used in this video's own intro.

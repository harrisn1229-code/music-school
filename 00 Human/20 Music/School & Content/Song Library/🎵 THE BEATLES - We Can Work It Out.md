# We Can Work It Out — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Verse · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Outro with a complete ending — the double-bridge model with **no intro at all** and a full ending rather than a fade, both unusual for it. The verse is **16 bars phrased 6 + 6 + 4** rather than four fours, which is why it scans like free verse over a steady 4/4. The bridge is 24 bars: a 12-bar unit of three 4-bar phrases, stated twice.

Nothing chromatic, no remote modulation — all the innovation is in phrasing and dissonance handling. The home key is established by **♭VII instead of V** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]); the actual dominant does not turn up until the very last bar of the verse, and when it does it is not resolved — the verse spills into the bridge by [[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]] to vi. That left-hanging dominant is exactly why the song needs its little outro to tie itself off.

The melody is appoggiatura-driven throughout ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]), and the showcase instance holds a ninth unresolved across the bar line, by which time its own resolution note has become a dissonance against the new chord. The idea is carried into the backing too: the harmonium in the bridge holds a B natural through one chord change where it belongs and **on through the next, where it does not**.

The bridge's third phrase shifts to a slow-triplet oom-pah-pah feel — three beats per bar where there were four, at the same tempo — so the returning verse **sounds** faster without being faster ([[RHY-C04 — Polyrhythm and hemiola]]).

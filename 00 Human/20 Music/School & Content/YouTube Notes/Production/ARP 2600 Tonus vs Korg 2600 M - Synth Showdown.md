# ARP 2600 Tonus vs Korg 2600 M: Synth Showdown

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mSdNJv_cm3s)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mSdNJv_cm3s)

## Synopsis

A deep, ear-focused A/B comparison between Anthony's own vintage ARP 2600 Tonus (used on well-known film/record cues, including the Thriller bass sound and Jerry Goldsmith's Logan's Run score) and a Korg 2600M reissue, waveform by waveform and module by module. Genuinely detailed critical-listening content on how two nominally-identical circuit designs actually differ, plus a clear verdict on the reissue's merits.

## Techniques

- **Waveform character differences**: raw sawtooth reads fuller/lower-content on the Korg (less inherent high-end sizzle — compensated by driving the preamp for saturation to bring back "crispiness"); sine wave is purer/more accurate on the Korg vs. a more character-distorted vintage sine; triangle wave is brighter/more harmonically buzzy on the vintage unit vs. a purer, lower-harmonic Korg triangle — a real programming consideration, since it means filtering more aggressively on the vintage unit to compensate; pulse wave gets subtly "skinnier" on the Korg at the 10% pulse-width extreme.
- **Filter sweep character**: the vintage unit's cutoff sweep is smoother/more linear low-to-high; the Korg's sweep concentrates more action near the top of its range, requiring a faster manual sweep to feel linear. Vintage unit has more resonance headroom in the high range.
- **Preamp-as-compensation move**: patching the filter output through the internal preamp and driving it (even lightly — roughly 100x gain rather than 1000x) restores high-end "crispiness"/edge the cleaner Korg lacks natively straight out of the box.
- **Envelope generator character is the single biggest functional difference between the two**: on the VCA's LINEAR input, the Korg produces very short, clean, percussive decay times, while the vintage unit reads scratchier/anomalous and "squishes" rather than cutting cleanly. On the EXPONENTIAL input, the Korg gives a crisp, near-click transient vs. a "pebbly" vintage attack — the Korg is the clear pick when a clean, percussive synth-drum-style attack is needed.
- **AR envelope generator**: the Korg has a quicker attack but a much longer maximum release time than the vintage unit, which has the reverse balance (slower attack, quicker release) — a genuinely different creative range rather than one simply being better.
- **New capabilities on the Korg reissue with no vintage-2600 equivalent**: a built-in reverb (smooth, long decay, praised as a standout feature); true 2-voice/unison capability via a second Keyboard Control Voltage output on its 3620 keyboard (play one note = unison, two notes = an extra independent voice); a ring modulator that functions cleanly as a second VCA when DC-coupled (nulls out completely, no leakage in audio mode).
- **Preamp character**: more linear/controlled on the Korg (good for adding subtle overdrive without losing control), though it can still be pushed into the same kind of "out of control" territory as the vintage unit's famously less-controlled preamp at extreme drive.
- **Everything else is functionally identical**: sample & hold generator, voltage processors, white noise generator.
- **Verdict**: the Korg reissue can replicate nearly everything the vintage Tonus does, with minor nuance adjustments, while adding real capabilities (reverb, cleaner/faster envelopes, two-voice capability, more controlled preamp) the original never had — judged a genuinely worthwhile "second/third 2600" rather than a mere approximation, especially at its price point.
- **Philosophy note**: the ARP 2600's direct, visible signal-flow architecture is framed as the best way to LEARN synthesis — the ability to dial in a sound close to what's imagined without lengthy trial-and-error, then fine-tune nuances by ear.

# Prophet 5 Under the Hood w/ Rob Rosen-Clip

## Synopsis

A synth tech's internal tour of a Rev 1 Sequential Prophet-5 (serial #14, signed by Dave Smith) — genuine engineering history rather than a playing/programming tutorial, but the most technically detailed source in the vault on why the Prophet-5 mattered: the first microcontroller-based synth, replacing the CS-80's room of discrete analog logic with a single digital brain, plus the specific chip architecture and a Rev 1/2-vs-Rev 3 sound distinction with named examples. Closes with a genuinely useful production-philosophy point about analog imperfection as character, echoing content already captured from the same interview's longer cut ([[Rob Rosen Reveals His Ultimate Synth Collection]]).

## Techniques

- **First microcontroller-based synth**: Dave Smith repurposed a Z80 chip (same class used in home computers of the era) to scan the keyboard/panel, assign voices, and generate control voltages — replacing what the CS-80 did with a literal room of discrete analog logic cards.
- **Self-calibration, a genuine first**: hold two specific keys simultaneously and the Prophet-5 auto-tunes all its oscillators. Pre-microcontroller synths (including the CS-80) required a tech to manually calibrate drift by hand.
- **Tradeoff of going digital-brain this early**: no velocity, no aftertouch — keys are strictly on/off.
- **SSM (Rev 1/2) chip specifics**: SSM2050 envelope generators (5 per section — filter and amp), SSM2030 oscillators, SSM2040 filter (described as "very revered... very hot resonance"), SSM2020-based VCAs.
- **SSM vs. Curtis (Rev 3) is a "different, not better" split** — most of the canonical "classic Prophet-5" studio records used Rev 3/Curtis; Yellow Magic Orchestra is a notable Rev 1/2 (SSM) exception. Oberheim's OB-X used Curtis envelopes; later Oberheims used Curtis across envelopes/filters/oscillators too.
- **A hidden Prophet-10 expansion path** existed in early units (nylon standoffs + a second ribbon connector on the analog board) meant to double voice count, discontinued almost immediately because the doubled circuitry overheated and baked the lower board.
- **Keyboard scanning via a diode matrix**: keys are wired in a matrix (roughly 8 keys per bus line) and scanned rather than individually wired, which is how a 61-key keyboard avoids needing 61+ discrete switch connections; a side effect is per-note voice-assignment logic that also applies the self-tuning offset for whichever voice a given key gets routed to.
- **The production-philosophy closer**: modern digital calibration tunes every voice/note-range to be perfectly identical; real analog never does, and that per-voice variance ("might be just slightly darker on one voice in the bottom than on the top") is framed as the character, not a flaw — same point, same interview, as the entry already captured on [[Voice Architecture]].

# Finally Master Sweep Picking: With THIS Easy Trick!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cplwCusY3lE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cplwCusY3lE)

## Synopsis

A single-trick sweep-picking video: solves the specific problem of reversing direction mid-arpeggio (finishing an ascending sweep with an upstroke, then needing a downstroke to descend) by anchoring the picking hand at the top note and adding a small wrist-rotation motion to escape the "dead zone" between strings, rather than the usual relaxed hand-follows-the-strings sweeping motion.

## What's taught

- **The problem, isolated:** normal sweep picking uses a fully relaxed hand with no anchor point, changing picking angle continuously as it follows the strings — this works fine for one-directional sweeps but breaks down exactly at a direction change, where the upstroke that ended the ascent leaves the pick trapped between two strings with no clean path to the downstroke needed for the descent.
- **The fix: anchor + wrist rotation.** At the top note of the arpeggio, anchor the picking hand (stop following the strings) and add a small upward wrist motion that lifts the pick clear of the dead zone before beginning the descending sweep. Everywhere else in the arpeggio, the normal relaxed hand-follows-the-strings motion continues unchanged — this is a change at one specific point, not a wholesale technique overhaul.
- **The motion shrinks (but never disappears) as tempo increases** — visually subtle at speed, but still felt/needed by the player even when barely visible on camera.
- **Practical result claimed:** eliminated dead notes and double-picked-same-string errors specifically at direction changes, including under a fast double-bass-drum backing track, after being a long-standing personal weak point.
- A short dedicated exercise isolating just this direction-change moment is recommended as the starting drill, before applying it inside full arpeggio runs.

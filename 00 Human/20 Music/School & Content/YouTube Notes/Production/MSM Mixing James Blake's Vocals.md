# MSM Mixing James Blake's Vocals

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QJYsvr1Cuk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7QJYsvr1Cuk)

## Synopsis

A short Mix with the Masters screen-share breakdown (engineer unnamed in the excerpt itself) of a vocal session built around **two co-lead vocalists trading lines** — James Blake plus a rapped feature credited only as "Slowthai" in the transcript. Upload date (Dec 2021) and the two-vocalist arrangement line up with slowthai's *TYRON* era (James Blake features on that album), though the source clip never names the song, so treat that pairing as a likely-but-unconfirmed identification rather than fact. The engineer opens on a mix-philosophy point worth as much as any setting: James's vocal was **delivered wet — already printed through the artist's own reverb** — and rather than reverse-engineer and replicate that space, he leaves it completely alone; the reasoning is as much political as sonic ("My vocals feel different" is a note nobody wants from the artist) and he genuinely likes the printed sound anyway. From there it's a fairly dense lead-vocal chain (de-ess → character/drive stage → basic parametric EQ → dynamic resonance suppression → a proximity-effect correction stage triggered via AudioSuite → parallel compression → a second de-ess) duplicated across a **lead vocal Group and a separate backing-vocal Group**, both feeding a combined vocal Group carrying the video's most interesting move: a **32-band dynamic-EQ "Trackspacer"-style processor, sidechained off Slowthai's vocal, set to Mid-only in M/S mode**, so the *center* of James's voice ducks out of the 1–6 kHz zone specifically at the moments the two vocalists overlap — clearing space for Slowthai without touching the printed reverb tails riding alongside on the same group. The chain closes with Clariphonic for a final top-end "shimmer" restoration pass. Everything is demonstrated by ear/screen (mutes, plugin GUIs glimpsed on-camera) rather than read off a settings sheet, so most values below are the handful the engineer states out loud plus qualitative description for the rest — still a genuinely useful ordering/routing recipe for a two-vocalist mix.

## Techniques

- **[0:00–0:35] Leave a delivered "wet" (pre-reverbed) vocal completely alone rather than reverse-engineering and replicating its space — a mix-philosophy call, not a technical one.** "Those are the wet files, so I got delivered... This is a reverb channel completely printed. I'm not going to change that stuff. Their reverb, they've chosen that, that's on purpose." The stated reasoning is twofold: replicating an artist's chosen reverb risks a mismatch the artist can feel but not articulate ("Something doesn't feel right... have you listened to the rough?"), and — separately — he genuinely liked the printed sound on first listen. Practical fallout for the rest of the chain: no reverb/space decisions get made on this vocal at all, and (later in the video) any dynamic processing applied to the *group* this vocal sits in has to explicitly exclude the printed reverb tails from being affected — see the Trackspacer bullet below. → [[Depth and Space]], [[Mix Handoff]]
- **[0:35–1:40] The lead-vocal processing order, stated explicitly as "started from here and built down, added this towards the end when I needed it": de-ess → character/Drive → basic EQ (HPF + one resonance pull) → Soothe → proximity-effect correction (AudioSuite) → parallel compression → de-ess again.** De-essing bookends the chain (once early to tame the raw take, once again at the very end after everything else has potentially reintroduced or exaggerated sibilance) rather than sitting once in the middle. "I love the FabFilter stuff" for the de-essing stage — read as [[Pro-DS]]. → [[Pro-DS]], [[De-essing]]
- **[0:50–1:00] A "Drive" stage described only as "a bit of character, distortion" — plugin identity unconfirmed (name spoken, not shown on camera in the transcript), positioned right after the first de-ess and before EQ.** No settings given beyond "a bit," and the exact product isn't nameable from audio alone (could be a channel-strip drive stage, not necessarily a standalone plugin called "Drive") — treat as a light saturation/character stage in this chain slot rather than a specific tool recommendation. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **[1:00–1:20] "Basic SSL EQ": a high-pass filtering the low end plus one narrow cut pulling out a single ugly resonance — described qualitatively, no exact frequencies given.** "Filtering in the low end... this is pulling out a resonance, there's something ugly there I don't like." An SSL-style channel EQ isn't owned — the equivalent move on the owned parametric is a standard subtractive pass: HPF somewhere in the 80–120 Hz range *(general starting point)* on the [[BASS Slot]]/low end, then a narrow-Q sweep-and-cut on whatever resonance turns up by ear, same method already documented on [[Pro-Q 4]]'s "hover-freeze resonance hunting" and narrow-notch bullets. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[BASS Slot]]
- **[1:20–1:30] Soothe run right after EQ specifically to catch a resonance the EQ notch didn't fully deal with — "softening the aggression."** Same dynamic-resonance-suppressor role already documented on [[Soothe2]] (harsh vocal resonance, 1–6 kHz focus); this instance sits mid-chain rather than as a final tame, consistent with the "chase resonances as they surface" placement logic. → [[Soothe2]], [[De-essing]]
- **[1:40–2:20] A proximity-effect correction stage, run as an offline AudioSuite (batch, non-realtime) pass rather than a realtime insert, keyed to trigger only on specific bothersome moments rather than continuously.** "It's just the proximity stuff again — whenever that triggers that Number 4 you can see here, it's just getting compressed... it might've bothered me once when I'm listening to this song, I'm going to go get that. You can do it through AudioSuite if you want." Reads as spot-fixing inconsistent low-mid buildup from the singer's mic distance changing between phrases (the classic [[Vocal Mic Technique|proximity effect]]) with a targeted dynamic-EQ/compressor move rather than a static cut, applied surgically to the specific word/phrase that triggers it instead of the whole take. This whole approach — chasing proximity-effect buildup after the fact with dynamic EQ — is only necessary *because* the vocal arrived wet/printed; a dry take would get this handled with a standard compressor instead ("if they were dry files and I was trying to compress... my approach is completely different"). → [[Vocal Mic Technique]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Compression]]
- **[2:20–2:40] Parallel compression as the ONLY compression on the lead vocal — explicitly chosen to preserve the artist's original dynamics rather than level them.** "The only compression I've got going on is parallel. It's tucked in, so I'm keeping all the dynamics of their song, but all I'm doing is tucking in, just a bit of a nicer version with all my work." A direct real-world case for parallel-over-serial compression specifically to respect a wet/printed vocal's existing dynamic performance instead of squashing it — see the general mechanic on [[Pro-C 2]] and [[Parallel Processing]]. → [[Pro-C 2]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[Compression]]
- **[2:55–3:40] Diagnosing sibilance by ear against the artist's rough mix, targeting three distinct consonant sounds by name (not just "esses"): 's', 'ch', and 'ts'.** "I noticed that the 'esses' and the 'ch' and the 'ts', all those sorts of sibilant sounds were the main concern, listening to the rough and going, okay, cool. Now I compare it to the wet files and say that's my biggest thing I need to work on." A useful reminder that "sibilance" isn't just S — affricates (ch, ts) sit in the same harsh consonant-cluster problem zone and get chased the same way. → [[De-essing]]
- **[3:40–4:10] Bus-level de-essing on the lead vocal Group, with a named parameter value: set to 3.7.** "I've taken all these leads... sent them to a lead Group, which also has... this will be doing the 'chhh'... what is that set to? 3.7. This is for the esses." No unit given for "3.7" (likely a threshold or range value on whatever de-esser is loaded on the group) — read as a bus-safety-net de-ess layered on top of the per-track de-essing already done upstream, same pattern already documented on [[Pro-DS]]'s "Ensemble Stack Group / Vocal Bus" composite de-essing use. → [[Pro-DS]], [[De-essing]]
- **[3:40–4:10] A gentle low-pass on the lead vocal bus around 10 kHz, paired with a broad mid-frequency cut — a mellowing/darkening move on the group rather than the usual "add air" EQ instinct.** "I'm even filtering the leads, I'm filtering everything above — I think, yeah, 10 kHz, relatively gentle slope... and I'm pulling a load of mids down." Framed by the engineer himself as unexpected even to him on review ("that's interesting even for me to look at") — worth noting as a case where a bus gets *darkened and thinned* rather than brightened, presumably to make room for Slowthai's vocal and keep James's lead from fighting for the same top-end/mid real estate. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[MIDS Slot]], [[AIR Slot]]
- **[4:10–4:20] A separate backing-vocal Group runs the same chain shape but with a different, specifically-named de-esser: the "Weiss Deess."** "So the BVs are all going to another Group with the same de-esser on it, the Weiss Deess." Not owned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. Confirms the same lead/BV split-group-processing pattern used elsewhere in this vault (separate de-essing per group so the loudest/most-forward voice sets the sibilance standard, backing layers get their own pass). → [[Pro-DS]], [[De-essing]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **[4:20–6:00] The chain's headline move: a 32-band dynamic-EQ processor ("Trackspacer") on the combined lead+BV vocal Group, sidechained to a DIFFERENT singer's vocal (Slowthai), set to M/S mode and Mid-channel-only, so only the center of James's voice ducks — and only in the 1–6 kHz zone — at the exact moments the two vocalists sing together.** "This is like 32 bands of EQ, individually being turned down — it's like a multiband compressor but loads of bands... I've set the sidechain to be Slowthai's voice. So at the point where they sing together, Slowthai comes in, James's vocal will duck down in the area which allows Slowthai to cut through the most... that is between 1 kHz and about 6 kHz... I've got it set to M/S and all the way in the Mid, so it's only in the center that this compression is happening." **Why M/S + Mid-only, explicitly stated:** the processor is inserted on the whole Group, which includes the printed reverb tails from the wet delivery (see the first bullet) — running it broadband/stereo would duck the reverb along with the vocal, which he doesn't want; restricting it to the Mid channel only touches the dead-center dry vocal content and leaves the (presumably wider/more diffuse) reverb largely untouched. The stated payoff without it: "you get a bit of like... mess in the middle" at the moments the two vocalists overlap — he calls out the specific word "champagne" as an example that bothered him pre-fix. This is a textbook **frequency-selective, cross-vocalist sidechain duck** — masking avoidance between two co-lead voices sharing the same 1–6 kHz "presence" real estate, solved with automatic, moment-to-moment ducking instead of manual volume automation. Owned-equivalent path: [[Pro-Q 4]]'s per-band **Dynamic EQ + external sidechain + Stereo Placement (Mid/Side per band)** combination — already documented on that note as capable of exactly this "duck one vocalist's specific frequency range off another's sidechain, mid/side-targeted" move; [[Soothe2]]'s **external sidechain** mode is a second, less surgical owned option for the same job (broadband rather than the 32-band per-bin control this tool has). Not owned itself — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Soothe2]], [[Sidechaining]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **[6:00–6:45] Clariphonic as the final chain stage, framed explicitly as restoring "shimmer" that earlier processing may have cost, not as a first-pass brightening move.** "And then after that I'm doing a bit of Clariphonic to then make up for overall Shimmer where I might have lost it along the way." Confirms the parallel-EQ/air-lift role already documented on [[Clariphonic DSP MKII]], with a specific placement rationale worth keeping: run it LAST, as a corrective top-up for whatever top-end got dulled by the de-essing/EQ/Soothe/Trackspacer stack upstream, rather than shaping brightness from scratch. → [[Clariphonic DSP MKII]], [[EQ]], [[AIR Slot]]

# Music theory you actually need, none of the sh*t you don't

## Synopsis

A BassBuzz theory-prioritization video, not a theory-content video — its subject is *how much and which* theory to learn, not the theory itself. Opens with a cold-open parody (memorize the circle of fifths, diatonic modes, upper extensions, functional harmonic analysis, syncopated 16th-note subdivisions — "and voila, you're a real bassist") to set up its actual complaint: most teachers dump theoretical jargon on students without ever asking what they're trying to achieve, illustrated with a gym analogy (a client who "just wants to lose eight pounds and not have back pain" getting handed an Olympic-weightlifting program). The fix is a **five-level goal ladder** — Bedroom Player, Casual Jammer, Bar Band Bassist, Working Pro, Extreme Jazz-Fusion Shredder — where each tier names the *minimum* theory that actually unlocks that goal, repeatedly undercutting its own list with the caveat that plenty of real gigging bassists skip the "official" theory for a tier and get there anyway through ear/feel/shapes. The video is explicit it isn't "theory is optional" (level four/five genuinely need real theory) nor "learn everything" (most of level five is actively the wrong material for a bedroom player or casual jammer) — it's goal-matching. Closes with a single portable filter for any theory topic from any source: *"will learning this make me play closer to my goals? If you or your teacher can't answer that question, don't learn that thing."* Two concrete, attributable content nuggets worth pulling out beyond the framework itself: a **Lee Sklar** quote on the specific reading skills a working pro needs (Nashville number charts, chord charts, notated music), and a pointed **downgrade of the circle of fifths** ("kind of useful for memorizing key signatures, but not the frigging Rosetta Stone... skip the circle and work on your groove") from a speaker citing 15 years of teaching experience. This is a sibling video to two other BassBuzz notes already in the vault: [[Goal-Tiered Practice and the Self-Taught Bass Commandments]] (the general goal-ladder/practice-design framework this video's theory-specific version sits inside) and [[Music Theory for Bass]] (BassBuzz's separate "what theory actually is" survey video, explicitly name-checked mid-video as the "avoid buying my course" alternative for level-three content).

## What's taught

The complete five-level framework, per-tier theory checklists, worked examples, and every direct quote are captured in full on the new synthesis note this video justified creating — **[[The Five-Tier Bass Theory Curriculum]]** — rather than duplicated here; that note is the master reference for the content itself. Summary of the ladder:

- **Level 1 — Bedroom Player** (play favorite songs alone, no pressure): minimum theory ≈ zero. Real unlock is **tab, not sheet music** (learnable in minutes vs. months) plus basic rhythm theory for tab's blind spot (timing), and strong basic technique matters more than theory at this tier.
- **Level 2 — Casual Jammer** (play with friends without embarrassing yourself): note names on the neck (so you can follow a called-out note/chord instead of just a fret number), basic drum/rhythm-section awareness ("the rhythm section is the master of the universe"), and common bass line shapes usable by pattern-recognition alone, no formal theory required.
- **Level 3 — Bar Band Bassist** (learn a real setlist fast under gig pressure): intervals + ear training, chord progressions ("the biggest key to understanding the *why* of modern music"), major/minor chords, arpeggios, diatonic progressions, Nashville numbers. Minimum-theory shortcut: three scales cover 90%+ of common riffs — **major pentatonic (Queen)**, **minor pentatonic (Audioslave)**, **blues scale ("Sunshine of Your Love")**. Explicit caveat that plenty of gigging bassists don't know this theory formally and get by on ear/shapes anyway — the tier's real value is *speed*, not gatekeeping.
- **Level 4 — Working Pro** (any gig, any band, no fixed repertoire safety net): major/minor scales beyond the pentatonics, reading music (opens specific gig types — musicals, cruise ships, session work — rather than making you a "better player" generally; **Lee Sklar** quote on reading Nashville charts/chord charts/notated music), and a deliberate downgrade of the circle of fifths to "useful for key signatures, not the Rosetta Stone — work on your groove instead."
- **Level 5 — Extreme Jazz-Fusion Shredder** (play anything, with anyone, any shred level — Jaco Pastorius/Stanley Clarke aspirational tier): all seven major modes, odd time signatures (5/7/9, for Snarky Puppy/Rush), whole-tone scale (Thelonious Monk), harmonic and melodic minor + their modes, upper chord extensions (9/11/13 + alterations, sus/dim/aug chords), polyrhythms, harmonics (Jaco's sound), and non-diatonic changes (tritone subs, secondary dominants, borrowed chords/modal interchange). Given as a rapid checklist explicitly so viewers can *recognize* over-scoped teaching aimed at a goal they don't have, not as content to absorb from this video.
- **Closing filter, applicable to any theory topic from any source:** "will learning this make me play closer to my goals? If you or your teacher can't answer that question, don't learn that thing."

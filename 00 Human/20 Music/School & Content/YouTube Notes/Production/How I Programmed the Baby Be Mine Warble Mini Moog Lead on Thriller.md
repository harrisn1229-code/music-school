# How I Programmed the Baby Be Mine "warble" Mini Moog Lead on Michael Jackson's Thriller

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bfqEW5drpuI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bfqEW5drpuI)

## Synopsis

A tight, numbers-first recreation of the "warble" Minimoog lead from Michael Jackson's "Baby Be Mine" (Thriller), by the synth programmer who played it on the original session. Real settings given, plus a genuinely useful articulation trick for phrasing on a non-velocity-sensitive keyboard.

## Techniques

- **Two sawtooth oscillators an octave apart (8' and 16'), both full volume; filter closed to zero so the envelope-to-filter amount does all shaping; filter Contour ~7.8/8 with resonance ALMOST as high (~8/8) — resonance this close to self-oscillation is described as extremely sensitive ("barely moving it" is the difference between clean and a "squirt" artifact).** Attack ~200ms (a little "lip" to the onset, not instant), Decay ~600ms, with enough sustain level that the sound doesn't fully die — letting phrases connect rather than each note starting cold.
- **"Getting it close with numbers only gets you to about 85% — then use your ear."** Explicit numeric settings are a starting point, not the final word; direct restatement of this channel's recurring 80%-by-panel, rest-by-ear discipline (see [[Synth Patching and Editing Method]]), here quantified slightly higher at 85% since exact numbers were given.
- **Phrasing/articulation control on a keyboard with NO velocity or aftertouch, purely via legato (connected) vs. staccato (disconnected) note timing** — playing notes connected (overlapping/no gap) produces a different phrase shape than playing them disconnected, entirely independent of how hard the keys are struck. A reminder that expressive phrasing doesn't require a velocity-sensitive controller if the envelope/legato behavior is used deliberately.
- **VCA decay set slightly LONGER than the filter envelope's decay time (not equal, not zero) — specifically so the filter's own decay shape remains audible rather than being cut short by the amp stage.** A more precise variant of the "VCA mostly acts as a gate" pattern seen elsewhere on this channel: here the VCA decay isn't just "long," it's deliberately tuned relative to the filter's own decay, and called out as a "less critical" control — small changes matter less here than on the filter stage.

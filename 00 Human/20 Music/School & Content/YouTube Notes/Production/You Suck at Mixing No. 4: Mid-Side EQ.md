# You Suck at Mixing No. 4: Mid-Side EQ

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MlGYmSPy5DE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MlGYmSPy5DE)

## Synopsis
A short, practical intro to mid/side EQ with two worked examples: taming a bass's stereo width by cutting the side channel, and widening a snare's reverb tail by boosting the side channel and cutting the same band in the middle to make room. Frames the whole mix as a tree — mono trunk (kick/bass) widening into branches (highs) — and shows the diagnostic step of checking a track's mid/side content with a Utility before reaching for a mid/side EQ.

## Techniques
- **Diagnose mid vs. side content with Utility before EQing** — put Utility on a track, right-click its Width knob, and switch to Mid/Side mode: this lets you solo/listen to just the mid content (identical on L/R) versus just the side content (out-of-phase, the "wide and weird" part) before deciding what to carve. On a bass, the mid content sounded appropriately mono while the side content was the muddy, club-unfriendly part. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Mono the bass's side content with a mid/side EQ cut** — switch EQ Eight to Mid/Side mode, leave the mid band untouched, and cut the low end specifically on the side channel — cleans up a bass's stereo smear (which won't translate on a mono system/club rig anyway) without narrowing or dulling the mono core of the sound. → [[EQ]]
- **Widen a snare's reverb by boosting the SIDE channel, not the middle, and carving the same band out of the middle to make room** — with EQ Eight in Mid/Side mode, boosting a snare's airy reverb band on the side channel reads as spacious and pleasant; boosting the identical band in the middle instead sounds completely different (and worse) — the airiness belongs on the sides. Cutting that same frequency range out of the middle channel afterward gives the side boost more room to be heard. The same side-boost move works on rides/cymbals to widen the whole drum bus. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]]

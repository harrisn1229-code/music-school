# 6 BEST Melodic Arpeggios (all guitarists MUST know!)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sd92rbX6YgA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sd92rbX6YgA)

## Synopsis

The core trick, stated directly: any chord you can build (not just a plain triad or diatonic 7th) can be broken into an arpeggio — so extended/altered/modal chord vocabulary is also a source of new arpeggio vocabulary, not just a comping concern. Demonstrated on six colorful chords built off A, each turned into an arpeggio: Am11 (with 9th and 11th added), A minor(major7) (harmonic minor color), Amaj7(13), Amaj♯11 (Lydian color), a stacked-major-3rds augmented arpeggio, and Am(add9).

## What's taught

- **The core trick: any chord — however extended or altered — can be broken into an arpeggio.** Reframes arpeggio vocabulary as directly tied to chord-extension knowledge: learning a new colorful chord voicing is also learning a new arpeggio shape, not two separate skills.
- **Am11** — A minor triad (A-C-E) with ♭7 (G), 9th (B), and 11th (D) added, arpeggiated — described as a dramatically more sophisticated sound than the plain triad most rock/metal sweep-picking defaults to.
- **A minor(major7)** — the A minor triad with a major 7th (G♯) instead of ♭7, i.e. harmonic minor's tonic chord — the "characteristic major 7th" is named as exactly what gives it the harmonic-minor color once arpeggiated (matches [[Harmonic Minor Modes]]'s existing minor(maj7) tonic-chord entry).
- **Amaj7(13)** — A major triad with major 7th and 13th added.
- **Amaj♯11** — A major triad with a raised 11th (D♯), the Lydian characteristic tone, explicitly framed as "think about modes when building arpeggio shapes, not just chord types" — the ♯11 is what makes it read as Lydian once broken into an arpeggio.
- **Augmented arpeggio (stacked major 3rds)** — an augmented triad (major 3rds stacked continuously) arpeggiated, named as an underused sound outside fusion/technical death metal.
- **Am(add9)** — described as a personal favorite: just the plain A minor triad with a 9th (B) added (no 7th) — the smallest single-note addition of the six, but stated to make a dramatic audible difference over a plain minor triad. Cited as a recurring sound across his album *Elevation*, both in chords and arpeggios.

# Songs that use the Creep chord progression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NyiEMdbfjG8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NyiEMdbfjG8)

## Synopsis

A deep dive on Radiohead's "Creep" progression — genuinely rare among songwriters despite the fame of the songs that use it — covering the secondary-dominant/deceptive-cadence reading of its distinctive I→III move, the minor plagal cadence that closes it, and the two real-world plagiarism disputes tied to it.

## Techniques

- **The progression: I–III–IV–iv** (G–B–C–Cm, in G).
- **I→III (G→B major)**: chromatic — B minor would be the diatonic chord here. Two readings: a chromatic mediant (two same-quality chords a 3rd apart), or — Bennett's preferred read — a secondary dominant (B is V of vi/Em) resolving deceptively to IV instead of the expected vi, "faking out" the anticipated destination while still functioning as a dominant pull.
- **IV→iv→I**: a minor plagal cadence — darkening the subdominant chord for a bittersweet, nostalgic color, glued by tight voice-leading (only one note moves at each step: E→E♭→D).
- **Note-spelling rule (tangential but demonstrated)**: triad spelling should always use a letter-skip-letter pattern (so a stack reads visually as a clean triad on the staff) — which is why the same physical note is called D♯ on the B chord but E♭ on the Cm chord in this progression.
- **Songs**: "That's When Your Heartaches Begin" (Ink Spots/Elvis, 1937 — earliest known use); "Space Oddity" (David Bowie — same chord order, different harmonic rhythm, so only an "almost" match); "Goodbye Moonmen" (*Rick and Morty*, likely influenced by Bowie's use); "Creep" (Radiohead); "The Air That I Breathe" (The Hollies) — Radiohead were successfully sued specifically over melodic similarity, not the shared chords alone (a shared progression isn't grounds for plagiarism on its own); "Get Free" (Lana Del Rey) — accused by Radiohead's publisher in 2018 of the same combination (chords + harmonic rhythm + melody), though the case never appears to have reached court.

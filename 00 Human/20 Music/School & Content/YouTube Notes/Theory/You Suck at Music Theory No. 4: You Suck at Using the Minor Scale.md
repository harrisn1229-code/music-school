## What's taught
Underbelly builds the Ionian (major) scale, then derives its Aeolian (natural minor) relative two ways — the relative-minor shortcut (start on the major scale's 6th degree) and the parallel/formula shortcut (flatten scale degrees 3, 6, and 7 of the same-root major scale) — before building the full diatonic chord set on an Aeolian tonic and isolating the one move that "instantly reads as Aeolian regardless of genre": the tonic sliding to its ♭VI. Real-song references (a well-known cinematic score cue, an EDM track) anchor that i–♭VI (and its EDM cousin ♭VI–♭VII–i) sound to "serious, cinematic, melancholic" across genres, plus a third example landing on an i–♭III–♭VII cycle.

## Techniques
- **Two ways to find/build Aeolian: relative shortcut vs. parallel-formula shortcut** — relative: play the exact same 7 notes as a known major scale but start on its 6th scale degree (G Ionian's 6th degree is E → E Aeolian is "G major played from E"); parallel: build off any root by flattening scale degrees 3, 6, and 7 of that root's own major scale (C Ionian → C Aeolian = 1 2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7). Both land on the same natural-minor formula. → [[Diatonic Modes]], [[Scales]], [[Keys]]
- **Build the full diatonic chord set on any Aeolian tonic** — stacking triads on every degree of natural minor gives i, ii°, ♭III, iv, v, ♭VI, ♭VII. → [[Chords by Key]]
- **The i → ♭VI two-chord move is the fastest way to "sound Aeolian," genre-independent** — demonstrated in D Aeolian (D minor → E♭ major, the second chord voiced as an inversion for a smoother top line) as a well-known dramatic cinematic-score cue, and the same relationship recurs as a ♭VI–♭VII–i cycle in an EDM track in A Aeolian (F major → G major → A minor, cycling). A third example in E Aeolian lands on an i–♭III–♭VII cycle (Em → G → D). → [[Chord Progressions]]

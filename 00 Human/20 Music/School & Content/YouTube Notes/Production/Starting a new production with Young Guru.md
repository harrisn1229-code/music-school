# Starting a new production with Young Guru

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QDvQ8x0QyFI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QDvQ8x0QyFI)

## Synopsis

A short, talking-head walkthrough from Young Guru (longtime Jay-Z engineer/producer) on how he starts a production completely from scratch — not a mixing tutorial, no plugin settings or gear chains, but a genuinely useful process/mindset breakdown of the pre-production step most breakdowns skip. He frames it around a real song made with his production partner Volley Contra (a duo since ~2014, Volley both artist and producer): starting in an MPC, moving into Ableton for some tricks, then handing the product to Volley to finish in Pro Tools. The most valuable material is the two threads that run through the whole video — where creative sparks actually come from (digging old records for hours, treating the dig as a study of production history, sampling around vocal words and manipulating them even when the sample itself never survives to the final track) and how he decides *what* to make when he's capable of many genres (reggae, rock, hip-hop) — namely, conversation with the artist about who they are and where they want to go, before any sound gets touched. He also states a clear tool-fluency philosophy: reach for the tool you know fastest during creative time rather than getting lost exploring new plugins.

**Transcript confidence: high.** Clean YouTube captions, a single unbroken spoken monologue with no music bed to interfere — no garbled numbers or dropped words.

## Techniques

- **Dig old records for hours as the primary source of inspiration, treating the dig itself as a study of production history — "how people got sounds in whatever era."** States this makes him "a better musician, engineer, producer" independent of whether any given sample is ever used; the digging is research, not just source-hunting. Even if the sample itself doesn't make the final product, it's what "creates and kicks off and sparks" the idea. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Sample around WORDS specifically, then manipulate what's pulled (speed up or slow down) purely to get into a mood — not necessarily to keep the result.** States this is his personal "calling card": hearing tones/instruments in a record he feels he can manipulate himself, sampling around the vocal words, and speeding/slowing the chop just to get himself started creatively. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Worked example of the dig-to-spark loop:** found Chairman of the Board's 1972 album *Bittersweet* while digging, and one song in particular — "As Much As I Need" — caught his ear and became the session's spark.
- **Multi-tool/multi-DAW production pipeline with a co-producer:** initial production starts in an **MPC** (his tool of choice for years), exports into **Ableton** for tricks/tools he says most people don't know about, then the product is shared with production partner Volley Contra who finishes the record in **Pro Tools**. A real cross-DAW handoff between two collaborators, not a single-person workflow. → [[Career & Release]]
- **Long-running two-person production team where the "artist" is also a co-producer** — Volley Contra (connected via the internet, partnership since ~2014) is both artist and producer on their joint output, which Young Guru describes as genre-spanning ("halfway between Pink Floyd and Biggie" — hip-hop, reggae, and rock influences, singing and chatting/toasting all present).
- **Tool-fluency over gear-chasing: grab the tool you know fastest once it's time to actually work, rather than exploring new plugins.** Direct quote reasoning: "us as engineers, we love the latest greatest, we love new plugins, we love to explore new things, but when it's time to really get to work you need to grab that tool that will allow you to do exactly what you want to do the fastest... you don't want to waste the inspiration time saying, how do I do this specific thing." For him that tool is the MPC (MPC 3000-rooted workflow, now carried everywhere via MPC Studio). → [[Career & Release]], [[Akai MPC3000]]
- **A large toolbox creates a paradox of choice at the start of a session — having many genres available (reggae/rock/hip-hop) makes it HARDER, not easier, to know what to make today.** Stated directly as the reason he needs an external anchor (the record dig) before he can commit to a direction — otherwise "it's kind of hard to figure out okay well what am I going to do today." → [[Career & Release]]
- **Pre-production is a conversation with the artist, not a solo sonic decision — understand where they are in their life, their catalog, and where they want to go vs. where you think they should go, before creating anything.** Explicitly contrasts this with what he frames as the current default (making beats alone and figuring out later who they're for) — his own process is built around creating FOR a specific person he already understands, arrived at through ongoing conversation and hanging out, not a brief. States this understanding informs "what tones to give that person" and "what sort of ideas you can suggest to them."
- **Knowing what an artist explicitly won't do or where they don't want to go is stated as just as valuable as knowing where they DO want to go** — a boundary-mapping step alongside the direction-finding conversation above.

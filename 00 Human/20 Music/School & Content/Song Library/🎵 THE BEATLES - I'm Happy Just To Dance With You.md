# I'm Happy Just To Dance With You — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A short song with a paradoxically long form — brisk tempo, small sections, a refrain heard three times plus its second half doing duty as the intro. Analytically the interest is the **major/minor pairing**: the relative keys keep swapping the role of home, with the verse always clearly major, the intro and refrains always starting minor, and the outro turning that duality into an actual fight before the major wins ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]).

**Form.** Intro and refrain share a plan of four 2-bar phrases, the first four bars instrumental and the last four turning out to be the refrain's back half. The verse is also 8 bars in four 2-bar phrases: a parallel couplet, then two phrases heard as one long balancing phrase. The outro is **7 bars, elided with the last bar of the final verse**.

**Harmony.** Chord selection is plain except for an **augmented V** substituting for the ordinary major one. The verse gets a jazzy touch from putting **iii between I and ii** where vi would have been the obvious choice — that parallel chord-stream motion is the whole flavour. The outro is the payoff, entered by a **deceptive cadence** off the augmented chord ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]): you expect the tonic and get vi instead, which flips the key reading back to minor. Their standard trick would be a triple rote repeat of the final phrase; here they pull the deceptive cadence **twice** and only play it straight the third time. The ending is on an added-sixth chord — I and vi superimposed, the major prevailing with the bitter-sweet vi still embedded in it.

**Melody.** The refrain tune is markedly pentatonic with downward gestures dominating; the verse tune is unrestricted in pitch content and shaped as an arch. That contrast, plus the key contrast, is what separates sections that are otherwise formally identical.

**Arrangement.** George's double-tracked lead. The apparently throwaway reverberated backing part is actually what carries the song's rhythmic hook: its move always lands with a heavy syncopation on the "two-and", punctuated by a drum fill in the intro and first refrain. Backing-vocal entries are staged rather than repeated — later on they enter a phrase earlier than they did the first time.

# When I'm Sixty-Four — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** a **perfect arch** — Intro · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Outro, symmetrical end to end. No section doubles up, there is no separate refrain, and the outro is the intro again. The verse is a four-square 16 phrased ABA′C; the bridge is an unusual **17**, a square 16 with one extra measure tacked on. The intro's four measures plus a **two-measure vamp** — the band waiting on the singer — is a convention of the style, not an accident.

The harmony sounds clunky on purpose while actually running secondary dominants, added-note chords and harmonised chromatic basslines underneath. The reason a busy progression still sits still is that **every phrase opens on the chord the previous phrase closed on**, with the harmonic rhythm accelerating toward the end of the verse ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]).

Two chromatic rising basslines carry phrases 2 and 4, and Pollack's distinction between them is the transferable part ([[POP-C04 — Four chords with a moving bass]]): the first is contrapuntal filling inside one sustained dominant, the second earns a Roman numeral per step because each bass note lasts twice as long and there is real root motion underneath. The diminished seventh in the last verse phrase is a first for the catalogue — a **subdominant** diminished seventh built on ♯2 rather than the usual VII-based dominant substitute ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]).

The two bridges tease the vocal differently — the first withholds the singer for a whole phrase so you start assuming it is instrumental.

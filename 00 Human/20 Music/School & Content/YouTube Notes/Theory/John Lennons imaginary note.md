# John Lennon's imaginary note

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7TouUqXIxOw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7TouUqXIxOw)

## Synopsis

A production-history investigation into a faint, "ghostly" D note that appears in some published transcriptions of "Imagine"'s piano riff but not others — tracked down using archival studio footage to an unintentional overdub artifact rather than a deliberate compositional choice.

## Techniques

- **Archival session footage as a verification method, more reliable than published sheet music.** Rather than trusting conflicting transcriptions (some include the extra D, some don't), the investigation goes to actual footage of Lennon performing the riff in the studio — confirming he does NOT play the D in his primary take, settling a dispute published notation alone couldn't resolve.
- **The real source: an unintentional double-tracking artifact.** Lennon double-tracked the piano part himself (they'd briefly experimented with a second session keyboardist, Nicky Hopkins, playing simultaneously, but scrapped that in favor of Lennon overdubbing his own performance). The faint D most likely comes from a slight difference between the two overlaid takes — audible specifically as the piano widens into full stereo image right as the vocal enters, the moment the second take layers in.
- **A useful diagnostic for identifying double-tracking in a mix**: listen for where a mono or narrow-image part suddenly widens into stereo — that's often exactly where a second, independently-performed take has been layered on top of the first, and any inconsistency between the two takes (like the stray D here) becomes an audible fingerprint of the technique.

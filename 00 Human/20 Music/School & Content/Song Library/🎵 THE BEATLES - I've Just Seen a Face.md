## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — I've Just Seen A Face]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #74.*

**Form.** Four chords deployed two ways — the pop-rock cliché **I – vi – IV – V** in the verses, the bluesy **V – IV – I** in the refrains — and everything else is structural sleight of hand. The layout borrows a different genre's habits in each of its three stretches: two verses back to back at the top is pop-rock, the strict verse/refrain alternation in the second half is folk, and closing on the **refrain three times over** is an R&B rave-up habit. Intro **10 bars**, fully instrumental, three phrases with the last foreshortened so the first verse arrives early ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]). Verse **12 bars** in three phrases with slow harmonic rhythm — the blues frame carrying distinctly non-blues harmony ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]). Refrain 8 bars, AAB. **The tempo never actually changes**: the intro's slow triplet pulse against the verse's four-square beat produces an illusion of acceleration.

**Harmony.** `Verse: I (4 bars) · vi (4) · IV (2) – V – I` · `Refrain: V (2) – IV (2) – I – IV – I`. The intro is the interesting passage — it opens on **vi under an unaccompanied bass note of the tonic** and stays unmoored until its final bars, moving vi → IV (with a 9/6/4 and then a 7th stacked on by passing tones) → V → I, with the V voiced with an **appoggiatura in the bass instead of the root**.

**Notable.** The intro's long scalar bassline spans a full octave and has the song's `D – C♯ – B – A – G♯ – A` hook phrase embedded in its tail, **so the hook is introduced as a bass line before it is ever a vocal one**. In the verse, bars 3–5 put the tune in parallel tenths with the bass and the identical gesture at bars 7–9 comes out as parallel fifths. The song's only melodic minor third appears in the refrain, which is why that section reads bluesier. Wire brushes rather than sticks; maracas overdubbed onto refrains and solo only.

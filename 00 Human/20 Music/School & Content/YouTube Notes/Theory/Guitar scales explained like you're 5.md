# Guitar scales explained like you're 5

## Synopsis

A beginner-level explainer of the seven major-scale modes and their intervallic structure relative to the major scale, plus the observation that five of the seven modes have a usable 5-note pentatonic subset, informally named after their parent mode. All of the concrete theory content is already documented in more depth by the vault's existing [[Diatonic Modes]] page.

## Techniques

- **Mode-per-pentatonic naming convention**: informally naming a pentatonic shape after the mode it's a subset of — major pentatonic (Ionian), Dorian pentatonic, Phrygian pentatonic, Mixolydian pentatonic, minor/Aeolian pentatonic — describing each as "the mode's skeleton, missing two notes." Locrian and Lydian are the two modes without a commonly-used named pentatonic subset in this framing. *(This naming convention already appears informally in [[Positional CAGED Soloing with Matched Modes and Arpeggios]] from earlier in this batch; no new mechanism here.)*
- **Total-scope framing**: across the major scale's 7 modes, harmonic minor's 7 modes, and melodic minor's 7 modes, plus their pentatonic subsets, a player is looking at roughly 26 distinct shapes to fully internalize — offered as context for why this is a genuinely large body of material to learn, not a quick-tip shortcut.

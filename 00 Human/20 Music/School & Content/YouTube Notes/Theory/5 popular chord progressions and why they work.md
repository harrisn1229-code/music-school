# 5 popular chord progressions and why they work

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cjOQIbkRQ-4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cjOQIbkRQ-4)

## Synopsis

David Bennett walks through five chord progressions that recur across many songs, but the point of the video isn't cataloging them — it's explaining the specific mechanism that makes each one work (a harmonic sequence, a scale/mode blend, a functional T/SD/D cycle, a modal color, an asymmetric bar-length tension trick). Each is demonstrated at the piano and illustrated with song examples.

## Techniques

- **I–V–♭VII–IV** (C–G–B♭–F) — a harmonic sequence: a major chord, then another major chord a 4th lower, repeated once. The repeated shape (transposed, not repeated literally) is what reads as cohesive/inevitable rather than random. Also blends two scales: I and V are diatonic to the major scale, but ♭VII belongs to Mixolydian (the major scale with a flattened 7th degree), borrowed in for a cooler, more mellow color than a pure major-scale progression would have. Heard in "Kryptonite" (3 Doors Down), "Waterfalls" (TLC), "Rio" (Duran Duran), "Wrapped Around Your Finger" (The Police), "Satellite of Love" (Lou Reed), "Turning Japanese" (The Vapors).
- **I–ii–IV–V, the "major scale climb"** (C–Dm–F–G) — nicknamed the "My Girl" progression after The Temptations' "My Girl." A plain diatonic step-up through the scale degrees used; the ascending motion itself is the whole character, well-suited to an uplifting lyric/mood.
- **I–IV–I–V** (C–F–C–G) — the T→SD→T→D functional cycle at its simplest: I sits fully at rest, IV (subdominant) introduces mild tension without demanding resolution, the return to I gives release, and V (dominant) then creates real pull back home. Heard in "American Pie" (Don McLean).
- **Phrygian vamp — i–♭II** (Em–F, in E Phrygian) — a two-chord loop that evokes Phrygian's dark/eerie quality purely from the semitone root relationship between i and ♭II, with no other chords needed to establish the mode.
- **IV–I–V, asymmetric bar lengths** (1 bar IV, 1 bar I, 2 bars V) — starting on IV instead of I signals immediate forward motion; briefly landing on I teases resolution without settling into it; the extra-long V bar (2 bars of maximum dominant tension) then creates a perpetual, unresolved pull, since the loop restarts on IV rather than resolving to I.

## Techniques
- **Find boxy "cardboard" resonance with a narrow-Q sweep, then cut it (subtractive EQ)** — add an EQ Eight, turn off band 1, drag band 2 all the way up and narrow its Q, then play the kick in the mix and sweep the frequency until the boxy/cardboard-sounding resonance is located (in the video, around ~121 Hz); cut it rather than boosting elsewhere. The frequencies on either side of the cut read as *bigger* once the offending peak is gone — that's the whole logic of subtractive EQ. A second narrow, steep cut nearby can clear out leftover "gunk" the same way.
- **Drum Bus's Boom feature adds sub weight, but only works if it's tuned to the song** — Drum Bus (Ableton's stock drum-glue device) has a Boom control for extra low-end weight under the kick; it needs to be in tune with the track's key or it reads as a clash rather than reinforcement — either drag the Boom frequency by ear, or use the built-in button that auto-snaps it to the nearest note, to land it on the song's root note (C# in the video's example).
- **Add a touch of Transients for extra punch, and group processing stages for a clean A/B** — a small boost on Drum Bus's Transients knob adds punch on top of the tuned Boom; group the EQ and Drum Bus together (Cmd+G) so both toggle on/off together for a clean before/after comparison against the untreated kick.
- **A dedicated fast "ghost" MIDI transient as the sidechain source instead of the kick itself, for tighter control over duck length** — a compressor sidechained straight off the kick sample has a hard floor on how tight the ducking effect can get, because the kick's own transient/sample length (measured in the video at ~128 ms) sets the *minimum* duck length regardless of how the compressor's Release is set. The fix: create a new MIDI track ("ghost"), load a very short, fast transient sound (a pitched-up glitch pluck), program it to hit everywhere the kick hits, and route the target's sidechain input to THAT track instead of the kick — now the compressor's Release knob has full, unconstrained control over how long the duck lasts.

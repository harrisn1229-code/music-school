# 8 Led Zeppelin Songs That 'Rip Off' Other Songs

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efuOELImxAc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efuOELImxAc)

## Synopsis

Made in collaboration with Lil's Music, David Bennett catalogs eight Led Zeppelin cases of borrowing from other artists — spanning direct lyric lifts, uncredited covers reworked into "originals," miscredited public-domain claims, and one case that went to court and was decided in Zeppelin's favor. Unlike this channel's usual influence-framed "rip off" videos, most of these are documented legal cases with actual settlements and credit changes.

## Techniques

- **Direct lyric/melody lift with a proven paper trail**: "Whole Lotta Love" lifted its vocal phrasing/lyrics from Muddy Waters/Willie Dixon's "You Need Love" (via the Small Faces' cover) — 1985 lawsuit settled, Dixon given an official songwriting credit; Plant later admitted the lift was deliberate.
- **Uncredited cover reworked into an "original"**: "Bring It On Home" (Willie Dixon/Sonny Boy Williamson II) — spliced with original material, no royalties paid; sued 1972, settled, now credited solely to Dixon. The same 1972 suit also covered "The Lemon Song," which began as a live cover of Howlin' Wolf's "Killing Floor."
- **Cover evolved into an "original" over a touring cycle**: "Dazed and Confused" began as a Jake Holmes folk song, covered live by The Yardbirds (Page's prior band) before Page carried it into Zeppelin with new vocals — Holmes sued in 2010, settled, now credited "Page, inspired by Jake Holmes."
- **Miscredited public domain**: "Babe I'm Gonna Leave You" — both Joan Baez's and Zeppelin's versions listed the song as "traditional"; it was actually written by Anne Bredon, later credited and reimbursed on both.
- **Arrangement lift shielded by public domain status**: "Black Mountain Side" borrowed Bert Jansch's original guitar arrangement of a genuinely traditional tune — no suit, since the underlying song was public domain even though the arrangement wasn't.
- **Uncontested homage**: "Rock and Roll"'s opening drum fill directly lifts Little Richard's "Keep A-Knockin'" — no lawsuit, likely because drum beats are harder to copyright than melody/lyrics and/or the borrowing reads as overt homage.
- **Sued and WON**: "Stairway to Heaven" vs. Spirit's "Taurus" — both use a similar A-minor arpeggiated progression; court ruled (2020) that a common chord progression, even played similarly, isn't infringement on its own.

## Synopsis

BERNTH teaches a "diminished pentatonic" scale — the minor pentatonic with its 5th flattened — as a one-note-edit alternative to sweeping symmetrical diminished arpeggios when soloing over a diminished chord, alongside the underlying diminished-triad theory and its diatonic placement.

## What's taught

Full extraction routed directly — see [[Diminished Pentatonic Scale]] and [[Diminished Chords]] for the complete breakdown: a diminished triad is a minor triad with a flattened 5th (root-♭3-♭5), occurring diatonically on the vii° of a major key and the ii° of a natural minor key (worked: B° in C major, D° in C minor). Combining that ♭5 color with the minor pentatonic box produces a five-note "diminished pentatonic" scale — identical to minor pentatonic except the perfect 5th becomes a ♭5 in every octave, no new shape to learn from scratch. Offered as a genuinely different-sounding alternative to defaulting to the same symmetrical diminished-arpeggio sweep shape every time a diminished chord shows up in a rock/metal context.

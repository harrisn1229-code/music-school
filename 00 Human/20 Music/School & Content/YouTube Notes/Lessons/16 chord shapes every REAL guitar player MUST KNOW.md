# 16 chord shapes every REAL guitar player MUST KNOW

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A2ITCYCV5Ik)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A2ITCYCV5Ik)

## Synopsis

One chord (Cmaj7) played 16 different ways — all 4 inversions of drop-2 voicings on A-string and D-string bass, plus all 4 inversions of drop-3 voicings on E-string and A-string bass. A complete, concrete catalog of the inversion/drop system already covered conceptually elsewhere in the vault.

## Techniques

- **The piano-voicing problem:** stacking a 4-note chord in strict linear order (1-3-5-7, 3-5-7-1, etc. — how a pianist would naturally voice it) is mostly unplayable as-is on guitar; drop 2 (move the 2nd-highest note down an octave) is the standard fix.
- **Drop 2, A-string and D-string bass, all 4 inversions each** — 8 shapes, same intervallic structures (1-5-7-3 / 3-7-1-5 / 5-1-3-7 / 7-3-1-5) refretted across two root strings for full fretboard coverage.
- **Drop 3, E-string and A-string bass, all 4 inversions each** — 8 shapes, structurally different from drop 2 at every inversion (1-7-3-5 / 3-1-5-7 / 5-3-7-1 / 7-5-1-3).
- **Bass note ≠ root**, explicitly flagged: the lowest sounding note of an inversion is whichever chord tone that inversion starts on, not necessarily the chord's true root.

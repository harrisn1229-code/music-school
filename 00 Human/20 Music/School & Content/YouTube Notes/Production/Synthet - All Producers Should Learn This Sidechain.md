# Synthet - All Producers Should Learn This Sidechain

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pDmLr-uHP40)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pDmLr-uHP40)

## Synopsis

A sidechain-ducking primer covering the standard signal-flow (kick triggers a limiter/compressor on another channel, dragged and thresholded until the duck is visible), a reminder that sidechaining works with *any* source triggering *any* target (not just kick-into-bass), a simpler manual alternative (drawing the volume-curve dip by hand instead of a live sidechain plugin), and a couple of creative uses beyond the standard pump: ducking a reverb tail after it's added for rhythmic clarity, and ducking chopped-up drum loops to "get anything flowing."

## Techniques

- **Standard sidechain routing** — connect the kick's signal to trigger a limiter/compressor on the target channel (a low-cut synth in the demo), drag the sidechain input level up, and adjust the compressor's threshold/ratio until the visible gain-reduction curve matches the intended duck depth. Works with any source triggering any target — a vocal was demoed ducking a background pad, not just kick→bass/synth. → [[Sidechaining]]
- **Manual volume-curve automation as a simpler alternative** — instead of a live sidechain compressor, just draw the volume-automation dip by hand; also called out as a good way to *understand* how a curve's shape affects the perceived pump, since you're drawing it directly rather than reading a compressor's response.
- **Duck a reverb tail after adding it, for creative effect** — add a large reverb, then sidechain-duck *that* reverb channel to the kick — keeps the wash from smearing over the transient of each hit.
- **Duck chopped drum loops to "get anything flowing"** — sidechaining a chopped-up drum loop to a kick pattern was cited as a reliable way to make otherwise-static loop chops feel rhythmically connected to the beat.

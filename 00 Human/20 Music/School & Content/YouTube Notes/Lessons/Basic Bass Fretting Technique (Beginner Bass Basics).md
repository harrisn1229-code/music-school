## What's taught

- **Why the left hand matters.** Without it, bass only has 4 notes (the open strings E-A-D-G); fretting unlocks roughly 80–100 notes depending on how many frets the bass has.
- **Use all four fingers — index, middle, ring, and pinky.** The pinky will feel weak at first but becomes just as usable as any other finger with time; nothing here is optional or index/middle/ring-only.
- **Thumb placement:** relaxed, resting on the **middle of the back of the neck** — not up near the top edge, not floating off in space. **Touching the neck but not pressing into it** — contact without grip, flowing around the neck rather than anchored.
- **Finger angle:** fingers **perpendicular to the strings** (equivalently, parallel to the fret wires), not slanted. Some slant is unavoidable and fine farther from the body, but the aim is to stay relatively perpendicular throughout — it's "important for a number of reasons," including (shown later) what keeps the pinky reachable.
- **Fret placement:** aim for the **middle-to-end of the fret**, close to the fret wire — never past it, or the next note sounds instead. **Why:** the closer to the wire, the stronger the angle of contact, which is what produces a clean, non-buzzing note.
- **Overall relaxed hand shape:** mimic the hand's natural resting position — arms hanging, loose shoulder, loose elbow, relaxed wrist, gently curled fingers. That curled shape is *more* relaxed (takes less tension to hold) than a flattened or backward-angled hand, a common beginner over-correction. Avoid backward angles and unneeded straightness — wasted effort.
- **No palm contact with the neck — especially on the index-finger side.** Visible negative space should exist between the palm and the neck; only fretting fingertips and the thumb should touch. **Causal chain given directly:** palm-on-neck forces the fingers into backward angles, twists them toward the body, and specifically makes the **pinky** hard to use because the twist pulls it away from the neck. Avoiding palm contact is what lets perpendicular fingers, all-four-finger use, and the relaxed forward curl all happen *simultaneously*.
- **Don't grip with the thumb** — paired with the no-palm-contact rule as the two named sources of unwanted friction that get in the way of moving fluidly up and down the neck.
- **Direction terminology — pitch-based, not gravity-based.** Strings rank low→high in pitch as **E < A < D < G**; "up a string" means moving toward a *higher-pitched* string (E→A), even though that's a physical move in the opposite direction from what "up" suggests in normal orientation. Same logic for frets: **up a fret / up the neck** = toward higher-numbered frets, toward the body, higher pitch; **down a fret / down the neck** = toward the headstock, lower pitch. Both conventions are internally consistent because both are always about the direction of pitch, never gravity or hand position.
- **Practice groove (all four fundamentals in musical context):** E string, index=9th fret, middle=10th, ring=11th, pinky=12th — then the identical index-middle-ring-pinky/9-10-11-12 shape repeated on the A string. That 8-note phrase is the whole line, repeating.
  - **Rhythm:** half notes — pluck on beats 1 and 3 only, each note held two full counts.
  - **Right hand:** index-only plucking is an acceptable fallback at this slow tempo; the target to build toward is strict index-middle **alternation** (a clean even split, since there are exactly 4 notes per string).
  - **Progression:** rehearsed a cappella, then against a backing track at **slow → medium → full** tempo, each pass re-cuing the same checklist (perpendicular fingers, no palm contact, relaxed thumb, gentle curl).
  - **Advanced aside (explicitly optional for beginners):** keeping fretting fingers *down* on their frets as later fingers land, rather than lifting each one before the next press, is named as an efficiency technique for later — not something to force as a beginner.
- **Closing framing:** left-hand technique has more depth than fits in one video ("a lot of stuff we didn't even get into today") — presented as an ongoing refinement, not a checklist to master once.

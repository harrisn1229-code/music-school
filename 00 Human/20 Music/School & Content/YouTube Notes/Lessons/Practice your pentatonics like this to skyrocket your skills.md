# Practice your pentatonics like this to skyrocket your skills

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uEYn-ntPrhU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uEYn-ntPrhU)

## Synopsis

A single relative-minor-pentatonic trick (credited to fellow teacher Daniel Seriff), applied over major/dominant/blues progressions: slide a minor pentatonic shape down 3 frets to get its relative major pentatonic, using the exact same fingering. Because both scales share identical pitch content, licks transfer directly between the two shapes, effectively doubling usable vocabulary from material already known.

## Techniques

- **The core move: slide a known minor pentatonic shape down 3 frets over a major/dominant progression to access the relative major pentonic with zero new fingerings to learn.** Worked example over an A blues backing track: A minor pentatonic (already known) slid down 3 frets lands on F♯ minor pentatonic — which shares an identical note set with A major pentatonic (F♯ is A major's relative minor), so playing "F♯ minor pentatonic shape" in that context sounds like A major pentatonic. **Scope restriction, stated explicitly: this works over major and especially dominant/blues progressions — it does not work over minor progressions.**
- **Because the two shapes are pitch-identical, the same lick can be played verbatim in both positions** — useful specifically for call-and-response phrasing (a short, simple motif stated in one shape, then immediately answered in the other).
- **Extends cleanly to the blues scale**: add the blue note (♭5) to both pentatonic shapes the same way you would to a single one, and the same relative-shift trick still applies — the added color note transfers along with the rest of the scale.
- **Extends cleanly to diagonal pentatonic shapes**: build a diagonal pentatonic line (a short 2-3-note cell repeated up successive octaves, ascending then descending) in the minor shape as usual, then copy the identical shape verbatim into the relative-shifted position (down 3 frets) to get the major-sounding version — same diagonal-shape mechanics, no new pattern to learn, plus the blue note can be layered onto the diagonal line the same way.

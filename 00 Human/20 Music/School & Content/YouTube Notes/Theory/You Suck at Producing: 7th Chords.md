## What's taught
A short, early-channel intro to the 7th chord: builds a C major triad (1-3-5), then adds the 7th scale degree on top to get Cmaj7, and demonstrates that transposing the exact same shape (same intervals, different root) preserves the chord's quality — moving the Cmaj7 voicing down 5 steps gives a Gmaj7 with the identical "maj7 feel," because it's the interval pattern, not the specific root, that defines the chord's character.

- **Build a maj7 chord by stacking the 1st, 3rd, 5th, and 7th scale degrees** — demoed on C major (C E G B = Cmaj7). → [[Seventh Chords]]
- **The same voicing shape, transposed, keeps the same chord quality** — duplicating a Cmaj7 clip and pitching every note in it down 5 half-steps gives a Gmaj7 that still reads as a maj7 chord, because the intervals between the notes (not the specific pitches) are what define "maj7-ness"; a fast way to write a second chord in a progression without recalculating scale degrees from scratch. → [[Chords]], [[Chord Progressions]]

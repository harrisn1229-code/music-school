# Behind the Track｜'12 to 12' by sombr

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TAdGk0Gzctc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TAdGk0Gzctc)

## Synopsis

Producer Tony Berg (Sound City) and artist sombr (Shane) discuss "12 to 12" and their broader working process: a young artist's fast, lo-fi home-produced ideas brought into a full studio and "expanded" rather than replaced, plus a same-week write-to-release workflow deliberately built against the industry's typical 6-month lead time.

## What's taught

- **Preserve a deliberately lo-fi/imperfect home recording rather than re-recording it "properly," when the imperfection carries the feeling.** The keyboard part central to the track was recorded on purpose with "the cheapest, shittiest piano sound" through an $800 mic — the producer's instinct was to re-record it cleaner once brought into the studio; the artist refused, insisting the raw, degraded character *was* the nostalgia the part was going for, not a flaw to fix. Framed as a real creative disagreement, resolved by trusting the artist's original instinct on his own material — "nine times out of ten, they're excellent... one time out of ten, they suck, and that's where we have our best time" (i.e. push back on the other 10%, not on everything).
- **A muted-string guitar part deliberately voiced/recorded to read as a different instrument (marimba) rather than as guitar.** Called out by the producer as "the most interesting thing on the whole record" — a texture trick: choose an unconventional playing technique/recording approach on a familiar instrument specifically to disguise its source and create a distinct, hard-to-place timbre.
- **Bongos as a consistent signature layer across an artist's whole discography, not a one-off percussion choice.** A dedicated percussionist adds bongos to the large majority of tracks worked on with this artist — described as "an integral part of what we do," i.e. a deliberate sonic-identity device (a recurring texture element that makes the artist's tracks recognizable) rather than a per-song creative decision.
- **Production goal: make the track identifiable before the vocal even starts.** Stated explicitly as an ambition — build a track's own sonic signature strong enough that a listener could identify the artist from the instrumental alone, before any singing begins.
- **A same-week write-to-release workflow, deliberately opposed to a typical 6+ month industry lead time.** Write and partially produce a demo alone, bring it to the studio to "make it into a smash," release within days — reasoning given: a 6-7 month gap between finishing a song and releasing it makes the material feel stale by release time; a record that's been out only a week is more exciting to tour behind.
- **"Loud" on a meter doesn't mean "as loud as it can go."** When a section needed to feel more powerful/impactful, the mix was pushed louder even though the meter already read high — a reminder that perceived loudness/impact and a meter reading aren't the same target.

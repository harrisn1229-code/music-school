# Kenny Beats Reacts: "My Song Only Has 100 Plays" (Can You Handle The Truth?) [Amazing Advice]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cj1dyKXLmko)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cj1dyKXLmko)

## Synopsis

A response to a producer asking whether to pull a low-stream first release. Kenny's position: every first release has low numbers, there's no way to skip that stage, and comparing yourself to an already-famous/label-backed artist's numbers is comparing against a distorted baseline. The more interesting idea underneath the reassurance: reframe any engagement — even criticism — as evidence your name is spreading, and protect your own relationship with the music from both the praise and the hate.

## Techniques

- **Don't hide or delay a low-stream first release — every artist's first release starts near zero, there's no way to skip that stage.** The instinct to pull a track because it "didn't get playlisted" treats a normal starting point as a mistake to hide rather than an unavoidable first step everyone goes through.
- **Reframe any stream count as "N people who might tell their friends" — the number matters less than whether it's moving.** 100 streams means 100 people who were exposed to the music and might talk about it, positively or negatively — both directions count, because either way your name is spreading. Cites Lil Nas X's "Old Town Road" as a worked example: a controversy (a Black, gay artist crossing into country) generated conversation regardless of whether that conversation was supportive, and the resulting attention compounded — a country-star co-sign, then escalating exposure — into one of the biggest songs of its era. Attention, not initial sentiment, is the thing that compounds.
- **Don't compare your starting numbers against an artist whose "first" release had a major label/pre-existing platform behind it.** Explicit caution: an artist whose breakout track had significant institutional backing (his example: Olivia Rodrigo, boosted by a pre-existing Disney Channel narrative plus label push) isn't a comparable baseline for an independent artist's actual first release — 100 plays with zero prior audience is a genuinely different starting line, not a failure by comparison.
- **Protect your own relationship with the music from both praise and criticism — "let it matter to me, don't let it matter to you."** His personal practice: some finished tracks matter to him regardless of whether they're ever released or how the world receives them, because his own confidence in the work isn't outsourced to audience reaction. Framed as a skill to build early, before public perception (accurate or not, generous or not) starts shaping how a producer feels about their own output.

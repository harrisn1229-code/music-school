# Electro Funk-Hop

**What makes it the genre:** a funk rhythmic backbone (syncopated bass, tight drum programming) fused with 8-bit/chiptune sound sources and a bright, "video-gamey" character — cool and groovy but energetic and slightly retro-digital rather than warm/vintage like [[Neo-Soul - Lo-fi]]. Composition carries almost the whole identity; the genre tolerates (even rewards) a near-bare mixing chain because the sound *design* is already doing the work.

## The pillars

### 1. Harmony as harmonized monophony, not block chords
Think orchestrally: harmonize 2–3 monophonic synth voices (each a slightly different tone, panned across the stereo field) instead of defaulting to one instrument playing a stacked chord voicing. Reads as more "composed," closer to a small horn/string section than a keyboard part. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Omnisphere]]

### 2. Lydian-forward, mode-relative writing
Lydian is the genre's default bright/floating color for intros and hooks; funk grooves lean Dorian. The two are relative modes (same 7 notes, different tonal center) — modulate between an E Lydian intro and a C♯ Dorian main groove by simply re-anchoring the bass/drone a third below, no new pitch material required. A i–IV (minor-to-major-and-back) funk cadence sits naturally inside the Dorian section. → [[Diatonic Modes]]

### 3. 8-bit/chiptune sound sources for the "video-gamey" layer
A dedicated 8-bit patch bank (square/triangle leads, arpeggiated chip harmonies) sits alongside "real"/sampled instrument sources in the same track — the genre fusion IS mixing chiptune timbre with funk/fusion writing, not a pure retro pastiche. Downsample/bitcrush modulated in real time (automate the crush amount + dry/wet together) keeps the crunch "clean enough to balance" rather than static. → [[Serum 2]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]

### 4. Bass does double duty as rhythm instrument
Short, muted, octave-jumping bass notes function almost as a percussion part (emulating a real bassist's palm-muted pop technique), and a polyphonic bass patch can carry its own harmony line above the root in contrary motion — bass is a melodic AND rhythmic voice here, not just low-end support. A bass fill (pulling the part out of its normal register for a run) is a legitimate substitute for a drum fill at a transition — the sub disappearing briefly reads as its own kind of release. → [[Bassline Writing]]

### 5. One deliberate non-diatonic "magic" moment
A single borrowed/non-diatonic chord (e.g. a major triad dropped into an otherwise-minor progression, voiced rootless) works as a genre-appropriate "wow" moment precisely because the rest of the progression stays diatonic — used sparingly, once per section, not as a recurring device. → [[Borrowed Chords|Borrowed Chords]]

### 6. Minimal-processing mixing
The genre's clean, punchy character comes from sound *choice*, not heavy processing — buses get light EQ/filtering, a touch of compression where volume is uneven, and the master gets a simple compressor → saturator → EQ → limiter chain. Resist polishing a sound that isn't working; swap it instead.

## Arrangement

| Section | Bars | Slots filled | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 8–16 | **Lydian** — harmonized synth voices · BED, no drums | 3 |
| Main groove | 16 | **Dorian** — BASS · MIDS · LEAD · TRANSIENT, bass carrying rhythm | 5 |
| Break | 8 | bass + drums only — the funk stripped bare | 2 |
| Hook | 16 | back to Lydian brightness over the groove, chiptune layer on top | 6 |
| Out | 8 | the harmonized voices alone, as they came in | 3 |

**Entrances & exits**
- **The mode change is the section change.** Re-anchor the bass a third below and an E Lydian intro becomes a C♯ Dorian groove — same seven notes, no new pitch material (pillar 2). Nothing else has to move.
- The bass is a rhythm instrument (pillar 4), so pulling the drums doesn't stop the groove. That's what makes the bass-and-drums break work.
- The chiptune layer is a *seasoning* entrance — it arrives at the hook and leaves. Running it the whole song spends the identity in the first sixteen bars.
- The one non-diatonic "magic" chord (pillar 5) lands once per section, not on a loop.

**The contrast move — modulate by re-anchoring.** Cheapest section change in any genre on this shelf: move the bass note, keep every other part identical, and the whole harmony re-reads. Bright↔funky with no rewriting.

**Density rule: 3–6.** Composition carries the identity, so the arrangement can stay moderate and the mixing chain near-bare (pillar 6). Density is not the tool here — modal colour is.

## Session recipe
Pick a Lydian key center for the intro/hook, its relative Dorian for the main groove · harmonize 2–3 monophonic synth voices instead of block chords · build the bass as a rhythmic/percussive part first, melodic second · layer one 8-bit/chiptune source against the "real" instrument palette · save ONE non-diatonic chord for a single high-impact surprise · keep the mix bus dead simple.

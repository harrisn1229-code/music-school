# Tape Echo on D'Angelo's Drums - Russell Elevado

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DB6kGF13hxA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DB6kGF13hxA)

## Synopsis

A short Mix with the Masters session walkthrough with Russell Elevado (D'Angelo's longtime engineer — *Voodoo*, *Black Messiah*) adding a hardware tape echo to a D'Angelo drum bus. The core move is a reamp send/return loop: the compressed drum bus feeds a Radial X-Amp (turns a line-level signal back into a speaker-level/DI-friendly signal), out to a DI, into a mic preamp (an API 512), and from there into a hardware tape echo unit — so the drum bus is physically leaving the box, running through the outboard delay, and coming back. Elevado leans into the tape unit's inherent randomness rather than dialing it to an exact value: he gets roughly close to a quarter-note repeat by ear, then keeps tweaking from there, treating the "wrong" echoes the unit throws up as a source of new ideas rather than a problem to correct. Once he lands on something he likes, he prints it (records it as audio) rather than running the hardware live for the whole song, both to free up the hardware and to lock in a specific happy accident. He also mentions a plate reverb recorded live during the original tracking session that's used as-is in the mix rather than re-created later, and closes by applying the same reamp/tape-echo treatment to a guitar overdub (Sharky Sharky's part), which D'Angelo calls "the drippings" — confirming the same signal path is a repeatable move across different sources in a session, not a drum-only trick.

## Techniques

- **Reamp send/return signal path for feeding a mix bus into hardware outboard: bus → Radial X-Amp (reamp box) → DI → mic preamp (API 512) → hardware effect → back to the board.** The reamp box is what makes a line-level bus signal safe/appropriate to feed back into a DI input; without it you can't easily round-trip a mixed signal out to guitar-pedal/tape-style hardware and back. → [[Delay]] (effect), [[EchoBoy]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Set a tape echo's repeat time loosely to a rhythmic reference (here, roughly the quarter note) by ear, then keep adjusting from there rather than trusting an exact readout — the unit's own drift/randomness is treated as a feature.** "It's a bit random, so sometimes you might come across an echo that's just kind of random and it gives you an idea for a different sort of flavor than you might have intended." Owned lane: [[EchoBoy]]'s tape Echo Type + Wow & Flutter reproduces this same "not quite locked" character deliberately rather than by accident. → [[EchoBoy]], [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[Depth and Space]]
- **A subtle, quarter-note-ish tape echo left running under the whole drum bus reads as "dirty quality" and "air" rather than an audible discrete repeat — used specifically so it's not consciously heard as an echo, just as part of the drum sound.** Confirms the "subtle enough to disappear into the source" school of tape-delay use already logged on [[EchoBoy]] (Greg Wells' vocal delay entries) — here applied to a full drum bus instead of a single vocal. → [[EchoBoy]], [[Delay]] (effect)
- **Print the reamped hardware pass once you land on something you like, rather than leaving the hardware running live for the whole song.** Frees the outboard tape unit for other uses and locks in a specific happy accident before it can drift further. Same discipline applies to a live plate reverb recorded during the original tracking session and kept as-is in the mix, rather than rebuilt later with a plugin — "commit at the source" extended from tracking through to outboard-effect passes. → [[Committing and Printing]]
- **The same reamp/tape-echo treatment, once dialed in on drums, gets reapplied to a guitar overdub in the same session — D'Angelo's own name for the effect is "the drippings."** Confirms this is a repeatable per-session move, not a drum-specific trick — worth reaching for on any track that wants the same "worn" character. → [[EchoBoy]], [[TEXTURE Slot]]

# Pro-Q 4

![[Images/Pro-Q 4.png]]

**Category:** EQ
**Vendor:** FabFilter

## What it is

The latest version of FabFilter's Pro-Q EQ, adding new dynamic EQ behavior, spectral grab tools, and workflow refinements over Pro-Q 3.

## What it's used for

Same corrective and tonal EQ role as Pro-Q 3, with newer dynamic-EQ and spectral-editing tools.

## FX chain notes

Same placement as Pro-Q 3 — pre- and post-compression EQ stages, or as a dynamic EQ substitute for narrow problem frequencies.

## Techniques

- **Cede low-end territory to the 808 on a competing percussion/loop element, then boost upper-high-mids for cut-through — a direct frequency-pocket assignment between two low-end sources rather than trying to EQ both to coexist untouched.** Jaycen Joshua, on a tambora/percussion loop sharing space with an 808: "you're taking a little bit of the low end off because obviously the 808 is taking, or occupying, all of that. And you're adding a little bit of what you need — that upper high-mid that just lets that cut through." Used as "Pro-Q 2" in the source; positioned after a CLA-76-style compressor and before a Lo-Fi/bitcrush stage in the loop's chain. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[SUB Slot]], [[BASS Slot]], [[UAD UA 1176LN]], [[Decimort 2]] — Mix with the Masters (Jaycen Joshua), [[Jaycen Joshua on creative drum mixing]]
- **Two separate roles in the same real vocal chain: an early basic-filtering pass, and a small ~2–2.5 dB broad boost pass positioned right after the compressor stage.** "My Pro-Q is doing like, basic filtering, and then the SSL is doing broad EQ... after all that processing, you might want to do a little bit more EQ and the Pro-Q 2 does a very good job of this. At this point, I am not doing anything more than, yeah, 2 dB here... 2.5, it looks like." Also the first stop for hunting a bright pop vocal's specific "pop-out" presence resonance, with a concrete starting range: **2.5–4.5 kHz** (narrowed further to "three and a half to four and a half" once found by ear) — "when her voice hits certain frequencies, they pop out a lot... I like to find the frequencies that bother me on vocals... I pull those out." A default/aggressive filter+EQ preset ("ZZZZ" — his own naming habit for a default-preset slot) is also the front stage of a parallel "girth" aux trick, duplicated off the main vocal and blended back at ~−33 dB. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[LEAD Slot]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]] — Mix with the Masters (Josh Gudwin), [[Mixing Dua Lipa's vocals with Josh Gudwin]]
- **A concrete 3-move lead-vocal corrective recipe, used as "Q3" in the source: steep low roll-off, a ~1500 Hz boost, and a gentle top-end smoothing filter — Linear Phase only at the very final master pass.** Illangelo (The Weeknd): "rolling off the low end, quite high, possibly some might consider doing a slope 1500 hertz up, 4 dB... trying to get rid of that low and trying to boost it up high... using a filter on the top end here, just trying to smooth it a little bit." Run on **Linear Phase, Medium** setting only when finishing a record; **Natural Phase** for the other 99% of the working process — confirms this note's own phase-mode-discipline bullet below from a second, independent source. → [[The Weeknd Vocal Chain]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]] — Illangelo (Mix with the Masters), [[Producing The Weeknd's vocals with Illangelo]]
- **Dynamic EQ used to dip the vocal's ATTACKS specifically — an unusual target for a Dynamic band, which is normally aimed at sibilance or a static resonant frequency.** "Doing a bit of dynamic EQ with a Pro-Q 3 and I can show you where that's dipping out as well... it's just the attacks." Reads as a Dynamic band tuned so the trigger fires on transient/onset energy rather than a sustained problem frequency — softens the front edge of syllables dynamically instead of a static cut, which would dull the whole word rather than just its onset. On this plugin (current version of the Pro-Q 3 named on screen): a Dynamic band with a fast attack/short release on the detector, centered wherever the offending transient energy sits, threshold set to trigger only on the harder hits. *(qualitative — no exact frequency/threshold given)* → [[EQ]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[Tony Maserati Vocal Chain]] — Tony Maserati (Mix with the Masters), [[Vocal chain with Tony Maserati]]
- **Owned best-match for a "Trackspacer"-style 32-band dynamic-EQ sidechain duck between two co-lead vocalists sharing register — build multiple Dynamic bands across the collision zone, sidechain each to the OTHER vocalist, restrict Stereo Placement to Mid only.** Source technique: on a combined vocal Group carrying two vocalists (James Blake + a second singer, "Slowthai"), a 32-band dynamic processor sidechained to the second singer's vocal ducks the **1–6 kHz** zone specifically when the two overlap, restricted to **M/S Mid-channel only** so the dry vocal ducks but a printed reverb tail riding on the same Group doesn't. This plugin doesn't have 32 fixed bins, but the mechanism is directly buildable: place several **Dynamic** bands spanning the collision range, point each band's **External Sidechain** at the other vocalist, and set **Stereo Placement** to **Mid** per band — the same per-band Dynamic EQ + external sidechain + Mid/Side combination already documented above for ducking an instrumental off a vocal sidechain, applied here vocalist-to-vocalist instead of vocal-to-instrumental. [[Soothe2]]'s external-sidechain mode is a rougher, broadband-only fallback for the same masking-avoidance job when per-band control isn't needed. → [[Sidechaining]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Soothe2]], [[James Blake Vocal Chain]] — Mix with the Masters, [[MSM Mixing James Blake's Vocals]]
- **Best-match for "basic SSL EQ" (unowned console-style channel EQ): a subtractive HPF + one narrow-Q resonance notch, both found and applied by ear rather than by number.** Source description is qualitative only — "filtering in the low end... pulling out a resonance, there's something ugly there I don't like" — no exact frequencies given. On this plugin: HPF in the **~80–120 Hz** range *(general starting point)* on the low end, then the same hover-freeze / narrow-Q sweep-and-notch method documented elsewhere on this note to hunt the offending resonance. Positioned right after a light drive/character stage and before [[Soothe2]] in the source chain. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[James Blake Vocal Chain]] — Mix with the Masters, [[MSM Mixing James Blake's Vocals]]
- **A proximity-effect correction pass run offline (AudioSuite/batch) rather than as a realtime insert, keyed to fire only on the specific words/phrases where mic-distance buildup is audible.** "It's just the proximity stuff again — whenever that triggers... it's just getting compressed... you can do it through AudioSuite if you want." Reads as a Dynamic EQ band (or dynamics-processor band) targeting inconsistent low-mid buildup from the singer's distance changing between phrases, applied surgically rather than across the whole take — only necessary here because the vocal arrived wet/pre-processed (a dry take would get this handled with an ordinary compressor instead). → [[Vocal Mic Technique]], [[Compression]], [[James Blake Vocal Chain]] — Mix with the Masters, [[MSM Mixing James Blake's Vocals]]
- **Matched cut-center/boost-sides at the SAME frequency — removes a frequency from the vocal's center pocket without losing the energy it carried, by putting it right back on the outside.** General mastering-EQ mechanism, stated then demoed on a drum bus: "if you have a synth and you want to do anything to the outside only and don't want to affect the vocal… deduct here, then bring it back where you deducted and boost it on the outside." Cut a frequency in the **mid** image (Stereo Placement = Mid), boost that exact same frequency in the **side** image (Stereo Placement = Side) — the energy isn't lost, just relocated away from the vocal's mono pocket. Demoed at **~112 Hz**, run extremely lightly ("kissed it, barely feel it"). Distinct from this plugin's other mid/side recipes above/below — those pair independent moves per image or a boost/attenuate pair at different frequencies; this one matches the exact same frequency on both sides of the cut/boost. → [[EQ]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters), [Jaycen Joshua's drum bus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LY-vU5MwBvs)
- **Dip the CENTER (mid) image to carve a literal pocket for the vocal, on a bus that isn't the vocal itself — used here on a drum bus, framed as a mastering technique newly available to mixers.** "You can EQ the center alone and find pockets for other instruments… nine times out of ten I'm using it to sit a place for my vocal." A drum bus's mid image (where the drums mostly live, since the low end is centered) gets dipped where it was carrying "a little too much weight," opening room for the vocal to occupy that same center space. Stereo Placement = Mid, broad dip, no exact Hz given. *(general starting point — no frequency stated for this specific dip)* → [[EQ]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters), [Jaycen Joshua's drum bus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LY-vU5MwBvs)
- **A "dipper" default: cut a broad problem zone before boosting anything, on a full drum bus.** "I'm a dipper if you want to call it — I love taking away before I add." Cut **~1100 Hz and below, ~2.5 dB**, on a drum bus reading "a little heavy" (that low-mid weight was over-driving upstream saturation/harmonic stages); once pulled back it read clearer, more open, more separated. A small presence-region boost follows, only after the cut restored headroom. → [[EQ]], [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters), [Jaycen Joshua's drum bus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LY-vU5MwBvs)
- **Aggressive, repeated narrow-Q notching by ear to dig out harsh resonances, encouraged explicitly as a "don't be afraid" move for less-experienced mixers.** On a rock vocal: "some harsh frequencies that I heard, that I notched out with really tight Q's, pretty tight Q's... don't be afraid, when you're mixing digitally, to go in there and find those frequencies that are harsh and pierce into your ear, find them, and pull them out... I'm not afraid to notch multiple times if I have to, whatever it takes." One problem zone is named but garbled by auto-captions — treat as roughly **~2.2–2.8 kHz** rather than exact. Framed as much a loud-playback-safety move as a tonal one — a mix meant to play loud can't have a frequency that spikes and "kills" the ear — and praised for staying low-artifact even under several repeated passes on the same vocal. → [[EQ]] (effect), [[De-essing]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[1 Main Vocal Track|Main Vocal Track]] — Mix with the Masters (Stuart White), [[Mixing vocals in the box with Stuart White]]
- **A fixed 3-band "just add high-end" recipe, run identically on every singer — a best-match stand-in for a named Waves Renaissance EQ move (unowned).** The source's actual recipe: push up "the red" (estimated ~10 kHz, a high shelf/band for air), bring "the purple" (mid) up a little, bring "the blue" up just a little too — same three moves every time, "all I need is just high-end." Framed explicitly as a non-technical, feel-first EQ pass rather than a diagnostic one. On this plugin: a high shelf ~8–10 kHz +2–3 dB (air), a broad mid bell ~2–4 kHz +1 dB, and a third gentle band to taste *(general starting point — the source gives no exact gain/Q, just "a little")*. → [[EQ]] (effect), [[RVox]], [[Compression Staging]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]] — Kuk Harrell, [[Radio-ready vocal effects with Kuk Harrell]]
- **A default four-band drum layout to drop on every kit lane, then move by ear.** Per lane: **kick** — HPF 40–60 Hz, low shelf +80–100 Hz (thump), peak 2–4 kHz (snap), a band at 8–10 kHz for click (see caveat below); **snare** — HPF 100–200 Hz ("more if necessary"), low shelf 200–300 Hz (umph), peak 5–7 kHz (crisp); **hats** — HPF 200–300 Hz or higher, high shelf 7–10 kHz+ (sizzle); **toms** — HPF 60–200 Hz *per drum* (floor low, rack high), cut 300–800 Hz (boxiness), boost 100–300 Hz (power) and 5–10 kHz (stick punch). For the tom boxiness cut, use this plugin's narrow-Q **sweep-to-find-the-honk** method — a +8–10 dB narrow band swept through 300–800 Hz until the box rings, then flipped to a cut of 3–5 dB *(general starting point — the source gives ranges only, no Q/gain/slope anywhere)*. ⚠️ The source labels the kick's 8–10 kHz move a **notch** while giving "for click" as the reason — contradictory as printed; read it as a boost for click, or a genuine narrow notch for a clicky resonance. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Kick Design]], [[Snare Design]], [[1 Drums Kick Track]], [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track]], [[1 Drums Hat Track]] — Whipped Cream Sounds, [[Drum Mixing EQ Guide - Enrichment|Drum Mixing EQ Guide]]

- **Independent-per-image mid/side EQ recipe — mono the side lows, then a separate carve on each image rather than a mirrored boost/cut pair.** Opening mastering-EQ move: attenuate the **side** image's low frequencies to mono the low end (⑦ Stereo Placement = Side, HPF or low shelf). On that same side image, boost a little **~400 Hz** for synth depth and dip **~2 kHz** to let the vocal cut through. On the **mid** image, attenuate **~250 Hz** to de-mask the vocal and boost **~2 kHz** for vocal presence. Distinct from this plugin's other mid/side recipes below (those pair a boost with a matching attenuation at the same frequency on the opposite image) — here each image gets its own independent set of moves. → [[EQ]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Master Chain]] — Sage Audio, [Creating a Hip Hop Mastering Chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4JXwuR6AUok)
- **Add mid-range power/impact with a dynamic band on the mid frequencies — a genuinely different result on the mid image vs. the side image.** Since the mid-frequency range holds most of a mix's instrumentation, boosting it and making that boost dynamic adds impact to a master overall. Run on the **mid** image, it mainly makes drums and vocal read more impressive/dynamic; run on the **side** image, it instead makes guitar body and temporal processing (reverb) more dynamic — worth experimenting with both to find the balance between them rather than defaulting to one. → [[EQ]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Master Chain]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)
- **Dynamically widen the stereo image with an expanding shelf on the side image around 7 kHz and up.** Shelf filter on the **side image**, placed around **7 kHz+**, range about **2 dB**, threshold lowered (or automatic) until the band audibly triggers and expansion occurs. Because it only fires when triggered, the widening isn't constant — it tracks the presence of high-frequency instrumentation moment to moment, so the width reads as a musical, dynamic effect rather than a flat always-on setting. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Master Chain]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)
- **Bump vocal presence with a dynamic expansion bell around 2 kHz on the mid or stereo channel — additive, not the masking-reduction move below.** Bell filter centered **~2 kHz** on the **mid or full-stereo channel**, set Dynamic, expansion rather than attenuation. Triggers whenever the vocal is actually sung, making it louder and helping it cut through the mix in that moment specifically rather than a static boost. → [[Masking and Carving]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)
- **Bump the kick with a dynamic expansion band centered exactly on its most prominent frequency, +3 dB.** Read the spectrum to find the kick's most prominent frequency, place a bell filter's center frequency directly over it, set the band Dynamic, and increase the range **above** the filter by about **3 dB** — an expansion move, not attenuation — with the threshold lowered manually or via the automatic threshold feature. Belongs downstream of compression/saturation — see this plugin's ordering-rule bullet below. → [[Kick Design]], [[Master Chain]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)
- **Additive dynamic EQ belongs AFTER compression and saturation in the chain, and stays small — the sequencing reason, not just a preference.** Running additive dynamic EQ before the compressor/saturator amplifies the signal ahead of those stages, provoking more aggressive compression than intended (a hotter signal trips the threshold harder/more often). Placing it after avoids the feedback loop; keep the moves themselves small too, since additive dynamic EQ increases a master's dynamic range regardless of which specific technique is used. → [[EQ]], [[Master Chain]], [[Compression Staging]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)
- **Tame hi-hats with a dynamic high shelf around 8 kHz and the fastest attack/release the EQ allows, range ≤2 dB.** Dynamic shelf filter starting **~8 kHz**; if the plugin offers it, pull attack/release down to roughly **2 ms** so the shelf snaps in and out fast enough to catch just the hat hits. *(Flagged in the source as a Pro-Q 3 limitation at the time — worth checking whether this plugin's newer dynamic-EQ behavior added faster attack/release, since it's the direct successor.)* Keep the range conservative — no more than **2 dB** — since high-frequency changes are easy to notice. → [[EQ]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)
- **"Augment" the vocal with a subtractive move: a dynamic bell on the SIDE image at 2 kHz reduces masking so the mono vocal reads louder without ever boosting it.** Bell filter on the **side image**, center **2 kHz**, Dynamic, range **2 dB**. Reducing the side image's content at the vocal's frequency reduces masking acting on that frequency, which increases the *perceived* amplitude of the mono-centered vocal — an additive-sounding result from a subtractive, side-only move; dynamic, so it triggers mainly while the vocal is actually singing. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Width and Stereo]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)
- **Tame low-mid muddiness with a dynamic band around 200 Hz, using the Isolate/solo tool to pin the exact culprit frequency by ear.** Band around **200 Hz**, set Dynamic, decide if the threshold needs attenuating. To find precisely where inside that bell's bandwidth the mud actually lives, use the **isolate feature** (solo just that band's frequency content) and listen for where the bass frequencies are most prominent — don't drag the band too far down the spectrum chasing it, or it starts attenuating content unrelated to the muddiness. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)
- **Tame sibilance by matching a dynamic band's timing to an analyzer's spikes, not by ear-sweeping alone.** Use a spectrum analyzer to see exactly where the signal spikes in the high-frequency range, and match that spike's timing against when an S is actually sung or spoken — confirming the spike is genuinely sibilance, not something else at the same frequency. Center a band on the most prominent part of the sibilance, set it Dynamic (automatic threshold, or set threshold/range manually) — cymbals and other high-frequency instrumentation, not sharing the sibilant timing, stay relatively unaffected even though they overlap the same frequency range. → [[EQ]], [[De-essing]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)
- **Advanced mid-side EQ: dynamically duck an instrumental's specific frequencies off an external vocal sidechain, with per-band mid/side placement.** Worked example, mixing a vocal against an instrumental: build **multiple bands centered on the exact frequencies that should dip whenever the vocal is present**, set each of those bands to **Dynamic**, then point each band's **external sidechain** input at the vocal — the band's threshold now measures the vocal's level instead of the instrumental's own, so the instrumental only ducks those specific frequencies while the vocal is actually singing. Taken further: assign **Stereo Placement per band** — e.g. one dynamic band ducking the **mid** image, another ducking the **side** image — combining the frequency-specific vocal-triggered duck with a stereo-imaging decision in the same move. A direct, more surgical alternative to the Soothe2-sidechain approach already documented on [[2 Inst-Synth Group]]'s sum-carve stage — same goal (clear the vocal's lane dynamically), but with explicit per-band frequency targeting and mid/side control instead of one broadband dynamic suppressor. → [[EQ]], [[2 Inst-Synth Group]], [[Sidechaining]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Width and Stereo]] — Sage Audio, [EQ Masterclass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPBnL6mx9c0)
- **A concrete natural-vs-digital EQ decision rule: phase mode + Q + slope, in one recipe each way.** For a natural, analog-emulating sound: **natural phase response**, **proportional Q enabled** (Q widens/narrows automatically with gain), and slopes capped at **12 dB/octave or less** — the lower the Q, the more natural it reads. For a digital/surgical sound: **low-latency linear phase**, plus **higher slopes and Q values** for pinpoint, aggressive-sounding cuts — best for finding and nailing a narrow problem frequency, not general tonal shaping. → [[EQ]] — Sage Audio, [EQ Masterclass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPBnL6mx9c0)
- **When linear phase actually earns its cost, versus zero-latency natural phase or plain low-latency linear phase as the personal default.** Reach for linear phase specifically to solve two problems: (1) equalizing a **parallel signal** whose original path isn't also filtered — low-latency linear phase avoids phase cancellation when the two paths recombine; (2) **high-pass filtering**, where it also helps. At high settings it introduces **pre-ringing** ahead of transients, though concerns about audibility are often overstated in practice. Default for everything else: **zero-latency natural phase** or **low-latency linear phase**, the latter chosen when accuracy matters more than avoiding any transient smear at all. → [[Parallel Processing]], [[Phase Alignment]] — Sage Audio, [EQ Masterclass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPBnL6mx9c0)
- **Dynamic EQ vs. multiband compression: this plugin's actual band-count ceiling as the concrete half of the comparison.** Pro-Q 3 (this plugin's immediate predecessor) tops out at **26 dynamic bands** with full per-band Q/slope shaping, versus [[Pro-MB]]'s 6 bands — but a Pro-Q dynamic band's dynamics section is thin, usually just a manual/automatic threshold, not the attack/release/lookahead/knee toolkit a dedicated multiband compressor offers. Reach for this plugin's Dynamic EQ when the job is pinpoint control over *which frequency* and *how narrowly*; reach for [[Pro-MB]] when the job is control over *how the dynamics themselves* behave. → [[Pro-MB]], [[EQ]], [[Multiband Compression]] — Sage Audio, [EQ Masterclass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPBnL6mx9c0)
- **Upward-EQ as an expander: a dynamic band set to EXPAND rather than attenuate when triggered, used to add punch to a kick and width to the highs.** Same per-band Dynamic EQ mechanism as any corrective dynamic-EQ move, run in the opposite direction: a mid-image bell over a kick's fundamental subtly expands whenever the kick hits (dynamic punch on the beat, mid-only so it doesn't touch the sides); a side-image high shelf subtly expands the highs (extra perceived width up top, side-only so it doesn't touch the kick's mono anchor). Net effect on a master: more dynamic in the lows, wider in the highs, using expansion instead of the usual attenuation. → [[EQ]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Master Chain]] — Sage Audio, [How to Master a Song Start to Finish](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aUygMNRx2yE)
- **A masking-driven dynamic EQ duck: one band's level triggers a DIFFERENT band's gain reduction, not itself.** Demoed as a 4-band mastering pass (source plugin unclear/garbled in captions, technique maps directly onto this plugin's per-band Dynamic EQ + external sidechain): a band around 2.5 kHz is configured purely as a trigger (it doesn't process anything itself), and a separate band around 250 Hz attenuates whenever that 2.5 kHz band crosses its own threshold — because 250 Hz commonly masks 2.5 kHz, ducking 250 Hz specifically when 2.5 kHz gets loud lets the higher content read through instead of getting buried. This plugin doesn't natively let one internal band drive a different band's dynamics the way the source plugin does — approximate it with **⑨ External Sidechain**, routing a second instance/bus isolating the 2.5 kHz content into the 250 Hz band's sidechain input. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Master Chain]] — Sage Audio, [How to Master a Song Start to Finish](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aUygMNRx2yE)
- **Dynamic EQ to attenuate out-of-key frequencies — a musicality move, not a corrective one.** In-key content reads relatively louder (and more musical) once out-of-key frequencies get gently ducked around it. Workflow: run the track through a free online tuner/key analyzer to find the key, look up which frequencies fall in vs. out of key, use **the piano roll** (snaps a band's center frequency exactly to a note — no manual Hz lookup needed), enable **Dynamic EQ** per band, target **0.5–1 dB of attenuation**, and keep **Q narrow** so the cut lands mostly on the out-of-key note rather than bleeding into neighboring in-key content. → [[EQ]], [[Master Chain]] — Sage Audio, [Mastering Compression Settings](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YUn31aE9X_8)
- **Two-pass mastering EQ: corrective before dynamics, tonal after — with a side-image-only HPF placement rule.** Pass 1 (corrective, before the RMS comp/upward processor): linear phase at a low-latency setting (minimal pre-ringing, less transient smear than true zero-latency), a **side-image-only** high-pass filter (18 or 24 dB/oct is safe at linear-phase low-latency) placed **between the source's fundamental range and its 2nd-order harmonic range** — this makes everything below the filter identical L/R without removing the fundamental itself — plus a small dip ~250 Hz (masking/mud) and a small dip in a resonant high-mid pocket (vocal nasal resonance and busy instruments). Pass 2 (tonal, after dynamics are settled): repeat the side-image HPF cleanup (upstream stages reintroduce side-image lows), dip low mids if still needed, boost lows + high mids, add a **side-image bell** in the mid-range for stereo impressiveness without unbalancing overall tone, and place the air band on the side (expand), the mid (focus), or both identically — a genuine creative choice, not a fixed rule. → [[Master Chain]], [[Width and Stereo]] — Sage Audio, [The Only Music Mastering Video You'll Ever Need](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M88T8jFL2uU)

Flip it when the last take lands — Kenny Beats, [Vocal Template](https://youtu.be/X_iDENABLmk) → [[Latency-Free Tracking]]

- **Dynamic EQ to tame vocal resonances** while boosting highs (notch a couple of mid resonances dynamically) — Disclosure, [Waterfall](https://youtu.be/sL5CG444qwQ)
- **Guy's go-to EQ** (Pro-Q 3 in the videos) — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8)
- **Dynamic-band Attack/Release as a creative expander/compressor on a percussive hit** — beyond corrective EQ, running a dynamic band as light expansion or compression and playing with its **Attack**/**Release** controls reshapes a drum one-shot's transient character (more boomy, more perky) — a distinct move from the resonance-hunting/corrective use above. → [[Snare Design]] — dnksaus, [craft UNIQUE snares](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WeHN9GPokMA)

## Used in my chains

**My EQ backbone — in ~36 chains.** The pattern is consistent: a **corrective** instance before compression (HPF + mud cut), a **tone** instance after compression/saturation (additive shaping), and a **band-limit** on support layers so they don't compete with the lead. Flip instances from Zero Latency (tracking) → **Natural Phase** once tracking's done:

- **Corrective (pre-comp)** — HPF 24 dB/oct @ 80–100 Hz, cut mud −2 to −4 dB @ 200–400 Hz, check 2–5 kHz harshness. E.g. [[1 Main Vocal Track|Main Vocal]] slot 2, [[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Bass DI]] slot 1, [[1 Drums Kick Track|Kick]]/[[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Snare]] shape.
- **Tone (post-comp/sat)** — small additive presence/air (dynamic mode so it de-esses as it lifts). E.g. [[1 Main Vocal Track|Main Vocal]] slot 6.
- **Band-limit on support layers** — hard HP/LP so doubles/harmonies/backups sit under the lead. E.g. [[1 Double Vocal Track|Double]], [[1 Harmony Vocal Track|Harmony]], [[1 Main Vocal High Track|High]], [[1 Backup Vocal Track|Backup]] slot 2.
- **Group/bus carve** — [[2 Main Vocal Group|Vocal Group]], [[2 Ensemble Stack Group|Ensemble Stack]] (composite carve), [[2 Guitar Group|Guitar]]/[[3 FX Bus|FX]] containment; **carve-vs-kick** on bass ([[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Bass DI]] slot 5, dynamic band), **carve-vs-vocal** on guitars.
- **On sends** — [[Saturation Send]] (shape the dirt: HP 200–300, LP 6–8 kHz), [[Parallel Comp Send]] (pre-EQ the "NY" part + post-tame).

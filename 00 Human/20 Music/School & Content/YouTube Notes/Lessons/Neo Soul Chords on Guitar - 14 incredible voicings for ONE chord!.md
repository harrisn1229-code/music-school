# Neo Soul Chords on Guitar - 14 incredible voicings for ONE chord!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dde0DF706DU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dde0DF706DU)

## Synopsis

A minor-chord sequel to an earlier video in the same series (7 voicings over E♭maj7, referenced directly but not yet a page in this vault). Jared plays through **14 distinct guitar voicings of a single chord — Cm7** — one at a time, naming every note in scale-degree terms as he goes, then plays the whole set through again as a small composed arrangement. The organizing idea, stated explicitly and repeatedly: only the **♭3 and ♭7** are non-negotiable — every other note (root, 5th) is fair game to swap out for a 9th or 11th when a voicing wants more color. Two voicings also carry **embellishments** (a slurred/trailed top note, a doubled-11-drop-♭3 hit) that Jared deliberately does *not* count toward the 14, on the grounds that they're ornaments layered on a fixed shape rather than new chord shapes — and he calls that slur/trail-on-top habit "a quintessential element of the neo-soul guitar sound." Jared's own working definition of "neo-soul," given directly in the video: rich, extended jazz chord voicings (the harmony) played in a groove/beat-based genre rather than swung jazz (lo-fi hip-hop, R&B), with those top-note ornaments as the genre-marking flavor on top. Ends with a plug for his "Any Jazz Chord" booklet (a shell-voicing-based method for accessing any jazz chord from as few as 8 shapes, two places on the neck) — noted here for context, not something to route anywhere since it's a paid resource outside the transcript's own content.

## What's taught

The complete 14-voicing breakdown (every note named per voicing, the root/5→9/11 substitution principle that generates most of the shapes, the two embellishment devices, and the closing bass-line-led final chord) is extracted in full on the new technique note **[[Neo-Soul Voicings for a Single Chord]]** rather than duplicated here — see that page for the voicing-by-voicing detail. Summary of the extraction:

- **Voicings 1–3** (0:47–2:47ish): a rootless Cm9 (5 on bottom), a favorite Cm9-with-♭3-on-top (♭7 on bottom, half-step top interval), and the classic root-position Cm7 shape (root-5-♭7-♭3). Jared moves the bass note between these three specifically to make the sequence sound melodic rather than static.
- **Voicing 3's embellishment** (~3:35–4:50): a whole-step-above-top-note slur/bar-lift ornament — explicitly *not* counted as a 4th voicing; this is where Jared states the "slurs or trails on top" neo-soul-flavor principle directly.
- **Voicings 4–5** (~5:00–5:45): reached by a Hendrix-style chromatic slide up the neck from voicing 3; a Cm7 inversion (♭3-♭7-root-5), then the same shape barred at fret 13 with the top 5 replaced by the 11 for a Cm11 — explicitly confirmed as two *separate* voicings, not variants.
- **Voicing 6 + its embellishment** (~6:00–7:00): the same Cm11 shape used in the companion E♭maj7 video, reinterpreted here as a Cm chord (root-11-♭7-♭3); its embellishment doubles the 11 and drops the ♭3 — again explicitly not counted as its own voicing, and called out as the quintessential neo-soul ornament sound.
- **Voicings 7–8** (~7:03–7:35): a standard root-position Cm7 with the low root moved to the top (♭7-♭3-5-root), then the same shape with that top root swapped for a 9 (rootless Cm9).
- **Voicing 9** (~7:44–8:20): an F7sus4-shaped grip reinterpreted as Cm11 (11 on bottom); moving the bottom note up a whole step turns it into the same shape as voicing 4/5's family (5 in place of 11).
- **Voicing 10** (~8:21): all four middle strings at fret 8 — another Cm11 (11-♭7-♭3-5).
- **Voicing 11** (~8:38–9:11): "one of my favorite minor-11 voicings, probably the most common" — root-♭7-♭3-11, with a whole step on top (the whole-step counterpart to voicing 2's half-step-on-top).
- **Voicing 12** (~9:11–9:34): a shape whose standard interpretation is E♭6/9, reinterpreted here as Cm11 (♭3-root-11-♭7).
- **Voicing 13** (~9:49–10:31): a shape whose standard interpretation is a major add9 chord (E♭maj(add9)), reinterpreted as Cm-with-11 (♭3-♭7-5-11) — checked explicitly against the ♭3/♭7 rule before being counted.
- **Voicing 14, the final chord** (~10:36–11:00): a bass line resolving ♭7→1 into a standard root-position Cm9 (root-♭3-♭7-9) — deliberately paired as the mirror of voicing 1 (root-on-bottom/no-5 vs. voicing 1's 5-on-bottom/rootless).
- **Recap and reframing** (~11:44 onward): Jared counts all 14 on camera, confirms the embellishments don't count toward the total, then states the actual point of the exercise — learn the set, then treat it as raw material to reorder and improvise with rather than a fixed sequence to memorize verbatim.

No tab/fret-diagram overlay is shown in the audio-only transcript beyond two named fret numbers (fret 8 for voicing 10, fret 13 for voicing 5's barre) — every other voicing is identified purely by scale-degree note names, which is what's captured above and on the technique note.

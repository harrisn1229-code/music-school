# Synthet - Every Trick to EAR CANDY

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MqqKtwdHAGw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MqqKtwdHAGw)

## Synopsis

Synthet's longest and densest video in this batch — a grab-bag tour of "ear candy" (small details that make a track memorable) covering seven distinct techniques: tape-stop variation, a pitch-up alternative to tape-stop, automated decay/arpeggio swells on pads, an isolated delay-return channel for word-targeted echoes and delay morphing, field-recorded/found-sound texture, high-pitch flourishes as a deliberate frequency-slot choice, and reusing familiar sounds/instrument roles from within a genre rather than reinventing them. Several of these — especially the delay-channel routing and the tape-stop variation — recur across other Synthet shorts in this batch and are cross-linked accordingly.

## Techniques

- **Tape-stop variation via automation, not just a plugin default** — using a free tape-stop plugin (Kilohearts), automate its start/stop rather than relying on the default curve: press play on the plugin, go to Tools → Last Tweak → Create Automation Clip, then adjust the automation's start and stop time to make the wind-down slower or faster — lets a sound "ring" one time through and tape-stop the next, for variation rather than repetition. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Pitch-up as a tape-stop alternative** — pitching a sound up an octave with a fast pitch-envelope/effect gets a similarly attention-grabbing result to a tape stop, including on full chords, without needing the tape-stop plugin at all.
- **Automate a pad/arp's Decay parameter while it plays, for a "door opening" swell** — locate the Decay knob on the instrument and ride it up during playback rather than leaving it static; described as feeling like "opening a door" onto the sound, and works well sped up for a faster build.
- **Delay-return channel for word-targeted echoes and delay morphing** — same core technique as [[Synthet - This Delay Trick Is Essential]]: put delay on a separate, dry-free return channel and automate the *send* so only specific words/hits get delayed, not the whole phrase. Extends it further here: automate a **slow LFO/vibrato + fast filter** on that isolated delay channel to make the echoes "nice and gnarly" (a morphing, degrading echo character distinct from the dry source), and use the same send automation for **rhythmical effects**, not just word-targeted delay. → [[Delay]]
- **Field-recorded/found sound as atmosphere** — record everyday real-world sounds and drag them into a track sparingly (background atmosphere or a direct hit); free-to-use sources exist (search Creative Commons on sites like freesound.org) even for commercial use — credit the source. Old, recognizable public-domain melodies are separately noted as fair game to replicate/reuse (not the same legal category as sampling a specific modern recording). → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]
- **High-pitch elements are an underused "flavor," used sparingly** — a bright, short-decay hit (crash, delayed) placed deliberately on the first beat of a bar, alongside the main percussion, reads as a memorable accent — don't overuse ("you don't want it every bar" — economy makes the surprise land). For sustained high content, use strings/drones as a binding top-layer texture; vocal chops in the same slot work too. → [[AIR Slot]]
- **High-pitch melodic phrasing is more memorable than low** — the same melodic idea played with its emphasis note an octave higher reads as noticeably more memorable/catchy than the low-register version, in a direct before/after comparison.
- **Reuse recognizable genre-convention instrument roles instead of inventing from scratch** — a genre already has "solved" sounds for certain roles (a bass slide, a piano lick, a trombone stab, an excited cowbell rhythm); borrowing a well-worn convention isn't a shortcut to avoid, it's efficient reuse of something that already reads clearly to a listener.

# Decapitator

![[Images/Decapitator.png]]

**Category:** Drive/Color
**Vendor:** Soundtoys

## What it is

Soundtoys' analog saturation plugin modeling several classic analog circuit types (tube and transformer-based) for harmonic distortion.

## What it's used for

Adding punchy analog-style saturation and harmonic drive to drums, bass, vocals, and mix busses.

## FX chain notes

A go-to coloration stage, often used per-track and again lightly on the mix bus.

## Used in my chains

- **[[Saturation Send]]** — *slot 1, "the dirt"*: the primary home. Style E, Drive 6.0, Low Cut 150 Hz, High Cut 10 kHz, Mix 100% — parallel grit blended under vocals / bass / drums.
- **[[3 Guitar Bus|Guitar Bus]]** — *variation*: swap [[Kramer Tape]] → Decapitator (Style A, Mix ~20%) for a rock-forward grit instead of tape gloss.
- **[[1 Synth Lead Track|Synth Lead Track]]** — *creative move*: Punish ~9–10 for a hero raspy-lead moment (MS-20 → 303 acid).
- **[[1 Main Vocal Track|Main Vocal Track]]** — *lo-fi lane*: [[Shure SM57]] → [[UAD UA 1176LN|1176]] slammed → Decapitator for a crushed section vocal.
- Also an attitude/texture option in the vocal stack: [[1 Harmony Vocal Track|Harmony]], [[1 Backup Vocal Track|Backup]], [[2 Ensemble Stack Group|Ensemble Stack Group]], [[3 Vocal Bus|Vocal Bus]].

## Manual

- 📄 [[00 Human/20 Music/School & Content/Production/Manuals/Decapitator.pdf|Local PDF]] · [Official manual](https://www.soundtoys.com/wp-content/uploads/Decapitator-Manual.pdf)

**High-level points** (the mental model — signal flow, modes, things the manual says that nobody discovers by knob-twisting):
- The 5 Style buttons are the actual "instrument" — each was built by analyzing a specific piece of real analog gear in Soundtoys' lab, not a generic distortion curve. Picking Style is the first decision, everything else shapes it.
- **A** = Ampex 350 tape preamp (ultra-smooth tube saturation — Sun/Stax/Motown/Chess era). **E** = Chandler/EMI TG Channel (beefy low end + airy top, the Abbey Road console sound). **N** = Neve 1057 input channel (Germanium transistors like a vintage Fuzz Face — weighty low end, focused mids, especially good on guitars). **T** = Thermionic Culture Vulture Triode setting (warm, punchy, even-harmonic tube distortion — good on drums/percussive material). **P** = Thermionic Culture Vulture Pentode setting (odd-harmonic distortion, more aggressive than Triode).
- **Signal path order matters**: Low Cut and Tone shape the sound *before* it hits the saturation circuit (they change what gets distorted); High Cut and Steep shape it *after* saturation (they tame the fizz the distortion itself created). Same knob position, opposite job.
- **Drive** is an input-gain control into the (virtual) saturation circuit — it doesn't just get louder, it changes *how* the signal breaks up, and every Style responds differently to being pushed.
- **Punish** adds a hard +20 dB of extra gain on top of Drive for all-out chaos — a different destination, not just "more drive."
- **Auto-Gain**: turning on Auto ties the Output knob to Drive automatically (as Drive goes up, Output comes down), so you can dial in tone/character changes without the volume jumping around and biasing your ear.
- **Mix** is built-in parallel processing (blend dry + saturated) — the same effect as a parallel-compression trick, but with no routing/submix needed.
- **Thump** and **Steep** are both "hidden" character switches worth knowing: Thump adds a tape-style low-frequency head-bump right at the Low Cut frequency (feeds *more* low end into the saturation circuit — can get flabby); Steep switches the High Cut filter from a gentle 6 dB/octave to a steep 30 dB/octave roll-off, useful for faking a guitar-cab high-cut when paired with a 4–5 kHz High Cut setting.

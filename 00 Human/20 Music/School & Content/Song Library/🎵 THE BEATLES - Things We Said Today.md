# Things We Said Today — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** the standard two-bridge model with one verse between and **no instrumental solo**. Intro (2) · Verse (16, four even phrases) · Verse · Bridge (8) · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Outro (a reprise of the intro material into a fade). Verses 2 and 3 end on A **major** instead of the expected minor, and the bridge's noisier texture starts in their final bar.

One of the earliest big harmonic stunts in the catalogue: a **♭II (Neapolitan) resolved directly to i**, skipping the V that classical practice requires, used in both verse and bridge and reused later in "You're Going To Lose That Girl". It is also one of very few early originals pervasively in a minor key — natural minor, using minor v7 rather than a functional major V ([[SCL-C01 — Natural minor]]), with the bridges shifting to the parallel major ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]).

The verse's third phrase is where the mood turns: you know immediately it is leaving home, you do not know where it is going, and at peak uncertainty the ♭II drops straight back to i. The bridge develops the same gambit — two phrases with an identical melodic backbone, the second substituting ♭II for the dominant, which is the source of their irony. That ♭II implies Phrygian throughout ([[SCL-C05 — Characteristic notes]]), running in the inner voices even though the figure never appears in the top-voice melody.

**Only Paul, on every vocal part**, and the variation is in the *tracking*: single-tracked verse 1 except for a parallel-thirds phrase and a sudden switch to double-tracking mid-phrase, with the title line harmonised in open fourths.

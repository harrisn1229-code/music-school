## Synopsis

A played-along ear-training quiz (using the ToneGym app) across 5 rounds: melodic intervals, triad chord quality, scale identification, harmonic (simultaneous) intervals, and sight-reading pitch on a moving staff. The value here is less the specific quiz answers and more the presenter's think-aloud identification heuristics for each round — genuinely reusable discrimination tricks.

## Techniques

- **Confirming a major 3rd by singing**: sing up the major scale from the lower note to its 3rd degree and check whether it matches what's heard — a concrete verification step, not just guessing from feel.
- **Diminished vs. augmented triad (both dissonant, easily confused)**: diminished sounds like it "wants to go somewhere" — directional, unresolved, actively pulling toward resolution; augmented sounds "wider" and comparatively static — dissonant but without that same forward pull.
- **Harmonic minor's audible giveaway**: the augmented 2nd between ♭6 and 7 (the raised leading tone) — described as an "Arabic"-sounding wide gap right before the top of the scale, distinct from natural minor's plain whole-step there.
- **Ascending vs. descending melodic minor, distinguished by the 3rd alone**: both use the natural (major-scale) 6th and 7th; the natural minor 3rd is the only tell that separates it from a plain major scale by ear.
- **Perfect 4th vs. perfect 5th as harmonic (simultaneous) intervals — genuinely easy to confuse since they're inversions of each other**: a 4th carries a "sus4"-like quality that sounds like it wants to resolve DOWN to a 3rd; a 5th sounds "clean" and doesn't carry that same pull to resolve.
- **Major 6ths and minor 7ths** are separately corroborated elsewhere in the vault as the hardest harmonic intervals to identify by ear — see [[Harmonic Ear Training]] § *Ear Training Difficulty Tier List*.

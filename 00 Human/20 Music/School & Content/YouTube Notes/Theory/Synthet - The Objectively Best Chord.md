# Synthet - The Objectively Best Chord

## Synopsis

Synthet's personal pitch for the minor 7th chord (minor triad + a minor 7th on top, 10 semitones from the root): it keeps a progression from feeling "stupidly positive" (all-major) or too dark (all-minor) by softening minor chords just enough to feel "easier to accept," while still being simple to bolt onto an existing progression — swap any minor (or major, via a major 7th) chord for its 7th-chord equivalent without needing to understand the full theory first.

## What's taught

- **Minor 7th = minor triad + a note a minor 7th (10 semitones) above the root** — demoed across Cm7, Gm7, Fm7; the extra note softens a minor chord's "sadness" without pushing it toward major, and travels well across many progressions/intervals. → [[Seventh Chords]]
- **Naming isn't the interval count** — "m7"/"maj7" name the chord *scale-wise* (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th) not by semitone distance; the actual semitone distance for a minor 7th is 10, for a major 7th it's 11. Worth flagging since the naming trips people up.
- **All-major reads as "stupidly positive," all-minor loses warmth — 7ths are the middle ground.** A/B'd in the video: same progression in only major triads (flat, too bright) vs. adding minor 7ths (a "house" jazzier feel) vs. adding major 7ths on the majors too (fuller, "housier" chord tones throughout).
- **You don't need the theory to use it** — if a progression has a mix of majors and minors, try slapping a 7th onto each chord (m7 on the minors, maj7 on the majors) and listen; a chord tool (FL's built-in one, or Nate's [[Chord]] / [[Chord Generator]]) removes the need to work out the notes by hand.

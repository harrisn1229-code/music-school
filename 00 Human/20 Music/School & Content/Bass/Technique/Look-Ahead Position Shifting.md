# Look-Ahead Position Shifting

BassBuzz's fix for a specific beginner failure mode that sits one rung past basic one-position fretting: once you can plant your hand in a single spot and play a few notes cleanly, the next thing that trips beginners up is actually **moving/shifting** the hand mid-riff — it's easy to stumble and get lost the instant a line asks the hand to relocate. The riff used to teach it is the main **Seven Nation Army** riff (The White Stripes) — chosen specifically because, of the several ways bassists adapt this famously-guitar riff, one particular fingering happens to force a real hand shift, which is exactly what makes it useful practice material rather than just another fixed-position pattern.

## The fingering

Pinky, pinky, index, pinky, index, **shift**, middle, index — the shift lands between the index note and the middle-finger note that follows it, moving the hand from the riff's home position (around the 5th/7th frets) down toward the 3rd fret to grab the last two notes.

## The core trick: let your eyes lead the hand, not the other way around

The mechanism that actually fixes the stumbling — described as "the secret" to the shift:

1. **While still playing the earlier part of the phrase** (the 5th-fret/7th-fret/5th-fret run before the shift), **look at the shift's target fret** — here, the 3rd fret — *before* the hand needs to be there. The eyes arrive at the destination well ahead of the fingers.
2. **When the shift happens, just "plop" the target finger down where you were already looking.** Because the eyes already located the target, the hand isn't searching for it blind mid-motion — it's landing on a spot already confirmed.
3. **Immediately after playing the note that follows the shift** (the index note here), **start looking back at the previous position** (the 7th fret) so the eyes are already re-aimed and ready for the next repeat of the loop, rather than scrambling to relocate it after the fact.

The general principle behind all three steps: a shift goes wrong when the fretting hand has to *search* for its target in the moment it's supposed to land — pre-aiming with the eyes removes that search entirely, turning a blind reach into a confirmed placement. This is the same shape of fix as [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns#Drill 2 — decide rake vs. alternate on every direction change|pre-deciding a plucking-hand choice before reaching the decision point]] — pre-committing before the moment of execution, rather than deciding live under time pressure — applied here to the eyes/fretting-hand pairing instead of a plucking-finger choice.

## Don't try to count the rhythm — vibe it out instead

A secondary tip taught on the same riff, not directly related to the shifting mechanic but bundled with it because the riff's rhythm is its other beginner trap: the feel sits ambiguously between a triplet subdivision and a 16th-note subdivision ("it's actually kind of both and in betweeny"). Stated advice: don't try to formally count it — listen to the recording and match the feel by ear instead. *(Contrast with [[Syncopation and Metric Displacement]]'s rhythm-mnemonic approach for [[5 Beginner Bass Riffs (You Can’t F*** Up)|a different riff in this same video series]], which invents a verbal phrase to lock in a hard-to-count rhythm — here the advice is simply to stop trying to count at all and imitate by ear.)*

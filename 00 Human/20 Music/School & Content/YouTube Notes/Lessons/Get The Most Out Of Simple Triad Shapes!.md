# Get The Most Out Of Simple Triad Shapes!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2RlQtsEHa1c)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2RlQtsEHa1c)

## Synopsis
Jack Ruch's lesson on a third way to use triads for soloing, distinct from his other two triad videos already in the vault ([[Triads as a Fretboard Map]]'s single-triad three-zone method and [[Upper Structure Triads from Seventh Chords]]'s extract-from-inside-a-chord method): here the **whole parent scale gets harmonized into seven triads**, one per degree, using the skip-a-note (tertian) derivation, and each resulting triad becomes a self-contained lick-starting motif. Worked entirely in the key of **A**, over an **A7** vamp — first building the A7 (A Mixolydian) scale, then deriving its seven diatonic triads, then laying those triads out as one continuous ladder on the top three strings moving up the neck, then combining each triad with two notes from a single-string scale ladder as a short "arpeggio + two notes" motif. The bulk of the video is seven worked lick examples — one per triad, played slow then up to tempo — each starting from the motif and then developing into a fuller phrase using blues/pentatonic vocabulary (flat 5th, minor-3rd/major-3rd blending, chromatic passing tones) rather than staying strictly inside the A7 scale. The video closes with a freely improvised bluesy solo over the vamp that drops individual triad-motif licks in wherever they feel musically right, rather than performing all seven in sequence — framing the triad ladder as a vocabulary source to dip into, not a fixed set of exercises. ## What's taught
- **Building the A7 scale.** "A7 scale" = A major scale with a flattened 7th = **A Mixolydian**: A, B, C♯, D, E, F♯, G.
- **Deriving triads from the scale by the skip-a-note method.** Play every other note of the scale, moving the starting point up one degree at a time: A (skip B) C♯ (skip D) E = **A major**; B (skip C♯) D (skip E) F♯ = **B minor**; continuing through the whole scale produces all seven diatonic triads: **A major, B minor, C♯ diminished, D major, E minor, F♯ minor, G major** — i.e. Mixolydian's I–ii–iii°–IV–v–vi–♭VII sequence.
- **The seven triads played as one ladder, moving up the neck, on the top three strings only** — a single continuous string set rather than scattered shapes, so the sequence is felt as one physical climb.
- **A second supporting shape: the A7 (Mixolydian) scale laid out horizontally on one string**, the high E string — open E, F♯, G, A (root), B, C♯, D, back to octave E — a nearby pool of scale tones for whichever triad shape is currently in hand.
- **The combining motif: triad arpeggio + two notes of the horizontal scale**, moved through all seven triads in turn, demonstrated stretched across the whole fretboard — "any one of these little triad patterns can be a jumping off point for a lick... the catalyst to get you started."
- **Explicit permission to leave the A7 scale once the motif lands** — the licks that follow each motif lean on **bluesy/pentatonic vocabulary**: the flat 5th as a passing tone, and the minor 3rd sounded together with or slid/bent into the major 3rd, the same blend documented on [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]].
- **Seven worked lick examples, one per triad, played slow then up to tempo, starting from C♯ diminished (not the tonic) and moving up through D, E, F♯, G, A, and finishing on B:**
  - **C♯ diminished (starting triad — "right out of this bar"):** arpeggio + two scale notes resolving to A, into a blended minor/major-pentatonic phrase with the flat 5th — descending A→G, a chromatic run, landing on the minor 3rd with room to bend/slide into the major 3rd.
  - **D major:** arpeggio + two scale notes, descend the A7 scale, deliberately using the **minor 3rd instead of the major 3rd** for a bluesier color.
  - **E minor:** arpeggio + two scale notes, skip down to A, back to B, down to G then F♯, a chromatic run built on the flat 5th (down to E♭, jump to B, up to D), sliding into the 3rd and root of an A chord.
  - **F♯ minor** — called out as the **relative minor of A major** (only one note differs between the two triads), which is why it sits so naturally with straight A-major/A-pentatonic vocabulary. Lick: arpeggio + two scale notes, descend the A7 scale, again accenting the minor 3rd for a bluesy flavor.
  - **G major:** arpeggio + two scale notes, then largely straight A7-scale material using three-note-per-string picking.
  - **A major (the tonic/"home" triad):** essentially the notes of A7 itself, decorated with chromatic passing tones — minor 3rd sliding up to major 3rd, a hammer-on between the 2nd and minor 3rd, a ♭7-down-to-6th passing move.
  - **B minor (final):** arpeggio + two scale notes, then a descending run of A7-outlining "mixolydian stuff."
- **The closing full solo doesn't run the seven licks in order** — it's a freely improvised bluesy solo over the A7 vamp with individual triad-motif phrases dropped in opportunistically, "in certain spots where it feels right," demonstrating the triad ladder as vocabulary to reach for rather than a fixed performance sequence.

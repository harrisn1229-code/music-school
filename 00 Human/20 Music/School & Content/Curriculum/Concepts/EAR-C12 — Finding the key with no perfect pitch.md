# EAR-C12 — Finding the key with no perfect pitch

**Q.** You can hear where the music resolves but you don't know what note it is. What's the move?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Sing the note that feels like the tonic** (you don't need to know its pitch), then play **any random note** on your instrument and judge what scale degree *that* note feels like relative to what you sang. Back-calculate: "I played a G and it feels like 4, so the key is D." This single move is the one that recurs in every real-time transcription example.

## Why it matters

It removes the perfect-pitch dependency entirely — you only ever need *relative* hearing plus one reference note you can produce on demand. Backups when it's murky: the last melody note, or the bass of the most stable chord.

## Drill

Put on a song you don't know. Sing the tonic, play one random note, name its degree, back-calculate the key. Check against a tuner. Five songs.

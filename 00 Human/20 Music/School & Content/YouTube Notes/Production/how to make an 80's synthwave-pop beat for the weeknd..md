# how to make an 80's synthwave/pop beat for the weeknd.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dWhh27UFWhY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dWhh27UFWhY)

## Synopsis

A from-scratch breakdown of an 80s synthwave/pop instrumental in the vein of The Weeknd's *Dawn FM*-era sound — loner's usual "here's exactly how I built this" format, walking through sound design, bass, drums, extra melodic layers, and arrangement in order. The build leans on a single Juno-style software synth for almost every melodic/harmonic role (main pad, bass, second "chorus-lift" pad, pluck texture, bell counter-melody), each instance run through its own small effects chain rather than one shared bus — the recurring recipe across all of them is dry-and-bright at the source, then chorus + reverb (sometimes heavy) + EQ (cut lows, sometimes mids) + delay to taste. The most valuable material is the layering logic: rather than one "big" pad doing everything, the pad, bass, and drums each carry a distinct piece of the bounce/rhythm, and additional layers (second pad, pluck, bell) are reserved for specific song moments (chorus lift, transitions, end-of-verse variation) instead of running throughout. Drums come from two Splice kits picked specifically for their "80s pop" character rather than a generic trap/hip-hop bank, with the video's now-familiar hi-hat recipe (shorten, pitch down, nudge off the kick with a named keyboard-shortcut workflow: Select None → Ctrl+A → Shift-right twice) making its third confirmed appearance across this channel's back catalog. Arrangement is kept deliberately simple — a quick two-part intro, panned descending toms into a Splice "BWB kit" riser, a chopped-and-duplicated snare roll built with the Cut tool, and a verse/chorus contrast built entirely from muting the main pad (verse = no pad, chorus = pad + arp + everything) rather than writing new parts per section.

## Techniques

**Sound design — main pad (the "Juno emulation")**
- Start with a Juno-style pad patch, then pull the **attack and envelope down** to cut the high end — the goal is a dry, un-hyped starting patch before any effects are added.
- **Chorus first, then arpeggiate the pad** — the arp is applied to the chorused chord voicing, not a dry signal.
- Effects chain after that: **Cassette** (tape/warmth) → **heavy reverb** (explicitly "quite a lot," reasoned as "it wants to be a big sound," and stated to work well stacked with the chorus — "this is like a chorus reverb so it makes the sound even bigger") → **EQ** (cut the audible low notes) → **delay**.
- **Negative result worth keeping**: a half-time effect was tried on the delay/pad chain and then removed — "I'd actually get rid of this half-time because I didn't use it." Audition optional chain stages and cut what isn't earning its keep, don't leave unused devices in a shipped chain.
- → [[5 Inst & Synth/1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]], [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]], [[TAL-Chorus-LX]], [[Cassette]], [[Synthwave]], [[Roland Juno-106]]

**Bass**
- Bass is written **early in the build process, deliberately** — "I like to do the bass early on when I'm making my beats so I can make sure it's right — it's a very important part and I feel like some people get that wrong."
- Bassline follows the **chord progression's own root notes**, with extra notes added on top purely "to give it a bounce and rhythm" — not a new independent line, an embellished root-following line.
- **Decapitator** added after writing, purely for crunch — no specific Style/Drive settings given (qualitative "give it more crunch" only).
- → [[4 Bass/1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]], [[Decapitator]], [[Bass Design]]

**Drums**
- Two Splice kits picked specifically for **genre-matched character** rather than a generic trap/hip-hop bank: **"Oliver's Power Tools" pack two** and a kit auto-captioned **"Medicine Hyper Color"** — described as having "unique drums, like 80s pop kind of drums."
- **Snare:** simple, velocity manually varied on individual hits for a less static feel.
- **Hi-hat:** shortened (tail/decay pulled down), **pitched down**, then **timing nudged off the kick** with a specific, repeatable keyboard workflow: **Select None → Ctrl+A (select all) → Shift-right arrow twice** — "so it's got like — it's separated from the kick, it's not hitting straight away." (This is the third confirmed instance of this exact shorten/pitch-down/off-grid hi-hat recipe on this channel — see [[Pocket and Microtiming]] for the two earlier sightings and cross-reference.)
- **Percussion:** several small percussion layers added on top of kick/snare/hat — described as "pretty much all the drums" needed.
- A **second percussion layer sourced from the Juno instrument itself** ("percussive-melodic, whatever you want to call it") placed only at the very end of the arrangement.
- Explicit priority statement: *"the drums are pretty simple... but for me the main part is the bass, the bass gives it the rhythm and the swing"* — the bass, not the drum groove, is treated as the primary rhythmic driver of this build.
- → [[2 Drums/1 Drums Hat Track|Drums Hat Track]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]

**Second pad — the "chorus-lift" layer**
- A second Juno-style patch, added once the bass+drums+main-pad foundation felt too small for the chorus section — "I wanted it to sound a lot bigger, especially for the chorus."
- Same envelope-down/frequency-down starting move as the main pad (kept dry and un-bright at the source).
- Chain: **EQ** (cut the lows AND "quite a lot of the mid-range" — a deeper cut than the main pad's lows-only EQ) → **delay** → **reverb**, with level pushed up a bit once wet.
- → [[5 Inst & Synth/1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]], [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]], [[Layering and Stacking]]

**Pluck texture layer**
- A "pluck-type thing" added for the **second half of the hook only** — explicitly a texture/ear-candy layer, not a melodic part: "it adds like some extra texture."
- Chain: **reverb → delay → EQ**, with the EQ cutting a lot of the lows specifically because the low end "isn't there" on this patch anyway — the cut is about cleaning up rather than shaping an audible low register.
- **Auto-panned side to side** for movement — used as the layer's defining character move.
- → [[Width and Stereo]], [[5 Inst & Synth/1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]]

**Bell counter-melody**
- A simple bell patch (again from the Juno instrument), added as a **one-off melodic variation device**: it only shows up "for the end of the verses and stuff, just to give it a bit of variation," not throughout.
- Delay recipe called out as a repeat favorite ("I like to use this delay a lot"): **tape-style, eighth-note, ping-pong** (bounces side to side), with **modulation turned down**, **drive set low**, and **mix pushed up quite a lot** — the wet delay signal is doing most of the audible work on this layer. Owned lane: **EchoBoy** (tape echo types + built-in ping-pong routing match this recipe closely).
- → [[EchoBoy]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

**Arrangement / structure**
- Kept deliberately simple: a **quick two-part intro** to get into the beat fast.
- **Toms** added into the arrangement, **panned across the stereo field** (a high tom, a low tom, a middle tom) and played descending.
- A **rise** used straight, un-layered, sourced from a **Splice "BWB kit"**.
- **Verse/chorus contrast built by muting one element, not writing new parts**: the hook carries the main pad + arp together (the "big" version); the pad is simply removed for the verses, leaving them "low down," and the bell counter-melody is what fills the end of each verse before the arrangement builds back into the hook.
- → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]

**Editing workflow — snare roll**
- A repeat-use editing move ("I've shown this in my previous videos"): use Ableton's **Cut/Split tool**, select the desired region size, hold **Shift** to make the cut — visualized as a line through the clip — to chop a section of the beat, then duplicate/rearrange the chopped pieces to build a quick snare roll.

# this is how frank dukes & cubeatz make vintage samples in 2022!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nak-TAIPQ-c)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nak-TAIPQ-c)

## Synopsis

Despite the clickbait title, this is loner's own from-scratch build of a vintage-style sample (not an actual Frank Dukes/CUBEATZ breakdown) — "flipped into a super hard dark beat for Future" — walking through nine layered elements: a Rhodes-style electric-piano chord bed, a doubled vintage upright piano, a Rickenbacker-style bass, a one-shot accent, an Embertone Joshua Bell violin melody, a subtle texture layer, two Splice perc loops, a two-vocal-loop stack (each with its own distinct processing chain), and a reversed/granular-chopped guitar — before the whole sample gets bounced into Fruity Slicer, pitched down 9 semitones, and chopped into a beat with programmed drums. The most valuable ideas are process-level rather than plugin-specific: **voice a chord progression so the top line moves in tight (semitone) intervals for tension** (all three chords in this build are the same D♯m/G♯m shape, just re-voiced), **use room-style reverb presets (not hall/plate) on real-instrument samples** for a "someone's actually playing it in a room" realism, **stretch a vocal loop to double its length in a beat-slicer** for an instant warped/vintage read (credited to fellow producer Hicks), and **give each vocal layer in a stack a genuinely different processing chain** rather than copying one chain across all of them (credited to Corbinian's Nami/CUBEATZ analysis videos). The guitar layer's "random chop + nudge back by two clicks, generic crossfade mode" granular-blur trick is credited to Simbo of Split Mind. Auto-captions are reasonably clean for a spoken breakdown but garble several plugin/preset names — flagged inline below and cross-referenced against prior loner-video name resolutions already logged in [[Plugin Wishlist]] and [[Sound Selection Guide]] where the same products recur.

## Techniques

**1. Chords — Arturia Wurli V2 ("funky cat" preset)**
- **Voice a repeating chord shape with the top note moving in semitone steps for a "creepy, eerie, mysterious" vibe.** All three chords in the progression are voicings of the same D♯m chord (with the final chord a G♯m) — chord 1 has the D♯ on top, chord 2 re-voices the SAME chord with the F♯ on top instead, walking the top line up over the first four bars then back down over the second four; the closing G♯m again puts adjacent chord tones (a semitone apart) on top. The tension comes entirely from **how close together the top-line notes sit**, not from the underlying harmony changing. → [[Chord Inversions]], [[Chord Progressions]] — mapped instrument: **Arturia Wurli V2** (heard/captioned "Whirly V2" — same auto-caption garble already resolved to **Arturia Wurli V** in [[Plugin Wishlist]] from a separate loner video; this is a second confirmed use of the same instrument/preset family).
- **Room-preset reverb (not hall/plate) for real-instrument samples, high wet, small decay.** "That's a tip I'd give you guys — use room presets when you're picking a reverb, especially if you're using a sample with real instruments like a piano or Rhodes: you wanted to have that real feeling like someone's actually playing it in a room somewhere." Small/reflective room preset specifically, decay kept small, wet kept high. → [[Reverb]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]]
- **EQ + dynamic EQ + valve compressor, comp bought down in the mix (not driven hard).** Chain after the reverb: EQ cutting the low end out → a dynamic EQ knocking down two specific resonant peaks → a valve compressor "to tighten the sound," with its mix pulled back so the part doesn't read as over-compressed. *(general starting point — no exact frequencies/gain given)* → [[EQ]], [[Compression]]

**2. Doubled piano — "SSS Vintage Upright"**
- **Double a chord part an octave up on a deliberately imperfect-sounding vintage piano instrument, velocities pulled down, pushed toward (not fully into) mono, panned to one side.** Same chords as the Wurli layer, an octave higher, played by the "SSS Vintage Upright" instrument — chosen specifically because "it doesn't sound too perfect, it has some imperfections, it sounds really old and vintage." Processing: velocities brought down significantly; stereo width pushed toward mono but not collapsed all the way; panned to the right. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Width and Stereo]] — same "SSS Vintage Upright" piano already logged in [[Plugin Wishlist]] from a different loner video (a Marvin Gaye-style soul recreation); this is a second confirmed use of the same instrument.
- **ValhallaVintageVerb, ambient-style room preset, short decay, high mix; a speaker/mic-emulation plugin for lo-fi color; a tape plugin's "excited"/wow-flutter-heavy preset for detune; a Pro-Q high-cut; then a delay.** Full chain on the doubled piano: [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] (ambient room preset, kept short, mix pushed high) → **"Speakers from Audio Thing"** — an AudioThing speaker/mic-emulation plugin that runs sounds through different speaker/mic emulations, "like a radio, certain microphones, pretend like it's in a bathroom" (already tracked unowned in [[Plugin Wishlist]] — this is a second confirmed use) → **Reels** (AudioThing tape plugin, "excited tape" preset, wow/flutter pushed up deliberately for detune — already tracked unowned in [[Plugin Wishlist]] under the same near-certain name resolution, third+ confirmed reuse across loner videos) → [[Pro-Q 4]] (cutting high end) → a delay. → [[EQ]], [[Delay]], [[Modulation]]
- **Automate the reverb's wet/dry mix up for a sparser section, so the space matches the arrangement density.** "I automated the delay of the wet/dry signal on the piano, because for the last four bars we only have one chord hitting and it doesn't feel too much of the space — so I wanted to make sure the reverb hits more in this section." Subtle but audible increase in reverb wetness specifically where the chord density drops. → [[Automation]], [[Reverb]], [[Depth and Space]]

**3. Bass — Scarbee Rickenbacker**
- **A walk-down fill plus a short connective phrase are what sell a bass part as "realistic," not just the tone.** "What makes it sound more realistic is this walk-down, and this little section here as well." → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Track the bass in a higher-than-usual octave, then pitch the whole thing down afterward — the register move itself (not just the final pitch) shapes the texture.** "I've got everything pretty high up, even the bass is in a higher octave — I can't really explain it, but I always find when I have things high up I love the textures of it, it sounds really good, and then when I pitch it down it sounds even better, as opposed to just having it in the low octave to start with." → [[Bass Design]], [[Pitch and Formant]] — instrument: NI Scarbee Rickenbacker (played inside owned [[Kontakt 7]]) — a second confirmed reuse of the same instrument already logged on [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Bass section) from a Lophiile bass-livestream breakdown.

**4. Accent hit — "Layers Volume 2"**
- **A single one-shot hit landed on the bass note's "one," then half-timed so it doesn't sit too high/busy in the arrangement.** A one-shot from the (then-unreleased) "Layers Volume 2" pack, played as a single accent hit on the bass note's downbeat; applying half-time to the hit brings it down from an overly-bright/busy read to something that sits in the mix. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — "Leia's Volume 2" in auto-captions, resolved against the many prior "Layers Volume 2" mentions already tracked under the Arturia Analog Lab entry in [[Plugin Wishlist]] (now an 11th+ confirmed reuse of that content pack).

**5. Violin — Embertone Joshua Bell**
- **A "harmonic" articulation preset on a solo-violin library reads closer to a flute than a violin — soft, airy, minimal attack — good for a simple melodic topline that needs to sit back, not lead.** "I chose the harmonic preset — it almost makes it sound like a flute, it doesn't have much attack, it's very soft and airy." A very simple melody, closed out with a short reverb. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — instrument: Embertone Joshua Bell violin (realistic solo-violin library, "lots of different articulations and different versions of it"). No owned equivalent tracked — see [[Plugin Wishlist]].

**6. Texture layer**
- A subtle texture layer added purely for depth, no further detail given in the transcript. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]

**7. Perc loops — Splice**
- **Two Splice perc loops (a simple drum bounce + a small transitional fill), both filtered to sit under the main elements rather than left full-range.** → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

**8. Vocals — two Splice loops, two DIFFERENT processing chains**
- **Stretch a vocal loop to double its length inside a beat-slicer for an instant warped/vintage/"chopped from somewhere" read — a Hicks trick.** "What Hicks showed me to do was stretch it out, double-time it — in FL literally you can go up to this little thing here, it says stretch, make sure that's selected, and you can drag it out, so now it's double the length. This makes it sound more warped, more vintage, more like you've chopped it from somewhere." Applied to both vocal loops used in the build. Owned-lane equivalent for the mechanism: Ableton [[Simpler]]/clip Warp in Complex/Complex Pro mode, dragging the clip's right edge to double its length without re-pitching. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Vocal loop 1 — short but heavy delay, then a reverse-pitch 4-bar reverse-loop reverb preset with the mix pulled well down (it's off-time), plus an EQ curve cutting a little low and a little high.** "I did the reverse pitch, four bar reverse loop preset from Valhalla Delay — make sure you bring the mix quite far down, because it's a bit off time or whatever, but it just adds this nice little texture at the end." → [[ValhallaDelay]], [[EQ]], [[Reverb]]
- **A second vocal chop underneath gets a DIFFERENT processing chain from the first — different vocals earning different treatment is itself the technique, credited to Corbinian's Nami/CUBEATZ sample-style breakdown videos.** "I learned this little trick from one of Corbinian's videos... he does lots of cool insights and in-depth reviews on different people's sample styles, like Nami, CUBEATZ, the Truth — basically when he's breaking down one of Nami's samples he showed that different vocals have different effects, some have lots of delay, some don't. So I wanted to copy that and add this one underneath that has a different processing chain." Loop 2's chain: a **plate reverb** (owned-lane [[ValhallaPlate]]) → a heavier and longer delay than loop 1's → more filtering, with more of the high end taken away than loop 1. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Reverb]], [[Delay]], [[EQ]] — this "give each vocal layer a genuinely different chain" idea generalizes past this one build; worth applying to any multi-vocal-layer sample.

**9. Guitar — reversed granular chop-and-shift**
- **Random-chop a reversed audio file into many small pieces, select all, nudge the whole selection back by "maybe two little clicks," set to Generic mode to avoid pops/clicks — a Simbo (Split Mind) trick.** "I reversed it and did the trick I showed you guys in the previous video... I actually got this from Simbo, from Split Mind — I saw it in one of his videos. He just adds a bunch of random chops to a sample, to an audio file, then he goes to None [Warp mode], selects it all, and just brings it back by maybe two little clicks — make sure it's set to Generic so there's no pops and clicks." A granular-blur effect distinct from a normal reverse. Owned-lane equivalent: Ableton clip Warp mode set to **Beats**, slice heavily, select-all + nudge, with **Complex/Complex Pro** for the click-free crossfade behavior Generic mode gives in FL. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Shaper (ShaperBox-style) added to pulse the reversed/chopped guitar, full-wet-mix but short reverb, and a tremolo panning it side to side as the final stage.** Owned-lane for the pulse device: [[Shaper]] — this is a further confirmed reuse of ShaperBox already tracked as a medium-impact wishlist item ([[Plugin Wishlist]]). → [[Reverb]], [[Modulation]], [[Width and Stereo]]

**10. Finishing chop + drums**
- **Load the finished layered sample into a beat-slicer set to even beat-slicing, pitch down 9 semitones, raise attack and decay slightly, then chop by ear on a keyboard "trying to find a bounce."** Fruity Slicer, beat-slicing mode for even, predictable chops; −9 semitones on the whole sample; attack and decay both nudged up slightly before playing chops in by hand until a rhythmic "bounce" emerges — first half of the beat is sparser, the second half repeats the same chop pattern with more elements layered in. Owned-lane equivalent: Ableton clip sliced to a MIDI/Drum Rack via **Slice to New MIDI Track**, or [[Simpler]] in Slice Playback Mode. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Simple "wheezy"-style drum layer under the chopped sample: clap → hi-hats → clap layered with a snare for grip → a second snare → 808 → kick → an open hat as a one-off switch-up.** No further processing detail given. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]]

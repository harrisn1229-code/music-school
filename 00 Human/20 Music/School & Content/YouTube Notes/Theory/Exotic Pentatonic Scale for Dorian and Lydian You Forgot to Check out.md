# Exotic Pentatonic Scale for Dorian and Lydian You Forgot to Check out

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCFhCBsan70)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCFhCBsan70)

## Synopsis

Jens Larsen introduces the **Hirajoshi scale** (a Japanese pentatonic, scale degrees 1-2-3-5-6 of a major scale) as an underused pentatonic option for Dorian and Lydian sounds, and uses it as a worked demonstration of a general method: examine any unfamiliar scale for the triads/arpeggios hidden inside it, then move a found voicing shape through the whole scale to generate a full set of usable chords.

## What's taught

- **The Hirajoshi scale, built and placed.** From E: E-F♯-G-B-C (repeating) — equivalently, the pentatonic built on scale degrees 1-2-3-5-6 of a major scale (so it's "C major's 1-2-3-5-6" reframed with E as the practical root, or symmetrically, a pentatonic built off the 6th degree of C major).
- **Where to use it — the concrete harmonic mapping.** Analyzing the scale's contents reveals a complete Cmaj7 arpeggio plus an added ♯11 inside it (C-E-G-B, with F♯ as the ♯11) — giving it a **Lydian** flavor when read as rooted on that major-7 chord (Cmaj7♯11). Built off the 6th degree instead (A, i.e. treating it as an A-rooted structure over an A-minor-family chord) gives an **A minor 13 Dorian** color — explicitly recommended over E is avoided since the scale lacks a 7th-degree-equivalent note needed to spell an E minor sound clearly.
- **Two-note-per-string fingering**, same physical approach guitarists already use for standard pentatonic scales — makes the exotic scale immediately playable using existing muscle memory, in five positions like any other pentatonic.
- **A small-cell octave-shape variant** — a compact 3-note cell repeated in octaves up the neck (the shape guitarists may recognize from heavy-metal pentatonic-run tutorials), an alternate way to visualize/play the same scale.
- **The general analysis method, worked in full:** (1) list the scale's notes across two octaves; (2) scan for known triads/structures hiding inside (found here: a C major triad, an E minor triad, a B sus4 triad, and an F♯ diminished-sus♯4 triad — plus the full Cmaj7♯11 arpeggio already noted); (3) take one found voicing (e.g. a Gmaj7 shell voicing, since G-B-F♯ are all in the scale) and walk it through the scale by moving each voice up to the next scale tone, generating a full progression of usable chords/arpeggios from the single starting shape — the same "walk the shape through the scale" method used throughout this vault's other Jens Larsen chord-voicing content, applied here to an unfamiliar scale rather than a familiar chord type.
- **A "split the scale into two rows, stack a note from each" chord-building method**, mirrored from standard pentatonic-scale chord-building — applied to the Hirajoshi fingering, yields voicings readable as a rootless D7(13), an Am6, or a Cmaj7(♯11) without the 3rd, depending on context.

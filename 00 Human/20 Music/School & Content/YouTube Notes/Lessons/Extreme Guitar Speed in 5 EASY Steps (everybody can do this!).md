# Extreme Guitar Speed in 5 EASY Steps (everybody can do this!)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5rJD2mG1qk0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5rJD2mG1qk0)

## Synopsis

A personal-history framed speed-building philosophy: five steps, from isolating one challenge at a time up through a specific short-burst/tension-relaxation practice routine, plus a closing safety/economy-of-motion checklist. The most quotable idea is a "walking vs. running" reframe of the classic +1 BPM/day metronome advice.

## Techniques

1. **Isolate one challenge at a time when starting out.** Worked on pure picking-hand speed on a single open string (no fretting-hand demands) before combining picking speed with fretting-hand movement — build the two independently before merging them.
2. **Push past your current comfort tempo early and often, sloppily, on purpose.** Playing along to fast recordings before being "ready," even producing bad results, is credited as the key to finding the fast picking motion at all — deliberately experiencing what a faster tempo *feels* like, rather than only ever practicing at tempos already under control.
3. **"Walking vs. running" reframe of the classic +1 BPM/day metronome method.** The stated claim: playing slow and playing fast are mechanically *different motions*, not the same motion done quicker — the same way walking faster and faster never turns into running, because running is a different gait. The classic advice (increase the metronome 1 BPM a day) works up to a "speed barrier" and then stalls, because at that point the fix isn't more of the same motion, it's switching to the fast-tempo motion directly (with expected initial sloppiness, same as a first attempt at running).
4. **Named daily speed-workout concept: short bursts + tension/relaxation + string pairing**, described as the personal most-effective routine. Practice in short, precise 2-string bursts (not long scale runs) that always end on a downstroke, so it's easy to self-check note count each rep; alternate a quick burst of fast notes with a deliberate rest, building a felt contrast between "tense/fast" and "relaxed" states rather than staying tense throughout — the rests are what let the fast motion register as genuinely small/relaxed rather than gripped.
5. **Closing economy-of-motion / injury-prevention checklist:** pick with the tip of the pick (not deep in the string); a small pick (the video's own preference: Jazz III-size) gives more control for string transitions; keep arms/hands relaxed even at high tempo — full-arm tension isn't needed and risks tendonitis; **stop on any pain or burning sensation**; keep fretting-hand fingers close to the fretboard (economy of motion) rather than lifting far off between notes; don't lock the wrist or default to a full-arm motion — stay wrist-driven with the arm only lightly assisting.

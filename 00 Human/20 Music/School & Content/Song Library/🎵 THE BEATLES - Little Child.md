# Little Child — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Four chords, no minor or altered harmony at all, and Pollack rates it the weakest of the five consecutive originals opening the album's side one — while arguing it works anyway as an unembarrassed rocker. Two features are worth the study: a **form that parses differently depending on whether you follow the music or the lyrics**, and a genuine instrumental break in strict 12-bar blues, which is rare this early.

**Form.** Musically it reads as the standard model. Follow the lyrics instead and it inverts — the "verse" repeats its words unchanged four times (so it's really a refrain) while only the "bridge" varies (so it's really the verse). Pollack keeps the musical reading but flags the ambiguity, and notes that "It Won't Be Long", written around the same time, poses the same puzzle. The 4-bar intro is in strict tempo despite sounding ad lib; a piano glissando is what lets your ear place the barline, and that moment of orientation is itself a hook. The bridge is **6 bars in three even 2-bar phrases** — a poetic triplet against the verse's quatrains, and the odd count reads as relief rather than error.

**Harmony.** Only four chords, all major: I, IV, V and V-of-V ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). The absence of anything minor, diminished, augmented or altered is what keeps the emotional tone flat and untroubled. The second verse is a small rewrite of the first purely to **close its second phrase on I** instead of leaving it open — one bar is the whole cost of a smooth handoff. The break is a pure 12-bar blues except that its **last two chords are replaced with the verse's own tag** ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]), which is what stitches an imported form into the song.

**Melody.** Facile and simple by design, with almost none of the appoggiaturas that fill something like "I'll Get You". The one exception is a sharp dissonance on a bridge downbeat.

**Arrangement.** Heavily overdubbed for such a plain track, muddy even in the original mix. The harmonica's spicy ninth in the intro comes back as the **major ninth chords** running through the verses and much of the break.

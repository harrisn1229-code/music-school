# how to make chill ambient beats for frank ocean (sad/melodic chord technique)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=whI75NJcom8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=whI75NJcom8)

## Synopsis

An early (2022) loner breakdown of a minimal, "could be on *Blonde*" chill R&B/ambient beat for Frank Ocean, built from a simple three-major-chord progression up through eight separate melodic/textural layers and a stripped-back drum pattern. The compositional hook is a **counter-melody-first writing habit**: write a counter-melody, then build chords underneath it, rather than the reverse — even though most of the actual counter-melody gets cut down to a single closing run by the end. The chord progression itself is a mini lesson in genre-defying voicing: three plain major triads (A♯/F/C) that read as "sad and mellow" purely because the progression *descends*, not because of any minor harmony. From there the video is essentially a sound-selection and effects-chain tour across eight elements — Kontakt flute, a one-shot bell synth, a free Pianobook violin bank, a texture perc loop, a water-stream SFX, a MODO ("Moto") Bass + AmpliTube bass, a Postcard Piano lead, and a minimal three-piece drum kit (rim shot, stick, two kicks, one hi-hat) — each given its own short, deliberately restrained FX chain. The explicitly stated production philosophy, and the most reusable idea on the page: **Frank Ocean's beats are simple — sometimes just one instrument and a vocal — so keep individual chains short and let restraint (not more layers) create the mood.** Several plugin/sample-source names are ambiguous in the auto-captions ("good hurts low high" for an EQ, "5 and xyz" for a one-shot kit, "one of only one's kits" for a perc pack) and are flagged rather than guessed. ## Techniques

### Chords — counter-melody-first writing + a plain-major "sad" progression
- **Write the counter-melody FIRST, then build chords underneath it — a stated habit for both playing keys more and for generating more unique progressions.** "A trick I've been using is starting off with a counter melody and playing chords underneath it — I feel like it helps give me a direction and allows me to come up with unique different progressions." In this build the roles ultimately inverted on screen (chords shown first, counter-melody — later cut down to one closing flute run — shown last), but the compositional order used to write the piece was counter-melody first. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Chord Progressions]]
- **Three plain major triads, voiced simply (3-note chords), read as "sad and mellow" purely because the progression descends — not because of minor harmony.** A♯ major → F major → C major: "obviously having three major chords helps it sound very mellow and melodic, but because the progression is going down it still gives you that kind of sad mellow sound." No processing on the chord layer beyond a plain low-end EQ cut — the harmonic/melodic choice alone (triad quality + descending motion) carries the mood, before any effect touches it. → [[Chord Progressions]], [[EQ]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **AAS Lounge Lizard's very first preset, completely unedited, as the chord source.** "I literally opened up Lounge Lizard, this is the very first preset, I didn't change anything, I just went straight in and laid down the chords" — a reminder that sound selection sometimes means trusting a factory default rather than sound-designing from scratch. Unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]] (already tracked from other loner builds). → [[Sound Selection Guide]]

### Flute — Kontakt "Ventus Ethnic Winds" (ocarina), 4-stage FX chain
- **A whole written counter-melody trimmed down to one closing run, once the chord progression it inspired was already locked in.** The flute originally carried the full counter-melody the chords were built around, but most of it got cut, leaving only a short run at the end — the counter-melody's job (giving the chords direction) was already done by the time it was written, so the majority of it wasn't needed as an audible part. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Kontakt 7]]
- **4-stage effects chain built specifically to make a soft ocarina flute "sound completely different": Reversed Rhythm reverb (textured) → medium vintage verb → half-time Cassette on the Crushed preset → EQ (cut low + some mids) → Portal's "Dreamy Music Box" preset at ~50% dry/wet.** The Cassette Crushed preset on a soft source is called a repeat favorite: "I love to put this on these types of sound — when you have a soft sound and you add this to it, it gives a nice little bit of crunch to it without doing too much and sounding too weird." → [[Kontakt 7]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]], [[Cassette]], [[Portal]], [[EQ]], [[Reverb]]

### Bell synth — one-shot kit, delay + spring reverb
- **A bell one-shot from an unnamed sample kit (auto-captioned "5 and xyz one shot kit" — vendor unconfirmed, matches an existing "5ive and XYZ" garble already logged from a separate loner video), run through a pretty heavy delay and a medium-sized spring reverb.** Combines a real-instrument palette (flute, violin, piano) with a synthy one-shot element specifically to mix organic and electronic textures in the same beat. → [[ValhallaDelay]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

### Violin — free Pianobook Kontakt bank, heavy reverb + forced-mono panning
- **A free violin Kontakt bank from pianobook.co.uk** — a second confirmed use of Piano Book as a repeat free-library source on this vault (see [[Kontakt 7]] for other Piano Book patches). "It's from pianobook.co.uk... it's super fire for free banks, you don't have to pay anything for it." → [[Kontakt 7]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **EQ cut to drown/darken the tone before the reverb, not after — an unresolved plugin name ("good hurts low high" in auto-captions, vendor/product not confidently identified).** "I did a [...] low/high, just to cut some high end out and kind of drown the sound out a bit" — a high-end cut used deliberately to soften/bury the violin's presence, then a very heavy [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] pushes it further back. *(plugin name flagged, not guessed — see [[Plugin Wishlist]] if it resolves)* → [[EQ]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]], [[Reverb]]
- **Force a stereo instrument almost fully into mono, then pan it hard right, specifically to carve it its own pocket in a busy mix.** "I found when it was stereo it was filling too much space, and I wanted to have its own little pocket." A clean, reusable mono+hard-pan move for any wide-by-default sampled instrument competing for room with other melodic layers — played here as a simple counter-melody line. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Stereo Field]], [[Melody Writing]]

### Texture perc loop + water-stream SFX — restraint as the Frank Ocean signature
- **A perc loop chopped for rhythmic gaps, then narrowed with a bandpass EQ and a medium reverb — kept deliberately subtle, and named directly as "the key to Frank Ocean's beats."** Source: an unnamed pack, auto-captioned "one of only one's kits" (matches this vault's already-tracked "Only Ones — Balenciaga Volume 8" pack — a third confirmed use of that source, after a percussion-loop use and a hi-hat use on two other builds). Stated philosophy: "it's very subtle, but I feel like that's the key to Frank Ocean's beats — they're all very simple, some of them are literally just one instrument and him singing over the top." → [[EQ]], [[Reverb]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A water-stream sound effect, volume pulled way down and low end cut, purely for atmosphere — "pointless having it in there" at full level/full-range.** A minimal-footprint field-recording-style texture layer: quiet enough to be felt rather than heard, with the low end removed since it wasn't doing anything useful in that register. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[EQ]]

### Bass — MODO ("Moto") Bass + AmpliTube's underwater preset
- **A muted, finger-plucked bass patch (MODO Bass, heard/captioned "Moto Bass" — the same near-certain garble already resolved elsewhere on this vault) run through AmpliTube's "underwater" amp-sim preset, which supplies both the bass's tone AND its reverb character in one preset.** "I did an AmpliTube, I used the underwater bass preset — it sounded really good and fit this vibe perfectly... you can hear how much of a difference that reverb on the bass adds, it gives it a super unique sound." Finished with a plain EQ cut for muddiness. Reads as a genre-matched one-preset shortcut (amp-sim + built-in space) rather than a multi-device bass chain. → [[Bass Design]], [[EQ]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

### Piano — "Postcard Piano" (Teletone Audio), separated from the Rhodes with EQ
- **Postcard Piano (Teletone Audio) sourced from an Instagram ad — a second confirmed use of this library on this vault (previously logged as a chord-progression source on a different build).** "This actually came up as an ad on my Instagram and I thought, oh, this sounds really fire, so I downloaded it... it doesn't sound too clean, it's got some character to it — you can mess around with the age so it detunes it, you can turn it mono or stereo, you can add noise, pedal noise, distortion, stuff like that." → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **A register/EQ separation move made explicit: cut low AND high end on the piano specifically to keep it out of the Rhodes/Lounge Lizard chord layer's way.** "I wanted to separate it from the Roads [Rhodes/Lounge Lizard chord bed], so I added this EQ cutting the low end and cut some high end as well" — followed by a valve compressor (owned-lane best-match [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII]]) "just to tighten the sound up a bit," then a vintage verb and a closing [[ValhallaDelay]]. Five-stage chain: EQ → valve compressor → [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] → [[ValhallaDelay]]. → [[EQ]], [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]], [[ValhallaDelay]], [[Layering and Stacking]]

### Drums — minimalistic, textural, built for restraint
- **Rim shot pitched down two semitones, then run through a delay deliberately tuned to sound bit-crushed: wet down, level down, a low-pass filter with the cutoff brought down (so the repeats lose their highs), and two further knobs pulled down described as affecting "the sample rate," for an almost-bit-crushed repeat character.** *(exact device not confirmed — likely built into the delay plugin itself rather than a separate crusher; described qualitatively per the auto-caption transcript rather than guessed)* → [[Delay]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **A stick sound layered underneath the snare/rim, barely audible on its own, purely to add depth.** "You can hardly even hear that, but it just adds a little bit more depth to the snare." A near-inaudible layering move — presence, not a heard element. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Snare Design]]
- **Two kicks layered, the second deliberately much lighter and more textural than the first** — rather than one kick doing both jobs (impact + character), split them across two samples. → [[Kick Design]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **A very light hi-hat whose end-of-pattern roll is deliberately off-grid: set to 1/3-step subdivisions so the roll reads "a bit off and a bit weird," finished with a fruity phaser (FL Studio's stock phaser, owned-lane best-match [[Phaser-Flanger]]) for extra texture.** A small, low-effort hi-hat texture move — an odd subdivision plus a phaser — rather than a full sound-design pass. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Phaser-Flanger]], [[Modulation]]

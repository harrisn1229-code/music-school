# You Suck at Mixing No. 9: EQing Before or After Compression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GDnfm7e_vWU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GDnfm7e_vWU)

## Synopsis
A tight demonstration of why chain order between EQ and compression matters, using both a drum example and a vocal example to show the two opposite answers. On drums, EQ-then-compress means every later tone tweak (like boosting the kick) forces the compressor to react differently and undoes the carefully dialed attack/release/ratio/threshold — so compress first, then EQ the settled dynamics. On vocals, the opposite applies when the source has non-musical content (breath, mic bumps, rumble) that would throw off the compressor's detector — that has to be EQ'd out *before* compression so the compressor only reacts to the actual sound.

## Techniques
- **Default order: compress first, EQ after — so tone tweaks don't disturb the compressor** — EQ-before-compression means any later EQ boost (e.g. making the kick louder) forces the compressor to react harder to that band, undoing carefully dialed-in attack/release/ratio/threshold settings and requiring readjustment. Compressing first and EQing after means the compressor's behavior stays locked in no matter what tone-shaping happens downstream — dial in dynamics once, then shape tone freely. → [[Compression]], [[EQ]]
- **Drum compressor starting point: slow attack, fast release, gentle ratio** — slow attack so the compressor doesn't clamp the transient at the start of each kick/snare hit; fast release so gain reduction doesn't linger after the hit has passed; a lower ratio for gentler, more musical compression; lower the threshold until you see meaningful gain reduction, then compensate with makeup gain. → [[Compression]]
- **Exception: EQ first when the source carries non-musical content the compressor shouldn't react to** — on a vocal, checking the EQ's analyzer can reveal a low-end rumble that isn't actually part of the voice (breath hitting the mic, mic-stand bumps). That content should be cut *before* compression, otherwise the compressor's detector reacts to noise that was never meant to be part of the performance, throwing off how it responds to the actual signal. → [[EQ]], [[Compression]], [[Gain Staging]]

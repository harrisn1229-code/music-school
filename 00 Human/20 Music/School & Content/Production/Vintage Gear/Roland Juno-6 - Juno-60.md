# Roland Juno-6 / Juno-60

## What it is
The original Junos — Roland's first affordable polysynths and the [[Roland Juno-106]]'s slightly wilder parents. Same one-DCO-plus-chorus recipe, but with an **arpeggiator**, punchier envelopes, and a filter many players swear is sweeter than the 106's. The Juno-60 (patch memory, DCB interface) is the studio darling that never went out of style.

## What makes the sound
- **Same DNA as the 106**: rock-stable DCO, sub-osc slider, PWM, the BBD chorus that turns one oscillator into an ensemble — see [[Roland Juno-106]] for the full anatomy.
- **What's different and why people care:** a hair more filter character (earlier IR3109 voicing driven harder), snappier envelopes, and the **onboard arpeggiator** — the up/down octave arp through that chorus is its own genre sound.
- No MIDI (Juno-6 has nothing, Juno-60 has DCB — modern retrofits abound) and no velocity: everything lands at one confident dynamic, which reads as *committed* in a track.
- The 60's patch memory made it the giggable one; the 6's pure-panel immediacy makes it the jam machine.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]] (Juno-family emulation — covers this sound; add Ableton's arpeggiator MIDI effect for the 6/60 arp), [[TAL-Chorus-LX]] (the chorus, free), [[Analog]]/[[Drift]] for stock approximations.
- **Paid:** Arturia Jun-6 V (models this generation specifically), TAL-U-NO-LX, Roland Cloud Juno-60.
- **Free:** [[TAL-Chorus-LX]]; TAL-U-NO-62.

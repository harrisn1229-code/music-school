# You Suck at Producing #49: Perspective & Objectivity

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6eSEW1HTcR8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6eSEW1HTcR8)

## Synopsis

A workflow/mindset video with five habits for maintaining objectivity on a track you've been staring at too long: taking breaks (the first listen back after time away is the most diagnostically useful), working a little every day so momentum doesn't die on an unfinished song, always checking a section against the full song rather than in isolation, not over-soloing individual tracks (the listener only ever hears the mix as a whole), periodically switching listening environments to reset perspective, and — for those with budget — hiring outside mixing/mastering engineers for a genuinely fresh, trained ear. Light on production technique but solid, reusable workflow discipline that complements the existing referencing/translation and commitment-discipline material in the vault.

## Techniques

- **Finishing is a practiced skill — work a little every day rather than leaving a song unfinished for long stretches** — momentum on an in-progress track dies fast; brief daily work keeps it warm, and the act of deciding something is done and shipping it is itself a skill that atrophies without repetition. One finished song beats many unfinished, more "ambitious" ones. → [[Referencing and Translation]]
- **Take breaks specifically to catch errors ear-fatigue had normalized** — the first listen after time away is often the most informative, because it surfaces problems repeated listening had quietly stopped registering; fix them the moment they resurface, since more listening erodes that fresh perspective again.
- **Always check a section against the full song, not just in isolation** — periodically play the whole track from the top while working on one section, to confirm it still serves the composition rather than just sounding good on its own.
- **Don't over-solo individual tracks** — time spent polishing a detail that gets buried in the full mix is wasted, since the listener only ever hears the mix as a whole and has no access to (or interest in) the isolated tracks.
- **Switch listening environments periodically to reset perspective, on top of (not instead of) sticking to 1–2 known systems for critical decisions** — a deliberate change of environment after a long stretch on one system is often enough on its own to regain objectivity, and doubles as a translation check.

# A Song That Actually Uses Locrian

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6JBsOzOFaQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6JBsOzOFaQ)

## Synopsis

A viewer-question video hunting for genuine Locrian usage in pop music — hard mode, since Locrian's diminished (flat-5) tonic triad makes it inherently unstable and rarely usable as a real home key. Finds one solid partial example ("Army of Me," Björk) and rules out two viewer-submitted candidates ("Juice Box," The Strokes; "Seek and Destroy," Metallica) after close inspection.

## Techniques

- **"Army of Me" (Björk) — the closest thing to a real Locrian pop song, and HOW it manages to work is the actual technique.** The bassline is unambiguously C Locrian (states the ♭5 directly, G♭). The vocal melody, however, deliberately never touches the 5th degree at all — by omission, the vocal line alone is technically C Phrygian (Locrian minus its most dissonant note), not Locrian. Layered over the Locrian bassline, the Phrygian vocal reads as "taking on" the Locrian color from context, without the melody itself ever having to voice the unstable flat-5. **General technique extracted**: build a mode's harmonic identity in one voice (bass/harmony) while keeping a melodic voice on a "safer," more consonant subset of the same notes — lets a genuinely difficult mode's flavor come through without forcing every part to be equally dissonant. Also notable: the bass uses fast, disjunct, chaotic rhythm/leaps while the vocal uses slow, smooth, small intervals — a deliberate rhythmic/textural contrast reinforcing the harmonic one. Caveat: the chorus abandons Locrian entirely (bassline moves to a chromatic D→C, vocal touches a natural 5th and natural 2nd), so the song is Locrian in its verse only, not throughout.
- **A worked "is this really Locrian?" test, demonstrated on two rejected candidates — useful as a general false-positive check.** "Juice Box" (The Strokes): opening bassline alone is genuinely Locrian (flat-5 and flat-2 both present), but once the guitar and vocal enter, both repeatedly state the NATURAL 5th and natural 2nd, actively fighting the Locrian color from the bass — verdict: Locrian is "an element," present and audible as an undercurrent dissonance, but not extensive enough to call the song Locrian overall. "Seek and Destroy" (Metallica): the very first bar (♭5 and ♭2 present) is genuinely A Locrian, but the next bar reintroduces a natural 5th, which is disqualifying for Locrian by definition — reclassified instead as E Phrygian (Locrian without the flat-5) with the flat-5 note better explained as a passing blues-style chromatic inflection than as a real modal commitment. **The test, generalized**: find the flat-5 AND check whether the natural 5th shows up anywhere nearby in another voice/bar — if it does, the mode isn't genuinely Locrian even if an isolated bar or line technically qualifies.

# JAZ-C21 — The characteristic note has to be sounded

**Q.** Give the characteristic note of Dorian, Lydian, Mixolydian and Phrygian, and say what happens if you avoid it.

> [!question]- Answer
> **Dorian — the natural 6** (against natural minor) · **Lydian — the ♯4** (against major) · **Mixolydian — the ♭7** (against major) · **Phrygian — the ♭2** (against natural minor).
>
> Land on it and the mode is established; avoid it and the music collapses into plain major or minor. In D Dorian the note is B: hit B and it is Dorian, dodge B and it is D minor.

## Why it matters

**A loop that never plays the ♯4 is not Lydian, it is major with a Lydian label.** The colour has to be sounded, not declared — which is the single commonest way a modal idea fails to arrive in a finished track.

The advanced case is the same rule applied in miniature: in a short vamp of several chords that are not functionally connected, treat each chord as its own **modal island** with its own scale, rather than hunting for one key that explains the whole progression.

## Drill

Loop one chord and improvise over it for a minute without ever playing the characteristic note, then again while landing on it deliberately. Name the mode you actually hear in each take.

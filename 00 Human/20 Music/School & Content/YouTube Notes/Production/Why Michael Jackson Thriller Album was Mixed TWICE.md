# Why Michael Jackson Thriller Album was Mixed TWICE

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jLVXmS3UzTA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jLVXmS3UzTA)

## Synopsis

Debunks the "Billie Jean was mixed 90+ times" myth with the math (the studio time available doesn't support it) and instead describes the real practice: comparing numbered mix passes against each other, sometimes landing on an earlier pass rather than the latest one. The video's real payoff is the story of *Thriller*'s mastering-stage discovery — engineer Bruce Swedien heard a problem in the finished mix only once he'd had distance from it at mastering, after weeks of round-the-clock work finishing the album.

## Techniques

- **Compare numbered mix passes against each other rather than assuming the latest is best.** After completing several full mixes, going back to listen to an earlier pass (mix #2, in the story) rather than the most recent one (mix #6) is a real, deliberate A/B practice — a later mix isn't automatically the better one, and it's worth re-auditioning earlier passes before committing.
- **Distance from a mix reveals problems that working inside it hides.** The engineer heard an issue with the final mix only once he'd had enough time away from it by the time he reached mastering — not while still deep in the mix session. A concrete case for building in a break before final mix judgment, since extended time on one mix erodes the objectivity needed to catch what's actually wrong with it.

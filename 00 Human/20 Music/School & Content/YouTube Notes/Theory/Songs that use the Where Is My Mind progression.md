# Songs that use the Where Is My Mind progression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ttJlMFsL2ro)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ttJlMFsL2ro)

## Synopsis

Names and analyzes a genuinely rare four-chord loop — I–vi–III(major)–IV — best known from the Pixies' "Where Is My Mind?," including the secondary-dominant/deceptive-cadence mechanism that gives the loop its perpetual, never-quite-resolving character, and a very recent (same-year) new example.

## Techniques

- **The loop**: I–vi–III(major)–IV. The non-diatonic chord is the III — a **major** chord built on the 3rd degree (in C: E major, containing G♯, not the diatonic E minor/G natural) — functioning as a **secondary dominant** (V of vi, i.e., pointing at the vi chord) that Bennett notates as "5 of 6."
- **Deceptive/interrupted cadence, and why it's what makes the loop perpetual**: a secondary dominant doesn't have to resolve to its own implied tonic — here, instead of resolving E→Am (the expected "perfect cadence" completion), the progression instead goes E→IV, an **interrupted/deceptive cadence**. Because that "expected" resolution never lands, the tension never fully discharges, so the loop keeps wanting to continue rather than sounding "done" the way a completed E→Am cadence would. The chain that follows (IV→I is a plagal cadence, closing the loop back to the top) reinforces the same "keeps circling, never truly resting" quality.
- **"Actually Romantic" (Taylor Swift, released the same year as this video)** — same chord loop, same guitar-based rhythmic feel, similar tempo, AND the same key (E major) as "Where Is My Mind?" — close enough on every axis that Bennett states he "wouldn't be surprised" if it was directly inspired by the Pixies track, while reiterating the now-familiar caveat that borrowing a progression (even a rare one, even in the same key) doesn't constitute plagiarism.
- **Other examples of the same rare loop**: "Piledriver Waltz" (Arctic Monkeys) — also notable for its own separate rhythmic quirk: reads as approximately 3/4 (waltz time, matching the title) but is actually in **6/4** (functionally 4/4 + 2/4), a distinction Bennett cross-references to a separate video of his on 6/4 vs. 3/4 time.

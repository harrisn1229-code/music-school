# Vocoder

**Category:** Pitch/Vocal
**Vendor:** Ableton

## What it is

Ableton's stock vocoder — a bank of tuned band-pass filters ("bands") that impose one signal's spectral envelope (the **Modulator** — internal noise, or an External carrier's audio) onto another, with Formant, Bandwidth, Depth, Release, and per-band Range controls, plus a **Carrier** section that can add a pitch-tracking oscillator underneath whatever's playing.

## What it's used for

Far more than the classic "robot voice": a genuinely versatile texture and cleanup tool — subtle enhancement/de-washing on an already-decent sound, a pitch-tracking color oscillator, noise-into-texture design on drums/cymbals, external-carrier chord-driven "watery" pads and rhythms, and narrow-bandwidth "bloopy" modulator-mode sound design. The routing recipe (mute-bus send as carrier/modulator input) is what unlocks the chord-driven and drum-bus uses below.

## FX chain notes

Insert directly on the signal being shaped (drums, a texture layer, a synth pad); for the chord-driven "watery" and constant-level-pad uses, route a **pre-fader send to a muted bus** as the External carrier/modulator input rather than trying to side-chain a track that's also being heard normally — see the routing recipe below.

## Techniques

- **Vocoder as a chorus-uplift color on one lead vocalist's chorus, while the OTHER lead vocalist gets an octave-up verbed vocal for the same job — a deliberate different-color-per-voice choice, not a fixed recipe reused across both.** On Normani's chorus in "Dancing With A Stranger": "they added a vocoder, same kind of vibe verbed out, nothing major, just to uplift the chorus slightly" — paired with heavy reverb, run at a subtle level rather than a full robot-voice effect. Sam Smith's chorus gets [[ValhallaRoom]] on an octave-doubled vocal instead — "it's just a different color for the chorus." *(qualitative only — no band count/formant/carrier settings given)* → [[ValhallaRoom]], [[Modulation]] — Kevin Davis (Mix with the Masters), [Mixing Vocals from 'Dancing With A Stranger' with Kevin Davis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bQIZONdZvk8)
- **One pass in a stack of "crazy" bridge-vocal texture effects, not the whole effect on its own.** Building a deliberately dense bridge vocal (distortion → delay/reverb → vocoder), a vocoder pass is added as the last layer specifically to push the vocal further into "pretty crazy" territory alongside the distortion and space effects already stacked on it — used here as one texture-thickening stage in a chain, not the featured effect. *(qualitative only — no band count/formant/carrier settings given)* → [[1 Main Vocal Track]], [[Saturn 2]] — Mix with the Masters (Benny Blanco & Selena Gomez), [Behind the Track｜'Younger And Hotter Than Me' by Selena Gomez & benny blanco](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rkJ-LaoArUY)
- **A Moog Sub 37 (or any owned analog-modeled synth) as the vocoder's carrier, driven by a spoken-word vocal instead of a sung take** — a technique seen in a Mike Dean session clip: record a spoken (not sung) vocal and feed it into the vocoder as the Modulator, with the synth as the carrier/tone source — for a robotic-but-musical lead texture built from speech rather than melody. No exact band count/formant/depth settings given (described qualitatively). → [[Pitch and Formant]] — loner, [mike dean's secret 'astroworld' synth techniques!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bJGXDlSFdY)
- **Vocoder as a standalone character effect on an instrument, not a vocal-morphing tool** — dropped straight onto a bass (no second vocal/audio source involved at all) purely for the robotic/formant coloring it adds — reads as a "Daft Punk"-adjacent texture. Adjust **Formant** for extra character, and keep **Carrier** set to its default Modulator/internal-oscillator mode rather than Noise — switching Carrier to Noise on a non-vocal source produces a harsh, unusable result. → [[Bass Design]] — You Suck at Producing (Underbelly ft. Pixelwax), [Sound Design walkthrough of my new song with Pixelwax!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IOc-lDT6_ZA)
- **Vocoder on pure white noise (no vocal at all) as an evolving atmosphere generator** — feed white noise into Vocoder for a smooth, textured atmospheric bed rather than the usual vocal-processing role: lower **Release** for a smoother, less choppy result; turn **Depth** down for a different textural flavor; raise **Attack** toward its maximum for the smoothest, most "sweet"/soothing version (turn it back down to bring the crackly character back); increase the **band count** (e.g. 8) for a smoother overall tone, or reduce it for more grit; adjust **Formant** to shift the atmosphere's perceived pitch/tonal center; use the width controls to make the result narrow or wide. Layer a light [[Vinyl Distortion]] crackle on top for extra texture, and map an **LFO to Attack** so the atmosphere's smoothness/character continuously evolves over time rather than sitting static. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Vinyl Distortion]] — You Suck at Producing, [Creating Atmospheres Using White Noise](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tGmpcPL3Sk)
- **Chain TWO vocoders: one for texture, a second (External carrier) to impose real pitched notes on an otherwise-unpitched vocal growl** — first warp a vocal's timing/pitch aggressively (drag its warp markers) and run it through OTT plus a first Vocoder (internal/Noise carrier) purely for a menacing, bass-adjacent texture and tone — at this stage the source is still just spoken syllables, not actual musical notes. Add a SECOND Vocoder after it, with Carrier switched to **External**, fed from a separately-played, properly pitched bass instrument — this second vocoder imposes that bass part's real note-to-note pitch onto the already-textured vocal signal, turning spoken/growled syllables into a genuinely playable, in-tune "vocal bass" that follows whatever notes the external carrier track plays. Turn down Dry/Wet on the second vocoder to taste. → [[Bass Design]] — You Suck at Producing, [Warping Vocals & Turning Them Into Instruments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sXp_cHMXhVM)
- **External-carrier vocoding to morph a vocal with a bass, instead of the default noise carrier** — switch the Carrier source from Noise to **External** and route in a second track's audio (a bass loop) as the carrier signal — the vocal now carries the tonal/harmonic character of the bass instead of a vocoder-typical robotic buzz. Mute the carrier source track while tuning so only its effect on the vocal is audible. Trade detail against character with the **band count** (more bands = more resolution/intelligibility, fewer = grittier/more processed), use **Enhance** to add back top-end clarity the vocoding process removes, and finish with [[OTT]] to glue the result and bring the low end back up. → [[OTT]] — You Suck at Producing, [How To Morph One Sound Into Another (4 techniques)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jdXhjfQhk3Y)
- **Modulator-mode cleanup — Depth/Release/Range as a subtle de-washing/brightening pass** — on a sound that reads a little washed-out, a light compression pass plus careful Vocoder settings (brightness/"the bands" pushed up, Depth adjusted for a wetter or drier result, Release and the band Range tweaked) can clean the sound up and add clarity rather than an obvious vocoded effect — an overlooked, subtle use of the device. — dnksaus, [Why Vocoder is Ableton's Most Versatile Device!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v6tJJ2Y8Eos)
- **Pitch-tracking Carrier oscillator adds a coloring layer under any source** — an easily-overlooked feature: enabling Vocoder's built-in pitch-tracking oscillator adds a synthesized tone that follows whatever's playing, without needing a separate doubling/unison instrument; adjust its Range to taste for extra flavor/color underneath the source. — dnksaus, [Why Vocoder is Ableton's Most Versatile Device!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v6tJJ2Y8Eos)
- **Vocode noise/cymbals/hats into textured "ride"-type character; pair with a high/low splitter for per-band snare use** — on hats or noise, turn on Vocoder, raise Formant, and dial Release so the result reads more like a ride cymbal than raw noise; adjust Depth and Brightness, and use the built-in **XY pad to control the grain/noisiness** of the result — good for texturing sounds generally. On a snare specifically, run this through a high/low splitter first so the vocoding only touches (or "polarizes") one band rather than the whole hit. → [[Snare Design]] — dnksaus, [Why Vocoder is Ableton's Most Versatile Device!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v6tJJ2Y8Eos)
- **External-carrier chord-driven "watery rhythm" — band count controls harmonic smoothness** — set Vocoder to **External**, feed it audio from whatever's playing chords (voice, a synth, a "dirty" bass — anything with harmonic movement), and adjust Release for a watery result; the **number of active bands** directly affects how harmonic/smooth vs. narrow/notchy the result sounds — framed as a way to build a "colorful watery rhythm" element rather than a literal vocoded voice. — dnksaus, [Why Vocoder is Ableton's Most Versatile Device!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v6tJJ2Y8Eos)
- **Narrow-Bandwidth modulator-mode "bloopy swoop" sound design** — back in Modulator mode, turn **Bandwidth** down: think of Vocoder as a bank of band-pass filters, and a narrow Bandwidth turns each band into an almost notch-filter-like, narrow resonant slice. More bands = a more harmonic-sounding result; the per-band **Range** controls are also worth adjusting individually. Modulating **Formant** on top gives "bloopy swoop" sweeps — his own workflow is to sit and play with it for ~5 minutes in a dedicated sound-design session until something cool emerges, rather than dialing in a target sound analytically. — dnksaus, [Why Vocoder is Ableton's Most Versatile Device!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v6tJJ2Y8Eos)
- **Textural "creepy noise" pad, External carrier, Range pulled down for calm** — on a noisy/textural sample chain, set Vocoder to External, feed it a chord-stack/noisy carrier signal (compressed/leveled and time-aligned), and **reduce Range** for a calmer, more pad-like result instead of an aggressive vocoded sweep; further EQ/band-range tweaks refine the texture from there. → [[Spectral Resonator]] — dnksaus, [Color Bass and Future Sound Design in 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Izo8-oYXvOQ)
- **Scale-aware texture pass: Bandwidth down, Depth down, Level up, Range explored, for a "watery" character** — the same narrow-Bandwidth logic as above, applied inside a larger scale-aware generative chain (a pushed Vinyl Distortion noise source into Vocoder): Bandwidth to its lowest, Depth brought down, Level brought up, and Range explored by ear — reads as "watery." → [[Randomized Rack Design]] — dnksaus, [Scale-Aware Abuse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NZyKN5Wn7A0)
- **The reusable chord-carrier routing recipe: pre-fader mute-bus send as the side-input** — on the source track (a vocal, a drum bus), add a **pre-fader** send to a bus (so its level is constant, independent of the fader), **mute that bus** (so you don't hear the raw send directly), then set Vocoder's **sidechain/side-input to that bus** and play chords into the vocoder synth. The source becomes the modulator, the chords become the carrier — the mechanism behind every "vocode this to chords" technique. Works equally on a **whole drum bus** for added tonality/texture, not just vocals. → [[Modulation]], [[Pitch and Formant]] — Disclosure, [Who Knew](https://youtu.be/e9d7KYEG3VM) & [Howard Is Here + Douha (Mali Mali) Breakdown](https://youtu.be/gCfU5Cq9WU4)
- **Constant-level pad: compress the vocoder output hard so the real vocal carries the dynamics** — send the lead to a muted bus (the routing recipe above) as the sidechain input, play block chords on a synth carrier (e.g. a legato Poly6-style patch), then **compress the vocoder's output hard** so it reads as a constant pad rather than a dynamic performance — the real, uncompressed lead vocal on top is what actually carries the song's dynamics and feel. Chained further with overdrive, an H-delay, Valhalla reverb, chorus, and a rotary-cabinet effect in the source breakdown. → [[Modulation]] — Disclosure, [Howard Is Here + Douha (Mali Mali) Breakdown](https://youtu.be/gCfU5Cq9WU4)
- **A detuned sawtooth-LFO carrier for a chord bed the singer "speaks through"** — a vocoder-synth carrier riding a sawtooth LFO that goes slightly out of tune on each pass (8th- or 16th-note rate), with block chords "spoken"/held into the vocoder (a vocoder needs held notes, not fast phrasing); bounce both an 8th-note and a more extreme 16th-note LFO version and pick/mix which plays when. → [[Modulation]] — Disclosure, [134bpm Halftime Vibe Beat Jam](https://youtu.be/aMvwdplJi_Y)
- **Save a custom default preset so a freshly-loaded (or randomly-spawned) Vocoder starts where you want** — Vocoder's factory default preset is a plain Noise patch; saving your own default (e.g. Modulator mode instead) means every new instance — including one spawned by a device randomizer — opens on your preferred starting point instead of the factory default. → [[Randomized Rack Design]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — dnksaus, [Randomly Insert Plugins (Live 12.3)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9qzAiUUHveQ)
- **Vocode a too-weird part to unify it with the mix** — a swung, oddly-timbred section read as wrong until a vocoder pass unified its timbre and a sidechain locked its rhythm to the groove — a "glue" use for vocoding rather than a lead sound-design move. → [[Sidechaining]] — Disclosure, [ENERGY Album Release Party](https://youtu.be/fMqPuf1UVzI)
- **A dedicated "messed-up" preset rack exists but is deliberately unrefined** — a rack version described as "not really a macro kind of rack, more of a put-it-on-and-let-it-do-messed-up-stuff-for-you" rack — a quick creative-chaos starting point rather than a tuned, parameter-mapped tool. → [[Plugin Wishlist]] (Dank Sauces entry) — dnksaus, [Instrument Racks + Dank Sauces Updates](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kd16qh8_h4g)

## Vintage reference

A **Roland VP-330 Vocoder Plus** is the historical hardware this device family descends from — used to run a whole vocal through for a noisy/lush vocoded-chord layer alongside the real vocal (chain: top-boost + roll-off → Maag EQ4 → gem Dopamine → distorting H-delay ping-pong → EMT 140 plate, sidechained). Not yet documented in [[Vintage Gear Index]] — already queued unchecked in [[Vintage Gear Worklist]]. → Disclosure, [Ultimatum Breakdown + Breakbeat](https://youtu.be/uWe3UvI4KlU)

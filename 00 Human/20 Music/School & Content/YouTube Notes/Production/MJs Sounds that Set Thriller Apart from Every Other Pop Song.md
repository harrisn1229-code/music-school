## Synopsis

The "specialty" sounds of Thriller — pipe organ, theremin, wind, and background counter-lines — performed live. The most useful new content is a real performance-compensation technique for playing a single-sample (non-multisampled) instrument, and an organ-registration-style build for stacking a pad section.

## Techniques

- **A single-sample (non-multisampled) instrument's pitch is tied to its playback SPEED, tape-machine style — lower notes play back slower/"bloom" later, higher notes play back faster.** With one sample stretched across the keyboard (rather than a note-accurate multisample), a low note doesn't speak as promptly as a high one; the performer has to play lower notes measurably earlier (ahead of the beat) to compensate, timing the part by ear against where the sample's onset actually lands rather than treating all notes as equally responsive.
- **Organ-registration-style layer build for a pad section**: add flute stops first, then reed stops, then a "full stops" combination, then bass pedals last — deliberately built in that specific order over a song section (each layer entering as the previous holds), mirroring how a real pipe organist stacks registration stops rather than triggering one static combined patch.
- **True melodic counterpoint used sparingly and repetitively so it doesn't compete with the vocal** — a moving countermelody line is deliberately simple/repeating across each chorus occurrence, which the source frames as what lets it "lift" the arrangement without interfering, since its complexity is absorbed by repetition rather than novelty.
- **A one-off sample layer used exactly once, in the intro only** (a Synclavier sample blending a guitar-like source with something else, unidentified) — reconfirms the established "special sound used exactly once, never repeated" principle already documented from the monster growl bass video.

# Skills Intermediate Guitarists Should Have

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PM8VI153SZ8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PM8VI153SZ8)

## Synopsis

A 13-skill checklist positioned as a self-assessment benchmark ("if you can't do these, you're not yet intermediate") rather than a deep-dive lesson — muting (both hands), raking, bending (several sub-skills), sliding, hammer-ons/pull-offs, trills, tapping, bar chords, tremolo picking, fingerpicking, and simple-harmony improvisation. Most of the individual techniques are already documented elsewhere in the vault at greater depth; the genuinely new mechanical details are extracted below.

## Techniques

- **Bending grip and motion: the Hendrix-style thumb-over-the-neck grip**, held the way you'd hold a baseball, is named as the most comfortable/stable bending grip. **Bending motion**: rotate/curl the fingers into the bend (described as a wrist-and-finger rotation) rather than the common intermediate mistake of pushing the string with a straighter, less leveraged finger motion — the rotational motion is described as noticeably easier once tried.
- **Unison bend vs. double-stop bend — a terminology nuance.** A unison bend (play two notes, bend the lower one up to match the pitch of the higher one) is technically a specific case of a double-stop bend (any bend played while a second note sounds simultaneously) — all unison bends are double-stop bends, but not all double-stop bends are unison bends (the analogy given: all thumbs are fingers, not all fingers are thumbes). Worth knowing since the two terms get used interchangeably by different teachers.
- **Bending failure modes, named directly:** the fretting finger slipping underneath the string instead of staying on top (causes unwanted noise); adjacent strings getting dragged along and released with the bend; using only one finger instead of multiple stacked fingers for support. *(These mechanics — support, stopping cleanly, intonation, string-hooking — are already documented in depth in [[String Bending Technique]]; this video's grip/motion tip is genuinely additive and was routed there.)*
- **Hammer-on/pull-off finger mechanics**: use the fingertip, not a flat finger, for a hammer-on (a flat-finger hammer-on reads as weak); a pull-off needs an active "flick" pulling the string slightly as the finger lifts, not a passive vertical lift-off — a passive lift produces a weak or silent pull-off.
- **Tremolo picking's actual difficulty threshold**: fast picking on a single fixed string is the easy (intermediate) version; the technique only becomes genuinely difficult once you have to sustain the same speed *while switching strings* — the single-string version alone doesn't yet demonstrate mastery.
- **Sliding into a bend as a compound three-part technique**: pick a note, slide into a second note, then bend from that second note into a third — three linked movements treated as one phrase rather than three separate techniques.

# You Suck at Producing: Using Silence

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YtT-Lh9uv24)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YtT-Lh9uv24)

## Synopsis

A genuinely useful, concrete lesson on using true silence (not just low mix level) as a dynamic-contrast tool: a specific Ableton multi-track workflow for clearing a clean silent gap before a drop (Loop Bracket select-and-delete across all tracks), softening that silence with a small reversed "breath" swell so it reads as deliberate rather than a dropout, and — the more broadly useful idea — applying the same silence-for-contrast principle at a much smaller scale, deleting tiny gaps between individual chord hits in a drop to make each one land harder while incidentally creating pockets for ear-candy details.

## Techniques

- **Clear true silence across all tracks with the Loop Bracket, then soften it with a small "breath" swell** — select the region to silence, Cmd/Ctrl+L to jump the loop bracket there, click the bracket to select that range on every track, Delete to clear it — real silence, not just a quiet passage, maximizes the contrast into a drop. Total dead air reads as a mistake though, so drop in a short reversed-synth swell (an "inhale before speaking") plus one extra hit to make the pause feel deliberate. → [[Depth and Space]]
- **Delete small gaps between individual hits, not just around whole sections, to break up a "wall of noise"** — a drop or chord progression where every hit runs directly into the next reads as one undifferentiated stream; small gaps between individual chords increase each hit's perceived impact through contrast, and double as free space for ear-candy plucks/details that would otherwise have nowhere to sit. → [[Depth and Space]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]

# how to make ambient synth beats for brent faiyaz (r&b music theory trick!?)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ugup5iVzGYY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ugup5iVzGYY)

## Synopsis

A from-scratch dark/moody ambient R&B beat-building breakdown, styled after Brent Faiyaz's *Mercedes*, from loner (the channel behind most of the loner-sourced material already in this vault). The headline hook is a **named music-theory trick**: a deliberately minimal two-chord loop built from the **vi and v chords of a natural minor scale** (F♯maj7 → Fm in A♯ natural minor), leaning on how close together the two chords' notes sit to generate tension purely from voice-leading rather than a functional dominant resolution. Loner states the wider principle directly — **"a big key to Brent's sound is simplicity."** Beyond the chord trick, the video is a compact tour of his usual arrangement/sound-selection method: doubling the chords with a strummed pluck/pad hybrid, layering in progressively more textural elements (a perc loop, a live-feel bass with slides, a panned drum fill, a delayed/reversed-feeling synth melody, a time-stretched vocal-chop hook), then building a drum kit around velocity-varied hi-hats and a deliberately sparse, ambient-perc-layered snare pattern modeled on the reference track's own unusual snare spacing. Nothing here is deeply processed — most FX chains are 2–3 stages (an EQ cut, a reverb, a delay) — the density comes from stacking many small, simple layers rather than heavily processing any one of them, the same ethos already documented on [[Frank Ocean Chill Ambient Chain]].

## Techniques

- **[0:40] The theory trick — vi→v in natural minor.** Scale: A♯ natural minor. Chord 1 = **F♯ major 7** (the vi chord, i.e. the relative-major's tonic borrowed as a major-7). Chord 2 = **F minor** (the v chord — diatonically minor in natural/Aeolian minor, no raised leading tone). Loner's stated reasoning: the two chords' notes sit very close together (a semitone apart in two voices), which "helps create a lot of tension," and moving from a **major** chord to a **minor** chord "helps resolve into a bit of a darker sound." → routed to [[Chord Progressions]].
- **[0:55] Passing/top-line notes added on top of the two-chord loop** — "little extra passing notes here and here and here" — kept the harmony at two chords but added melodic motion above it rather than a third chord. → [[Melody Writing]].
- **[1:05] Chord chain: spring reverb (heavy but short) → EQ (cut lows, cut some highs).** Minimal processing — the whole "chord bed" sound is two devices.
- **[1:15] Doubling layer: Analog Lab (Aperture bank), "Pluck Heaven" preset** — described as "a mix of a pluck and a pad." Same chords as the main progression, played strummed rather than block-triggered, then EQ low-cut. A doubling part given its own character via preset choice + performance (strumming) rather than new harmony.
- **[1:35] Texture layer: Analog Lab (Aperture bank) perc loop**, added purely "to add this little bit of texture."
- **[1:45] Bass: a "technical bass" one-shot from the 5ive & XYZ kit**, simple root-driven pattern. **Technique: use live-bass ONE-SHOTS rather than Kontakt-bank multisamples** specifically because one-shots let you draw in pitch **slides** (not possible from a Kontakt bank), which reads more realistic; velocity is also easy to vary on a one-shot for extra realism. Motivated directly by "a lot of Brent songs have real basses."
- **[2:05] Textural drum fill, chopped out of a perc loop from the 5ive & XYZ kit**, run through a slow auto-pan so it moves side to side for stereo width.
- **[2:20] Melodic texture: a synth sound from the 5ive & XYZ kit**, a simple melody placed toward the end of the 4-bar loop. Chain: **Portal ("echoes" preset) → half-time (1-bar setting)**. The Portal preset delays the signal so the melody doesn't sound where it's triggered — it lands later, giving a reversed/echoey feel that reads almost like an accent/hit landing on beat one rather than a normal melodic phrase.
- **[3:05] Hook-section vocal chop: a loop called "Talk to Me" from the Layers kit ("my bundle")** — picked for a tempo already close to the track's, then time-stretched and pitch-fit into the song's key. Framed as another texture layer, not a lead vocal part.
- **[3:35] One more textural transition element — an unnamed "fill impact" sound** used purely to smooth the transition into the drums/drop.
- **[3:45] Hi-hats: two layers, both velocity-varied for realism/bounce.** Hat 1: straight pattern with **every-other-note velocity changed** specifically "to give it a bit of a more realistic sound and to give it more of a bounce." Hat 2: a second, staggered hi-hat with only a few notes placed here and there, layered under the first.
- **[4:15] Snare pattern modeled directly on the Brent Faiyaz reference track's own "kind of weird" snare** — rather than a dense/frequent snare hit, it's placed only **once per bar**. This deliberately creates rhythmic contrast against the fast hi-hat pattern (hats moving quickly, snare spaced out and slower).
- **[4:35] Snare layered with a separate ambient percussive sound (its own reverb added)** — recommended explicitly as a general fix: **"I'd really recommend layering your snares like this especially if you're finding it hard to get the snare to sit in the mix nicely."**
- **[4:50] Rest of the kit, in build order:** perc → rim shot → open hat → another perc → kick → sub bass (the sub was "designed... based on one of Brent's [sounds]," no further detail given).

# Maestro Rhythm King

## What it is
A late-60s preset rhythm box built for lounge organists — until Sly Stone put one on a #1 record and accidentally founded the drum-machine-as-groove tradition every 808 record descends from. Sly called his "the Funk Box."

## What makes the sound
- **All-analog preset voices**: resonant-circuit kick/bongos, noise-burst snare/brush/hats — soft, thumpy, cardboard-y; even less hi-fi than the [[Roland CR-78]], with a woolly warmth that sits behind vocals beautifully.
- **Preset rhythms combined/abused**: like the [[Korg Mini Pops]], pressing patterns together and riding the tempo slider turned accompaniment presets into hypnotic grooves — "Family Affair" is a preset pattern, essentially unedited, and it *grooves*.
- The dry, steady, unexcited pulse became an aesthetic: drum machine as deadpan timekeeper under deeply human playing (Rhodes, clav, bass) — the blueprint for [[Neo-Soul - Lo-fi]] fifty years early.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Impulse]] with Rhythm King samples; [[Cassette]] + [[RC-20 Retro Color]] for the woolly print; pair with real Rhodes ([[Keyscape]]) for the *There's a Riot* palette.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2; vintage archives.
- **Free:** Rhythm King sample sets circulate widely.

## Used on
- Sly & the Family Stone — "Family Affair" (1971): the first US #1 driven by a drum machine; the whole *There's a Riot Goin' On* album leans on the Funk Box.
- Shuggie Otis — *Freedom Flight* / *Inspiration Information* era ("Strawberry Letter 23"): Rhythm King as psychedelic-soul heartbeat.
- Genre/era: early-70s soul/funk experimentation — the origin point of programmed groove as a *feel* choice, not a budget compromise.

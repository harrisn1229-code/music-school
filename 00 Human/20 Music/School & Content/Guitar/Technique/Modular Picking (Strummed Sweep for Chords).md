# Modular Picking (Strummed Sweep for Chords)

No prior home in the vault — created 2026-08-23 from a BERNTH lesson. Registered via cross-links from [[Sweep Picking Arpeggio Shapes]] and [[Directional Alternate Picking System]].

## What it is

A sweep-picking-derived technique for playing **full chords** (not single-note arpeggios) very fast, built from small symmetrical **modules** — a repeating picking/fretting shape that mirrors itself, so each module can be split in half and each half is a reflection of the other. Distinct from plain strumming: the notes are played tight against a metronome subdivision (16th/32nd notes, or triplets) rather than loosely scraped, and distinct from a normal arpeggio-shape sweep because the fretted shapes are chord grips, held down like a barre, rather than single notes picked one at a time.

## Why it's not "just strumming"

The uploader's own framing, in response to viewer comments: it looks like sweep-picked full chords, but the difference from plain strumming is that the technique is played with real rhythmic precision — 16th or 32nd notes, or triplets — locked to a grid, not loose chord scraping. A chord voicing that consciously **overlaps notes and intervals** (rather than the cleanly-separated-note approach of a normal arpeggio sweep) is chosen specifically to support this — e.g. a simple Am7 shape barred at the 5th fret with the index finger, with the other fingers free to move around and extend the chord/arpeggio on top.

## Worked exercise 1 (F power chord + 9th, mixed subdivisions)

Chord: F power chord (F-C-F) plus the open G string added as a 9th. The picking hand lets the hand "fall" through the shape in one direction, plays the added G string, then reverses direction (upstrokes) into the next module, which restarts on F with a downstroke — producing a characteristic repeated-note feel at each module boundary. The exercise deliberately alternates between **32nd notes and 16th-note triplets** within the same pass — training both of the two most commonly needed rhythmic groupings (fours and threes) in one workout. The hardest part is staying precisely in time through the subdivision switch, not the picking motion itself.

## Worked exercise 2 (Cm(add9), 3-string moving modules)

Chord: Cm(add9). Picking pattern: groups of three (three downstrokes, three upstrokes) — but the tricky detail is that the 3-string module **moves** through the chord: first played on the E-A-D strings, then A-D-G, then D-G-B, then G-B-E — a moving-window version of the module rather than a fixed one.

## Practical application

Used for both **rhythm/chord work** and **lead/solo work** (arpeggios), with both a clean tone and a heavily distorted guitar-solo tone — not a niche-sound-only technique. Originated as an evolution of what the uploader initially called "strummed sweep picking" before settling on "modular picking" as a clearer name once the symmetrical-module structure became the actual teaching point.

# How The Beatles used Indian music theory

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y2GmTD9Q4iM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y2GmTD9Q4iM)

## Synopsis

Traces the Beatles' (mainly George Harrison's, with John Lennon's contribution) absorption of Indian classical music — how they discovered it (a chance encounter with Indian musicians on the set of *Help!* in 1965, leading George to Ravi Shankar's records), which core Indian concepts (raga, tala, drone-based harmony) they borrowed, and how faithfully vs. loosely each song adopts them. Full raga/tala theory extracted to its own reference page since it has no prior home in the vault.

## What's taught

See [[Raga and Tala (Indian Classical Music)]] for the full extracted theory (raga structure, drone harmony, tala/rhythm cycles) and the song-by-song breakdown (*Norwegian Wood*, *Love You To*, *Tomorrow Never Knows*, *Within You Without You*, *The Inner Light*, *Blue Jay Way*, *If I Needed Someone*, *Strawberry Fields Forever*, *Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds*, *Here Comes the Sun*) — that page is the master record for this video's content, since the underlying theory genuinely had no existing home in the vault and is reusable well beyond this one video.

Discovery story specific to this video: George Harrison first encountered Indian musicians on the set of the Beatles' film *Help!* (1965), playing background music for a restaurant scene — he picked up a sitar out of curiosity. Repeated encounters with Ravi Shankar's name (including via David Crosby of The Byrds) led him to buy a Shankar record, which "hit a certain spot" he described as strangely familiar. That encounter is the direct origin of the sitar's first appearance on a Western pop record (*Norwegian Wood*) and everything that followed.

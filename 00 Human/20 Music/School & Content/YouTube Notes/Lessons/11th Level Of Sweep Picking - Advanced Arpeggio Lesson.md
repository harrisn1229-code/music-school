# 11th Level Of Sweep Picking? - Advanced Arpeggio Lesson

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IfH6VvHiCKQ)

## Synopsis

A follow-up to BERNTH's "10 Levels of Sweep Picking" video (not in the vault): rather than just going faster, "level 11" is composing genuinely musical, chord-tone-accurate arpeggio shapes over a real chord progression in D harmonic minor (Dminmaj7 – Gm9 – Faug – A7), including a jazz-style tritone-adjacent chord substitution.

## What's taught

- **Chord progression worked:** Dminmaj7 (tonic, carries harmonic minor's characteristic raised 7th/major 7th) → Gm9 (iv, also called Gm7add9) → Faug (two stacked major 3rds, deliberately dissonant) → A7 (V, creates tension resolving back to i).
- **Start each arpeggio on the chord's most characteristic/colorful note, not the root** — for the Dminmaj7 arpeggio, lead with C♯ (the major 7th), which is also harmonic minor's defining raised-7th degree; demonstrated as the note that gives the scale its "exotic" sound when isolated.
- **Keep the harmonic-minor color note present even when the chord changes** — over Gm9, still include C♯ (dissonant against that chord in isolation but reads correctly in the context of the full phrase), rather than switching to a "safe" note set per chord.
- **Ornament arpeggio shapes with hammer-ons/pull-offs/slides** instead of picking strictly up/down — stated as what separates "musical" sweep picking from mechanical scale-running.
- **Break up the sweep-picking motion with alternate picking for augmented chords** — for Faug's stacked-major-3rds arpeggio, mix in alternate picking rather than a pure sweep, and use a finger-roll to separate same-fret notes.
- **Chord substitution trick: substitute a diminished 7th arpeggio a half step above the root of a dominant 7th chord.** Worked example: A7 (V) substituted with a B♭ diminished 7th arpeggio — creates a more suspenseful, angular sound leading into the tonic resolution. Directly usable wherever a dominant 7th needs extra tension before resolving.

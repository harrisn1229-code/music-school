# Baby You're A Rich Man — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Written and cut fast, and the interest is almost entirely modal. Pollack calls the key **G Mixolydian major** on literal evidence: the D major V chord never appears anywhere in the song, and F♯ never appears in the tune. What holds the key together is a **tonic pedal** plus a three-chord vocabulary ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]) — the same drone logic as "Rain".

**Form.** An 8-bar I–IV vamp states the whole harmonic idea up front. The verse is three phrases (ABB) across an odd **11 bars**, because its A phrase runs three; the refrain is also three phrases but ABA across an even 12, squaring off against the lopsided verse. The verse's B phrases open toward **IV rather than V**, so the section has no forward pull — with two verses back to back at the top it is genuinely hard to hear where one ends.

**Harmony.** Three chords: I, IV and **♭VII**, the last reading here as IV-of-IV ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). Because so many phrases close on the IV, the centre of gravity sits high for a song supposedly in G. The one outside chord is **♭III in the refrain**, reached by a chromatic bass climb, and the reason it works across the catalogue is the cross-relation it strikes against the chords either side ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]) — so it should not be voiced smoothly.

**Arrangement.** A crowded track, but the craft is in the intro entrances: bass arrives halfway through bar 3 and the clavioline immediately after, rather than on the downbeat of bar 4. That single mid-bar choice is what keeps the vamp from feeling boxy.

# Hello Goodbye — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A song built on **withholding V–I**. The lyric's approach-avoidance gets an exact harmonic mirror: the music flirts constantly with a full authentic cadence and then actually cadences plagally or deceptively nearly every time.

**Form.** **No intro at all.** Verse and refrain strictly alternate four times, then two outros — a first with a complete ending and a second that fades. The verse is an unusual **17 measures**, its last arguably overlapping the start of the refrain; the refrain is a true 16. The second outro is a four-measure vamp played six full times before the fade begins: **46 seconds of a 3:31 record**.

**Harmony.** A **non-I opening** — theorists split on how to label the first chord, but either reading makes the arrival on the tonic a plagal cadence ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]). The verse then plays V twice and **resolves it deceptively to vi both times** ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]); the third V finally gets its I, but only after a five-measure pedal-point build ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]). The refrain then does the thing actually worth learning: **V is excluded from it entirely**, because the verse has already saturated the ear with it, so both its cadences use substitutes — ♭VI as a plagal substitute and ♭VII as a laid-back dominant substitute ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). Put a real V in either slot and the song goes flat.

**Melody.** Almost nothing but **arpeggio fragments** — Pollack's standing challenge is to find two consecutive steps anywhere in the lead vocal that aren't broken by a leap or a change of direction.

**Arrangement.** A **running scale motif unifies the whole record** ([[POP-C04 — Four chords with a moving bass]]): the bass descends in both verse and refrain, balanced by an ascending line in the refrain's background, and in the refrain the descent runs four times slower than in the verse. The backing vocals are the case study in scheduling — used sparingly through the body precisely so their full participation in the second outro reads as an arrival. Absent from the body entirely, that entrance would be arbitrary; exposed more, it would already be stale.

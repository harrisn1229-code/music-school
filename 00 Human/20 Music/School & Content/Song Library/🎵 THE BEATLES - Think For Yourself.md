# Think For Yourself — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (**2 bars** of I-chord vamp that quietly declare the whole song) · Verse (**12 bars** as two parallel **6-bar** phrases, not three fours) · Refrain (**8 bars**) · Refrain · Outro (two more refrains plus a *petit reprise*, complete ending). The seams are smoothed over deliberately: the verse's vocal-against-bass-lick byplay carries straight through the refrain, and the refrain loops back into the next verse with no join.

The distinguishing move is that Harrison **blends** the parallel major and minor rather than switching between them ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The I chord is bi-modal, carrying **B natural in an inner voice against a B♭ in the melody** ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]), and minor v, ♭III and ♭VI belong only to the minor. Never quite major, never quite minor, which is exactly the lyric's posture.

Home arrives late and by a wandering route — the verse opens on **ii**, and only at the end of the first six-bar phrase does G register as home at all; the second phrase is identical until its last bar, where landing on ii erodes whatever arrival you just got. In the refrain the harmony parses two phrases while the tune parses three, which is what makes the section loop.

Tambourine and maracas go four-in-the-bar in cheerful contradiction of the slow triplets everything else plays ([[RHY-C04 — Polyrhythm and hemiola]]), and the "fuzz bass" panned right does not carry the bass part at all — it selectively doubles the real bass an octave up.

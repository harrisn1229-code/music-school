# Economy Picking Lesson | World's Fastest Guitar Technique?

## Synopsis

BERNTH's introduction to economy picking, framed as a direct answer to "is it a legit technique or a cheap trick" — worked through a descending chromatic 2-notes-per-string pentatonic lick, compared head-to-head against pure alternate picking on the same lick. First video to actually define the mechanic on the channel (later videos, e.g. [[4 Techniques You Need To Know To Play FAST]], assume it).

## What's taught

- **The core mechanic: the last downstroke on one string doubles as the first downstroke of the next string, in a single connected sweeping motion — no separate second stroke.** For a 2-notes-per-string descending pattern this reads as down-up-down (down = shared/doubled) repeating, rather than alternate picking's down-up-down-up-down-up. The doubled downstroke IS a small sweep-picking motion grafted onto an alternate-picking line — economy picking is explicitly framed as "combining alternate picking with sweep picking," not a third independent system.
- **Picking angle matters even more here than for pure alternate picking** — pick tip pointed away from the guitar so it doesn't get physically trapped between strings during the string change; get the angle wrong and you're forced to jump the pick over the string instead of gliding through it.
- **Head-to-head trade-off vs. alternate picking, demonstrated on the same lick:**
  - *Economy picking wins on:* right-hand relaxation (no separate stroke per string-transition note) and raw ceiling speed on 2-notes-per-string material specifically — pure alternate picking has to work much harder on 2-note-per-string patterns than on his more comfortable 3-notes-per-string material.
  - *Economy picking loses on:* timing feel. The doubled-downstroke motion feels "counterintuitive" and hard to place accurately against a click at slow tempo — alternate picking's strict down-up-down-up grid is easier to lock to a metronome, especially in a recording/studio context where nailing exact quarter-note placement matters.
- **Explicitly debunked misconception: economy picking does NOT require hammer-ons/pull-offs.** Every note in the demonstrated lick is picked — the down/up strokes are just distributed differently across the strings than alternate picking would place them. Conflating economy picking with legato is a common misunderstanding worth correcting directly.
- **Getting-started drill, kept deliberately small:** isolate a single 2-string transition first (e.g. within one A minor pentatonic box position) before attempting the full repeating descending pattern — practice just the doubled-downstroke motion on one string pair, with the two upstrokes/downstrokes explicitly *not* separated (played as one continuous connected motion, not two individual strokes) until that feels natural.

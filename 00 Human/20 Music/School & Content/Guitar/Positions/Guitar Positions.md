# Guitar Positions

A **position** is the 4-fret span the fretting hand sits in on the guitar neck — the index finger anchors the lowest fret, middle/ring/pinky cover the next three. This set covers **positions 1 through 12** across the whole neck, in **standard tuning (E A D G B E, low string 6 → high string 1)**. Each page maps every note available in that position — as a lookup table and an ASCII fretboard diagram — notes the finger assignment and the reachable stretches just outside the position, and leaves stub sections for chord shapes, scales, and arpeggios to be mapped in later. This mirrors and generalizes the first-position structure of the Berklee Modern Method for Guitar (Book 1) across the full neck.

## Enharmonic key
Sharps are used throughout these pages. Flat equivalents: F# = Gb · G# = Ab · A# = Bb · C# = Db · D# = Eb

> [!note] Extensibility
> Each position page has empty **Chord shapes**, **Scales**, and **Arpeggios** sections — populating those is future work (relates to idea #22 Scales and Modes by Box and String, idea #15 Arpeggios). Progress-tracking through the Berklee book itself is a separate idea, out of scope here.

> [!note] Related training method — reading, not soloing
> The position map above is built for scale/arpeggio/chord vocabulary. For **sight-reading melodies specifically**, there's a narrower, faster-to-fluency alternative: instead of learning to read across all positions, freeze **one** fret/string spot per written note name in a single register and drill until it's automatic — see [[Reading Notation on Guitar]] §7a, from [[Shortcut to Reading Sheet Music for Guitar - Read Guitar Lead Sheets]]. Complementary, not competing: the one-spot drill gets you reading fluently fast in one zone; the full position map (this page) is still what you want for soloing/vocabulary that needs to move around the whole neck.

[[L-Shape Root Map for Blues Chords]] — a *comping*-side movable-shape system: finds the IV/V (and minor-blues ♭VI) chord roots by counting strings and frets from the I chord's shape, rather than mapping scale/triad positions — the missing "where does my hand go" layer under [[Blues Form and Harmony]]'s harmony and [[Chord Voicings]]'s shell shapes.
[[One-Position Fretboard Overview Drill]] — the practice method that makes these position maps usable for actual soloing: confine a whole song's changes to one area, troubleshoot the hard transitions chord by chord, then run restricted solo passes (chord-tones-only, scale-only, pentatonic-only) to force new vocabulary out of the same confined area.
[[Circle-of-Fourths One-Position Scale Drill]] — a pure vocabulary-mastery gate rather than a tune-based drill: play one scale or mode through all 12 keys, circle-of-fourths order, one continuous clean take, staying in roughly one position — the proof-of-execution step that precedes applying a scale/mode musically.
[[CAGED Chord Shapes by String-Shift]] — a comping-side derivation system: instead of walking one chord shape up the neck, slide it sideways one string at a time (thick strings → thin) and compensate the B string, mechanically deriving the A and D shapes from E (and C, F from G) — a different lens than [[CAGE System Scale Shapes]]'s up-the-neck walk, explaining *where* each shape's fret pattern comes from.
[[CAGED Minor Chords by Flatting the Third]] — the minor-chord counterpart to the five major CAGED shapes: find the note that's each shape's major 3rd and flat it one fret, leaving everything else unchanged, including a doubled-3rd special case on the G shape and an extension of the same rule to bare triads.
[[Dominant 7 Arpeggios Over Blues Pentatonics]] — overlays full dominant-7 arpeggios (root-3rd-5th-♭7) for the IV and V chords on top of the I chord's pentatonic scales at each of the five CAGED positions, targeting the specific chord tones (like a V chord's major 3rd) the pentatonic scales don't contain — a lighter-footprint sibling of [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]]'s full CAGED-arpeggio-per-chord method.
[[Chromatic Root-to-Third Connector Lick]] — the lightest-weight member of the family: targets just the V chord's root and connects it to the IV chord's 3rd with a single chromatic half-step, then relocates that one connector lick to four ad hoc fretboard positions rather than mapping full CAGED shapes.
[[Rootless 9th Chords and Chromatic Movement in Blues Rhythm Guitar]] — a rhythm-guitar comping vocabulary built on one rootless dominant-9 shape: relocated through I-IV-V, slid a half step for an implied altered V, and reharmonized with inserted 2-5-1's — the comping-side counterpart to this list's soloing-focused entries, sitting on top of [[L-Shape Root Map for Blues Chords]]'s I/IV/V hand-position map.
[[Weaving Melodic Lines Between Rhythm Guitar Chords]] — breaks each open chord into its three stacked triad shapes (root/1st/2nd inversion), connects adjacent chords' triad fragments with the progression's one parent major scale, then relocates the whole open-position chord/triad/scale system up the neck with a CAGE shape — a chord-connecting-line application rather than this list's soloing- or pure-comping-focused entries.
[[Guide-Tone Diads for Blues Rhythm Guitar]] — the smallest-footprint comping vocabulary in this set: a movable 2-note diad (just the 3rd and ♭7) relocated by half steps across I-IV-V, built up with a sus4 embellishment, a kick/snare-style high/low register-alternation technique, and a second higher-position diad drawn from a different pair of chord tones (5th-3rd).
[[Mapping 6ths Across the Five CAGED Positions]] — maps the diatonic 6th interval, played as a harmonized double-stop, through all five CAGED scale-form positions of C major, with per-position fingering choices (including a deliberate ascending/descending bar asymmetry) and a five-step build order: map the plain scale form, harmonize in 6ths, improvise per position, connect positions, alternate strict/loose practice.
[[Triad Scale Patterns Across the Five CAGED Positions]] — a scale-pattern (not chord-connecting) triad drill: four ordered 1-3-5-triad patterns (ascending, descending, alternating both ways) run through each of the five CAGED scale-form positions (A-G-E-D-C), 20 drills total, milestone-gated practice, then a three-step process for working the vocabulary into real playing over a jazz standard.
[[Diatonic Tenths Over a Drone with Chromatic Passing Shapes]] — a composition-focused sibling to [[Mapping 6ths Across the Five CAGED Positions]]'s harmonized-interval mapping: walks diatonic 10ths over an open-string drone to generate an original fingerstyle part, then adds chromaticism as a half-step passing SHAPE (not a passing note) between two diatonic target shapes, plus the deliberate borrowed-♭6 version of the same move.
[[Six Devices for R&B Rhythm Guitar Fills]] — six standalone gospel/R&B rhythm-guitar fill devices (butterfly hammer-on/pull-off licks, three-string triad-grouping fills, diatonic 6ths/4ths/3rds double stops, single-note pentatonic call-and-response) worked over a diatonic I-ii-vi progression — a fill-device catalog rather than a chord-connecting or soloing-position system.
[[Minor 6 Pentatonic Scale]] — a one-note scale swap (drop the ♭7 a half step to the major 6) rather than an added color note or a chord-tracking edit; a static alternate-color sibling to [[Dorian from the Minor Pentatonic Box]] (which adds the 6 rather than swapping it) and [[Single-Note Pentatonic Substitutions Over Changes]] (which edits a note per chord change rather than as a whole-phrase color choice).
[[Neo-Soul Voicings for a Single Chord]] — a single static chord (Cm7) taken to 14 distinct voicings by one generative rule (only ♭3/♭7 are fixed, root/5th freely swap for 9/11), plus two neo-soul top-note ornamentation devices deliberately not counted toward the 14 — a voicing-variety catalog rather than a movable multi-chord comping system like this list's other entries.
[[Root-to-Root Scale-Form Mapping Method]] — a scale-agnostic shape-learning method, distinct from both this page's fret-span position numbering and [[CAGE System Scale Shapes]]'s CAGED-anchored positions: five scale-form shapes per scale, each drilled by starting on the lowest root, repeating every root landed on while never repeating any other note, and playing the shape's full range before bouncing back — demonstrated across all five harmonic minor shapes and, in a second worked example, all five blues-scale shapes (plus a rule for sliding into the blue note when it falls outside the box shape), with a melodic-pattern/"maze view vs. map view" add-on once a shape is solid.
[[Octave and Unison Shapes for Fretboard Navigation]] — a different kind of fretboard-mapping skill than the shape/position systems above: relocating a single already-known pitch elsewhere on the neck, either an octave away (the Closest Octave Exercise, drilled on every string in both directions) or the identical pitch on a neighboring string (major-scale-chunked unison finding, replacing raw fret-counting) — the actual mechanism underneath moving a chord voicing or scale shape to a new area, stated as prerequisite groundwork for a follow-up video on chord-voicing relocation.

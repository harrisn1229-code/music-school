# Every Beatles key change EXPLAINED

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JKdr4zHa7Z8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JKdr4zHa7Z8)

## Synopsis

A full catalog study: every one of the Beatles' 186 original songs checked for a key change, with the 54 that qualify (29%) sorted into modulation-type categories, each with 2-4 concrete song examples. Effectively a taxonomy of real-world modulation types grounded entirely in one songbook.

## What's taught

- **Study framing/stats**: 54/186 Beatles songs (29%) change key; peaked at >45% during the 1967 Sgt. Pepper era, declining afterward as the band leaned back toward R&B roots. By writer: 23 mainly-Lennon, 21 mainly-McCartney, 6 joint Lennon/McCartney, 3 Harrison, 1 Starr (*Octopus's Garden*, co-written with Harrison).
- **Up a semitone**: *If I Fell* (D♭→D major by end of intro), *And I Love Her* (semitone jump for the acoustic solo), *Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds* (A major verse → B♭ major pre-chorus, a semitone up, then down to G major for the chorus).
- **Up a whole tone**: *Being for the Benefit of Mr. Kite!* (C minor verse → D minor chorus), the Sgt. Pepper reprise (F→G major), *Martha My Dear* (subtle internal tone shift), *Penny Lane* (B major → parallel B minor by end of verse → down a tone to A major chorus → up a tone to B near the end — three modulations in one song), *Day Tripper* (E verse → F♯ chorus), *Magical Mystery Tour* (E major → D major chorus), *Good Day Sunshine* (B major chorus-first → A major verse → D major for the solo), *I Am the Walrus* (B major opening → A major for most of the song).
- **Minor third (3 semitones)**: *Here, There and Everywhere* (G→B♭ chorus), *You're Going to Lose That Girl*, *Lady Madonna*, *Two of Us*, *Dr. Robert*, *When I'm Sixty-Four* (bungalow Bill-style down-a-third phrase repeat), *Something* (C→A middle-eight), *A Day in the Life* (Lennon's G-major section → McCartney's E-major middle, though flagged as barely a "key change" since the connecting crescendos are essentially atonal), *The End* (A→C for the very last bars), *Sun King* (major third — 4 semitones, technically a step further than this category but grouped nearby in the walkthrough).
- **Closely related keys (a 4th/5th away — swap one sharp/flat, most of the notes stay shared)**: *Do You Want to Know a Secret*, *I Want to Hold Your Hand*, *I Want You (She's So Heavy)* (D minor riff/intro → A minor verse), *Strawberry Fields Forever* (F major-ish intro pivots to B♭ major by end of intro — flagged as an especially smooth example specifically because F and B♭ are closely related; full "between concept keys" nuance for this song is covered in a separate David Bennett video), *Julia* (D major, with a C minor chord in the middle eight pulling toward the related key of F minor), *Help!* (a particularly subtle B minor ↔ A major shift), *No One* (B major verse → C minor chorus, driven home with repeated V-I cadences in the new key), *Octopus's Garden* (E major → A major for the guitar break).
- **Tonicization (a very brief, few-bar "not quite a key change")**: *From Me to You* (C major → F major → back to C, all within the middle 8), *Yes It Is*, *Another Girl*.
- **Parallel major/minor modulation (same tonic note, different quality)**: *While My Guitar Gently Weeps* (A minor verse → A major chorus), *I'll Be Back*, *Things We Said Today*, *Michelle*, *Fool on the Hill*, *You Never Give Me Your Money*. Video's stance: even though the tonic note doesn't move, the key *signature* changes, so this counts as a real modulation, not just a borrowed chord.
- **Parallel mode shift (subtler cousin of the above — same major/minor root but different modes)**: *Norwegian Wood* (E Mixolydian A-section → E Dorian, counted as a real key change because it crosses a major mode into a minor mode); *Hey Jude* (F Ionian verse → F Mixolydian coda, explicitly **not** counted as a key change in this study, reasoning: both modes are "major-family" modes, so the shift felt too minor to qualify — video flags this as a judgment call it was on the fence about).
- **Relative major ↔ relative minor**: *Yesterday* (F major → D minor via a ii-V-i cadence in D minor). Video's test for whether this "counts": does the new minor key get a regular, functional use of its own V chord (not just borrowed as vi of the major)? By that test: *Got to Get You into My Life*, *I'm Only Sleeping*, *Happiness Is a Warm Gun*, *When I'm Sixty-Four*, *Carry That Weight*, *We Can Work It Out* all qualify.
- **Relative mode pairs beyond major/minor** (same logic as relative major/minor, generalized to other diatonic mode pairs): *Lovely Rita* (B♭ Mixolydian → E♭ major, same 7 notes, different tonic); *If I Needed Someone* (A Mixolydian verse → B minor middle eight).
- **Ambiguous/miscellaneous cases**: *Come Together* (D minor verse → a chorus analyzable either as B minor, a minor-3rd shift, or as parallel D major — the video leaves this genuinely ambiguous); *Cry Baby Cry* (only "changes key" because of its unrelated tacked-on "Can You Take Me Back" coda, arguably a separate hidden song); *Piggies* (a very last-second chord resolution to a new tonic, judged too fleeting to count and excluded from the final tally).

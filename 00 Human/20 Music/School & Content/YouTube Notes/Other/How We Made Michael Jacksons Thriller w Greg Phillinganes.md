# How We Made Michael Jackson's Thriller w/Greg Phillinganes

## Synopsis

An interview/reflection segment from the Thriller recreation collaboration — mostly personal reminiscence about Quincy Jones, Rod Temperton, and Bruce Swedien, plus a couple of arrangement observations that reconfirm points already documented from other videos in this series (the synth-as-orchestra layering, parts deliberately NOT playing for space/breathability).

## What's taught

- **Playing a full part straight through in one take, with no cutting/pasting between takes, was itself part of how the groove was built** — Phillinganes describes 1980s-era recording as requiring an accurate full performance every time, and frames the repetition as what let the player settle into the groove's "center," rather than a limitation to work around. Worth remembering as a deliberate discipline (a genuinely different take on comping vs. full-take philosophy) even when comping tools make cutting between takes easy.
- **Synth-as-orchestra framing, reconfirmed**: the Prophet-5 read as a "brass" voice, the Jupiter-8 pad as a "strings" voice, arranged in stereo the way real strings-and-brass would be — the same synth-orchestration logic already documented from the Billie Jean CS-80 breakdown.
- **Parts that deliberately don't play provide "breathability"** — reconfirms the arrangement-as-container principle already on [[Arrangement and Song Form]] from Steve Porcaro's interview.

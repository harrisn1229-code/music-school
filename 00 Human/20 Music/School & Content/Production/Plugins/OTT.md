# OTT

![[Images/OTT.png]]

**Category:** Compressor/Limiter
**Vendor:** Xfer Records

## What it is

Xfer's free multiband upward/downward compressor, famous for its extreme, 'over-the-top' dynamic-leveling character.

## What it's used for

Aggressive multiband dynamic leveling for a dense, in-your-face EDM/trap-style loudness and energy.

## FX chain notes

Used on individual tracks or a bus for heavy dynamic leveling, often blended in parallel to taste.

## Techniques

- **One of three parallel layers building a reverse-reverb vocal "backgrounds" swell — pushing mids/highs hard while reining in the lows.** Illangelo's Weeknd backgrounds chain (the reverse-reverb intro texture): an octave-up layer + a dry layer + an OTT'd layer, this one specifically "really pushing the mids and the highs, really controlling the lows" — a tonal-shaping role for OTT alongside the two unprocessed layers, not the whole texture's only source. Feeds into a long chain of unowned modulation plugins (ShaperBox, Sinevibes Blend, MondoMod) and a ~19-second reverb — see [[The Weeknd Vocal Chain]] for the full breakdown. → [[The Weeknd Vocal Chain]], [[Layering and Stacking]] — Illangelo (Mix with the Masters), [[Producing The Weeknd's vocals with Illangelo]]
- **A simple beef-up pass on a plain "super deep bass" one-shot for UK garage: Depth reduced, low boosted, highs and some mids taken out.** After picking a one-shot bass (Oliver's Power Tools kit) and writing a simple root-following line, OTT went on first as the multiband stage — Depth pulled down from the extreme default, the low band boosted, the high band cut, and some of the mids cut too, "so it's really hitting in the low end." Paired with a little [[MicroShift]] for chorus-like thickening and an [[EQ Eight]] 30 Hz cut underneath. → [[Bass Design]], [[MicroShift]], [[EQ Eight]], [[BASS Slot]] — loner, [i combined r&b melodies with uk garage drums to make an insane beat (crazy bounce!?)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I4-jcoCVdC0)
- **As a mastering upward-compressor: frequency-specific maximization, but incredibly subtle settings only.** Named alongside Waves MV2, Oxford Inflator, and the free Melda M Compressor as one of four tools for bringing quiet detail up without attenuating peaks — OTT's specific pitch here is *frequency-specific* maximization (its multiband architecture), but the explicit warning is that OTT stays "a really aggressive effect" even at mastering-appropriate settings, so depth needs to sit far below the defaults that work for sound design. Consistent with the vault's own existing convention of running OTT at Depth 15–30% (never 100%) for mixing/mastering duty — see [[Master Chain]]. → [[Clipping and Loudness]], [[Oxford Inflator]] — Sage Audio, [Mastering Compression Settings](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YUn31aE9X_8)
- **Skip OTT in its default early-chain spot, or push it to the very end instead** — a deliberate break from reaching for OTT first on every sound; explicitly removing it or reordering it after the rest of the chain changes what it's doing (glue/character on an already-shaped sound rather than shaping the sound itself). → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Flux Pavilion, [Never Gonna Be Alone Breakdown (17 Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C75vq9Wf7d8)
- **When one OTT isn't enough, add a second instance in series, plainly, on the same chain** — no macro or fancy rack required — chords/stabs already run through one OTT for brightness can simply get a second OTT instance stacked directly after it for more intensity; the compounding effect of two instances in series reads noticeably different from pushing one instance's Depth/Amount further. → [[Multiband Compression]] — You Suck at Producing (Underbelly ft. Pixelwax), [Sound Design walkthrough of my new song with Pixelwax!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IOc-lDT6_ZA)
- **OTT ("squeeze") straight onto duplicated/randomized vocal chops for extra energy** — reached for immediately after arranging chopped vocal stabs, no gain-staging fuss — a fast energy/punch pass on the chop layer itself rather than a considered mix move. → [[Sound Selection Guide]] — You Suck at Producing, [The Most Slept On Ableton Synth for UK Dubstep](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vLsEQAxir1o)
- **A "multiply" macro that chains progressively more OTT instances in series** — a macro-mapped rack wraps stock multiband/OTT territory with control over band crossovers and per-band time-scaling, plus a **multiply** knob that turns on additional OTT instances in series as it's turned up (rather than manually duplicating the device by hand) — a preset nicknamed "OTT ×10" exists purely to show how far the multiply knob can push the effect; a "MSG" preset and a bass-focused multiband-compression preset are the more usable defaults. → [[Multiband Dynamics]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — dnksaus, [Dank Sauces Rack Pack - Demoing Every Rack!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xuKc_a6xxjk)
- **On a dubstep bass, protect the sub in Multiband Dynamics first — OTT can strip a sound's "magic"** — explicit caution from a full FM-bass sound-design pass: run [[Multiband Dynamics]] ahead of OTT with the low band bypassed/reduced so the sub-bass fundamental is preserved before OTT's up/down pumping ever touches it; used carelessly or too deep, OTT can take the character/"magic" out of a sound, bass especially. Reinforces the existing "Lows bypassed on bass buses" key-parameter row from a dedicated dubstep source. → [[Multiband Dynamics]], [[Operator]], [[EDM Subgenres]] (Dubstep) — dnksaus, [Operator in Depth (Dubstep Sound Design)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0tFb6vqzuHo)
- **Multiband gain-riding drags high-end partials out of phase with the low end's zero crossings — the real mechanical reason to avoid OTT, not just taste.** Because OTT rides low/mid/high bands independently, a sound's high-frequency partials get offset from the fundamental's zero crossings; demonstrated on a plain square wave (a stand-in for "perfect distortion") where the high end visibly detaches from the low end after OTT — a genuine problem once mastering/limiting around a bass-heavy low end, not merely an aesthetic complaint. → [[Phase Alignment]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Fox Stevenson "Why I NEVER use OTT"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)
- **Reverb → OTT → multiband compression = a drop "womp" one-shot** — drown a plain synth zap in [[Hybrid Reverb]], run OTT after it, then a multiband compressor on top — the combination turns a basic hit into a huge-impact drop one-shot; works on sub-bass just as well as anything else. → [[Hybrid Reverb]], [[Multiband Dynamics]] — dnksaus, [reverb can be POWERFUL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gi_y81NmoGg)
- **OTT as a sound-design tool, not a mixing tool — on a vocal chop** — [[Hybrid Reverb]] into OTT afterward on a chopped vocal phrase, deliberately reaching for OTT's extreme multiband up/down character as creative sound design rather than glue/mix compression — a reminder that OTT's home isn't only the mix bus. → [[TEXTURE Slot]] — dnksaus, [the best knob in ableton's stock plugins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dcP02dHWgu4)
- **"Get information across at low volume"** — Ian Kirkpatrick's main use: OTT expands *and* compresses whatever you put it on, so a part buried very low in the mix (a harmony, an atmospheric synth, an ascending Nexus line) still reads. Push it toward max for that. → [[TEXTURE Slot]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Pretty Please (24 Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j5aA59B_T58) & [Move Me Synths](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RXm50CdQ9-o)
- **Restrained master OTT: ~22% depth, upward OFF** — a light OTT to brighten/glue a master without lifting the noise floor ("N-O-T-T, not over-the-top"); Steinberg **Squasher** is "a better OTT." Abusing it wrecks a mix — he agrees with the haters. → [[Master Chain|8 Master]], [[Multiband Compression]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Don't Start Now Master Chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nk1ECJzCiVA) & [FFF](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ge9PAxBdOg8)
- **Swap OTT for a saturation chain (Saturn 2 + Radiator) on a bass sound for a near-identical tone with far more control.** A/B test: matching the low end and taming the highs on a Saturn 2 + Radiator chain gets close to the same tonal result OTT would give, without OTT's uncontrolled, phase-offset high end — a go-to alternative when a sound needs harmonic excitement rather than multiband up/down squashing. → [[Saturn 2]], [[Radiator]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Fox Stevenson "Why I NEVER use OTT"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=64ka_BNfx1o)
- **"Zero upward" for pure downward glue** — on layered synths/snares, OTT with the upward band disabled tightens without inflating hiss. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [New Rules (25 Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iHSmH57_wY8)
- **Upward push to keep quiet parts up + tighten a mono center** — a tiny amount of OTT (its upward action) lifts the low-level detail without squashing, and firms up the mono low end of a chorus-wide synth bass — Disclosure, [Deeper (with Leon Thomas)](https://youtu.be/SM0FZ58ckVw)
- **Subtle OTT (~23% depth) as vocal glue** — ties a 3-vocal stack together like a G-bus comp with a bit of life, without the pumped OTT sound — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8)
- **OTT for vocal presence, then EQ specifically to clean up the artifacts OTT itself introduces.** OTT used on the vocal chain purely to add presence/brightness; because it also brings up unwanted low-mid buildup, an EQ immediately after OTT cuts those newly-exposed frequencies rather than shaping the vocal's original tone. → [[De-essing]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)

## Manual

- **No official manual exists** — OTT is Xfer freeware shipped without documentation. It's a recreation of Ableton's stock "OTT" preset for **Multiband Dynamics**, so the real manual is that chapter: [Live 12 manual — Multiband Dynamics](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-audio-effect-reference/#multiband-dynamics).

### Sources
- Community documentation: Xfer forum threads, r/edmproduction "how does OTT actually work" threads, and Mr. Bill's OTT breakdown videos on YouTube are the canonical explanations.

**High-level points:**
- Three bands, and **every band compresses in both directions at once**: downward (loud → quieter) *and* upward (quiet → louder). That upward half is why it sounds like nothing else — it drags noise floors, tails, and breaths up into your face.
- The preset it clones was nicknamed "over the top" for a reason: default settings are **already extreme**. Depth is your wet/dry-of-intensity.
- It reshapes tone as much as dynamics — the fixed crossovers + heavy upward action scoop mids and hype highs; treat it as an exciter/EQ hybrid.
- Time scales all attacks/releases at once: shorter = more aggressive/pumpy, longer = smoother glue.
- Gain-staging matters: In gain drives the bands harder (more effect); Out compensates. Don't confuse "louder" with "better."

## Used in my chains

3-band up+down compression — highs near-limited, mids breathing, output hyped ("bright, dense, modern" in one knob). Works best on sounds that already *move*. **Depth 15–30%, never the default 100%** (that's sound design, not mixing):

- **[[1 Synth Lead Track|Synth Lead Track]]** — *slot 2, multiband control*: Depth 15–30%, Time up to relax pumping on slower material.
- **[[1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]]** — *slot 2, even the wash*: Depth 10–20% so no single note swells over the mix.

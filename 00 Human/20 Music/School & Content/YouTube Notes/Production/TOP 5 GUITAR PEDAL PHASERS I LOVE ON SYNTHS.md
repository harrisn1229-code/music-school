# TOP 5 GUITAR PEDAL PHASERS I LOVE ON SYNTHS!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VBYshRKdmB0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VBYshRKdmB0)

## Synopsis

A hands-on tour of five hardware phase-shifter pedals (Moogerfooger 12-stage, Boss PH1R, Mu-Tron Phaser II, Roland Jet Phaser, Maestro PS1A) run on synths rather than guitar, with the useful content being what actually differs between phasers beyond "sounds swirly" — stage count, CV-modulatable rate, built-in drive, and a genuinely distinct inertial/Leslie-style speed-ramp behavior on one unit.

## Techniques

- **Stage count (4/6/12) is a real, audible variable on a phaser, not a spec-sheet number — more stages means more/deeper notches and more perceived depth.** Boss PH1R = 4-stage, Mu-Tron Phaser II = 6-stage, Moogerfooger = 12-stage; described as different "flavors" rather than better/worse, worth choosing deliberately by ear rather than defaulting to whatever's on hand.
- **A phaser with CV/patch jacks (the Moogerfooger, being a modular-era Bob Moog design) can have its LFO entirely replaced by an external modulation source, or have its rate/depth CV-modulated from elsewhere in a patch** — a much deeper modulation option than a phaser's own onboard rate knob, worth exploiting whenever a semi-modular rig is already in the signal path.
- **A drive/saturation circuit built INTO the phaser itself (not a separate pedal) — turn output down to compensate and get "really good saturation" as a phaser side effect.** Treat a phaser's own drive stage as a legitimate distortion source in its own right, not just a phase effect, when the unit has one.
- **Layer noise underneath a phased signal deliberately, for a "talking wind" character distinct from the phase effect alone** — a combination technique (phaser + noise + distortion together) rather than three separate isolated effects.
- **A phaser's speed control can be INERTIAL/ramping (Leslie-cabinet-style — gradually accelerating/decelerating to a new rate rather than jumping instantly) as opposed to a standard LFO's immediate rate change.** The Roland Jet Phaser's speed control ramps up and down on its own timescale as it's adjusted mid-phrase, producing an organic, "has its own personality" modulation character layered on top of whatever's being played — a genuinely different modulation behavior than most rate knobs, worth seeking out specifically when a static-feeling phaser/tremolo/rotary effect needs more life.
- **Run two identical mono phaser pedals, one per channel, for a true stereo phase effect** rather than a single stereo-capable unit — demonstrated on the Boss PH1R pair.
- **A dedicated distortion circuit built INSIDE a phaser as a second, switchable mode** (the Jet Phaser's "jet" circuit vs. its clean phase-only circuit) — effectively two devices in one housing, worth checking for on any vintage phaser before assuming it's phase-only.

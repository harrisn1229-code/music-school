# Why Most People Use Sequencers Wrong (Do THIS Instead)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MCvw4XCVoh0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MCvw4XCVoh0)

## Synopsis

A single, genuinely unusual and reusable idea: route a step sequencer's pitch-CV output to the FILTER instead of the oscillator, so the sequencer generates a rhythmic accent pattern instead of a melodic line — freeing the keyboard to play actual notes/chords in real time on top of that rhythm, live and improvisationally, rather than programming a fixed melodic sequence.

## Techniques

- **Repoint a step sequencer's pitch-CV output at the filter cutoff instead of oscillator pitch, turning "pitch steps" into a rhythmic accent/gate pattern.** The sequencer's per-step voltage values (normally read as a melody) instead open and close the filter step by step, producing a rhythmic pulsing texture; the actual PITCH is then played live on the keyboard on top, so the same patch can hold a bassline, a chord, or a melodic phrase while the sequencer drives an independent, spontaneous-feeling rhythm underneath — a live, in-the-moment take rather than a fixed programmed line.
- **Any pitched CV source can be substituted for the sequencer as the filter-modulation input — not just a dedicated step sequencer module.** Demonstrated by patching a second, polyphonic synth's own CV/audio output directly into the first synth's filter input in place of the sequencer: playing chords on the second instrument now drives complex, chord-dependent rhythmic filter patterns on the first, rather than a fixed step pattern.
- **Changing the "rhythm pattern" is just re-programming what would normally be pitch values on the sequencer** — since the CV stream is now read as filter-accent data rather than melody, editing step values (what used to feel like "changing notes") is functionally editing the groove/accent pattern instead. A concrete example given: dialing in a clave-like accent pattern this way, purely by editing sequencer steps.
- **Workflow benefit, stated directly**: this generates unplanned chord progressions and rhythmic ideas by playing/recording into the DAW and reviewing takes after the fact, rather than programming everything deliberately in advance — a deliberate "give up some control, get spontaneity" trade worth trying when a part feels over-planned.

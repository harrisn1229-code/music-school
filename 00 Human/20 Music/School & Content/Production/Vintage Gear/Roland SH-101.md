# Roland SH-101

## What it is
A cheap plastic monosynth (came in red and blue, with an optional keytar grip) that sold poorly, then became **the bass synth of dance music** — probably the most-recorded analog bassline machine of the 90s rave/techno era and still a first-call sound.

## What makes the sound
- **One VCO, mixed waveforms**: saw + square/PWM + sub-oscillator sliders blend into a single fat mono voice — the mixer-of-waves design is why 101 basses sound *dense* despite one oscillator.
- **The Roland 24 dB filter driven by a snappy one-shot envelope** — instant rubbery attack, quick close; acid-adjacent squelch when resonance rises, warm round thump when it doesn't → [[BASS Slot]] archetype.
- **The onboard step sequencer** (plus arp) locks tight, hypnotic 16th-note bass patterns — the [[Synthwave]] "16th-note bass engine" and 90s techno's pulse both live here.
- Noise source + white-simple architecture: commit fast, tweak live, no menu anywhere.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Analog]] (mono patch: saw+square+sub into the 24 dB filter — very close), [[Serum 2]]/[[DUNE 3]] one-osc-plus-sub recipes, [[kHs Ladder Filter]] to Roland-ify any bass.
- **Paid:** TAL BassLine-101 (the reference emulation), Roland Cloud SH-101, D16 LuSH-2; Behringer MS-1 in hardware.
- **Free:** the Analog recipe above covers it in stock Ableton.

## Used on
- Genre/era: 90s techno/rave/big beat basslines wholesale, UK acid/electro, and the modern synthwave bass engine — cited constantly across that era's artist interviews (Aphex Twin's early rig among the best-documented 101 fanbases), with individual track credits rarely pinned; largely genre-level per the rule.

# Chord Progressions - Beginner's Guide - Part 4 (The Diatonic Secret)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N9GEHITwkoE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N9GEHITwkoE)

## Synopsis

The payoff video of the series: builds the diatonic triads of a major scale by stacking every scale degree in 3rds (root, +1 scale step, +1 scale step) and shows the result is six usable chords plus one oddball (the vii° diminished chord, set aside for later). Names the qualities-per-degree pattern as a memorizable chant — **Ma-mi-mi-Ma-Ma-mi-dim** — and frames a chord progression as simply reordering those six diatonic chords. Content-wise this duplicates the "Major keys — the pattern" table on [[Chords by Key]] exactly, but the chant format is a genuinely useful memory device the vault didn't have in prose form, so it's worth adding as a quick-recall line.

## What's taught

- **Build the diatonic triads of a scale by stacking every scale degree**: take chord I (root, +4+3 half steps = major), then shift every note up to the next scale tone to get chord II, and so on through all 7 degrees. In C major: **C Dm Em F G Am B°**.
- **The universal major-key chord-quality pattern, as a chant: "Ma-mi-mi-Ma-Ma-mi-dim"** — major, minor, minor, major, major, minor, diminished, scale degrees I–VII, true in every major key with no exceptions. The I, IV, and V chords are always major; ii, iii, vi are always minor; vii is always diminished.
- **The vii° (diminished) chord is set aside deliberately** as a "chord we'll come back to" — beginner framing to avoid overwhelming a first pass at diatonic harmony; it's covered properly in [[Diminished Chords]] already.
- **Chord progressions = reordering the six diatonic chords** — no new theory beyond permutation; the video demos several manual reorderings (F–D–E–C, D–C–D–G, A–G–F–G) as examples of "just try an order and use your ear."

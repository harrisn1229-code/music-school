## Synopsis

A near-3-hour, 8-level masterclass covering the harmonic minor scale end to end: construction theory, its seven modes and their five practicable fretboard shapes, chord-building from each mode, a graduated tempo/subdivision practice ladder across all 12 keys and multiple starting strings, positional improvisation, and a capstone "chain exercise" (continuous, unstructured improvisation across the whole fretboard). ## Techniques

- **Level 1 — Construction theory + the two-octave shape.** Harmonic minor = natural minor with the 7th raised back to natural. One key needs a double-sharp: G♯ harmonic minor's 7th is F𝄪, the only harmonic minor scale requiring one. One movable two-octave shape, transposable to any root.
- **Level 2 — The seven modes.** Harmonic minor (Aeolian ♮7), Locrian ♮6, Ionian ♯5, Dorian ♯4, Phrygian dominant (aka Spanish Phrygian), Lydian ♯2, Super Locrian (aka Ultralocrian, altered dominant ♭♭7) — same relative-mode construction logic as the major scale's seven modes, reduced in practice to five distinct fretboard shapes rather than seven.
- **Level 3 — Eighth notes at 100 BPM, all keys.** Drill the five shapes across every key at a fixed moderate tempo/subdivision before adding speed or complexity.
- **Level 4 — Chord construction per mode.** Stack thirds off each mode's root using only notes native to harmonic minor: i minor(maj7), ii half-diminished, III augmented(maj7♯5), iv minor7, V7 (major, not minor — the scale's signature harmonic move), VI major7, vii° diminished7. Explicit theory point: there's no true "key of harmonic minor" — compositions are written in the relative natural-minor key, borrowing chords (most commonly the major V/V7) from the harmonic minor scale for cadential strength.
- **Level 5 — Three-notes-per-string, 16th notes, 80 BPM, all 12 keys, E strings only.** Ascend on the low E string's shape, descend on the high E string's shape, through all 12 keys at this fixed tempo/subdivision.
- **Level 6 — Same drill, rotating starting string.** Repeat Level 5's full 12-key pass starting from the A string, then B, then D, then G — not just the E strings — so shape recall isn't anchored to one starting string.
- **Level 7 — Positional improvisation over a backing track.** Work through five fretboard positions over a chosen key (e.g. C minor using C harmonic minor), identifying each position's native mode before improvising in it — same position-mapping logic as [[Positional CAGED Soloing with Matched Modes and Arpeggios]], applied to harmonic minor instead of a major-key progression.
- **Level 8 — The chain exercise (hardest, most effective drill, saved for last).** Pick a random harmonic minor key and improvise continuously across the whole fretboard without stopping, in a fixed subdivision matched to your actual skill level (demoed at 16ths, but 8ths/triplets/quarters all work). Framed as the single most effective scale-internalization method in his teaching, specifically because it can't be practiced along with a fixed reference recording — it forces genuine internalization rather than pattern-copying.

# Background Vocals - Tony Maserati

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a6agrpAFmGw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a6agrpAFmGw)

## Synopsis

Tony Maserati works on K.Flay's "Dreamers," demonstrating how he blends background vocal doubles to raise a track's energy without competing with the lead. His core ideas: keep background parts a supportive role rather than a competing one; vary the blend across sections (a phrase before the chorus drops, or the end of a chorus) so repetition doesn't get boring; de-ess doubles aggressively since the ear can't distinguish multiple simultaneous S sounds anyway, only out-of-time ones; and repurpose a double that isn't earning its place (too much low end, redundant body) into a distinct textural layer — in this case EQing the bottom out of a third double and compressing it hard to turn it into a "breathy" high-frequency energy-lift layer rather than muting it.

## Techniques

- **De-ess background vocal doubles more aggressively than the lead, because the ear can't discern multiple simultaneous S sounds unless they're out of time.** "We don't need more than one S your ear doesn't really discern between how many S's are there unless they're out of time... we want to hear a little bit of that double S but much lower level, that'll keep the lead vocal in the center in front of the listener." Owned lane: [[RX De-ess]] or [[Pro-DS]]. → [[De-essing]], [[RX De-ess]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **Repurpose a redundant double into a distinct "breathy" texture layer instead of muting it: EQ out the low end, then compress hard so it sits up-front, adding energy without adding body.** "For some reason for me it's adding a bit of bottom end that I'm not crazy about, so I think what I'm gonna do is turn this into a breath, a sort of breathy version... EQ out the bottom and see if I can't get this to be a breathy addition to that group... I'm gonna compress that package a little bit more so it's really up in your face." → [[EQ]], [[Compression]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Assign each background layer a distinct functional role rather than stacking identical doubles: two main doubles add body, a third repurposed double adds breathy high-frequency lift.** "The two main doubles are adding body, and with this double is adding breathy high frequency — you can't really tell what it's doing, but it's just lifting the energy up especially in those highs." → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **Vary the background vocal blend section-to-section rather than repeating the exact same treatment every chorus, specifically to hold listener attention.** "It's a very rare thing that I'm deciding whether the third harmony is louder or lower... [when I do] I can change that energy or that vibe just for perhaps the words right before the chorus drops, or at the end of the chorus... if the blends are the same in every chorus, sometimes it gets boring. It's highly important for me to make sure people don't get bored." → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **When backgrounds arrive already blended/effected by the artist/producer in Pro Tools, treat the mixer's job as light compaction and energy work rather than re-doing the blend from scratch** — confirmed by ear (checking for existing delay/EQ/compression already baked in) before doing anything else. → [[Client Communication and Revisions]], [[Mix Handoff]]

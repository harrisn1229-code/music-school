# You Suck at Producing #50: Making Instruments More Expressive

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R2EEvUd1mcY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R2EEvUd1mcY)

## Synopsis

A genuinely well-structured lesson on the two layers of instrument expressiveness: basic envelope humanizing (Attack/Release/Sustain shaping to avoid a static, clicky, or abrupt sound), and — the more valuable half — velocity mapped to far more than just amplitude. Demoed on Wavetable but explicitly stated to apply to any synth with a mod matrix. The core insight: once a patch is velocity-sensitive at all, that same velocity value can drive amp decay, oscillator wave position, filter cutoff/resonance, and unison amount simultaneously, so a single instrument's character shifts subtly with how hard each note is played — the layered, compounding effect is what reads as "alive."

## Techniques

- **Basic envelope humanizing: a touch of Attack, some Release, lower Sustain** — removes an unnaturally clicky onset, avoids an abrupt cutoff, and gives a plucky rather than static character — the baseline pass before any velocity mapping.
- **Draw a velocity contour across a whole phrase at once (Cmd/Ctrl-drag)** — instead of setting each note's velocity individually, hold Cmd/Ctrl and drag a line across the velocity lane to establish a smooth dynamic arc spanning several notes in one gesture. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Map velocity to amp Decay/Release, oscillator Wave Position, filter cutoff/Resonance, AND Unison amount — not just amplitude** — once a patch is velocity-sensitive at all, the same velocity value becomes a free, compounding expression source: louder notes can ring out longer, land on a subtly different timbre, read brighter, and pick up more Unison-driven noise/texture — several small, subtle velocity-driven moves stacking into something that reads as genuinely played rather than triggered. → [[Wavetable]]

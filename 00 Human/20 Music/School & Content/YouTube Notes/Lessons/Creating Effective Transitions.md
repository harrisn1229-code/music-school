# Creating Effective Transitions (You Suck at Producing #37)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCZIW0ZDEgA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCZIW0ZDEgA)

## Synopsis

A short transitions lesson covering four escalating fixes for an abrupt section change: carrying one recognizable element through the boundary as connective tissue, adding white noise to signal an upcoming change, stripping busy elements for space right before it, and — the most concretely useful technique — extending the lead-up itself by duplicating an arbitrary time range (not just a clip) via the Loop Bracket's Duplicate Time command, then filling that extra room with a low-content interlude.

## Techniques

- **Carry one recognizable element through a big transition as connective tissue** — keep a melodic motif or signature sound playing continuously across a structural boundary between two otherwise very different-sounding sections, giving the listener something familiar to hold onto while everything else changes. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Extend a section by duplicating a TIME RANGE (Loop Bracket → Duplicate Time), not just a clip** — select the range to extend with Cmd/Ctrl+L, right-click the loop bracket, and choose Duplicate Time to repeat that exact range across every track in one move — a fast way to buy more room before a transition (a longer breakdown/interlude) instead of duplicating individual clips one at a time. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

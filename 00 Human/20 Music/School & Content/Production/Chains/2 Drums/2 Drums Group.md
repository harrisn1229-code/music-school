# Drums Group

**Role:** The kit summed (kick, snare, hats group, percussion). This is your current working drums chain (Step 1 Drums export), kept as the default — it's a solid EQ → comp → saturate → transient structure.

**Source note — all drums come from packs/loops** (no live kit): [[XO]] to browse/audition one-shots by similarity, [[Drum Rack]]/[[Simpler]] to play them, [[Serato Sample]] for chops. That means: (1) sound selection replaces mic technique as "Step 1 — Record" — swap samples before reaching for plugins; (2) samples arrive pre-processed, so track chains stay light; (3) **full loops**: split stems or slice into the Drum Rack when possible so kick/snare/hats can hit their own chains — an unsplit loop gets treated on this group directly ([[RC-20 Retro Color]] texture + the chain below).

## Chain overview
Corrective EQ → kit compression → color → punch restore. The Transient Master at the *end* is the clever part of your chain: it restores the attack that compression and saturation soften.

## Chain

### 1. [[Pro-Q 4]] — Kit balance
- Broad moves on the summed kit: low-mid mud cut 250–400 Hz · any whole-kit tilt
- Per-drum EQ already happened on tracks — this is about the *kit* as one instrument.

### 2. [[Pro-C 2]] — Kit compression
- Punch style · 4:1 · attack 20–30 ms (transients pass) · release 100–150 ms or auto — time it to "breathe" with the tempo · **2–3 dB GR**
- **Why:** Compressing the kit together is what makes separate samples sound like a *band's drummer* — they start moving each other's levels, which reads as cohesion.

### 3. [[Saturn 2]] — Kit color
- Tape, single band or gentle multiband · low drive
- **Why:** Rounds the digital edges of the whole kit uniformly — shared harmonics are shared identity.

### 4. [[Transient Master]] — Punch restore
- Attack +5–15%
- **Why:** Both previous stages shave transients slightly; adding attack back *after* them gives you compression's glue AND the punch — order is the trick.

## Variations
- **Harder hip hop:** raise Pro-C 2 GR to 3–4 dB and lean on [[Parallel Comp Send]] blended under for size.
- **Live-feel/Beatles:** swap Saturn for [[Kramer Tape]] pushed harder, slow the Pro-C release, let it pump musically.

## From books

- **Tom EQ starting point — parked here because toms have no track note of their own in this system.** Four moves per tom, tuned *per drum* rather than across a tom bus: **low cut 60–200 Hz** ("stop clashing" — floor toms near 60, rack toms near 200, each filtered out from under the drum below it) · **cut 300–800 Hz** for boxiness (sweep a narrow +8–10 dB band through the range until the honk rings, then flip it to a 3–5 dB cut — *general starting point, the source gives no Q or gain*) · **boost 100–300 Hz** for low-end power ([[BASS Slot]], top edge into the [[BED Slot]]) · **boost 5–10 kHz** for stick attack / high-end punch ([[LEAD Slot]] into the [[AIR Slot]]; a [[TRANSIENT Slot]] job by role). The low cut and the low boost overlap deliberately: cut *below* that specific drum's fundamental, boost *at* it. Set toms this way on their own tracks before this group's broad 250–400 Hz kit-mud cut, which will otherwise fight the tom body boost. → [[EQ]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Kick Design]], [[Snare Design]] — Whipped Cream Sounds, [[Drum Mixing EQ Guide - Enrichment|Drum Mixing EQ Guide]]

## From videos

- **Four stackable drum-bus compression techniques, fully worked with settings.** Standard glue: slow-enough attack to preserve transients, release above ~20 ms (avoids low-frequency distortion), 3-6 dB gain reduction. Parallel: a unity-gain SEND (not an output change) to a separate heavily-compressed aux, blended back via fader to reveal masked low-level detail (snare buzz, stick rattle) without flattening the main bus. Emphasis/de-emphasis: EQ before the compressor attenuates a range you want spared (e.g. dip the kick's lows), letting the compressor bear down harder elsewhere, then an equal-and-opposite boost after restores tonal balance — a single-band-compressor workaround for frequency-selective (multiband-style) compression. Harmonic distortion before vs. after compression changes whether the added harmonics get compressed too; both at once is the most intense, deliberately reserved for drums that need to cut aggressively. Full breakdown: [[4 Ways to Use Drum Bus Compression]]. → [[Compression Staging]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[Multiband Compression]], [[Saturation and Distortion]] — Sage Audio, [4 Ways to Use Drum Bus Compression](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eY-vIUZzA4s)

Both run into independent mixer channels, EQ'd separately, then get **summed to a single input** — a hardware pre-sum before the DAW. → [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]] — Zach Scholz, [Recording Old School "Hip-Hop" Drum Tones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R14N3T5AikU)
- **Push mic input hard into an analog stage for deliberate clipping, kept as intentional lo-fi character, not gain-staged away** — both snare mics driven hot enough to clip an analog mixer produced "a very distorted snare" that read as a genre-appropriate choice in the context of a full lo-fi drum mix; the kick got the same treatment (EQ boosted hard around 60 Hz until it clipped) for a deliberately weird low end that "sounds really cool" once blended with the overhead. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[SUB Slot]] — Zach Scholz, [Recording Old School "Hip-Hop" Drum Tones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R14N3T5AikU)
- **When a source can't go through the analog stage (no phantom power for a condenser overhead), emulate the same character in the box instead** — drive a preamp plugin hard on the direct-to-interface overhead to match the vibe of the already-driven kick/snare, rather than letting one mic sound cleaner than the rest of the kit. → [[UAD Neve 1073]] — Zach Scholz, [Recording Old School "Hip-Hop" Drum Tones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R14N3T5AikU)
- **Budget stereo-pair trick: cheap pencil condensers hung directly from the cable instead of mounted on stands** — light enough to skip buying stands entirely; used here as a subtle stereo-widening pair kept very low in the mix (the kit stays mostly mono), with extra top-end attenuation since cheap pencil condensers tend to read bright. → [[Stereo Field]] — Zach Scholz, [Recording Old School "Hip-Hop" Drum Tones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R14N3T5AikU)
- **Ambience trick: a spare mic in an unrelated adjacent room, processed through an amp sim purely for reverb** — reads "more natural" for a bigger drum sound than throwing reverb on the drum bus directly; general takeaway — if there's a spare mic during a drum session, throw it somewhere far away and process it separately rather than leaving it unused. → [[Depth and Space]], [[Reverb]] — Zach Scholz, [Recording Old School "Hip-Hop" Drum Tones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R14N3T5AikU)
- **Snare/hi-hat bleed fix: expansion instead of compression on a bussed snare pair** — when a mic position makes hi-hat bleed close to unavoidable, expanding the dynamic range (rather than compressing, which would pull the bleed UP toward the snare) pushes snare hits further out and drops the hi-hat bleed between them down. Once separated this way, the snare can take additional distortion for extra lo-fi grit without dragging the hi-hat bleed along with it. → [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]], [[Sidechaining]] — Zach Scholz, [Recording Old School "Hip-Hop" Drum Tones](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R14N3T5AikU)
- **Group saturation IS the drum glue (and drums don't get limited)** — kick+snare+hats saturated together so hits interact; his control doctrine: saturate/distort drums, limit everything else; alternate section trick: print a "wavy" version of this whole group through filter+phaser+auto-pan performed by hand for verse 2 — Lophiile, [Make Your Drums Slap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v4ZhwlhF5yE) & [Maria Isabel breakdown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RXrF27xqogg)
- **Scratch-kit chain order: Pultec EQ → Gate → [[Soothe2]] on the GROUP → [[Devil-Loc Deluxe]]** — EQ first on the snare, gate the noise between hits, one Soothe2 resonance pass over the summed kit (not per mic), Devil-Loc for the crush; then warp the timing and **pitch the drums down** for weight (the sampled-break move on your own kit) — Kenny Beats, [Cook Up From Scratch](https://youtu.be/l7AjRTXex2U)
- **Four close mics, NO room mic** — U87 overhead placed low at knee height **between snare and floor tom, aimed at the kick, far from the hi-hat** (bright condensers near the hat only add ping; from there one mic hears the whole kit incl. kick) + ribbon over the kick + snare + kick-front. Rooms are optional, placement isn't — Kenny Beats, [Drum Recording Process 2023](https://youtu.be/HTbdB6rWHNI)

# TRANSIENT Slot — broadband hits

The rhythm skeleton. Drums and percussion don't occupy one frequency slot — each piece **straddles** the melodic slots, which is exactly why they collide with everything.

## Swap list (interchangeable occupants)

- **Kick** — straddles [[SUB Slot]] + [[BASS Slot]]; [[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]] chain
- **Snare/clap** — straddles [[MIDS Slot]] + [[LEAD Slot]] presence
- **Hats/shakers** — live in [[AIR Slot]] (see that page)
- **Percussion/toms** — BASS through MIDS depending on tuning
- **AnalogKick-style synthesized hits** — [[AnalogKick.amxd]]
- **Foley hits** — clicks, snaps, stomps replacing traditional drums

## Mix rules

- Transients win by **being first**, not loudest — a clean 5 ms attack cuts through a wall.
- The kick/808 relationship is the mix's foundation: sidechain, offset, or merge ([[SUB Slot]] rules).
- Parallel compression ([[Parallel Comp Send]]) adds density without killing attacks.
- Drum bus glue: [[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]] chain; [[OTT]] in moderation on top loops.

## When it's the hook
Beat-switch moments, drum fills, the trap hat-roll, the iconic break: percussion as hook = push the pattern, strip melodic slots for its bars (the carve is *arrangement*, not EQ).

## When it competes
- **Snare vs vocal** ([[LEAD Slot]]): the 2–4 kHz crack pokes the vocal's home — tuck the snare's presence when the vocal is on, or write around it.
- **Kick vs bass**: pick who owns the low attack; sidechain the loser ([[BASS Slot]]).
- **Hats vs vocal air**: [[AIR Slot]] rules.
- Cymbal wash masking everything: [[Soothe2]] on the drum bus, sides-only.

## Drum lanes in this slot (from books)

- **The four-lane EQ map for kick/snare/hats/toms** — which band makes each hit read as a transient: kick snap **2–4 kHz** (+ a click band at **8–10 kHz**, printed as a "notch" but almost certainly a boost — see [[Kick Design]]) · snare crack **5–7 kHz** · toms stick attack **5–10 kHz** · hat sizzle high-shelf **7–10 kHz+**. Body counterparts, set first: kick **80–100 Hz**, snare **200–300 Hz**, toms **100–300 Hz**. Every lane also gets a high-pass, ascending down the kit (kick 40–60 · toms 60–200 · snare 100–200 · hats 200–300) — that ladder *is* the kit's internal lane assignment. → [[EQ]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[2 Drums Group]] — Whipped Cream Sounds, [[Drum Mixing EQ Guide - Enrichment|Drum Mixing EQ Guide]]

## From videos

- **Rebuild transient life lost to heavy bus compression/limiting via a dedicated parallel aux, rather than backing off the crush itself.** Jaycen Joshua's "Transformer" aux on a drum bus: [[Decapitator]] (dark tone, mid-aggressive drive) → an SSL-style "Cross-Eyed" comp (Attack 30, Release 1, Ratio 2:1, ~8 dB GR — fast-ish attack lets the transient sneak through before the comp grabs it) → low end stripped so only top-end "spark" returns. "I always try to remove stuff so things can be on its own and separated."

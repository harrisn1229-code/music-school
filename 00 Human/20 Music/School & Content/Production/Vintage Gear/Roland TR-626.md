# Roland TR-626

## What it is
The [[Roland TR-707|707]]'s successor and the last of the classic TR line — 30 PCM sounds (double the 707), tunable voices at last, latin/orchestral extras, and the same beloved X0X sequencer. Never iconic in the West, it quietly became a workhorse in late-80s budget studios worldwide, most famously in Jamaican digital-riddim production.

## What makes the sound
- **Cleaner 12-bit-class PCM than the 707** with **per-voice tune and level** — finally a TR sample box you could pitch, which producers used hard (detuned toms/timbales as melodic riddim voices).
- Bigger palette: kits + timbales, congas, claves, tambourine, even orchestra hit-adjacent colors — a rhythm-section box, not just a kit.
- Still dry, still boxy, still committed — no velocity nuance, no tails; patterns come out machine-tight, which suits genres built on riddim repetition.
- X0X grid + shuffle/flam and MIDI made it the affordable heir to the whole TR programming vocabulary.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Impulse]] with 626 samples ([[Simpler]] repitch mimics the tune knobs); [[RC-20 Retro Color]] for era texture.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2; vintage sample archives (Samples From Mars et al.).
- **Free:** 626 kits are standard in free archives.

## Used on
- Genre/era: widely adopted in late-80s/90s dancehall and digital-reggae studios (the tuned-tom riddim voice), plus budget house/hip hop demos of the era — record-level credits are thin in documentation, so associations stay genre-level.

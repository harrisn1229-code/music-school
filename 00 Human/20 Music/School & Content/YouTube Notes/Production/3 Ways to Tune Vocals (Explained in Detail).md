# 3 Ways to Tune Vocals (Explained in Detail)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JX93IhTnuno)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JX93IhTnuno)

## Synopsis

Sage Audio walks through vocal tuning at three levels of control: real-time autotune-style tuners (fast, limited), Melodyne-style note-by-note editing (the standard for most projects), and free DAW-native alternatives (Logic's Flex Pitch demoed). Explains the humanize/vibrato distinction, format-shifting for character changes, and a Melodyne trick for building pitch-shifted harmonies from a single take.

## Techniques

- **Real-time autotune tuners: enable only the notes actually in the performance, not a full preset key/scale.** A key typically has 7 in-key notes and 5 out; but the actual performance may use fewer — find the exact notes sung and enable only those rather than the full key, for tighter correction. Alternative approach: leave all 12 notes enabled with a subtle tuning amount — some notes stay slightly imperfect, but the overall in-tune ratio improves and it reads more natural than hard-quantizing to a preset scale. → [[Vocal Tuning]]
- **"Humanize" on a real-time tuner is really a vibrato/pitch-modulation control, not a separate humanizing effect.** A sung note's fundamental naturally drifts above/below the target pitch (and its overtones drift with it) — that drift is vibrato. Humanize either preserves most of that natural modulation or reduces it toward the intended frequency. Named real-time tuners: Auto-Tune (classic), Slate Digital's Meta Tune (adds doubling, look-ahead, minimum time-between-note-changes), and Spot-On by Sixth Sample (free). → [[Vocal Tuning]]
- **Melodyne splits the vibrato concept into two separately-controllable parameters: Pitch Modulation (the vibrato/wobble itself) and Pitch Drift (the gradual bend up or down INTO or FROM a note).** Editable globally, per-note, or on a highlighted range — the level of control that makes Melodyne-class editors the standard choice for most projects over a real-time tuner. Pitch reference: **every 6 cents = 1 Hz** at the note level. For natural-sounding tuning: use the macro pitch control to snap phrases to the nearest in-key note, then subtly reduce pitch drift rather than zeroing it. For deliberately unnatural/robotic tuning: apply the macro correction, then flatten modulation and drift toward zero so notes sit unnaturally static and locked to pitch. → [[Vocal Tuning]]
- **Formant shifting (Melodyne) changes perceived voice character — deeper or higher — without moving the actual sung pitch.** A voice has roughly three formants (frequency clusters shaped by vocal-tract articulation); shifting them up or down independent of pitch is how an editor makes a take sound like a deeper or higher voice while every note stays on its original pitch. Can be applied globally or to individual notes. Melodyne also exposes attack and amplitude per note, which is closer to editing than tuning but lives in the same tool. → [[Vocal Tuning]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Build a harmony from a single vocal take by copying a phrase in Melodyne, pasting it at the same timestamp on an unhighlighted selection (so it stacks instead of replacing), then pitch-shifting the pasted copy to a third or fifth.** The duplicate is independently editable (pitch, modulation, formant) without touching the original performance it was copied from — a fast way to generate a harmony line that still carries the same phrasing/timing as the lead, rather than re-recording or programming one from scratch. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Vocal Tuning]]
- **Free DAW-native alternative: Logic's Flex Pitch, workflow-equivalent to Melodyne but rougher-sounding.** Double-click the vocal region → edit the *track* (not the file, to avoid permanent changes) → enable Flex (bowtie icon) to segment the take into phrases by timing → switch to Flex Pitch for the piano-roll pitch view. Per-note editing exposes the same conceptual controls as Melodyne (pitch drift to/from a note, fine-tune in cents, amplitude, vibrato/modulation, formant) via six small draggable dots around each note; multi-select notes to apply one edit across a group. Harmonies require duplicating the whole track and editing the duplicate's notes, a less granular approach than Melodyne's copy-paste-per-phrase. Named here as a reference point only — Logic isn't Nate's DAW; the equivalent workflow in Ableton would be Warp/pitch editing on an audio clip, which is far less granular than either of these. → [[Vocal Tuning]]

# This Boy — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (**5½ bars** — three bare guitar chords, the first landing mid-bar, functioning as a stretched pickup into a 4-bar intro proper) · Verse (16) · Verse · Bridge (16, two neat equal phrases) · Verse · fade. Unusually for them, **all three verses carry different lyrics**, and the form runs short because the bridge is too intense to repeat.

The sharpest structural observation is that the voices and the band are on **different grids**: the backing loops a four-bar cliché four times while the vocal lays three unequal phrases across it that start in the wrong places — phrase 2 entering on a long anacrusis in the middle of bar 7, phrase 3 finishing early enough to leave two bars of filler.

The verse progression is I – vi – ii – V, and the point is the **ii rather than IV**: it lines the final three chords up as descending fifths, so the loop gains an inevitability the IV version does not have ([[POP-C02 — Why four chords is satisfying]]). In the bridge the harmonic rhythm drops to one chord every two bars while the chords themselves get *more* restless — a high density of secondary dominants that never actually leaves the key ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]], [[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]).

Appoggiaturas turn nearly every chord into a seventh or ninth and characteristically resolve **late**, after the harmony has already moved ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]) — that delayed resolution is the source of the song's ache. One of only three sustained three-part-harmony vehicles in the catalogue, and **the ostensible lead is the bottom voice**, which is why the climb above the other two in the bridge lands so hard.

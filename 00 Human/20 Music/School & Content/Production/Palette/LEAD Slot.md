# LEAD Slot (PRESENCE) — 2.5–6 kHz

The thing the listener is listening *to*. Mix priority #1 by default — every other slot yields to this one.

## Swap list (interchangeable occupants)

- **Lead vocal** — the default occupant; [[FX Chain System]] vocal chains ([[Auto-Tune Pro]] first)
- **Lead synth** — [[Serum 2#Patch Recipes|Serum 2 supersaw/trap lead recipes]] · [[1 Synth Lead Track|Synth Lead Track]] chain
- **Bell/whistle hook** — [[Omnisphere#Patch Recipes|Omnisphere Mike Dean lane]]
- **Lead guitar** — solos and melodic hooks
- **Top-line vocal chop** — a chop promoted from MIDS to carry the hook

## Mix rules

- **Only one occupant at a time. Non-negotiable.** Lead vocal in? The synth lead waits its turn.
- Center, upfront, dry-ish: predelay on its reverb (20–40 ms, [[ValhallaVintageVerb]]) keeps it in front of its own tail.
- Presence 3–5 kHz is its home — that band belongs to this slot in *every* competing track's EQ.
- De-harsh here, don't dull: [[Soothe2]] over static high cuts.

## When it's the hook
It always is — that's the definition. **Hook-focus workflow:** decide per section what the hook is (vocal, riff promoted from [[MIDS Slot]], 808 pattern from [[SUB Slot]]) — whatever it is *becomes* the LEAD occupant for that section, gets pushed high in the mix, and every competing track gets carved around it (dynamic dips keyed by the hook, [[Masking and Carving]]).

## When it competes
- It doesn't — it wins. Everything overlapping 2.5–6 kHz gets a dynamic dip keyed by this slot ([[Soothe2]] sidechain on guitars/keys = automatic vocal pocket).
- **vs AIR sibilance** ([[AIR Slot]]): the one fight it can lose — de-ess the vocal rather than dulling the hats.
- Two hook candidates in one section is an **arrangement bug**, not a mix problem: demote one to [[MIDS Slot]] or move it to another section.

## Drum lanes in this slot (from books)

- **Drum presence/crack lives across 2.5–6 kHz — and this is the slot the vocal wants.** Kick snap **2–4 kHz** · snare crisp/crack **5–7 kHz** · toms stick punch **5–10 kHz** (both running up into [[AIR Slot]]). Three drum lanes boosting into the vocal's presence range at once is the most common reason a mix's drums and lead vocal fight; stagger them (kick low in the range, snare/toms high) rather than boosting all three at the same frequency. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Snare Design]] — Whipped Cream Sounds, [[Drum Mixing EQ Guide - Enrichment|Drum Mixing EQ Guide]]

## Bass tone lanes in this slot (from videos)

- **"Edgy the dwarf" — ~3 kHz clank + ~5 kHz+ hiss, two separate sub-problems in the same slot.** A stock amp treble knob is often the wrong tool (centered as high as 10 kHz on some amps — reduces hiss but not clank); a graphic EQ splits it into two moves, a 3 kHz cut for clankiness and a separate 5 kHz+ cut for hiss. Boost it (plus fresh strings and a high-mids nudge) to chase Tim Commerford's metallic cut-through-the-mix tone on Rage Against the Machine's "Bombtrack"; push further into modern-metal territory (Nolly/Periphery) with a pick instead of fingers (more natural energy in this range) plus distortion. Full breakdown: [[Bass EQ Frequency Zones — The Five Dwarves]]. → [[EQ]], [[MIDS Slot]] — BassBuzz, [[How to Get the PERFECT Bass Tone]]

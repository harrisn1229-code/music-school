# Speed-Burst Tension-Release Picking Drill

A short-burst-then-rest alternate-picking drill, consolidated from three separate BERNTH videos that independently teach the same drill shape on different scale material — a chromatic two-string burst, a one-string 16th/16th-triplet burst, and a 7-note odd-grouping chromatic burst against a drum backing track. All three share the same core mechanism and are one drill with three worked variations, not three separate near-duplicate notes.

## The shared mechanism

- **A brief fast burst (4-8 notes) followed by a short rest, repeated.** The comparison between the burst and the rest is the actual training signal — it teaches how little picking-attack force a fast burst needs by contrasting it against the felt relaxation of the following rest.
- **Target tension curve is flat, not spiky.** Don't alternate between fully tense (during the burst) and fully relaxed (during the rest) — the goal is staying close to relaxed *through* the burst too, using the rest only as a comparison reference, not a recovery period.
- **Progression: once the burst-then-rest version is controlled, remove the rests** and chain the bursts back to back for a pure-speed variant.

## Three worked variations

1. **Two-string chromatic, 4+1 grouping.** 4 notes on one string (down-up-down-up), 1 downstroke on the next string, chromatic. Trains the specific string-switch of ending a group on an upstroke and immediately needing a downstroke on the next string. Self-check: if the first note on the new string ever lands as anything but a downstroke, hand sync is off.
2. **One-string, straight-16ths-into-16th-triplets.** A comfortable mid-tempo run of straight 16th notes (e.g. ascending A minor on one string) with a short burst of 16th-note triplets inserted, then back to the comfortable tempo. Prerequisite: don't start below ~80-90 BPM continuous straight 16ths — this is a speed-extension drill, not a beginner exercise.
3. **Odd 7-note chromatic grouping against a drum backing track.** 6 sixteenth notes + 1 eighth note + 1 eighth rest, repeated — the odd count against 4/4 constantly shifts the accent measure to measure, forcing active note-spacing awareness rather than muscle-memory autopilot. A double-bass drum backing track (not just a click) gives continuous feedback on whether the burst lands early/late/exactly on the grid.

## How to run it

- Pick any one variation, or rotate through all three across a week.
- Keep the picking motion the same physical size at high tempo as at low tempo — a growing motion signals tension and wastes energy/creates noise.
- Left hand: fingers stay close to the fretboard through the burst, worst-case check is the pattern's highest-to-lowest-finger reversal (pinky-down-to-index).

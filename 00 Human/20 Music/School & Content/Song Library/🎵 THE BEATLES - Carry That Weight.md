## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Carry That Weight]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #190.*

**Form.** He calls it the most symphonic thing in the catalogue, built as an A-B-A arch — except that **both the B section and the outro are lifted wholesale from "You Never Give Me Your Money"**. The reprise is transformed twice over: functionally, because an opening verse is re-cast as a mid-flight bridge, and rhetorically, because the massed orchestration realises heroic potential only latent in the original. There is no real intro; the last bar of "Golden Slumbers" is filled with a drum figure that launches this one without dropping a beat. Refrain is a square 16 (an 8-bar phrase repeated almost literally) whose **poetic pattern is ABBC** — phrase two opens by restating how phrase one closed, which is what makes a symmetrical section sound syncopated at the phrase level. Where "Golden Slumbers" runs on uneven phrases, this track is deliberately four-square.

**Harmony.** Refrain: `I – V | V – I` across eight bars, twice, with the bass walking down to B in the final bar. Bridge: the complete diatonic circle of fifths in A minor, `i – iv – ♭VII – ♭III – ♭VI – ii° – V – i`, then G13; the pivot both directions is C, read as III in A minor and I in C major ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]). Outro: `I – V(6/3) | A – (held)`. That last chord is the interesting one — the same progression slid into A major at the end of "... Money," but with C as home here the identical chords sound like drifting *away*, and only resolve retroactively once "The End" opens in A.

**Notable.** The bridge tune keeps one triadic outline that is a **literal inversion** of the refrain's ([[MEL-C03 — Motifs and how to develop them]]), and chord changes land squarely on downbeats while the tune repeatedly anticipates them ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]). The handoff into "The End" is the album's one *inexact* metric shift.

**Against this page.** Agrees on C major.

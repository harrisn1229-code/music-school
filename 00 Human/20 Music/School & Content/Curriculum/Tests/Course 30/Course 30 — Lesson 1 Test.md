> [!danger]- 🔒 Answer key — do not open until you have answered every question
> **Q1.** **Chord = the pitch content · voicing = the order and spacing · shape = one fingering**
> C major is C-E-G in any order and any octave — that is the chord. Arranging those notes in a particular order, spacing and register is the voicing. Fingering that voicing somewhere on the neck is the shape, and the same voicing has several shapes.
>
> **Q2.** **The voicing stays identical; the chord changes**
> Every interval relationship inside the grip is preserved, so it is the same voicing of a different chord. That is the cleanest proof the two are separate things.
>
> **Q3.** **Register and doubling**
> *Also accepted:* octave placement · which instruments play which notes · decay and reverb
> A 12-string doubles four of its courses in octaves, so a pitch nobody fingered can be one of the most audible notes in the sound. None of that survives into the letters "G7".
>
> **Q4.** **3rd and 7th · the extension you want · the root · the 5th**
> The guide tones define quality and function, the extension is the colour of the moment, the root is the bass player's job, and the 5th contributes nothing to identity.
>
> **Q5.** **The 5th — unless it is altered**
> A ♭5 or ♯5 is a colour tone rather than a 5th, so it stays.
>
> **Q6.** **Shell = root, 3rd, 7th · rootless = 3rd, 7th, extensions**
> The shell is three notes, unambiguous, and works anywhere. The rootless version hands the root to the bass and spends the freed finger on colour.
>
> **Q7.** **Drop 2 moves the 2nd-highest note down an octave, drop 3 the 3rd-highest — drop 2 is the workhorse**
> Drop 2 sits on strings 5-4-3-2 or 4-3-2-1 and covers most comping situations. Drop 3 is wider, with a bass note and a gap.
>
> **Q8.** **Perfect 4ths — instead of 3rds**
> 1-4-7 off a scale degree rather than 1-3-5, and you can keep stacking.
>
> **Q9.** **A stack of 3rds names a quality and so implies a function; a stack of 4ths names no quality**
> Major, minor and dominant are all defined by the arrangement of 3rds, so a tertian chord arrives already committed to a job. Quartal harmony states a set of notes and commits to nothing, so nothing wants to resolve.
>
> **Q10.** **Which note the ear treats as the root**
> The grip never moves. Depending on the assumed root the same three notes read as minor 11, ♭maj7♯11, sus4, 6/9, major 13 or 7sus4.
>
> **Q11.** **No player played the chord — it is a 12-string, a bass, a piano, a second guitar and room bleed at once**
> *Rubric (5):* says the sonority is produced by several instruments together, so no single instrument can reproduce it (2) · says register and doubling are therefore part of the chord's identity (2) · draws the conclusion that a chord symbol names pitch content and not a sound (1)
> The recognisable sound is the sum of instruments in a room, including the acoustic guitar's soundbox picking up everything else. Guitarists have chased it for six decades and every published solution keeps some elements and sacrifices others, because there is no single-instrument answer. Even the guitarist who played on the record named his own four-note shape correctly and said you would have to ask the bassist about the bass note. What that proves is the limit of chord symbols: a name fixes pitch classes, and everything that actually made the sound — register, doubling, decay, the room — lives outside the name.
>
> **Q12.** **A shell or rootless voicing, up out of the vocal's register**
> *Rubric (5):* keeps the 3rd and 7th and says why they are non-negotiable (2) · drops the root and the 5th, with the bass named as the reason the root is safe to drop (2) · addresses register — comping higher so the low mids stay clear under the voice (1)
> The 3rd and 7th are what make the chord that chord, so they stay; the 5th adds nothing to identity and goes first; the root is covered by the bass and its finger is better spent on the extension you actually want to hear. That leaves a shell or a rootless grip, which is also the answer to the arrangement problem: the same chord low on the neck is mud under a vocal and fine as a solo statement, so when something else occupies the low mids you comp higher. On an unfamiliar tune that combination is also the fastest to find, because a shell is unambiguous and transposes without thought.

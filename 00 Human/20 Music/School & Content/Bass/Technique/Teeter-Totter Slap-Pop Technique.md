# Teeter-Totter Slap-Pop Technique

BassBuzz's fix for slap-and-pop playing that's technically correct but slow and wasteful: a single continuous forearm motion — down for the slap, up for the pop — instead of two separate up-and-down trips. Named "teeter-totter" for the seesaw-like single arc; the video's own line is blunt about the alternative: **"you must teeter-totter, or else you're a teeter-noter."**

## The problem it fixes

The naive way to slap-and-pop looks correct in isolation but has a hard speed ceiling: rotate the forearm down to slap, rotate **all the way back up**, then come back down to grab the pop, then back up again — a **down-up-down-up**, four-part motion just to get one slap and one pop. It works, but "there's just no way in hell to play fast like that" — the wasted travel between the slap and the pop is the bottleneck, not finger speed or coordination.

## The fix — one continuous cycle

Slap the string, and **without twisting the forearm back up first**, get the thumb off the string — either by letting it bounce off naturally or by striking through the string — then hook the pop finger under the target string on the way past, and **twist back up with the forearm** to execute the pop. The whole slap-then-pop sequence becomes **one down/up cycle** instead of two round trips, which is what actually unlocks speed over time.

- Either exit style off the string works — bouncing off it or striking through it — the technique doesn't require one specific hand shape, just that the thumb doesn't reverse direction and travel back up before the pop happens.
- The pop finger gets positioned **under the string during the same upward half of the cycle** that lifts out of the slap — it isn't a separate motion tacked on after.

## Practicing it — string difficulty and rhythm discipline

- BassBuzz's paired drill pattern to test this directly: **slap–mute–slap–slap–pop**, repeated, watching for wasted motion creeping back in.
- **Harder variant: pop finger moves from the G string to the D string** — flagged as trickier specifically because the finger has to tuck further in to get under the D string, so accuracy (not just the teeter-totter motion itself) needs extra attention on that string.
- **Keep listening to the drums (especially the kick) for rhythm** while drilling this — the motion-efficiency fix doesn't replace the general rhythm discipline from [[Slap Thumb Technique — Angle, Anchoring, and Muting]]'s and [[Bass Note Muting Technique]]'s underlying accuracy/muting/note-length work; teeter-totter only fixes the *pop* half of the equation.

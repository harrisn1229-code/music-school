# The Evolution of Michael Jackson's Vocals: From Thriller & Beyond

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=14aAR1IgKVc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=14aAR1IgKVc)

## Synopsis

A genuinely detailed, era-by-era breakdown of Michael Jackson's vocal recording chain across his career with engineer Bruce Swedien — mic choice, preamp choice, and how the compression philosophy shifted with the format (analog tape vs. digital). Real, specific, actionable vocal-recording technique, not just history.

## Techniques

- **Thriller-era chain: Shure SM7 (dynamic) → Harrison console's own transformer mic pre → an 1176 set at an extreme 12:1 ratio used purely as a peak-catching safety net, not for musical compression** — the 1176 barely moves ("wasn't compressing, was just capturing, preventing overloads") whenever Michael's dynamic exceeded the normal window. The actual "compression"/coloring on these vocals came from **hitting analog tape hot deliberately** and relying on tape saturation's natural leveling as the real dynamics tool — engineer Bruce Swedien was famously anti-hardware-compressor ("compression is for kids") while being an expert at using tape compression skillfully in its place.
- **Mic choice is matched to the singer's specific vocal delivery, not fixed by artist or era**: an SM7 (low output level) suits a singer who "can pack a punch" — it was chosen because Michael could drive it hard; a soft/whispery singer would need a different, more sensitive microphone. All of *Thriller*'s vocals were cut on the SM7 for this reason.
- **Bad-era chain: same SM7, but a different, deliberately high-fidelity mic preamp (Studio Technologies) powered from an external marine/car battery** (charged separately, off the studio's own power) specifically to eliminate any power-supply noise or leakage in the signal path. This extreme noise-floor discipline was driven by the move to **digital recording** (a 32-track Mitsubishi digital format, described as flat and extremely transient-accurate — "what you put in is what you got back," unlike Sony's competing digital format which deliberately added coloration/warmth to compensate): digital is "less forgiving" than tape, so signal-path accuracy became critical in a way it wasn't when tape's own coloration was doing forgiving work.
- **Dangerous/HIStory-era chain: a specific Neve mic pre (a Brent Averill-built module, not a stock console channel) with the SM7 for most songs — but switched to a tube Neumann U47 specifically for ballads**, where Michael sang noticeably softer. The mic choice tracked the vocal delivery within a single artist's catalog: punchy uptempo vocals stayed on the low-output dynamic SM7, softer ballad delivery moved to the more sensitive tube condenser.
- **General principle across all three eras**: neither mic nor preamp choice was a fixed "house sound" — both were re-evaluated against (1) how hard/soft the specific vocal performance would be driven, and (2) how forgiving or unforgiving the recording medium (tape vs. a specific digital format) was of signal-path imperfection.

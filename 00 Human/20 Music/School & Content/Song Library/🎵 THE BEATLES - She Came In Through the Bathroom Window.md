## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — She Came In Through The Bathroom Window]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #188.*

**Form.** The nearest thing to a conventional song form anywhere in the medley, and it plays much bigger than it is because the two fragments ahead of it act as a foil. The formal wrinkle is that **a lone verse sits out front and the pair of verses falls between the two bridges**, reversing the usual distribution. The intro is not the song's own material: it is the last four bars of "Polythene Pam"'s outro, and the E resolving to A on the verse downbeat is the payoff for that entire build. Verse 16 bars, four equal phrases, poetic pattern AAAB; bridge an unusual **15 bars**, one short of the sixteen it seems to want, best parsed as **overlapping the verse's last two bars** so it begins before the verse has finished. The verse is rhythmically lopsided on purpose — the first three lines dodge the phrase downbeat and shift weight onto bar 3, the fourth lands on the downbeat for the first time.

**Harmony.** Very few chords. `Verse: I7 – IV7 (×3) · IV – I`. The bridge modulates to **♭III**, giving the medley another head-on A-versus-C confrontation: `I – iv | I – iv (pivot: iv = ii in C) | V – I | V – I → A`. The exit uses the **minor iv in a major key** as an unmistakable pivot ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]], [[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]), and the return has essentially no preparation — the A chord lands on the section's very last beat, which makes the next verse enter crooked the first time and is the punchline at the end.

**Notable.** The tune leans hard on **♭3 and ♭7** ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]), and that emphasis is what converts the chords into dominant sevenths — non-functional ones, since I7 can be rationalised as V-of-IV but IV7 has no such excuse ([[BLU-C02 — Why every chord is a dominant 7th]]). Two descending scalar basslines in the bridge, the second playing its changes out in the **3+3+2** grouping that also drives "Here Comes The Sun" and "Because." The Get Back-era take used a I – vi – IV verse; the release strips it to the starker I – IV.

**Against this page.** Agrees on A major.

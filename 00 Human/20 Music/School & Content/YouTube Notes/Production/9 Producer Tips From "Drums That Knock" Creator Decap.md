# 9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)

## Synopsis
Decap — creator of the widely-used "Drums That Knock" sample packs and a working trap/hip-hop producer with major placements and a UMPG publishing deal — runs through nine lessons that split evenly between craft and career. On the production side he covers his drums-first workflow, two different ways to capture natural-feeling grooves (micing a hit or playing live into Ableton's Capture function), his favorite method for 808 glides using Simpler/Sampler's MIDI pitch-bend range, and a direct rebuttal to the old "Ableton can't hit as hard as FL Studio" myth by pointing at the Glue Compressor as Ableton's answer to FL's Soft Clipper. On the career side he talks through what actually broke his own independent releases (SoundCloud's Slow Roast, then DistroKid to a Spotify Viral 50 placement with zero promo budget), why leverage — not a label deal — is the thing to build, how to handle creative dry spells depending on whether they're a few weeks or several months old, and the blunt, practical warning to never sign a publishing contract without a lawyer, drawn from his own experience with what three separate lawyers called one of the worst contracts they'd seen.

## Techniques
- **The "secret sauce" is just consistent grinding: work daily, listen constantly, study the greats.** Decap credits no shortcut — daily practice, deep listening to a mix of legends (JD, Pete Rock, Dr. Dre) and current releases, and a refusal to settle for "good enough" as the actual mechanism behind improvement. — Decap (Sol State), [9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)
- **Drums first, melody second — the drums set the groove that the melody then locks into.** Decap almost always programs the drums before writing melodies, because starting with drums gives the track its energy and groove, and everything layered on top ends up locking to that pocket; writing melody first still works but produces a noticeably different, less-bouncy result. — Decap (Sol State), [9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)
- **Groove secret #1 — record a percussion pass onto a microphone instead of programming it, since a mic'd take naturally lands in human pockets a drawn-in pattern won't.** Recording live vocal-percussion or hand-drumming and tracking it in captures the same micro-timing inconsistencies a real player has — you naturally hit pockets you wouldn't think to draw in by hand on a grid. — Decap (Sol State), [9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)
- **Groove secret #2 — load one-shots into a Drum Rack and play the groove live on a keyboard using Ableton's Capture button, so you never have to pre-arm record to keep a good take.** Load a kit of one-shots (Decap's own example: Drums That Knock Vol. 9) into a Drum Rack and freely play/improvise on a MIDI keyboard; Ableton's Capture feature is continuously recording in the background, so once you land a groove you like, you hit Capture retroactively instead of needing to have hit record before you started playing. → [[Rhythm and Groove]] — Decap (Sol State), [9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)
- **808 glides via Simpler/Sampler's MIDI tab: push Pitch Bend Range to 24 semitones, then map Pitch Bend to MIDI Control for the most control over the slide.** Decap's favorite of "a few ways" to do 808 glides: on a Simpler or Sampler instrument, open the MIDI tab and turn Pitch Bend Range all the way up to 24 semitones (two full octaves of bend range), then go to MIDI Control and assign Pitch Bend — this routes the pitch-bend wheel/lane directly into Ableton's pitch modulation, giving finer, more continuous control over the glide than drawing a pitch envelope by hand. → [[Bassline Writing]] — Decap (Sol State), [9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)
- **"Ableton can't hit as hard as FL" is false — Glue Compressor on the master is Ableton's equivalent of FL's Soft Clipper.** Pushing back on a persistent producer myth: FL Studio has a dedicated Soft Clipper that many credit for FL drums sounding louder/harder; Ableton has no device by that name, but the Glue Compressor behaves almost identically when driven on the master bus — meaning Ableton drums can knock just as hard as FL's once you reach for the right device instead of assuming the DAW is the limitation. → [[Glue Compressor]] — Decap (Sol State), [9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)
- **You don't need a label — independent distribution alone got a Decap beat to #6 on Slow Roast (SoundCloud) and #6 on Spotify's Viral 50, with zero promo.** Two separate real outcomes cited as proof: a track uploaded to the independent SoundCloud imprint Slow Roast hit #6 on their charts, and a separate upload through DistroKid reached #6 on the Spotify Viral 50 chart — both achieved with no paid promotion, just uploading and letting the platforms' own discovery do the work. — Decap (Sol State), [9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)
- **Build leverage — a strong social following, streaming numbers, or an already-strong product — before you ever need a label deal, and never sign away ownership for an upfront "bag."** The long-term goal should always be understanding your own leverage and owning your masters/brand rather than chasing a label signing; the more leverage you walk in with (audience size, streaming platform strength, an already-proven product), the better the terms you can negotiate if a deal does make sense. The single highest-leverage move of all, per Decap, is simply making great music — that's what generates every other form of leverage. — Decap (Sol State), [9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)
- **Fixing a creative dry spell depends on how long it's been going — a short slump wants a break, a long one (months) wants the opposite: make beats without judging any of them.** If you've been productive recently and just feel temporarily uninspired, step away entirely (walk, sleep, watch something unrelated, get outside) and come back with fresh perspective. If it's been a genuine multi-month block, taking a break is the wrong move — instead grind out beat after beat without judging quality (the first few will likely be bad), because somewhere around the fifth or sixth attempt an idea worth chasing tends to surface; a large share of Decap's ~3,000 beats exist purely from this unjudged, high-volume mode. — Decap (Sol State), [9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)
- **Never sign a publishing contract without a lawyer, and never take the upfront money at the cost of ownership.** Decap's own UMPG deal — after 450+ records delivered since signing — has been called the worst publishing contract by all three lawyers he's since hired to review it; his hard rule now is that any contract, no matter how many pages or how tempting the upfront advance, gets read by a lawyer first, because a lawyer is what translates the fine print and helps you actually find your negotiating leverage. Young artists chasing the upfront "bag" without that step routinely trade away ownership of their own career for a short-term payout. — Decap (Sol State), [9 Producer Tips From "Drums That Knock" Creator Decap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGCa4b49blA)

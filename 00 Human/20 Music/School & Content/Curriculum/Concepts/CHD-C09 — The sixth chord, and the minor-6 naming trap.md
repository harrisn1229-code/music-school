# CHD-C09 — The sixth chord, and the minor-6 naming trap

**Q.** How is a 6th chord built, what is the minor-6 naming trap, and which other chord shares its notes?

> [!question]- Answer
> A major or minor triad with the **6th degree added on top and no 7th** — a milder colour than a 7th. The trap: **the added 6th is always the major 6th, even on a minor triad** — Bm6 = B D F♯ **G♯**, never G natural. The "minor" names only the triad. A minor triad with the actual flat 6 is a different chord, written m♭6. And a 6th chord is note-for-note its **relative minor 7th**: C6 = Am7, B♭6 = Gm7.

## Why it matters

The 6th is where colour and naming collide, and the collision is real rather than pedantic. When there is no bass to anchor a root, C6 and Am7 are genuinely undecidable and either name is defensible — the opening a cappella chord of "Bohemian Rhapsody" is the standing example. There is also a notation collision worth carrying: **classical figured bass writes a superscript 6 for first inversion**, so "C6" in a harmonic analysis means C/E, while "C6" on a jazz chart means C E G A. Which tradition you are reading disambiguates it; nothing in the symbol does.

## Drill

Spell Bm6 and Bm♭6 and hear the difference. Then play B♭6 and Gm7 back to back and find a bass note that makes each one unambiguous.

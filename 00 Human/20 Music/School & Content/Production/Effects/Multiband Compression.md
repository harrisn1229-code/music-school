# Multiband Compression

General best practice for band-split dynamics — compressing, ducking, or sidechaining one frequency range without touching the rest. Specific tools: [[Pro-MB]], [[OTT]], [[Saturn 2]] (dynamic bands). See also [[Compression]], [[Sidechaining]], [[Masking and Carving]].

## What it is
Dynamics per frequency band — so you can duck only the sub, tame only the low-mids, or glue with a gentle upward multiband, instead of clamping the whole signal.

## Owned (first-call)
[[Pro-MB]] · [[OTT]] (upward/downward multiband) · [[Pro-Q 4]] (dynamic bands) · [[Ozone 9 Vintage Compressor]]

## From videos
- **A fixed dB of attenuation moves a track's perceived loudness (LUFS) far more when targeted at ~3.5 kHz than at ~30 Hz — a mastering-relevant multiband lever for reducing loudness with minimal audible change.** LUFS metering already weights frequencies by perceptual sensitivity; on a complex full track, cutting a mid-high band (2–5 kHz, a major loudness contributor) drops overall LUFS far more than cutting the same dB amount from a sub-heavy band, since sub content contributes far less to perceived loudness in the first place. A multiband compressor with a band centered in the 2–5 kHz zone is a targeted way to pull loudness down without an audible change to the low end. → [[Compression]], [[Clipping and Loudness]] — Sage Audio, [The Science of Perfect Compression](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L_MSH8_HCek)
- **Multiband/dynamics processing on the low-mids carries a real risk of shifting a note's perceived pitch by a semitone — a waveshaping side effect worth knowing before compressing that range hard.** Each semitone in the low-mids is a small absolute Hz gap (A3/220 Hz to A♯3/233 Hz is only ~13 Hz apart), so any processor that reshapes the waveform enough to double a peak/trough (heavy compression, saturation, clipping, gating) can shift perceived pitch by that margin. When multiband-compressing a low-mid band specifically, keep an ear on pitch stability, not just level — this compounds with the fact that the low-mids are already the busiest, most-overlapping zone in a mix (see [[Masking and Carving]]). → [[Masking and Carving]], [[MIDS Slot]] — Sage Audio, [Mids - the Most Important Frequency Range](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23pezFNXW_4)
- **Three-tool division of labor across one lead-vocal chain: a resonance suppressor for dynamic harshness, a subtractive multiband EQ for static problem zones, and a plain EQ for the only boosting.** [[Soothe2]] (Mix 38%) chases harsh frequencies as they occur; a McDSP C6-style ALL-subtractive multiband stage (owned stand-in [[Pro-MB]]) then does low/high shelving, problem-frequency notches, and de-essing — "I'm not doing any boosting with this multiband, I'm doing all subtractive work getting rid of what I don't like." Boosting is reserved entirely for a separate SSL-style EQ upstream. A narrow multiband-cut sweep on the lead vocal, 2.5–4.5 kHz, targets where "her voice hits certain frequencies, they pop out a lot." Stated rule of thumb: never run a resonance-suppressor-style plugin near 100% Mix on vocals. → [[Soothe2]], [[Pro-MB]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[De-essing]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]] — Mix with the Masters (Josh Gudwin), [[Mixing Dua Lipa's vocals with Josh Gudwin]]
- **Reactive multiband dynamics as a spectral-balancing alternative to static EQ on a lead vocal — most reduction kept on the low end, top end left almost fully open except at moments that trigger it.** Illangelo (The Weeknd), on [[Pro-MB]] late in his lead-vocal chain: "the most of the reduction is happening on the low end. In the top end, it's allowing almost all of it in except for some moments of the audio will trigger it and that's good, we want that... if you were to do this just with a standard EQ, it's not going to sound as alive as when you're doing it with something that reacts instantly in the moment as needed." Describes the underlying approach as something he developed himself years ago "out of necessity," not a plugin default. → [[Pro-MB]], [[The Weeknd Vocal Chain]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]] — Illangelo (Mix with the Masters), [[Producing The Weeknd's vocals with Illangelo]]

C4 is doing good work, I really like that." - **Dynamic EQ vs. multiband compression: a decision rule by which kind of control you actually need.** Dynamic EQ offers more Q/slope control per band and typically far more available bands — Pro-Q 3 up to **26 dynamic bands** versus [[Pro-MB]]'s **6** — but its dynamics section is thin, usually just a manual/automatic threshold. Multiband compression trades band count for a full compressor's toolkit per band: attack, release, look-ahead, knee. Want pinpoint control over *which frequency* and *how narrowly* → dynamic EQ; want control over *how the dynamics themselves behave* → multiband compression. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Pro-MB]], [[EQ]] — Sage Audio, [EQ Masterclass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iPBnL6mx9c0)
- **Multiband compression as dynamic EQ — duck a problem band only when it's actually too loud, not statically.** Two mastering examples: on a muddy mix with buried vocals, center a band on **250 Hz, attenuate 1–2 dB**; when reverb masks the vocal, dynamically attenuate **2–5 kHz on the side image only** so the mid-image vocal cuts through whenever it's present, without a static cut dulling the reverb tail the rest of the time. The highest band can run fast attack/release safely — the harmonic-distortion risk from quick settings is mainly a low-frequency artifact. → [[Pro-MB]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Master Chain]] — Sage Audio, [Mastering Compression Settings](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YUn31aE9X_8)
- **Tune multiband compression to mimic how the human ear itself compresses loud sound.** The ear attenuates frequencies **below 1 kHz** by 1–20 dB depending on loudness, and the physical structures responsible can't react instantly — ~**40 ms** to contract, staying contracted ~**150 ms**. Recipe: a band below 1 kHz, a couple dB of attenuation, **Attack 40 ms, Release 150 ms**, a softer knee for gradual compression, no lookahead. Reads as more naturally "compressed" than an arbitrary setting because it reproduces the ear's own response curve rather than fighting it. → [[Pro-MB]] — Sage Audio, [Mastering Compression Settings](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YUn31aE9X_8)
- **[[Saturn 2]]'s multiband architecture as a per-frequency loudness-without-limiting move — vary the amount of upward saturation by band instead of one blanket setting.** Applied to a master alongside Subtle Tube/Tape saturation + the Dynamic style (a limiting-free way to amplify quiet detail, see [[Clipping and Loudness]]): introduce more density on a band that needs it, none on a band that's already loud enough, rather than driving the whole spectrum the same amount. Oversampling and linear-phase processing are available if higher settings start introducing mix/dry-blend artifacts or aliasing. → [[Clipping and Loudness]], [[Master Chain]], [[Limiting]] — Sage Audio, [Stop Limiting Your Masters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s_LDCzWx20g)
- **Research-backed dynamic-range target by octave, and a way to measure your own master against it.** A 2014 AES paper (Pastana & Rice, 53rd AES convention, 928 commercially successful songs) measured peak-to-RMS crest factor per octave: ~16:1 in the lowest octave (most compressed/controlled) up to ~28:1 in the highest (least controlled). For mastering, collapse the 8 measured octaves into 3 wider bands so corrections spread more naturally rather than targeting one narrow octave. Method: split into those 3 bands with a free multiband splitter (**Isolate**, not owned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]), insert a peak meter + RMS meter, solo each band, play the track, and compare the measured ratio to the study's figures — compress a band that's too dynamic relative to the target, expand one that's over-squashed, then re-check overall tonal balance. Too slow for every session, but worth doing once to calibrate what "standard" actually measures like, then deviate deliberately rather than by accident. → [[Clipping and Loudness]], [[SUB Slot]], [[BASS Slot]], [[MIDS Slot]], [[AIR Slot]] — Sage Audio, [Audio Mastering Science: Using Research to Make Informed Decisions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21dpmumO4So)
- **Sidechain the *sub band only*, leave the highs moving** — on a Juno bass, multiband-sidechain ~100 Hz-and-down to the kick and leave the upper bands unducked: the high harmonics don't clash with the kick, so keeping them un-pumped preserves the note's life while the sub still gets out of the kick's way. → [[Sidechaining]], [[SUB Slot]] — Disclosure, [Never Enough](https://youtu.be/lGopQwQo9Ag)
- **Double-sidechain: gentle wideband + aggressive lows-only.** A wideband ducker (~3–4 dB) plus a [[Pro-MB]] band ducking *only* the sub (~12 dB) off the kick keeps a bass present while its low end gets fully out of the kick's way — Disclosure, [Deeper (with Leon Thomas)](https://youtu.be/SM0FZ58ckVw)
- **[[OTT]] to tighten a wide bass's mono center** — on a chorus-heavy synth bass, a touch of upward OTT makes the mono low end punchier without squashing ("a push, not a squash") — Disclosure, [Deeper (with Leon Thomas)](https://youtu.be/SM0FZ58ckVw)
- **Frequency-specific sidechain: duck only the sub, keep the detail.** With [[Pro-MB]], trigger from the kick (or vocal) and compress *only* the bass's sub band — leave everything above it untouched, instead of ducking the whole bassline's volume — Disclosure, [Higher Than Ever Before](https://youtu.be/9XkHSronZck)
- **Dip a single problem band on a bus** — [[Pro-MB]] slightly pulling the low-mids on a drum bus is cleaner than a static EQ cut because it only acts when they get loud — Disclosure, [Higher Than Ever Before](https://youtu.be/9XkHSronZck)
- **Subtle [[OTT]] as glue, backed right off.** At ~23% depth OTT ties a stacked vocal together like a bus comp ("sounds like a G-bus") without the pumped, over-processed OTT sound — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8)
- **Multiband on the lows so the kick pumps the sub loop** — compress the low band keyed off the kick so a bassy groove ducks to let the kick knock through — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8)
- **[[Soothe2]] as a precise dynamic sidechain** (a multiband cousin): key it off the vocal on the synth bus so the synths give up *exactly* the frequencies the vocal occupies — sharper and more surgical than a wideband ducker like Trackspacer. See [[Sidechaining]] — Disclosure, [Looking For Love](https://youtu.be/NlJt0pCptGk)

# Most countries don't use ABCDEFG for music notes (Solfege explained)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MVA8bgSBt5A)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MVA8bgSBt5A)

## Synopsis

The A–G letter-naming convention is actually a minority system globally — most countries (most of Europe, Latin America, French Canada, Arabic/Persian-speaking countries, many Asian countries) use solfège (Do Re Mi Fa Sol La Ti) as their default note-naming vocabulary, not just as an ear-training mnemonic the way it's used in English-speaking contexts.

## Techniques

- **Solfège is a full 1:1 replacement system, not an ear-training add-on**: C D E F G A B map directly to Do Re Mi Fa Sol La Ti — same seven pitches, same function, just different syllables. Sharps/flats get modifier words the same way English says "D sharp"/"D flat."
- **Geographic split**: English and Dutch-speaking countries use the alphabetical A–G system; most of continental Europe, Latin America, French-speaking Canada, Arabic/Persian-speaking regions, and many Asian countries default to solfège as the actual naming convention musicians use day to day — not the fixed-do ear-training tool it's often treated as in English-language pedagogy.
- **Practical implication for reading foreign-language music theory sources or working with international musicians**: a chord chart or theory discussion in a solfège-default language will name pitches as Do/Re/Mi rather than C/D/E — worth recognizing so it doesn't read as an unfamiliar system rather than just a different vocabulary for the same notes.

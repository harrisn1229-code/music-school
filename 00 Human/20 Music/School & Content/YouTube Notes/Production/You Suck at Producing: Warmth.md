# You Suck at Producing: Warmth

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yv3v9SsoDMw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yv3v9SsoDMw)

## Synopsis
A "cold beat → warm beat" makeover built through a running bit (drums and keys personified as two unruly kids who need "disciplining"), but underneath the comedy is a genuinely reusable warmth checklist. On drums: Glue Compressor's "Drum Full Parallel" preset for one-device parallel compression, backed off from its default aggressiveness and gain-matched with a Utility, with the resulting makeup-gain push producing audible soft clipping/crunch that's kept deliberately (A/B'd against the clean, uncrunched version to prove the point). Silence between hits gets filled with a looped white-noise sample so there's no dead air. On the melodic element: a very light touch of Overdrive (~5% wet) for subtle grit, plus a sidechain compressor keyed from the kick so the part ducks slightly on every hit — glue and warmth from movement, not just tone. The whole thing is finished with a soft ambient "blanket" — a vinyl-crackle-plus-nature-sounds texture sample laid under the entire mix — to read as one cohesive, worn-in recording rather than a pile of separate, pristine digital elements.

## Techniques
- **Glue Compressor's "Drum Full Parallel" preset as one-device parallel compression** — rather than routing a separate parallel-compression send, load Glue Compressor's Drum Full Parallel preset directly on the drum bus; it blends a heavily-compressed copy with the clean signal inside the one device. → [[Glue Compressor]], [[Parallel Processing]]
- **Back the preset's Threshold off, then gain-match with a Utility afterward** — the preset's default gain reduction is extreme (visibly hammering the meter); raising the Threshold reduces how hard it's ducking so the drums stay louder and less squashed, then a Utility after the compressor brings the level back to match the original for a fair comparison. → [[Glue Compressor]], [[Utility]]
- **Pushed makeup gain after parallel comp produces deliberate soft clipping — a wanted "crunch," not an accident** — turning up the compressor's makeup/output gain hot enough to soft-clip is A/B'd directly against the same signal with the clipping bypassed; the un-clipped version is flatly worse — the crunch is the point. → [[Glue Compressor]], [[Clipping and Loudness]]
- **Loop a white-noise sample to fill silence between sparse drum hits** — pull a short noise sample (from the same drum sample/kit) and loop it under the gaps between hits so there's no dead air between them, reading as a warmer, more continuous drum bed. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]
- **A very light touch of Overdrive (~5% dry/wet) on a melodic/keys part for subtle warmth** — a much stronger Overdrive setting was tried first and rejected as too much for a "sensitive" part; pulled back to roughly 5% wet it adds just a touch of crunch without becoming an obvious distortion effect. → [[Overdrive]]
- **Sidechain the keys/pad to the kick for movement-based warmth, not just tone-based warmth** — a compressor on the melodic track, sidechained from the kick drum, makes that part duck slightly on every kick hit; audibly, the part is heard "ducking out of the way" of the kick, and that rhythmic interaction reads as extra warmth/glue on top of the EQ/saturation moves. → [[Sidechaining]]
- **Blanket the whole mix with a soft ambient/vinyl-crackle texture sample for cohesion** — a single sample combining vinyl crackle with soft ambient/nature sound (birds) is dragged under the entire mix at a low level; the "blanket" framing is literal — one shared texture layer gluing the separate elements into something that reads as one cohesive, worn recording rather than a pile of clean digital parts. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]

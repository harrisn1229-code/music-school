> [!danger]- 🔒 Answer key — do not open until you have answered every question
> **Q1.** **Lengthens the first and shortens the second**
> An uneven long–short division of the beat, leaning toward a triplet feel.
>
> **Q2.** **A shuffle is a triplet with the middle note removed**
> Long, short — the first and third of the three.
>
> **Q3.** **Exactly 2:1 — swing sits anywhere between straight and full triplet**
> Straight is 1:1; full triplet swing is 2:1 (long = two of the beat's three triplet parts, short = one); swing is the continuum between. The triplet itself divides the beat into three *equal* parts — the 2:1 is what's left once the middle one goes.
>
> **Q4.** **Because the endpoints are two ratios and everything between them is playable**
> Any point on that continuum is a valid feel, which is why a swing control is a percentage.
>
> **Q5.** **Where a part sits against the grid, on purpose — late is relaxed, early is urgent**
> Late is heavy and laid back; early is driving.
>
> **Q6.** **Roughly 5–30 ms — past about 40 it reads as a mistake**
> Beyond that the ear stops hearing feel and starts hearing a timing error.
>
> **Q7.** **Elements disagreeing — kick and snare on the grid, hats late**
> Everything equally late just sounds slow. Pocket is a relationship between parts, not an offset applied to all of them.
>
> **Q8.** **Quantize anything meant to be machine-tight; humanize anything meant to read as played**
> 808s and sidechained elements quantize. Parts standing in for a performance humanize.
>
> **Q9.** **Full quantize kills the feel and full humanize scatters it — use 50–75% strength**
> Partial strength pulls a performance *toward* the grid while keeping its character.
>
> **Q10.** **Timing and velocity offsets extracted from one piece of material**
> The payoff is extraction: pull the feel off a break you like and apply it to your own stiff MIDI.
>
> **Q11.** **Polyrhythm stacks conflicting subdivisions; hemiola regroups the same pulse**
> Polyrhythm is three against two at once. Hemiola is three groups of two against two groups of three.
>
> **Q12.** **"Nice cup of tea" — *nice* and *of* are the 2, *nice cup tea* are the 3**
> *Rubric (5):* gives the phrase and assigns the syllables to each side correctly (2) · describes a dotted-quarter pulse against 4/4, three eighths against a bar of eight (2) · says it realigns every three bars (1)
> "Nice" is the shared downbeat where both cycles start together. "Nice" and "of" fall on the duple side; "nice", "cup" and "tea" fall on the triple side. The practical hemiola is a dotted-quarter pulse laid over 4/4 — each hit is worth three eighths against a bar of eight, so the pattern is out of phase with the barline and drifts through it, coming back into alignment after three bars: eight dotted quarters make twenty-four eighths, which is exactly three 4/4 bars.

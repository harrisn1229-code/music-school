## Synopsis

BERNTH takes a plain, "boring"-sounding A minor cadence (Am–G–C–G) and turns it atmospheric using nothing but **open (spread) triads** — the same root-3rd-5th notes already known, rearranged with wider intervals and different inversions, no added theory complexity. This is the **chord/comping-side companion** to [[Open Voice Triads for Soloing]] (that page's source video is explicitly the soloing half of a two-part lesson, with "the companion covering using the same shapes as chords/comping" flagged as not yet in the vault) — this video fills that gap.

## What's taught

- **Two moves that turn a close, root-on-bottom triad into an "open" one: (1) use inversions — don't always put the root on the bottom, and (2) spread the three notes across a wider interval (skip strings) instead of stacking them adjacently.** Worked on A minor (root A, ♭3 C, 5th E): the close voicing (root-♭3-5th stacked tight) sounds flat; spreading the same three notes wider with the ♭3 moved to a higher string produces the "much more interesting" atmospheric sound — same pitches, no new notes, just re-ordered and spread.
- **Full worked cadence, chord by chord, showing an open-triad voicing for each:**
  - **Am** — open A string (root), E (5th), A (root, octave), C (♭3, on G string), E (5th, open high E) — root-position but spread across all six strings.
  - **G major** — inverted with the 3rd (B) as the lowest note (7th fret low E), then root G (5th fret D string), then 5th (D) on top — a 3rd-position inversion, not root-position.
  - **C major** — root C on the A string, 5th G below it, major 3rd E on top (fingered root-index/5th-ring/3rd-pinky in that string order) — another inversion choice, not the "obvious" root-lowest voicing.
  - **G major (return)** — deliberately voiced *differently* from the first G major shape (5th as the bass note this time, then 3rd, then root on top) specifically to avoid repeating the same inversion twice in one cadence, and to preserve an ascending bass-note contour across the progression instead of resetting downward.
- **Avoid voicing repetition across a cadence — vary the inversion each time a chord repeats**, both for a less predictable sound and to let the lowest notes trace a intentional melodic contour (ascending, in the worked example) across the whole progression rather than jumping around.
- **Extending open triads with one extra color tone on top, once the plain shapes are comfortable** (optional, not required for the core sound): Am + a 9th (D) on top; the first G major voicing + its 9th (E, i.e. the 6th of the key) on top; C major + major 7th (B) on top; final G voicing + root dropped a half step for a major-7-flavored close. Framed as a later refinement — "the basic open triads already sound really amazing" without any extensions.
- **Practice value called out directly:** open triads with wide stretches double as a genuinely useful finger-independence/stretching exercise, separate from their harmonic payoff.

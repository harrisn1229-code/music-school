# Jack Ruch — Start Adding the Melodic Minor Scale to the Minor Blues

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EDFzv95I9Vk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EDFzv95I9Vk)

## Synopsis

Jack Ruch (blues/jazz guitar teacher — see also [[Jack Ruch - Tricks and Tips for Soloing Over the Most Common Blues Progression]], [[Secondary Dominant Insertion at a Minor Blues Chord Change]], [[Dominant-Altered Superimposition Over a Minor Chord]]) plays down a full two-chorus solo he wrote and transcribed over a **12-bar minor blues in A** (`Am7 / Dm7 / F7 / E7`, with a **minor ii–V–i turnaround** — `Bm7♭5 – E7 – Am` — closing each chorus instead of the page's more commonly-logged ♭VI7–V7 turnaround), then breaks it down chord-change by chord-change to show exactly which mode of the A melodic minor scale (and its relatives) covers which spot. This is a compact, systematic "one scale family, mapped across an entire form" lesson rather than a single-lick video — every chord of the progression gets a named mode, and the same mapping repeats (with busier phrasing) on the second chorus, making the video a ready-made drill sheet for transposing the whole system into other keys. The most concrete, drillable material is the chord-by-chord mode map itself (below), the "relate every altered/Lydian-dominant mode back to a melodic-minor scale you already know" mental shortcut Ruch uses throughout, and the two specific minor-major-9 arpeggio shapes (off Am and off Dm) that anchor the tonic-chord lines.

## What's taught

**Form:** 12-bar minor blues in A — `Am7 (i, 4 bars) / Dm7 (iv, 2 bars) / Am7 (i, 2 bars) / F7 (♭VI7, 1 bar) / E7 (V7, 1 bar) / Bm7♭5 (ii) – E7 (V) – Am (i) turnaround (2 bars)`. The **V (E7) stays dominant** even though the tonic is minor, matching [[Blues Form and Harmony]] § *Minor blues*. Two choruses are played back to back — chorus 1 is deliberately simpler, chorus 2 is "a little bit busier" using the same mode map, framed explicitly as a build-intensity structural device ("since it's the second time through the solo, I want to start building it up").

**The melodic minor scale itself:** natural minor with the **6th and 7th degrees raised** — A melodic minor = A B C D E F♯ G♯. Raising those two degrees is what gives it a "really bright sound" over minor chords and opens up extra arpeggio options (specifically the minor-major 9 chord/arpeggio) that plain Aeolian/Dorian minor pentatonic vocabulary doesn't have.

**Chord-by-chord mode map (the spine of the lesson):**

| Chord | Bars | Mode used | Relationship stated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Am7 (i) | 1–4 | **A melodic minor** (tonic, straight) | Raised 6th/7th; A minor-major-9 arpeggio |
| A7 (superimposed secondary dominant, bar 4) | — | **A altered** (VII mode of melodic minor) | Same notes as B♭ melodic minor |
| Dm7 (iv) | 5–6 | (resolution target — chord tones of Dm7, esp. the 9th/A) | — |
| Am7 (i) | 7–8 | A minor pentatonic blues + raised 6th | melodic-minor flavor without leaving pentatonic |
| F7 (♭VI7, "flat 6 dominant") | 9 | **F Lydian dominant** (IV mode of melodic minor) | Same notes as C melodic minor; F7 reasoned as the **tritone substitution of B7** (the "two dominant," i.e. V7/V in the key of A) — so F Lydian dominant plays like "a B altered scale," letting Ruch borrow B7→E7 dominant-blues-in-B vocabulary wholesale for the F7→E7 move |
| E7 (V) | 10 | **E altered** (VII mode of melodic minor) | Same notes as F melodic minor |
| Bm7♭5 (ii, turnaround) | 11 (beat 1–2) | **B Locrian natural 2** (vi mode of melodic minor) | Same notes as D melodic minor, started on B |
| E7 (V, turnaround) | 11 (beat 3–4) | **E altered** (same as above) | Same notes as F melodic minor |
| Am (i, resolution) | 12 | A minor-major-9 arpeggio / chord tones | closes the chorus |

This table is a worked instance of [[Scale Modes Reference]]'s own Melodic Minor mode chart (tonic i = mΔ/Aeolian nat.6,nat.7; IV = 7/Lydian ♭7; VII = 7/Locrian ♭4,♭♭7; vi = ø/Locrian nat.2) — every mode Ruch names in this video maps directly onto that table's rows.

**Arpeggio detail on the tonic chord:** the opening A-melodic-minor line is spelled out explicitly as an **A minor-major 9 arpeggio** starting on the 9th and descending — 9th → major 7th → 5th → ♭3rd → 9th → root. A second minor-major-9 shape is used **off D** (over the Dm7 chord in the turnaround section of chorus 2) the same way. Both times the raised 6th and major 7th are called out as the notes to specifically emphasize — "really hitting those altered notes... resolving to some chord tones," "really emphasizing that major 7th note from the melodic minor scale" — the single defining color that separates a melodic-minor-flavored line from a plain Dorian/Aeolian one (same distinction already logged on [[Scale Modes Reference]] and [[Tonic Minor Soloing]] from other Jack Ruch/Jens Larsen videos).

**Secondary dominant at bar 4 (Am → Dm):** A7 is superimposed as **V7/iv** (the dominant five chord of D minor) in the bar before the Dm7 change — the exact same device already documented in detail (arpeggio → diminished-7 substitute → altered voicings) on [[Secondary Dominant Insertion at a Minor Blues Chord Change]]; this video adds a fourth confirmation that the **A altered scale** (not just altered-chord-tone voicings) is a valid way to play that same one-bar insert, phrased as a line built from "B minor-major 9" territory resolving down to the 5th of Dm7 (the note A).

**The tritone-sub mental shortcut over ♭VI7:** rather than deriving F Lydian dominant from scratch, Ruch reasons it as "F7 is the tritone sub of B7, and B7 is the two-dominant (V7/V) in the key of A" — so any B7-to-E7 dominant-blues-in-B vocabulary a player already knows transfers directly onto the F7-to-E7 move, just relocated to F Lydian dominant instead of B altered. This is a reusable derivation shortcut, not just a one-off fact about this progression.

**Minor ii–V–i turnaround, played whether or not the band plays it:** the closing `Bm7♭5 – E7 – Am` is explicitly framed as implyable harmony — "even if the backing track or the band isn't playing these chords, you can still imply this harmony to create some really interesting lines in your playing." This is a different turnaround shape from the ♭VI7→V7 turnaround already logged multiple times on [[Turnarounds]] and [[Blues Form and Harmony]] for other minor blues in A — a genuine ii–V–i substitute for the turnaround slot, not the ♭VI-then-V shape.

**Split-bar practice note:** the turnaround's Bm7♭5–E7 is a **split bar, two beats per chord** — Ruch calls out directly that there isn't much time to fully flesh out either scale's sound in that space, so the line has to weave in a few colorful notes rather than run the full scale — a real-time-budget constraint worth practicing on its own (playing a full mode in two beats vs. four).

**Ending color:** over the final E7 of chorus 1, Ruch hits the **♭9** (the transcript garbles this as "flat N," almost certainly ♭9) for an extra altered color before resolving.

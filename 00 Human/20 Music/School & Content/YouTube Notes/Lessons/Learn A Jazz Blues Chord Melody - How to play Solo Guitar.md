# Learn A Jazz Blues Chord Melody - How to play Solo Guitar

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YStKvDWkqSc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YStKvDWkqSc)

## Synopsis

Jens Larsen breaks down a full 12-bar-blues chord-melody solo bar-by-bar, using it to teach the mechanics of playing an improvised melody *and* a harmonized/bass-line accompaniment simultaneously on one guitar — the technique, not just the specific solo, is the point. The core device is **call-and-response between chord and melody**: state a shell voicing, then answer it with a single-note melodic line, repeated bar to bar, rather than trying to sustain a full chord under every melody note.

## What's taught

- **Shell voicings are the backbone of chord-melody, not full voicings.** "There's no way I can lay down a complete chord" while also playing a moving melody — shell voicings (root-3rd-7th) leave enough free fingers/strings to add a melodic line on top.
- **Call-and-response structure, bar by bar.** Play the shell voicing first (states the harmony), then move into a single-note melody answering it — demonstrated on the C7 (bar 1: shell, then a C6-arpeggio-based melody with a ghost-note bass fill to keep momentum) and F7 (bar 2: shell, then a harmonized melodic idea built from an F6 arpeggio).
- **Ghost notes and repeated lowest-chord-tone as a pseudo bass line.** When there isn't room for a real walking bass line under a solo, repeat the chord's lowest note (often the 3rd, not the root) on the beat to imply groove and forward motion — "not really perceived as a walking bass... but it does work to just have that movement in there."
- **Melody has priority over harmony.** "The melody is actually the most important thing" — worked example on `Gm7 → C7alt` (bar 4): the melody line comes first, landing cleanly on the target note, and the chord underneath is added *after*, almost as an afterthought/embellishment, sometimes even omittable entirely ("this would have worked too").
- **Splitting a shell voicing into bass-then-melody.** Rather than stating bass note and melody note simultaneously, play the bass note first, let it ring, then add the melody on top — gives the line room to breathe and separates the two functional layers audibly.
- **Fingerstyle right-hand approach.** Mostly thumb + 3 fingers, no fixed system — three-note chords typically use three fingers with the ring finger doubling for melody; four-note chords bring in the thumb, which is also the finger used for the repeated bass-note device (a practical reason the two techniques pair naturally).
- **Multiple fingerings for the same shell voicing, chosen by what's needed next.** Different fingerings of the same shell (e.g. with the ♭5 vs. with the natural 5th reachable) unlock different melodic continuations — deliberately practice switching between fingerings of a shape rather than settling on one, since "there's not one version that's gonna be better than the rest."
- **Barre vs. fingerstyle tradeoff on shell voicings.** A barred version of a shell voicing is fine fingerstyle but breaks down with a pick (extra strings ring that shouldn't) — worth knowing both a barre and a non-barre fingering of key shells.
- **Diminished-chord melody-first phrasing.** On an F♯dim passing chord: state the melody note alone first, then add the rest of the diminished chord underneath afterward (two different inversions shown, ♭5-in-bass and root-in-bass), keeping the line — not the chord — as the primary event.
- **Reading a "hidden" bass line inside a chord's inner voice.** Letting go of one note of a chord (an E) can expose another note (an A) still ringing, which — followed across a couple of chords — outlines its own implied bass motion (a leading tone up to B♭, then down to A7) even though no separate bass line was deliberately composed; a byproduct of voice-leading awareness rather than a planned line.
- **The whole solo is over a blues form**, moving through C7 → F7 (with a 9 and a 13 as top-note color choices, named loosely — "you just have to think C7 and these are things you can choose to use") → F♯dim (passing) → back to C7 → Gm7/C7alt turnaround territory (transcript continues past the excerpted portion into the final four bars).

# VOI-C16 — Guide-tone diads: the minimum viable voicing

**Q.** What is the smallest thing you can play that still states a dominant chord, and how does it move through a blues?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Two notes — the 3rd and the ♭7.** They alone fix the quality: change the 3rd and the chord flips major to minor, change the ♭7 to a major 7 and it stops being a dominant. With a bass player covering the root, that is all the guitar owes the chord. Through a blues in A the **identical shape moves by half step** — down a half step for the IV, up a half step for the V.

## Why it matters

It is the floor of the whole strand, and everything larger is built on it: the rootless 9ths and 13ths are this pair with colour added on top. It is also the arrangement fix for a crowded track — two notes leave room for a vocal where a barre chord does not. The sus4 raise and the low-diad-against-high-stab alternation are embellishments of the same two notes.

## Drill

15 min: play a 12-bar blues in A with nothing but the 3rd-♭7 diad, moving by half step for the IV and V. Then add the raise of the 3rd to the 4th and back.

## Synopsis

A full riff-writing dissection: start from a plain 3-power-chord foundation (E-C-D in E minor) and layer in successive details — extended-chord color, a meter change, a chord-melody hybrid technique, a fixed-voicing-over-moving-bass device, a harmonic-minor-flavored interval lick, and a pinch harmonic — building the same simple foundation into a genuinely interesting modern-metal riff. Two devices are new enough to route as standalone technique entries; the rest restate or lightly extend ground already logged from earlier videos in this batch.

## What's taught

- **Extended-chord color over plain power chords (restated — see this batch's other add9/extended-arpeggio entries).** His favorite voicing here: E-B-F♯-G (root, 5th, 9th, ♭3) — the 9th and ♭3 sit a half-step apart and clash directly in isolation, but read as interesting rather than harsh inside the full voicing.
- **Meter change for a progressive feel (restated, see [[Time Signatures & Note Values]]).** Shifts from 4/4 (straight 8th notes) to 3/4 mid-phrase for an immediate "progressive" quality.
- **New: chord-melody hybrid — lock the fretting hand into a chord shape, add melody notes from the key with the picking hand.** While the fretting hand holds a static chord voicing, the picking hand adds single-note melody fragments from the E minor scale on top (slides, pull-offs, and a hammer-on/tapped note timed with the picking hand) — distinct from [[Chord Melody Arranging]]'s already-logged "move a note inside the voicing" technique: here the melody notes are added ON TOP of an unmoving chord shape rather than altering the voicing itself.
- **New: fixed upper voicing over a moving bass note (a slash-chord device).** Keep the exact same upper 3-note voicing (B-F♯-G, the top of the Emadd9-type shape above) constant while walking the BASS note underneath it through the key (C, then D, then back to B) — since all three upper notes stay diatonic to E minor throughout, the harmony never leaves the established key even though the chord's function/color shifts with each new bass note. A cheap way to generate a chord progression from one memorized voicing rather than learning a new shape per chord.
- **Rhythmic phrase locked to drum accents (restated, see [[Syncopation and Metric Displacement]] and prior practice-timing entries in this batch) — not detailed further here.**
- **Harmonic-minor-flavored interval lick.** A unison (the same pitch E found in two different neck positions) followed by a descending pinky slide through intervals drawn from harmonic minor, including the scale's raised-7th-derived minor 2nd, for a brief "exotic" color inside an otherwise diatonic riff.
- **Pinch harmonic** as a texture accent (a single named articulation, not a system).
- **Closing device:** extend the original power chords again — add the 5th on the bottom AND the 9th on top (matches the extended-power-chord idea already logged from [[The 6 BEST Music Theory TRICKS (all guitarists MUST know!)]]) — with a final harmonic-minor-derived ♯7 (D♯) sliding into the E root for cadential tension before resolving.

# Course 03 — Scale Construction

Turns scales from shapes you've memorised into things you can **build from a formula on any root**. Reciting the seven modes in order is a different skill from spelling one, and this course is about the second.

**What it assumes**

- [[Course 01 — Derivation Rules]] — spelling a mode is interval arithmetic.
- [[Course 02 — Chord Function]] — Lesson 3 matches scales to chords, which needs function first.

## The lessons

### Lesson 1 — The Minor Family and the Pentatonics

**Read**
- [ ] [[Scales]] in full — it's 65 lines and covers everything in this lesson

**The material**
- **[[SCL-C01 — Natural minor|Natural minor]]** is the white notes from A — the same notes as C major with a different tonic. From the parallel side: major with **♭3, ♭6, ♭7**.
- **[[SCL-C02 — Harmonic and melodic minor|Harmonic minor]]** raises the 7th, and it exists for one reason: to give minor keys a real leading tone and therefore a functioning V chord. That's the direct link back to [[Course 02 — Chord Function]] — without the raised 7th, minor has no dominant.
- **Melodic minor** raises the 6th *and* 7th ascending, smoothing out the augmented 2nd that harmonic minor leaves between ♭6 and ♮7. That gap is the reason the third scale exists at all.
- **[[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales|Pentatonics]]** are the major scale **minus the 4th and 7th**: C D E G A, no F. Those two are exactly the notes that create half-step tension against a major triad, which is why removing them makes the scale nearly impossible to play wrong.
- Minor pentatonic is the same five notes taken from the relative minor root — which is why one shape covers both and why guitarists learn it first.

**Concepts**
- [[SCL-C01 — Natural minor]]
- [[SCL-C02 — Harmonic and melodic minor]]
- [[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]

**Homework**
- [ ] **Review** — spell A natural minor, A harmonic minor and A melodic minor ascending. Then C and G major pentatonic, and A minor pentatonic. Say what got removed or raised, and why.
- [ ] **Apply** (~15 min) — play all three A minor scales on guitar, one octave up and down, then A minor pentatonic over the same drone. Say aloud which note you changed at each step.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Three-Stage Warm-Up (Physical, Mental, Resonance)]] · guitar, Stage 1 only to start: major pentatonic around the cycle of fifths, ascending and descending. Stage 2 adds the change-key-mid-pattern twist once the shapes are automatic.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Three-Stage Warm-Up (Physical, Mental, Resonance)]] · guitar, Stage 1 only to start: major pentatonic around the cycle of fifths, ascending and descending. Stage 2 adds the change-key-mid-pattern twist once the shapes are automatic.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Improvising Over a Drone Track]] · any fretted/keyed instrument — freeform improv against a sustained root drone, first playing [[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales|the pentatonic]] straight through, then breaking from strict order (or running scale-degree number sequences) to internalize the shape by ear rather than by rote recall.

### Lesson 2 — Modes as Construction

**Read**
- [ ] [[Diatonic Modes]] in full

**The material**
- The shift this lesson exists to make: **stop thinking "white notes starting on D" and start thinking "D minor with a raised 6th."**
- Those are the two ways in, and [[SCL-C04 — Parallel vs derivative mode thinking|the distinction matters]]. **Derivative** treats a mode as a rotation of a parent scale — fast to find, but it keeps your ear anchored to the parent's tonic.
- **Parallel** treats it as an alteration of major or minor *from the same root* — slower to work out, but it puts the characteristic note in front of you and keeps the mode's own tonic as home.
- **Parallel is the useful one for playing**, and it's the only one that makes [[SCL-C06 — Spelling a mode from its root|spelling from an arbitrary root]] possible.
- Each mode differs from its major or minor baseline by **one [[SCL-C05 — Characteristic notes|characteristic note]]** — Locrian is the single exception, carrying two — so memorise that degree rather than the whole formula:

| Mode | Baseline | Characteristic note |
|---|---|---|
| Ionian | major | — (it *is* the baseline) |
| Lydian | major | **♯4** |
| Mixolydian | major | **♭7** |
| Aeolian | minor | — (it *is* the baseline) |
| Dorian | minor | **♮6** |
| Phrygian | minor | **♭2** |
| Locrian | minor | **♭5** — plus a ♭2; the one mode with two |

- The ♭5 is what makes Locrian unusable as a home key — its tonic triad is diminished, so there's nothing stable to come home to.
- Sound the characteristic note or the mode doesn't exist. A loop in "D Dorian" that never plays B is just D minor.

**Concepts**
- [[SCL-C04 — Parallel vs derivative mode thinking]]
- [[SCL-C05 — Characteristic notes]]
- [[SCL-C06 — Spelling a mode from its root]]

**Homework**
- [ ] **Review** — spell D Dorian, E Phrygian, F Lydian and G Mixolydian, each **from its own root**, by altering the parallel major or minor. Then name each one's characteristic note without looking.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Full Scale Workout]] · guitar · 60→100 bpm. The modal runs are this lesson with the fretboard attached.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Parallel Scale Alternate-Picking Drill (One-Note Mode Switching)]] · guitar — the drill's whole point *is* [[SCL-C04 — Parallel vs derivative mode thinking|parallel mode thinking]]: alter one degree of a memorized scale shape live (E natural minor → E Phrygian by lowering the 2nd) instead of recalling a second shape from a different parent.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Beyond-Linear Scale Practice Progression]] · guitar — Rounds 2 and 4 (root-interpolation, say-the-note-names-aloud) build the [[SCL-C05 — Characteristic notes|characteristic-note]] recall this lesson needs past shape memorization.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Chord Scale Up Down]] · guitar · 50→120 bpm — the failure mode section is this lesson's own point restated: playing the same parent scale from seven roots isn't spelling a mode, and the fix is naming each [[SCL-C05 — Characteristic notes|characteristic note]] as you play it.
- [ ] **Apply** (~15 min) — over a droning D, play D Dorian then D Aeolian, changing only the 6th. Do the same pair for E Phrygian vs E Aeolian (the ♭2). Record both A/Bs so you can hear the one-note difference cold.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 03 — Lesson 2 Test]]

### Lesson 3 — Scales Against Chords

**Read**
- [ ] [[Scale Modes Reference]] → the mode/chord sections
- [ ] re-read [[Borrowed Chords]] from Course 02

**The material**
- Two applications that turn all of the above from trivia into a tool.
- **[[SCL-C07 — Modal interchange as scales|Modal interchange, seen as scales]].** Borrowing from the parallel minor — Course 02's [[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange|borrowed chords]] — is the *chord* view of what this course does with *scales*: keep the tonic, change the collection. Fm, A♭, B♭ and E♭ in C major are just the triads you get from C natural minor.
- **[[SCL-C08 — Matching a scale to a chord|Matching a scale to a chord]].** Over a **Cmaj7♯11** the chord is asking for a ♯11, and Lydian's raised 4th *is* that note. Plain C major gives you a natural F — a half step above the E, and the classic avoid note over a major chord.
- The general rule underneath: **the chord names the notes that must be there, and the scale supplies the rest.** Pick the scale whose characteristic note matches what the chord is asking for.
- The blues case is the honourable exception. Minor pentatonic works over dominant chords precisely *because* its ♭3 grinds against the chord's major 3rd — that friction is the blues sound — and because three of its five notes are the chord's own tones (root, 5th and ♭7).
- It does **not** sidestep the natural 4th: the 4th is in the scale (A minor pentatonic is A C **D** E G), and blues passes through it rather than resting on it.

**Concepts**
- [[SCL-C07 — Modal interchange as scales]]
- [[SCL-C08 — Matching a scale to a chord]]

**Homework**
- [ ] **Review** — for Cmaj7♯11, C7, Cm7 and Cm7♭5, name a scale that fits and say which note makes it fit.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Full Scale Workout]] · guitar · 60→100 bpm
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Chord Scale Up Down]] · guitar · 50→120 bpm — one scale per chord, up to the 7th and back down. This lesson's concept as finger work: [[SCL-C08 — Matching a scale to a chord|picking the collection]] fast enough that it stops being analysis.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Beyond-Linear Scale Practice Progression]] · guitar — Rounds 1 and 5 only: solo the scale over its own diatonic chords, then harmonize it into moving voicings. Both are [[SCL-C08 — Matching a scale to a chord|scale-against-chord]] work done with the hands rather than on paper.
- [ ] **Apply** (~15 min) — build one-bar vamps in Ableton on Cmaj7♯11, C7, Cm7 and Cm7♭5. Improvise 30 seconds over each with the scale you picked in the drill, and land the phrase on the note that makes it fit.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 03 — Lesson 3 Test]]

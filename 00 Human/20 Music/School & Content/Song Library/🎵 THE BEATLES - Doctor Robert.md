# Doctor Robert — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

The interesting thing here is a **home-key trick**. The song opens on A and sits there unbothered for eight bars, long enough that you take A for the tonic. Then the first real cadence lands on B, and in retrospect that A was **♭VII the whole time** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). Pollack rates it among John's more daring harmonic experiments short of "Strawberry Fields Forever", and uses it to make a general point: the song feels *slight*, and that comes from its short form, absent instrumental break and static arrangement — not from its running time.

**Form.** A 4-bar intro vamping on ♭VII. The verse reads as four square phrases but a rhetorical extension in the third pushes the real length to **18 bars**. The refrain reads as a standard 8 and rounds itself out to **10**, its last two bars eliding into the next verse. No instrumental break, and nothing new enters the arrangement after the first refrain.

**Harmony.** Those opening eight bars establish A by drone, not by progression — which is exactly why the key stays equivocal. Tonal music normally establishes a key through chord motion, and the first genuine cadence doesn't arrive until the end of the verse. In the moment it feels like a modulation up a step; the honest reading is retroactive relabelling. In the refrain, the same E chord that is unambiguously IV in the first phrase begins behaving like **V-of-♭VII** in the second.

**Melody.** The intro's lead guitar plants a **4–3 appoggiatura** that becomes the song's connective motif ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]), reappearing in the verse's third phrase and again in the refrain.

**Arrangement.** Rich but recorded plainly, with staggered layering: backing vocals from the second verse, guitar overdubs just before the refrain, and a harmonium plus stacked vocals giving the refrain an incongruously carol-like texture. The ending is mastered as a fade but appears to be covering a breakdown.

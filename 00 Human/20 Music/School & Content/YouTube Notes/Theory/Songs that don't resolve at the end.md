# Songs that don't resolve at the end

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ElEZEJWWZdc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ElEZEJWWZdc)

## Synopsis

A taxonomy of ways to deliberately end a song WITHOUT resolving to the tonic — a spectrum from "obviously unresolved" through "partially resolved" to alternate chords that still carry genuine tonic function.

## Techniques

- **End on V — the most obvious unresolved sound**: hanging on the dominant chord (the chord that normally points back to I) reads as clearly, deliberately unfinished. "You're Still the One" (Shania Twain, E♭ major, ends on a dragged-out B♭7), "Mr. Brightside" (D♭ major, ends on A♭).
- **End on IV — a more subtle, "tasteful" unresolved sound**: because IV contains the tonic note itself, the chord as a whole still feels like it needs somewhere to go, but the vocal melody CAN still resolve successfully to the tonic note even though the chord underneath doesn't — a partial resolution. "Your Bird Can Sing" (Beatles) goes further: ends on IV in first inversion (A/E in the key of E) — root note correct, bass note correct, everything but the actual tonic chord itself.
- **A hybrid: V with the 4th added (Csus4-type voicing) for partial resolution inside a still-unresolved chord.** "We Are the Champions" (Queen, F major) ends on C7sus4 rather than plain C7 — the sus4 pulls the tonic note (F) directly into the otherwise-unresolved V chord, so it reads as unresolved overall but with a partial resolved feel baked in, distinct from a plain V ending.
- **End on a chromatic mediant for a cinematic, "unresolved unrest" effect**: "Live and Let Die" (Wings, G minor) ends on E♭ minor — not diatonic to the key, but sharing enough notes with the tonic chord to work, chosen deliberately to close the film's opening titles on a sense of unease.
- **Alternate chords that still carry genuine tonic FUNCTION, not just "not the tonic."** The vi chord (relative minor) is widely considered a second tonic-function chord — a resolution, just a darker/weaker one: "Take It Easy" (Eagles) and "From Me to You" (Beatles) both resolve to vi rather than I at the very end. The iii chord (mediant) is a more debatable, weaker third tonic-function option — "Ask Me Why" (Beatles) ends there, giving a sense the song CAN end without the full satisfaction of resolving to I or even vi.

# Five Painful Finger and Picking Exercises

Five drills, ranked worst-to-best by the source teacher for actual playing transfer (5 = least valuable, 1 = most valuable).

## 1. Three-notes-per-string modes (rank: best, "10/5")

Play through all seven modal shapes using a three-notes-per-string fingering, worked in the key of G (chosen because it lays out cleanly for this fingering). Trains both hands, covers key/fretboard layout, and is musical — the source's top-ranked exercise for that reason. See [[CAGE System Scale Shapes]] / [[Diatonic Modes]] for the underlying shapes.

## 2. Odd/even finger-pair pattern (rank: 4/5)

Number the fretting fingers 1–4. Only ever use fingers 1+3 together or 2+4 together — never mix an odd-numbered finger with an even-numbered one. Play a fixed pattern down the strings using only those two-finger pairings, then turn around and play it back. To increase difficulty, let notes ring longer instead of damping them. Ranked "tricky but not too musical."

## 3. Anchor-finger exercise (rank: 3/5)

Anchor one finger (ring finger recommended as the hardest, though any finger works) on a fixed fret — the 7th fret in the demo — and keep it planted there for the entire exercise while the other fingers move. Good for left-hand independence and works at any skill level by adjusting the metronome. Ranked above the fan drill below because it functions closer to real playing (a static reference finger while the hand moves around it).

## 4. 3-minute continuous chromatic picking (rank: 2/5)

Play a simple chromatic exercise continuously for 3 minutes without stopping, metronome around 130 BPM. The goal is sustaining tempo, not perfection — occasional mistakes are fine as long as speed doesn't drop. Track pick slips as a data point: more than ~4 slips means the tempo is too fast, dial it back. **The "3-minute rule" generalizes to any exercise** — pick one, run it continuously for 3 minutes, repeat up to 3 sets a day, with rest between sets since this is physically demanding.

## 5. One-finger-per-string fan exercise (rank: worst, 5/5)

Plant all four fingers on the G string, then alternate the first finger between the G and E strings 20–30 times while the other three fingers stay completely still; repeat for each finger in turn (ring finger is typically hardest, pinky close behind). Move to a higher, tighter-fret position to make it harder, or a lower, wider-fret position to make it easier. Attributed to Scott Tennant's *Pumping Nylon*. Good for pure finger independence but ranked lowest here for being the least musical of the five.

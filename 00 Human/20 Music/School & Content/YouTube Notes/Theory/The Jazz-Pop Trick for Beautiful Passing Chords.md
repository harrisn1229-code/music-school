# The Jazz-Pop Trick for Beautiful Passing Chords

## Synopsis

Max Konyi reverse-engineers the harmonic principle behind a reference track's "gorgeous," tension-and-release chord progression, then applies the same principle to build an original short track of his own in a different key and genre, walking through both side by side. The core idea — alternate stable, extended diatonic "target" chords with altered-dominant "approach" chords that resolve into them, using a mix of standard 5–1, tritone-adjacent half-step-above, and backdoor (♭7–1) root relationships — is a generalized, jazz-derived reharmonization trick directly applicable to pop/lofi/RnB-adjacent chord writing. **Transcript confidence note:** captions are clean for the spoken narration, but auto-captions consistently garble the spoken chord symbols and voicings (e.g. "trionee substitution" for "tritone substitution," ambiguous renderings like "9 flat 5" / "flat major 9"); exact chord spellings below are reconstructed from context and described qualitatively rather than quoted as verbatim numbers.

## Techniques

- **Core principle: alternate stable "target" chords with altered-dominant "approach" chords that resolve into them, over and over across the whole progression.** The target chords are diatonic and made lush with extensions (9ths, 11ths, 13ths); the approach chords are always *altered* dominants (not plain, unaltered dominant 7ths) — it's the alternation itself, not any single chord, that produces the tension/release "spine-tingling" quality of the reference track. → [[Altered Chords]], [[Secondary Dominants]]
- **The approach chord must be altered, specifically** — a plain dominant 7th targeting the next chord doesn't create the same effect; reach for ♭9, ♯9, ♭13, and especially ♯11 on the approach chord. ♯11 was called out by name as a major source of the "magical, mysterious" quality throughout the reference track's voicings — worth deliberately voicing in on approach chords, not just the more familiar ♭9/♯9.
- **Standard 5–1 approach:** an altered dominant built on the target chord's own 5th resolves normally into it — e.g. a G-rooted altered dominant (with ♭9/♭13-type color) resolving into C minor 9, since G is literally the 5th of C. This is a generalized/altered case of an ordinary secondary dominant. → [[Secondary Dominants]]
- **Tritone-adjacent half-step-above approach ("tritone substitution" — misheard as "trionee substitution" in captions):** instead of approaching from the target's own 5th, build the altered-dominant approach chord a half step *above* the target's root. Demonstrated going into a ii chord: approaching F♯ minor 11 (the ii in the key of E) via an altered dominant built a half-step above it (G-rooted, extended/altered), functioning as a tritone-related substitute resolution rather than a straight 5–1. → existing tritone-substitution rule in [[Chord Progressions]] ("any chord → the chord rooted a flat 5th away, generalized past dominants") and [[HAR-C06 — Why V–I resolves: the tritone]]
- **Backdoor resolution (♭7–1) into the final/tonic target:** approach the tonic not from its own 5th but from a whole step below — a "backdoor ii–V"-style move. Demonstrated resolving into the I chord (E major 9) from a D-rooted altered dominant (13♯11-type voicing) a whole step under E, rather than the expected B-rooted V.
- **You don't have to start (or loop) the progression on the I chord.** The original demo track starts on IV, treating it as the loop's first "stable" target and only reaching I later in the cycle — the alternating target/approach principle works regardless of which diatonic degree opens the loop.
- **Workflow for stealing this trick from any reference track:** (1) identify which chords in the reference are "stable" (diatonic, extended, functioning as points of rest) vs. "approach" (altered dominants targeting the next stable chord); (2) for each approach chord, identify its root relationship to its target — 5–1, half-step-above (tritone-adjacent), or whole-step-below/backdoor; (3) reuse that same sequence of root relationships in a new key, a different starting scale degree, and a different genre to generate an original progression carrying the same harmonic "flavor" without copying the actual chords. This is exactly how the video's own original track was built from the reference.

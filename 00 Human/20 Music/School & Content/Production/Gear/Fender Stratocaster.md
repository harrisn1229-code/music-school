# Fender Stratocaster

**Category:** Electric Guitar
**Vendor:** Fender
**Ballpark value (used, 2026):** $1,100–1,500 — US-made premium, ~$1,700 new in 2014 (likely American Deluxe; confirm exact model from the headstock/neck plate when convenient)

## What it is

Fender's flagship double-cutaway electric — three single-coil pickups, 5-way selector, synchronized tremolo. The most-recorded electric guitar design in history.

## What it's used for

Everything from glassy clean chime (positions 2/4 "quack") to blues lead and indie jangle; the clean/funk/bedroom-pop workhorse.

## Signal chain notes

Into the pedalboard ([[Wah Pedal]] → [[Big Muff Pi]] / [[Boss DS-2]] → [[Line 6 DL4]]) → [[Fender Blues Junior]]; mic'd with the [[Shure SM57]].

**High-level points** (the mental model, not a feature list):
- Fender ships one universal electric-guitar owner's manual (no per-model booklet for the Strat itself) — model-specific detail lives in the "Specs" tab on fender.com and the parts/service manuals, not a printed manual.
- Every Strat leaves the factory hand-adjusted to Fender's factory setup spec — string height, relief, and intonation are all interrelated, so change one and expect to recheck the others.
- **Truss rod** counteracts string tension by bowing the neck; too much forward relief (concave, "bowed away from strings") needs the rod tightened (clockwise), too little relief / back-bow (convex) needs it loosened (counterclockwise). Check with a capo at fret 1 + fretting the low E at the last fret, then measure the gap at the 8th fret.
- **Intonation** is set per-string at the bridge saddles, front-to-back, using the string's own gauge as the spacing reference between adjacent saddles (the manual gives an exact worked example: measure 1st string nut-to-saddle-center, then offset the 2nd saddle back by that string's gauge, and so on) — this is the step most bedroom players skip and then wonder why chords go out of tune up the neck.
- **Pickup height** is set per-pickup by ear/feel, not a fixed number — closer to the strings = louder and more midrange bite but more risk of magnetic pull dulling sustain (especially on the low E/high E), farther = cleaner/quieter. Adjust with the 5-way selector in each pickup's own position so you're only hearing that pickup.
- **String height (action)** is personal-preference-driven — the manual's own reference point is ~4/64" at the 12th fret on the low E (12th-fret radius gauge method), treble side lower — but tone/feel tradeoffs (buzz vs. stiffness) matter more than hitting a spec number.
- Body/neck wood and finish shift slightly with humidity/temperature over the guitar's life — a setup that felt right at purchase can drift and is worth rechecking seasonally, not a sign anything is "broken."

## Techniques

- **This is the exact guitar demonstrated in a from-scratch 60s/70s psych-rock sample-flip build** — a Stratocaster was confirmed as the historically-accurate choice (commonly used by the Beatles, Pink Floyd, etc. on psych-rock records) before the video's producer reached for the real instrument they own. Full recipe: amp sim ("British Guitar A1" model, Drive-voiced, "Wide Large" cab, built-in reverb OFF) → vibrato pedal (called out as *more* important than the amp for the psychedelic character) → ADT for width (Waves Real ADT — a guitar use case for ADT, see [[Artificial Double Tracking (ADT)]]) → Abbey Road Chambers reverb → tape delay → compressor → vintage-mic emulation (Waves King's Microphones, compensating for period mic quality) → EQ (cut lows, boost highs) → hard-pan to one side, collapsed almost to mono. ## Key Parameters

The controls/adjustment points that actually matter, worst-documented first:

| # | Parameter | What it is / does | Technique | Artist / record example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ① | 5-way pickup selector | Chooses bridge / bridge+middle / middle / middle+neck / neck pickup combinations | Positions 2 & 4 give the classic Strat "quack" (out-of-phase-feeling hum-cancelling blend) — the clean funk/[[MIDS Slot]] rhythm tone; bridge alone for cutting [[LEAD Slot]] lines, neck alone for warm jazz-adjacent cleans | Position 2/4 quack is all over Nile Rodgers-style funk rhythm and Mark Knopfler/Dire Straits lead tone |
| ② | Volume knob | Master volume for whichever pickup(s) the selector has active | Roll back for cleaner breakup on a driven amp ([[Fender Blues Junior]]) without touching the amp itself — a fast way to ride dynamics mid-take | Hendrix-style volume-swell/rollback technique |
| ③ | Tone knobs (neck / middle) | Low-pass filter darkening the neck and middle pickup circuits (bridge is usually wired hot with no tone control on vintage-spec Strats) | Roll the neck tone down for a woolier jazz-clean; leave bridge un-filtered for the brightest cutting lead tone | — |
| ④ | Synchronized tremolo (whammy bar) | Floating bridge that raises/lowers all 6 string pitches together via a springs-in-cavity counterweight system | Subtle pitch wobble/vibrato on sustained notes; dive bombs for more aggressive parts — floating setup means bending one string detunes the others slightly, a known Strat quirk to work around, not fight | Hendrix/Clapton-era subtle vibrato use |
| ⑤ | Truss rod | Internal steel rod counteracting string-tension neck bow | See high-level points above — tighten (clockwise) for too much forward relief, loosen (counterclockwise) for back-bow; always recheck tuning + gap after each small turn | — |
| ⑥ | Bridge saddle height/intonation | Per-string height + front-back position at the bridge | Height sets action/buzz per string; front-back position (intonation) keeps fretted notes in tune up the neck — set via the gauge-offset method in the high-level points | — |
| ⑦ | Pickup height (per pickup) | Distance from pole pieces to strings | Closer = louder/brighter with more magnetic pull risk on sustain; set per-pickup with the selector isolating it | — |

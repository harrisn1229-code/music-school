# STOP Practicing Scales!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ftmWkiawbqU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ftmWkiawbqU)

## Synopsis

BERNTH argues most players study a scale's positions on the neck as interchangeable boxes of the same scale, missing that each 3-note-per-string position of harmonic minor is actually a distinct-sounding **mode** with its own character and use case. He walks all seven modes of A harmonic minor — every mode independently **transposed to the same root note (A)** rather than left at its "natural" starting fret — so each is heard and practiced as its own standalone scale, not as "position 2/3/4 of harmonic minor." All seven mode names/formulas match the vault's existing [[Harmonic Minor Modes]] table exactly; this video's real contribution is the **practice framing**, not new theory.

## What's taught

- **The core reframe: practice each harmonic-minor mode transposed to a shared root, not left at its natural starting position.** Instead of playing harmonic minor's 2nd 3-notes-per-string shape (which naturally starts on B, the scale's 2nd degree) and thinking "that's just harmonic minor moved up," relocate that same shape's root down to A and hear/practice it as **A Locrian ♮6** — a scale in its own right, with its own formula and sound, independent of the harmonic-minor scale it was derived from. Repeated for all seven modes, always re-rooted to A: 1) A harmonic minor itself (1 2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 7), 2) A Locrian ♮6 (1 ♭2 ♭3 4 ♭5 6 ♭7), 3) A Ionian ♯5 (1 2 3 4 ♯5 6 7 — major scale plus a dissonant ♯5), 4) A Dorian ♯4/Romanian (1 2 ♭3 ♯4 5 6 ♭7 — Dorian's major 6th plus Lydian's ♯4), 5) A Phrygian dominant/Spanish Phrygian (1 ♭2 3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7 — used as the compositional key for the opening track of BERNTH's album *Elevation*), 6) A Lydian ♯2 (1 ♯2 3 ♯4 5 6 7 — Lydian with a raised 2nd), 7) A Super Locrian (1 ♭2 ♭3 ♭4 ♭5 ♭6 𝄫7 — "crazy dark and dissonant," but still a defined harmonic system, not random atonality).
- **Full daily practice routine, built from the reframe:** work through all seven re-rooted modes on the same root note in one workout session, rather than drilling harmonic minor up and down the neck in its five/seven natural positions. Stated payoff: after enough repetition, each mode registers as one of "seven really cool unique sounding scales" available for composition, instead of one scale with several interchangeable box shapes.
- **Derivation reminder used throughout:** harmonic minor = natural minor with the 7th raised a half-step (formula 1 2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 7) — the one-note-change framing already documented in [[Harmonic Minor Modes]].

# Dilla Rhythm Ornamentation

**A named ornamental rhythmic cell (the source calls it the "dilloda"/"dilla" rhythm) that fluidly interrupts a straight eighth-note line to move through scale or arpeggio tones — Grant Green's stated device for constantly shifting between eighth notes, quarter notes, and triplets inside one solo, rather than committing to a single note value.** Distinct from a pure subdivision change (like dropping into a triplet run) — it's a small syncopated embellishment figure dropped *into* an otherwise-straight eighth-note line at a chosen spot, which is what makes it reusable as vocabulary rather than a one-off fill.

## What it is

Instead of walking a scale or arpeggio in plain, evenly-spaced eighth notes, the "dilla rhythm" breaks the evenness at a chosen point — a quick syncopated embellishment (the name itself is onomatopoeic for the rhythmic feel, "dil-la," a clipped, slightly displaced grouping) inserted mid-line before the line continues. It works identically whether the notes underneath it are:

- **Scale tones** — e.g. embellishing a straight walk down the tonic minor scale.
- **Arpeggio tones** — e.g. embellishing a minor 6 arpeggio (worked example: a B♭ minor 6 arpeggio run through the dilla rhythm).

Both produce the same rhythmic-variety payoff: a line that reads as alive and unpredictable rather than a metronomic run, without changing the pitch content at all — it's a phrasing/rhythm layer applied on top of whatever note choices (see [[Tonic Minor Soloing]], [[Chord-Tone Soloing]]) are already in play.

## How to use it

1. Pick a straight eighth-note scale or arpeggio line you already play (a tonic-minor walk-down, an arpeggio run — see [[Tonic Minor Soloing]] for the tonic-minor material this pairs naturally with).
2. Drop the dilla rhythm cell in at one point in the line rather than repeating it identically throughout — the effect is variety, not a new ostinato.
3. Mix it with the line's other rhythmic material (straight eighths, quarter notes, occasional triplets) the way Green does across a full chorus, rather than making the whole solo one uniform rhythmic texture.

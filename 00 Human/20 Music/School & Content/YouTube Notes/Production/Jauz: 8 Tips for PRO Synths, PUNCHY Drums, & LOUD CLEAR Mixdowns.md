# Jauz: 8 Tips for PRO Synths, PUNCHY Drums, & LOUD CLEAR Mixdowns

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn3Izw3-Qmw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn3Izw3-Qmw)

## Synopsis
A clip-style breakdown of dubstep/bass producer Jauz narrating his own habits live while finishing a track on stream — carving space in his synths so drums can breathe, swapping EQ Eight for Pro-Q mid-session, layering a weak sound under a stronger one to reinforce it, reaching for overdrive and convolution reverb for character and space, and closing out with a mastering pass that stays just over -6 LUFS instead of chasing the genre's loudness wars. The most valuable, most repeated idea in the video is the 7-7.5kHz synth filter trick he credits to Sullivan King — cutting synths above that point clears room for hats and white noise so nothing fights for space, and he explicitly ties that same move to why the finished master can sit loud while staying "pristine" instead of harsh in the high end.

## Techniques
- **High-pass/filter synths above 7-7.5kHz to make room for hats and noise** — cutting everything above ~7kHz on a synth leaves the transient/high-frequency content of hats and white-noise layers with clear space instead of masking each other, described as making the mix "a hundred thousand times more clean"; picked up from Sullivan King. — Jauz (Sol State), [Jauz: 8 Tips for PRO Synths, PUNCHY Drums, & LOUD CLEAR Mixdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn3Izw3-Qmw) → [[Masking and Carving]]
- **That same synth-filtering habit is what lets the master go loud without getting harsh** — he directly credits the 7k+ cut on synths as the reason a master can be pushed loud while the top end still reads as "pristine" rather than fatiguing, i.e. the mixing move pays off again at the mastering stage. — Jauz (Sol State), [Jauz: 8 Tips for PRO Synths, PUNCHY Drums, & LOUD CLEAR Mixdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn3Izw3-Qmw) → [[Clipping and Loudness]]
- **Reach for Pro-Q over EQ Eight for precision moves** — catches himself using EQ8 mid-session and switches, treating a sharper parametric EQ as the default tool for surgical cuts rather than Ableton's stock EQ. — Jauz (Sol State), [Jauz: 8 Tips for PRO Synths, PUNCHY Drums, & LOUD CLEAR Mixdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn3Izw3-Qmw) → [[Pro-Q 4]]
- **Layer a thin/weak sound underneath a stronger one to reinforce it** — a sound he calls "kind of whack on its own" becomes usable once stacked underneath another element, using layering to compensate for a source sound that doesn't hold up solo. — Jauz (Sol State), [Jauz: 8 Tips for PRO Synths, PUNCHY Drums, & LOUD CLEAR Mixdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn3Izw3-Qmw) → [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Add overdrive when a sound needs more "sauce"** — when an element feels like it's missing a little extra edge, he reaches for overdrive/an extra layer rather than more EQ, for harmonic bite and energy. — Jauz (Sol State), [Jauz: 8 Tips for PRO Synths, PUNCHY Drums, & LOUD CLEAR Mixdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn3Izw3-Qmw) → [[Overdrive]]
- **Reach for a convolution reverb for realistic space** — explicitly picks a reverb because "that one can convolve," using a convolution engine rather than an algorithmic reverb for a more natural sense of space on an element. — Jauz (Sol State), [Jauz: 8 Tips for PRO Synths, PUNCHY Drums, & LOUD CLEAR Mixdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn3Izw3-Qmw) → [[Hybrid Reverb]]
- **A wide mix doesn't need multiband width boosts at mastering** — going into mastering already this wide, he skips his usual per-band width boost entirely, treating a mix that's already stereo-wide as a reason to leave width alone rather than push it further. — Jauz (Sol State), [Jauz: 8 Tips for PRO Synths, PUNCHY Drums, & LOUD CLEAR Mixdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn3Izw3-Qmw) → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Target just over -6 LUFS and stop fighting the loudness wars** — barely touches the limiter and deliberately keeps masters around/just past -6 LUFS rather than pushing further, calling out that he doesn't try to compete in dubstep's loudness wars — prioritizing dynamics and clarity over sheer perceived loudness. — Jauz (Sol State), [Jauz: 8 Tips for PRO Synths, PUNCHY Drums, & LOUD CLEAR Mixdowns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pn3Izw3-Qmw) → [[Clipping and Loudness]]

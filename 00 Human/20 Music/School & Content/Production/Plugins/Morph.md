# Morph

**Category:** Modulation
**Vendor:** Ableton

## What it is

Ableton's stock spectral-morphing device (Live 12 Suite) — takes a second audio source (sidechain input) and continuously blends its spectral content with the main signal via an XY control, with a Formant control for tonal shaping. Sits in the same "spectral" stock-device family as [[Spectral Resonator]], [[Spectral Time]], and [[Spectral Blur]].

## What it's used for

Blending two unrelated sound sources into a single hybrid texture — a fast from-scratch sound-design move for combining, e.g., an instrument and a field recording, without manually crossfading or automating two separate tracks.

## FX chain notes

Used as an insert on the primary source with the second source routed in as sidechain/audio input; typically followed by reverb once the blended texture is dialed in.

## Techniques

- **Route an entire group's output as Morph's second source, to crossfade a whole submix against a single loop** — group every non-drum element into one Group track, drop Morph on that group, then route a *separate* track's audio (a bass loop, in this example) into Morph's Audio 2 input via that track's output routing (Audio To → the group carrying Morph) — Morph's crossfader now blends the entire multi-element submix against the single external source, not just two individual sounds. A more advanced version of the basic two-source XY blend below, useful when one side of the morph needs to be a full arrangement rather than one instrument. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — You Suck at Producing, [How To Morph One Sound Into Another (4 techniques)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jdXhjfQhk3Y)
- **XY-blend two unrelated sources (e.g. guitar + a noisy texture) with Formant control, then add reverb after** — route a second, contrasting sound source into Morph's sidechain input, use the XY pad to continuously blend between the two, adjust **Formant** for tonal character, and finish with reverb on the blended result — a quick way to build a new hybrid texture from two existing sounds rather than manually crossfading/automating them on separate tracks. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — You Suck at Producing, [5 Ways To Make Any Genre Sound 'Hyper'](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MFCiyKwWjeQ)

# Songs that use the Just The Two Of Us chord progression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vT0RxwL1HE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vT0RxwL1HE)

## Synopsis

Names and explains the "Just the Two of Us" progression (Grover Washington Jr. ft. Bill Withers) — a minor-key vi–V–i loop with a jazzy ii–V-of-vi "enclave" detour spliced in — then traces its wider use, including as the harmonic basis for New Jack Swing (dubbed "New Jack changes" per a viewer comment that prompted this video).

## Techniques

- **The base loop**: vi–V–i in the minor key (i.e., in F minor: Db–C–Fm), a common minor-key loop covered generally elsewhere.
- **The "Just the Two of Us" turn — a temporary tonicization detour**: right when the loop's i chord (Fm) would sound like a fully "arrived" destination, the song instead treats the NEXT chord (D♭) as a temporary tonic and approaches it with its own **ii–V** (Ebm7–Ab7 → Db) — two chords that don't belong to the home key of F minor at all — before "psych"-ing back into the original vi–V–i loop. Described as "a little enclave of the key of D♭ in a song that's in F minor" — Bennett labels this shape "2-5-1 of 6" (the ii-V-I of the vi chord) and calls it a classic jazz-harmony move for keeping a loop moving past the point where it would otherwise sound resolved.
- **Songs using the identical enclave-detour version**: "Nonsense" (Sabrina Carpenter); "Stronger Than You" (Estelle, from the *Steven Universe* soundtrack).
- **Close variants**: "Emotions" (Mariah Carey) — near-identical, but the V chord is sometimes minor instead of major, for a more mellow/laid-back color. "Teddy's Jam" (Guy) and "Finesse" (Bruno Mars) — both use the full "Just the Two of Us" progression; explicitly stylistically linked (Bruno Mars's "Finesse" is described as "a love letter" to Guy's New Jack Swing style specifically).
- **"New Jack changes" — genre-level naming**: New Jack Swing (a late-80s/early-90s R&B subgenre blending hip-hop drum-machine/sample elements with conventional R&B) uses this progression often enough that a viewer proposed calling it the "New Jack changes" — the comment that directly inspired this video. New Jack Swing's influence carried into '90s boy-band pop (NSYNC, Backstreet Boys) — Backstreet Boys' "Everybody" uses the same vi-V-i core but reoriented to start on i instead of vi (same notes, different starting point/orientation).

# Oberheim DX

## What it is
The [[Oberheim DMX]]'s cheaper little sibling — same sampled-drum architecture, fewer voices, no individual tuning knobs out of the box, about half the price. That price cut mattered: the DX put the Oberheim slam within reach of the small studios feeding early Chicago house and mid-80s hip hop, and EPROM-swapping made it a hackable favorite.

## What makes the sound
- **Same 8-bit companded EPROM samples through analog VCA output stages as the DMX** — the hard, boxy, concrete-slab kit character, marginally simplified in the voice architecture.
- **Swappable EPROM chips** were the killer feature: producers burned their own sounds onto chips, making the DX one of the first *user-sampled* drum machines in practice — a proto-sampler workflow.
- Slightly leaner voice count and simpler panel than the DMX; patterns come out just as blocky and confident.
- Punches through small speakers — the same reason the DMX ruled early hip hop applies down-market.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Impulse]] with DX/DMX sample packs; [[kHs Bitcrush]] + [[Saturator]] for the 8-bit slam; [[RC-20 Retro Color]] for era texture.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2, Samples From Mars (DMX pack covers the family character).
- **Free:** DX/DMX kits are in every free vintage archive.

## Used on
- Genre/era: mid-80s hip hop/electro and early Chicago house budget studios — the DX shows up constantly in gear lore for that scene, but clean record-level credits mostly attach to its DMX sibling, so this note keeps the association genre-level.

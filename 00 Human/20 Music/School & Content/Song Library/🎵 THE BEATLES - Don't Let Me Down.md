# Don't Let Me Down — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A slow, hard-driving screamer with John's structural fingerprints all over it: strictly symmetrical, with a one-off **Alternate Verse** occupying the exact keystone position at the centre, the instrumental break displaced to the outro, and a tune so pentatonic it never touches scale degree 7 in either form.

**Form.** A mirror around the middle section. The 2-bar intro is lifted from the last two bars of the verse, and the slow triplets declaiming the title start in the second half of bar 2 — where Buddy Holly banked slow triplets for a late climax, this song **spends them in the first two bars** as a warning. Refrain 8 bars AA; verse 8 bars AA with the bar preceding each phrase **extended by one extra beat**, one line of loose pentameter dropped into an otherwise four-square setting. The Alternate Verse is the only section not built on AA.

**Harmony.** Three chords stated — I, ii, V — plus a IV that is heavily implied and almost never actually played. Refrain and verse both start away from I and converge onto it; the Alternate Verse breaks the mould by starting *and* ending on the tonic. The subdominant is a phantom: where you think you hear IV you're usually hearing the tune's extensions over V, which stacks 7, 9, 11 and 13 to produce the effect of **IV superimposed on V** ([[POP-C06 — Sus and add9 under a vocal]]).

**Melody.** Pentatonic in the strict sense ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]); degree 4 appears only in the backing vocal shadowing the tune in parallel thirds. The refrain contour drives downward, and the verse opens as if a broad upward arch is coming and delivers the same descent instead. The Alternate Verse is the only place a complete arch is permitted.

**Arrangement.** Live and impromptu, of a piece with its single's A-side; the only obvious retouch is a short stretch of double-tracked lead in the Alternate Verse. That section also carries a **counterpoint line in octaves shared by bass and lead guitar**, one of the more unusual instrumental ideas in the catalogue.

# 5 Habits I Had To UNLEARN To Become A Better Music Producer

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wTugkLmMSCM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wTugkLmMSCM)

## Synopsis

A workflow/mindset video (Underbelly, comedy-framed) covering five self-diagnosed bad habits and their fixes: fearing clipping instead of using it deliberately as a loudness tool (with a Saturator Digital Clip demo on both a sine wave and a full drum loop); over-processing a kick instead of just swapping the sample; "loopitis" (all creative energy spent on the first 8 bars) and its cure (start with several energy-tiered drum clips, and use the early "honeymoon phase" of a new song for broad strokes, not fine details); skipping reference tracks, with a concrete Youlean-Loudness-Meter + spectrum-analyzer A/B workflow AND a novel use of reference tracks — mapping their arrangement structure with Arrangement View locators as a section-by-section blueprint; and chasing "sounds good on every system" instead of committing to 1–2 known monitoring setups and matching a bad reference's badness on weak systems rather than over-correcting. Lighter on plugin-specific detail than most videos in this channel's catalog, heavier on mixing/arranging philosophy — most of it maps onto existing technique notes rather than plugin notes.

## Techniques

- **Embrace clipping as a deliberate loudness tool, not a mistake to avoid** — demoed on a sine wave (Saturator's Digital Clip, Drive cranked, turns the sine into a square wave and adds harmonics visible on a spectrum analyzer) and then on a full drum loop, where the same Digital Clip setting visibly squashes the loop's peak meter — functioning as an informal limiter/compressor that lets the whole loop's level be pushed louder afterward without re-clipping. → [[Clipping and Loudness]]
- **Stop stacking processing on a kick that "doesn't hit" — swap the sample instead** — the habit being corrected: piling compressor after compressor, EQ after EQ, onto a kick that fundamentally isn't the right sound; the actual fix is almost always to pull a different sample rather than add another device. → [[Kick Design]]
- **Cure "loopitis" with 4 pre-built energy-tiered starter clips, and treat a new song's early "honeymoon phase" as broad-strokes time** — loopitis: dumping all creative effort into the first 8 bars with nowhere left to go. Fix: start every song with at least 4 different drum clips at different energy levels already built (multiple jumping-off points instead of one loop to overfill), and use a new song's early excitement window to nail arrangement/broad strokes rather than perfecting the mix of the first 10 seconds. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Reference-track A/B: Integrated LUFS match (Youlean Loudness Meter) + a low-end-zoomed spectrum analyzer comparison** — load a reference track loudness-matched via a free LUFS meter's Integrated reading, temporarily turning the reference down to match your own track's level pre-mastering if needed; separately, reset a spectrum analyzer, disable auto-resize, and zoom the sliders into the low end specifically to compare how much sub the reference carries versus your own mix, using that as the basis for turning your own sub up or down. → [[Referencing and Translation]]
- **Use a reference track's own arrangement as a section-by-section blueprint via Arrangement View locators** — right-click in Arrangement View → Add Locator at each structural change in the reference track (Intro, Build-up, Build-up part 2, Drop, etc.), naming each as it's placed — the resulting locator map becomes a literal blueprint to follow when arranging your own song, removing a lot of the arrangement guesswork. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Referencing and Translation]]
- **Commit to 1–2 known monitoring systems; on a weak system, match the reference's badness instead of over-correcting** — chasing "sounds good on every system" is a losing habit since some playback systems are simply bad; pick one or two systems you know intimately for critical listening. When forced onto an unfamiliar/weak system, use the reference track as the honesty check — if the reference itself sounds bad there, matching your own track's badness the same way (not fighting to "fix" it) keeps the comparison valid. → [[Referencing and Translation]]

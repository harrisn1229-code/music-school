# Across the Universe — The Beatles

> Lennon's mantra-inspired ballad. Two fans (Lizzie Bravo and Gayleen Pease) provided backing vocals on the original. George Martin added Hammond organ. Phil Spector added orchestration for the Let It Be release.

## Production & Arrangement
A floating, meditative drone-song built on a **tanpura/tambura drone** and Lennon's stream-of-consciousness "Jai guru deva om" refrain. The released *Let It Be* version (Spector) added lush orchestra + choir and slowed the tape; an earlier version had **bird sounds** and backing "aahs" — either way, the arrangement is *atmosphere over motion*.

- **Modal, drone-anchored harmony** — the tonic never really leaves, so it feels suspended.
- Heavily reverbed, slowed, dreamy vocal.

## Steal this
- **Drone anchor:** hold a tonic pad/tanpura under the whole song ([[Omnisphere]] / a sustained synth) so it floats → [[Scale Modes Reference]].
- **Mantra refrain:** a repeated sung phrase as the emotional center.
- **Slow the tape** + heavy reverb for a suspended, dreamy lead ([[Little AlterBoy]] pitch-down, [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] long).
- **Atmosphere over chord motion** — let texture, not changes, carry a section.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Across The Universe]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #169.*

**Form.** A flat folk-ballad shape whose repeated group is *two verses → Mantra → Refrain*, run three times. The interpolated **"Mantra" section before every refrain** is the unorthodox part, and the two near-identical verse variants are reordered each time round (V/V′, then V/V, then V′/V) so the listener registers freshness without being able to name what changed ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]). Almost nothing is four-square: intro 6 bars, verse 8 (with an extra half bar in the later verse of the first two pairs), verse′ 7, Mantra 6, refrain 16.

**Harmony.** `Verse: I – vi – iii · ii – V` · `Verse′: I – vi – iii · ii – iv` · `Mantra: I (4) – V (2)` · `Refrain: V (4) – IV (2) – I (2), ×4`. Diatonic apart from the **minor iv**, a Lennon staple ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). His argument is that the interest is entirely in where sections *stop*: only the refrain closes, everything else opens out to V, and verse′ is aimed at minor iv purely to break the monotony of a repeated target. The refrain's V–IV–I is the blues trope, and he flags how differently it reads from IV–V–I.

**Notable.** Every section gets its own contour ([[MEL-C01 — Melodic contour]]) — a patter-syllabic inverted arch, then a descending syllabic verse, then the Mantra as a rising triadic fanfare, then sixth-leaps in the refrain. The outro shortens the Mantra so its two-bar move to V disappears, leaving it over a drone into the fade.

**Against this page.** Same key reading (D). His form is finer-grained than the `structure:` field: what the page counts as a chorus he splits into Mantra plus Refrain.

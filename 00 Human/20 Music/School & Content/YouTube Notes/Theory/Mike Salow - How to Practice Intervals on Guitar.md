A one-minute shorts video containing one genuinely good drill — what his music school called the **"interval hip-hop exercise."** Filed as a drill: [[Interval Ladder]].

## The exercise

Starting from **A at the 5th fret of the low E string**, play every interval in order from minor 2nd up to the octave. The twist that makes it worth doing:

- Play each interval **first on one string**, then **on adjacent strings**.
- As the intervals widen, the string pair widens with them — E+A for the smaller intervals, then E+D around the tritone, then reaching the G string by the major 7th and octave.

So the ladder is: minor 2nd · major 2nd · minor 3rd · major 3rd · perfect 4th · tritone · perfect 5th · minor 6th · major 6th · minor 7th · major 7th · octave.

## Why it's worth the minute

Most interval practice is either purely aural (name what you hear) or purely theoretical (count the semitones). This is **neither — it's spatial.** It teaches what each interval *looks and feels like as a shape on the fretboard*, in two forms: along one string, and across strings.

That second form is the one that matters on guitar and the one most players never drill. The same interval has a completely different shape depending on which string pair it crosses, and the B-string tuning offset breaks the pattern. Doing it in order, small to large, is what makes the offsets learnable rather than surprising.

Pairs naturally with [[FND-C07 — Letter distance decides the interval number]] and [[FND-C06 — Interval quality names]] — this drill supplies the hands, those concepts supply the names.

# JAZ-C11 — The backdoor: ♭VII7 in place of V7

**Q.** Give the backdoor cadence in C, say where its chords come from, and say when you would reach for it instead of V7.

> [!question]- Answer
> **Fm7 – B♭7 – Cmaj7** — iv7 – ♭VII7 – I, often landing on Cmaj9 rather than a plain triad.
>
> Both borrowed chords come from **C minor**. B♭7 contains A♭, the ♭6 of C, which resolves down to G, and D, which is already in the tonic chord once you land on Cmaj9. **Reach for it when V7 sounds too final or too bright** — it is warmer, plagal-flavoured and more ambiguous. The lighter shorthand is the **backdoor dominant**: ♭VII7 straight to I with no minor iv in front.

## Why it matters

This is the substitution that already lives in the music Nate makes. Rihanna's "Stay" is Am7 → D in E; the whole chorus of "No Such Thing" is one long backdoor; Zero 7's "The Waiting Line" uses it as the main progression; and "Lights Up" resolves an A♭ minor song to A♭ major through it. Stevie Wonder used it twice on one record, each time set up by a ii–V aimed at IV that gets redirected onto the minor iv instead.

The backdoor dominant on its own — no minor iv — is even more common: "New York State of Mind", "Layla", "You Are So Beautiful".

## Drill

Take a loop that ends V–I and replace the cadence with iv7–♭VII7–I. Then strip the iv and use ♭VII7 alone. Decide which of the three endings the track wants.

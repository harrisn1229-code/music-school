# IN THE STUDIO w LENNO - Drum Layering, Sampling, Mixing Tips, The "-7LUFS Test" (Genius Twitch Tips)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)

## Synopsis
A broad, session-style walkthrough of Lenno working a track in progress, covering more ground than a single-topic breakdown: atmosphere/Foley layering under intros, a philosophy of giving each layer in a stack genuinely different musical content instead of doubling the same MIDI, a from-scratch drum-layering workflow built on clean base sounds plus a self-maintained bank of reusable drum WAVs pulled from old tracks, a baritone-guitar bass-doubling trick for width without widening the actual low end, Q&A on learning chords/harmony by ear from favorite songs, monitoring/translation philosophy (trust your setup, verify in the car, lean on references early and your own ears later), a multiband-exciter-instead-of-EQ move for making a vocal read without raising its level, and the video's namesake "-7 LUFS test" — an informal, feel-based loudness diagnostic rather than a number to chase. It closes on his clipping habits: self-clippers at the end of individual chains so peaks are already controlled before anything hits the master bus.

## Techniques
- **Layer in low-level Foley/atmosphere — birds down around −50 dB, tape-echo spring-reverb noise — especially under intros, so a lone piano or keyboard doesn't sit on "digital silence"** — without it, a solo instrument playing over true digital silence reads as claustrophobic; the human ear also craves small imperfections, and anything too static for too long starts to feel boring. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Run a low-level atmospheric loop underneath the entire drum layer stack purely to glue it into one instrument** — barely registers consciously on its own, but its presence is a big part of why a pile of individually-sourced drum layers reads as "a drum set" instead of "a collection of samples." → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Don't have every layer play the same MIDI part — give different layers different rhythmic/melodic roles** — worked example: a synth lead carries the chords while a separate wobbly arpeggiator layer plays a distinct line on top of those same chords rather than doubling them; varying WHAT each layer plays, not just how it's processed, is what builds richer texture. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **The wobbly-arp layer is a Serum preset run through modulation/lo-fi processing (sounds like an RC-20-style noise-and-wobble chain)** — even when it's barely audible on its own, this kind of subtle, unstable modulation layer contributes a felt sense of movement/life that a static layer doesn't. — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Double the bass with a subtle baritone-guitar layer to make the low end feel wider without actually widening the low end itself** — the guitar quietly tracks the bass part underneath it, adding perceived width/dimension while the actual bass signal stays narrow and mono-safe — avoids the phase problems a literal stereo-widened bass would create. → [[Width and Stereo]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Start every layered drum sound from a solid, clean base — go crazy on the layers on TOP, not on the foundation** — find a genuinely clean kick and a genuinely clean snare first; without a solid base to build on, extra time layering on top is "wasting your time." The actual source sounds in a stack can stay simple/plain as long as the base is solid — personality gets added by what's layered over it. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **When stuck on drums, drag layers in from an older finished track instead of starting from nothing — and make a habit of exporting every drum layer as a WAV once a track is done, building a "bank of things you know worked" to raid on future stuck days** — demoed pulling this session's kick, snare, and clap straight from an older remix ("Breathe"). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **A deliberately simple, tight, "old-school" clap sample from a pack, used as-is rather than heavily processed** — reinforces the "simple/solid base, get creative in the layering" philosophy at the individual-sample level: the clap itself doesn't need to be fancy if it's doing its job in the stack. — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Subtle drum-bus glue compression that isn't doing much visible gain reduction, plus a touch of added noise/character, is kept anyway "because it makes it sound more like a record"** — piling on small, mostly-inaudible imperfections like this is part of the same philosophy as the intro Foley/atmosphere layering above: static perfection reads as less real than a little grit. — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Reach for a multiband exciter instead of an EQ boost to bring up a problem frequency — it adds new harmonic content an EQ physically can't create** — find the frequency zone that needs to come forward (demoed on a vocal via iZotope Neutron's exciter, running on most channels in the session) and drive the exciter there instead of raising the band's level. A/B'd with it bypassed, the vocal audibly disappears back into the mix; even after compensating for the resulting volume difference, the exciter version stays more present/audible than a plain EQ boost would, because it's generating genuinely new harmonics rather than just amplifying what already exists. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[Ozone 9 Exciter]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **The "-7 LUFS test": if you can't get a mix feeling good, clean, and loud around −7 to −6 LUFS (already very loud), something is probably wrong with the mix** — common culprits: too much bass stealing all the headroom, or a low-end/low-mid buildup somewhere. It's used as a diagnostic threshold, not a target to chase — the actual goal is a nice, balanced, loud-and-clean feel, not hitting a specific number. → [[Clipping and Loudness]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Loudness stays "in the back of the head" during mixing, not something actively hunted** — thinks about loudness constantly given his genre (hard-hitting drums, heavy bass, compact arrangement) but deliberately avoids obsessively chasing extra dB while writing/mixing — it's a background consideration, not a mid-session task. — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Run self-clippers at the end of individual track/chain processing, not just on the master bus, so peaks are already controlled by the time signal reaches the master** — keeps the master's own processing from having to do "a ton of heavy lifting" since each element arrives already dialed in. → [[Clipping and Loudness]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **No fixed mixing rules — level/EQ decisions are made by ear and by feel, gated entirely by having a monitoring setup you can trust** — if your setup makes you turn the vocal up, but then it's way too loud in the car, that's a monitoring setup that lies to you (for one of several possible reasons) and it's untrustworthy until fixed. His current reference point: a Barefoot monitor setup in an acoustically-treated room (currently working in a temporary, less-treated space and noting the difference). → [[Referencing and Translation]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **References matter most early on; over time, with a consistent room and setup, you develop your own feel for translation and lean on them less** — no amount of rules substitutes for developed ears, but references are the fastest way to build that sense before it's internalized. → [[Referencing and Translation]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Learn chords/harmony by ear from favorite songs rather than through formal theory — you don't need to be able to play it on keys first** — his own approach had no formal keys education; Prince was a major influence on his harmonic vocabulary, learned section by section by ear. If you can't play a progression yet, you can still capture it by drawing it into the piano roll or writing it out in notation instead of waiting until you can perform it. → [[Melody Writing]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Some formal music theory training exists in his background but isn't treated as necessary for starting to produce** — a small amount of theory education helped, but he's explicit it isn't a prerequisite for getting started. — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)
- **Resample a stack of layers through a driven guitar-amp emulator and pedal, then chop the result into a usable sample** — printed the combined chord/arp layers through an amp sim pushed hard (boosted "all the way," acknowledged as a bit much but kept for character) into further pedal-style processing, then auditioned shorter chopped variants of the resulting sample for the arrangement. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Lenno (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO w LENNO](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9DhEPnRaKKw)

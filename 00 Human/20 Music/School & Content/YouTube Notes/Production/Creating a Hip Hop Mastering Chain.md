## Techniques

- **Opening mid-side EQ: mono the side lows, then a matched shape/carve on both images.** Attenuate the **side** image's low frequencies to make the low end more mono (club/vinyl-safe punch). On the same side image, boost a little **~400 Hz** for synth depth and dip **~2 kHz** to let the vocal cut through. On the **mid** image, attenuate **~250 Hz** to de-mask the vocal and boost **~2 kHz** for vocal presence. A different recipe from this vault's other Sage Audio mid/side-EQ entries (those pair a boost + matching side attenuation at the same frequency, or a side HPF + mid boost) — this one runs independent moves per image instead. Framed as a subtle, additive-feeling move despite being mostly subtractive. → [[EQ]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Resonance reduction via a "balance" preset + hand-adjusted pre-emphasis bands, confirmed against the Delta signal.** Very subtle overall attenuation of excess/resonant frequencies for a clearer, more balanced master. The workflow — start from a preset, then adjust what this vault's existing [[Soothe2]] note calls "pre-emphasis EQ bands," then A/B against a **Delta** solo of what's being removed — matches Soothe2's own documented mechanism and parameter vocabulary (⑥ Delta monitor) from a *different* Sage Audio video almost exactly, just starting from a different named preset ("Balance" here vs. "Bounce to the Grammy Awards" there). Read with high confidence as the same plugin despite auto-captions rendering the name as "resonance producer." → [[Soothe2]], [[EQ]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Maximizer dialed via its parallel-mix "amount" until output just barely shows attenuation, "Loud" style preferred.** Goal: maximize the signal without changing the mix's timbre. Increase the amount in the parallel mix level until the output meter is just barely starting to show attenuation — not further — and audition the unit's style/character options; the **Loud** setting read best on this master. Result: quieter details get pulled forward and the mix reads fuller. Reads as a **Weiss-style mastering maximizer** (Softube's Weiss MM-1, per this vault's existing [[Plugin Wishlist]] entry, based on the "parallel mix" architecture and style-selector language) — unowned; owned equivalent for the general upward-processing role: [[Oxford Inflator]]. → [[Oxford Inflator]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Split EQ used specifically for its mid-side panning of transient vs. tonal content — the standout move of the whole video, per the narration itself.** Base moves: boost the transience of the lows plus some low tone, dip tone **~250 Hz**, add tone and transients to the vocal range, boost some high-frequency transients. The move that actually mattered: **mid-side pan the split bands themselves** — center the transient band on the **lows** (huge, aggressive 808, mono-anchored punch) and spread the bands to the **side** image on the **highs** (extra width up top). Output had to be pulled down afterward to compensate for the added transient energy. This is Eventide Split EQ's documented mechanism (transient/tonal split + mid-side capability), already flagged unowned in [[Plugin Wishlist]] with a similar mid-side use case from a different video — this entry adds the *specific* low-transient-centered/high-transient-sided recipe. → [[EQ]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Program-dependent multiband saturation — different tube color per band, envelope-followed drive per band.** Split lows/mids/highs; warm tube on the lows and mids, clean tube on the highs specifically to keep the high-frequency transients detailed and bright rather than smeared. Envelope followers modulate each band's drive amount off the incoming transient, so distortion character tracks the signal dynamically instead of sitting at a fixed amount. Owned equivalent: [[Saturn 2]] (per-band Type + Dynamics/envelope modulation covers this exact recipe). → [[Saturn 2]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Fresh Air for hip-hop top-end: push both bands until it sounds bright enough, then Trim back to compensate.** Owned [[Fresh Air]] (Slate Digital) — amplify Mid Air and High Air until the mix reads bright enough, then pull Trim back down to compensate for the added gain (standard Fresh Air gain-staging, per this vault's existing note). Explicit caution: don't overuse it, it turns aggressive quickly. Called out as sounding especially good specifically on a hip-hop master. → [[Fresh Air]], [[EQ]]
- **A from-scratch parallel high-frequency bus: linear-phase EQ isolate highs → mid-side router mute the mid → transient expander on the isolated side highs, blended back as one control over brightness/width/punch.** Build a new bus with three processors in series: (1) a linear-phase EQ that isolates just the high frequencies, (2) a mid-side router that mutes the mid image (so only the side content of that isolated high band passes), (3) a transient expander on that isolated high-side-only signal. Blend the resulting bus back into the mix via its own fader — one parallel add-in that simultaneously shapes brightness, stereo width, and punchiness, rather than three separate moves. No home for this exact bus shape in [[Master Chain]] yet — logged as a Variation. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[Master Chain]]
- **An EQ pass that reads as [[Gullfoss]] by its own documented behavior, despite the auto-captions garbling the name.** On the master output: isolate the processing to the low-mids into the highs specifically so the kick and 808 stay unaffected, then use a good amount of the **Brighten** function for clarity — framed explicitly as **reducing masking**. This matches Gullfoss's actual parameter set (frequency-range limiters to exclude the kick/low end, a **Brighten** parameter, and Soundtheory's own "automatically reducing masking" framing) closely enough to read with real confidence as the same plugin used elsewhere in this vault's other Sage Audio sources, just with a heavier hand on Brighten than the mids/highs-only Recover-led approach documented there. Owned. → [[Gullfoss]], [[EQ]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Hard clipping on transients — the noise-as-exciter mechanism, framed around loudness this time rather than brightness.** A hard clipper distorts transients whenever they cross a threshold; the white-noise-like content generated at each clipped transient makes that transient read as *more* apparent even though its peak is being attenuated, and nets an overall loudness increase. The same mechanism already documented on owned [[kHs Clipper]] (there framed around brightness/exciter behavior) — this source frames the identical effect around perceived loudness instead, a useful second angle on the same technique. Exact plugin/model unresolved from captions ("hard clipper... with some detail preservation") — distinct enough phrasing from kHs Clipper's actual 3-knob panel (In Gain/Knee/Out Gain, no "detail" control) that it may be a different, unowned clipper; treat the *mechanism* as transcript-grounded and the specific product as unconfirmed. → [[kHs Clipper]], [[Clipping and Loudness]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Two-limiter finish: Sonnox Oxford Limiter's Enhance function into a Waves L2 running its dynamic release algorithm, landing ~−8 LUFS.** Limiter 1 — the (unowned) **Sonnox Oxford Limiter**, used specifically for its **Enhance** function (amplify quieter mix detail, further accentuate the clipped transients from the prior stage) — the same unit and function already extensively documented in this vault's [[Plugin Wishlist]] from three other Sage Audio videos, now with a third named pairing partner. Limiter 2 — **Waves L2 Ultramaximizer**, its **dynamic** release algorithm engaged, both limiting the signal and expanding some peaks for an aggressive, loud result — new to this vault (no prior Waves L2 citation). Final master: roughly **−8 LUFS integrated** — louder than [[Master Chain]]'s general −9 to −7 LUFS-I pop/trap target, offered explicitly as adjustable to taste rather than a fixed number. → [[Limiting]], [[Clipping and Loudness]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Master Chain]]

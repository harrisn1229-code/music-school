# BASS Slot — 60–250 Hz

The groove's pitch and the mix's warmth. Where the note of the low end actually reads.

## Swap list (interchangeable occupants)

- **Bass guitar** — the band feel; [[FX Chain System]] bass chains
- **Juno/analog synth bass** — [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]] (square+sub, unison)
- **Reese / growl low end** — [[Serum 2#Patch Recipes|Serum 2 growl recipe]] (sub split out to [[SUB Slot]])
- **808 harmonics** — the audible top of an 808 lives here even when its body is SUB
- **Piano left hand** — [[Keyscape]]; on sparse ballads the LH is the bass

## Mix rules

- Mono-ish below ~120 Hz; width only in the upper octave.
- Don't HPF a bass guitar past its ~100 Hz fundamental (house rule from [[Production System Report]]).
- Octave discipline vs [[SUB Slot]]: if both are occupied, they need different octaves or the sub yields.
- Compression: consistent bass = consistent mix; this slot takes heavier leveling than any other melodic slot.

## When it's the hook
The McCartney lane: melodic bassline as the hook (Beatles core taste) — push +1.5–2 dB, brighten 700 Hz–1.5 kHz so the melody reads on phones, and dip BED low-mids around it.

## When it competes
- **vs BED** ([[BED Slot]]): HPF beds/keys at 120–150 Hz; dynamic dip 200–350 Hz in the bed keyed by the bass ([[Masking and Carving]], [[Soothe2]] sidechain).
- **vs Kick**: classic pocket problem — sidechain or arrangement offset, same as SUB.
- **vs Vocal warmth** (200–350 Hz overlap): the vocal wins; static 1–2 dB dip in the bass there if the vocal sounds thin.
- **vs a hip-hop/trap percussion loop sharing this territory with an 808:** cede the loop's low end to the 808 outright (cut it, don't just duck it) and reclaim its cut-through higher up — Jaycen Joshua: "you're taking a little bit of the low end off because obviously the 808 is taking, or occupying, all of that. And you're adding a little bit of what you need — that upper high-mid that just lets that cut through." → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[SUB Slot]] — Mix with the Masters (Jaycen Joshua), [[Jaycen Joshua on creative drum mixing]]

## Drum lanes in this slot (from books)

- **Drum body/weight starting points inside 60–250 Hz.** Kick thump = low shelf **80–100 Hz** (what phones and small speakers actually reproduce as "kick"). Toms low-end power = boost **100–300 Hz**, cut below each drum's own fundamental (**60–200 Hz**, floor toms low / rack toms high) so they stop clashing. Snare body starts at the top of this slot (**200–300 Hz** shelf, running into [[BED Slot]]) — HPF the snare *below* its body, not at a fixed 200 Hz. → [[EQ]], [[Kick Design]], [[Snare Design]], [[2 Drums Group]] — Whipped Cream Sounds, [[Drum Mixing EQ Guide - Enrichment|Drum Mixing EQ Guide]]

## Bass tone lanes in this slot (from videos)

- **Plucking-hand position as a zero-gear bass/mids control — the fingerboard and neck ends of the spread.** Moving the plucking hand toward the fingerboard/neck bumps this slot up and pulls [[MIDS Slot]] down (visible directly on a spectrum analyzer); fingerboard position specifically reads warm/bassy/round — good for sitting back in the mix, e.g. a reggae bass brief ("nice and subby, sit back, fill out the bottom"). No EQ, no gear — full three-position framework: [[Plucking Position — Fingerboard, Neck, and Bridge Tone Shaping]]. → BassBuzz, [[Better Bass Tone with “The Producer Switch” (costs $0)]]
- **"Tubby the dwarf" — ~80 Hz rumble/boom, the bottom edge of this slot.** Cut it (counterintuitively, the amp's own bass knob) to fix stage-shaking rumble and free space for the kick; boost it (bass knob up, bass's passive tone knob rolled to zero) to chase James Jamerson's girthy Motown low end ("What's Goin' On," 80–120 Hz dominant) — genre-dependent, the same setting reads as far too much on an aggressive rock tune. Full five-zone breakdown on [[Bass EQ Frequency Zones — The Five Dwarves]]. → [[EQ]], [[SUB Slot]] — BassBuzz, [[How to Get the PERFECT Bass Tone]]

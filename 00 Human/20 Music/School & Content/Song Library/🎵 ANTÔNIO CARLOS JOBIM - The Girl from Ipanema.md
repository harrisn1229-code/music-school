# The Girl from Ipanema — Antônio Carlos Jobim

> Bossa nova standard, 32-bar AABA, typically played in F major (the famous Getz/Gilberto recording is in D♭) — a melody built on sustained chord extensions and a notoriously tricky bridge.

## Chords (as taught for guitar)
Key: F major. **A section:** Fmaj7 (I) → G7 (II7, often with ♯11) → turned minor, ii–V–I back to F — the exact same relative shape as the A section of Ellington's "Take the A Train" (reportedly an intentional Jobim homage). Then a direct half-step-up modulation into the bridge with no ii–V approach.

**Bridge (the confusing part):** minor9 → dominant-7-off-the-♭6, repeated at two more pitch levels, then a plain 2-5-5 back home. Unlocks with a reinterpretation trick: read the "confusing" chord's implied major-6 partner as its **relative minor** (a major-6 chord and its relative-minor-7 chord share every note) — reveals all four opening bridge chords as the *same* relationship repeated three times, not four unrelated grabs. → [[Chord Naming by Context]]

## Scales (for improvising)
A section: F major over Fmaj7 → G Mixolydian (= C major, upgrade to G Lydian dominant if the ♯11 is voiced) over G7 → back to F major. Bridge: G♭ Lydian (= D♭ major) over the maj7-type chords, Mixolydian (or Lydian dominant) over the dominant 7ths, Dorian over the minor chords (preserves the same relative-major/minor relationships as the chord analysis above), altered scale over the closing ii-V-ii-V's dominants. Default to plain Mixolydian/Dorian first — save the altered scale for once the basic changes are comfortable, since jumping straight to "what's the tritone sub" slows down real-time playing. → [[Scale Modes Reference]]

## Steal this
- **Reinterpret a puzzling chord as its relative major/minor to reveal a hidden repeating sequence** — turns "four chords that don't seem related" into one pattern moved three times. Works as a general reharmonization-reading trick, not just on this tune. → [[Chord Naming by Context]]
- **A rootless m9 voicing and a first-inversion maj7 voicing, one string set apart, are the same physical shape** — memorize one shape, reuse it as either chord depending on what root you're thinking against. → [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Sustain a melody's extensions (9ths) as main tones, not passing ones**, to get a floaty/soft feel rather than a plain chord-tone melody.

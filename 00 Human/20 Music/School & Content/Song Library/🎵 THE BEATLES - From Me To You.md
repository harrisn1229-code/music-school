# From Me To You — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

The third single, assembled almost entirely from techniques already in circulation — Pollack's framing is that a small fixed vocabulary still produced a first crop of originals that sound nothing like each other. Two things here are genuinely new: a **pivot modulation in the bridge**, among the first in their released catalogue, and an ending that lands on vi instead of I.

**Form.** Sprawling on paper — two bridges, two verses between, intro and outro — but tiny in time, because the verse is only 8 bars. Every section enters on a pickup. The solo verse is a **semi-solo**: an instrumental first half over the usual changes giving way to a verbatim sung return of the refrain half, a trick reused in "A Hard Day's Night". It doesn't exist in take 2, so it was presumably added because the song ran short.

**Harmony.** The verse is closed and stays inside the key; its one gratuitous chord is there purely for the bluesy bite. What lets verse 2 follow at all is the vi occupying the back half of the closing bar — take 2 held the tonic across the whole bar and the join is audibly worse. The bridge modulates on a chord already played: the previous verse's last chord is heard as I when struck, then **retroactively re-read as V of the new key** ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]). The common chord isn't required for a key change, but it's what makes this one seamless. The outro throws off an ambiguous augmented chord that could fall either way; the song takes the major, states the hook once more, then stops abruptly on the relative minor ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]) — the hook's own finality covering for the fact that nothing resolved.

**Melody.** More chromatic than usual for them. The hook appears in two rhythmic settings with identical pitches — square in the intro and the solo verse, bouncy and syncopated in the verses ([[MEL-C07 — What makes a hook]]) — and retreating to the square version is what makes the syncopated return feel bouncier ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]).

**Arrangement.** A duet essentially throughout, with no solo vocal anywhere. Paul's harmony crosses John's lead: above him in the verse's first phrase, below in the second.

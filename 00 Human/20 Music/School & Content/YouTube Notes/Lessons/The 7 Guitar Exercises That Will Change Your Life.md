Several exercises carry real, reusable technique content beyond the workout structure itself.

## Techniques

- **2-notes-per-string workout as a mid-tempo warm-up — deliberately countering a 3-notes-per-string bias.** BERNTH names his own past mistake: favoring fast 3-NPS shred patterns and neglecting 2-NPS drilling, which created a real gap in controlled, tight mid-tempo alternate picking (the tempo range actually used most). Goal checklist: to a click, no overlapping notes/unwanted noise, 100–120 BPM in 16th notes. Works with any chord shape, arpeggio, or cadence, not one fixed pattern.
- **Isolate-the-failure-moment methodology for building your own exercises.** When a specific lick or transition is especially hard, isolate exactly that moment and turn it into its own targeted drill rather than only drilling the whole lick — BERNTH's own fix for a "flying pinky"/finger-independence problem came from isolating precisely where fingers failed to stay close to the fretboard during a string change. A generalizable exercise-design principle, not tied to this specific problem.
- **7-note-group picking-direction-shift exercise.** Groups of 7 shift the down/up starting stroke on every string (starts down on string 1, but the first note on string 2 is an upstroke, continuing to shift throughout) — trains clean string transitions (goal: transitions inaudible to a listener) and hand synchronization on an odd, non-repeating pattern; loops indefinitely as an endurance workout since the shape just moves up/down the neck.
- **Advanced: two repeating one-note-per-string shapes across the neck, played with a sweeping motion.** Deliberately stacks multiple advanced skills in one exercise — economy of motion, finger independence, sweep-picking mechanics, and pull-offs — rather than drilling each separately. Explicitly adaptable to alternate picking instead of sweep picking if that's the skill being targeted.

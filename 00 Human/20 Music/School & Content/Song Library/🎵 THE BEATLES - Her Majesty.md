## Production & Arrangement
A **23-second solo-acoustic McCartney fragment** — the first-ever **hidden track**. It was snipped out of the Abbey Road medley, tossed onto the end of the reel by an engineer (instructed never to throw anything away), and the accidental placement stuck.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Her Majesty]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #193.*

**Form.** A fragment deliberately placed to sound accidental — one crashing D chord (the discarded final chord of "Mean Mr Mustard," slightly out of tune against what follows) and then a single verse, cut off mid-cadence. The verse is more carefully made than the joke implies: an unusual **18 bars**, a four-square 16-bar quatrain phrased AA′BA″ plus a 2-bar *petit reprise* of the last phrase. Phrase by phrase the harmonic shape runs closed · open to V · starting at vi and finishing at IV (a real harmonic misadventure at this scale) · convergent on home.

**Harmony.** `I (walking bass) – V-of-V – V – I | I – V-of-V – V | vi – V-of-IV – IV | ♯ii°(4/2) – I – V-of-ii – ii – V – I – V-of-ii | ii – V – I (cut)` — secondary dominants pointing three different directions ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). The first two bars of each opening phrase are what he calls a **harmonic envelope**: the D chord is genuinely sustained while the bass walks D–C♯–B–A underneath, and every half-bar does not need relabelling as a new chord. Harmonic rhythm follows no fixed pattern and then noticeably accelerates for the final phrase ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). The F♯ sitting high in the tune is what turns the E chords into E9s and the A chords into A13s. The diminished seventh opening the fourth phrase is the same chord as in "The End"; the chromatic voice leading forces an E♯ root rather than the tempting D.

**Notable.** The master ends on an A on beat 2 of the last bar and the two beats of D that should follow are simply gone — a clean demonstration of how thoroughly a V conditions the ear to expect its I, and how much the absence stings ([[HAR-C02 — Authentic cadence]]). The simplest recording in the catalogue: acoustic guitar and a single-tracked vocal, no claps, no taps, no click.

# Jaycen Joshua's drum bus

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LY-vU5MwBvs)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LY-vU5MwBvs)

## Synopsis

A short, tightly-focused Mix with the Masters clip of Jaycen Joshua walking through his drum bus chain stage by stage: a Shadow Hills Mastering Compressor barely engaged for character rather than gain reduction, a gate used only to manage the raised noise floor that heavy compression/plugin chains introduce (not to remove real drum noise), a broad low-end dip followed by a small presence boost, and — the video's own "game changer" — a mid/side EQ pass that carves a pocket for the vocal by dipping the drum bus's center image, plus the complementary Ozone-style Mono Maker (collapsing everything below ~61 Hz to mono) and a matched cut-center/boost-sides move at ~112 Hz that recovers "excitement" without re-crowding the vocal. Companion piece to the same channel's longer [[Mixing Summits with Jaycen Joshua]] session, which covers his broader mixing philosophy (gain staging, NLS, sidechain theory); this one is narrowly about the drum bus signal path itself. Everything here is stated as deliberately subtle — every processor is described as "barely" touched, "kissed," or dialed back once it did its job, which is itself the throughline lesson: get what you need from each stage, then back off.

## Techniques

- **Shadow Hills Mastering Compressor, run via the plugin (not hardware), barely engaged — reliance is on the unit's character, not gain reduction.** A/B'd against the hardware insert ("we beat it, we did the test") and the plugin won nine times out of ten for its automation flexibility, sounding "so close" to hardware that the choice came down to workflow, not tone. On the drum bus specifically, the compressor is driven "to give me my sound" but barely triggers GR — described as "I just kiss it." The unnamed opening riff about theme-park sound systems pumping upper harmonics to fake a heavier low end (low confidence — the transcript is garbled here) seems to be framing the same idea: it's the harmonic/tonal effect of driving the unit that matters, not the amount of compression. → [[Compression]] (effect), [[Gain Staging]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters)
- **A gate used ONLY as noise-floor control on a heavily-processed bus, not as a transient/dynamics tool — hold + release set to roughly 1 second combined so it never chatters between hits.** "I don't take away any of the noise, I just don't want a high noise floor" — heavy compression plus a chain of plugins raises the audible noise floor, so the gate's only job is making sure it's silent between hits and present while playing, with hold/release tuned generously (~1 second) specifically so it doesn't close and reopen between closely-spaced hits within a phrase — most engineers ("a lot of people") leave it faster/tighter than this. → [[Gate]], [[Gain Staging]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters)
- **"I'm a dipper" — cut before you boost, philosophy stated explicitly as a default EQ approach.** On the drum bus: a broad low-end dip from **~1100 Hz and below, about 2.5 dB**, applied because that region was reading "a little heavy" and was over-driving the upstream saturation/harmonic stages (too many upper harmonics being generated as a result); pulling it back **~2.5 dB** restored clarity, openness, and separation. Followed by a small presence-region boost ("a little bit more top") once the low-mid weight was already dealt with — cut the offending zone first, then add only what's still missing, rather than boosting broadly from the start. → [[EQ]] (effect), [[Pro-Q 4]], [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters)
- **Mid/side EQ used to physically carve a pocket for the vocal — dip the drum bus's CENTER (mid) image specifically, because that's where the drums are living.** Framed as a mastering-engineer technique newly available to mixers: "playing with the three sides that you have — the center section, the left, and the right… you can EQ the center alone and find pockets for other instruments." Used here to dip the drum bus's mid image where it was carrying "a little too much weight," making explicit room for the vocal to sit in that same center pocket rather than fighting the drums for it. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters)
- **Mono Maker: collapse everything below a set crossover to mono/center — named directly, crossover set at ~61 Hz.** "It's called the mono maker… taking all your low-end signal from wherever you set the cross point — 61 hertz and below — and putting it right in your middle." Stated preference (not a hard rule): a centered low end reads better/tighter, "period, point blank," though he frames it as taste rather than a universal law — sometimes a stereo low end or unconventional width is the right call instead. Directly matches [[Ozone 9 Imager]]'s own Mono Maker control (adjustable crossover, forces everything below it to mono). → [[Ozone 9 Imager]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters)
- **Matched cut-center/boost-sides EQ move at the SAME frequency (~112 Hz) — removes a frequency from the vocal's pocket without losing the energy it carried, by putting it back on the outside instead.** The general mastering-EQ mechanism stated first ("if you have a synth and you want to do anything to the outside only and don't want to affect the vocal… deduct here, then bring it back where you deducted and boost it on the outside") then demoed on the drum bus: cut a frequency in the center image (protecting the vocal's space), boost that exact same frequency on the side/outside image so the energy isn't simply lost — "you didn't lose the excitement because you added it back on the outside, away from the vocal." Run extremely lightly — "kissed it… you barely can feel it" — at **~112 Hz**. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters)
- **Order of operations across the whole clip: subtle glue compression → noise-floor gate → broad EQ dip + small presence boost → mid/side pocket carve → mono maker → matched mid/side EQ recovery.** No single stage does much on its own by design — each is described as barely engaged, and the cumulative effect is the point. Worth reading as a template for a mastering-style bus chain applied to a mix-stage drum bus, not just a mastering chain proper. → [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]], [[Compression Staging]] — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters)

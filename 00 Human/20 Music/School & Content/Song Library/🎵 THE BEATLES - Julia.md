# Julia — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A fingerpicked acoustic song whose harmony is the least folk-like thing about it: **strong chord colours over deliberately weak root motion**, with dissonances that arrive unprepared and leave unresolved. Pollack's phrase is *free dissonance*, and this is the best single case of it in the catalogue — there's enough V–I to keep the key in place, and almost nothing else behaves.

**Form.** The section labels are genuinely arguable; what settles them is the title being repeated mantra-like in the refrain regardless of which other words change. The **verse is 9 bars as 4 + 5**, the **refrain 13 as 4 + 4 + 5**, and both elide forward into whatever follows; the bridge is 10 bars scanning 4 + 2 + 4 after a two-bar wind-down.

**Harmony.** The vi7 is the model case of **free dissonance** — the seventh exists only because one note is held across the whole phrase, and it does no harmonic or contrapuntal work ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]). Root motion is intentionally slack: vi→iii is a fourth down, passive like a plagal move rather than driving like V–I, and iii→V is weaker still because the two triads share two of their three notes. The refrain is where the real work happens: **vi into a minor v** in a major key, then a chord that reads as **V-of-ii** and retroactively recasts what preceded it as a pivot ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]) — and the promised modulation is then simply refused, the next chord being the Lennon-standard **minor iv** ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]), which throws off a double cross-relation and withholds its own root until beat 3. That root-delay is systematic: **the picking pattern often postpones the root until the middle of the bar in which the chord nominally changes**, so for two beats you can't name it. The bridge modulates to the relative minor ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]) and does it weakly on purpose.

**Melody.** Confined to a **sixth**, which is where the held-back quality comes from. Exactly two escapes from that box, and both are saved for late.

**Arrangement.** Fingerpicked acoustic, sung sotto voce, recorded at a level so low that playing it audibly makes everything else on the album too loud. The lead is single-tracked and deliberately flat in delivery, **doubled only for the last phrase of each section**. In the outro John **drops the iii chord from the penultimate pass and restores it for the very last one** — its absence is what tells you it had been carrying the song's yearning all along.

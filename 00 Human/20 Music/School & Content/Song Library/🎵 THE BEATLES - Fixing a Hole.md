# Fixing a Hole — The Beatles

> McCartney on harpsichord, George Martin plays a second harpsichord countermelody. Descending chromatic bass line anchors the verses. Harrison guitar solo opens the bridge. Recorded at Regent Sound — the first time the Beatles used a studio other than Abbey Road.

## Chords
**Key:** F major (slipping to F minor) · the verse drifts toward C in the bridge

**Verse:** F – C+ – Fm7 – Fm6
→ **I – V+ – i7 – i6** (the C augmented and the slide into F **minor** create the woozy chromatic inner line)

**"…really doesn't matter if I'm wrong":** F – C – F
→ **I – V – I**

**"See the people standing there":** C – G – C – G – C
→ briefly tonicizes C (**I – V – I**, i.e. **V – V/V** of the home F)

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Fixing A Hole]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #110.*

**Form.** A **split stylistic personality by section**: the verse a Gershwinesque jazz/blues hybrid in a mixed F minor, the bridge a harmonically clean major-mode torch-song march. The frame itself is an ordinary two-bridge model of the sub-type with two middle verses, the second instrumental. Intro is a strange **two and a half bars** — a brief harpsichord figure, then hi-hat. Verse 8 bars, parsed as one long 6-bar phrase plus a trailing 2-bar obbligato and derived directly from the intro's progression. Bridge 8 as 4+4. The outro is built on a verse and a half, with the fade well underway before its first eight bars are done.

**Harmony.** `Intro: I – V+ | i7♭ – IV | (held)`, inner voices descending A – G♯ and F – E – E♭ – D · `Verse: I – V+ | i | i7♭ | IV6/4 · i | IV | i | IV` · `Bridge: I – V ×3 · I · V – V-of-V ×3 · V`. The rapid **F major to F minor** shift comes straight out of the intro, and the verse mode is a variety of F minor tinged by **both the blues ♭7 and the Dorian raised 6** ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]), with a chromatic descent running through a middle voice throughout. He spells the augmented chord's fifth as G♯ rather than A♭, hearing it as V with a raised fifth.

**Notable.** The two sections' contours are as complementary as their harmony ([[MEL-C05 — Register and intensity]]): the verse spans an octave plus a third (F up to A♭) mixing steps and skips, the bridge restricts itself to a fifth and hammers a subset — and although the bridge *sits* higher it **tops out a half step below the verse**, so the verse keeps the song's melodic peak. The vocal plan is scheduled section by section, with backing voices withheld until the second bridge.

**Against this page.** The page reads F major slipping to F minor; **Pollack inverts that emphasis** — his heading is "F (Dorian minor and Major)," and his signature reading is the *reverse* of the familiar borrowing: a **major IV inside a minor home key**, which reads hard-boiled rather than sentimental ([[POP-C10 — Dorian IV vs Aeolian iv]]).

## Synopsis

BERNTH teaches one specific voicing — E5#11 — as a dark/mysterious alternative to a plain power chord, then generalizes it into a reusable method for adding chord tensions (9/11/13) to any chord shape without memorizing new fingerings. A second, independent data point for adding "jazz" tension color to metal/rock power chords, already partly covered on [[Chord Voicings]] from a different BERNTH video.

## What's taught

- **E5#11 voicing:** built from an E5 power chord (root E, perfect 5th B, doubled across strings) with the sharp-11 interval (A♯) added on top instead of the expected minor 3rd (G) that would make it a plain E minor. Fretted: E and B doubled (2nd fret A string, 2nd fret D string, open B, open E), sharp-11 (A♯) voiced above. Compared directly A/B against plain E minor — described as far darker/more mysterious/suspenseful than the "bland" minor triad.
- **Tension-interval mapping (9/11/13), stated directly and worth keeping as reference:** tensions sit above the octave, so they're numbered starting from 9 — **9th = major 2nd** (one octave up), **11th = perfect 4th** (one octave up), **13th = major 6th** (one octave up). To add a sharp 11 over a root: find the perfect 4th above the root, raise it a semitone, then voice it an octave higher in the chord.
- **The reusable method — add tensions to a shape you already know, instead of memorizing a new chord shape per tension.** E.g. an "E minor add 9" needs no new shape: start from the plain E minor triad shape already under the fingers, then add the major 2nd (F♯) an octave up on top. Framed explicitly as freedom from constantly learning new chord-shape vocabulary — once the interval math is internalized, any known triad/seventh shape can take an added tension by ear/theory rather than by rote shape memorization.
- **Application — arpeggiating and octave-shifting the tension chord for lead/riff material.** Demoed moving the top three notes of the E5#11 voicing (A♯, B, E) up one octave for a related but higher-register riff idea, and arpeggiating the full chord (E, G, A♯, B, E, G) to outline the same tension melodically rather than only as a block chord.

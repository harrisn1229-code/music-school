# 7 SOLO IMPROV MISTAKES EVERY INTERMEDIATE GUITAR PLAYER MAKES

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OibqN017nd0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OibqN017nd0)

## Synopsis

Seven soloing mistakes; five overlap ground already documented on [[10 Hidden Soloing Mistakes and Constraint-Based Fixes]] and elsewhere, two are genuinely new: mistaking metronome-drilled shape recall for real improvisational fluency, and playing purely mechanically without enough spare attention to actually listen to the ensemble while soloing.

## Techniques

- **New — "convincing yourself you know a shape when you don't":** playing a scale shape cleanly with a metronome is a different, easier skill than using it to improvise; freezing/sticking in one spot the moment real improv starts reveals the gap. Fix: practice soloing with one shape at a time, in every fretboard position, until it survives actual improvisation.
- **New — "playing with your fingers, not your mind":** even once shapes are internalized across the neck, execution can still consume all conscious attention; deliberately build spare mental bandwidth during a solo to listen to the rest of the ensemble, not just your own hands.
- The other five (bad rhythm, staccato-by-default tone, scale-running over chord-tone targeting, only using the high strings, botched chord transitions) duplicate existing coverage.

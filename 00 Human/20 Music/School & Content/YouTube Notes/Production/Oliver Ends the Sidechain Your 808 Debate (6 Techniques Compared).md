# Oliver ENDS The "Sidechain Your 808" Debate (6 Popular Techniques Compared)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ORENdwJV15Y)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ORENdwJV15Y)

## Synopsis

Oliver A/B-tests six different ways of getting a kick and an 808 to coexist without clipping, on the exact same kick + 808 pair each time, then prints and compares all six. The verdict isn't "sidechain always" or "never sidechain" (the video's title is a bit of a bait — his real point is closer to Kenny Beats' [[Kenny Beats - 808 & Kick Together + Subtractive EQ|"sidechain isn't a band-aid"]] stance, arrived at from the opposite direction: he tries every fix, hears that most of them sound equally clean, and lands on "there's no right or wrong way, it depends on the sound and the genre"). His own go-to when it works: nothing at all except a limiter on the print, which "sounds sick" because the limiter itself handles the transient clash. Multiband sidechain is his pick specifically when the bass patch has mid/high content worth protecting (acoustic-kick samples, hip-hop 808s with real character above the sub).

## Techniques

- **Six ways to resolve kick/808 clash, ranked by what actually sounds different:**
  1. **Do nothing** — clips, the only method he rules out outright.
  2. **Manual volume automation ("volume fade") on the 808** — hand-draw a dip in the 808's level right where the kick hits, instead of a compressor. Clean, simple, no plugin needed.
  3. **Subtractive EQ (roll off the 808's low end where the kick lives)** — sounds bad to him, "loses all the fundamental of the kick." A caution against this specific move, distinct from Kenny Beats' broader subtractive-EQ approach which cuts *other* instruments' lows, not the kick/808 pair itself.
  4. **Phase alignment** — nudge/crossfade the 808 sample against the kick sample until their waveforms line up and stop cancelling. Technically the cleanest result of all six, but only workable if the 808 sits on the exact same note/timing every single time — impractical across a normal arrangement with note changes.
  5. **Standard sidechain compression, triggered off a separate click track rather than the kick itself** — send a click (not the kick) into the sidechain input so the duck timing can be tuned independently of the kick's own envelope; raise the compressor's Release until the post-release volume spike right after the kick's attack disappears — that release value is "the sweet spot."
  6. **Multiband sidechain** — a free Max for Live frequency splitter isolates just the low band of the 808 to duck, leaving the mid/high content untouched and un-ducked. His pick whenever the bass sound has real mid/high character worth preserving (an acoustic-kick sample, or hip-hop-style 808s), so the duck doesn't chew through the parts of the sound that aren't actually masking the kick.
- **His actual preference in practice: nothing on the 808 except a stock Ableton Limiter on the printed pair.** Once he heard all six side by side, this was the one that "sounds sick" to him — the limiter itself absorbs the clipping/clash instead of a dedicated ducking or EQ move. → [[Limiter]]
- **Sidechain the compressor off a click track, not the kick, so the release can be tuned independently of the kick's own transient shape** — a distinct move from the usual "sidechain input = the kick track" setup; decoupling trigger timing from the actual kick lets the release knob be dialed purely by ear against the resulting artifact (the post-attack spike) rather than fighting the kick's own envelope shape.
- **There's no universally correct method — the "right" one depends on sound selection and genre**, and even a deliberately distorted/clashing kick is a legitimate choice on the right track. The skill isn't picking the "correct" technique, it's knowing what each one actually does so a bad-sounding result is a choice, not an accident.

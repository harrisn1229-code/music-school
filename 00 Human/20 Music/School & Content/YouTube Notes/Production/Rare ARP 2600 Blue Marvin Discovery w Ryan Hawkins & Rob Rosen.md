# Rare ARP 2600 Blue Marvin Discovery w/ Ryan Hawkins & Rob Rosen

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hAq6uXENKr8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hAq6uXENKr8)

## Synopsis

A provenance story and design-archaeology deep-dive on the "Blue Marvin" — one of the earliest ~20-25 ARP 2600 units ever built (this one is #19), rediscovered in a stranger's basement via a Reddit post and restored by tech Ryan Hawkins in partnership with the Alan R. Pearlman Foundation. More gear-history/collector documentary than a playing tutorial, but genuinely rich in concrete engineering differences between this earliest production run and the familiar later gray-face 2600s.

## Techniques

- **No "multiples" (mult jacks) on this earliest run** — a feature added starting with the next revision, the "Gray Meanie."
- **No fine-tune control on the filter's initial frequency** — coarse-only, even though the oscillators already had fine-tune from the start; added on later units.
- **An unlabeled, ambiguous voltage-processor section** — no clear +10V/-10V/keyboard-CV source labeling the way later units have, suggesting the section's final design wasn't locked yet at this point in production.
- **Modules are a different physical shape (rectangular vs. later square modules) and fully epoxy-potted** — unrepairable if they fail, a real long-term reliability risk unique to surviving units this early.
- **Production timeline**: ~20-25 "Blue Marvin" units → the "Gray Meanie" (same case, ~35 units, added multiples + filter fine-tune) → switch to a more durable road-case-style chassis, since the original case design cracked easily.
- **A hand-corrected silkscreening error**: this unit's waveform icons were originally printed swapped/wrong, individually hand-repainted by an ARP employee's artist wife.
- **Wide-open input headroom compared to later units**: mixing multiple oscillators in past roughly halfway clips immediately into rich, warm, Moog-Modular-like harmonic saturation; later "cleaned up" revisions need levels driven much further to reach the same character — a real gain-staging difference between eras.
- **A microtonal keyboard feature dropped in later revisions**: the earliest ARP keybeds had a variable-tone-interval control, letting a physical half-step key movement output any interval instead of a fixed chromatic half-step — reconfigurable to whole-tone, quarter-tone, or even a full 13-tone-per-octave scale. Later 3620 keybeds dropped this in favor of a built-in LFO and pitch/mod wheel.
- **Keybed mechanism changed generation to generation**: earliest units used a Pratt-Read-style spring keybed (feel closer to a Minimoog's); later 3620 units moved to a cheaper contact-wire mechanism.
- **A flaky but real "gated reverb" trick**: patching sample & hold or a slow sine wave directly into the reverb input (rather than through the normal signal path) can produce a gated-reverb-like effect — unreliable on this specific unit's aging jack, but a real technique worth trying on more stable hardware.
- **General ARP 2600 pedagogy point, restated**: the layout (audio path one direction, control path another) is framed as ideal for learning synthesis — "your imagination's the limit."

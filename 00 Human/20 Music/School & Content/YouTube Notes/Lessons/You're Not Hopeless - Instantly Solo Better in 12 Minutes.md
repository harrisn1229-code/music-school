# You're Not Hopeless: Instantly Solo Better in 12 Minutes

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-uPmYkbIwvc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-uPmYkbIwvc)

## Synopsis

A single worked example — soloing over an Em–G–D–C progression (key of E minor) — walked through all five CAGED positions on the neck, at each stop naming the matching mode, pentatonic shape, and arpeggio shapes for all four chords. The real teaching point isn't "learn more positions" — it's that most players who complain about being "stuck in the box" haven't actually mastered chord-tone targeting even inside the one box position they already know, and jumping to new positions won't fix that gap.

## Techniques

- **The core reframe: staying in one position isn't the problem — not knowing it well enough is.** Most self-diagnosed "stuck in the box" players can play the pentatonic shape but can't reliably target chord tones within it — so the fix isn't escaping the box, it's getting good enough at the one you're in that you're properly outlining the chords rather than just running the scale shape.
- **The five-position CAGED map for Em–G–D–C (key of E minor), each with its matching mode/pentatonic/arpeggio set:**
  - **3rd position (frets 2–7ish, index around fret 3):** G major scale / G major pentatonic / arpeggios for all four chords in position. Recommends leaning on **arpeggios specifically** here since most players' scale and pentatonic playing is already decent but their arpeggio vocabulary is weak — deliberately practicing the weaker skill.
  - **5th position (frets 5–8ish):** A Dorian / "A Dorian pentatonic" (his informal name for the D-CAGED-shape pentatonic).
  - **7th position (frets 7–10ish):** B Phrygian / B Phrygian pentatonic (C-CAGED-shape).
  - **10th position (frets 10–13ish):** D Mixolydian / D Mixolydian pentatonic (A-CAGED-shape).
  - **12th position:** E Aeolian / E minor pentatonic (G-CAGED-shape) — the "infamous box," i.e. the position most intermediate players default to and never leave.
  - 15th position is skipped as redundant — it's the same shapes as 3rd position, one octave up, since the five positions are chosen by walking the G major pentatonic scale's own notes (3, 5, 7, 10, 12).
- **At every position, work out and physically locate the chord tones for all four chords (Em, G, D, C) in that position** — not just the scale/pentatonic shape. Framed as the real differentiator between amateur and advanced improvisers: amateurs play the scale shape regardless of the chord underneath; advanced players shift which notes they emphasize as the chords change, using the same fixed hand position.
- **Chord tones extend beyond the notes of a barre-chord grip.** E.g. Em's full chord tones (E, G, B) may appear in more places within a given position than the notes actually fretted in the barre-chord shape — those extra instances are still legitimate targets when soloing even though they're not part of the compact chord voicing.
- **The graduation test, explicitly named:** in the 12th (box) position, play four passes over the same progression — (1) scale only, (2) pentatonic only, (3) chord tones only, (4) all three blended, following the chord changes. Being able to do pass 4 fluently is described as "officially graduating from the box."

# How I Programmed the Lead Synth on Michael Jackson's PYT

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mOm5FPGrvOs)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mOm5FPGrvOs)

## Synopsis

A compact patch recreation of the flute-like lead on "P.Y.T. (Pretty Young Thing)" on the original session Minimoog, doubling as a clean statement of Marinelli's general 4-part patch-diagnosis method.

## Techniques

- **A 4-part ear-diagnosis framework, mapped directly to synth sections**: pitch → oscillators, timbre → filters, duration → envelope generators, amplitude → VCA. Explicitly the same listening order used across his other patch-recreation videos in this batch, stated here as an explicit named method rather than implied through practice.
- **Minimoog's filtered-down triangle wave as the practical substitute for a pure sine**: the Minimoog has no dedicated sine oscillator, so a triangle wave with the filter closed most of the way is used to get the closest available "pure," low-harmonic tone — single oscillator only, no layering.
- **Filter set low with a touch of resonance** — enough resonance for a bit of body/presence, but not pushed further because heavy resonance thins the tone out here.
- **Filter envelope**: long decay, mostly sitting at a high sustain level — a slow-evolving, mostly-static filter shape rather than an audible sweep.
- **VCA envelope**: high sustain, with a SLIGHT slow attack — demonstrated A/B, a fast/instant attack reads harsh; softening the attack slightly gives a noticeably more "beautiful," flute-like onset.
- **Vibrato via a dedicated third oscillator used purely as an LFO**: oscillator 3 carries no audio — it's switched to low-frequency mode and routed to modulate oscillator 1's pitch. Waveform shape changes the vibrato character audibly: sine gives smooth, musical vibrato; sawtooth and square each give a distinctly different (less musical) vibrato shape — sine is the correct choice here.

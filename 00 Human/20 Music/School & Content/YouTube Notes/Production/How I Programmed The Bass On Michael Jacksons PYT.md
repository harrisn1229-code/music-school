# How I Programmed The Bass On Michael Jackson's PYT

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EVx9CnUqXGM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EVx9CnUqXGM)

## Synopsis

The ARP 2600 (Tonus module, Moog-ladder-filter variant) bass from "P.Y.T." — doubling a live electric bass part rather than standing alone — plus a real production-history anecdote (the song was written and demoed last-minute, in a hallway, on a LinnDrum borrowed from the *E.T.* sessions). The core technique is a detuned-oscillator-cancellation fix and a well-characterized envelope-modulation sweet spot.

## Techniques

- **Deliberately push one oscillator's level slightly ABOVE the other on a two-oscillator bass specifically to prevent fundamental cancellation when the oscillators are closely in tune.** Two very-in-tune oscillators can partially cancel each other's fundamental; making one dominant (even by a small, barely-audible-on-its-own amount) guarantees the fundamental is always present from at least one source. The same fix already logged more briefly elsewhere on this channel (see the "10 Tips" checklist entry on [[Bass Design]]) — this video is the fuller explanation of the mechanism (cancellation risk), not a new technique.
- **Oscillator role split: a pulse wave (~22% width, "reedy," closer to acoustic/electric bass than a pure square) an octave up for definition/personality, a sawtooth an octave down for low-end support — neither alone has "enough personality," the combination does.** Pulse width dialed by ear between "square" and "reedy" rather than to a fixed percentage.
- **The envelope-to-filter modulation amount has a real, narrow sweet spot on a bass patch: too low reads as "plucky doll" (thin, toy-like), too high loses the bass fundamental entirely — get it "low enough" while retaining "enough articulation" to cut through the track.** A concrete framing for tuning Contour/envelope-amount by ear rather than a fixed target value.
- **Keyboard filter tracking OFF for this bass (reinforcing the general bass rule elsewhere on this channel), with the explicit caveat that SOME parts do want a little tracking** — stated as a case-by-case judgment, not an absolute rule.
- **Skip the VCA entirely and patch straight from the filter output to the mix — deliberately, "because I like the sound of not coloring it with a VCA."** Same non-default patching choice already logged from the Billie Jean bass breakdown (see [[Voice Architecture]]); a second independent instance of the same technique, reinforcing it as a real recurring choice on ARP 2600 bass patches specifically.

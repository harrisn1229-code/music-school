# Roland D-50

## What it is
Roland's answer to the DX7 and one of the best-selling synths ever — the machine that invented the "attack sample + synth sustain" trick and, with it, the entire late-80s digital soundscape. Its factory presets are so famous they're practically session musicians.

## What makes the sound
- **LA (Linear Arithmetic) synthesis**: the psychoacoustic insight that a sound's identity lives in its *attack* — so the D-50 splices a short PCM transient (breath chiff, pluck, mallet strike) onto a cheap synthesized sustain, and the ear hears an expensive complete instrument.
- **Onboard chorus + reverb** (a first) meant every patch shipped pre-produced and glossy — the "digital sheen" of 1987–91 records is partly just D-50 effects.
- The **preset canon**: *Fantasia* (bell-pad), *Digital Native Dance* (rhythmic texture), *Pizzagogo* (plucked pizz), *Staccato Heaven* — instantly recognizable across hundreds of records.
- Glassy, breathy, slightly cold — the anti-analog aesthetic done with total confidence.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Omnisphere]] (contains D-50-lineage sampled sources and does the attack+pad splice natively — layer a transient sample over a pad in one patch), [[Serum 2]]/[[Wavetable]] with a noise-transient layer; [[Nexus]] is spiritually the D-50's ROMpler descendant.
- **Paid:** Roland Cloud D-50 (exact software recreation, presets included).
- **Free:** the layered transient-plus-pad recipe in any owned synth.

## Used on
- Enya — "Orinoco Flow" (1988): the *Pizzagogo* pluck is the hook's sound (widely documented).
- Genre/era: 1987–91 pop/ballad/new-age wholesale, film/TV underscoring, late-80s R&B — the presets were unavoidable; specific credits beyond Enya generally stay era-level.

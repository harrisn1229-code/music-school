# E-mu Drumulator

## What it is
E-mu's $995 shot across the Linn's bow — a sampled-drum machine at a quarter of the [[Linn LM-1]]'s price that sold ~10,000 units, saved the company, and funded everything E-mu did next (Emulator II, [[E-mu SP-12]], [[E-mu SP-1200]]). The people's Linn of 1983.

## What makes the sound
- **8-bit companded ROM samples** of a tight studio kit — dry, mid-forward, a little dark; less hi-fi than a LinnDrum, less brutal than a [[Oberheim DMX]], sitting in its own polite-but-punchy pocket.
- **Fixed kit, minimal shaping** (levels and limited dynamics) — the identity is the ROM print itself and E-mu's companding grit.
- Solid pattern/song sequencer with individual outs at a hobbyist price — its real contribution was *access*, seeding sampled drums across thousands of small-studio records.
- Third-party and E-mu **replacement ROM sets** (rock kits, latin, electronic) gave it more voices than any competitor's fixed chips — an early "expansion pack" culture.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Impulse]] with Drumulator sample sets; [[kHs Bitcrush]] for the 8-bit edge; [[Cassette]]/[[RC-20 Retro Color]] for the era.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2; vintage archives (Samples From Mars et al.).
- **Free:** Drumulator ROM-set samples circulate freely.

## Used on
- Genre/era: 1983–85 new wave, synthpop, and budget studio pop/R&B — it was *everywhere* in small studios, but that ubiquity means few marquee record-level credits are cleanly documented, so this stays genre-level.

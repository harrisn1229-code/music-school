# The HARDEST Riffs: How To Play EPIC Modern Guitar Riffs!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ybIp3ZkPmq4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ybIp3ZkPmq4)

## Synopsis

A workflow breakdown for writing a technical riff (four chords, B minor–C major–A minor–G major) by layering complexity in passes: basic triads → 7th chords → a modal reharmonization of the last chord → a "chord melody" pass that overlays a scale/arpeggio idea onto each chord shape using tapping and sweep picking. The riff-writing process itself (painter/builder analogy — foundation first, then details) is as much the lesson as any single technique.

## What's taught

- **Layering pass 1 — 7th chords instead of triads.** Add the major 7th to major chords and minor 7th to minor chords (not a dominant 7th) — B minor becomes Bm7, C major becomes Cmaj7, etc. Immediately reads as more atmospheric/dissonant than plain triads.
- **Layering pass 2 — modal reharmonization on the final chord.** Instead of a plain G major 7 to close the progression, use **Lydian** (raised 4th/11th) for "one of my favorite major modes" — the classic G major 7 voicing with the raised-4th interval barred on top.
- **Layering pass 3 — chord melody: pick a scale or arpeggio that matches each chord shape, then decorate it with tapping.** Per chord: for Bm7, visualize the B minor pentatonic box overlapping the chord's root, then tap a note to extend the phrase rhythmically with percussive muting mixed in. For Cmaj7, switch from a scale-based approach to an **arpeggio shape** (root-3rd-5th-maj7-octave-9th, all tapped) specifically because tapping lets the picking hand add notes independent of the fretting hand's stretch limits — likened to a two-handed piano approach.
- **Tapping harmonic.** For the Em7 chord, tap the string *without* pressing down (touching lightly at the octave node) to sound a harmonic rather than a fretted note — different technique from a normal tapped note.
- **Sweep-picking a chord voicing as an arpeggio** (not a scale run) — reuse the same chord-shape voicing already fretted for rhythm, then sweep up/down through it with the picking hand, letting notes overlap slightly and using palm muting to keep the pulse defined; framed as "arpeggiating a chord" rather than a lead-solo sweep run.
- **Rhythmic phrasing over the final Lydian chord** — deliberately not a straight ascending/descending run; the root gets a small muted accent, then the rest of the arpeggio rings out, to make the rhythm itself melodically interesting rather than just fast.
- **Big-picture takeaway (stated directly):** visualize scales/arpeggios *from* chord voicings you're already fretting, rather than treating rhythm and lead guitar as separate systems — this is what makes the "chord melody" hybrid possible.

# Rick Beato - How to Play Bossa Nova Guitar - Jobim Style

![](https://youtu.be/vuEa03KJIgs)

Nate's capture note: *"Bossa nova technique. Add to genre note."*

Teaches **"The Girl from Ipanema"** exactly as played on the Astrud Gilberto / João Gilberto / Stan Getz recording — unsimplified. The claim that makes it worth the seven minutes: **the whole tune is seven chord shapes and one right-hand pattern.**

## The seven shapes

Beato gives these as *shapes*, movable and reusable, not as one-off chords. Fingerings are from the video, low string to high.

| # | Chord | Fingering | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A♭6/9** | index bars D+G strings; 3rd finger 4th fret B; index 4th fret low E | A section, opening vamp |
| 2 | **B♭m6** | 2nd finger 6th fret low E; index 5th fret D; 3rd finger 6th fret G; pinky 6th fret B | A section |
| 3 | **E♭m9/B♭** | shape 2 with the index dropped **one fret** | A section |
| 4 | **A6** | 2nd finger 5th fret low E; index 4th fret D; pinky 6th fret G; ring 5th fret B | B section opening |
| 5 | **dominant 7th** | index 3rd fret low E; 2nd 3rd fret D; 3rd 3rd fret B; pinky 4th fret G | three occurrences |
| 6 | **m7, 5th-string root** | bar at 5th fret; 3rd finger 7th fret D; middle 6th fret B | e.g. Dm7 |
| 7 | **7♭5** | 2nd finger 6th fret (B♭); 3rd 6th fret D; pinky 7th fret G; index 5th fret B | hardest of the seven |

**Shapes 2 and 3 differ by one finger moving one fret** — that's the derivative-fingering logic [[A Modern Method for Guitar - Enrichment|Leavitt]] teaches from p. 67, arriving from a completely different direction. Worth noticing: it's the same pedagogical idea, that new shapes should be one small change from a known one.

## The right hand

**Pluck only the notes you're fretting** — no strumming, and no open strings ringing. Thumb plays the bass as one unit; the three fingers play the upper chord as another.

The basic pattern: **chord · chord · thumb · chord · chord · chord · thumb · chord**, with the first stroke being thumb-and-chord together.

Beato notes the record makes it *"slightly more complex as the song goes on"* and that the real skill is improvising across two or three pattern variations rather than locking to one. That's the actual bossa nova technique — the chords are the easy half.

## The bridge, as motion

He describes the B section as movement rather than as a chord list, which is the more useful way to hold it:

`A6 → down a step to G7 → up a fifth to Dm7 → down to B♭7 → across to E♭m7 → to B7 → F♯m7 → down a fifth to B♭7♭5 → E♭m7 → A♭7♭5`

Mostly **root motion by fifths and steps** — the same machinery as [[The ii-V-I]] and the [[Rhythm Changes]] bridge, in a different idiom.

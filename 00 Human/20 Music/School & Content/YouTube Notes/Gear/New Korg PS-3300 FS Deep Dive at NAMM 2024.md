# New Korg PS-3300 FS Deep Dive at NAMM 2024

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2knVPwKEu14)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2knVPwKEu14)

## Synopsis

A NAMM 2024 walkthrough (with Korg's Luke) of the reissued Korg PS-3300 FS — a recreation of a legendary late-1970s fully-polyphonic analog synth (fewer than 50 originals ever built). Genuinely useful as a compact primer on semi-modular patching concepts, alongside standard gear-demo history.

## What's taught

- **Fully polyphonic via three complete, independently-mixable voice sections per note**: each of three identical "modules" carries its own oscillator, filter, and level control, freely blendable against each other or switched on/off individually — every key played gets its own full 3-oscillator/3-filter stack, unlike most vintage polysynths, which is the instrument's core rarity/expense driver.
- **A dedicated extra AR (attack/release only, no decay/sustain stage) envelope WITH a delay stage before it starts**, patchable to any of several destinations — used specifically for effects that shouldn't start the instant a key is pressed: a delayed vibrato (the LFO doesn't kick in until partway through a held note) or a delayed filter opening (a pad that stays dull for a beat before blooming) are both cited as go-to uses. A second, general-purpose envelope beyond the main ADSR is genuinely useful whenever a modulation needs its OWN timing independent of the primary amplitude/filter envelope.
- **Sample & hold can sample a source other than noise, changing its output character entirely**: sampling noise (the typical usage) gives fully random stepped voltages; sampling a slow sine-wave LFO instead gives a smoothly-trending, non-random stepped output that follows the sine's rise/fall shape at the sample rate — useful whenever a "stepped" modulation feel is wanted without full randomness.
- **A voltage processor (attenuator) as a general utility for scaling ANY patched control voltage** — turning a source up or down can make a filter brighter, pitch rise, or level increase, depending purely on what it's patched into; framed as a basic but essential modular-workflow building block for scaling modulation depth after the fact rather than re-patching.
- **Modern additions to a vintage-format reissue**: MIDI + USB (absent on the original), onboard patch memory plus companion "librarian" software for backing up and sharing patches, and shareable microtonal temperament/tuning scales.
- **Tuning a multi-oscillator analog patch by ear, module by module**: demonstrated live — tune module 1's pitch/filter as the reference, bring in module 2 and use its chorus/fine-tune controls against module 1 (audible beating while tuning), then repeat for module 3 — the same sequential reference-oscillator tuning discipline documented elsewhere in this vault, applied here to a 3-voice-per-note instrument rather than a 2-3 oscillator monosynth.

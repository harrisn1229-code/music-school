# Miss Ann's Tempo — Grant Green

> A swing blues in B♭. Jack Ruch transcribes the opening chorus of Green's solo, phrase by phrase, as concrete jazz-blues vocabulary — see [[How Grant Green Played Jazz Blues]] for the source video and the broader routing. For the underlying 12-bar jazz-blues form and its 6-2-5-1 turnaround, see [[Blues Form and Harmony]] and [[The Key To Understanding A Jazz Blues]], which analyzes the identical turnaround in the abstract. A second Jack Ruch video, [[Transcribing Licks And Making Them Your Own]], transcribes the tune's opening five bars with a different framing — a 2-5-1-of-the-IV cadence — and demonstrates moving that exact line to fit the tune's other 2-5-1 (inside the main 6-2-5-1 turnaround).

## Chords

**Key:** B♭ · **Form:** 12-bar jazz blues, swing feel (from *Grant's First Stand*, 1961). Bars 1–4: **B♭7 (I)**. Bars 5–8: **E♭7 (IV)** back to **B♭7 (I)**. Bars 9–12: the jazz-blues turnaround — **G7 – Cm7 – F7 – B♭7** → **VI7 – ii7 – V7 – I** (the "6-2-5-1"). → [[Blues Form and Harmony]], [[Turnarounds]] § *VI7*

A second reading of bars 1–5 (from [[Transcribing Licks And Making Them Your Own]]) frames the approach into the IV chord as its own **ii–V–I of E♭**: **Fm7 – B♭7 – E♭**, resolving to the local tonic before the form's own IV arrives — the same **ii–V–I** shape that reappears at the turnaround's **Cm7 – F7 – B♭7**. → [[The ii-V-I]]

## Solo breakdown (Jack Ruch's transcription, opening chorus)

- **Bars 1–4 (I, B♭):** opens with a slide-based lick out of a chord-shape fingering — **minor 3rd up to major 3rd** (D♭→D), then **5th up to 6th up to root** (F→G→B♭) — then descends into B♭ minor pentatonic/blues-scale territory: **♭7 down to the 5th** (A♭→F), a slide from the **4th up to the ♭5** (E♭→E), back down to the **♭7**. → [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- **Bars 5–8 (IV, E♭):** the I→IV chromatic pivot — the **major 3rd of B♭ (D) drops a half step to D♭**, which is the plain scale tone over I but becomes the **♭7 of E♭7** the instant the harmony changes. → [[Chord-Tone Soloing]]
- **Bars 9–12 (6-2-5-1 turnaround, G7–Cm7–F7–B♭7):** lands the VI chord (G7) on nothing more than its **root, G**, then plays a signature line targeting the **♭9 of G7 (A♭)**, dense with enclosures and chromatic pedal tones — the same altered/bebop device [[The Key To Understanding A Jazz Blues]] extracted from Green's style in the abstract. Followed by a related "common Grant Green / bebop phrase" bridging back from the IV-chord material into the G7 line, taught slow then at tempo. → [[Turnarounds]] § *VI7*, [[Bebop Vocabulary]] § *Target the guide tones*
- **[[Transcribing Licks And Making Them Your Own]]'s reading of bars 1–5:** frames the same opening material as **B♭ major triad → G minor triad (with chromatic passing notes) → F minor 7 → B♭7 → resolving to the 3rd of E♭** — an explicit **2-5-1 of the IV chord** (F minor 7 = ii of E♭, B♭7 = V of E♭, E♭ = local I). Stated method behind this reading: "always learn the chord progression first — that gives you context for what's happening in the solo." Ruch then **reuses this exact line over the tune's other 2-5-1** — the C minor 7 → F7 → B♭7 inside the main 6-2-5-1 turnaround — adjusting the phrasing to fit the new chord tones. A concrete demonstration that a 2-5-1 lick learned in one spot in a tune is directly reusable at any other 2-5-1 in the same key. → [[The ii-V-I]], [[Chord Substitution]]

## Steal this

- **The I→IV half-step pivot** (major 3rd of I drops to become the ♭7 of IV) — cheap, guitar-idiomatic reharmonization-by-reinterpretation of a single held note, no scale change required.
- **Land the VI chord on the plain root** before spending your vocabulary on the denser 2-5-1 that follows — not every chord needs decoration, especially right before one that will get plenty.
- **Target the ♭9 over a VI7/secondary-dominant** for the altered bebop color, confirmed here as genuine Green vocabulary (not just a Ruch teaching example) since it recurs almost identically in [[🎵 GRANT GREEN - Number One Green Street]].
- **Learn a 2-5-1 line once, then reuse it at every other 2-5-1 in the same key** — the opening bars' F minor 7 - B♭7 - E♭ line reappears, adjusted, over the turnaround's own C minor 7 - F7 - B♭7 later in the same chorus. → [[The ii-V-I]]

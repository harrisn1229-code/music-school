# Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite" [Music Production Quick Tips]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)

## Synopsis
Habstrakt opens his own Ableton session for "Infinite" and walks through it element by element rather than teaching a fixed method. The kick is one old sample he retunes and resamples per project instead of hunting new ones; the clap stacks three near-identical layers plus a white-noise layer from Operator's default patch; hats come from Splice loops nudged around rather than programmed from scratch. He leans on breath/whisper sounds pulled from a cappella leftovers for pre-hit tension, builds the sub from a resampled duplicate with baked-in reverb and hard sidechaining, and stresses that background "ear candy" elements are what separates a track from feeling empty. The main bass patch is Operator FM built to behave like a Serum-style detuned saw, and nearly everything in the project — not just drums — feeds one MIDI-triggered sidechain bus that he treats as its own sound-design element (bitcrushed, filtered). He also covers vocal chopping/pitching for mood, why he stacks new EQ instances instead of reopening old ones, a stray high-frequency cleanup EQ after the master limiter/compressor, Ableton's newer Morph filter, and his mixdown/mastering handoff: he limits for reference loudness but hands the actual mastering to someone else and doesn't reopen the project after. He closes with a practice tip — setting a timer and finishing a full beat every 30 minutes to train speed and decisiveness.

## Techniques
- **Retune and resample the same kick sample per track instead of sourcing new ones** — keeps one old kick drum, tunes it differently each time, drops it into Sampler and resamples the result; if it reads too low or high he just drags in another pass and reshapes the amplitude/pitch envelope rather than auditioning new samples. → [[Sampler]], [[Kick Design]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Linear-phase EQ on the kick group for precision moves** — groups kick and snare/clap together and reaches for a linear-phase EQ specifically when he wants to be more precise/surgical rather than his usual chained EQs. → [[EQ Eight]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Snare/clap kept extremely short and dry** — barely uses reverb on the snare, cutting the tail hard in the clip rather than shaping it with a release/decay device.
- **Clap built from 3 near-identical layers plus an Operator white-noise layer** — three layers of essentially the same clap sample run through Waves S1 Stereo widening, plus a layer built from Operator's default patch with the oscillator waveform set to Noise > White for extra flexible texture. → [[Operator]], [[S1 Imager]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Splice loops for hats instead of programming from scratch** — pulls hi-hat loops from Splice and repitches/nudges them rather than building patterns manually; keeps VST usage minimal overall, mostly stock Ableton devices and EQ. → [[Splice Sounds]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Breath/whisper texture pulled from a cappella leftovers, placed right before the first hit** — takes whisper/breathing sounds from between-sentence leftovers of a cappellas, runs them through two or three stages of Overdrive plus a touch of reverb, and places the result immediately before the song's first kick hit to add anticipation. → [[Overdrive]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Sub built from a resampled duplicate with baked-in reverb, then hard sidechained to itself** — the main sub layer gets resampled, then a duplicate of that resample is drenched in reverb and re-resampled so the reverb is printed into the audio, EQ'd down to just a filtered band, and hard sidechained against the dry layer purely to create a stereo-pumping effect.
- **Background "ear candy" is treated as essential, not decoration** — calls out that missing (or overdone) background elements is a common gap in other people's mixes; keeps a dedicated layer of small rhythmical/ambient details built from simple Operator patches chopped into pieces to fill that space. → [[Operator]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Operator FM bass built to behave like a Serum-style detuned saw** — sets one oscillator to a saw wave, copies it to the second oscillator (A to B), then detunes both simultaneously via the Fine knob to recreate the "massive" wide-detune character he associates with Serum; duplicates the patch and shifts one copy up an octave for a second layer. → [[Operator]], [[Bass Design]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Echo in ping-pong mode, very short time and low feedback, used purely for stereo width** — sets Echo's delay to ping-pong mode with a very short time and minimal feedback so it never audibly repeats, then blends in dry/wet just to widen the stereo image. → [[Echo]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Overdrive (scooped ~500 Hz) always paired with OTT** — a recurring combo across his synth/bass layers: Overdrive with the mids scooped around 500 Hz feeding into OTT for glue and grit. → [[Overdrive]], [[OTT]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **One MIDI-triggered sidechain bus that almost the whole project routes through** — nearly every element (not just bass) is sent to a single sidechain bus driven by a volume-shaper device that's triggered by a dedicated MIDI signal rather than an audio detector, giving him a duck shape fully independent of the kick's actual audio. → [[Sidechaining]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **The sidechain bus itself is sound-designed, not just a ducking utility** — bitcrushes and band-filters the shared sidechain bus so mostly the drums come through unaffected, turning the duck bus into its own textured, rhythmic element rather than a transparent utility.
- **Delay-freeze-autopan recipe for a quick ear-candy texture layer** — takes a random short sound, adds delay, freezes it, adds a touch of reverb, then auto-pans it for stereo movement before routing it into the shared sidechain bus. → [[Auto Pan]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Resample a synth part back to a clip, then use clip transpose for exaggerated pitch-bend FX** — bounces a synth performance to audio, then uses the clip's own transpose control (not a live pitch-bend) to create funky, exaggerated pitch-bend movement that wouldn't be achievable live.
- **Vocal layer tuned and vocoded for a robotic texture** — duplicates a vocal, tunes it to F# with Ableton's Tuner, and runs it through a vocoder for an extra robotic layer. → [[Tuner]], [[Vocoder]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Ableton's stock "Washout" rack for build-up risers** — uses the stock Ableton audio effect rack called Washout, which smears everything with reverb and delay, and automates its intensity through the buildup for a riser effect.
- **Two-stage limiting, then a dedicated mastering engineer — never reopens the project after handoff** — runs one limiter during the mix for reference-level loudness/punch and a second at the very end for extra punch, but deliberately does not master his own tracks; sends the mixdown to someone who masters for him specifically to avoid the headphone-switching, endless-tweaking rabbit hole, and once it's handed off he doesn't go back into that project. → [[Limiter]], [[Mix Handoff]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Chop and pitch a cappella vocals down to flip a happy vocal dark** — chops an a cappella into small pieces and pitches them down significantly, deliberately inverting a "happy"-sounding original vocal into something darker as a general creative approach.
- **Chains a new EQ instance for each later fix instead of reopening an earlier one** — when doing multiple mix passes over time, adds another EQ instance for each new correction rather than editing an already-set EQ, so earlier decisions on that band stay untouched; explicitly not claimed as "the right way," just his workflow. → [[EQ Eight]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **A final cleanup EQ after the master limiter/compressor to remove artifact clicks** — on the master chain, after the limiter and compressor, adds one more EQ pass specifically to shave stray high-frequency clicks that the limiting/compression itself can introduce, calling it a less-common but worthwhile extra step. → [[EQ Eight]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Ableton's Morph filter, automated, for full timbral sweeps rather than a simple cutoff move** — automates the Morph knob on Ableton's newer Morph filter device across a sound to sweep its whole character over time, not just a cutoff frequency. → [[Auto Filter]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **A gritty modular-style sine layer, tuned into key, plus stereo delay and chorus** — layers a plain sine wave through a gritty, funky-sounding distortion styled after a modular synth patch, tuned into the song's key, with stereo delay and chorus added for width. → [[Chorus-Ensemble]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Speed-training discipline: finish a full beat every 30 minutes** — when asked the fastest way to get good at producing, his answer is a self-imposed timer challenge: block out a session (e.g. 6:00–10:30), set a 30-minute timer, and finish a complete beat idea before it goes off, repeating through the block to train fast, decisive workflow.

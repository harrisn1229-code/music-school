## What's taught

- **Lydian's characteristic note, restated with derivation.** Lydian = the natural major/Ionian scale with the 4th degree raised a semitone (F→F♯ in C) — the one-note-change relationship already logged from [[Harmonic Minor Modes]] and other batch entries, here applied to the major-mode family.
- **Lydian pentatonic (restated, see [[Modal Pentatonic Shapes and the Six-Level Framework]]'s already-logged inverse-direction technique).** Add the raised 4th to the standard major pentatonic scale (C D E G A → C D E F♯ G A) to get a 6-note "Lydian pentatonic" hybrid — the characteristic dissonant color without learning the full 7-note Lydian scale shape.
- **New: fluidly switch between plain pentatonic and the Lydian-hybrid version WITHIN one solo, staying in the same 2-notes-per-string mindset throughout.** Rather than treating the hybrid as a separate scale to commit to for a whole phrase, dip in and out of the added ♯4 note mid-solo — play standard major pentatonic, briefly introduce the Lydian color, return to plain pentatonic — all without switching physical picking approach (still 2-notes-per-string, not jumping to a 3nps mode shape). Framed as the practical payoff of knowing a mode's ONE characteristic note: it becomes a color you can sprinkle in and out of familiar vocabulary in real time, not a separate scale you have to fully commit to.

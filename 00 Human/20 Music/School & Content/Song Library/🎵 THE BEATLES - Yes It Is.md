# Yes It Is — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (2 bars) · Verse (**15 bars** the first time — two 4-bar phrases, a 6-bar closing phrase, then one bar of breathing space) · Verse · Bridge (10, split 4 + 6) · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Outro with a complete ending. Verses 2 and 3 extend to 16 by repeating the closing hook, and **that extra sustained tonic is exactly what makes the pivot into the bridge possible**.

The mature rewrite of "This Boy": same three-part-harmony ballad genre, richer palette, a real if short-lived modulation. **♭VII appears twice inside one verse and behaves differently each time** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]) — first as a surprise substitute for the ii that filled the same slot in the previous phrase, then, considerably stranger, resolving to **vi**. That late swerve to the relative minor is a [[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]] in a song otherwise plainly major, and it leaves the home key faintly ambiguous.

The sustained tonic ending verses 2 and 3 puns as **I at home and V in the bridge's key**, which buys a genuine modulation with no setup bar ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]) — though it is not consummated until the bridge's third bar.

Free dissonances are everywhere, and they resolve **late, after the root beneath has already changed** ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]) — several never resolve at all. The best instance is a note that wants to rise across a bar line and never gets the chance: the voices drop out, leaving the resolution implied rather than sung.

# Learn These 4 Outside Blues Licks!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JTjQ0vr7JFY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JTjQ0vr7JFY)

## Synopsis

Jack Ruch teaches four licks for getting an "outside" sound over a **minor blues / static minor-chord vamp**, worked entirely over a **G minor one-chord vamp**. The core idea: over Gm, borrow the sound of its **own V7 chord (D7)** — specifically a **D7 altered** sound — by superimposing the **D altered scale** (D melodic minor's 7th mode), the **D half-whole diminished scale**, or (in lick 4) the **G whole-tone scale**, then resolving back into plain **G minor pentatonic/blues** vocabulary. This is the same "imply V7–i over a static minor chord" principle documented on [[Tonic Minor Soloing]], but approached here through **full alternate-scale superimposition and memorized licks** rather than single guide-tone devices. Licks 1–2 are Jack Ruch's own; licks 3–4 are credited directly to **Scott Henderson**. Ruch's explicit teaching philosophy, stated at the end: learn licks first to get the sound in your ear, rather than starting from scale theory — "the best way to do it is to start with lines, listen to great players, figure out what they're doing." He names **Robben Ford, Scott Henderson, and John Scofield** as reference players for this outside-over-minor vocabulary. Full tab/notation is gated behind his Patreon — no tab data in the transcript, only spoken note names/intervals, so all fingering below is reconstructed from those verbal degree names rather than transcribed dots.

## What's taught

- **Concept setup — three scale choices for the D7alt sound over Gm.** Over a G minor one-chord vamp, imply the D7 (V7 of Gm) as **D7 altered**. Three usable scale sources named for that sound: the **D altered scale**, the **D half-whole diminished scale**, and the **D whole-tone scale** — Ruch notes there are "a few scales you can use to get this sound" but deliberately skips scale-theory drilling in favor of teaching licks directly, "because that, in my opinion, is the best way to get these sounds into your head." → [[Altered Chords]], [[Scale Modes Reference]], [[Diminished Chords]]

- **Lick 1 — pentatonic setup → D-altered descent → B♭maj7 arpeggio tag, low position.** Structure: (1) open with straight **G minor pentatonic** bluesy phrasing to establish the tonality; (2) shift into the **D altered scale**: play **♯9 up to the major 3rd** of D (an octave leap/skip), back down to the **♯9** — described as landing "this really nice dissonant interval" between the major 3rd and ♯9; (3) descend the D altered scale from there, **deliberately skipping the ♯4/♭5 degree** of the scale on the way down; (4) resolve back into **G minor pentatonic**; (5) tag the phrase by running **up a B♭ major 7 arpeggio** — Ruch calls this "inside" harmony, not outside (B♭maj7 is the relative-major chord tone set of Gm, giving a **G minor 9** color) but flags it as "a really cool thing you can do over a G minor," a nice-sounding addition rather than a tension device. → [[Diagonal Pentatonic Blues Soloing]], [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]

- **Lick 2 — same shape, moved up to 10th position, resolving onto a chord tone instead of a tag.** Same overall arc as lick 1 (G minor pentatonic setup → D altered outside phrase → back to pentatonic) but played **around the 10th fret**, in a different fingering than lick 1 despite the same underlying scale choice — demonstrating the phrase is a **movable, relocatable idea**, not a fixed-position lick. Degree path through the D altered scale: **♯9 → major 3rd → back to ♯9 → ♭9 → root → ♭7 → ♭6**, again **skipping the ♯4** on the way down. The line then lands on the **5th of the G minor chord** (a chord tone) before dropping back into G minor pentatonic — Ruch frames this explicitly as "taking it out a little bit and then bringing it back home by landing on a chord tone." → [[Guitar Positions]]

- **Lick 3 (Scott Henderson) — an A-minor-pentatonic-shaped opening that's really D7/D-Mixolydian, plus a bebop enclosure, then the signature ♭9→♭7→root outside move.** Starts by playing what visually looks like the **top of an A minor pentatonic box**, but Ruch reframes it as coming from a **D7 bar-chord shape** — i.e. functionally D7/D Mixolydian material (flat-7 down to the 5th), even though the fingering pattern resembles A minor pentatonic. From there: a **bebop enclosure** targeting the **3rd of the D7 chord** — approach from **above the target, then two chromatic notes below it, then slide up into the 3rd**. This full opening section (box shape + enclosure) is stated to work over a **straight D7 or D Mixolydian** context on its own. The "outside" hook comes next: **♭9 → ♭7 → root** (in the key of D) — the ♭9-down-to-♭7 move Ruch calls "a common sound," found in both the **half-whole diminished scale** and the **altered scale**, and names it "kind of a signature thing of these outside sounds." Resolves back into G minor pentatonic to close. → [[Bebop Vocabulary]], [[Downbeat and Upbeat Enclosures]], [[Enclosures Through the Scale]], [[Diminished Chords]]

- **Lick 4 (Scott Henderson) — the most outside of the four: D-altered opening, then a G whole-tone tag, bookended by Gm chord tones.** Starts on the **root of D**, moves up to the **♭9**, then to the **♯4/♭5** — all straight out of the D altered scale. Then shifts into **G whole-tone scale** territory for the tag — Ruch calls this "a really wild sound to play over a G minor7 or G minor chord because it's got the major third in it," i.e. the whole-tone scale's major 3rd directly clashes against/colors the minor tonic, pushing "outside of the normal harmony" harder than licks 1–3. **Bookending discipline stated directly**: the line starts on a **chord tone of Gm (the 5th)** and ends on a **chord tone of Gm (the root)** — the outside content is framed as "gets from point A to point B in a really interesting way" between two anchored chord tones, not as free-floating tension. Resolves back into G minor blues/pentatonic phrasing to close. → [[Scale Modes Reference]] (whole tone)

- **Stated teaching philosophy (closing).** Ruch's explicit pedagogical point: "the whole idea here really is to just get a few cool licks under your fingers and really get your ear working to start picking up some new sounds, because none of this stuff really works unless you can hear melodically how it fits into place." He describes his own struggle turning memorized scale patterns into usable music, and states the fix: **"start with lines, listen to great players, figure out what they're doing"** — licks-first, theory-second. Reference listening named directly: **Robben Ford, Scott Henderson, John Scoffield [Scofield]** — "guys that really use this kind of outside stuff to great effect." → [[Solo Analysis and Concept Extraction]]

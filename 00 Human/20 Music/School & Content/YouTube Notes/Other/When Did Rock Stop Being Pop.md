# When Did Rock Stop Being Pop?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yAm1UWQSriI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yAm1UWQSriI)

## Synopsis

A data-driven pop-culture essay, not a technique video: David Bennett spreadsheets the UK top-10/US top-5 best-selling albums from 1960–2017, marking each "rock" (defined narrowly as 4+ tracks with prominent distorted electric guitar) or not, then plots the yearly rock percentage. The graph shows a 60s/70s peak, an 80s synth-driven dip, a 90s Britpop/Nirvana bump, and a steady decline after 2000 (Eminem's mainstream breakout, then the "Coldplay effect" of rock bands swapping electric for acoustic guitars) until 2010, after which no new mainstream rock breaks the charts. He closes with a genre-cycle parallel: jazz was pop music until rock displaced it in the 1950s-70s, then thrived as its own niche, freed to get more adventurous (Coltrane, Miles Davis) — the implication being rock may be heading the same way rather than dying outright.

## Techniques

None in the technical sense — this is chart-history analysis, not a how-to. The two claims with any transferable substance: (1) synths took over guitars' traditional rhythm/lead roles in mainstream pop specifically in the 1980s (Wham, Frankie Goes to Hollywood, Culture Club), and (2) the "Coldplay effect" — acoustic guitar + softer dynamics reading as less "rock" than the same chord/song structure on distorted electric — both already-known trends elsewhere in the vault rather than new information.

## What's taught

- **Evertune bridge — what it does and how it's used.** A mechanical bridge using an individual lever-and-spring mechanism per string that, once correctly set up, keeps the guitar in tune indefinitely regardless of playing intensity or low tuning. Addresses a specific named problem: low, extended-range tunings (this guitar tuned to A standard) drift out of tune notably faster and more audibly on very low strings. Common misconception corrected: an Evertune does NOT prevent string bending/vibrato by default — full string-locking is one selectable mode, not the only one; normal expressive playing remains possible. Requires a specialized workshop retrofit (not a DIY swap) — Caesar Handmade Guitars performed this install.
- **Fishman Fluence Modern pickups — tonal character.** Described as delivering a clearer, more defined, less muddy high-gain/distorted tone than the stock pickups, with the practical mixing payoff of cutting through a mix with less post-EQ correction needed. Each pickup offers two selectable voicings (a brighter/modern voicing and an alternate voicing) — voicing-to-playing-style matching was still being worked out at the time of the video, not a settled recommendation.
- **A/B comparison method used:** both takes recorded through the same amp-sim preset (Neural DSP Archetype: Plini, stock preset, no extra tweaking) specifically to isolate the guitar/pickup/bridge change as the only variable.
- **Gear philosophy, stated directly:** prefer upgrading and using a smaller number of already-owned high-quality instruments more often, over continuously buying new guitars — an underused instrument is a better upgrade candidate than a new purchase.

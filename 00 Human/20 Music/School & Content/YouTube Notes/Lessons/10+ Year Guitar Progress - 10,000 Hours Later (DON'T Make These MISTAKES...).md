# 10+ Year Guitar Progress - 10,000 Hours Later (DON'T Make These MISTAKES...)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6seQwWSxbhg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6seQwWSxbhg)

## Synopsis

A retrospective comparing old (2007) and current footage, breaking down the three biggest early mistakes that slowed real progress: a self-taught "inverted" picking angle adopted to mask a gear problem, a thumb-over fretting grip that blocked stretching, and — named as the single biggest regret — never studying music theory alongside technique.

## Techniques

1. **The "banana grip" — an inverted picking angle adopted to fix the wrong problem.** Early scratching/scraping pick noise was actually caused by cheap picks with rough, hard edges, but was misdiagnosed as a technique issue — an inverted picking angle happened to reduce the noise and got adopted as a personal style instead of just replacing the picks. The inverted angle also caused loose, splayed non-picking fingers that constantly brushed the guitar's knobs/pickup switch (visible physical damage cited: a sticker torn off and finish scratched from years of contact) and, more importantly, never developed a steady, reliable motion across tempos or across material the player hadn't personally written around that quirky technique. Fix required completely relearning picking-hand technique from scratch (closed hand, controlled motion, standard angle) — described as a painful, time-costly redo that a correct pick choice early on would have avoided entirely. Explicit caveat: some great players have genuinely unconventional techniques that work well for them — this is personal experience about what unlocked the most possibilities for this player, not a universal rule.
2. **Thumb-over-the-neck grip blocked stretching; thumb-behind-the-middle-finger unlocked it.** With the thumb hooked over the top of the neck, a fairly modest stretch (frets 5-7-9 on one string) was physically impossible past the 8th fret; moving the thumb to the area behind the middle finger removed that ceiling entirely and also gave meaningfully more snap/power on hammer-ons and pull-offs. Named as the direct cause of years of "flying pinky" (Fretting and Picking Hand Mistakes Checklist point 7/11 territory). **Explicit exception, not a blanket rule:** thumb-over is still the right choice specifically for expressive bends/wide vibrato, where the extra leverage and support genuinely help — the fix is knowing which grip serves which task, not abandoning thumb-over entirely.
3. **Never studying theory — named the single biggest regret, bigger than either technique issue.** Years of learning isolated licks/patterns and moving them chromatically up and down the neck without understanding what scale or key was actually being played — meaning a lick would sometimes land right by luck (e.g. A minor pentatonic over a song in A minor) and fail unpredictably as soon as it was transposed or the harmonic context changed. Fast, distorted playing could disguise the gap for a while, but it collapsed immediately under a real request (e.g. "solo over this in F♯ minor"). Stated takeaway: technique practice without any parallel theory study is a ceiling that speed and distortion can only hide, not remove.

# CHD-C07 — Sus removes, add adds — neither is an alteration

**Q.** What does a sus chord remove, what does an add chord add, and what single test tells an add chord from a sus chord?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Sus replaces the 3rd** — with the 2nd (sus2) or the 4th (sus4) — so the chord is neither major nor minor. **Add keeps the whole triad and adds one note with no 7th**: Cadd9 = C E G D, while C9 = C E G B♭ D. The test is **is the 3rd still there?** Present → the added note is the 9 (add9). Absent → it is the 2 (sus2).

## Why it matters

Neither device alters anything — nothing is sharpened or flattened — which is exactly what separates both from [[Altered Chords]]. Removing the 3rd costs the chord its quality, and that is the point: sus4 carries a pull and usually resolves, sus2 has a weaker pull and often just sits as an open colour. In practice only two get real use, **sus4 on major triads and 7sus4 on dominants**, and add9 is the pop and acoustic workhorse because it shimmers without the tension a 7th brings.

## Drill

Play Csus4 → C and hear the 4th fall to the 3rd. Then play Cadd9 and Csus2 back to back and name which note is missing. Finally hold a sus4 while adding the 3rd underneath it, and hear a resolution that keeps its residue.

# Transcribing Complex Music by Ear in Real-Time | Ep.03

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zC_wH5bW3Iw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zC_wH5bW3Iw)

## Synopsis
The third and shortest entry in Max Konyi's real-time transcription series, working through two unnamed tracks with jazzy suspended/dominant harmony, the Crosscode video-game soundtrack (composer Deniz Acay / Hagane), and an unnamed classical piece with dense inner voicings. Confirms the same toolkit as Ep.01/02 at speed, and is the clearest single demonstration in the whole batch of Lydian's ♯4 half-diminished chord and of deliberately choosing NOT to chase every inner voice on a first pass.

## Techniques
- **Baseline-first key ID, same no-perfect-pitch trick:** heard the tonic in the bass (first chord of the progression), sang it, played a random note, judged it as scale-degree 4 relative to the sung tonic, and back-solved the key from there — the same move opens every episode in the series.
- **A bass-line spelling out 2–5–1 is recognized as a shape before the chord qualities on top are even confirmed** — on the first unnamed track, the ii–V–i bass motion was caught first, then each chord's quality on top was worked out as a **suspended dominant** rather than the "default" minor 7 flat 5 a ii–V–i might imply — explicitly flagged as a case where the bass motion alone would have suggested one answer (standard ii–V–i qualities) but the actual chord qualities didn't match that default, so the ear corrected mid-analysis.
- **Crosscode soundtrack (Deniz Acay / Hagane) — the Lydian ♯4 half-diminished chord identified as a named, recurring "flavor" chord.** Within a 2–5–1–4 progression, the IV chord region produces a **♯4 half-diminished** sound — Konyi explicitly ties this to music theory: "if you think about the chords that come out of Lydian mode, the fourth chord of Lydian mode is a sharp-4 half-diminished" — and notes it "gets used all the time in various genres," worth training as its own standalone vocabulary item rather than deriving it from scratch every time.
- **A whole B-section can avoid the tonic chord entirely while staying "in the key"** — described on the Crosscode track: after the ♯4 half-diminished Lydian chord, the progression moves through several "modally related" chords and doesn't return to the tonic chord for an extended stretch, which Konyi notes is a recurring device across the entire Crosscode soundtrack (worth studying as a case study in departure-without-modulation).
- **"Hundreds and hundreds" of prior transcriptions is what makes chord *function* itself instantly recognizable** — on the unnamed classical piece, Konyi states plainly that recognizing "the fourness, the twoness, the fiveness" of a chord — its functional position in the key, independent of exact voicing — comes specifically from the sheer volume of prior transcription reps, the same way individual scale degrees become palpable through repetition.
- **Deliberately choosing NOT to resolve every inner voice on a first pass is a legitimate, stated strategy, not a shortfall.** On the dense classical piece: "he's got so much inner stuff going on, I'm not going to get all that stuff... for now I'm just content to get sort of a lead-sheet version, melody with the changes underneath" — explicitly frames a full multi-voice transcription as a separate, optional deeper pass rather than something a first listen owes you.
- **A secondary dominant of IV is recognized by ear via a specific added tone**, demonstrated on the classical piece: hearing a B♭ enter that "raises back to four" identifies a V/IV (the dominant of the IV chord) resolving into an inverted I — Konyi calls this a sound he's "heard a million times," i.e. a fully drilled vocabulary item triggering instant recognition rather than deduction.

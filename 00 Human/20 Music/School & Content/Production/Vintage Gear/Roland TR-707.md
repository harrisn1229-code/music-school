# Roland TR-707

## What it is
Roland's first fully sample-based TR box — the X0X sequencer everyone loved, now driving PCM snapshots of a real kit instead of analog circuits. Dismissed at launch as a plasticky budget compromise, it got scooped up cheap by the same Chicago crowd that rescued the 909, and its dry little kit ended up all over acid and early house.

## What makes the sound
- **Short, heavily companded low-rate PCM samples in ROM** — a real kit squeezed through mid-80s memory limits: crunchy, boxy, no tails. The cheapness *is* the character.
- **No tuning, no tone controls on the drums** (just level faders) — every 707 track uses the same kit, which is why it reads as a genre signifier the instant it enters.
- **The full TR sequencer** — 16-step X0X programming, shuffle, flams, and the big pattern-display LED grid (the best sequencing workflow of any TR to that point). The *patterns* people wrote on it matter as much as the sounds.
- Individual outs + MIDI/DIN sync made it a hub box — many acid records ran the 707 as both kit and clock for a [[Roland TB-303|303]].

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Impulse]] with 707 sample packs (the whole kit is ~15 one-shots); [[kHs Bitcrush]] + [[Saturator]] to push the companded crunch; [[RC-20 Retro Color]] for era glue.
- **Paid:** Roland Cloud TR-707 (official), UVI Beat Box Anthology 2, Samples From Mars.
- **Free:** 707 kits are in every free vintage-drum archive.

## Used on
- Phuture — "Acid Tracks" (1987): the 707 drives the acid-house founding document alongside the TB-303.
- Genre/era: Chicago acid house and early house 1986–90 ([[House - EDM]], [[EDM Subgenres]] acid card), Italo/Euro productions of the same window.

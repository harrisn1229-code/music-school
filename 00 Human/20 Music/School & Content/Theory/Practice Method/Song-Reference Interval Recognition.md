# Song-Reference Interval Recognition

A song-anchor method for identifying intervals by ear: memorize one famous melody per interval (its opening two notes ARE that interval), ascending and descending separately, then compare an unknown interval against the anchor set until one matches. Instrument-independent — pure listening/singing, no fretboard or keyboard needed — which is why it sits in `Theory/Practice Method/` rather than under Guitar/Piano/Bass.

## The anchor set

| Interval | Ascending | Descending |
|---|---|---|
| Minor 2nd | *Jaws* theme | "I Am the Walrus" (Beatles) |
| Major 2nd | "Ferrari" (opening) | "Mamma Mia" chorus (ABBA) |
| Minor 3rd | "Mad World" | "Come Together" bass line close (Beatles) |
| Major 3rd | "Sir Duke" (Stevie Wonder) | "The National Anthem" bass line (Radiohead) |
| Perfect 4th | "Summer Nights" bass line | "Under Pressure"/"Ice Ice Baby" bass riff |
| Tritone | "YYZ" intro vamp (Rush) | "Black Sabbath" bass line (Black Sabbath) |
| Perfect 5th | Star Wars main theme | "My Girl" bass line (The Temptations) |
| Minor 6th | "Baker Street" sax line | "One of Us" (Joan Osborne) |
| Major 6th | "The Holly and the Ivy" | "No Surprises" opening riff (Radiohead) |
| Minor 7th | "Somewhere" (*West Side Story*) | "Watermelon Man" trumpet (Herbie Hancock) |
| Major 7th | "Take On Me" chorus leap | "Have Yourself a Merry Little Christmas" closing line |
| Octave | "Somewhere Over the Rainbow" | "Bulls on Parade" guitar riff (RATM) |
| Minor 9th *(bonus, beyond the octave)* | "Killing in the Name" bass line (RATM) | — |

## How to run it

- **Drill format**: play (or imagine) an interval, run through the anchor list until one matches by ear, name the interval. Do both directions — ascending and descending sets are genuinely different sounds for the same interval, not mirror images.
- **Sing, don't just recognize**: hum the anchor melody's first two notes before checking the answer — connects the sound to a physical/vocal memory, not just a label.
- **Weak anchors are still useful**: a couple of the descending examples (minor 6th, major 7th) are flagged by the source as imperfect — use them anyway until a better one is found; a weak-but-memorized anchor beats no anchor.
- No tempo target, no gear needed — a few minutes any time, ideally spread across days rather than crammed once.

# Songs that use 13/8 time

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C9NJeteDIys)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C9NJeteDIys)

## Synopsis

Extends the odd-meter series with 13/8 and 13/4 song examples, but the real payoff is a sharper, more general version of the x/8-vs-x/4 notation question already touched on for 11/8: WHY an x/8 meter feels less stable than the equivalent x/4 meter, stated as a precise mechanism rather than just "it's a tempo/notation choice."

## Techniques

- **The x/8-vs-x/4 instability mechanism, generalized and sharpened beyond the earlier 11/8-vs-11/4 tempo test.** Any odd meter with an EIGHT on the bottom (13/8, 11/8, 7/8...) will feel LESS stable/more chaotic than the equivalent meter with a FOUR on the bottom, because an odd top number over 8 always leaves the felt quarter-note pulse with one beat "sliced in half" at the end of the bar — 13/8 naturally settles into a felt quarter-note pulse of 6½ beats per bar, not a clean 7, because 13 eighth notes only make 6 full quarter-note beats plus one half-beat remainder. An odd meter with a FOUR on the bottom (13/4, 7/4...) has no such remainder — all of its beats are felt in FULL, which is why it reads as more grounded/"normal" despite having the identical odd beat-count. This is a more precise version of the earlier tempo-based 11/8-vs-11/4 test logged on [[Time Signatures & Note Values]] — same underlying phenomenon, explained here via the felt-remainder mechanism rather than just "which tempo makes you count which way."
- **13/8 grouping catalog**: 7+6 (Weapons Factory theme, Super Mario RPG; "Track Stars," Bobby McFerrin ft. Zap Mama); 3+3+3+4, a waltz pulse with the 4th group "dragged out" every fourth repetition ("Push Me Pull Me," Phish); a 12/8 shuffle with one extra 8th note tacked on ("Lifeboy," Phish, both Phish songs from the same album); further examples not analyzed in the same depth: "To Negate" (Tigran Hamasyan); the B section of "Schism" (Tool, whose main 6/4 section is itself subdivisible as alternating 5/8+7/8 — an odd-meter-within-odd-meter song).
- **13/4 grouping catalog**: 4+4+5 (the chorus of "Yoga," Björk, alternatively readable as two bars of 4/4 plus one bar of 5/4 — the composite-meter framing from [[Songs that use 10-4 time]]); 7+6 ("The Becoming," Nine Inch Nails — same 7+6 split as the 13/8 Mario RPG example above, just felt as quarter notes instead of eighth notes, a direct demonstration of the x/8-vs-x/4 stability difference on IDENTICAL beat-grouping math).
- **13/16 exists too, but conventionally only as a single one-off bar mixed into another meter, not as a whole-song time signature** — the /16 denominator is reserved for brief insertions rather than sustained use.

# Thank You Girl — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4, and it returns intact as a full section before the final verse) · Verse/Refrain · Verse/Refrain · Bridge/Refrain · **Intro** · Verse/Refrain · Outro (6+4+4, the first phrase quoting the intro verbatim). Verse 8, refrain 4, bridge 8; lyrics across the three verses run ABA.

Three chords for better than 90% of its length, and the twists are not in the chord chart. The intro is `V – IV – V – IV → I` — **four bars that never state the tonic**, so the key is genuinely open and could as easily be read a fifth up until bar 5 confirms it. The refrain is four bars that never resolve, which is exactly why it has to follow a bridge that closes firmly on I. The vi and ii of that bridge are the only two chords outside I/IV/V in the entire song.

The real device is **harmonic rhythm**: one chord per bar in intro and refrain, two per bar in the verse. The verse feels faster and more driven without the tempo moving an inch ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]).

The vocal method is the payoff. Sing a phrase in unison, then on its repeat **keep that exact line as the lower voice and write a new upper part against it**. What comes out is fourths, fifths and the occasional sixth rather than thirds — dissonant on paper, motivated in the ear because the bottom line is already familiar. Three texture changes happen inside the 4-bar intro alone, including offbeat neighbour-tone embellishments ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]).

# The 6 WORST Guitar Solo MISTAKES! (Fix These Bad Habits TODAY)

## Synopsis

BERNTH plays six "bad" solo takes over a simple E minor backing track, then shows the fix for each, all before/after pairs over the same track so the difference is directly audible. Several mistakes corroborate the vault's existing [[10 Hidden Soloing Mistakes and Constraint-Based Fixes]] list (root-fixation, no silence) with a second independent source and a concrete worked example each; two (motif-based scale-run fix, non-root harmonic centering) add specifics that list doesn't spell out.

## What's taught

- **Mistake 1 — playing a scale straight up/down; fix — build from a small motive instead.** A short, catchy fragment (a mini-melody, scale pattern, or arpeggio snippet) repeated and rhythmically varied across a few measures reads as far more memorable/melodic than a scale run, even when the fragment itself is simple — demonstrated by taking a small scale fragment as a motive and altering its rhythm on the second pass.
- **Mistake 2 — always moving to the scale's adjacent note; fix — space notes out.** Constantly playing "the next note up or down" in a scale position is diagnosed as a habit, not a skill gap; deliberately increasing the interval distance between consecutive notes (skipping around the scale rather than stepping through it) immediately sounds more professional/interesting with no new theory required.
- **Mistake 3 — centering phrases on the root; fix — pick a more interesting harmonic center note.** The root is called "the least interesting note" to emphasize; demonstrated by re-centering ideas around F♯ (the 9th over the backing E power chord) instead of E — same backing track, dramatically more interesting result. Suggests avoiding both the root AND the perfect 5th as default centers.
- **Mistake 4 — staying in the same scale/vocabulary every time (framed as optional, "a matter of taste"); fix — explore more exotic scales.** Over a simple E–D power-chord backing (harmonically open enough for wide exploration), demonstrates a take built from harmonic minor and Phrygian dominant instead of natural minor/pentatonic — explicitly not a "wrong vs. right" mistake, just a suggestion for players who feel stuck in the same two scales.
- **Mistake 5 — monotone rhythm (all quarters, or all eighths, or all triplets); fix — syncopate.** Diagnoses a common blind spot: analyze your own solos for a default note-value habit (always quarters, always straight eighths, etc.), which reads as predictable even when the pitch content is fine. Fix demonstrated by reworking the same melodic idea with a syncopated, dotted-note rhythm so accents land off the beat instead of squarely on it — pitches unchanged, only the rhythm shifted.
- **Mistake 6 — constant note stream, no rests (his own "biggest guilty pleasure"); fix — add space/rests for dynamics.** Common shred-guitarist habit of trying to cram every idea into a solo slot; adding deliberate rests lets a solo "breathe," builds tension more effectively, and lets a later burst of fast playing land as a genuine climax rather than more of the same density.

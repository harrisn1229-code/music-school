# Sun King — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** a **bonsai medley inside the medley** — a small self-contained song (refrain, bridge, refrain′) wrapped in verse-like material on both sides that could not stand alone. Intro (4 bars, two identical 2-bar tiles) · Verse (the intro tile with a convergent tile bolted to the front, stated three times) · Refrain · Bridge · Refrain′ · final Verse, with a complete ending. John assembles the track from short phrases repeated and re-sequenced like mosaic tiles rather than writing anything through.

The key scheme is E → C → E, a **third relation rather than a fifth one** ([[POP-C11 — Chromatic mediants and pop modulation]]), which is exactly where the cross relations come from: four sharps against none. The move into the refrain has no pivot at all — two bars of G11, then an abrupt cut to C major with rhythmic activity stopped dead ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]). The chord looks like F stacked on G and functions unmistakably as V.

The refrain then targets a **V-of-V-of-V**, an odd destination in its own right ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]), whose resolution the bridge delays by inserting IV. Coming home, refrain′ lands on F and re-reads it as **♭II of E** — a Neapolitan pivot, the same gambit as "You're Going To Lose That Girl". Chords are heavily embellished, added sixths especially, which produces the lounge-jazz luxury and the unbothered affect at once.

The melody is secondary throughout: the lead line does not carry a section, the block "ah" vocals do.

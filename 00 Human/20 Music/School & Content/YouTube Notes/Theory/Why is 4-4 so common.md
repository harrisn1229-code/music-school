# Why is 4/4 so common?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FC6zLP97wWA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FC6zLP97wWA)

## Synopsis

A genuinely open question the presenter crowdsourced (largely from a Reddit thread), landing on "probably some of both" rather than a single tidy answer: is 4/4's dominance in pop music an innate human preference, or a purely cultural/learned one? Backed by the presenter's own sample of 450 pop songs across 70 years, finding 94% in 4/4.

## Techniques

- **The "innate" camp's arguments**: (1) **Bipedalism** — humans walk/run on a 2- or 4-beat cycle (left-right-left-right), and rhythmic entrainment (moving the body in time with music — a rare cross-species ability) may have evolved partly to improve running efficiency, since humans are unusually good long-distance runners (argued as the second-best in the animal kingdom, behind only huskies, via an endurance-hunting strategy of running prey to exhaustion). (2) **Breathing** creates a natural in-out 2-part (arguably 4/4-adjacent) cycle. (3) **Heartbeat**, "in sets of two" — flagged by the presenter as the weakest of the innate arguments, since a heartbeat's pulse doesn't exclusively map onto 4/4 over any other meter. (4) **Symmetry** — humans generally find symmetry aesthetically pleasing (cited: facial symmetry and attractiveness), and 4/4 divides evenly at every level (bar, phrase, section), unlike odd meters.
- **The "culturally learned" camp's arguments**: preference for 4/4 tracks what you grew up hearing, not an innate universal. Counter-evidence: many non-Western traditional musics (cited: Greece, Turkey, Bulgaria, and neighboring regions) routinely use meters a Westerner would call "odd" (7/8, 9/8, 5/8, 11/8) as everyday, comfortably-danced-to music — undercutting the idea that 4/4 is simply the natural human default.
- **A wrinkle in the cultural argument, addressed directly**: even non-Western pop music (as opposed to traditional/folk music) trends heavily toward 4/4 too — attributed to global pop music being a fundamentally Western-derived commercial industry (cited: Turkish pop originating in the 1950s as "light western music," literally Turkish-language covers of Western songs), so 4/4-heavy pop spreading worldwide reflects industry lineage more than universal listener preference.
- **A further wrinkle: 4/4-heavy pop doesn't even trace to Western folk tradition**, which is presented as a point against a simple "West = 4/4 culturally" story — European folk dance traditions actually lean on 3/4 and 2/4 rather than 4/4. The presenter's own resolution: modern pop's 4/4 dominance traces specifically to the **blues** lineage (blues → jazz → R&B → rock and roll → the bulk of punk/disco/EDM/country/hip-hop), which is "almost exclusively" 4/4 — framed as pop's real shared-ancestor genre, not European folk music.
- **Open-ended close**: the presenter notes 3/4 was comparatively popular in the 19th century, and speculates 4/4's current dominance might not be permanent.

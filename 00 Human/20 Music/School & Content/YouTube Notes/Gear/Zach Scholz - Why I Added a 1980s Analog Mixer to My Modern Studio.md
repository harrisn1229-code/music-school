# Zach Scholz - Why I Added a 1980s Analog Mixer to My Modern Studio

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B_0d-qmbOXs)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B_0d-qmbOXs)

## Synopsis
Zach Scholz explains why he added a 1982 TASCAM M-30 (introduced once as "TASCAM 30," then called "M30" throughout) — an 8-channel analog mixer — to his modern in-the-box studio, framing it not as a nostalgia piece but as a practical extension of his setup. He bought it mainly because it looked cool, but it ended up solving a real bottleneck: his interface (captioned "Clarit 2 Pre," likely a Focusrite Clarett 2Pre) only has two inputs, which forced constant cable swapping, gain riding, and "band-aid" gain-boost plugins that added noise. By panning every M30 channel hard left or hard right, he uses the mixer as an 8-into-2 extension of that interface, gain-stages entirely off the mixer's built-in preamps (interface gain knob set to zero), and prints EQ, saturation, and even a guitar-into-mic-input fuzz-bass trick onto the signal before it ever reaches the DAW; he later bought a second M30 specifically for drum tracking, using it to sum multiple mics down to a stereo pair for a simpler, more "committed" mix.

## Techniques
- **1982 TASCAM M-30 replaces multiple plugins and workflow steps** — bought mainly because it looked cool, but ended up fundamentally changing how he tracks instruments and records drums, and even gives him a distinctive fuzz-bass sound.
- **Core problem it solves: his interface only has two inputs** — his interface (captioned "Clarit 2 Pre," likely a Focusrite Clarett 2Pre) forced constant plugging/unplugging and gain riding; he was already running a separate 18i20 for drums, but says even that "would not have solved all of the issues" and "certainly wouldn't have looked as cool."
- **Mixer fundamentals recapped: sums multiple inputs to a stereo pair** — faders balance levels, panning places sounds across the stereo space, on-board EQ shapes tone on the way in, and channels can be sent to effects or bussed together.
- **Routing trick: every channel panned hard left or hard right to consolidate 8 channels down to his interface's 2 inputs** — a hard-panned channel resolves to a single output, so channels panned hard left feed interface input 1 and channels panned hard right feed input 2, turning the M30 into an extension of the interface rather than a separate box. → [[Stereo Field]]
- **Gain staging moved entirely onto the mixer** — because the M30 has built-in preamps, he sets the interface's own gain knob to zero and gain-stages purely off the mixer's channel faders/preamps.
- **Solves a real noise problem: the interface alone couldn't deliver enough gain** — his dynamic mics couldn't get enough gain from the interface, and even his condenser needed the interface gain maxed out plus another 10-20 dB pushed through a plugin, which added noise; he finds the M30 preamps sound better and boost the signal more, and says stacking the M30's preamp with the interface's gain gets him roughly double the gain.
- **Each channel has two rear inputs (line + mic) with a front-panel mic/line switch** — channels 7 and 8 are unique on this mixer for having quarter-inch mic-line inputs instead of the standard connector.
- **Fuzz-bass technique: plugging a guitar/bass into a mic input overdrives the preamp** — the mic input expects a low-level signal, so a guitar or bass's hotter pickup output creates analog fuzz; he says it's especially cool on bass because it retains all of the low end. → [[BASS Slot]]
- **Three EQ controls per channel: two semi-parametric bands plus one fixed high shelf** — semi-parametric means the frequency itself is adjustable; lets him add "top end" to a mic signal directly on the way in, before it ever reaches the interface. → [[AIR Slot]]
- **Pre/post switch bypasses a channel's EQ on demand** — flipping a channel to "pre" sends the signal through clean even with an EQ setting dialed in, so he doesn't have to zero out knobs when a setting he likes doesn't suit a particular instrument.
- **Values physically turning knobs and dials because it removes the temptation to overthink** — he says it helps him commit to a sound earlier in the process instead of endlessly second-guessing in the DAW.
- **Bought a second M30 specifically for drum tracking** — used to move tracking/mixing decisions out of the DAW: he can record EQ on the way in, and driving each channel strip hot adds natural saturation ("breakup") that helps glue the drum sound together, which he says is easy to do with drums since they run louder. → [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Consolidating multiple drum mics down to a stereo left/right pair before they hit the interface simplifies the mix** — he also notes the M30's preamps have a different character than his interface's preamps. → [[BED Slot]]
- **Also runs a turntable through the M30 purely to EQ records while listening** — an admittedly impractical but fun personal-listening use, separate from his tracking workflow.

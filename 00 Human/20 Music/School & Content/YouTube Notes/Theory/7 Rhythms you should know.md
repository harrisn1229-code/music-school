# 7 Rhythms you should know

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fyVFC9JI_Pw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fyVFC9JI_Pw)

## Synopsis

A follow-up to [[18 Rhythms you should know]] (this video assumes that video's tresillo/son clave/bossa nova/3:2 and 4:3 polyrhythm vocabulary): seven more named, recognizable rhythmic patterns with song examples, each identified by name so musicians can reference them in conversation.

## What's taught

- **Double tresillo** — a 3-3-3-3-2-2 grouping of the 16 eighth notes across two bars of 4/4 (doubling each number of the single-bar tresillo's 3-3-2). Works against 4/4's natural pulse hard enough to briefly suggest a 6/8-or-12/8 feel until the final 2-2 pulls it back into 4/4. Examples: *Heat* (unnamed artist cue), *We Found Love* (Rihanna).
- **Gallop rhythm** — one eighth note + two sixteenth notes, repeating continuously; nicknamed the "heavy metal gallop" for its predominant use in that genre.
- **Habanera rhythm** — associated with the Cuban habanera/contradanza dance; famous examples include *La Paloma* and the "Habanera" aria from Bizet's *Carmen*; also common in tango (Carlos Gardel's *Por una Cabeza*). Structurally, it's a tresillo played against two regular quarter notes — tresillo again functioning as the underlying rhythmic cell, same as in son clave/bossa nova. Also used in film scoring (Michael Giacchino's recent Batman theme). **Independently confirmed** in [[Tresillo - The Rhythm of 2010s Pop Music]]: habanera = tresillo + an added backbeat, the one change contradanza makes crossing from Cuba into Europe.
- **"Barbara Ann" rhythm** (named for the Regents/Beach Boys song) — starts as regular quarter notes, but the 4th beat is delayed by an eighth note, and that syncopation carries into the next bar before resolving — one "in time" bar followed by one "out of time" bar.
- **5-over-4 polyrhythm** — extending the previous video's 3-over-2 ("nice cup of tea") and 4-over-3 ("pass the goddamn butter") mnemonics: the composite rhythm for 5:4 is cued with the phrase "I'm looking for a home to buy." No song example was found by the presenter.
- **Turkish aksak (9/8, "limping" rhythm)** — 9 eighth notes grouped 2+2+3 (not evenly/isochronally distributed), felt as "quick-quick-slow" rather than counted 2-2-3 at speed. Famous use: Dave Brubeck's *Blue Rondo à la Turk* (title references the Turkish exposure that inspired it, from the *Time Out* album). Also found in Balkan folk music (e.g. the North Bulgarian dance Devetorno Horo).
- **Skank / ska upstroke** — a repeated emphasized offbeat, most commonly played as guitar upstrokes (also common on keys); defines ska's bouncy, light feel. Beatles example with ska influence: *Ob-La-Di, Ob-La-Da*.

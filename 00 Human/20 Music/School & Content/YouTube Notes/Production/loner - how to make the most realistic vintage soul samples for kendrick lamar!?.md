# how to make the most realistic vintage soul samples for kendrick lamar!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oMbeATqXOww)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oMbeATqXOww)

## Synopsis

loner builds a from-scratch "vintage soul sample" in the style of Kendrick Lamar's piano-heavy *Mr. Morale & the Big Steppers*-era production, working from the observation that a lot of the album leans on piano rather than the usual chopped-record loop. The build stacks a Cinesamples Blue acoustic piano chord bed (voiced with a spread/open-voicing trick and deliberately low velocity), a mallet one-shot layered on top using a stretch-independent-of-pitch trick, two synth layers (one pushed through a long delay + Decapitator, one through a short heavy reverb/delay/shaper chain), and — the video's real centerpiece — a three-layer vocal stack (center, hard right, hard left) sharing one base chain but each carrying a distinguishing variation, explicitly built to sound like a genuine vintage record rather than a filtered-and-buried sample vocal. The whole thing finishes by bouncing the piano+vocal portion into a slicer, pitching it down, boosting the attack, and nudging each note's timing back by a hair for "bounce," then contrasting that chopped first half against a second half where the natural (unchopped) sample plays with the synth layers reintroduced. The chord progression itself is a legitimate, reusable piece of theory — VI–III–v–VII in B natural minor (Gmaj9 → Dmaj9 → F♯m → Amaj7) — a common neo-soul/R&B move dressed up as "6-3-5-7." Auto-captions garble several plugin names badly (a recurring problem on this channel): "altar boy" reads almost certainly as **Little AlterBoy**, "vagina delay" as **Valhalla Delay**, and "louie and xyz impulse analog lab bank" as the same **Arturia Analog Lab "Impulse" bank** already resolved from a prior loner video in this vault.

## Techniques

**Piano / chords:**
- **Cinesamples Blue (Contact bank) as the go-to piano VST, chosen because a lot of Kendrick's newer material (this build is inspired by the piano-forward tracks on the *Mr. Morale* era) is piano-led rather than the usual chopped-loop starting point.** Called out explicitly as "probably my favorite piano vst." No owned equivalent tracked — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **VI–III–v–VII in B natural minor (Gmaj9 → Dmaj9 → F♯m → Amaj7), spelled on-screen as "6-3-5-7."** First chord: G major 9 (root low, then the rest of the chord — including the raised octave 7th and 9th — voiced up an octave higher, described as "helps to create such a different vibe... you can get much more emotion out of your chords" from re-spreading the same chord tones across a wider register). Second chord repeats the exact same major-9 shape a whole step down (D major 9) but is intentionally re-voiced slightly differently (bringing the F♯ up) purely for variety between two structurally identical chords. Third chord is a plain F♯ minor (root on the bottom). Chord four closes on A major 7. Root motion 6-3-5-7 in natural minor is a real, reusable neo-soul progression — see [[Chord Progressions]] and [[Chord Inversions]] (spread-voicing move) for the theory writeup.
- **Velocity dropped to near-zero on every note of the chord part, as a deliberate texture choice, not a mix mistake.** "Personally I just feel like the texture sounds better doing this... when the velocity is super high the notes can be a bit too harsh." A general lesson: try a whole chord part at very low velocity before reaching for EQ/compression to soften a harsh-sounding source.
- **The recurring "go-to piano chain" from this channel, confirmed a 4th time: Waves King's Microphones (vintage mic emulation) → a Waves EQ ("snare preset," chosen by ear while clicking through presets, brings out a lot of high end for a vintage character) → a second EQ (cuts some low end, boosts a little high end).** Same three-stage shape documented from an earlier loner video ([[Sound Selection Guide]]). No owned direct mic-emulation equivalent; closest owned coloration stage is [[UAD Century Tube Channel Strip]] or [[UAD Neve 1073]] ahead of [[EQ Eight]]. → [[EQ]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**Mallet layer:**
- **A one-shot from the unreleased "Layers Volume 2" sample pack, set to stretch mode so every note plays back the same LENGTH regardless of octave/pitch.** Copies the piano's chords, hitting in between the piano's own notes. "When you have a one-shot and you set it to stretch mode and then pitch it in a higher octave, I often find you get some really cool textures from that." Owned-lane equivalent: Ableton [[Simpler]] in a Complex/Complex Pro warp mode keeps one-shot length independent of pitch the same way. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Simpler]]

**Synth layer 1 (delay + drive):**
- **"Chiclets" preset from the "Louie and XYZ" bank inside the Impulse patch set (near-certainly Arturia Analog Lab, per a previous video's resolved research on this same channel — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]).** A textural counter-part, one chord copied and hit on the second bar. Too quiet/dry on its own, so processed with: a long **Valhalla Delay** ("vagina delay" in the raw auto-caption — a near-certain homophone garble, not a real plugin) set super long/dragged-out, then **Decapitator** — driver turned up, mix brought down a little, a bit of a low cut. Learned directly from Louis, who reportedly runs this on a lot of his synths. → [[ValhallaDelay]], [[Decapitator]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**Synth layer 2 (texture + shaper):**
- **"The Machinist" / "Dying" preset (garbled auto-caption, exact preset name unconfirmed) from the same Impulse bank, layered in as a simple counter-melody.** Sounded dry on its own, finished with: EQ (cut low, boost high), a very short but heavy reverb, a "fruity delay" (FL Studio's stock delay — owned-lane equivalent [[Delay]]/[[Echo]]), and a shaper box (owned-lane equivalent [[Shaper]]/[[Transient Shaper]]; a dedicated multiband shaper is still a wishlist gap, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]). → [[EQ]], [[Reverb]], [[Delay]]

**Vocals — the real focus of the video:**
- **Three vocal takes (not doubled the same take three times — three separately-written performances) placed center / hard right / hard left, each carrying a different melody and harmony, explicitly framed as the move that takes a sample "from good to great."** "This is a great way to make your vocals sound much more complex and much more interesting" — a stack, not a double. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **Base chain shared by all three takes: Auto-Tune → a Waves compressor ("really brings the vocal out, makes it stand out really well") → Little AlterBoy ("altar boy" in auto-captions — near-certain match, standard/neutral setting) → an EQ cutting low end and a little high end (lighter than his usual cut).** Deliberately processed to sound like a real song's vocal rather than the usual sample-vocal move of filtering it distant and low in the mix — "this time I wanted to make it sound like a real song, and obviously that means the vocal is going to be much more present." → [[Auto-Tune Pro]], [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Vocal Tuning]], [[Compression]]
- **Each of the three takes gets ONE distinguishing extra stage on top of the shared base chain, rather than three fully different chains.** Take 1 (center): Little AlterBoy's "vocal dub" preset. Take 2 (right): the exact same base chain, but Little AlterBoy's "vocal chorus" preset instead. Take 3 (left): the base chain plus **ValhallaVintageVerb** ("vintage verb") added — "just to slightly switch it up, give it a different sound." → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]

**Finishing/chop pass:**
- **Bounce the piano+vocal portion, load it into a slicer (Fruity Slicer, FL Studio's stock slicer — owned-lane equivalent [[Simpler]] in slice mode), pitch it down a few semitones, and raise the attack.** Framed explicitly as the single best way to make a from-scratch build read as "a really real sample" rather than a programmed one. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Simpler]]
- **Per-note micro-timing nudge inside the slicer: zoom all the way in and drag each note back very slightly — "you almost can't hear it but it just adds a little bit of bounce, a little bit of variety."** A clean, concrete demonstration of deliberate off-grid placement as a groove tool rather than an accident; direct real-world example for [[Pocket and Microtiming]]'s "laying back" concept. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]]
- **Two-half arrangement contrast: first 8 bars = the chopped/pitched sample with the synth layers removed (vocals + piano as the sole focal point); second half = the natural, unchopped sample playing normally WITH the synth layers reintroduced.** A deliberate structural device so each half of the beat "sounds completely different" and "feels different," rather than one texture running start to finish. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

# SYN-C08 — Why FM does bells

**Q.** Why is FM good at metallic and bell-like tones that subtractive struggles with?

> [!question]- Answer
> FM's sidebands land at positions set by the **carrier-to-modulator ratio**. **Integer ratios give harmonic sidebands** (pitched — basses, e-pianos). **Non-integer ratios give inharmonic sidebands** that fall between the harmonics, so the ear can't fuse them into one pitch — metallic and clangorous. **Bells and metal are physically inharmonic**, so FM matches them naturally.

## Why it matters

Subtractive can't get there: its oscillators are harmonic by construction and a filter can only attenuate existing partials — it can't relocate one to a non-integer position.

## Drill

On [[Operator]], one modulator into one carrier: sweep the index at ratio 1:1, then 1:2, then 1:1.41. Name the moment it turns metallic and say why.

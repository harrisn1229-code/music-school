# Tracking Drums - Joe Chiccarelli

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UKbLf_dvx9A)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UKbLf_dvx9A)

## Synopsis

A short Mix with the Masters tracking-stage session with Joe Chiccarelli (My Morning Jacket, The White Stripes, Beck), working with drummer Joey and a live rock kit. The whole video is a real-time diagnose-and-fix pass: Chiccarelli listens to the current mic setup, identifies small problems (not enough hi-hat clarity, unbalanced toms, kick lacking bottom, room mics too narrow), and fixes nearly all of them by physically repositioning mics rather than reaching for EQ — a live worked example of the "fix mic problems by moving the mic" discipline already documented on this vault's [[Drum Mic Technique]]. Along the way he covers two processing habits (a compressor on the far room mics for excitement, a Pultec-style EQ on kick and snare as a "bigger version of the same sound" tone/loudness control) and closes with a concrete kick-head recommendation: the Remo Powerstroke 3, his LA session standard for a big rock kick sound.

## Techniques

- **Fix drum mic problems by moving the mic in small increments, not by EQ-ing around them.** Working through the kit live: hi-hat mic moved up slightly for more stick attack (it was reading "a taste dark"); rack tom mic aimed closer to where the drummer actually hits the head for more presence/attack; floor tom mic brought in and pointed more toward the middle of the drum for the same reason; kick's inside mic nudged a tiny bit closer to the shell for more bottom weight; kick's outer mic angle changed slightly to reduce beater click. Every fix is a physical reposition, none is a plugin move. → [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]]
- **Widen room mics for stereo spread by moving them apart, keeping their distance from the kit the same.** A one-parameter fix for a room pair reading too narrow — spread the pair wider without changing how far back they sit, rather than fixing width in the box afterward. → [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Remo Powerstroke 3 named as the go-to LA session kick head for a big rock sound, valued specifically for punch and clarity; a coated head is the alternative when more tone (less punch) is wanted instead.** "We have our favorite kick drum heads here in LA, and there's something called a Remo Powerstroke 3 that seems to be the winner for most sessions... this is a big rock sound, so you want punch and clarity from the kick drum." → [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[Kick Design]]
- **A "wild card" character mic thrown into the room mix on purpose — a trashy Altec mic placed out in the middle of the room, distinct from the main room pair.** Named as a standing habit ("sometimes I throw some wild card mics into the equation"), confirmed sounding good once the other mics were repositioned around it. → [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A stereo compressor on the far room mics adds excitement/energy to the room sound, but can tip over into too much — the fix is backing the compressor off or bypassing it, not re-EQing.** "I like what my far room mics sound like with a compressor, but they might actually be a little over the top, so I might just back the compressor off, or maybe not even use it... my spot where the compression brings up the room is almost too much, but without that stereo compressor it's a little boring sounding." A direct, qualitative before/after on how much a room bus compressor should be trusted. → [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[Compression]] (effect)
- **A Pultec-style EQ (transcribed in captions as "pole tech," clearly Pultec) run on kick and snare purely as a big, musical tone/loudness control — described as making the same kick and snare sound "just a bigger version of it," like a tone control on an old stereo, not a corrective move.** Direct owned match: [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]]. → [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]], [[EQ]] (effect), [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]], [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]]

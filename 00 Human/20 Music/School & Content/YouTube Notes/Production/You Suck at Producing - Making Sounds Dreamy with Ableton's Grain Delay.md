## Techniques
- **Feedback sets the number of repetitions, same as any delay** — turning Feedback up increases how many times the signal repeats.
- **Delay time runs on a 16th-note grid by default, or frees to raw milliseconds via Sync** — the grid slider sets spacing between repeats in 16th notes (e.g. 4/16ths = a quarter note); clicking Sync and switching to a time value removes the grid entirely for a free-running delay time.
- **Pitch shifts each grain by semitones — 12 (octave) and 7 (fifth) read musically, small values don't** — 12 semitones gives a clean, even octave-up; 7 gives a perfect fifth; something like 3 semitones just sounds unpleasant rather than intentional.
- **Frequency is the grain-size knob, not a tone control** — maxed out, grains are tiny and heavily processed, reading metallic and "unhealthy"; turned down, grains get big and "whole," reading natural and musical.
- **Feedback also controls how many times the pitch-shift gets reapplied** — at 0% Feedback the signal goes up one octave once; turning Feedback up stacks that same pitch interval repeatedly, layering multiple octaves (or fifths, etc.) into the result for a brighter, denser texture.
- **A Random pitch option adds an unpredictable wobble** on top of whatever fixed Pitch/Feedback setting is dialed in.
- **Spray randomizes delay TIME, not pitch, and matters most with small grains** — with Frequency low (big, whole grains) Spray barely does anything audible; with Frequency high (tiny, metallic grains) Spray smears the timing of left/right channels and de-metallizes the result, at the cost of losing some clarity.
- **The XY pad maps any two parameters to one control surface at once** — pick which parameter drives the X axis and which drives the Y axis (e.g. Frequency and Spray) to sound-design two dimensions simultaneously with a single drag.
- **Pair with an Auto Filter afterward to tame excess brightness** the granular processing adds, rather than living with a harsh top end.
- **Build a parallel wet/dry rack to keep the original signal's full character present** — group Grain Delay + Auto Filter into an Audio Effect Rack (Cmd/Ctrl+G), open the Chains list, and add a second chain that's just the untouched dry signal; blend the two chains' volumes to taste so the source's original high-frequency detail survives underneath the granular treatment instead of being filtered away. → [[Auto Filter]]
- **The same rack works on percussion, not just melodic/harmonic sources** — dropped onto a hi-hat loop with Sync off and Delay Time set very fast, it reads as a pleasant stereo/high-frequency smear rather than an audible discrete echo.

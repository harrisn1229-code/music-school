# Blue Jay Way — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Indian influence at one remove — no Indian instruments and no metric fireworks, just **a drone bass and a raga-like modal tune**. The mode is the event: a major scale with *both* the second and fourth degrees raised ([[SCL-C05 — Characteristic notes]]). Folk-ballad form, the same shape as "The Fool On The Hill", which makes the two an instructive pair on one record.

**Form.** Intro, then verse–refrain three times, a fourth refrain, and a long outro. The intro is *ad libitum* with no meter but about eight measures' worth of duration. The verse is **one beat short of eight measures**, which renders the impatience of waiting structurally rather than lyrically ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]); the refrain is a full eight plus a pickup measure, more than paying the beat back.

**Harmony.** Effectively **two chords for the whole song over a bass that never moves** ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]). The ♯ii°7 is worth separating from the familiar vii°7: vii°7 is a dominant surrogate whose four notes all resolve stepwise into I, while ♯ii°7 is a subdominant surrogate where three resolve and the fourth is sustained across the change. George reaches it by outlining the diminished seventh already sitting inside his chosen scale, so the progression is implied by the tune as much as played.

**Melody.** The refrain is where the raga flavour actually lands, taking its pathos from leaning on the seventh and the raised fourth over the drone, the ♯4 supplying an appoggiatura the solo cello previews in the intro ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]).

**Arrangement.** A kitchen sink of every effect accumulated across *Revolver* and *Pepper*, thickening steadily. The outro only sounds improvised: each successive refrain is shorter and plainer, and the apparently new tune the last ones carry had already been slipped into a backing vocal several sections earlier — a composed ending disguised as a fader move.

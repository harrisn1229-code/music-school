# DRM-C05 — Anchors stay on-grid; secondaries go late

**Q.** Which hits get timing variation, which don't, and in which direction?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Anchor hits** — primary kick, primary snare — stay exactly on-grid at full, static velocity; softening them loses the dependable strength the groove needs. **Secondary hits** — ghost snares, offbeat claps, secondary kicks — take the variation, shifted deliberately **late, never early**, by a *different* amount each occurrence (uniform lateness reads as a setting, not a performance). The rule tracks metric **role**, not instrument: a hi-hat marking a strong beat *alone* stays on-grid because it has inherited the anchoring job.

## Why it matters

This is the rule that makes hip-hop swing read as pocket instead of sloppiness. Late-only is a stylistic law here — loose-and-late is the genre, loose-and-early is a mistake.

## Drill

On a lo-fi beat, nudge only the off-8th hats late by varying small amounts. Leave kick and snare untouched. Then try nudging them early and hear it fail.

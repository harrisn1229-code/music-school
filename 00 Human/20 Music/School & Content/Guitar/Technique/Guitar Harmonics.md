# Guitar Harmonics

Part of the guitar technique side of [[Music Theory]] — **overtones sounded directly on the string** rather than the fretted fundamental, producing a pure, bell-like, higher-pitched tone. Two families: **natural harmonics** (touched at a fixed node on an open string — 12th, 7th, 5th frets etc.) and **artificial harmonics** (the same touch-and-release technique applied to a *fretted* note, so any pitch can produce a harmonic, not just the open-string nodes).

## Artificial harmonic technique
1. **Fret the note normally** with the fretting hand, as if playing it straight.
2. With the **picking hand**, lightly touch the string exactly **12 frets above the fretted note** (an octave up) — the same light touch used for a standard open-string natural harmonic, not a full press.
3. **Pluck with the same picking hand** while still touching — the harmonic sounds an octave above the fretted pitch, brighter and thinner than the plain fretted note.

Because the touch point is defined relative to the fretted note (always +12 frets = one octave), **any fretted note anywhere on the neck can become a harmonic** this way — unlike natural harmonics, which are limited to the open strings' fixed nodes.

## Three ways to execute the touch-and-pluck
The picking hand has to do two things at once (touch the node, pluck the string) — three common ways to split that job:
1. **Pick + index finger** — hold the pick normally, extend the index finger out to touch the node, pluck with the pick while the index finger holds the touch.
2. **Pick + ring ("a") finger** — same touch, but pluck with the ring finger instead of the pick. Common in classical guitar technique.
3. **Thumb only** — touch with one finger, pluck with the thumb (a bit of thumbnail helps articulate it cleanly).

Any finger can touch and any finger can pluck — these three are just the most common combinations, not a fixed rule.

## Sound and use
A blues-scale run played entirely as artificial harmonics sits an octave higher than the same run fretted normally, with a much brighter, more bell-like/glassy character — a way to get an unusually high, pure-toned line out of registers the guitar doesn't naturally reach by fretting alone.

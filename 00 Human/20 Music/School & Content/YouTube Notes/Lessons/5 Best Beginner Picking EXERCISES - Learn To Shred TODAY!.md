# 5 Best Beginner Picking EXERCISES - Learn To Shred TODAY!

## Synopsis

A beginner-focused return to fundamentals — BERNTH's own first five alternate-picking exercises, opening with picking-hand setup (pick choice, wrist anchoring, hand shape) before the exercises themselves, which are built around switching note-value groupings mid-pattern to a metronome rather than raw speed.

## Techniques

- **Picking hand stays anchored to the guitar body** (resting near the bridge, not floating free) for consistent control — contrasted directly against an unanchored/floating picking-hand style some players use successfully but BERNTH doesn't recommend for himself.
- **Hand forms a loose fist while picking** — fingers not splayed/flopping — cited as a direct control improvement once adopted.
- **Small, wrist-driven motion even at speed**; more arm gets involved only at higher tempos, but the base motion stays relaxed, never full-arm.
- **Jazz III-size pick with a grip/rough surface** (Dunlop Max Grip) — control plus sweat resistance on stage.
- **Exercise 1 — two 2-pattern combination**: down-up-down (8th-8th-quarter) then quarter-down-up (quarter-8th-8th), isolatable individually before combining.
- **Exercise 2 — mid-measure note-value switching, 8ths → 16ths**, one measure each, continuous alternate picking throughout. The core difficulty: no adaptation gap is allowed at the switch — timing must lock in immediately, and BERNTH notes players (including himself) tend to rush into the switch early.
- **Exercise 3 — same switching principle, 8th-note triplets → straight 8ths**, now with the fretting hand involved (open-E-only exercises 1–2 give way to a real fretted pattern).
- **Deliberately muting notes to isolate rhythmic accents** — makes picking-hand timing audible/checkable independent of pitch, a practice technique in its own right, then reintroducing open/palm-muted tone once timing is solid.
- **Exercise 4 — chromatic scale, full picking + fretting-hand warm-up**, described as a distinct step up in left-hand involvement from the open-string exercises.

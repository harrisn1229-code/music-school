# Eight Days A Week — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Pollack's object lesson in **false (cross) relation** — a chromatic contradiction between two notes across adjacent chords. The whole song runs on one pair, **G natural against G♯** in D major, the natural pulling down to F♯ and the sharp pulling up to A, with one instance in every phrase. The second thread is how long the V chord is withheld and how badly it behaves when it finally shows up.

**Form.** Double bridge with one verse between. The verse is a four-square **16 bars phrased AABA′** — A phrases harmonically closed, B phrase classically transitional. The bridge is 8 bars in four short phrases, so **phrase lengths halve while the harmonic rhythm doubles in length**: a two-sided contrast out of one decision.

**Harmony.** Six chords. The V chord is the tease — **it never appears in the verse at all**, even though V-of-V keeps setting it up ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). Its first entrance is the downbeat of the bridge, where it resolves **deceptively to vi** ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]). The only ordinary V–I in the song is the bridge-to-verse return, which means **the verses establish the key entirely by plagal IV–I** ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]). The verse's cross relation is the subtle one: the sharp sits in a middle voice while the natural that contradicts it sits in the outer voices.

**Melody.** Confined to a **sixth** for the whole song. Individual phrases still get real contour out of that, but the narrowness may be what pushes attention onto the chord changes.

**Arrangement.** Texture change articulates the form ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]). The 4-bar intro is **faded in** over a pounded **tonic pedal** in rapid triplets heard nowhere else on the record ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]). Handclaps mark 2 and 4 in the A phrases and switch to a snapping dotted rhythm in the B phrase, where the kit drops out entirely; they sit the bridge out altogether. Changing what the handclaps *do* rather than whether they exist is the trick — nobody hears the plan, everybody hears the lift. The outro is musically the intro, but they refuse to make it symmetrical: no fade-in, no fade-out.

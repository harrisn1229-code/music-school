# Come Together — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Four and a half minutes of extremely little material, proportioned to feel unhurried over a driving tempo: **the first refrain doesn't arrive until 1:10**, the harmonically frozen outro eats about the same span at the other end, and the four-bar intro keeps returning in between. Pollack's stylistic point is evocation rather than imitation — an old style updated so knowingly the antecedents stop mattering.

**Form.** Intro 4 bars, verse 8, refrain just **2 bars**, outro just under 24. The craft is editorial: **two of the later intro reprises are cut in half** to stop the second half sagging, and the full-length intro returns right before the closing section specifically to telegraph the ending.

**Harmony.** Only i, IV and V, plus one vi in the refrain. The verse is open on **IV rather than V** — Pollack reads it as a twelve-bar frame with its middle four bars deleted. The two-bar refrain is the **only place in the song where the harmonic rhythm speeds up at all** ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]), which is enough to make it the hook by contrast alone. The best analytical catch: the track sounds full of minor/major collision and almost never actually is — the friction is a side effect of using **major IV under a minor tonic** where the diatonic iv would be expected ([[POP-C10 — Dorian IV vs Aeolian iv]]), with the major third kept out of the tonic chord entirely.

**Melody.** Verse tune is pentatonic Dorian: minor third, minor seventh, sixth degree avoided outright.

**Arrangement.** Produced to sound larger than life rather than stranger than life — the technology is spent on weight, not weirdness. The vocal staging is almost entirely a schedule of absences ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]): verse 1 is John alone; later verses bring Paul in late in the first phrase and drop him before the end, so John is exposed at both edges.

# Dorian from the Minor Pentatonic Box

Part of [[Guitar Positions]] and [[Diatonic Modes]]. The core idea: don't learn Dorian as a new seven-note shape — start from the **minor pentatonic box you already know** and add exactly two notes (the 2 and the major 6th) onto it. Two fingerings are given below: the classic box shape (fixed low-string position) and a movable "diagonal" shape that copy-pastes to any root/string-set. Both worked in **D minor** (D Dorian).

## The two notes that turn minor pentatonic into Dorian
D minor pentatonic is D (1) F (♭3) G (4) A (5) C (♭7) — no 2, no 6. Dorian adds:
- **The 2 (E natural)** — an easy, safe color note; "you can land on it almost any time and it's going to sound great."
- **The major 6 (B natural)** — the note that actually *defines* Dorian. This is "the sound of Dorian" and the single highest-value target once you're past plain pentatonic soloing. → the character difference vs. [[Diatonic Modes|Aeolian]] (natural minor, which has ♭6 instead) comes entirely from this one note — demonstrated as a direct A/B in [[NO ONE is teaching Dorian LIKE THIS! (Daniel Seriff)]].

**Optional stopping point: the hexatonic scale.** Pentatonic + just the 2 (six notes total, no 6th) is a legitimate, complete-in-itself sound some players lean on rather than pushing to the full seven-note mode — cited players: **Derek Trucks**, **Jimmy Herring**. Useful permission to think of "add the 2" and "add the 6" as two separable steps, not an all-or-nothing jump to the full mode.

## Fingering 1 — the classic box (fixed position)
Built on the familiar D minor pentatonic box (root at the 10th fret, low E string):
- **Add the 2 (E)** at three spots around the box: **12th fret, low E string** · **12th fret, high E string** · **9th fret, G string**.
- **Add the major 6 (B)** at: **12th fret, B string** · **9th fret, D string**.
- **Practice habit:** center your improv on the plain D minor pentatonic first; don't reach for the 2 or the 6 right away — save them for later in the phrase so they read as deliberate color choices, not just extra notes. Demoed over a D minor 7 → G7, ~80 BPM funky backing track.

## Fingering 2 — the diagonal pentatonic (movable, copy-paste anywhere)
A second system Seriff frames as the more practical one specifically *because* it isn't tied to one fixed box — the same relative fingering relocates to any root on any adjacent string pair. Built here on the two thinnest strings (B string + high E string), still in D minor:
- **Find the root** on the B string (worked example: **15th fret** = D). Ring finger on the root.
- **Index finger one whole step below** the root, same string (worked example: **13th fret** = C, the ♭7) — together these two notes are the "two-note set": ♭7 → root.
- **Three-note set on the next string up (high E):** F (♭3), G (4), A (5) — completes the 5-note diagonal minor pentatonic across just two strings. *(Transcript doesn't give absolute frets for this set — find it relative to the two-note set on the B string below it.)*
- **Add the major 6:** one fret **below** the ♭7 on the B string (worked example: ♭7 at fret 13 → 6th at **fret 12**). Found *relationally* off the ♭7 you already have, not memorized as a new absolute position — the fastest way to relocate this shape to a new key.
- **Add the 2:** on the high E string, at the **12th fret** in the worked D example — stacks alongside the existing three-note pentatonic set on that string.
- **Full Dorian diagonal shape: three notes on the B string (6, ♭7, root), four on the high E string (2, ♭3, 4, 5).** The whole shape — pentatonic base plus both added notes — copy-pastes to any root by sliding it up/down the neck, and to any string pair where you can find the root, same as the box shape's transposability but without being locked to one fixed position.

## Why this matters
Both fingerings reduce "learn Dorian" to "learn two notes relative to a shape you already play," which is a much smaller ask than memorizing a new seven-note pattern from scratch — and the diagonal version generalizes the same two-notes-added logic to a movable system instead of one fixed box.

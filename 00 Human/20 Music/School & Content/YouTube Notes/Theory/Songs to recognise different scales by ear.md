# Songs to recognise different scales by ear

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Al1Smeh4usc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Al1Smeh4usc)

## Synopsis

The song-anchor method applied one level up from individual scale degrees — a full song example per SCALE (major, minor variants, pentatonics, modes, and some "unorthodox" scales), each chosen where possible to literally run the scale up or down so its full character is audible.

## Techniques

- **Major scale** — "Do-Re-Mi" (*The Sound of Music*) literally spells the scale out; "Here Comes the Bride"/"Wedding March" bells descend straight down from the top.
- **Natural minor (Aeolian)** — "Everything Means Nothing to Me" (Elliot Smith) repeatedly climbs the scale, though the song doesn't stay in one key throughout; "Losing My Religion" (R.E.M.) stays in A natural minor for its entire duration — the cleaner example.
- **Harmonic minor** — natural minor with a raised 7th, described as giving a distinctly "classical" sound. "Plug In Baby" (Muse, classical-influenced).
- **Minor pentatonic** — "Shape of You" (Ed Sheeran) — melody built entirely from it. "Long, Long, Long" (Beatles) opens with a literal bottom-to-top climb of G minor pentatonic.
- **Major pentatonic** — "My Girl" (The Temptations) intro guitar riff climbs bottom-to-top of C major pentatonic.
- **Whole tone scale** — "mysterious" character (every step a whole step). "You Are the Sunshine of My Life" (Stevie Wonder) — the 2nd chord of the intro climbs the full whole-tone scale.
- **Octatonic/diminished scale (tone-semitone-tone... pattern)** — "Just" (Radiohead) opens by climbing this scale.
- **Blues scale** — minor pentatonic + one added note between the 4th and 5th degrees. "Fill Your Heart" (David Bowie, Rick Wakeman piano solo races down the A blues scale over two octaves — caveated as a slightly impure example since the song isn't actually in A). Cleaner example: the closing lick of "Sweet Child O' Mine" (Guns N' Roses), descending the E♭ minor blues scale.
- **Dorian** — natural minor with a raised 6th degree. "Riders on the Storm" (The Doors) — a descending line works through E Dorian.
- **Mixolydian** — "Bitter Sweet Symphony" (The Verve) — string melody and vocal both stay in E Mixolydian for the entire song, without a literal scale run. Also cited: The Sims 1 build theme (mentioned as the presenter's own first exposure to the sound of Mixolydian, modulating between different Mixolydian keys throughout — credited to a separate Charles Cornell video for a full breakdown).
- **Chromatic scale** — "Entrance of the Gladiators" ("circus music") moves almost entirely by consecutive half steps.
- **11 scales covered total.**

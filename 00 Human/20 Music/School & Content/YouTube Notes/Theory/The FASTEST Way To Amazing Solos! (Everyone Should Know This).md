# The FASTEST Way To Amazing Solos! (Everyone Should Know This)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fV78mXrPQOA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fV78mXrPQOA)

## Synopsis

Answering a Patreon student's question ("how do you choose interesting notes for a solo?"), BERNTH plays two melodies over the same D minor vamp — one built from root+5th, one deliberately avoiding them — to demonstrate that note *choice*, not scale knowledge or phrasing, is what separates a boring take from an interesting one. The core system: stop visualizing scale-box diagrams and instead visualize intervals chromatically around the chord's root on the fretboard, deliberately favoring the less-obvious color tones.

## What's taught

- **Root and perfect 5th are the least interesting notes to target, because they're already in the chord.** The "boring" first take used only root + 5th. Not forbidden — root is used plenty in real playing — but leaning on them for melodic emphasis reads as generic.
- **The "interesting" take favored major 2nd, minor 3rd, minor 7th, and — briefly — major 6th** (the characteristic note of D Dorian, giving a light modal color) over the same D minor vamp. Framed as the actual, sole difference between the two takes — no new scale, no different phrasing, just different target notes.
- **Visualize intervals chromatically around the root, not as a scale-box shape.** Practical fretboard map given for a root on the A string (worked from D, 5th fret): perfect 5th is one string lower same fret, or two strings higher two frets down (multiple locations shown); perfect 4th is one string higher same fret; minor 7th is two strings higher, two frets up; major 2nd is one string higher, two frets up; minor/major 3rd and minor/major 6th similarly mapped as fixed string/fret offsets from the root. The pitch is: know the offset to each color tone the way you already know the shape of a chord, so during improvisation you can consciously *start* a phrase on (say) the major 2nd instead of defaulting to the root.
- **Minor 6th flagged as usable but riskier** — technically the "correct" note in natural minor, but can sound dissonant in context; major 6th (borrowed, Dorian-flavored) was BERNTH's actual choice in the demonstrated take, used to slide up into the minor 7th.
- **Daily practice exercise:** pick a root note on the A string, then locate and play (in order) the octave, the perfect 5th, the major/minor 3rd, the major 2nd, and the minor/major 7th around it — 5-10 minutes a day. The goal isn't memorizing note names but internalizing interval *offsets* on the neck well enough to consciously target a specific color tone (not just "the scale") the instant a chord change happens.

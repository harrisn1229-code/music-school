# Ableton Tricks I Learned From Remixing Mr. Bill

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk8HsgS9Gbc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk8HsgS9Gbc)

## Synopsis

Underbelly walks through a real remix (Mr. Bill & Scope's "2020") end to end, framed around two core remix strategies: amplifying an idea that was only sparingly present in the original, and re-harmonizing the original's melodies with new chords/basslines. Dense with concrete sound design — a Serum "gun bass" percussive-kick trick via Envelope-to-Master-Tuning (credited to producer Eli derp), Operator wobble chords built from a plain sine wave plus LFO and Auto Pan modulation, dub-echo and late hi-hat placement on the drums, a power-chord re-voicing move for a harder dubstep-appropriate sound, and a genuinely clever use of Spectral Resonator's MIDI input to re-harmonize an *existing* melodic part rather than rewriting it. Closes with a bass glitch-fill warp technique and a Chord-MIDI-effect trick for turning a one-note synth patch into a full chord layer.

## Techniques

- **Remix strategy #1: take an idea used sparingly in the original and repeat it heavily in the remix** — identify an element the original artist only touched on briefly, and build a whole section/hook around it instead — a fast way to make a remix feel distinct from the source without writing from scratch. *(General approach — no dedicated technique-note home; held on this page.)*
- **Gate to remove reverb/noise tails, "gun sub" underneath for weight** — a gate on a perky synth strips its trailing reverb/noise, cleaning up the tail; layering a distorted sub ("gun sub") underneath the same synth gives it low-end weight without changing its top-end character.
- **Serum "gun bass": Envelope 2 → Master Tuning for a whole-patch percussive kick** — mapping a fast envelope to the synth's overall Master Tuning (not a single oscillator's pitch) pitch-drops every oscillator at once for an instant at note-on, adding a percussive "kick" that a per-oscillator pitch envelope doesn't give the same way. Credited to producer Eli derp. → [[Serum 2]], [[Kick Design]]
- **Hi-hats nudged late for a "drunk" feel; occasional dub echo on snares for a "Rastafari" character** — a small deliberate timing offset on the hi-hat layer only (not the whole kit) for looseness, plus a dub-style echo thrown on select snare hits (not every hit) for genre-appropriate flavor. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Delay]]
- **Operator wobble chords: plain sine chord + LFO → Osc B Level + Auto Pan (1/4, Amount max, Phase 0)** — strip the patch back to a bare sine wave playing a full chord (F minor + a half-step passing move), then build the "wobble" entirely from modulation: an LFO mapped to Oscillator B's Level for pulsing harmonic content, plus Auto Pan synced to quarter notes with Amount maxed and Phase at 0 for a hard rhythmic stereo wobble on top. → [[Operator]], [[Auto Pan]]
- **Power chords (root + fifth, third dropped) instead of triads, for a harder dubstep-appropriate re-harmonization** — when adding new chords under an existing melody, dropping the third entirely and keeping just the root and fifth reads as noticeably "harder"/more appropriate for dubstep than a full triad. *(Single mention — no dedicated chord-voicing technique note yet; held on this page.)*
- **A richer chord progression: double root+fifth down an octave, then add 7ths and 9ths** — for a second, more harmonically ambitious section: take a basic 4-chord progression (Db major, Eb major, F minor, Ab major), double each chord's root and fifth an octave down for size, then layer 7ths and 9ths on top for a prettier, more complex voicing.
- **Re-harmonize an EXISTING melodic part via Spectral Resonator's MIDI input, instead of rewriting it** — rather than re-recording a stuck melodic/bell synth part to match a new chord progression, drop Spectral Resonator on it, set the input to MIDI, and point it at a separate MIDI track playing the new (power-chord) progression — the resonator re-pitches the existing part live to follow the new harmony. A/B'd bypassed vs. engaged; described as a dramatic improvement. → [[Spectral Resonator]]
- **Bass call-and-response + eighth-note triplet groove + a "laser" layer + vocal-chop melodic content, for a drop section** — alternate phrases between a chopped original bass and a gun-sub layer (call and response), run the gun sub in an eighth-note triplet rhythm rather than straight time, layer a comedic "laser" sound over the sub for texture, and pull vocal chops from the original song for melodic interest over the top of the bass content.
- **Bass glitch fill: Tones warp mode over a repeated ×2 stretch, plus Cmd/Shift-drag to extend a warp region past normal bounds** — a repeated ×2 time-stretch (the general warp-glitch move) underwhelms specifically on bass material; switching the snippet's warp mode to Tones and adjusting grain size gets a noticeably better bass-glitch result. To extend the glitched region cleanly into the next hit, hold Cmd/Ctrl+Shift while hovering the clip edge (a distinct arrow cursor appears) and click-drag — stretches the warp region further than a normal edge-drag allows. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Chord MIDI effect turns a one-note patch into a full chord layer** — placed ahead of a simple sawtooth "womp" patch (built in Vital, unowned), the Chord device makes a single played note trigger a full chord (A minor here), with a small half-step passing move added on a triplet subdivision for motion. → [[Chord]]
- **Extend the harmony further at the climax, and pitch-shift the original artist's own audio stems to match** — push the chord progression higher/further (up to Bb minor → Db major) for the track's ending, and time-stretch/re-pitch the remix source's own audio (not just MIDI parts) so it follows the new, more extended chord progression rather than clashing with it.

# King Gizzard used EVERY mode on this album

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gNT1jSbgXDo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gNT1jSbgXDo)

## Synopsis

King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard's 2022 album *Ice, Death, Planets, Lungs, Mushrooms and Lava* (the title itself spells out the seven modes' first letters) dedicates one track to each of the seven modes of the major scale — a rare full-album demonstration of how to actually establish each mode's character in a real arrangement, not just define it on paper. This is the richest single source in the vault for real per-mode arrangement technique.

## Techniques

- **Ionian (Melium, E♭)** — the "obvious" mode was reportedly the hardest to write, per frontman Stu Mackenzie: finding an Ionian sound that didn't feel "too happy, too Disney." Built on the three main chords of the key (I–IV–V), but the bass line deliberately doesn't follow the chord roots straightforwardly — kept the harmony from sounding as plain/generic as root-position I–IV–V normally would.
- **Dorian (Ice V, G)** — established via a vamp between the tonic minor chord (Gm) and the ii chord (Am). Between just those two chords you get almost every scale degree, and crucially the ii chord supplies Dorian's defining note: the **raised 6th** (E natural, vs. E♭ in a plain minor scale) — hearing that note inside the ii chord is what locks the ear into Dorian rather than plain natural minor.
- **Phrygian (Magma, D)** — no real chord progression at all: the track largely drones on the tonic note D, with occasional turnarounds from the bass. **Droning on the tonic is the standard way to present modal (as opposed to tonal) music** — tonal music navigates between different chords/keys, modal music instead sits still and lets the mode's color emerge from a fixed center. The lowered 2nd degree (Phrygian's defining note) gives it an "exotic, almost Arabesque" sound.
- **Lydian (Lava, F)** — drones on the tonic for the first two minutes, then introduces a short vamp: tonic chord (F) moving to **G/F** — described as "the Lydian chord," since a G triad over an F bass puts Lydian's defining raised 4th (B natural) directly in the chord while the bass keeps the true tonic (F) grounded underneath. **Why keeping the tonic anchored matters especially for Lydian**: the sharp 4th constantly wants to reorient the ear toward hearing the music in its parent major key (C major, in this case) rather than F Lydian — an unstressed tonic lets Lydian's "floating, cloud-like, unsolid" character slip back into a plain major-key reading.
- **Mixolydian (Hell's Itch, D)** — built on a D7–Bm7–Cadd9–D chord loop. Mixolydian's defining lowered 7th removes the leading-tone pull back to the tonic that a major scale would have, giving the mode a "cooler, more mellow, laid-back" feel that can float rather than resolve — the track leans into that by staying in the loop for its full 13½-minute runtime rather than ever cadencing away from it.
- **Aeolian (Iron Lung, A)** — plain natural minor. Opens with a vi–i vamp (F major7–Am). Explicitly pushes back on "minor = sad" as an oversimplification, offering "solemn, serious, mournful" as better descriptors for the specific Aeolian color.
- **Locrian (Gliese 710, A)** — the hardest mode by the band's own account, because Locrian's tonic chord is **diminished** (unstable, the opposite of what a tonic chord needs to be). **The fix: remove the problem note.** They dropped the diminished chord's 5th (E♭) and replaced it with the 7th (G natural), turning an unusable Adim into a stable **Am7♭5** — a genuinely reusable technique for any Locrian-tonic writing problem. That reworked tonic chord loops with B♭/A (highlighting Locrian's defining lowered 2nd) while keeping A anchored in the bass. Because the true diminished 5th (E♭) is now absent from the harmony, it's reintroduced melodically instead — sprinkled into guitar/keyboard lines so the mode still reads as Locrian by ear even though the chord progression itself avoids the interval that makes Locrian hard to use. Also notable: the track opens in 7/4 and later shifts to 9/4 — the band saved their odd-meter tendencies for the album's final, hardest-mode track specifically.

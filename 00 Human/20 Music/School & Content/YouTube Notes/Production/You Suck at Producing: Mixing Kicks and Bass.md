# You Suck at Producing: Mixing Kicks and Bass

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MSyMma9M6AA)

## Synopsis
A tight, practical mixing lesson wrapped in the channel's signature crude-comedy framing ("who gets to be on top" standing in for "who owns the sub"). The actual content is a clean, complete example of diagnosing and fixing a kick/bass frequency clash: find where each element's fundamental actually peaks using an EQ's analyzer view, decide which one gets to keep the sub, high-pass the other, and — the video's most useful real-world wrinkle — recognize when high-passing the kick has gutted its punch and swap in a different kick sample rather than continuing to EQ a lost cause.

## Techniques
- **Find each element's actual resonant frequency with an EQ's analyzer/spectrum view before touching any knobs** — add an EQ Eight to both the kick and the bass, open the frequency-spectrum display on each, play them together, and visually + audibly identify where each one's energy is actually concentrated rather than guessing. In the video's example, both the kick and the bass turn out to peak at the same ~55 Hz, with the kick carrying a secondary overtone bump around ~120 Hz.
- **Decide who "owns" the sub, then high-pass the loser — don't EQ both to compromise.** Once the clashing frequency is identified, pick ONE element to hold that frequency range (here: the bass, since it's already carrying more of the low-end weight) and introduce a low-cut filter on the OTHER element (the kick), raising the cutoff while listening until the clash resolves — rather than notching both elements and hoping they coexist.
- **If high-passing guts the kick's punch, swap the sample instead of continuing to EQ it** — a kick with all its sub removed can become a "one-trick pony" that no longer holds its own in the mix; when that happens, don't keep processing the same sample — audition a different kick with more inherent high-end/character, then apply the same low-cut treatment to the NEW sample so it locks in with the bass the same way.

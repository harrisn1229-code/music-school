## Techniques — workflow & delivery

- **Sample management via Cubase Media Bay (don't organize folders — rate + index)** — he never files samples; Media Bay **indexes ~2 TB** and he **rates** the ones he likes, so a search returns "all the kicks I've liked in 15 years" instantly (works off a text-file database). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Project Logical Editor for batch stem naming** — a Cubase "mini-programming"/macro tool; he made a **"prefix" preset** to prepend `db1-` (etc.) to every track name in a group when exporting stems. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Stem-delivery method: give the mixer the *bus* AND the *elements*** — export `Drum Buss 1` (his processed sound, e.g. **parallel Saturn at ~23% wet**) *and* a subfolder of every individual element prefixed `db1`, so the mixer can either use his sound or rebuild a better version. **Blank track names = combine to one stem**; leave FX on a bus only when he wants to retain that specific sound (and says so). → [[Referencing and Translation]], [[3 Drums Bus]], [[Saturn 2]]
- **Chords from Scaler + Cthulhu** — "I suck at playing chords," so he uses Scaler/Cthulhu to generate progressions. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **"Chaos to find order" chopping (Cubase slide)** — select all the vocal-chop blocks and **slide each to a random point in the sample**, hunting for a rhythm your ear catches — "staring at infinity." Cubase's per-clip fades + slide make aggressive chopping fast. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Start the song from the bassline, by ear** — he plays a bass idea until Julia says "that's cool," then builds; chords come by ear (mistakes and all). → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Parallel distortion on the drum bus** — Saturn mixed low (~23%) across the drums for parallel grit. → [[Saturn 2]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]

## Philosophy

- **Bad Liar is his "ultimate example of cutting back"** — when a song needs minimalism, take everything out and leave only what's needed (contrast to Pretty Please's gap-filling). → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - Bad Liar (Selena Gomez) Breakdown + Q&A]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **"I bat ~8%"** — most songs he works on never come out; he keeps a folder of unreleased starts to pitch in sessions. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - On Loops, Artist Ego and Originality]]

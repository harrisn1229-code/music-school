# LinnDrum

## What it is
The LM-2, sold as "LinnDrum" — Roger Linn's cheaper ($2,995), more successful follow-up to the [[Linn LM-1]] (~5,000 units). This is the drum machine you're hearing on a huge share of mid-80s radio pop: tight sampled kit, huge gated-reverb-friendly snare, crisp hats. If a track sounds like a neon jacket, it's probably a LinnDrum.

## What makes the sound
- **8-bit drum samples on EPROMs** like the LM-1, but with **real hi-hats and crash/ride added** (hats even respond to a decay knob via VCA — a sampled hat with analog envelope control).
- **The snare** is the star: fat, mid-forward, slightly crunchy — and it became the 80s *gated reverb* snare once producers slammed it through an AMS RMX16 or SSL talkback compression → the [[Synthwave]] playbook's snare recipe.
- **Tunable voices** (clock-rate repitch) but tighter/cleaner overall than the LM-1 — less funk grit, more pop gloss.
- **Linn swing/quantize** again — patterns sit *perfectly*, which is the "drum machine pop" feel of the era; the stiffness is the aesthetic.
- Individual outputs per voice → per-drum console processing → that produced, layered 80s drum mix.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Simpler]] with LinnDrum samples (the standard synthwave starting kit); [[RC-20 Retro Color]] for vintage sheen; gated-verb the snare with [[Ghz Megaverb 3]] or [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] (NonLin mode) to complete the 80s move.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2, Samples From Mars "Linn From Mars", Wave Alchemy Revolution.
- **Free:** LinnDrum sample packs are ubiquitous — the whole machine is ~28 sounds.

## Used on
- a-ha — "Take On Me" (1985): LinnDrum pattern under the arpeggio, the canonical example.
- Tears for Fears — "Everybody Wants to Rule the World" (1985): LinnDrum groove (with live overdubs), that shuffled 12/8 feel is Linn swing.
- Hall & Oates — "Maneater" (1982) and the *H2O* era: LinnDrum as the pop-radio backbone.
- Genre/era: mid-80s pop/synthpop/R&B wholesale; the entire modern synthwave revival ([[Synthwave]]) builds kits from LinnDrum samples.

## Techniques

- **A straight, unswung LinnDrum blended against a heavily-swung bass part specifically to create rhythmic tension — the "straight" half of the record's core sonic pairing.** Jack Antonoff, on Sabrina Carpenter's "Manchild": "on Manchild the two main feels is the LinnDrum, which is as straight as can be, and then this galloping swung thing on top of it which kind of pushes and pulls in different directions... straight and swung are the spectrum, there's everything in between, the space between is where the magic happens." Paired with a Juno bass (see [[Roland Juno-106]]) as the track's other founding element. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — Mix with the Masters (Jack Antonoff), [[Behind the Track｜'Manchild' by Sabrina Carpenter]]
- **Click-layer duty** — Guy Lawrence layers the LinnDrum kick's top end quietly under modern kicks purely for attack ("I love a LinnDrum top end of a kick") — Disclosure, [Making Beats + Talk stream](https://youtu.be/1GhqAf9lc3A)

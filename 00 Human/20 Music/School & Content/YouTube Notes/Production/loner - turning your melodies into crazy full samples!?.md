# loner - turning your melodies into crazy full samples!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M8saMeKUdTE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M8saMeKUdTE)

## Synopsis

A community-stem sample-flip video: loner asked Discord members to send stems (vocal loops, counter-melodies, guitar parts, perc loops) from a prior "million stream placement" video, and this episode is him downloading a fresh batch and building three complete, fully different-sounding samples from them live on screen — a guitar+vocal stem into a lush layered R&B-ish flip, two vocal chops into a second R&B piece built around a Postcard Piano progression, and a dark bell loop into a hard, 808-driven finale. It's less a single-technique deep-dive than a rapid three-times-repeated workflow demo, and the repetition is the value: the same small toolkit (Shaper Box for pulse/duck movement, a "double-time + reverse" pass loner calls "the trick I normally do," time-stretch, octave shifts, bounce-then-rechop) recurs across all three builds, showing which few moves he leans on again and again rather than one deep technical breakdown. Settings given are mostly qualitative (preset names, "mix down to about 50," "super short," "quick two-step") rather than exact numeric mix values — a fast narrated screen-capture style. Community sourcing itself is also a first-class technique here: he explicitly credits each stem's sender (estrox, "nice little bounce," stavo, ll, sendo, iamquietfire) by name on screen, reinforcing [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]'s "trade in the community" thesis from earlier loner breakdowns.

## Techniques

### Sample 1 — guitar+vocal stem (estrox) → layered R&B flip
- **Build a brand-new chord progression around an isolated vocal stem rather than reusing the sender's own chords/guitar** — the stem came with BOTH guitar and vocal stemmed out separately, but instead of building on the guitar, loner takes only the vocal and writes fresh chords under it with a strings patch. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Spitfire LABS Augmented Strings, "Del Mar" preset** as the chord bed under the vocal. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Double-time the vocal via the DAW's stretch/warp tool** (FL's Stretch control — Ableton-native equivalent is Warp mode / doubling clip length and re-warping), then **Shaper Box on the vocal specifically "to make it pulse"** — a rhythmic volume-gating move on the sped-up vocal, not a static effect. → [[Shaper]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Pitch the pad up an octave**, then **layer AAS Lounge Lizard electric piano underneath, copying the same chords but adding a few extra notes** rather than a literal duplicate — a layering move that keeps harmonic cohesion while giving the added layer its own identity. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **A community perc loop ("nice little bounce") time-stretched and layered underneath for texture** — picked specifically because "the texture is going to sound crazy underneath what we have," i.e. chosen for feel against the existing layers, not for its literal drum content. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Chop two specific chord hits out of the electric-piano ("roads") part, make each one unique, then reverse both** — a targeted two-hit chop-and-reverse rather than reversing the whole part, deliberately called out as taking the sample "to a whole other level." → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Guitar amp (Stark, Klevgrand) run on a synth/pad element, not a guitar — a texture/grit re-tone move, not a guitar-processing move** — same non-guitar reuse pattern as Stark's other logged uses (a distorted pad texture, a road accent); here it's specifically used to "change" a sound loner didn't like, followed by an EQ cut on the low end and a check-listen for a specific high frequency. → [[Morgan Amps Suite]] (owned amp-sim equivalent), [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[EQ]]
- **A second, separate chop-reverse pass, this time WITH a rhythmic-feel change: double-time it, reverse it, then remove the time-stretch and use only part of the result** — distinct from the first chop-reverse pass above because the stretch is deliberately un-done afterward, and only a fragment of the resulting audio is kept, not the whole chopped region. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Subtractive arrangement move: pull the perc loop OUT for this section** — rather than adding a new element to mark the section change, an existing layer (the perc loop) is simply muted/removed for that stretch, letting the newly-chopped-and-reversed part read clearly on its own. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Waves J37 tape added to the electric-piano ("roads") part, plus "a little reverb from Waves"** as the finishing color/space stage on that layer. → [[Kramer Tape]] (owned tape-sim equivalent), [[Reverb]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Augmented Strings reused a second time in the same build, for a counter-melody/texture layer** — same instrument, different chord content, layered again with **Shaper Box "to make this pulse"** — the pulse move recurs a second time in the same sample. → [[Shaper]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **A half-time pass added as the closing move on the whole arrangement** before bounce. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Bounce everything to audio, then load the bounce into a slicer (Fruit Slicer)** as the final step, turning the finished composite into a chop-ready one-shot/loop for further flipping. → [[Sampler]] (owned Slice-to-New-MIDI-Track path documented on [[Sampling and Sound Selection]])

### Sample 2 — two vocal chops (ll) → second R&B flip
- **Two separate vocal-chop stems from the same sender, already in the same key, downloaded and used together in one sample** — key-matched source selection before any processing begins. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **A second community perc loop (stavo)** layered in early, alongside **Teletone Audio "Postcard Piano"** as the chord source — a chord progression built by auditioning the sample library's own preset chords rather than programming from a blank instrument. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Consolidate the vocal stem, then trim its remainder/tail** before further processing — cleanup pass for a cleaner chop target. → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]]
- **"Double-time it and reverse it — that's the trick I normally do."** Loner names this explicitly as his own signature/default move on a vocal chop: warp the vocal to double speed, then reverse the result, described as reliably making things "sound really cool." This is the same double-time+reverse mechanic used (without the label) on Sample 1's chord chops above — worth treating as loner's standing default move for turning a plain vocal/chord chop into a "vintage sample"-reading texture. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Pitch the processed vocal up an octave, route to its own mixer track**, then **Valhalla VintageVerb, room-reverb-family preset, mix pulled down to ~50%, size also brought down** — a short, contained room character rather than a big wash, on the piano part specifically. *(exact size value not given — "bring that down")* → [[ValhallaVintageVerb]], [[Reverb]]
- **Bandpass EQ on the perc loop** — narrows the loop's frequency content so it sits as texture rather than competing across the full spectrum with the vocal/piano. → [[EQ]]
- **Rhodes Affair VST for a walk-down fill at the end of every 4-bar phrase**, bringing the progression back to the top — a small connective arrangement fill, not a new section. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Augmented Strings reused a third time in this video (different preset from Sample 1) — "Impetuous," described as a violin-type sound**, for the melody, paired with **a super-heavy, very short delay specifically to fake a chorus effect** rather than reaching for an actual chorus plugin — a delay-as-modulation substitution. → [[Delay]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Spitfire LABS Augmented Voices layered on top of the piano, copying the same chords**, with **Shaper Box added specifically because the pad layer was interfering with the piano's attack** — Shaper used here as a corrective/ducking tool (clear the piano's transient) rather than a rhythmic-pulse effect, a third distinct Shaper Box role in this single video (pulse on Sample 1's vocal, pulse on Sample 1's counter-melody, attack-clearing duck here). Quarter-note rate chosen for the shape. → [[Shaper]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Bounce the pad layer to audio, then chop out small sections at the SAME beat position in every bar, leaving deliberate pockets of silence** — a rhythmic subtractive edit applied consistently across the bar grid (not random chops), explicitly for added "vibe." Same family of move as loner's "chop deliberately around silence" technique logged from an earlier video, here applied to a bounced synth-pad layer instead of a piano part. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

### Sample 3 — dark bell loop (sendo) → hard/808 finale
- **A dark, hard tonal direction chosen deliberately as contrast to the two R&B-leaning samples before it** — a dark bell loop, dragged straight into the slicer (Fruit Slicer) as the starting point. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Arturia Analog Lab, "Layers Volume 2" preset bank, "Marathon Not Sprint" preset** — a specific named preset from a specific named expansion, layered in early. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] (Analog Lab already tracked — new preset-pack reuse)
- **A second Analog Lab pad preset** added on top of the Layers Vol. 2 element.
- **808s + a quick two-step hi-hat pattern, with the hi-hat pattern doubling as a rhythmic "hit on the one"** — reusing a percussive element's own pattern as an accent trigger rather than adding a separate hit sound. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **A community counter-melody stem (iamquietfire) layered in**, run through **Shaper Box, "Volume Basic" preset, quarter-note rate** — the same device family used across all three samples in this video, here specifically its stock "Volume Basic" rhythmic-gating preset rather than a custom-drawn curve. → [[Shaper]], [[Layering and Stacking]]

### Cross-sample thesis (repeats across all three builds)
- **Shaper Box (ShaperBox) is the single most-reused tool in this video — 5 separate uses across 3 samples**, each with a different intent: rhythmic "pulse" on a sped-up vocal (Sample 1), rhythmic "pulse" on a counter-melody (Sample 1), attack-clearing duck on a pad competing with a piano's transient (Sample 2), and a stock "Volume Basic" quarter-note gate on a counter-melody (Sample 3). Confirms [[Shaper]] (Ableton's own Max for Live device) as the standing owned equivalent for all of these roles — see [[Shaper]]'s own Techniques section for how its Loop/1-Shot/Manual modes map onto pulse vs. duck vs. gate use. → [[Shaper]]
- **"Double-time, then reverse" is loner's repeatable default chop move**, used at least three times across this video (twice unlabeled on Sample 1's chord chops, once explicitly named on Sample 2's vocal) — worth treating as a first-reach technique whenever a chop needs to read as more "vintage sample," not a one-off. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Community-sourced stems as the raw material for every sample in the video, credited by name on screen** (estrox, "nice little bounce," stavo, ll, sendo, iamquietfire) — reinforces the "trade in the community" sourcing thesis already logged on [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] from earlier loner breakdowns, here shown as loner's own actual sourcing workflow rather than advice given to others.

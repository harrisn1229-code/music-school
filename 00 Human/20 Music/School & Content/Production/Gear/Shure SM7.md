# Shure SM7

**Category:** Microphone
**Vendor:** "Shure"

## What it is

The Shure SM7B, a broadcast/studio dynamic mic. Not currently owned — referenced as a comparison point to the owned [[Shure SM57]] (a "close cousin").

## What it's used for

- **Used as the sole vocal mic across an entire album — a habit/comfort choice over "fancier" alternatives, not a fidelity call.** Arlo Parks names it as her second must-have piece of gear on *Ambiguous Desire*: "that's the microphone we used for the entire project... I've tried so many other mics that are fancier or whatever and I just... I'm a creature of habit." → [[Arlo Parks Vocal Chain]] — Arlo Parks & Baird (Tape Notes), [[TN 183 Arlo Parks & Baird - Full Video Episode]]
- **A repeatable "performability beats fidelity" mic choice, used two ways on the same album.** Myles Smith / Peter Fenn: most ad-libs/BVs run through this "just because it sounds raw" (part of a broader raw-over-clean philosophy — one full song, "Solo," was tracked on an iPhone for the same reason); separately, Miles' entire *Dying Day* lead vocal ended up on this instead of their usual Neumann U87 purely because it let him sit back and relax into a more natural take. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]] — Myles Smith / Peter Fenn (Tape Notes), [[TN 184 Myles Smith & Peter Fenn - Full Video Episode]]
- Listed in Myles Smith / Peter Fenn's studio gear list (as the "SM7B"), no specific technique given. → [[TN 184 Myles Smith & Peter Fenn - Gear List]] — Tape Notes
- **Kevin Parker (Tame Impala) tracks his own lead vocals on it, held in hand while performing.** Chose it over a U47 clone specifically because he could hold and move with it naturally while singing — the U47 clone "sounded incredible" but he couldn't hold it, and that mattered more than the tonal upgrade. A performability-over-raw-tone mic-choice principle, not a tonal endorsement. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]], [[Tame Impala Vocal Chain]], [[TN 188 Tame Impala - Full Video Episode (Part 1)]] (Tape Notes)

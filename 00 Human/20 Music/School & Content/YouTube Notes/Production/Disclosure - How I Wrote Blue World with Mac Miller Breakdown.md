# Disclosure - How I Wrote 'Blue World' with Mac Miller Breakdown

Guy tells the story of "Blue World" (Mac Miller, *Circles*) — an experimental, off-grid boom-bap beat built from a **Four Freshmen** jazz-acapella sample, written in a day with Mac at Capitol in 2016. One of his proudest, simplest beats ("five things in harmony"). Best material: **sampling what has meaning to you** (chopping a vintage vocal into a new chord pattern), the **Neve-EQ high-boost** as the go-to air move, the **gramophone intro mixed deliberately low to trick listeners into turning it up**, the **ride cymbal built from a flanger**, **~40% parallel drum compression to keep it open**, and the recurring **double-sidechain / "pick one sub" bass philosophy**. Whisper transcript (no captions) — high confidence.

## Techniques

### Sampling & the beat
- **Sample what has *meaning* to you, not what's "undone."** Guy sampled the **Four Freshmen** (30s/40s jazz-acapella pioneers — advanced jazz harmony in barbershop; Brian Wilson's #1 influence → Beach Boys → Beatles), bought ~30 of their records. "I'll sample anything meaningful; you're unlikely to chop it the same way as anyone else." → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].
- **Chop a vintage vocal sample into a *new* chord pattern.** Took snippets of the acapella, mapped three chords across the bottom of the keyboard, **played them in like a drummer** (super off-grid/loose), then adjusted *note lengths* for the rhythm. The sample's vibrato is built-in. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[BED Slot]].
- **Keep it to ~5 elements in harmony** — drums, bass, a couple of synths, and the sample; the simplicity *is* the beat. Day-one demo ≈ the final ("I really didn't touch it, just gave it a mix + a few drum fills"). → arrangement.

### Arrangement / loudness psychology
- **Gramophone intro mixed *deliberately low* to trick listeners into cranking the volume.** A lo-fi tape/film-projector sample, all lows cut, placed very quiet ("oh it's quiet / not enough bass" → they turn it up) so the drop *slams*. Wanted the intro to feel like an old gramophone in the corner of a room. → [[Clipping and Loudness]], [[Depth and Space]].
- **Return to that lo-fi feeling in the post-chorus** — push the vocal's high-pass up to strip the lows and bring the tape noise back, so it feels like snapping "back to the 40s, then back to now." → [[Automation]].

### Sample-chord processing (the air chain)
- **Neve 1073 EQ = crank the highs; that's its best use.** "The high boost on a Neve is so smooth, natural, and human." Guy barely uses Neve EQ to cut — he reaches for it to *lift highs* (and sometimes roll a little bass on vocals). → [[EQ]].
- Full chain: Logic EQ (notch weird resonances + brighten) → **Neve 1073 (crank highs)** → 1176LN (light leveling) → **[[Gem Dopamine]]** (the heavy lifting — super bright) → [[Oxford Inflator]] → pan the outer chops L/R (center + slight-L + slight-R) → Pro-Q2 (filter automation) → delay + Logic bright-chamber reverb (automated to bring out long notes, adds stereo + helps the swing) → [[Soothe2]] (final resonances) → sidechain to kick+clap → Chorus + UAD reverb buses. **Octave-up send to *find air* in a low-fi 5th-hand-vinyl voice.** → [[AIR Slot]].

### Synths
- Chords (D♯m7add9 · B♭maj9 · Cm7/B♭ · F♯dim) on the **classic Disclosure sine vocoder synth** (stock Logic) + a **Waves Rhodes** (pre-Keyscape — less authentic, but fine when blended into a wash of sines/chorus/delay) + a Logic sawtooth on simplified triads. **[[EXS24]] with *no sample* = sine beeps** via a plucky envelope (0 ms attack / 58 ms decay), tremolo/pan/delay, velocity-for-volume so the top note self-compresses. → [[Sound Selection Guide]].
- **Choose a delay to sit *behind* the beat** — the Rhodes H-delay is set late/off-beat on purpose to lock against the hats. → [[Delay]].

### Bass (subtle, 2 layers) — kept low so Mac + sample lead
- **Ample J-bass with all sub rolled off (mid/low-mid harmonics only) + a Logic ESP square-wave sub** (octaves, plucky), overdriven, ~20 Hz roll-off. The played bass gives "feel," the ESP gives weight; Saturn tape glues them so they interact. → [[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]].
- **Double-sidechain the bass + "pick one sub."** One comp on the whole signal (light, ~5 dB) + a **multiband ducking *only the sub* with kick+clap (~70 dB)**, letting the rest through. Philosophy: don't let the kick's sub and the bass's sub clash — "wasted energy you can't perceive; pick one — punchy kick *or* subby bass, sidechain the other's sub out." The sidechain also **glues** (drums pull the bass, squash the pads — "everything holding hands"). → [[Sidechaining]], [[Multiband Compression]], [[SUB Slot]].

### Drums (his favorite — off-grid boom-bap)
- **Heavily off-grid/random placement** ("looks like the Matrix — no one's on the grid"), flams, classic boom-bap. Custom kit; hats ~⅓ off the beat and claps *ahead* for swing. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].
- **Kick:** skippy late garage-kick sample + a battery layer (noise/air on top); heavy **Logic Overdrive** (does most of the grit) + FabFilter EQ (cut highs, boost lows) + **DBX 160** ("my favorite comp for kick — punch, big transient"). Clap: 4 dB overdrive + 3k cut + DBX 160. → [[Kick Design]], [[Compression]].
- **Build a ride cymbal's character out of a flanger** — a 909 ride through a loaded Flanger; the flanger *is* the bulk of the sound (crank it for a build). → [[Modulation]].
- **Drum bus:** tape machine (saturation, in the red but kept relatively clean — the pure Four Freshmen sample doesn't want stomping) → **parallel compression ~40%** keyed to the kick (keep it open/dynamic) → [[Soothe2]] (ride/clap/tambourine resonances) → light limiter (leave the dynamics). → [[Parallel Processing]].

### Vocals (Mac Miller)
- **Frank Sinatra's Capitol U-47 → Neve preamp → Tube-Tech → Pro Tools** (engineer Ira) — sounded great straight out of the box. Chain: Tube-Tech (match the tracking, a touch more comp) → EQ (roll sub rumble — his sub bled from the next room; brighten a hair) → light de-ess → **Saturn clean-tube at 20%** (harmonics/smooth — full distortion would be cool but wrong for the vibe) → [[Oxford Inflator]] → Echo-Boy (low ⅛-note delay, widened) → low Valhalla reverb. Minimal. → [[2 Main Vocal Group|Main Vocal Group]], [[LEAD Slot]].
- **BVs:** same bus, cut more low-mids (higher notes); hard-pan + quarter-pan. Mac wove the sample into his lyric ("without you it's a blue world / the color blue") — a sign he'd locked into the beat. → [[Width and Stereo]].
- *Note:* stems went to the *Circles* producer (John Congleton); some reverbs/delays and the intro tape-noise may differ on the released version.

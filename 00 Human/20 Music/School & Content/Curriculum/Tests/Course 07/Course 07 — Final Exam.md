>
> **Q13.** **The friction is the sound · blues isn't functional major/minor · the note is a region, not a pitch**
>
> **Q14.** **Rock took the form and the pentatonic wholesale · hip hop inherited it through sampling**
> Soul and funk records built on blues harmony were sampled, so the vocabulary survives even where the 12-bar doesn't.
>
> **Q15.** **The dominant-7th tonic · the blues scale over everything · the circling form**
> *Rubric (5):* names three consequences (2) · shows the connection for at least two of them (2) · frames all three as following from the same premise rather than as separate facts (1)
> Because there's no functional grammar in play, the tonic chord doesn't need to be a stable triad — it can be a dominant 7th and simply sit there, since nothing is obliged to resolve. Because the scale isn't derived from the chords, one scale can be laid over all three of them and the resulting clashes aren't derivation errors. And because no chord in the form is a genuine point of arrival, the twelve bars circle rather than close, which is why they need a turnaround to hand the form back to bar 1. One premise, three consequences.
>
> **Q16.** **They've correctly identified the clash and wrongly assumed the framework that makes it an error**
> *Rubric (5):* says the semitone collision between ♭3 and ♮3 is real and correctly heard (2) · says functional harmony is the wrong framework to judge blues by (2) · says the friction is the intended sound rather than a mistake (1)
> They're not hearing things — the ♭3 genuinely sits a semitone from the chord's major 3rd, and inside functional harmony that's a dissonance wanting resolution. What they're missing is that the framework doesn't apply. Blues doesn't derive its scale from its chords, so there's no rule being broken, and the grind between the two thirds is the sound the idiom is built to produce rather than a byproduct to be cleaned up. Add that players bend between the two pitches deliberately, and the "wrong note" turns out to be the most characteristic thing in the solo.

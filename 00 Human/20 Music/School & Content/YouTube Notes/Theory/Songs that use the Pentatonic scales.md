# Songs that use the Pentatonic scales

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGpUscFY9RA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGpUscFY9RA)

## Synopsis

Argues the pentatonic scale is not a "beginner guitarist" scale but arguably the most versatile scale in music — explaining precisely why it can't clash with anything (it has no semitones), demonstrating it on piano's black keys, and tracing its use as a cross-cultural universal from Chinese and Hindustani classical music through Western rock, blues, and folk.

## Techniques

- **Why the pentatonic scale is "foolproof": it's the parent major/minor scale with its two tension-generating semitones removed.** Major pentatonic = major scale minus the 4th and 7th degrees; minor pentatonic = natural minor minus its own two semitone-adjacent degrees. Those degrees are removed specifically because a semitone-away note strongly pulls the ear toward resolution (4th wants to fall to the 3rd, 7th wants to rise to the octave) — with them gone, no note in the scale creates strong dissonance against a diatonic chord, so a pentatonic melody can be played freely over any chord in the key and reliably sound acceptable.
- **A direct practical consequence: pentatonic melodies are unusually easy to reharmonize**, since the melody notes don't lock in a specific harmonic reading the way a full 7-note scale's tension tones would — cited as part of why "Uptown Funk" (Bruno Mars) reharmonizes cleanly.
- **The piano's black keys ARE a pentatonic scale** (the same relationship as "all white keys = C major"): depending on chosen root, they form either G♭ major pentatonic or E♭ minor pentatonic — meaning any combination of black keys played together is guaranteed consonant, since none of pentatonic's semitone-adjacent tension pairs exist among them. Demonstrated with the Boomtown Rats' "I Don't Like Mondays" intro, a literal downward sweep across the black keys.
- **The pentatonic scale functions as a near-universal cross-cultural "default" musical vocabulary, not a Western guitar convention**: Chinese traditional music's gong/yu scales map directly onto Western major/minor pentatonic; the Chinese guqin's open strings are typically tuned to major pentatonic; in Hindustani classical music, the Bilaval raga matches major pentatonic and the Kafi/Dhaivat-based raga matches minor pentatonic (source audio names "Bali rag"/"Dahini rag," likely a caption-garbled raga name — treat the specific raga names as approximate, the scale-mapping claim as the confirmed point). Demonstrated with Indian musician Aruna Sairam singing the identical pentatonic melody in Chinese-, Hindustani-, and Western-flavored vocal styles, and Bobby McFerrin's crowd-participation "Don't Worry Be Happy" bit, where different global audiences reliably land on the same pentatonic intervals by ear regardless of musical background.
- **One real limitation, stated honestly rather than glossed over**: the pentatonic scale is excellent for melody but poor for harmony — chords built purely from pentatonic-scale notes are limited (fewer usable triads/extensions than a full 7-note scale offers), which is why virtually every cited pentatonic-melody example is harmonized with chords that draw notes from OUTSIDE the pentatonic set. A song that stays 100% pentatonic both melodically and harmonically is rare in Western music for exactly this reason.

## Catalog (pentatonic-melody song examples cited)

Ed Sheeran, "Shape of You" (C minor pentatonic, melody) · Elton John, "Your Song" (E♭ major pentatonic, with occasional ornamental non-pentatonic notes) · Red Hot Chili Peppers, "Under the Bridge" (verse + chorus entirely pentatonic) · Queen, "We Will Rock You" (verse, E–A pentatonic) · Beatles, "Things We Said Today" (almost entirely pentatonic except one blue note) · The Temptations, "My Girl" (melody AND the opening guitar riff, a literal climb up C major pentatonic) · Black Sabbath, "Paranoid" (riff, E minor pentatonic) · Led Zeppelin, "Whole Lotta Love" (riff, minor pentatonic) · "Amazing Grace," "Auld Lang Syne," "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" (traditional folk/spiritual, pentatonic melodies) · Bobby McFerrin, "Don't Worry, Be Happy" (100% pentatonic melody).

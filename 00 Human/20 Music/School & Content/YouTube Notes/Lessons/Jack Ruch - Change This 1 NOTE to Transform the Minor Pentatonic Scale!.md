# Jack Ruch — Change This 1 NOTE to Transform the Minor Pentatonic Scale!

## Synopsis

Jack Ruch (blues/jazz guitar teacher, previously sourced in this vault for [[Dominant 7 Arpeggios Over Blues Pentatonics]] and amp/pedal tone-dialing content) teaches a single, high-leverage scale edit: the **minor 6 pentatonic scale**, made by dropping the ♭7 of the standard minor pentatonic down a half step to the major 6. He credits the sound to **Albert Collins and B.B. King**, both known for leaning on the major-6-over-minor-pentatonic color in blues lines, and frames it as a low-effort way to add real variety to vocabulary a player already knows. Unlike a chord-tracking device, this isn't triggered by a specific chord change — it's a static alternate color choice over a dominant/blues backing, usable on its own or blended freely with straight minor pentatonic. The teaching method is entirely paired-comparison: five lick examples, each played first in straight minor pentatonic (in the 10th-fret D minor box, then one position down), then replayed identically with every ♭7 swapped for the major 6, so the ear and the hand register the edit directly against the familiar version. Ruch also flags the practical fingering cost — swapping ♭7 for major 6 in a box shape often forces new index-to-pinky stretches where the original lick used index-to-ring, which he frames as a useful side effect: it breaks fixed pentatonic muscle memory even when the player isn't consciously chasing that goal. Video closes with a plug for his paid "Melodic Blues Soloing" course (context only, no additional technique extracted from it).

## What's taught

- **The core edit:** minor pentatonic (root, ♭3, 4, 5, ♭7) → minor 6 pentatonic (root, ♭3, 4, 5, 6) by dropping the ♭7 a half step to the major 6. Worked in D minor: C (♭7) → B (major 6). Full theory and all five worked licks (paired minor-pentatonic-then-minor-6 versions) are on the new technique note [[Minor 6 Pentatonic Scale]] — this page is the master record, that page is the reusable extraction.
- **Sourcing:** heard from **Albert Collins** and **B.B. King**, cited as players who "lean heavily on that major 6 interval" in their blues lines.
- **Harmonic context:** demonstrated over a backing track voicing the ♭7 and major 3rd (a dominant/blues-type chord) — a context where the minor 3rd is already commonly bent toward the chord's major 3rd; the minor-6 swap is offered as a complementary color choice in the same harmonic setting, not a replacement for that bend.
- **The tritone hook:** bending the minor 3rd while playing minor-6 pentatonic briefly sounds the minor-3rd-to-major-6 tritone — called out explicitly as the "unique sound" that makes the substitution worth reaching for on purpose.
- **Five paired lick examples**, each demonstrating the swap on a different phrase shape/position (10th-fret box, then one position down), including a hammer-on/pull-off embellishment, a string-jump move (minor 3rd–4th–5th), a finger-roll technique for grip/control on a bendable note, and one lick that also incorporates the 9th as a passing color. Full breakdown of all five on [[Minor 6 Pentatonic Scale]].
- **Blend, don't replace:** explicitly stated that minor pentatonic and minor-6 pentatonic can and should be freely combined once both are under the fingers, rather than treated as an either/or scale choice.
- **Fingering/muscle-memory side effect:** because the ♭7 and major 6 sit a half step apart but often want different fretting fingers in a box shape, the minor-6 version of a lick frequently forces an index-to-pinky stretch where the original used index-to-ring — flagged as a useful way to break fixed pentatonic habits even independent of the tonal change.
- **Practice framing:** take licks a player already knows and already may have "gotten bored of," and mechanically drop every ♭7 in them to the major 6 — a generalizable transform-your-existing-vocabulary method, not a fixed set of licks to memorize verbatim.
- **Sponsor content flagged, not extracted as technique:** mid/end-video plug for Ruch's paid "Melodic Blues Soloing" course (50% off at time of posting) — noted for context only.

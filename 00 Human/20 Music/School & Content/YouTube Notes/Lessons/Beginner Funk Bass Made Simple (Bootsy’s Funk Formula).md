# Beginner Funk Bass Made Simple (Bootsy’s Funk Formula)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0gDK98JVAnQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0gDK98JVAnQ)

## Synopsis

A beginner-targeted BassBuzz lesson that distills a formula Josh credits directly to **Bootsy Collins** (James Brown's JBs, Parliament-Funkadelic, Bootsy's Rubber Band) into two teachable pieces: **"everything is on the one"** — lock a big root note to beat one and almost anything played in between will resolve and feel good — and a movable four-note **"funk box" shape** (root, 5th, ♭7, octave) for filling that in-between space with notes that are guaranteed to work over a major or minor chord alike. The video is upfront that this isn't the only way to sound funky, just a reliable, theory-free formula for the specific James Brown/Parliament sound. Structurally it's a felt-demonstration-first lesson (Josh has the viewer feel the difference between hitting and not hitting the one before explaining why), then a graduated play-along build of a real funk groove in D — rhythm and plucking pattern first, then notes, then slow/medium/full-speed play-alongs, then a live "funk boxing" improvisation exercise where the viewer plays their own fills over Josh laying down the one. This is the vault's first dedicated "on the one" note and its first documented movable shape beyond the existing root-fifth/octave/triad vocabulary — closing a gap two other BassBuzz notes already in the vault ([[Locking In With the Drummer]], [[The 10 Rules of Bass Playing (That Pros Break)]]) had flagged by name without being able to link.

## What's taught

### 1. "Everything is on the one" — the anchor concept

- **The felt demonstration:** play a root note (D, 5th fret of the A string) on beat one against a drum groove, then noodle freely with fills and "weird stuff" in between. As long as the one keeps landing, the groove holds and "it all resolved and felt good." Stop hitting the one, and the groove immediately reads as unstable — direct proof that the one is the load-bearing element, not whatever fills the space around it.
- **Bootsy's own quote, relayed by Josh:** "everything is on the one... it's the rhythm of life... you got to count 1 2 3 bang." You can play anything in the space between ones, "but always bang bring it back so that people can be right with you."
- **The classic James Brown/Parliament gesture:** the drummer hits a big crash on beat one and the bass player hits a big, loud root note in the same instant — sometimes only every two bars rather than every bar.
- **Explicit caveat:** "what Bootsy doesn't really talk about is that this isn't the only way to sound funky" — it's a reliable formula for one specific sound (the James Brown/Parliament sound), not a universal law of funk.
- Full write-up, plus its cross-links to the vault's two prior unlinked "Bootsy Collins/the one" references: [[Bootsy's Funk Formula — On the One and the Funk Box Shape]] § *"Everything is on the one" — the anchor concept*.

### 2. The funk box shape — a movable root/5th/♭7/octave run

- **Geometry:** starting note (root); up one string + up 2 frets (5th); up two strings, same fret as the root (♭7); up two strings + up 2 frets from the root (octave). Works "almost all the time whether it's a major chord or a minor chord" — no need to know the chord's quality first.
- **Worked in the video's own key, D** (root on the 5th fret of the A string): 5th = A (D string, 7th fret), ♭7 = C (G string, 5th fret), octave = D (G string, 7th fret).
- **How to use it on your own:** find the root by ear or by asking a bandmate, hit the root on the one, then fill the rest of the bar with any funk box note(s) placed wherever feels good rhythmically — same permissive "no wrong answer" framing as the felt demonstration above.
- Full geometry, the worked example, and how it relates to the vault's existing root-fifth/octave shape vocabulary: [[Bootsy's Funk Formula — On the One and the Funk Box Shape]] § *The funk box shape*.

### 3. Fingering and plucking pattern for the funk box

- **Fretting:** index finger on the root and ♭7 (same fret), pinky on the 5th and octave (two frets up) — an index-pinky-index-pinky pattern that mirrors the shape's own geometry.
- **Plucking:** the same index-pinky-index-pinky pattern on the plucking hand, rather than the usual index-middle default — a shape-specific variant worth treating as its own tool alongside the vault's existing index-middle plucking vocabulary.
- **Underlying groove rhythm** (root note only, before the funk box notes are added): a syncopated, "and"-count-heavy pattern plucked with standard alternating index-middle, resetting back to index at the top of each phrase. The spoken counting-and-fingering callouts here are dense and partially hard to parse from captions (a fast-spoken, music-under-talk passage) — captured qualitatively rather than as an exact hit-by-hit sequence; see the note below for the confidence flag.
- Full detail: [[Bootsy's Funk Formula — On the One and the Funk Box Shape]] § *Fingering and plucking pattern*, and the plucking-hand cross-reference on [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]].

### 4. Building your own funk groove — the practical recipe

1. Find the root (by ear or by asking a bandmate).
2. Hit the root hard on beat one — every bar, or every two bars for the fuller crash-and-hit.
3. Fill the rest of the bar with funk box notes, placed wherever feels good rhythmically.
4. The one fixed rule: always resolve back to the one.
- Demonstrated live as a call-and-response improvisation exercise: Josh lays down the one on a drum-track play-along while the viewer freely "funk boxes" fills in the space between, with the only stated constraint being "it has to feel good to you." Full recipe: [[Bootsy's Funk Formula — On the One and the Funk Box Shape]] § *Practical application*.

## Synopsis
Opening lesson of Underbelly's "You Suck at Drums" course: before writing a single drum pattern, get a working grip on beats, bars, and time signatures. Covers what a beat and a bar actually are, how to read the two stacked numbers of a time signature (beats-per-bar and which note value gets the beat), and — the most useful part for a working producer — a repeatable by-ear method for figuring out an unfamiliar song's time signature from listening alone. Framed as absolute basics but genuinely useful as a refresher, and sets up the vocabulary ("downbeat," "the *and* of a beat," "quarter/eighth/sixteenth note") the rest of the drum series leans on.

## Techniques
- **A beat is a regular pulse; a bar (measure) is a container for a musical idea** — same relationship as a clock's tick (beat) vs. a full lap of the clock face (bar); a bar is the space a bassline, chord progression, or melody gets to live in.
- **The downbeat is the first, strongest beat of a bar** — audible on a metronome as a higher-pitched click; every other beat in the bar is metrically weaker by comparison.
- **Reading a time signature's top number: beats per bar.** Changing it (4 → 5, or up to 99) changes how many beats are grouped into each bar/cycle — demonstrated by ear: a metronome in 5 counts "1 2 3 4 5" per cycle instead of "1 2 3 4."
- **Reading a time signature's bottom number: which note value receives one beat** — only 2, 4, 8, or 16 are valid options; 4 = quarter note, 8 = eighth note. Changing 4/4 to 4/8 doesn't change the beat *count* per bar, it changes how much time each beat covers — two eighth notes fit in the space one quarter note used to occupy, so the same bar plays back twice as fast at the same tempo marking.
- **Right-click the piano roll to change the fixed grid** (quarter/eighth/sixteenth) to draw notes at the resolution you're currently counting in, and to see visually how many of a given note value fit inside one bar (four quarter notes = one bar in 4/4; two eighth notes = the space of one quarter note).
- **Ear-training method for identifying an unfamiliar song's time signature, step by step:** (1) find the bar length first — play the track and pause right before the main musical idea feels like it's about to repeat; if a riff spans multiple bars of literally the same content (e.g. a two-chord vamp), count only the smallest repeatable increment, not the full spanned length. (2) Count how many beats fit inside that span — that's the top number. (3) Decide the bottom number by how you naturally want to nod your head: nodding on *every* counted beat usually means a quarter note is receiving the beat (bottom number 4); wanting to skip every other counted beat (nodding roughly half as often) usually means an eighth note is receiving the beat (bottom number 8).
- **Applied twice in the video to verify the method** — a sample where the ear wants to nod on every one of 5 counted beats confirms 5/4; a guitar riff that first seems to span a longer idea turns out to be a 6-beat idea nodded at half-speed once the smallest-repeatable-increment rule is applied, confirming 6/8.

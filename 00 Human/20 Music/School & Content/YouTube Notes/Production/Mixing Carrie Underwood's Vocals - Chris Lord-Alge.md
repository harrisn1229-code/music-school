# Mixing Carrie Underwood's Vocals - Chris Lord-Alge

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HrXy9w6GnDA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HrXy9w6GnDA)

## Synopsis

Chris Lord-Alge works a Carrie Underwood lead vocal in context against the track, then extends the same starting treatment to her harmony and background vocals. Central technique: **"EQ into the compressor/limiter"** — boost aggressively before a limiter so the limiter's own gain reduction tames the resulting peaks, producing excitement without harshness.

## What's taught

- **Never judge a vocal solo — get the track sounding right first, then bring the vocal in and evaluate the fit.** The vocal EQ/compression decisions are made entirely by ear against the full track, never in isolation, since a vocal heard alone never tells you how much treatment it actually needs in context.
- **"EQ into the compressor/limiter" — boost specific resonant frequencies aggressively BEFORE the limiter stage, letting the limiter's gain reduction tame and control the resulting peaks instead of fighting them separately.** Concrete boosts used: ~2 kHz ("telephone" presence frequencies), ~4.5 kHz, and a high shelf/boost around 8-10 kHz for "shine" — all added ahead of a limiter so its compression reacts to and reins in what would otherwise be a harsh, over-hot EQ move, producing controlled excitement rather than raw brightness. Framed as a general principle applicable in any DAW: "EQ into the plugin, and let the plugin's character pull back some of the things you don't like about the EQ" — treat the compressor/limiter as part of the EQ decision, not a separate downstream stage.
- **Cut low end to counter proximity effect and avoid "woofing" once heavy compression is applied.** Bottom-end buildup from mic proximity effect gets worse once the vocal is compressed harder — cutting bass frequencies before/alongside the aggressive mid/high boosts keeps the combination from turning muddy.
- **Light parallel sends (slap delay + reverb), "seasoned to taste" rather than dialed heavy.** A short slap-delay pair plus a vocal reverb send are brought in at low, subtle amounts specifically to add three-dimensionality without an obviously wet/processed character — described explicitly as finding "a start point," implying further adjustment happens later in context.
- **Use an already-EQed lead vocal as the starting template for its own harmony parts, before evaluating each individually.** Once a lead vocal EQ sounds right, apply a similar EQ starting point to the same singer's vocal harmony (same mic, same recording chain) rather than starting each harmony track's EQ from a blank slate — efficient because they share a source and character.
- **Background vocals get a warmer, different limiter setting than the lead, with more midrange boost tolerated.** Because backgrounds sit further back in the mix than a lead vocal, a bigger midrange boost (~3 kHz cited) can be pushed on background vocals without it reading as harsh/"biting" the way the same boost would on a lead — the acceptable aggressiveness of an EQ move depends on the part's role in the mix, not a fixed rule.

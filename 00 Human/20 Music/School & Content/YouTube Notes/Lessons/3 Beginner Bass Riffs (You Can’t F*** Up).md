# 3 Beginner Bass Riffs (You Can’t F*** Up)

## Synopsis

An earlier, longer BassBuzz beginner-repertoire video (2019, 17:31 — the same format later re-run as the shorter, five-riff [[5 Beginner Bass Riffs (You Can’t F*** Up)]]) built around three real, recognizable riffs, each taught rhythm-and-plucking-pattern first and fretted notes second so a beginner isn't juggling both at once, then drilled slow-with-Josh-only before a full-band play-along. The three: **Psycho Killer** (Talking Heads, bass by Tina Weymouth) for locking note-for-note with the drummer and controlling note length via a press/rest fretting-hand mute; **21 Guns** chorus (Green Day, bass by Mike Dirnt) for alternating index-middle plucking, "chugging" (continuous straight eighths), and — the standout teaching moment of the whole video — a fully demonstrated index-finger **finger roll** across adjacent strings at the same fret; and **Walk on the Wild Side** (Lou Reed, bass by Herbie Flowers) for slide technique, taught on *both* of the song's two overdubbed bass parts (a low upright line and a higher electric line) so two players can "duet" it, using a look-ahead-with-your-eyes trick to avoid getting lost mid-slide, plus a thumb-anchor mute for an all-on-one-string riff. Every riff gets real artist/bassist credit rather than being presented as an anonymous tab.

## What's taught

- **[~0:00] Framing.** Three real-song riffs, each targeting a different beginner skill — groove/note-length, plucking/chugging, and slides — taught rhythm-first, notes-second so the two layers don't have to be learned simultaneously.

- **[~0:20–4:53] Riff 1 — Psycho Killer (Talking Heads; bass: Tina Weymouth) — drummer lock-in + note-length control.**
  - **Rhythm (taught before any notes, on the open E string):** a repeating two-bar phrase — bar A is straight quarters (**1 2 3 4**), bar B adds an eighth-note pickup into the last beat (**1 2 3 & 4**). Thumb rests on the pickup; any plucking-hand pattern the player is comfortable with is fine at this stage — the drill is the rhythm, not a specific finger choice.
  - **Fretting, added second:** pinky on the **5th fret of the E string**, plucked seven times in a row, then the **open E string**, then the **3rd fret of the E string** with the index finger — the whole line is *pinky ×7 → open → index*, looping back to the top.
  - **Note-length technique — press/rest on the repeated pinky note:** holding the 5th-fret note down through all seven plucks lets it ring together into a smear; the fix is to **release fretting pressure after every pluck while staying in contact with the string** (not lifting off entirely, which lets it ring open) — "press rest, press rest, press rest." This is exactly the **short mute** mechanic [[Bass Note Muting Technique]] already documents (its "string sitter" fix), now confirmed on a second real riff with the explicit press/rest phrasing.
  - **Why this riff is a good drummer-lock check:** because the bassline lands on **every single beat**, it's an unusually good real-world test of staying glued to the drummer through the whole bar, not just the easy downbeat — direct reinforcement of [[Locking In With the Drummer]]'s central "why beat 1 isn't enough" thesis. Explicit advice: open your ears to the band and check whether your plucks are landing in the pocket, not just whether the notes/rhythm are technically correct.
  - Slow play-along, then full-speed play-along.

- **[~4:53–11:03] Riff 2 — 21 Guns chorus (Green Day; bass: Mike Dirnt) — alternating plucking, chugging, and the finger roll.**
  - **Alternating plucking, drilled first on the open E string:** thumb on the pickup, strict index-middle alternation. First rhythm encountered in the chorus: **1 & 2, 3 & 4** → pluck-pluck-rest, pluck-pluck-rest (two eighth-note pairs per bar, each followed by a quarter rest) — index-middle on each pair, no repeats.
  - **Chugging, the second rhythm:** **straight eighth notes for the whole bar** (1&2&3&4&), alternating index-middle continuously with no rests, moving between root notes — called out as extremely common ("happens in a million songs"). Explicit plucking-hand form cue bundled in: **pull across, not up and away from the bass** — the same tone/muting-as-a-side-effect cue [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]] already documents from other BassBuzz beginner-lesson videos.
  - **Fretting for the full riff:** index (1st fret E) ×4 → shift up, pinky (7th fret A) ×4 → stay in position, index (5th fret A) → shift back down, pinky (3rd fret A) ×4 → index (1st fret A) ×4 → index (1st fret **E**, via the finger roll below) → pinky (3rd fret A), loop to top. Fingering shorthand: index–pinky–index–pinky–index–index(rolled)–pinky.
  - **The finger roll, demonstrated in full mechanical detail (a first for this vault):** the riff's back half asks for the 1st fret of the A string immediately followed by the 1st fret of the E string — same fret, adjacent string, no time to reposition the whole hand. Fix: fret the **A-string note on the back of the index finger's knuckle**, then **roll the pressure forward toward the fingertip** to land the **E-string note at the same fret** with the same finger, instead of physically jumping the fingertip between strings. Explicitly flagged as optional — jumping the fingertip works too, "just a little bit sloppier." **This fills a documented gap:** [[5 Beginner Bass Riffs (You Can’t F*** Up)]]'s "Under Pressure" section *names* an index-finger finger roll as an option but couldn't demonstrate the mechanics (gated behind BassBuzz's paid course in that video) — this video is the free-tier source for exactly that move, now written up in full on [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]].
  - **Note-length control during the shift:** same press/rest logic as Psycho Killer above, but plucking-hand-anchored this time — pluck the two notes, then **touch (don't press) the strings** with the fretting hand while shifting position, so nothing rings during the move; once the chugging section starts this isn't needed since every note there is meant to be long and continuous.
  - Slow play-along, then full-speed play-along.

- **[~11:03–16:31] Riff 3 — Walk on the Wild Side (Lou Reed; bass: Herbie Flowers, both parts) — slide tracking and single-string muting.**
  - **Setup:** the song actually has *two* overdubbed bass parts, a low upright line and a higher electric line, both played by session bassist Herbie Flowers — BassBuzz teaches both so two players can "duet" the song together.
  - **Low part — fret-and-hold slide, index finger only:** 8th fret of the E string, slide **down** to the 1st fret, then slide back **up** to the 8th fret, repeating. Mechanic: **keep fretting pressure on continuously through the whole slide** in both directions — release partway and it breaks the glide (same "full contact, don't over-press" principle [[Bass Slide Technique]] already documents).
  - **The look-ahead fix for getting lost, worked reciprocally in both directions:** move your **eyes** to the destination fret *before* moving your fingers there. Coming down: look at the 1st fret while still fretting the 8th. Going back up: look at the 8th fret while still fretting the 1st ("I move my eyes now, and then shift, and I move my eyes again"). Stated bluntly: "if your eyes aren't ahead of your fingers, crazy stuff's gonna happen." This is the identical eyes-lead-the-hand principle [[Look-Ahead Position Shifting]] and [[Bass Slide Technique]] both already document for fretting shifts and slides respectively — worth noting here that it's demonstrated **reciprocally**, both directions of the same slide, not just the one-way case those notes currently carry.
  - **High part — a finger-switch slide, a genuinely new slide variant for this vault:** index finger on the **9th fret of the G string**, slide **up** to the **14th fret** (same finger), then the **pinky** grabs the **17th fret**, slides back **down**, and switches to the **index** finger, which lands back on the **9th fret**. Fingering: pinky, index, index, index, pinky, index. Unlike every slide previously documented on [[Bass Slide Technique]] (all fret-and-hold on one finger the whole way), this slide **changes fretting fingers partway through** — pinky sliding down hands off to index rather than staying on one finger. Explicitly reassured as a non-issue: there's a small gap in the glide right at the finger switch, but "you can't really hear that in context" — don't worry about it.
  - **Muting for an all-on-one-string riff:** because the entire high part sits on the G string, the lower strings can ring unwantedly underneath it. Fix: **anchor the plucking-hand thumb on the A string, angled so it also touches the E string** (muting both at once), and let **pulling across** (not up and away) on each pluck automatically mute the D string as a side effect. This is the exact **thumb mute (fix 1) + pull-across/rest-stroke (fix 2)** combo [[Bass Note Muting Technique]] already documents in the abstract, now confirmed as a real worked example on a genuinely single-string riff.
  - Slow play-along for both parts, then full-speed play-along for both.

- **[~16:31–end] Closing.** Recap of the three skills earned: alternating-pluck-and-chug, keeping notes short, and not getting lost in slides. Standard subscribe/bell CTA.

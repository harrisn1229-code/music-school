# Lenno: Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)

## Synopsis
Lenno walks through a fast diagnostic method for figuring out exactly what's different between a mix and a reference track when a full-range A/B isn't giving useful answers: split both into narrow frequency bands with EQ and compare within just that band, rather than the whole spectrum at once, to pin down which element actually owns a given frequency range. He demos it on the low end (kick vs. bass), then extends the same isolation logic to fixing the identified problem — using a cheap transient designer on the specific narrow band that's lacking, instead of just turning the whole element up and risking mud or distortion. The video closes with two shorter but pointed opinions: monitor EQ'd to your own taste beats chasing "flat," and LUFS obsession at the mix stage is largely a waste of energy — that's the mastering engineer's job, and the track is going to get re-compressed by streaming/radio/car playback anyway.

## Techniques
- **Multiband referencing: split reference and mix into narrow bands (e.g. 200 Hz–1 kHz, 1 kHz–5 kHz, up to ~5 kHz–20 kHz) with EQ, then A/B within just that band instead of full-range** — solo the reference (turn off any compression on it) against your mix through the same narrow band and switch back and forth; isolating the comparison this tightly makes differences audible that a full-range A/B buries. → [[Masking and Carving]] — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
- **The narrow-band A/B can reveal which element actually carries a frequency range, not the one you assumed** — worked example: a mix seems to have a weak low end, but banded referencing shows the bass (not the kick) is carrying most of the low-end weight, or vice versa — a full-range comparison hides which culprit it actually is. — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
- **Isolate the issue "as fine-grained as possible" instead of just turning the weak element up** — if a kick reads too low in the mix and you simply raise its fader, the fix often makes everything "distorted and mushy" instead of fixing the actual problem; narrowing the diagnosis to a specific band first avoids that blunt-instrument mistake. — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
- **Once a narrow band shows a reference has more click/transient energy than your mix, add punch with a cheap transient designer centered on that band rather than raising the whole element's level** — e.g. if the reference's kick has noticeably more transient click in the 200 Hz–1 kHz zone, band a transient designer to that rough area and push punch there specifically, which avoids messing up overall low-end balance the way a blanket level increase would. Cheap transient-designer plugins are usually $10–29. → [[Transient Master]] — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
- **Always judge the fix in context of the whole mix against the full reference track, not just the isolated band** — after dialing in a band-targeted move, switch back to comparing the complete mix vs. the complete reference to confirm the change actually reads as intended impact, not just a fix that looks right in isolation. — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
- **This is a shortcut, not a replacement for developed ears — framed explicitly as "years to be able to do it confidently" made faster** — banded referencing and targeted transient shaping are positioned as a way to get to the crux of a mix issue quicker, not a substitute for the years of listening experience that make full-range judgment reliable on their own. — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
- **EQ your monitoring to your own taste — there's no such thing as truly flat, and everyone hears differently** — don't be afraid to put an EQ on the master and boost the lows until it feels good on your system; obsessing over "flatness" in a monitoring discussion misses that nothing is genuinely flat and every listener's hearing and taste differ, so tuning toward what feels good to you personally is the more honest target. — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
- **A reference track you actually like on average speakers beats a "neutral" system you don't enjoy listening on** — if what genuinely sounds good to you doesn't sound good to you, you have no honest yardstick; favorite music you know and love is a better reference point than headphones whose sound you don't even like. → [[Referencing and Translation]] — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
- **Stop obsessing over LUFS at the mix stage — that's the mastering engineer's job, not yours** — the priority should be making the track feel good, not hitting a specific number on a loudness meter; chasing a LUFS target while mixing is treated as largely beside the point. — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
- **Still glance at a loudness meter, but only to see how much headroom is being eaten, not to hit a specific LUFS value** — smashing the mix bus is useful purely as a way to gauge whether too much headroom is getting consumed; the meter reading itself isn't the goal. → [[Clipping and Loudness]] — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)
- **The track gets re-compressed by playback anyway, so smashing dynamics to chase a LUFS number doesn't make it sound better** — even a mix that avoids getting crushed in mastering will still pick up more compression once it's heard through radio, a car system, or streaming playback, so a lot of the "loud" work is happening downstream regardless of what's targeted at the mix stage. — Lenno (Sol State), [Use Multiband Referencing To Fix Mix Problems](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QQU2RDzoFQA)

# the secrets behind mike dean's 'rodeo' production!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9FhndW8t74)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9FhndW8t74)

## Synopsis

loner remakes three beats inspired by three different songs off Travis Scott's *Rodeo* (2015), reverse-engineering Mike Dean's (and Metro Boomin's, and Southside/Sonny Digital's) production choices track by track: the intro ("Pornography"-style guitar-led beat produced by Mike Dean, Metro Boomin, and Sonny Digital), a moodier ambient beat inspired by "Impossible," and a melodic ambient beat inspired by the second half of "Oh My Dis Side." The throughline across all three is the *Rodeo* sound formula — a dry element (guitar, pad, bass) pushed through a stack of coloration effects (detuning, saturation, tape, distortion) until it's woozy and psychedelic, classic Atlanta trap drum programming laid under it, and Mike Dean-style synth "ear candy" and outro sections layered on top once the main song's job is done. Most valuable here: the specific multi-stage effects chain on the guitar (autotune → tape/cassette → EQ → delay → radiator/trash → half-time automation), the "hybrid" boom-bap/trap drum programming Travis's records use (kick layering, toms doubling as snares), the named Travis Scott two-chord progression (v–VI in a minor scale), and the repeated principle that Mike Dean's outro/ear-candy synth sections deliberately fill the space vocals aren't using.

## Techniques

**Intro-style track (Mike Dean / Metro Boomin / Sonny Digital):**
- **Guitar as the dark, psychedelic core element** — the intro's main element is a single eerie, repetitive electric guitar line played by Mike Dean; recorded/played dry, all the "Rodeo sound" comes from what's stacked on top of it afterward, not the performance itself.
- **The Mike Dean guitar-woozing chain, in order:** (1) auto-tune on the guitar itself for detuning (an instrument, not a vocal, run through pitch correction to create a warped, slightly-off pitch character — see [[Auto-Tune Pro]]); (2) a tape/cassette stage (AmpliTube "a Reels" + a "Sketch Cassette"-style plugin) for detuning + saturation/tape warmth (owned equivalent: [[Cassette]]); (3) an EQ cutting the high end down (owned: [[EQ Eight]]); (4) a delay, described as "very important in achieving that woozy Travis Scott sound" (owned: [[Delay]]); (5) a Radiator-style distortion stage plus iZotope Trash for extra grit (owned Radiator equivalent: [[Radiator]]; Trash is unowned — see wishlist); (6) half-time automation applied on top to add to the psychedelic feel. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Delay]] (effect)
- **808/synth bass: Metro's "Spin808"** — the synth-bass/808 element on the intro track used a plugin called (per captions) "Spin[z]808"; not confidently identified/owned, flagged for the wishlist rather than guessed at.
- **Leave a gap in the 808 loop specifically to drop in a Mike-Dean-style synth** — after programming the 808 pattern, a deliberate gap was left at the end of the loop, filled afterward with a dark/ambient analog synth pad (from an "Analog Lab" preset bank) — arranging space for a second melodic element before writing it, rather than layering blind.
- **"Ear candy" textural synths at the end of a track** — once the main beat elements are locked, Mike Dean-style records add 1–2 extra textural synth layers purely as ear candy: one gritty/aggressive texture that fills otherwise-empty mid-range frequencies, and one more ambient/atmospheric texture layered underneath it. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Classic Metro-style drum programming, hi-hats manually shifted off-grid** — after building the drum kit, some hi-hat notes are deliberately nudged off the quantized grid by hand for extra bounce/groove rather than left perfectly on-grid. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]]
- **Pull the hero element out to make room for the vocalist** — once Travis's vocal is due to enter, the guitar (the track's main element up to that point) is dropped out entirely to open up space for him, and a new section is built underneath with a different bassline + one more synth texture to keep momentum without competing with the vocal. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **The Mike Dean outro-synth-section formula, used to close nearly every Travis Scott album track** — because there's no vocal to leave room for, an outro section can push synth density much further than the verse/hook sections: (1) a pad with a long, deliberately winding chord progression that "goes down the scale and then back up again" for a suspenseful feeling; (2) a synth bass chosen for aggressive character (used preset: "Cage Beast"); (3) a synth brass part that follows the top note of each chord (not a fixed line — tracks the harmony). → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

**"Impossible"-inspired track:**
- **A super-detuned, atmospheric pad as the whole track's foundation** — mood-first writing: pick a heavily detuned pad sound first, then write the chord progression around how it feels rather than the reverse.
- **The Travis Scott two-chord progression: v and VI of any minor scale.** Named explicitly as "a great progression to know" for starting a Travis Scott-style beat quickly — the fifth and sixth chords of a minor scale, looped. → [[Chord Progressions]]
- **A bright, ambient bell-type sound layered alongside the pad** for extra texture on top of the main progression.
- **Plucky synth bass with a simple pattern, dressed up with delay** for bounce — the pattern itself is described as "super simple," with the delay doing the work of making it feel more alive/moving.
- **Hi-hat programmed to follow the bass pattern** rather than a generic trap hat pattern — ties the hat rhythm directly to the bassline's rhythm.
- **Layered trap kick: a hard trap kick + a lighter top layer** — Rodeo's signature drum sound blends modern trap programming with old-school boom-bap; the kick specifically is built from two layers, a hard/aggressive trap kick underneath and a lighter, softer top layer on top. → [[Kick Design]]
- **Toms doing snare duty at certain points, boom-bap style** — inspired directly by how "Impossible" uses a Tom hit in place of a snare at certain points in the pattern; two Toms were used to build a small drum groove/fill around this idea. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **Finish the kit with a boom-bap snare + open hat** — after the trap-style kick/hat/tom groove, a boom-bap snare and open hat close out the pattern, reinforcing the "modern trap meets old boom-bap" hybrid the whole album leans on. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]]

**"Oh My Dis Side"-inspired track (second-half beat switch):**
- **A melodic, ambient beat built for a beat-switch/second-half section** — same chord progression as the earlier pad section, but the sound swapped to a softer sine-style pad for a gentler, more melodic feel in the second half.
- **A "stabby" piano layered on top**, called out as inspired by the piano approach on "90210" and "Apple Pie" — a rhythmically percussive, stabbed piano part rather than a sustained/legato one.
- **An arpeggiated piano added second-to-last** purely for high-end texture — arpeggiation used here as an ear-candy/texture device, not a lead melodic part.
- **A synth lead + drum fill as the transition device** between the two beat sections — laid a synth lead line in the gap between sections and a drum fill under it to bridge the switch.

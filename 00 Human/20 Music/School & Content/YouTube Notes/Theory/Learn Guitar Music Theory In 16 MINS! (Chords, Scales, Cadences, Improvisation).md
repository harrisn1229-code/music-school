## Synopsis

A full pre-production composition demo, not a standard "here's a scale" lesson — BERNTH writes two short pieces live (one in G harmonic minor, one in E Hungarian minor) to demonstrate two different entry points for composing in an exotic/uncommon minor scale: harmonizing the scale into seventh chords and building a cadence (chord-first), versus scanning a labeled scale diagram for distinctive interval shapes to build a riff from (riff-first). Includes drum/bass programming and take-recording workflow alongside the theory.

## What's taught

- **Method 1 (G harmonic minor, chord-first):** harmonize every scale degree with a stacked 3rd/5th/7th using only in-scale notes — G harmonic minor (G A B♭ C D E♭ F♯) yields i minMaj7, ii m7♭5, III maj7♯5 (augmented-major7), iv m7, V7 (dominant), VI maj7, vii°7 (fully diminished). Build a progression/cadence from a subset of these, favoring the harmonic-minor-signature colors: the **i minMaj7** and the **III augmented-major7** (dissonant but scale-derived, not random).
- **Fast minMaj7 voicing trick:** a power chord with the octave doubled on top, move just that top note down a half step to the major 7th — one-finger move to the minMaj7 sound.
- **Augmented-triad symmetry, applied compositionally:** the same augmented shape moves cleanly in major 3rds (same notes, same fingering) — used to generate a chord run by sliding one voicing.
- **Method 2 (E Hungarian minor, riff-first):** E Hungarian minor = E F♯ G A♯ B C D♯ (harmonic minor with an added raised 4th). Skip chord-building; instead scan a full-neck scale diagram that labels intervals (not just fingering dots) for distinctive recurring shapes — a near-chromatic run, symmetric perfect 4ths, major 3rds a half-step apart — and build a riff directly from whichever shape stands out. Framed as the faster entry point for riff/interval-driven rock-metal writing versus Method 1's chord/cadence focus, and a good way to get a feel for an unfamiliar scale fast.
- **Sparse octave harmonization for a busy lead line:** over an already-dense riff/lead, double the melody in bare octaves at reduced volume rather than adding a fuller harmony — thickens the line without adding rhythmic/harmonic clutter.
- **Pre-production workflow notes:** program a rough drum beat before writing guitar parts (helps timing and generates rhythmic ideas — moving a chord change to land on beat 4 instead of beat 1 read as more interesting once heard against the loop); record until two similar-quality takes exist, then pan them hard left/right for a doubled rhythm-guitar sound.

# Two-Handed Tapping With Independent Arpeggios

No prior home in the vault for tapping as its own topic — created 2026-08-23 from a BERNTH lesson.

## What it is

An 8-finger tapping approach where **each hand taps a separate, independent arpeggio shape simultaneously**, rather than the more common one-hand-taps/one-hand-frets division of labor. Up to four fingers of the fretting (left) hand articulate one arpeggio shape (the same way they would for sweep picking), while up to four fingers of the tapping (right) hand independently articulate a *second* arpeggio shape — two different harmonic ideas running at once rather than one line split across two hands.

## Origin and worked example

Grew out of practicing basic single-arpeggio tapping licks (root, minor 3rd, perfect 5th — the smallest possible minor arpeggio, e.g. E-A-C for A minor) and moving the tapping finger around within the scale. The idea of assigning a **second, independent** arpeggio shape to the second hand came from asking "what if each hand thinks in its own shape instead of one continuous line?" Reported as initially dismissed by a teacher as impractical/pointless, kept working on independently regardless.

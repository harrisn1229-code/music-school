# Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)

## Synopsis

This is part 2 of a two-part Sol State breakdown of Laxcity's future-garage/two-step track "Focus," covering the back half of 18 total production tips split across the two videos. Where part 1 presumably covers the earlier build (drop 1, initial drums, harmony writing setup), this half picks up with drum layering and rhythm variation, vocal harmony arrangement and formant manipulation, the philosophy behind writing a genuinely different second drop, Laxcity's honest take on arrangement paralysis and knowing when a song is "done," and finally a full mixing/mastering walkthrough of the drum bus, snare chain, and master bus clip-into-limiter stage. It closes with his general workflow note that he now works almost entirely with stock plugins.

## Techniques

- **Use vocal samples/effects to bridge 16-bar sections and manufacture progression.** The single biggest note he gives when reviewing other people's tracks is lack of variation between consecutive 16-bar sections — a short vocal sample or vocal effect dropped at the seam signals "something's changing" and primes the listener for the next section, even without changing the underlying instrumental. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Layer multiple drum-sample sources rather than relying on one pack.** His kit stacks a kick from a Swindle sample pack, several different hi-hat layers (including a stock "tape 808" hi-hat pulled from a default 808 drum-machine patch), and a two-layer snare (one edited Swindle hit, one from an unidentified pack) plus a separate reverb-drenched snare layered in purely to add variation within the mix. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Bounce the drums with a subtle slapback delay baked in underneath.** A quiet, tucked-under slapback layered into the bounced drum stem is a small, easy-to-miss move but reads as genuinely effective at making the drums stand out more than the dry version. → [[Delay]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Program drums like an imagined live drummer would play through the song, not a static loop.** Add toms and the occasional crash cymbal as the song progresses, the same way a real drummer would embellish a groove over time rather than repeating it identically — something extra at the end of the first 16 bars, and a distinct rhythmic flourish/ending gesture at the close of a 32-bar section (he cites literally adding a quick extra snare in an odd time signature as one example). → [[Rhythm and Groove]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Change your MIDI snap resolution to something unusual (e.g. a quarter-step grid) to land on weirder, more human-feeling rhythms.** Deliberately loosening the grid off the standard subdivisions is a fast way to stumble into rhythmic placements you wouldn't have programmed on purpose. → [[Rhythm and Groove]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **A bare, completely unprocessed lead vocal can beat a full stacked-harmony arrangement.** He originally had additional harmony takes from the featured vocalist (Clementine) but pulled them because the single lead take by itself was more memorable — and left it with zero processing since it already "sounds good as it is." → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Route harmonies to a dedicated bus, pan it wide, and shape it with a mid-side EQ.** The harmony section (four additional harmony voices at one point in the arrangement) gets its character mostly from how that bus is panned and processed on the sides independently from the center via mid-side EQ, rather than from processing on the individual takes. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Width and Stereo]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **When a feature doesn't write a needed section, repeat an existing verse instead of leaving it blank or forcing a new write.** Clementine hadn't written for one section, so he literally reused one of her existing verses there — a quick way to keep a drop feeling consistent with what came before while still reading as slightly different in context. — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Automate an Auto-Tune plugin's "throat length" parameter to push a vocal toward a more masculine or more feminine character.** With the automation off the voice stays static throughout; automating throat length shifts perceived vocal gender/character across the section rather than leaving pitch correction as a static, always-on setting. → [[Pitch and Formant]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **A second drop needs to hit as hard as the first, even if only through small changes.** Listeners who skip straight to a song's drop will judge the second drop just as harshly as the first, so he swaps the drums (or swaps which instruments are playing) between drop one and drop two — here the change is as small as swapping the snare for a rim sound, which fit the future-garage/two-step context nicely — while keeping the underlying arrangement essentially the same. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Arrange with a "how would a DJ mix this" mental model, and default to a commercial intro-chorus-breakdown-chorus-outro template.** When going for a more commercial-sounding arrangement he reaches for that standard structure most of the time, and imagines throughout how a DJ would actually mix the track (transition points, section lengths) as a sanity check on the arrangement. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[DJ Mixing]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **When arrangement itself is the hurdle, experiment with whatever alternate-arrangement tools your DAW offers rather than forcing one fixed structure.** He's open about struggling with arrangement personally and suggests actively trying different arrangement options inside your DAW as a way through the block, instead of defaulting to the same structure every time out of habit. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Staying committed to one idea is what actually finishes songs — arrangement paralysis is his #1 reason for abandoning a project.** He's honest that not knowing where to take a strong intro or drop next is exactly what makes him close a session and never reopen it; it's fine to step away from an idea occasionally, but making that the habitual response is what kills songs before they're finished. → [[Committing and Printing]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Budget real time for the tedious, unglamorous detail passes near the end — they're disproportionately worth it.** Small, tiny sound-design tweaks that take ages and feel like a slog are exactly the stuff that's "super worth it in the end," even though they're the part of finishing a track he likes doing the least. → [[Committing and Printing]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **There's no objective "done" — trust a felt sense of finished rather than chasing an endless "could always be better" voice.** He never fully felt "Focus" was finished in an absolute sense (there's always a longer version or a different treatment of some section he can imagine), but after enough hours of work he called it — noting that indulging the "could always be better" voice too long can actually make a track worse rather than better. → [[Committing and Printing]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Keep drum-bus processing minimal — a simple low-pass/high-pass EQ shape, not compression, adds most of the variation needed.** The whole drum bus runs through just an EQ doing low-pass/high-pass shaping; he likes this specifically for the variation it adds rather than reaching for a compressor on the bus. → [[EQ]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Snare chain: fast/aggressive distortion for transient beef-up, then an EQ that cuts low-end artifacts and boosts the frequencies that make it stand out.** The snare gets hit with fast distortion first, then an EQ pass that cuts unwanted low-end artifacts he can visually spot and boosts the parts of the snare that actually stand out — his framing is that the sample itself is doing most of the work and this is a light touch on top. → [[EQ]], [[Snare Design]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Deliberately mix drums louder than everything else so a master-bus distortion plugin clips them with crunch, not mush.** His master chain runs a distortion plugin across the whole mix so it "clips nicely" — the trick is making sure the drums sit louder than the rest of the mix going in, so when the clip happens the drums are what get the crunch on top, rather than the clip smearing everything evenly. → [[Clipping and Loudness]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **FabFilter Pro-L as the final master-bus stage after the clip, just bringing levels up.** After the distortion/clip stage does the character work, Pro-L sits as the last device purely to bring up the overall level of everything — a clean division of labor between the clipper (tone) and the limiter (loudness). → [[Pro-L 2]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **A/B the stereo imager on the master to hear how much width it's actually adding, and expect the mastering chain to change per genre.** Toggling Ozone's Imager on and off on the master demonstrates an audibly wider result with it engaged; he notes his mastering chain isn't fixed and changes depending on the genre of the song. → [[Ozone 9 Imager]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)
- **Mostly stock plugins now.** He closes by noting his current workflow leans heavily on stock plugins rather than third-party processing across the board. — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)

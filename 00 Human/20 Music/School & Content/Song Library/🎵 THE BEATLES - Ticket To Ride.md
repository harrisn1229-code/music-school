# Ticket To Ride — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** two-bridge model, one verse in the middle, **no instrumental section**. Verse is **16 bars** in four equal phrases, but the real split is 8 + 8 — an expository half opening out to V, then a refrain-like half heading back toward I. Bridge is 8 bars, one 4-bar phrase stated twice.

Ordinary form, ordinary chord vocabulary; everything interesting happens in texture, rhythm and dissonance. What keeps it from going flat is an **exceedingly slow harmonic rhythm** — four bars of nothing but I to open the verse — which creates suspense the chords themselves do not ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]).

The novel bit is a **three-way pivot off vi**: the same chord is answered first by IV, then by ♭VII ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]), and finally by V, which after the first two feels like the only functional move left on the board. The bridge provides contrast without modulating at all — it simply refuses to touch I for eight bars.

Free melodic dissonance is the main spice ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]), climaxing over the ♭VII chord where the sung line has **no note in common with the chord underneath**.

The ostinato does structural work far beyond where it actually plays — hear it at the start and end of a section and you assume it ran through the middle, which it often does not. And the payoff detail: Ringo's syncopated verse pattern, derived from the guitar figure, is **quietly retired after the second verse** for even eighths, so the two drift apart and the listener feels a release without locating it ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]).

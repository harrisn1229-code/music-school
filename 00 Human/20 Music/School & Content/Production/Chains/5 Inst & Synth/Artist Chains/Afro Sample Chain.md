# Afro Sample Chain

**Reference/vibe:** the exact 3-element sample loner built and sent out that ended up placed on an unnamed song with over a million streams — reverse-engineered from his own breakdown, [how i made the sample that got a 1 million stream placement (it's easier than you think!?)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=atjGOpxBzag). Unlike most other chains on this page (a specific artist's record being copied), this one is loner's own confirmed working sample, built specifically as an "afro sample" for another producer to develop. The processing across all three elements is deliberately plain (EQ, reverb, chorus, delay — nothing exotic); the real lesson of this chain is what got LEFT OUT (a perc loop and a horn layer, both built then cut) so the sample stayed open for a collaborator to build on. See [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] and [[Arrangement and Song Form]] for the restraint argument in full.

## Chain overview

Two AAS Lounge Lizard EP-3 layers (a dry chord bed and a heavily-processed pad built from the same chord progression) plus one Kontakt "Evolution Strawberry" guitar counter-melody. A perc loop and a horn layer were built and then deliberately removed before the sample was sent out. The whole finished mix gets pitched down 2 semitones on the master as the last step.

## 1. Chord bed — Lounge Lizard "blues comp" preset

- **Instrument:** AAS Lounge Lizard EP-3, "blues comp" preset. No owned electric-piano VST equivalent tracked — see [[Plugin Wishlist]].
- **Chords:** F major → G major (C passing note) → A minor → D passing note → E minor (pitched up an octave to match the preceding chord). Two majors then two minors, a deliberate melodic→emotional arc. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **Feel:** strummed (not block-triggered) for a natural feel, with deliberate gaps left between chord hits — the simplicity is what makes the gaps stand out, giving the part "bounce" before drums are even added. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]]
- **FX:** [[EQ Eight]] cutting low end + some mid-range, then a light spring reverb (owned lane: [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] spring-modeled algorithm, or [[UAD RealVerb-Pro]]). *(general starting point — no specific frequencies given)* → [[EQ]], [[Reverb]]

## 2. Pad — Lounge Lizard "broken" preset, manipulated

- **Instrument:** same plugin, "broken" preset — "I use this all the time," per loner — copying the Step 1 chord progression but placed slightly differently rhythmically.
- **Pre-bounce chain:** reverb (preset named "clean chorus," mix ~50%) → [[EQ Eight]] cut low → [[Chorus-Ensemble]] for width → [[ValhallaDelay]] for texture.
- **Bounce + rechop:** bounce the processed layer to audio, then chop it up and re-place the pieces slightly differently — a second manipulation pass distinct from the FX chain, and the step that actually turns a keys sound into a pad.
- **Post-bounce chain:** [[Kickstart 2]] set to pulse at half time → a SECOND, different-type reverb → a final EQ cutting both low AND high end (narrowing it into a supporting textural layer). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- 🎬 *From video:* bounce-then-chop is the actual pad-making move here — the pre-bounce FX chain alone (reverb/EQ/chorus/delay) wouldn't have gotten there on its own.

## 3. Guitar counter-melody — Kontakt "Evolution Strawberry," wire preset

- **Instrument:** Kontakt 7, "Evolution Strawberry" bank, "wire" preset. Played dry first to establish tone. → [[Kontakt 7]]
- **FX chain:** Bias FX guitar-amp plugin ("between us" preset, unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) → [[EQ Eight]] cutting low + mid-range → [[Chorus-Ensemble]] with BOTH factory chorus presets stacked ("to make it super wide") → light ping-pong-mode delay (width tool, not an audible echo) → room reverb closing the chain. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Delay]], [[Width and Stereo]]

## 4. What got cut (the headline lesson)

- **A percussion loop** — built, then removed specifically because keeping it would pre-commit the eventual collaborator's own drum programming; loner wanted the other producer to have full creative control over the drums.
- **A horn layer** — built, then muted/cut after loner caught himself adding it "just to flex my muscles as a producer" rather than because the sample needed it — a named trap of getting caught up in what other producers' samples sound like.
- Both cuts are framed as directly responsible for the placement: keeping either would have left no room for the eventual producer ("a producer tempo," per the captions) to add the melodic elements and transitions that filled out the finished song. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]

## 5. Finishing move

- **Pitch the ENTIRE finished sample down 2 semitones on the master**, using a "sound shifter"-type plugin (near-certainly Waves SoundShifter, already tracked on [[Plugin Wishlist]]), as the literal last step before sending it out. Owned lane: Ableton [[Shifter]] in Pitch mode. → [[Pitch and Formant]]

## Arrangement

**This sample is arranged by what got cut** — Stage 4 is the arrangement lesson, and it's the inverse of every other chain here. Two finished layers were deleted specifically so the eventual producer had room to add melodic elements and transitions. Keeping either would have pre-committed a collaborator's choices and, per the source, cost the placement.

**The move — leave slots empty on purpose.** A sample built for someone else to develop should fill BED and MIDS and stop. Filling LEAD and AIR too means there's nothing left to write.

**Density: 3 elements. That's the finished state, not a draft.**

Generalizes past afro flips to anything you're sending out — see [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] and [[Sound Palette]]'s "empty slots are a choice, not a gap."

## Variations

- The two-Lounge-Lizard-layer trick (one dry chord bed, one heavily processed into a pad from the SAME progression) is reusable on any two-VST-instance keys build — cheaper than sourcing two different instruments for a "keys + pad" combo.
- The restraint discipline (build extra layers, then cut anything that pre-commits a collaborator's choices) generalizes to any sample built for someone else to develop, not just afro-genre flips — see [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].

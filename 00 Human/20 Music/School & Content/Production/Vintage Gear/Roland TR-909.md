# Roland TR-909

## What it is
The TR-808's successor and the drum machine of dance music. Another commercial failure in its day (~10,000 units, killed within two years by the sample-based LinnDrum/DMX wave), the 909 got dumped into pawn shops cheap — which is exactly how Chicago house and Detroit techno producers got hold of them. Its kick, clap, and open hat are the rhythmic DNA of house, techno, and basically every four-on-the-floor genre since.

## What makes the sound
- **Hybrid architecture — the key fact**: kick, snare, toms, clap are *analog synthesis*; hi-hats and cymbals are *6-bit samples* clocked through analog VCAs. That crunchy, gritty 909 hat sizzle is literally low-bit-rate digital audio — lo-fi by necessity, iconic by accident.
- **The kick** is a triangle-wave VCO shaped toward sine with a fast pitch envelope and a click transient circuit on top — punchier, more mid-forward and shorter than the 808's sub ring. It's a *dance-floor thump* (energy ~60–100 Hz) where the 808 is a *bass note* → [[Kick Design]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]].
- **The clap** = filtered noise retriggered through a multi-tap envelope (the "several people clapping" flam) + a reverb-ish noise tail — the house-music clap.
- **The open hat's 6-bit crunch** + accent structure defined the offbeat "tsss" of house; **shuffle/flam and per-step accent** gave 909 patterns their swing.
- MIDI (it was one of the first drum machines with it) + the 16-step X0X sequencer made it the live techno instrument.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Microtonic]] (nails analog-style 909 kicks/percussion), [[Kick 3]] (909-style kick synthesis), [[Drum Rack]]/[[Impulse]] with 909 sample packs, [[kHs Bitcrush]] to fake the 6-bit hat crunch on any noise/cymbal source.
- **Paid:** Roland Cloud TR-909 (official ACB), D16 Drumazon 2; Behringer RD-9 in hardware.
- **Free:** high-quality 909 sample packs are everywhere (Samples From Mars 909 etc. often on sale/free).

## Used on
- Chicago house from the mid-80s on — Frankie Knuckles famously bought one of Derrick May's 909s, wiring the Detroit↔Chicago origin story into one machine.
- Daft Punk — "Revolution 909" (1997): a homage in the title; French house runs on 909 kits.
- Jeff Mills — "The Bells" and his live three-deck-plus-909 sets: the 909 as a *performance instrument* in techno.
- Genre/era: Chicago house, Detroit techno, acid house (with the [[Roland TB-303|TB-303]]), 90s rave/hardcore, French house — see [[House - EDM]], [[EDM Subgenres]].

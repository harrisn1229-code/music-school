# Ian Kirkpatrick — "Pretty Please" (24 Big Tips)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j5aA59B_T58)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j5aA59B_T58)

## Synopsis

Ian's favorite song he's worked on, torn apart tip-by-tip: the Nate-Smith-inspired laid-back hi-hat feel, LFO Tool turning a crowd sample into a riser, the philosophy of **bouncing 15 tracks down to 2 to force commitment**, the multi-layer kick/snare where "the listener only hears 4 elements," the deliberately sloppy P-bass with an accidental reverb kept in, the sub-bass reveal on the hook, and the whole **"rate of information / fill-the-gaps-but-serve-the-song / make it sound like a phrase"** framework. One of the densest arrangement lessons in the whole batch.

## Techniques

- **Steal a *feel*, not a part — Nate Smith hi-hats** — he studied how Nate Smith *lags* his hi-hats (not a triplet, not straight — a push/pull) and chased that placement for Pretty Please's faster hats. Reference great players for timing feel. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[1 Drums Hat Track]]
- **LFO Tool to turn a crowd sample into a riser** — "of all the ideas I've ever had that's the coolest": used LFO Tool to shape a crowd sound into an "up"/riser. The *only* difference between verse 1 and verse 2 is that crowd. → [[LFO Tool]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Marginal, barely-noticeable differences still register subconsciously** — small V1-vs-V2 changes that you don't consciously clock still work, as long as they don't distract from the songwriting. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - New Rules (25 Big Tips in 13 Min)|rate of information]], [[Automation]]
- **Fade the ends of drum hits to tighten them** — "I wanted them tighter so I faded the ends." → [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track]]
- **Atmosphere layers: a clock ticking under the whole song** — much of the "drums" is actually ambience (a ticking clock, noise) that isn't kick/snare; mute the main transients and there's a whole ambient bed left. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[TEXTURE Slot]]
- **LFO Tool to soften a transient / clean a messy hat** — ducking "makes it more about the hit and softens the transient"; started as a way to get a second-hat feel and clean up messiness. His favorite plugin. → [[LFO Tool]], [[Sidechaining]]
- **Bounce 15 tracks → 2 to force commitment** — 15 clap tracks get bounced to two "so I have to commit to it" (learned from a producer interview on "the art of not being precious"). A commitment device for the indecisive. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Snare as one unit from 3 jobs** — one layer for the beef, one for the transient, one that's a loop from another song — "no one knows how many layers as long as it sounds like one unit." → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Kick = 3 layers, and pull low end from the room layer** — transient layer + sub layer + a room kick; since the tight kick already has the sub/low end, **high-pass the room layer** so you get "one kick, but roomier" without doubling the lows. → [[Kick Design]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[SUB Slot]]
- **Final chorus is always the biggest — schedule additions** — hook 1 → 2 → 4: add a **tambourine, clap, ride/tang** only by the final chorus. Subtraction/addition by section builds arc. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Deliberately sloppy P-bass, expertly edited** — "my shitty playing of my shitty P-bass," then tightened with hard edits (chopping out line noise/silence between notes so it's dead silent except the notes). → [[1 Electric Bass DI Track]], [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Keep a happy accident — reverb left on some bass notes** — he'd accidentally tracked bass with reverb, noticed halfway, stopped, and *kept* the reverbed notes. You can see the reverb as noise in the waveform between edits. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - The Production Secret Behind Huge Pop Hits|commit to accidents]], [[Reverb]]
- **"Violate space" — establish a room then cut the reverb off** — his favorite production move (not in real life): set a reverb/room, then abruptly cut it so the song "feels like it stops for a second." → [[Depth and Space]], [[Reverb]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Hook reveal: introduce a sub-bass under the live bass** — a sub comes in only on the chorus to complement the P-bass and add lows it doesn't have (won't hear it on an iPhone). → [[SUB Slot]], [[Bassline Writing]], [[Referencing and Translation]]
- **Chop a hook guitar to match the live bass rhythm** — third-hook guitar is chopped/LFO-ducked to lock to the bass "so it doesn't sound like a separate element" — multiple layers, one element. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[LFO Tool]]
- **Synths chosen to complement the lower-mids** — pre-chorus stabs + synths picked specifically to fill the lower-mid frequencies the guitars leave. → [[MIDS Slot]], [[Masking and Carving]]
- **Bridge built from "a bunch of random stuff"** — including a **re-pitched vocal from New Rules ("rules them to myself") in Melodyne**, "Hit Kit," and deliberately off-grid timing ("if I quantized it, it would be awful"). → [[Melodyne]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Humanize after the fact (Flume's habit)** — go back in and push things off the grid to "put the organic back into the non-linear editing world." → [[Automation]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Tempo track to follow the singer's rall.** — Julia Michaels suggested slowing down before the chorus; he had her slow at whatever rate felt natural, then drew the **tempo track to match her performance** (not force her to a click). → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Automation]]
- **Fill gaps progressively for a sense of progression** — "there's a lot of space in the song; as it goes on I fill more gaps so it subconsciously feels like progression" — but don't overfill; serve the song. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Ian Kirkpatrick - New Rules (25 Big Tips in 13 Min)|rate of information]]
- **OTT to make low-volume elements audible** — harmonies "barely there in the mix but there": OTT expands/compresses so you can bury a part way in the back and still hear it. His main use of OTT. → [[OTT]], [[Multiband Compression]], [[TEXTURE Slot]]
- **Auditioning kicks/snares wastes time — he admits it** — "I get insecure about which kick/snare sample, lose a lot of time auditioning, end up with nothing." Fix: think of each layer's *purpose* first (transient? sub? room?) and pick to fill that role. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Kick Design]]
- **The goal: "only hear 4 elements" / "sounds like a phrase"** — the listener never counts layers; a great production, however many layers, reduces to a memorable phrase you hum walking around the house. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

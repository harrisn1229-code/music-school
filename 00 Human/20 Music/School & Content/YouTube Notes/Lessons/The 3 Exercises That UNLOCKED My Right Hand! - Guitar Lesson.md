# The 3 Exercises That UNLOCKED My Right Hand! - Guitar Lesson

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6O5Wdfyy3NQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6O5Wdfyy3NQ)

## Synopsis

Three funk/fusion-inspired chord-strumming rhythm exercises, all built on one chord shape (the "Hendrix chord," E7♯9), aimed at loosening the picking-hand wrist and improving rhythm/timing — explicitly pitched at rock/metal players who normally only drill single-string riffing and rarely work multi-string strumming.

## Techniques

- **Base chord/voicing: E7♯9 ("Hendrix chord")** — root E (A string, 7th fret), major 3rd G♯, minor 7th D, minor 3rd G (the chord's characteristic major-3rd/minor-3rd clash). Low E string blocked/muted with the fretting thumb.
- **Two muting approaches for dead notes, both drilled:** (1) fully percussive/staccato — mute with the picking hand at the bridge in addition to the fretting-hand block, for a very tight, dead thump; (2) the more traditional approach — lift fretting fingers just slightly off the strings (no picking-hand mute) for a softer, more open dead-note sound. Explicitly practice telling the two apart and choosing deliberately, not defaulting to one.
- **Exercise 1 — two-part pattern alternating open E7♯9 chord stabs with tight muted dead notes**, with a clear dynamic/tonal separation demanded between the "big, tight, accented" chord hits and the fully-muted dead notes (no blurring between the two).
- **Exercise 2 — syncopated group-of-3 rhythm twist** on the same chord/dead-note vocabulary, worked first at 32nd-note groupings (not 16th-note triplets — a specific rhythmic distinction the video calls out directly, since the two read very differently at speed) before introducing 16th-note triplets in exercise 3.
- **Exercise 3 — triplet feel, with a specific down/up-stroke distribution rule:** rather than continuous strict alternation through the triplet (which puts the strong beat's accent on alternating down/up strokes depending on where you are in the bar), deliberately place a **downstroke on every strong beat/accent**, accepting two downstrokes in a row where needed. Two consecutive downstrokes is harder at first but gives a more powerful, more consistent accent on every pulse — traded off explicitly against the "just keep alternating" default.
- **Right-hand wrist mechanics note:** for all three exercises, the wrist moves similarly to standard alternate picking, with one adjustment — the hand isn't resting on the bridge, since bridge-anchoring restricts the motion these multi-string strumming patterns need.
- **Framing:** presented as cross-training — even players with no interest in funk get real timing/rhythm/wrist-looseness benefit from this vocabulary, and it's a deliberate break from single-string riff-only practice.

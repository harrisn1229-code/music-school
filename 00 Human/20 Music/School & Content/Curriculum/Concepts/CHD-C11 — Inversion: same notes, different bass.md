# CHD-C11 — Inversion: same notes, different bass

**Q.** What does inverting a chord change, how many inversions does a chord have, and what are the reasons to invert?

> [!question]- Answer
> Only **which chord tone is in the bass** — the notes themselves are unchanged, and the bass note is the single most important note for a chord's feel. A triad has **two** inversions, a seventh chord **three**. Reasons: **voice leading** (keep common tones, move the least), **bass-line control** (walk the bass by step under static harmony — C → C/B → Am), and **hand economy** (nearby shapes instead of jumps).

## Why it matters

On a piano this is a choice about which note goes lowest. On guitar it usually is not: with four fingers and six strings the live decision is **which tones to drop**, and the bass note follows from that. Keep the 3rd and 7th — the guide tones carry quality and function — drop the 5th first, then the root. A rootless voicing is the default when a bass player is present, not an exotic case. An altered 5th is a colour tone rather than a 5th, so it stays when a plain 5th would go.

## Drill

Take a four-chord loop and re-voice it so the bass walks by step. Then play the same loop with 3rds and 7ths only, and check that nothing about the progression's identity was lost.

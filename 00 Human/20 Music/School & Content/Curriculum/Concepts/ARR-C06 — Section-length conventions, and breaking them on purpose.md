# ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose

**Q.** Contrast the house and pop templates, and say what makes a broken structure read as deliberate.

> [!question]- Answer
> **House** — a flat 16-bar-everywhere rule. **Pop** — a proportional template that holds across very different songs: a short ~4-bar intro with verse, pre-chorus and chorus each roughly double it (~8), *plus* the pop-specific move that **the chorus gets longer each time it returns** — teasing on first appearance, longest at the very end. Breaking it reads as considered rather than sloppy **only because the convention is well established**: a Billie Eilish-style verse→drop→verse→drop at speed, with a 3-bar buildup, violates a pattern the listener already knows.

## Why it matters

You can't deliberately break a rule you can't state. And a 10-bar loop against a vocal written in 8-bar phrases just reads as off — trim to 8 rather than leaving the odd length in.

## Drill

Count the bars of every section in three reference tracks. Note where the chorus lengthens on repeat.

## Synopsis

The shortest of Synthet's three-part rhythm series: putting rhythmic emphasis on the "and" between beats (the upbeat/offbeat) rather than the beat itself, tied directly to reggae's skank guitar (muted downstroke, open upstroke) as the textbook example. Ends with a listening test built around a cover of Miike Snow's "Animal," where the intro chords land on the offbeat and the ear only locks the "real" beat once drums enter — a demonstration of how disorienting a pure-offbeat pulse can be without a beat reference.

## What's taught

- **Offbeat emphasis = accenting the "and" between counted beats** — count a bar as "1 2 3 4," then place the rhythmic emphasis on the "and" in between rather than the numbered beat itself. A small timing shift with an outsized effect on feel.
- **Reggae's skank guitar is the canonical example** — muted strum on the downstroke, open/accented strum on the upstroke, which puts the audible chord hit squarely on the offbeat. Named as foundational to reggae and dancehall-adjacent styles.
- **Offbeat-only rhythm is genuinely disorienting without a beat anchor** — demoed via a cover of Miike Snow's "Animal": the intro is chords-only, landing on the offbeat, and it's hard to count reliably until the drums enter and establish where "1" actually is — a useful ear-training example of how strongly the beat vs. offbeat placement changes perceived timing.

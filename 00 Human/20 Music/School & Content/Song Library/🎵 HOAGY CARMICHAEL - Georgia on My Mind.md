# Georgia on My Mind — Hoagy Carmichael

> 1930 standard (Carmichael/Gorrell); Ray Charles' 1960 recording (key of G) is the definitive version and Georgia's official state song. Chord chart below is Jared Borowski's ([[Georgia on My Mind Chords (Guitar Lesson) - Easiest way to play the REAL chords|Sound Guitar Lessons with Jared]]) open-position arrangement — deliberately "the REAL chords" (jazz-informed harmony, not a simplified 3-chord version) fingered so any guitarist with basic open chords can play it. A companion video from the same channel, [[Georgia on My Mind Fingerstyle Guitar Tutorial (With Sheet Music & Tab)]], builds the same harmony (also transposed to the key of C) into a full solo fingerstyle chord-melody arrangement — see the *Fingerstyle arrangement* section below and [[🎵 HOAGY CARMICHAEL - Georgia on My Mind (Guitar)]] for the measure-by-measure breakdown. Jared's own recommended order is chords first (this note), then the fingerstyle arrangement.

## Chords

**Taught key:** open-position shapes in the key of C. **Capo 5** to play along with sheet music (usually published in **F**, the most common key). **Capo 7** to play along with the **Ray Charles** recording (key of **G**).

**A section — "Georgia, Georgia, the whole day through, just an old sweet song keeps Georgia on my mind":**

| Chord (C-shape) | Lyric anchor | Alt / color options |
|---|---|---|
| **C** | "Geor-gia" | Cmaj7 (lift one finger, jazzier) |
| **E7** | "Geor-gia" | open E7 (melody on pinky, 2nd string/3rd fret) *preferred* → std. E7 shape (can read muddy); jazz option: **Bm7♭5 → E7** (insert the half-diminished as a passing chord in front of E7) |
| **Am** | "song of you" | — |
| **Am/G** | (bass walk) | A minor stays the chord; G forced as the bass note — deliberate descending bassline |
| **D/F♯** | (bass walk) | D7/F♯ (more spice, melody note included) → **D9/F♯** (root omitted from the shape, singer supplies it) |
| **F minor 6** | "the whole day through" | Jared's favorite chord in the arrangement |
| **E minor 7** | — | plain Em, fully interchangeable |
| **A7** | "old sweet song keeps" | only option, no alternates |
| **D minor 7** | "Georgia" | plain Dm |
| **G13** | — | G7 w/ open high-E on top *preferred* → std. open G7 |
| **E minor 7 → E minor** | — | deliberate double voicing back to back |
| **C major → (delayed) E minor** | "on my mind" | expected cadence delayed — see *Delayed resolution* below |
| **Em → Em7 → C → A7 → Dm** | "old sweet song keeps Georgia on my mind" | Em→A7 functions like a ii–V pulling toward the coming Dm |
| **D minor** (resolution) | "my [mind]" | Dm7 / plain Dm *simple* → **G7♯5** (chromatic pre-chord, "spicy," ♯5 outside the key) *jazzier* → **G+ (augmented triad, no 7th)** alt to the dominant version, same color, lower register |

A section repeats with the same chord menu (2nd verse).

**Bridge into pre-chorus — "through the Pines...":**
- **F7** (leaves the tonic) → alt **F minor 6** (reused favorite) → alt **B♭9** (near-identical voicing to Fm6, bottom note on a different string; B♭9→C is another delayed-resolution move)
- Strumming note: avoid the top two strings on the F7 shape, mute if strumming through
- Back to **C** (or Cmaj7)

**B section — "The arms that reach out to me...":**
- **E7** (any voicing) → **Am** (or **Am7** for a floatier/jazzier sound)
- **E7** again OR **D minor 6** ("more traditional") — both work, melody note fits either
- 3rd alt at same spot: **F minor 6** again (open D = melody note here too)
- **Am7** — physically the *same shape* as the E7 voicing on a different string set, so Am7→E7→Am7 is one repeated shape shifted across string groups
- **F7** again (same strum caution)
- B section repeats ("the eyes smile...")
- **E7** — no alternates here — "still in peaceful dreams I see"
- **C6** — Jared's own arrangement choice (C shape + added note, 3rd string/2nd fret); steps down a half step for smooth bass motion
- **B7** — hard fingering (pinky on the 5th string), give it extra practice time. Many charts insert a chord before it (same device as Bm7♭5→E7); this arrangement goes straight to B7.
- **E minor → A7** — a ii–V in the key
- **D minor 7** — deliberately correct even though it does **not** contain the sung melody note (E) — stated exception to the "melody note should be in the chord" rule. Alt: plain Dm. Alt: **D minor 9** (adds open high-E, restores the melody note to the voicing)
- **G13** (preferred) or plain G7

A section repeats to the end.

**Turnaround** (cycling back to repeat the form — solos, repeats): **E♭ diminished 7** *preferred, "more jazz"* or plain **A minor** *more classic*.

**Ending** (last time through): land on **C major** (or **Cmaj7**), arrived at via any of the leave-and-return pre-chords above (F7 / F minor 6 / B♭9).

### Reharmonization devices used throughout
- **Delayed resolution** — the expected tonic (C) is deferred by substituting a chord that shares the melody note (e.g. C → Em instead of landing on C, B♭9 → C) → [[Reharmonization]].
- **Passing-chord insertion** — a chord placed in front of the "real" chord for extra harmonic motion (Bm7♭5 before E7; a chord before B7) → [[Passing Chords]].
- **Chromatic altered pre-chord** — G7♯5 / G augmented before Dm, an out-of-key color tone resolving into the target → [[Passing Chords]] · [[Altered Chords]].
- **Slash-chord bass walks** — Am/G and D/F♯ keep the chord identity fixed while the bass descends stepwise underneath → [[Slash Chords]].
- **Melody-note-in-the-chord substitution rule** — any chord containing the sung melody note is a legal reharmonization at that spot, whether or not it's the "obvious" chord (stated explicitly, then also shown broken once on purpose at the D minor 7 in the B section) → [[Reharmonization]].
- **Turnaround color choice** — E♭dim7 (jazz) vs. Am (classic) at the repeat point → [[Turnarounds]].

## Fingerstyle arrangement

Jared Borowski also built a **full solo fingerstyle chord-melody arrangement** of the tune (same channel, also key of C) — melody, harmony and a moving bass line all fingerpicked at once on one guitar, rather than strummed chords. Full measure-by-measure breakdown lives on [[🎵 HOAGY CARMICHAEL - Georgia on My Mind (Guitar)]] and the source video is [[Georgia on My Mind Fingerstyle Guitar Tutorial (With Sheet Music & Tab)]]. Highlights:
- Opens with a Travis-picking/Chet-Atkins-style alternating bass under a plain C chord, then a **chromatic walking bassline** carries the harmony down toward E major — at one point there's no chord at all, just a moving bass note doing the harmonic work.
- Structure: **A section (measures 1–5) repeats with a 1st/2nd ending**, into a **B section**, then **DC al Coda** (repeat to the top, jump to the Coda after measure 5 the second time) — a compact, through-composed AABA-shaped form matching this note's `structure` field.
- The song's signature "Georgia... on my mind" hook is voiced with a "haunting," out-of-key neighbor chord **rearticulated** multiple times (fingering forces the re-strike) rather than sustained once — an intentional ornamentation device, not a limitation.
- Ends on a genuine **ii–V voice-leading move** into the Coda, singled out by Jared as one of his favorite moments in the arrangement.

## Steal this
- **"Chord in front of a chord" as a general jazz-harmony move** — insert a related chord (often the ii or a half-diminished) immediately before a target chord to add motion without changing the underlying changes → [[Passing Chords]] · [[Reharmonization]].
- **Delayed resolution as a phrase-ending device** — substitute at the expected cadence point instead of landing, then land later → [[Reharmonization]].
- **Slash chords for intentional bass motion under a static chord identity** (Am/G, D/F♯) → [[Slash Chords]].
- **Walking chromatic bass notes between chord changes** (5th of the current chord → passing tone → root/bass note of the next chord) — [[Georgia on My Mind Chords (Guitar Lesson) - Easiest way to play the REAL chords|full detail on the video page]].

## Techniques

- **Song context:** composed 1962 by Antônio Carlos Jobim (Portuguese lyrics Vinícius de Moraes; English lyrics Norman Gimbel, 1964), reportedly the second-most-recorded song in music history after "Summertime." Real-person inspiration: Heloísa, who walked past the bar now called Garota de Ipanema. Global fame via the 1964 *Getz/Gilberto* album (Stan Getz, João Gilberto, Astrud Gilberto) — Grammy Album of the Year 1965, the song itself won Record of the Year. Bossa nova, 32-bar AABA with an unusually long, harmonically dense B section; typically played in F major (the famous recording is in D♭, taught here in F as the more common jam-session key). → [[🎵 ANTÔNIO CARLOS JOBIM - The Girl from Ipanema]]
- **Melody emphasizes chord extensions as main tones, not passing tones** — e.g. the bridge's opening phrase sustains the **9th** of the F♯m9/Gm9 chords for more than a measure, not just brushing past it. This is what gives the tune its "floaty, soft" character; playing the extensions as structural rather than decorative is part of nailing the sound. Also notably **syncopated** — most people's mental version of the melody is a "watered down," less-syncopated version than what's actually written. → [[🎵 ANTÔNIO CARLOS JOBIM - The Girl from Ipanema]]
- **A section harmonic analysis:** Fmaj7 (I) → G7 (II7, commonly played with ♯11) → ii–V–I back to F, no tritone-sub or altered dominant needed for the famous recording (a G♭7 tritone sub does appear in some real-book versions, but a plain 2-5-1 works and is simpler to improvise over). Then a direct half-step-up modulation into the bridge with **no** ii–V approach chord — just a jump. This A-section shape is identical to the A section of Ellington's "Take the A Train" (Jobim reportedly borrowed it as a fan homage) — learning one reinforces the other. → [[🎵 ANTÔNIO CARLOS JOBIM - The Girl from Ipanema]]
- **Bridge-unlocking trick (full detail already on [[Chord Naming by Context]]):** reinterpret each "confusing" opening bridge chord as its relative-minor/major partner (a maj6 chord and its relative-minor-7 chord share every note) to reveal the same relationship repeated three times at three pitch levels, rather than four unrelated grabs. → [[Chord Naming by Context]]
- **The bridge's physical guitar-shape payoff — not yet captured anywhere else in the vault:** a first-inversion G♭maj7 voicing (3rd on the bottom: 3-root-5-7, skip the A string, string 6 fret 6 / string 4 fret 4 / string 3 fret 6 / string 2 fret 6) is the **exact same physical shape** as a second-inversion F♯m9 voicing (5-♭3-♭7-9) one string set over — meaning the bridge's opening four chords aren't four shapes to memorize, they're **one shape moved to three positions** (up 3 frets, then up 1 more fret). → [[Chord Voicings]], [[Chord Naming by Context]]
- **Two levels of chord shapes taught, demonstrated at 130 BPM:** (1) **shell voicings** — root/3rd/7th (or root/3rd/6th) only, the minimum information needed, widely used even by professional jazz players; (2) fuller 4-note jazz voicings adding a 5th or an extension for more color. → [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Scale-per-chord map for improvising over the A section:** F major scale on Fmaj7; **G Mixolydian** (= C major scale, 5th mode) on G7, upgradeable to **G Lydian dominant** (Mixolydian with a ♯11/♯4) if the chord is voiced with a ♯11; back to F major. Default recommendation is to start with plain Mixolydian rather than jumping straight to an altered/tritone-sub reading, since that "slows down" real-time playing — save the **C altered scale** (playable over any resolving dominant 7th for tension-then-release) as a later refinement once Mixolydian is comfortable.
- **Scale-per-chord map for the bridge:** G♭ Lydian (4th mode of the major scale, same notes as D♭ major) on the G♭maj7-type chords — thinking of it as D♭ major preserves the same relative-minor relationships used in the chord analysis above. All the bridge's dominant 7th chords: **Mixolydian** by default, **Lydian dominant** as the richer alternative (same as the A section's G7). All the bridge's minor chords: **Dorian** (preserves the same relationship-based thinking — e.g. an E♭m chord reads as E♭ Dorian, the ii of D♭ major). The two-five-two-five closing the bridge resolves with **Dorian** on the minors and the **altered scale** on the dominants (both chords are "messed with" — altered 9th and altered 5th — making the altered scale the direct fit).
- **Practice method for the scale set: "root-to-root"** — play each scale starting on its root, pausing on the root partway through, and ending on the root, to internalize how each mode's specific color actually sounds rather than just running the shape.
- **Chord-melody arrangement walkthrough of the tune's opening (bottom-four-strings voicings), demonstrating a general chord-melody principle: always keep the guide tones (3rd and 7th) present under the melody note, with the root and 5th optional.** Worked example: Fmaj7 with the melody on the 9th uses a voicing built major-7 (bottom) → 3rd → 5th → 9th (melody, top); moving to G7 with the melody descending through 7th/6th reuses a ♭7-3-5-root shape; the same physical shape recurs (transposed) across Gmin7, C7, and back to Fmaj7 — the same "one shape, multiple contexts" principle the bridge analysis demonstrates, now applied to a full chord-melody arrangement rather than just the bridge. → [[Chord Melody Arranging]], [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Internalization tip: attempt a chord-melody arrangement of any standard you want to deeply learn, even if you "fail" at it** — the value isn't a performable arrangement, it's what studying the fretboard and the song's harmony under arrangement pressure teaches you; failures still teach real things about your own fretboard limitations as an arranger. → [[Chord Melody Arranging]]
- **Listening recommendation: Baden Powell's guitar version** (the song note currently has this misspelled as "Ben Powell" — corrected on [[🎵 ANTÔNIO CARLOS JOBIM - The Girl from Ipanema]] as part of this enrichment pass).

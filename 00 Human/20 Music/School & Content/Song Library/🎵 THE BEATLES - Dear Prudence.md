# Dear Prudence — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A song built on **near-total stasis** — one chord under almost everything and a floor plan that's just a chain of identical verses with one short bridge at the midpoint — that nonetheless produces a genuine build and release. Since neither harmony nor melody can supply the arc, the job goes entirely to **texture and a long dynamic crescendo**.

**Form.** The intro is really an intro to the intro: four bars of drone while the lead guitar walks down a scale, before the ostinato proper begins. The verse is **14 bars** phrased AA'B, rounded off by a two-bar instrumental washer; the bridge is an odd **5½ bars** — the verse in miniature. The outro returns abruptly to the quiet intro texture, and unlike the "Hey Jude" coda it turns the corner and is gone.

**Harmony.** Nearly everything sits over a **D pedal** ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]). What you could label as a chord progression is better heard as upper voices harmonising a **semi-chromatic tenor line descending D–C–B–B♭–A**, and that reading holds even when the bass later takes the descent over. The one explicit harmonic move in the verse is a single bar where the bass abruptly gets busy — it registers as an event precisely because nothing else has moved. **♭VII harmony plus a melody that never touches the seventh** is what makes the mode read Mixolydian ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]).

**Melody.** Pentatonic — the fourth and seventh avoided outright ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]) — and assembled from short repeated motifs rather than a spun-out arch.

**Arrangement.** One continuous crescendo from near-silence, then a hard reset ([[ARR-C05 — The energy curve — one apex, and pull back before the end]]). Each section gets exactly one new element. The bass outlines **both the pedal and the walking tenor at once**, a Baroque trick executed on one instrument. The climax is marked by **stretching the crux phrase to double length** rather than by more volume.

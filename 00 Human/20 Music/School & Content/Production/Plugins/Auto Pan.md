# Auto Pan

**Category:** Pan/Imaging
**Vendor:** Ableton

## What it is

Ableton's stock rhythmic auto-pan/tremolo device — an LFO-driven amount/phase/rate control that moves a signal's level and/or stereo position on a synced or free rate.

## What it's used for

Rhythmic panning and tremolo movement — pumping a pad or texture layer left/right, or adding amplitude chop synced to the grid.

## FX chain notes

Inserted on a texture/pad layer where rhythmic movement is wanted; often layered with an external modulator automating its Shape/rate over time rather than leaving it static.

## Techniques

- **Amplitude tremolo on an electric bass, deliberately, so the bass can't "take over" the low end or read as a static wash.** Peter Katis's stated reasoning for reaching for tremolo (owned lane: Auto Pan with Phase kept at 0° so both channels chop together as volume, not the mirrored-phase auto-pan setting) on TWO separate bass parts in the same mix (a lick that enters at the second chorus, and a real sub bass that joins only once the vocal returns after the bridge): "I still want it to hold back the low end in the song, and so I put the tremolo on it. The tremolo just kind of breaks it up... it doesn't let the bass take over like it would otherwise. I'm a big fan of tremolo, and anything that modulates the sound in and out, up and down, so it isn't just a wash." A genuinely different job for tremolo than this vault's usual pan-tremolo entries (see [[Modulation]]) — here it's arrangement/low-end crowd-control on a bass part, not a texture or width move. → [[Modulation]], [[Bass Design]], [[BASS Slot]], [[SUB Slot]] — Peter Katis (Mix with the Masters), [Mix breakdown with Peter Katis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o4F2EO_KMLk)
- **Auto Pan as a pure modulation LFO (not for panning) — a "swishy" movement layer late in a chain** — deep in an already-dense processing chain, Auto Pan gets used less for its literal stereo-panning job and more as a convenient free LFO shape generator, adding a swishy, breathing modulation character to a heavily-layered sound-design texture. → [[Modulation]] — Eli derp, [How To Make Insanely Phat Basses with @eliderp_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8a84OhMSfzM)
- **Quarter-note sync + Amount maxed + Phase at 0, layered on an LFO-modulated sine chord, for a hard rhythmic "wobble"** — synced to quarter notes with Amount all the way up and Phase pulled down to 0, Auto Pan adds a hard, rhythmic stereo wobble on top of an already-modulating source (here, an LFO pulsing Operator's Oscillator B Level) — the combination of an internal harmonic wobble and an external panning wobble is what sells the "wobble chords" effect, not either alone. → [[Operator]] — You Suck at Producing (Underbelly), [Ableton Tricks I Learned From Remixing Mr. Bill](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xk8HsgS9Gbc)
- **Amount cranked + Sample & Hold + full Width as a free "dropouts" alternative** — turn Amount up, switch the LFO shape to **Sample & Hold**, and push Width all the way — stock Auto Pan then reproduces a randomized, jumpy panning/level-dropout movement similar to a paid tape-emulation plugin's dedicated dropouts control, adding fragility and motion to an otherwise static ambient layer. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — You Suck at Producing, [The best FX for creating LUSH Soundscapes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wysKsyJf7dg)
- **Continuous circular pan for extra spatial depth on an SFX layer** — set to circle continuously (rather than a slow sync-rate sweep) on a laser/riser-type sound-design element, adding a sense of movement/depth to what would otherwise be a static mono-panned hit. → [[Operator]] (Laser SFX recipe) — dnksaus, [How to Sound Design G JONES Style](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d6d7VA0jAW0)
- **Sample-and-hold mode on a texture layer** — running Auto Pan in its sample-and-hold LFO mode (stepped random values rather than a smooth sweep) reads well specifically on ambient/texture layers, giving a jumpy, stepped pan movement distinct from the smooth sync-rate "talky" pattern below. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — dnksaus, [Texturing Your Tracks in Ableton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Bab-gq4E00)
- **Sync-rate auto-pan for a "talky" texture layer** — bump Amount, turn down Phase, set to Sync rate (eighth notes) for a chattering, talky rhythmic movement on an atmospheric layer — then use an external multi-target modulator (his own "Multi-Shaper" M4L device — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) to change Auto Pan's shape *over time* rather than leaving the pan pattern static for the whole section. → [[Width and Stereo]] — dnksaus, [Scale-Aware Abuse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NZyKN5Wn7A0)

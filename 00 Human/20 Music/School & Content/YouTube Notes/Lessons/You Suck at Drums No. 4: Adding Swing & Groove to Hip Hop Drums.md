# You Suck at Drums No. 4: Adding Swing & Groove to Hip Hop Drums

## Synopsis
Fourth lesson in the "You Suck at Drums" course — applies the energy-tier method from episodes 2–3 specifically to a hip-hop loop, and introduces the video's headline technique: hip-hop swing built by leaving strong-beat hits exactly on-grid while nudging only the weak-beat hits late. Also covers an LFO-modulation trick for keeping a repeated static sample (jingle bells) from sounding identical every hit, gate mode for open hi-hats, and several concrete "swap the element" energy-raising moves (ride swap, transposed toms, an off-beat vocal-chop ornament) that extend the 3-changes rule from earlier episodes.

## Techniques
- **Hip-hop swing = leave strong beats on-grid, nudge only weak-beat hits late.** The key insight: instead of pushing every hit late (which just sounds janky), keep the kicks landing on the beat exactly on time while dragging the kicks on weaker beats a little late — the timing offset tracks metric weight rather than applying uniformly. Applied hard enough this reads as "drunk"/lazy in a good way; applied only to the weak hits (not the whole pattern) it reads as pocket instead of just late. → [[Rhythm and Groove]] (already routed)
- **Exaggerate the effect by also nudging rims/ghost hits early** (instead of late) and lowering the velocity of weak-beat kicks — stacking a timing push on some elements against a timing pull on others, plus a velocity drop, sells the "lazy" feel harder than timing offset alone.
- **LFO-modulate a repeated static sample so it doesn't sound identical every hit** — on a percussive one-shot that plays the same every time (jingle bells), turn on the device's LFO, route it to pitch, and turn OFF Retrigger so the LFO keeps running continuously in the background instead of resetting on each new note — every hit then lands at a different point in the LFO cycle and picks up a slightly different pitch bend. Tighten the character further by raising the sample's own pitch and shortening it, then compensate with a longer fade-out so the shortened tail doesn't cut off abruptly. → [[Rhythm and Groove]] (already routed)
- **Gate mode for open hi-hats** — an open hi-hat by default trails out longer than wanted; switching its Drum Rack pad to Gate mode makes the sample stop exactly when the note ends, so note length (and fade-out) can precisely control how long the open hat rings.
- **Swapping an element counts as an energy-raising change too, not just add/activate.** Going from A to B: swap the jingle-bell layer for a ride (re-auditioning a noisy stock ride, then Gate-moding and shortening it into something usable) instead of adding an entirely new track; transpose an existing tom way up until it reads as a different percussive voice rather than "a tom," turning one element into what functionally feels like two. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] (already routed)
- **An off-beat vocal-chop/ad-lib as a percussive ornament** — a pitched-up vocal one-shot placed on the off-beats (nudged late like the other weak-beat hits) works as a rhythmic texture layer, not just a melodic hook — copy it across the loop and offset it the same way as the swung hi-hats.
- **Outro (O) energy drop follows the same "strip recent additions first" logic** as episode 3 — pull the busiest recently-added layers (ride, toms, hi-hat) while keeping just enough kick to mark the downbeat, so it reads as low-energy but still recognizably part of the same loop family.

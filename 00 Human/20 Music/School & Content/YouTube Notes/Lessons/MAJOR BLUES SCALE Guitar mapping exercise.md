## What's taught

- **Direct confirmation of the shared-shape fact**: "the major blue scale in the minor blue scale... they're the exact same collection of notes... it's how you treat the root and that's why this exercise is so important." Framed as the reason the root-to-root method matters more than for most scales — without deliberately targeting the root, a player risks accidentally landing on the minor-blues-scale sound rather than the major one they intended.
- **Blue-note handling — same rule as the minor-blues sibling video** (play the outside blue note on the string of the note you're resolving to; ascending on the left side of the shape, descending on the right), with one small addition: Jared explicitly recommends **sliding into the blue note from the scale tone beside it** wherever possible, calling out that this specific slide "just sounds great" — stated as a standing preference, not just an option.
- **Full 5-rule root-to-root method demonstrated on all 5 positions of the major blues scale** (root-to-root already fully documented → [[Root-to-Root Scale-Form Mapping Method]]) — not re-extracted here.
- **Practice-progression confirmation, same steps as the minor-blues video:** every root/every key (checklist-tracked, "not as heavy duty as it sounds"), a melodic pattern ("skip up, come down one" — same pattern already documented from the sibling video), metronome-locked root-to-root, then improvising while deliberately over-targeting the root in practice (to be dialed back to tasteful/subtle later).
- **Three general playing-quality habits, same as the sibling video**: alternate picking, periodic tone/tension check-ins, and working toward legato/connected phrasing.

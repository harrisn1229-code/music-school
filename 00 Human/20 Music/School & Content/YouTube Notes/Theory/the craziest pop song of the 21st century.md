# the craziest pop song of the 21st century

## Synopsis

A short analysis of Willow Smith's "Symptom of Life," a 7/4 pop song with irregular beat-grouping and major/minor tonic ambiguity, framed as an unexpected turn from an artist best known for "Whip My Hair."

## Techniques

- **Odd meter from irregular subdivision, not just an odd top number**: the verse's 7/4 bar is felt as four groups of three eighth notes with the last note tied over the bar line — the grouping (not the raw 7) is what makes it feel odd, mirroring how the piano arpeggio is voiced.
- **Bright-over-dark tonic ambiguity**: the song is in A minor but repeatedly resolves to an A **major** chord instead of A minor — blending major brightness with minor darkness in a way compared to Radiohead's harmonic language.
- **Phrygian touches for extra darkness**: brief use of Phrygian (minor scale with a lowered 2nd degree), audible in a G minor 7 chord after the verse.
- **Pre-chorus rhythmic displacement**: a two-bar, 14-beat pattern subdivided 3+5+4+2 rather than as two clean bars of 7/4 — the same 14 beats read as if grouped 3/4, 5/4, 2/4, 4/4, so the meter feels displaced even though the total beat count matches.
- **Modal-interchange-adjacent chord move**: alternates F major7 to B♭ major9 — could be read as Phrygian (via the B♭ chord) but the back-and-forth motion argues against a strict modal reading.

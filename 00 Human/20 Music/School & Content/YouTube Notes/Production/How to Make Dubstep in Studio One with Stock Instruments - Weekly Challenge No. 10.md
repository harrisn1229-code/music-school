# How to Make Dubstep in Studio One with Stock Instruments | Weekly Challenge No. 10

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sD3mE-siI5M)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sD3mE-siI5M)

## Synopsis

Weekly Challenge #10: dubstep at 140 BPM, stock-instruments-only, built live in **Studio One** (a different DAW than Nate's Ableton Live — techniques below are DAW-agnostic). Max builds drop-first (drums → bass sound design → hype vocals/riser/buildup → intro), which he frames as the right order because "if the drop's not good, why are you even making the song." The heart of the video is an extended, very concrete bass sound-design walkthrough: starting from a sine wave, chaining distortion ("Red Light," Studio One's stock saturation/distortion plugin) into a faux-OTT multiband-compressor patch he calls "the Smasher," EQ-automating a notch sweep, then bouncing/resampling the result to keep layering more processing on top — including the side-effect that resampling and pitching the bounced sound also pitches every effect baked into it (phaser, chorus, everything), which reads as a distinct, useful "everything moves together" character rather than a plain pitch-shift. He designs three separate bass tones (long fuzzy, short plucky, high screechy), a hype-vocal chant stack used both as a texture layer and — pitch-bent two octaves — as a riser, and a "dup and loop" vocal-phrase stutter as a pre-drop buildup device.

## Techniques

**Order of operations**
- **Build the drop first, then the buildup, then the intro** — "if the drop's not good, why are you even making the song" — front-load the highest-stakes section of an EDM arrangement rather than working chronologically from the intro.
- **Straight rock-beat kick pattern (quarter or half notes) as the basic dubstep drum foundation** — one of several dubstep drum-pattern families named (alongside funkier/hip-hoppy/trappy variants); started with the simplest for speed. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[EDM Subgenres]]
- **Compress and saturate the drum bus immediately, before any other processing** — glue the kit together with bus compression, then push it through distortion ("Red Light" → best-match Ableton [[Saturator]]/[[Overdrive]]) right from the start rather than as a late polishing step.

**Bass sound design — the "chain it over and over" method**
- **Start from a plain sine wave, then drive it through distortion until it's no longer a sine wave at all** — the raw waveform is treated purely as a seed; the distortion stage does the actual tone-shaping. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Faux-OTT via a custom 3-band multiband-compressor preset ("the Smasher")** — instead of a dedicated OTT-style plugin, build the upward-multiband-compression effect from a stock multiband dynamics processor: three bands (low/mid/high), heavy ratios, heavy... *(the exact remaining parameter is garbled in the transcript)* on each band. Confirms the same "[[Multiband Dynamics]] as a faux-OTT" pattern already documented on [[EDM Subgenres]]'s dubstep card, now sourced independently from a second breakdown. → [[Multiband Dynamics]], [[OTT]]
- **"Just chain stuff together over and over" as the actual sound-design method** — no single magic device; an EQ dropped in after the multiband stage, more processing stacked past that, auditioned by ear until it "sounds sick" — sound design as iterative accumulation, not a fixed recipe.
- **Automate an EQ band for a rhythmic notch/wah sweep on the bass tone** — once a decent base tone exists, automate the EQ (rather than a dedicated filter/envelope-follower device) to create moving notch character — a formant/wah-like movement layered onto the distorted tone. → [[EQ]], [[Automation]]
- **Bounce/resample the designed tone, then keep processing the resampled version** — rather than endlessly tweaking one device chain live, print the current result to a new sample and continue layering effects on the *printed* audio — a checkpoint-and-continue workflow for deep sound design.
- **Resampling and pitching a processed sound pitches ALL of its baked-in effects along with the note, not just the pitch** — playing a resampled tone faster/higher runs the whole processed signal (phaser, chorus, distortion artifacts, everything) through faster too, since it's all baked into the audio now — a genuinely different, often more interesting result than pitching a live synth (which would normally hold effect rates constant while only the oscillator pitch moves). A useful, repeatable "resample-then-pitch" sound-design move.
- **Design multiple distinct bass layers for one patch: a long fuzzy sustained tone, a short plucky hit, and a high screechy layer** — three separate designed elements meant to combine into one composite bass sound rather than one patch doing everything. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Bass Design]]
- **A compressor placed AFTER a reverb creates a pumping effect as the tail decays into audibility** — when the reverb return feeds into a compressor, the reverb's tail gets let through more as the dry signal decays below the compressor's threshold, reading as a rhythmic "pump" — an unconventional but effective ordering (compressor after reverb, not before). → [[Compression]], [[Reverb]]
- **A swelling chord layered in between rhythmic bass hits** — a sustained/swelling chord element placed in the gaps of a stuttering bass pattern for harmonic interest without competing with the bass's rhythm.
- **Layer an octave-up duplicate of a sub/bass element on the same track** for extra harmonic presence — bounce/mix down the original and a pitched-up-an-octave copy, stacked together, when a sub feels thin on harmonics. → [[Bass Design]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Crank distortion specifically to add harmonics to a thin sub** — rather than EQ-boosting a sub that's missing overtone content, driving distortion harder generates the missing harmonics directly. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]

**Hype vocals & buildup**
- **A stacked chorus of hype-vocal chants ("hyperventilate"), grouped and bus-processed together** — multiple takes of a shouted hype phrase, routed to a shared group, compressed and EQ'd (with a high-pass) as one unit, then sent to a room reverb — the classic EDM hype-vocal-stack texture layer. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **Turn one hype-vocal layer into a riser via an extreme pitch bend** — take one of the hype chant hits, pitch-bend it up two full octaves rising over its duration, for an instant riser built from vocal material instead of a synth/noise sweep.
- **"Dup and loop" a short vocal phrase, pitched down, then sped back up and chorused, as a pre-drop buildup stutter** — repeat/loop a short chanted phrase (pitched down for weight), tighten it back up rhythmically, and add chorus, placing it immediately before the drop as a tension device — a vocal-based alternative to the standard snare-roll/riser buildup.
- **Multiband compression on the master bus specifically at the drop, for extra impact** — reach for master-bus multiband compression as a drop-arrival intensifier, on top of whatever's already on individual elements. → [[Multiband Compression]]
- **A forgotten background element rescued by automating its filter open over time** — rather than removing an element that "you can't really hear," automate its filter cutoff to open gradually so it emerges into the mix instead of sitting buried and unused.

## Sound/tone selection

- **A sine wave as the seed for a heavily processed dubstep bass** — the starting waveform barely matters once distortion/multiband/EQ chains are layered on; character comes entirely from the processing chain, not the oscillator choice.
- **Three-layer bass design: long fuzzy / short plucky / high screechy** — a template for building a composite dubstep bass from purpose-designed layers. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Hype-vocal chant stack** as a texture/riser source, distinct from a lead vocal.

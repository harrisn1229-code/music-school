## Synopsis

A hands-on NAMM demo of the Expressive E Osmose keyboard, played as a standalone synth (its own Haken Audio EaganMatrix sound engine, not driving external analog hardware — that's the companion setup already documented from [[The future of EXPRESSIVE analog synths is HERE]]). The real content here is performance/expression technique specific to the instrument's per-key polyphonic sensors, distinct from that earlier CV-conversion video.

## Techniques

- **Wavefolder distortion emerges from pushing the internal preamp gain past a threshold**, not from a dedicated distortion effect — a clean patch's character shifts into genuine wavefolding once input gain crosses that point, giving access to harmonically complex, "sound design territory" tones from an otherwise mellow patch without switching effects.
- **Dual independent sustenuto pedals instead of a single sustain pedal** — rather than 0–127 continuous sustain on one pedal (already a step up from binary on/off), the instrument supports TWO separate sustenuto pedals, each independently capturing whatever notes are held when it's engaged while the keyboard stays free to play new, unpedaled notes on top — enabling layered/dual textures (a held pad plus fresh unpedaled runs) that a single sustain pedal can't produce.
- **"Pressure glide" — portamento triggered by relative pressure between two notes, not a pitch wheel or portamento switch.** Set a minimum interval threshold; playing two notes at or below that interval automatically glides between them based on the RELATIVE pressure difference the player applies to each — a continuous, touch-driven portamento that reads as natural phrasing rather than a triggered effect.
- **MPE arpeggiator sensitivity is a double-edged setting**: cranked high enough for maximum expressiveness, an unintentionally-resting finger on a key can silently trigger/advance a step ("ghost" notes/rhythm changes) — deliberately dial sensitivity down when a part needs to be reliable rather than maximally touch-responsive, matching the same gotcha already documented from the CV-conversion demo.
- **Design philosophy for a preset**: the stated goal for each of the instrument's 500 factory presets was to need NO additional knob-turning once you start playing — the patch itself should already be mapped so that all expression comes from how you touch the keys, not from adjusting parameters mid-performance.

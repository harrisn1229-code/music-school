# Disclosure - Help Me Lose My Mind with London Grammar Twitch Breakdown

Guy breaks down the Settle-era "Help Me Lose My Mind" (feat. London Grammar) — an early, stripped-back track he re-mixed fresh for the stream. The standout is a candid **"what I did wrong then vs how I'd do it now"** vocal-EQ lesson (the infamous +12 dB high boost), plus **sidechaining the synth off the vocal** through an empty bus to make room for a soft lead, and **Dilla-sample drums**. A great example of the taste-over-gear ethos: almost entirely stock Logic (ESP synth, Ring Shifter). High caption confidence.

## Techniques
- **The +12 dB vocal-high confession (additive vs subtractive EQ):** the original boosted the vocal highs a "psychopath" **+12 dB**. How he'd do it now — boost only ~3 dB in **dynamic mode** (so it de-esses as it lifts), work **subtractively** (cut the lows) instead of piling on treble, add **[[Gem Dopamine]]** for air *with* compression so it's not a raw boost, and de-ess. Also HPF the **bass rumble + plosives** first. → [[EQ]], [[De-essing]], [[LEAD Slot]] / [[AIR Slot]]. *(Now-approach: HPF ~80–100 Hz, dynamic bell +3 dB in the air band, then Gem Dopamine + de-ess.)*
- **Sidechain the synth off the VOCAL (empty-bus trick):** send the vocal to a muted empty bus (bus 12), and key a compressor on the chords off that bus so **the vocal ducks the synth** — making room for a soft, sensitive lead (Hannah) over a slamming bassline. **Slow release** so it flows naturally (a fast release sounds like "radio compression, always full"). Mentions **Trackspacer** as the frequency-domain alternative. → [[Sidechaining]], [[BED Slot]] ducks for the [[LEAD Slot]]. *(Ratio to taste, slow release ~150–300 ms, key = lead vocal.)*
- **Kick from two layers** (a sample + a kick sound) → [[Kick Design]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]] / [[SUB Slot]].
- **Dilla-sample drums** — a chopped hi-hat loop cut on the train + a rattle from a Dilla pack; "still my favourite sounds, use them today." A 909 hi-hat over the top. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Sound Selection Guide]].
- **Korg Mini Pops** drum machine for delicate cowbell/ride/cabasa (Howard's favourite) → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]].
- **Crashes made from noise** — the ESP noise generator + resonance + filters, then pan + tremolo, instead of a crash sample. → [[Modulation]] / [[TEXTURE Slot]]; a sound-design staple.
- **Sample the one good note** — the Juno bass resonance didn't track evenly across the keyboard, so he sampled the single note where it behaved and pitched *that* around (+ a rim on top for attack). "Three notes but it gels." → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[BASS Slot]].
- **ESP (Logic Juno copy) is the early-Disclosure sound** — free with Logic, sawtooth + square (toggle, not blend), cutoff automated throughout; "made hits with it." → [[BED Slot]], [[Sound Selection Guide]].
- **Ring Shifter on the sawtooth = "the key to this sound"** — movement + stereo width + resonance/phase; "our sound for a while." → [[Modulation]], [[Stereo Field]].
- **[[Pro-Q 4]] to carve vocal space** — cut low-mids on the chords so they stop clashing with the vocal. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[BED Slot]] vs [[LEAD Slot]].
- **Beeps** = ESP + tremolo pan + a **bit crusher** for "silk"/character on top. → [[MIDS Slot]], [[Saturation and Distortion]].
- **Logic Sampler factory violins (legato)** as a "stringy synth" for movement/single notes; mostly muted, filling a spot under a belting vocal. → [[BED Slot]] / [[AIR Slot]].
- **Fresh-mix rationale (drum bus):** re-mixed because the old track sounded quiet next to newer ones in DJ sets — added **API bus compression + tape** he "didn't know about back then." → [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]], [[Compression]], [[Limiting]], [[Gain Staging]].
- **Reverb:** Space Designer "Sax reverb" — a dull/dead preset that "doesn't sound too digi." Subtle quarter-note delay. → [[Reverb]], [[Delay]].
- **Chords:** B♭7sus4 → Fm add11 → E♭11; middle-eight adds one chord; harmonies a **tone apart (2nds)** that work over both chord sets — harmonically rich. Extended **feedback-delay outro** for DJ-friendly mixing.

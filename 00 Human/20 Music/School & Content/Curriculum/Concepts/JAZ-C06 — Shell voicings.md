# JAZ-C06 — Shell voicings

**Q.** What is a shell voicing, which of its two orderings usually sounds better, and when is it the right voicing rather than the cheap one?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Root + 3rd + 7th — three notes, no 5th, no extensions.** Root–7th–3rd, with the 7th below the 3rd, is usually the better-sounding of the two orderings.
>
> It is the *right* voicing, not the lazy one, whenever something else needs the register: under a busy vocal, or with a second chordal instrument in the arrangement. A full five-note chord in that spot is the mistake. Extensions stack on top of it — shell plus 9th, shell plus 13th — which is the whole vocabulary.

## Why it matters

Shells are the practical form of guide-tone thinking. They are easy to grab on guitar or piano, they never sound muddy, and they leave the entire register above free for melody or for another player.

For Nate's own tracks that is the direct payoff: a shell under a vocal leaves the topline alone, and it is the same fix as cutting a pad's low mids, done at the writing stage instead of the mix stage.

## Drill

Play a ii–V–I as shells in root–7th–3rd order, then again as full five-note chords, both under a vocal or lead you already have. Decide which one you would actually keep.

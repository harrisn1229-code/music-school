## What's taught

- **Deliberate practice is the proven mechanism for improvement, and talent is explicitly not a factor** — Ericsson's *Peak* is cited as finding no evidence of innate talent across thousands of studied world-class experts, including musicians. Deliberate practice is described as genuinely hard, unrewarding-in-the-moment, and taxing ("like a super hard workout") — not something willpower alone sustains.
- **Consistency-before-quality is the strategic on-ramp, not a lowering of standards.** Committing directly to hard deliberate practice without first building a consistency habit is framed as a common failure mode — perfectionism means unpolished early attempts read as "not having what it takes" and cause people to quit. The fix: make the goal *only* "did I show up today," decoupled entirely from how good the session was, until an identity shift happens ("I am someone who plays guitar every day").
- **The "don't break the chain" / Seinfeld method, with a specific twist — the daily minimum is deliberately trivial.** Commit to touching the guitar every single day, but set the daily bar as low as playing **one single note or one single chord** — that counts. The point isn't the one note itself; it's keeping the habit's "barrier to entry" permanently unlocked so a bad day never compounds into a lost week. Track it (a wall calendar with X's, a journal, or a habit-tracking app — Jared uses one called Momentum). One flagged exception: genuine no-access days (travel, no guitar around) get a pass without guilt.
- **The 10-minute focused deliberate-practice challenge — the second option, for players already consistent.** Once the daily-touch habit is solid, add a single **timed, intense 10-minute block of deliberate practice** to every practice session (not necessarily daily) — full focus, no distractions, working on something you genuinely can't yet do. Requires a clear **mental model of the target** going in (what "success" sounds/looks like) so you know when you've hit it or where you're missing it — named as a core piece of what makes practice "deliberate" rather than just effortful.
- **Two sequencing rules, stated directly:** (1) the consistency habit should generally come *before* leaning hard into deliberate practice, because attempting difficult, ego-bruising work before the identity/habit is stable risks abandoning the habit entirely; (2) "playing guitar" and "practicing guitar" (deliberate practice specifically) are named as two distinct activities — both worth doing, but conflating them is part of why people feel like they're practicing when they're actually just playing.
- **A personal case study used as the worked example of the method:** Jared applied this same consistency-first framework to starting the YouTube channel itself — one rule only (a lesson video every Tuesday, no matter what), sustained for a full year (53 consecutive weekly videos) before he felt ready to prioritize quality/effectiveness over just showing up.

# Yer Blues — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Verse-A ×2 · Verse-B ×3 · Verse-C (guitar solo) ×2 · Outro (a Verse-A) into a fade. **Every section in the track is the same twelve-bar frame** ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]), and all the variety comes from re-parsing and re-feeling it in the foreground — which puts it in surprising company with "Rocky Raccoon".

The device is **splitting the same twelve bars two different ways**: Verse-A as **8 + 4**, Verse-B as **4 + 8**, with the opening phrase declaimed over dramatic pauses. The joke is that the same music fills the second and third phrases of every section regardless — the structure changes purely from where you cut it.

Harmonically it is the blues trio plus **♭III**, sitting where the approach to V would normally be ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]); the tune is genuinely blues, ♭3 and ♭7 throughout, with no stylisation there.

Pollack parses the meter as **6/8**, which contradicts the numbers Ringo counts in but is what makes the form come out right ([[RHY-C01 — Compound meter is felt in dotted beats]]). Two metric games run on top: most sections carry **one deliberately spastic extra beat in bar 10**, and the last Verse-B drops it, which is what lets the backbeat shift while the bar lengths stay put; and the instrumental sections run their eighth-note triplets at **double speed**, flipping the feel from ternary to four-square with no tempo change. The counterweight comes late, in a deliberately rough splice.

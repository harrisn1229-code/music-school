# Slap and Pop Guitar Technique

Bass-style slap-and-pop technique adapted to guitar — a percussive, pickless right-hand vocabulary for rhythmic, groove-driven riffs. Distinct from [[Fingerstyle Thumb Independence]] and [[Right-Hand Pinky Fingerstyle Technique]] (melodic fingerstyle vocabulary) — this is a dedicated rhythmic/percussive technique built from three combinable motions, not a fingerpicking pattern. Compare the bass-side version at [[Teeter-Totter Slap-Pop Technique]] — same family of motion, different instrument and string gauge.

## The three building blocks

1. **The slap.** Strike a string (typically the low E) with the side of the thumb, angled so the thumb doesn't hurt on contact — finding that angle and the right amount of force (not too hard) takes some experimentation. Goal: a clean, open ringing note from the slap, not excess buzz or pain.
2. **The pop.** Hook a finger (index to start; middle finger — or both together — is common for higher strings once comfortable) underneath a string and pull up until it snaps back against the fretboard. Guitar strings are lighter than bass strings, so less force is needed than the bass version of the same motion — aim for clear articulation, not maximum snap.
3. **The dead note.** Lightly touch (don't press down) the fretboard with the left hand to generate a percussive click with no pitch — a third rhythmic element, not a mistake to avoid.

## Combining them

- **Alternate slap and pop to a metronome first**, purely to build the switch between the two motions (slap on beat 1, pop on beat 2, etc.) before adding rhythmic variety — described as harder than it sounds because you're not just repeating one motion, you're switching between two different ones in time.
- **Left-hand muting is essential throughout.** For the slap specifically, block every string except the one being slapped so only the intended note rings — the left hand does more of the muting work in this technique than it might appear from watching the right hand alone.
- **A core 4-motion pattern:** slap (open string) → dead note → slap (again) → pop. This is the basic building block riffs get constructed from.
- **String-skip between the pop and the slap** for variety — assign the thumb to one string and a finger to a different, non-adjacent string; slap/pop's separated-finger mechanic makes this easier than jumping a pick between non-adjacent strings, since there's no picking angle to manage.

## Riff-building devices once the basics are solid

- **Extended hammer-on phrases in triplets**, layered onto the slap for the low note and the pop for the high note of each triplet group — adds legato motion inside the percussive pattern rather than every note being a fresh slap/pop.
- **A quick one-finger chord** (e.g. a power chord built from one fretted note plus two open strings) dropped into a slap riff for harmonic variety without needing a strumming or full-chord break in the rhythmic flow.
- **Every individual motion should stay audible, even at speed** — don't let the pops disappear under the slaps; equal articulation across all three motions is the technical bar to hit, same discipline as this page's sibling legato/picking-dynamics notes.

## Getting started safely

- Take it slow — both the slap and the pop can cause blisters or soreness early on; don't force volume/force before the motion is comfortable.
- Practice to a metronome from the start — this is an inherently rhythmic, groove-locked technique, so timing discipline matters more here than for most lead vocabulary.

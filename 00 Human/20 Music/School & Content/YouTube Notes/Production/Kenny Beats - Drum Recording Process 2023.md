# Kenny Beats — Drum Recording Process 2023

![](https://youtu.be/HTbdB6rWHNI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/HTbdB6rWHNI)

## Synopsis

Kenny's current live-drum recording rig: **four close mics, no room mic**, one deliberately weird overhead position, budget Telefunken dynamics on snare/kick next to vintage everything else, a 70s Neve broadcast console used purely as preamps, then hardware EQ → saturation → compression on the way back. Nate doesn't track a live kit today, but this is the reference for any future drum tracking and the signal-order lesson applies to drum processing in the box.

## Techniques

- **Overhead in the "wrong" place on purpose** — Neumann U87 NOT over the snare: low, out at knee height **between the snare and floor tom, aimed at the kick, far from the hi-hat**. A bright clean condenser close to hat/cymbals only adds ping; from this spot the single overhead hears the *whole kit including kick* and becomes a usable full-balance mic. → [[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]]
- **Four close mics, no room mic** — U87 overhead-ish, Stager SR-3 ribbon on top of the kick facing inward, Telefunken M80 on snare, M82 in front of the kick. He stopped using the across-the-room mic entirely: "I've been getting exactly what I want with just four mics." Rooms are optional; placement isn't. → [[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]], [[Depth and Space]]
- **Snare mic: ~1" above the rim, aimed near-straight down at the rim**, with a **Crash Guard shield** over it blocking hi-hat spill — mechanical bleed control beats gating after the fact. → [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]]
- **Budget mics can be first-choice** — the Telefunken M80/M82 are ~$200 each next to his $6k vintage options and the M82 is "my favorite kick drum mic by far, tested against all the vintage ones." Pick by result, not price. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Console as preamp only** — the vintage Neve broadcast console (modded with direct outs per channel) runs **no EQ, no filters: preamps + volume only**; all shaping happens later. Same discipline as the Apollo Unison stage: gain character in, decisions later. → [[Gain Staging]]
- **Post chain order: EQ → saturation/weirdness → THEN compress** — off the interface into hardware: Terry Audio CEQ first, then saturation or "some weird old tape echo thing" to mess the sound up, then **compress the messed-up version**. Compressing after the character is printed glues the dirt in. (Note: Disclosure runs comp → tape for peak control — both orders are valid tools; choose by intent: comp-last = glue the dirt, comp-first = tape as transient limiter.) → [[Compression Staging]], [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]]
- **Otari MX5050 tape machine — "almost too clean"** — recommended over pricier decks; a reminder that tape ≠ automatically dirty. Nate's [[Sony TC-630]] is the dirtier lane by design.
- **Triad-Orbit quick-release stands** — push-pull mic swaps, no re-threading clips; friction removal keeps you experimenting with placement. *(workflow, not gear-lust)*

# Little Darlin' — Joe Pass

> Jens Larsen transcribes and breaks down the opening ~20 bars of Joe Pass's chord-melody arrangement of this standard from *Intercontinental* — see [[This is How Joe Pass Plays Amazing Solos]] for the source video and the full routing. **Caption quality flag:** the source captions are garbled/auto-generated-style throughout (labeled "en" but reading like an auto-translated track); chord names below are Larsen's reconstructed reading, not verbatim caption text — treat as medium confidence.

## Chords
**Key:** C major (this arrangement) · **Form:** 32-bar-family swing standard — Neal Hefti's "Li'l Darlin'," famous from the Count Basie Orchestra's 1958 recording at a notoriously slow ~64 BPM ("an object lesson in how to swing at a slow tempo"); this page covers Joe Pass's solo-guitar chord-melody reading of it, in C.

The arrangement never opens on the tonic — **D7 → G7 → Cmaj7 (V7/V → V7 → I)** — before the same phrase recurs twice more, reharmonized differently each time (see the arrangement breakdown below). Recurring devices across the excerpt: the diatonic **iii (Em) turned into its own dominant, E7**, a Joe Pass trademark that doesn't need to resolve anywhere; a basic **I–IV–I** passage under the F6–B♭7–C6 phrase (IV7 borrowed as a color, not a real modulation); and, by bars 17–20, the same opening phrase harmonized a third time as a **straight ii–V throughout** (a Dm11-type ii moving to G7) — denser than the V7/V opening, the clearest ii–V–I stretch in the excerpt.

This is one specific arrangement's harmonization, not "the" one published chart for the tune — Pass reharmonizes the same phrase differently every time it returns, by design (see Steal this below). The analyzed excerpt covers roughly the first 20 bars (opening through the approach to the bridge), harmonized as chord melody (block chords under the melody, fills in the sustained-note gaps). See [[Secondary Dominants]], [[The ii-V-I]], [[Chord Melody Arranging]].

## Arrangement breakdown (Jens Larsen's transcription, opening ~20 bars)

- **Bars 1–2 (D7–G7–Cmaj7):** block-chord melody harmonization opens on V7/V rather than I; the D7 bar reads equally as a rootless D9 or an F♯ half-diminished voicing (same notes, root-dependent identity); a ♭9 color lands on G7 immediately before the Cmaj7 resolution. → [[Chord Voicings]], [[Secondary Dominants]]
- **Bars 3–4 (fill, Cmaj7–F7–E7–A7–D7):** the first sustained-note fill, built by moving through one voicing rather than a scale run; the diatonic iii (Em) is reharmonized as **E7**, the Joe Pass "turn a diatonic chord into a dominant" trademark documented in full on [[Secondary Dominants]] § *From videos*. A ♭9→♯9 slide colors the same E7 mid-fill. → [[Secondary Dominants]], [[Altered Chords]]
- **Bars 5–8 (phrase repeated, reharmonized):** the same melodic phrase returns with different harmonization — a rootless D7 voice-led by a 3rd interval, a shape-shift-by-two-frets converting F-triad material into C7, and a stripped-down G7-to-rootless-C7 fill (root, tritone, resolve) with a concrete fingering for adding the ♭9. → [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Bars 9–12 (IV-chord passage, F6–B♭7–C6):** a fuller melodic statement over basic I-IV-I harmony, voiced partly in 4ths rather than tertian stacks; the video's clearest worked example of "arpeggiate a complete chord voicing instead of harmonizing note by note" appears here (an F7-flavored fill). → [[Chord Melody Arranging]], [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Bars 13–16 (phrase repeated again):** the trademark dominant-substitution recurs (E7♯9 standing in for the diatonic chord, possibly Pass's own addition per Larsen), plus a ♭13-to-natural-13 A7 reharmonization Larsen associates with bossa nova, bridging into D7. → [[Secondary Dominants]], [[Altered Chords]]
- **Bars 17–20 (approach to the bridge):** the opening phrase returns a third time, now harmonized as a straight ii-V throughout (denser than the V7/V opening); a Dm11 (ii-7/11) voicing arpeggiated as a triplet is the fullest worked "voicing-as-fill" example in the video; a repeated "circling the tritone of A7" figure is named as a recurring, genuinely typical Pass device beyond this one tune. → [[Chord Melody Arranging]], [[Bebop Vocabulary]]

## Steal this

- **Harmonize a repeated melodic phrase differently each time it recurs** rather than reusing the same voicings — Pass reharmonizes the same opening phrase three separate times across this short excerpt.
- **Use the tune's sustained/held melody notes as the designated slot for chord-derived fills** — look for where a melody holds before deciding where a fill belongs, rather than filling everywhere.
- **Turn a diatonic minor chord (especially the iii) into its own dominant seventh as a standing color, without needing it to resolve anywhere** — a genuine, repeatable Joe Pass trademark, not tune-specific; full harmonic detail on [[Secondary Dominants]].
- **Arpeggiate a chord voicing you already know instead of harmonizing a fill note by note** — the single biggest reusable idea in the whole video; full writeup on [[Chord Melody Arranging]].

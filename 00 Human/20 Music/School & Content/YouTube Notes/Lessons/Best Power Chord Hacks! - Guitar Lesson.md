## Synopsis

BERNTH teaches five power-chord replacement voicings for song/riff writing, all built by the same method: replace the octave (the "most boring note" in a plain power chord) with a color tone drawn from the scale, found by ear/visualization rather than memorized as a named chord shape. A rich complement to the existing power-chord-tension material on [[Chord Voicings]].

## What's taught

Full extraction routed directly — see [[Chord Voicings]] for the complete breakdown: A5(♭6) for a dark, semitone-rub tension against the 5th; A5(♭6)+octave+♭3 for a settled natural-minor/Aeolian color; A5(9,♭3) for an open "floating" sound usable in major or minor context with the ♭3 omitted; A5(♭9,♭3) for a harsh Phrygian-flavored dissonance needing deliberate resolution; and A5(♯7, no 3rd) for a harmonic-minor-colored, tonally-ambiguous sus-like voicing. General method: visualize the scale on the fretboard, pick a color interval, find any nearby fingering — don't look up fixed shapes.

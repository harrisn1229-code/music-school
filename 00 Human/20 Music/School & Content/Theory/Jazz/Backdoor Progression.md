# Backdoor Progression

Part of [[Music Theory]] → [[Jazz Harmony]]. A jazz-standard alternative to the plain V7→I perfect cadence: reach the tonic via the minor iv chord and the ♭VII dominant instead.

## The progression

**iv7 – ♭VII7 – I** (in C: Fm7 – B♭7 – C, often voiced Cmaj9 for a soulful, "flavorsome" landing). A borrowed/modal-interchange move rather than a secondary dominant proper — see [[Secondary Dominants]] for the related-but-distinct device.

## Pop/R&B examples

- **"Stay" (Rihanna)** — Am7 (minor iv) → D (♭VII), key of E.
- **"No Such Thing" (John Mayer)** — the entire chorus is essentially one long backdoor progression.
- **"Underground" (Ben Folds Five)** — quick backdoor progression, key of B.
- **"I Want to Be Where You Are" (The Jackson 5)** — Gm7–C7–Dmaj7, key of D.
- **"The Waiting Line" (Zero 7)** — the song's *main* progression is a backdoor progression in full, key of C.
- **"Lights Up" (Harry Styles)** — the chorus resolves an otherwise A♭-minor song to A♭ **major** via the backdoor move — a minor-key song deliberately resolving major.

## Stevie Wonder's repeated use — the ii-V-of-iv setup

Wonder used the backdoor progression twice on *Songs in the Key of Life*, both times preceded by a **ii–V targeting the IV chord** that gets redirected to land on the *minor* iv instead of the expected major IV — so the "2-5 of 4" flows directly into the backdoor progression's opening chord.

- **"Knock Me Off My Feet"** — key of C: the 2-5-of-4 setup redirects into Fm7–B♭7–Cmaj7.
- **"Ordinary Pain"** — key of F: same device, Fm7's-family setup into B♭m7–E♭9.

## Jazz standard usage

- **"Misty"** — backdoor progression under the lyric "clinging to a cloud."
- **"Just Friends"** — Cm7→F7→Gmaj7, key of G.
- **"Stella by Starlight"** — an unusual case: the backdoor move resolves not to the piece's ultimate tonic (C) but to a *local*, temporarily-tonicized G (the tune has modulated to G at that point in the form, common in older jazz standards) — a "backdoor progression of five" rather than of the true I.

## The "backdoor dominant" — a lighter shorthand

♭VII7 resolving straight to I with **no preceding minor-iv chord** — the same resolving effect in miniature.

- **"New York State of Mind" (Billy Joel)**
- **"Layla" (Derek and the Dominos)**
- **"Again and Again and Again" (Wings)**
- **"You Are So Beautiful" (Billy Preston)**

## Major-iv variant

**"Midnight at the Oasis"** uses the *major* IV chord (not minor) leading into the ♭VII7→I resolution — a related but distinct variant, key of E♭ (A♭→D♭9→E♭maj7).

# Disclosure's Signature Kick (How To Make It & Why It Sounds So Good)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N60Jcn9zfBo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N60Jcn9zfBo)

## Synopsis

An analytical breakdown (Sol State studied 9-10 Disclosure records directly) of what makes Disclosure's kick sound distinctive, paired with Guy's own explanation of his synthesis process. The single most transferable idea: decompose any kick into transient/body/tail and diagnose problems by section instead of judging "the kick" as one whole thing. The analytical finding: Disclosure kicks are short, dark, and deliberately sit *behind* the bassline in the mix — the bass, not the kick, usually owns the low-end spotlight.

## Techniques

- **Decompose every kick into three parts — transient (the click), body (the punch), tail (the sub decay) — and diagnose which section actually needs fixing** rather than judging "the kick" as one indivisible thing. A kick that feels wrong is almost always wrong in one specific section, not uniformly. → [[Kick Design]]
- **Disclosure's kick transients are consistently darker/less clicky than typical EDM kicks** — a genre-level tonal choice, not a mistake to correct toward brighter/clickier.
- **The bassline, not the kick, is the intended low-end focus — the kick body sits deliberately behind it in the mix.** A/B'd directly: kick level vs. bass level shows the kick is still audible/felt but the bass is the actual foreground low-end element — a genre-specific balance choice distinct from EDM/house conventions where the kick usually dominates. → [[Bass Design]], [[Masking and Carving]]
- **Extremely short kick tails — mostly around an 8th note, some as short as a 16th note** — measured directly across 9-10 records. A short tail leaves maximum space for a busy bassline underneath it, which is the real reason the kick can afford to sit behind the bass without disappearing. → [[Kick Design]]
- **A pitch envelope that starts higher (around 73 Hz) and falls to a lower sub note** is the typical Disclosure kick envelope shape — distinct from a kick that's simply short in amplitude; the pitch itself is moving, not just the volume.
- **Starting point: a kick-synthesis plugin (Big Kick), tuned to the song's key, with Attack and mostly Decay/length as the main per-song adjustments** — described as no fixed "process," just a saved preset loaded and then reshaped per track; length (Decay) is the parameter reached for most, tuned specifically shorter/punchier when the bassline is busy so the two don't compete rhythmically.
- **Layering caution: more layers isn't automatically better — careless layering causes phase cancellation that can silently undo the layering's whole benefit.** Before adding a new layer, diagnose whether the kick is actually missing something a new layer would add, or whether the real fix is swapping the base kick sound entirely.
- **A concrete 3-layer kick recipe, each layer with ONE job:** a body layer for punch (with sub content deliberately rolled off so it doesn't fight the sub layer), a dedicated sub layer for the low-end weight you feel physically ("what you feel at the festival"), and one or more click/transient layers for top-end definition. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Kick Design]]
- **A downward pitch envelope on an 808 (demoed in Battery) adds or subtracts perceived "pop"/punch depending on its depth** — comparing the same 808 with the pitch envelope on vs. off shows a clear difference around the 100 Hz region; the envelope amount is a direct punch dial, exaggeratable for more attack or minimized for a smoother tone. → [[Kick Design]]
- **"Frequency guides": build a reference EQ curve from a target artist's kick/bass sound, then match your own against it instead of judging by ear alone.** Concretely: analyze where a reference kick/sub peaks and extends in the spectrum, then check your own kick/bass against that curve to see exactly where it's diverging (the worked example catches a kick running slightly loud around 50 Hz and in the sub range, fixed with a targeted EQ cut) — turns a vague "my kick doesn't sound right" into a specific, correctable difference. → [[Referencing and Translation]]
- **Three big-picture takeaways:** there's no universally "perfect" kick — it's genre- and taste-dependent; Disclosure's kicks specifically ride short and high in the sub range to leave room for a busy bassline; and Disclosure's overall mixdown balance reads closer to a pop record than a typical house record — the kick sits a few dB lower overall than a comparable melodic house/Afro house/big-room EDM mix would run it.

# Casio RZ-1

## What it is
Casio's consumer-grade sampling drum machine — a preset PCM kit *plus* four user-sample pads (0.8 seconds total!) at a toy-store price. The cheapest ticket into sampling in 1986, which makes it a folk hero of lo-fi and early bedroom hip hop/house.

## What makes the sound
- **8-bit / 20 kHz user sampling** — dark, aliased, crunchy; anything sampled into it comes back sounding like a memory of itself. The RZ-1 crunch is a lo-fi texture people now chase on purpose.
- **Preset PCM kit** in the bright-but-thin Casio mold — serviceable 707-adjacent drums with less body.
- Four sample pads with tuning meant chopped vocals, stabs, and found sounds landed *next to* the drums — a proto-[[E-mu SP-12]] workflow at 5% of the price.
- Plastic-box converters and a noisy output stage complete the aesthetic.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Sampler]]/[[Simpler]] + [[kHs Bitcrush]] (8-bit, ~20 kHz) nails the user-sample crunch; [[Drum Rack]] with RZ-1 kit samples; [[RC-20 Retro Color]] for the noise floor.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2; vintage archives.
- **Free:** RZ-1 kits and the classic crunch recipe above.

## Used on
- Genre/era: 1986–90 bedroom house/hip hop demos and the lo-fi/DIY lineage that followed — a democratization box whose credits live in obscure records; genre-level per the rule.

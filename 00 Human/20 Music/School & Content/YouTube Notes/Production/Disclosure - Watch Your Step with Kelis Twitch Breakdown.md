# Disclosure - Watch Your Step with Kelis Twitch Breakdown

Breakdown of "Watch Your Step" (ENERGY, with **Kelis**) — a breakbeat/garage cut written bass-line-first in a day at Capitol. Best material: the **lo-fi "megaphone" post-chorus vocal** built by recording Kelis through *the worst mic in the building* (a Shure 520DX harmonica mic) into a Decapitator chain; a clear statement of the **"a DAW loses the analog interaction — saturate/compress groups together to get it back"** philosophy; **layering three *different* saw synths** so a stacked chord has character; the **Dilla-clap-ahead-of-the-beat flam**; and a signature **dub-siren noise reused across the album for cohesion** (nicked from Pearson Sound's "Wad"). Whisper transcript (no captions) — high confidence.

## Techniques

### Writing & arrangement
- **Write to the bass line, add chords last for texture** — unusually for them, the breakbeat came first, then the bassline, then chords only for texture (normally chords-first). The chords are literally **one chord (B minor) in two inversions** with a 9th/7th on top; the moving bass makes it feel like two. Fits the album ethos: get the initial idea out fast, keep it fun; "the song wrote itself" — if it's the one it's easy, else write something else (no 30 versions / 5 masters). → songwriting.

### Drums (breakbeat + 909)
- **Dilla clap pushed *ahead* of the beat for a human flam** — layer 2–3 snares + a Dilla clap that lands early, so it flams against the breakbeat and reads as a real hand clap that wouldn't be perfectly in time. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[2 Drums Group|Drums]].
- **909 ride 16ths + 909 hats, heavily sidechained**; garage hats (downbeat/offbeat/cabasa/tapy), a background tom, Splice shaker loops, noise sweeps, a sneaky cowbell tiny in the right speaker. Take the breakbeat out and it's a straight 4-4 — the break is what makes it busy. → [[2 Drums Hats Group|Drums Hats Group]].

### Synths & the "cohesion noise"
- **Layer three *different* saw synths, not one tripled.** All the chord synths are sawtooth (Juno beeps, an Arturia "Prophet" on the "old butt" preset with lots of noise/grit, [[Poly6]]) but chosen as different synths so each has slightly different attack/character and they sit together. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[Sound Selection Guide]].
- **Reuse a signature sound across the album for cohesion** — a "vibrato sine / dub-siren" noise (and the [[Poly6]]) recur on many ENERGY tracks (incl. the title track) as a deliberate cohesion device, since every song has a different singer. Nicked from **Pearson Sound – "Wad"** (late-2000s UK dub-siren). → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[House - EDM]].

### Mixing philosophy (the key one)
- **A DAW loses the analog "everything runs through the same wires/transistors" interaction — recreate it by saturating/compressing *groups* together.** Even a barely-doing-anything [[Saturn 2|Saturn]] (clean-tube) across a pad bus matters because it *sums and interacts* the audio, the thing you miss vs a tape machine or desk. Do it on pad/bass/drum buses. → [[Compression Staging]], [[Saturation and Distortion]].
- **Bus processing is the final 10%, not the fix.** The drum bus (comp → tape → [[Oxford Inflator]] → Saturn → EQ → limiter) adds the most character — night and day — *but* "if you choose bad sounds, no bus processing saves it." Get the sounds/groove right first; the bus polishes. → [[Compression Staging]].
- Pad bus = dynamic-EQ the low-mids (they boost when 2–3 synths overlap) + Saturn clean-tube glue. Bass bus = heavier (two bit-crushers, two Saturns — tape + amp). Most delay/reverb/overdrive/pan/tremolo happens on each synth *before* the bus. → [[Multiband Compression]].

### The lo-fi "megaphone" post-chorus vocal (signature move)
- **Record the vocal through the *worst mic in the building* — a Shure 520DX "Green Bullet" harmonica mic** — it captures no low-mids and no harsh super-highs (and has a built-in gain/preamp), giving the megaphone/telephone base tone *before* any plugins. Tracked **right in front of the speakers, no booth**, so the kick bleeds in. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Saturation and Distortion]].
- **Processing chain to "1940s gramophone":** cut rumble → hard comp → lots of overdrive → de-ess → **[[Decapitator]] taking the lead** → extra crackle/distortion → more comp → **brutal EQ (boost ~1 kHz for the "little old radio" sound**, lift the distorted crackle up top) → [[Oxford Inflator]] (driven into distortion) → de-ess + **[[Soothe2]]** (keeps it from being painful) → delays → reverb → bit-crusher (demonic finish). The plosive P's breaking up *are* the character. → [[Saturation and Distortion]].
- **Pre-grit the clean vocal toward the distorted section** — on the main vocal bus, push a distortion (CamelPhat) hard but at ~20% mix so a little lo-fi radio grit sits under the clean verses, easing the ear toward the fully-crushed post-chorus (a "halfway house," not clean→distorted with no bridge). → [[Saturation and Distortion]].

### Clean vocals (Kelis)
- **M49 → Neve 1073 → Tube-Tech → Pro Tools** (their favorite mic — Howard has "49" tattooed; a whole desk of 1073s). Light Melodyne, mostly *timing/alignment* — dislikes Logic's warp algorithms (they degrade quality), Melodyne moves audio more cleanly. → [[LEAD Slot]].
- **Per-vocal:** Tube-Tech (~3 dB) → **Surfer EQ** (note-following low-mid/tone control that tracks the root of her voice — "one notch instead of five"; reinforces [Birthday](https://youtu.be/b-6x_4XRwOQ)) → EQ (notch a resonance) → cut more low-mids (two vocals double them) → [[Gem Dopamine]] (a little air) → de-ess → Saturn. → [[Masking and Carving]].
- **Vocal bus:** LA-76 (catch peak tips) → CamelPhat grit (20%) → EQ → multi-stage de-ess → [[Oxford Inflator]] (2–3 dB) → [[Soothe2]] (catches harsh moments) → **echo-boy automated to fill the gaps between phrases** → reverb → an **80s short slapback delay** (Howard's "short delay on every vocal" habit) → sidechain heavy to kick. → [[2 Main Vocal Group|Main Vocal Group]], [[Delay]].
- Whispers panned ~49% L/R; **[[Little AlterBoy]] low octave** (subtle fill — didn't need much lows, the bass is huge). **One harmony only** — "one chord, one harm, make them both really good" (restraint). Kelis half-talks/half-sings (raspy tone) — they specifically wanted talking, not just singing. → [[Width and Stereo]].

# Course 28 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part Two

The second half of Section Two of Leavitt's *A Modern Method for Guitar, Volume 1* — pages 100 to 123, which closes the volume: the last of the five second-position fingerings, the last Reading Study, the review that plays all five fingerings back to back, and the same shapes moved to positions III, I and IV.

**What it assumes**

- [[Course 27 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part One]] — pages 60 to 99: the position system, finger stretch, and fingering types 1, 1A, 2 and 3 read in the second position with no open strings.
- A guitar, [[A Modern Method for Guitar - Volume 1.pdf]] open on a stand, and a metronome.
- No theory. This course teaches no ideas and tests none — it is the place you play the page.

| Lesson | Title | Pages | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fingering Type 4 and the Second Position Review | 100–111 | performance check |
| 2 | Positions III, I and IV | 112–123 | performance check |

**What this unlocks**

- The complete set of major-scale fingering types — 1, 1A, 2, 3, 4 — which are the foundation for every other kind of scale, and the direct on-ramp to *A Modern Method for Guitar, Part II*.
- Reading the most-used keys in positions I, III and IV, with no new shapes to learn — only new notes to read.
- The rest of the derivative-fingering chord sequence of Rhythm Accompaniment Part Two: roughly sixty forms across the two Reading II courses, each derived from the one before it by moving or removing a finger.
- The end of Volume 1, and the outside-reading habit p. 123 prescribes — which outlives the book, the course and the class.

## The reading-study rule still applies

The fifth and last of the Reading Studies is pp. 102–103, in Lesson 1. Leavitt's instruction from the top of p. 64 governs it exactly as it governed the four in [[Course 27 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part One]]:

> **DO NOT PRACTICE these two pages** — just READ them, but not more than twice through in any single practice session. Do not play them on two consecutive days. Do not go back over any particular section because of a wrong note. **DO KEEP AN EVEN TEMPO** and play the proper time values.

A Reading Study you have memorised is no longer a reading study. Everything else in this course is practice material and should be practised. That one page pair is not — and the way it is read is the way p. 123 asks you to read everything after the book runs out.

## How to run it

- **Position and finger stretch, from pp. 60–61, still govern every page.** Position is the fret the **first finger** plays, written as a Roman numeral, and a position occupies four adjacent frets; anything outside that span is a **finger stretch (F.S.)** made without shifting the hand, and you always move *back into* the position from a stretch. Lesson 2 moves that four-fret span to three new places.
- **Nothing new is taught after p. 111.** The box at the foot of that page is the hinge: the same five fingerings transpose to any position, so position III gives C♯/D♭, F♯/G♭, A♭, E♭ and B♭ with no new shapes to learn. Lesson 2 is reading, not learning.
- **The chord track finishes here.** Rhythm Accompaniment Part Two's forms stay ordered so each new shape prepares the next: *"Memorize the fingering for all chord structures in the order of their appearance… Do not skip around."*
- **The duets need a second part** — a recording, a teacher, or DAW playback of the other line. Both guitar parts are written to be studied, and all three position reviews are optional duets.
- **Pages 124–127 are the author's closing notes and the index.** Read once; not a lesson.

## The lessons

### Lesson 1 — Fingering Type 4 and the Second Position Review

**The material**

- **Pages 100–111.** The last of the five second-position keys, the last Reading Study of the volume, and the review that plays all five fingering types back to back.
- **The 2nd Position Review, pp. 108–109**, runs types 1, 1A, 2, 3 and 4 in sequence over chord symbols, and can be taken as a duet — melody as written against a Latin beat, or melody in a dotted-eighth feel against straight four.
- **Quarter-note triplets, p. 111**, taught as a physical trick rather than a count: play two sets of eighth-note triplets with alternate picking, then two more sets missing the string on the up strokes.
- **The box at the foot of p. 111** is the hinge into Lesson 2: the same five fingerings transpose to any position, so position III gives C♯/D♭, F♯/G♭, A♭, E♭ and B♭ with no new shapes to learn — only new notes to read.

**Homework**

- [ ] **Play** — the *A Major* scale, fingering type 4, 2nd position, p. 100, and the *Eighth Note Study* under it.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Arpeggio Study* in A, p. 101.
- [ ] **Play** — *Chord Etude No. 5*, p. 101. Rubato.
- [ ] **Read** — the *Reading Studies* in A major, fingering type 4, pp. 102–103. Twice through at most, never two days running, even tempo through the wrong notes.
- [ ] **Play** — *Tres Sharp* (duet), pp. 104–105. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — the *16th Note Study*, p. 105, counted carefully against p. 31.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Chord Forms* on p. 105 and their exercise.
- [ ] **Play** — *Speed Study – fingering type 4*, p. 106, as written and with a dotted-eighth feel, with and without repeats.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Chord Forms* on p. 107 and all three exercises, then transpose and write out each one a key or more higher, as the page asks.
- [ ] **Play** — the *2nd Position Review*, pp. 108–109. All five fingering types in sequence, then again as a duet with a Latin-beat rhythm part.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Chord Forms* on p. 110 and both exercises. The third major 6th form is the one worth keeping — no first string, better rhythm sound.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Quarter Note Triplets* exercise, p. 111, foot tapping in four, until the figure is felt rather than counted.
- [ ] **Check** — a cold read of an unseen page at or below p. 111, once, at tempo, with no practice run.

### Lesson 2 — Positions III, I and IV

**The material**

- **Pages 112–123.** The same five fingering types moved to three new positions, each block closed by its own review. Nothing new is taught here — it is reading the fingerboard with patterns the hand already knows.
- **Position III, pp. 112–113** — B♭ as type 4, E♭ as type 3, A♭ as type 2, D♭ as type 1. **Position I, p. 117** — A♭ as type 4, D♭ as type 3, with no open strings. **Position IV, pp. 119–120** — G as type 1A, D as type 1, A as type 2, E as type 3.
- **The three position reviews** (pp. 114–115, 118, 122–123) are all optional duets with rhythm guitar, and 118 and 122 are in waltz time.
- **Leavitt's closing note, p. 125:** *"I cannot over-emphasize the importance of learning the four major scale fingering types well as they are the foundation for other kinds of scales."* Volume 2 learns the whole fingerboard by running every type **in the same key**.

**Homework**

- [ ] **Play** — *Major Scales in 3rd Position*, p. 112 — B♭ major as fingering type 4, E♭ major as type 3.
- [ ] **Play** — *Major Scales in 3rd Position*, p. 113 — A♭ major as type 2, D♭ major as type 1.
- [ ] **Play** — the *3rd Position Review*, pp. 114–115. Types 4, 3, 2 and 1 in sequence, then again as a duet with a Latin-beat rhythm part.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Chord Forms* on p. 116 and both exercises.
- [ ] **Play** — *Major Scales in 1st Position*, p. 117 — A♭ major as type 4, D♭ major as type 3, both closed fingerings with no open strings.
- [ ] **Play** — the *1st Position Review*, p. 118. Types 3 and 4, as a waltz-time duet with rhythm guitar.
- [ ] **Play** — *Major Scales in 4th Position*, p. 119 — G major as fingering type 1A, D major as type 1.
- [ ] **Play** — *Major Scales in 4th Position*, p. 120 — A major as type 2, E major as type 3.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Chord Forms* on p. 121 and both exercises, including the minor sequences written with augmented substitutes.
- [ ] **Play** — the *4th Position Review*, pp. 122–123. Types 1A, 1, 2 and 3, as a waltz-time duet with rhythm guitar.
- [ ] **Read** — pp. 124–125, the chord-form caution about weak bass degrees and the author's closing notes. Once, and not as a lesson.
- [ ] **Check** — a cold read of an unseen page at or below p. 123, once, at tempo, with no practice run.

## Course final

A **performance check**, not a test page. There are no `type: school-test` pages in this course and nothing here enters the spaced-repetition drip — sight reading is a motor skill, and the drip is for facts.

The check is the one the book itself prescribes on p. 123, and it outlives the course: *"Find additional reading material — be sure it is easy to execute — then read five or more pages every day. Play each page not more than twice through. Do not practice. Do not memorize. Do not use the same pages on consecutive days. Vary the material, and READ, READ, READ."*

So: an unseen page of outside material, played once at tempo with no practice run, keeping an even tempo through the wrong notes. Pass is your own honest read of whether it held together. Nothing in the system can hear the guitar, so nothing here pretends to grade it.

This is also the end of [[Class 8 — Guitar Sight Reading]]. The class has a finish line; the reading does not.

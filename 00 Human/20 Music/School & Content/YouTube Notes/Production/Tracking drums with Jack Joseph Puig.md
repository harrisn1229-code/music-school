## Synopsis

A short Mix with the Masters clip of Jack Joseph Puig (JJP) walking engineer Josh through a live drum mic setup by **deconstructing it live** — building the full kit sound back up mic by mic, soloing and A/B'ing each addition so the listener hears exactly what each individual mic contributes before hearing the whole blend. It's the tracking-stage counterpart to this vault's other Mix with the Masters drum sessions ([[Tracking live drums with Chris Lord-Alge]]), but where CLA's clip is almost entirely about EQ/level philosophy, JJP's is almost entirely about **mic choice and mic layering itself** — a genuinely dense multi-mic kick/snare/cymbal setup (five-plus mics on the kick alone once the character mics are counted) demonstrated component by component. Standout moves: a passive speaker used as an extra mic ON the snare (not just the kick) specifically to fill the 200–400 Hz lower-mid pocket and add tail/ambience; a subtle SPL transient-designer pass on the underneath mic; a heavily processed mono front/back "punch" mic pair; a deliberately bad-sounding "birdcage" mic layered in purely for attitude, contrasted against a cleaner "American" mic; and spot mics on individual cymbals layered under the overheads specifically to capture overtone/ring character the overheads alone don't carry. ## Techniques

- **Core teaching method: deconstruct the full kit sound live, adding one mic (or mic pair) at a time and soloing/A-B'ing each addition before moving to the next.** "What I'm going to do is start off by having you hear just the close... and then I will start to deconstruct it and let you hear the things individually." Every technique below is demonstrated this way rather than just described — the same "build it up, hear each layer's job" instinct already logged from Nick Launay's parallel-drum solo demos on [[Drum Mic Technique]], applied here to close-mic layering instead of parallel processing. → [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Kick built from a close dynamic ("D30," auto-caption unresolved — likely a D-series dynamic) + a Neumann U47 FET added on top, then a passive speaker added as a third kick mic and switched on/off to demonstrate its contribution.** Sequence: D30 alone → add FET47 → add the "sub speaker" → remove it again — the same unconventional-mic-as-extra-source move already logged from Paul Epworth's NS10-driver-on-kick trick, JJP's own variant of it. → [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Lo-fi / character mics)
- **Snare built from three layered mics — a dynamic (Shure "545"), a condenser (Neumann KM64), and a passive speaker used as a mic — with the speaker's job stated explicitly: fill the 200–400 Hz lower-mid pocket and add ambience/tail.** "If I build the components — 545, add the 64, then add a speaker — you can hear how it fills in that lower mid-range area, that two-three-four-hundred area, and actually adds a little bit of ambience to the snare, a little bit tail. So without it, it gets really tight... you want to use it to taste." A concrete frequency-and-purpose statement for a technique this vault previously only had on the kick (Epworth's NS10) — confirms the speaker-as-mic trick generalizes to snare, aimed specifically at lower-mid fill + tail rather than attack. → [[SUB Slot]], [[BASS Slot]], [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Lo-fi / character mics), [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]]
- **An SPL transient-designer pass run very subtly on the underneath/bottom mic (the KM56 from the intro) — "not a lot, just subtle, just add a little bit of attack," demonstrated with a direct A/B.** Owned best-match: [[Transient Master]] — a third confirmed use of "SPL-style" attack-only transient shaping on this vault, alongside Marcella Araica's printed-stem use and Tony Maserati's attack-reduction use, both already logged on that plugin's note. *(no attack amount given — qualitative, "subtle")* → [[Transient Master]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Front + back "mono, tight, center" mic pair — a distinct pair from the main kick/snare close mics — run through heavy compression and EQ (auto-caption unresolved unit, heard as "the 769"), demonstrated by pulling the processing out entirely.** "Take the 769 out — pretty big difference. I'm dropping the level, but listen to the EQ curve at the front mic... you can also hear that silky top end." Soloed alone the back mic "might not sound like it was that hard," but blended with the rest of the kit "it gets really — like, punchy" — a reminder that a support mic's contribution should be judged in context, not solo, which is the same discipline this vault already has for the room mic on [[Drum Mic Technique]]. *(exact EQ/compression amounts not given — described qualitatively per garbled unit name)* → [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[Compression]], [[EQ]]
- **The kick's front head is "flocked" — fabric/foam-lined for damping — mentioned in passing as an obvious pairing with the tight, punchy front/back mono mic pair.** "Of course the face has been flocked on, right — would only make sense." A drum-prep detail (damping material on the batter/front head to control ring and tighten the transient) rather than a mic-position or processing move, but it's the physical reason the front/back pair reads so controlled/punchy once summed. → [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[Kick Design]]
- **A deliberately bad-sounding "birdcage" character mic, run through its own distortion-flavored processing ("got the boiler on it"), added purely for attitude — explicitly NOT for fidelity.** "Obviously not great sound, but a [good] sound you can add for attitude." Immediately contrasted against a cleaner "American" mic that picks up the front end of the kick and snare well — the pairing is a clean/dirty character-mic contrast, the same family as this vault's other junk-mic entries (Disclosure's harmonica mic, the Shure 520DX "Green Bullet") but applied to a full drum kit rather than a vocal. → [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Lo-fi / character mics), [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Spot mics on individual cymbals, layered under the overheads specifically to capture overtone/ring detail the overhead pair alone doesn't carry.** "Here's just the overheads by themselves, now let me add the spots... you get a lot of the character of the cymbals — not only are you getting a lot of the character, but you're hearing all the overtones and all the ringing... it's really thick sounding." A direct extension of this vault's existing "overhead is the single most important mic" rule ([[Drum Mic Technique]]) — the overhead still carries the whole-kit picture, but a dedicated cymbal spot mic adds character the overhead's wider pickup pattern doesn't resolve. → [[Drum Mic Technique]], [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Final A/B: the birdcage + American character-mic pair switched fully off vs. on across the whole kit.** "With them off, when I turn them off, it gets very natural sounding. Turn them on — gets... much cooler." Confirms the character-mic layer is a deliberate, switchable "flavor" pass on top of an already-complete natural kit sound, not a fix for anything missing. → [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Lo-fi / character mics)

# The Modes Ranked by Brightness

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNY_ZCUBmcA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNY_ZCUBmcA)

## Synopsis

David Bennett argues that "happy/sad" is the wrong axis for describing modal tonality (Mixolydian has a major 3rd but doesn't read as simply "happy") and reframes it as a brightness spectrum instead — building it live by sharpening or flattening one scale degree at a time from the major scale, extending past the seven diatonic modes at both ends. This is largely a second confirming source for a brightness ladder already documented on [[Diatonic Modes]] from an earlier video, but adds the derivation method and two extra modes beyond the diatonic seven.

## Techniques

- **Build method: one degree at a time.** To brighten a mode, sharpen one scale degree; to darken it, flatten one. C major (Ionian) → sharpen the 4th → C Lydian (brighter). C major → flatten the 7th → C Mixolydian (darker) → flatten the 3rd too → Dorian → flatten the 6th too → Aeolian (natural minor) → flatten the 2nd too → Phrygian → flatten the 5th too → Locrian (darkest of the diatonic seven).
- **Past the diatonic seven, in both directions:** Lydian → sharpen the 5th too → **Lydian augmented**, brighter than plain Lydian. Locrian → flatten the 4th too → **Super Locrian / the altered scale**, darker than plain Locrian.
- **Mixolydian's character**: the flattened 7th weakens the strong resolve-to-octave pull major has, making it feel less directional/more content to just sit — described as "major's edgier cousin" rather than simply happy. This weaker pull makes it a common choice in rock, arguably more common there than the plain major scale. "You Really Got Me" (The Kinks) is cited (via a Leonard Bernstein clip) as pure Mixolydian throughout.
- **Dorian's character**: a halfway house between major and minor — has some of minor's darkness but without minor's full weight, giving it a cool/jazzy quality common in funk. The natural 6th (vs. minor's flat 6th) is the one distinguishing note. "Uptown Funk" (Bruno Mars) is built on the same two-chord Dorian vamp (i–IV) as "Mad World," just in the key of F instead of D. The Force/Binary Sunset theme (John Williams, *Star Wars*) briefly shifts from minor into Dorian for a single bar — a deliberate moment of brightening inside an otherwise minor piece.
- **Locrian's character**: the darkest of the diatonic seven, described (via the same Bernstein clip cited elsewhere in the vault) as "the unusable mode" because its diminished tonic triad never reads as a stable home. Cited example of real usage: "Dust to Dust" (John Kirkpatrick).

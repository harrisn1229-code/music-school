# Songs that use 11/8 time

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wS9t4Ftkz7Q)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wS9t4Ftkz7Q)

## Synopsis

Continues the odd-time-signature series (11/8, and its 11/4 sibling) with worked song examples, plus a genuinely useful mechanism section on how to decide whether an odd bar is better notated as 11/8 or 11/4 — the same question applies to any x/8-vs-x/4 pair.

## Techniques

- **11/8 vs. 11/4 is a notation choice, not a different sound — either can describe the identical bar, and the deciding factor is tempo, not the music itself.** Test: does the tempo make you naturally count "5 and a half" quarter-note beats per bar (too fast to subdivide further — notate as 11/8, doubling an impossible "5½/4") or a genuine 11 distinct beats (slow enough to feel 11 — notate as 11/4)? "Skirting on the Surface" (The Smile) is fast enough to read as 11/8; Dave Brubeck's "Eleven Four" is unambiguously 11/4. Many musicians sidestep the distinction entirely and just say a song is "in eleven."
- **A one-off odd bar embedded in an otherwise regular-meter song can be reinterpreted as several smaller bars for performer clarity, and which split you choose changes what gets emphasized.** "Here Comes the Sun" (Beatles): one unusual passage is transcribable as 11/8 + 4/4 + 2/4 + 3/8, but the video argues breaking the 11/8 bar itself into 3/8 + 3/8 + 3/8 + 2/8 is the better choice — same total beats, but it tells a performer exactly which beats to accent, rather than leaving an undifferentiated 11-beat bar to be felt by ear.
- **Grouping patterns catalog (how the beats within an odd bar are subdivided, each with a distinct feel)**: 3+3+3+2 (11 by Primus) reads as a 12/8 shuffle with the last beat dropped — "a wonky shuffle." 3+4+4 ("Crowned and Kissed," Esperanza Spalding, 11/4). 3+3+3+2 again but at a slower tempo reads as a lopsided waltz (Whipping Post, Allman Brothers, 11/4). 4+4+3 (a third grouping option for 11/4). General point: the SAME 11-beat bar can be subdivided multiple different ways, and the choice of grouping — not just the raw beat count — is what defines the bar's characteristic feel.
- **Odd meter, defined precisely**: a time signature is "odd"/complex specifically when the top number can't be evenly divided into groups of 2 or 3 (5, 7, 11 all qualify) — the resulting uneven subdivision is the actual source of the "lopsided" feel, not just an unusually high beat count.
- **Additional cited 11/8 examples**: Altered Beast IV (King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard); the verse of "Booji Boy" [transcribed "Blockhead" in the auto-caption] (DEVO); the Mario Kart 64 race-results music and Sagat's Street Fighter II theme (both video-game examples); Rosetta Stoned and "The Sound of Muzak" [likely mis-transcribed] (Tool, both 11/8), Fear Inoculum (Tool, also 11/8), "Litanie" [captioned "writing 2"] (Tool, better described as 11/4 per this video's own tempo test).

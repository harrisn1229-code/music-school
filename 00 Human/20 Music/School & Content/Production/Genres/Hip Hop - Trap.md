# Hip Hop / Trap

**What makes it the genre:** the low end IS the song — 808 + kick carry melody and groove simultaneously; everything else is negative space. Halftime feel (~130–160 BPM, snare on 3), hats as a lead instrument, hard-tuned vocal as texture, dry and upfront presentation (space comes from throws, not reverb).

## The pillars

### 1. 808 + kick — one instrument, two jobs
- 808 = sustain + note (owns the sub); kick = transient + knock (HPF ~45–50, keeps the click and 60–100 Hz). Strict lanes ([[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]], [[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]]).
- 808 follows the *chord roots* — it's the bassline, write it like one; slides (glide) between phrase-ends; don't kick every 808 note (copy pattern → delete most kicks — keep emphasis hits only).
- Audibility: clean sub + driven mids ([[Saturn 2]] split — the "heard vs felt" model); phone-check every bounce.
- The genre schism: **no sidechain** (Mixed by Ali school — EQ pockets + gate the kick tail) vs duck ([[Kickstart 2]]) — sparse trap = pockets, busy = duck.

### 2. Drums — selection over processing
- Crunchy one-shots from packs, already processed — audition until it knocks raw ([[XO]]), then almost nothing: clip ([[Gold Clip Track]]), maybe transient attack.
- Snare/clap on 3 · counter-percs in the *gaps*, never stacked · velocity-0 note trick to cut 808 tails.
- **Hats are the melody of the drums:** two-step base, rolls into snares (1/16→1/32), triplet-grid switches, pitch slides ("zaps"), alternate-hit panning, dropped-beat silences ([[1 Drums Hat Track|Drums Hat Track]]).

### 3. Vocals — hard-tune as timbre
- [[Auto-Tune Pro]] retune 0–20, Humanize low — the tune is a *sound*, not correction. Comp harder (4–6 dB), [[Saturation Send]] pushed, **dry**: slap delay instead of reverb ([[Delay Send Slap]]).
- Ad-libs: opposite of the lead — panned hard, RC-20'd, delay-thrown on line-ends ([[1 Backup Vocal Track|Backup Vocal Track]], throw-track workflow in [[Delay Send Other]]).
- Doubles only on hook emphasis words, not whole verses.

### 4. Music — dark loops, negative space
- 2–4 chords, minor, half-step motion for menace · dark pads LPF'd to 5–6 kHz, buried · bell/pluck/flute leads carry the hook ([[1 Synth Lead Track|Synth Lead Track]] hard-tune lane).
- The loop can be simple because the *drums* evolve — mute/bring back elements every 8 bars (the arrangement IS drum muting).

### 5. Mix presentation
- Loud, dry, compressed: master pushes −7 to −8 LUFS ([[Master Chain]] trap variation), clipper-forward.
- Vocal sits ON the beat (not blended into it) — minimal room send, presence boost, beat ducks spectrally under it ([[Sidechaining]] frequency-domain tricks).

## Arrangement

**Section map** — *"SICKO MODE"*, *"Mask Off"*, *"Superhero"*

| Section | Bars | Slots filled | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 4–8 | BED (loop, filtered) · LEAD (ad-lib) | 2 |
| Hook | 16 | SUB · BED · LEAD · AIR · TRANSIENT | 5 |
| Verse | 16 | SUB · BED · LEAD · TRANSIENT (hats thinned) | 4 |
| Drop-out | 2–4 | LEAD alone, or total silence | 1 |
| Beat switch | — | a different set entirely (see below) | — |

**Entrances & exits**
- **Write the hook first, then remove things to make the verse** — the verse is the hook minus hat detail and minus one melodic layer.
- The 808 leaving is the biggest event available. Four bars with no sub before the hook returns does more than any riser.
- Hats are the finest-grained lane: rolls, dropped beats and grid switches carry the movement while everything else loops.
- **The arrangement IS drum muting** — see pillar 4. Elements mute and return every 8 bars; the loop itself never changes.

**The contrast move — subtract, then reintroduce.** No builds in the EDM sense. You take something out and put it back, and the return reads as a lift. Kenny Beats' version: claps out for a section, everything out at bar ends. The Southside version on this page: no kick for the hook's first 8 bars, then the kick and a counter-melody toggle together.

**Density rule: 3–5 slots, never more.** Negative space is the genre. SUB + LEAD + TRANSIENT is already a complete trap section. The 5–7 sound ceiling Kenny Beats names in pillar 4 is the same rule counted a different way.

**Beat switches** are a structural stunt, not a section — a full replacement of the loop and often the drum kit at the 2/3 mark. *SICKO MODE* runs three.

## Session recipe
140–150 BPM halftime · dark loop first (or sample flip: chop, pitch ±4 semis, halve/double speed, reverse a layer) · 808 pattern → kick emphasis → snare on 3 → hats last and most detailed · arrange by muting.

## From videos

- **Dynamic trap drums** — swing/off-grid hits, weirder perc, multiple beat-sections (Sicko Mode = 3 beats in one song), pan the aux percussion (kick/snare/808 stay center), drum-drop arrangement (claps out for a section, everything out at bar ends), and multiple bass *sections* (synth-bass "Mike Dean" section vs 808 section) — Kenny Beats, [Trap Drums More Interesting](https://youtu.be/fo-E8dY81Nw) → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Artists are the simplicity referee** — Lil Baby reacted at 5–7 sounds; the extra layer matters less than it feels while producing — Kenny Beats, [Trap Drums More Interesting](https://youtu.be/fo-E8dY81Nw)
- **The melody collage + bass reharm** — stack ~3 unremarkable airy melodies in one key (high octave / low octave / bell flutter) into a drone texture, then let a hard 808/bassline walking different roots imply the chord changes (Young Chop Chicago blueprint: the 808 line IS the song) — Kenny Beats, [Beat Mix & Creation Process](https://youtu.be/FC__hbvnETY)
- **No-kick trap is a real option** — an 808 with enough front-end attack carries modern trap alone; audition harder 808 samples before adding kick + sidechain — Kenny Beats, [808 & Kick + Subtractive EQ](https://youtu.be/4iAKvWWlCvY)
- **A concrete worked example of pillar 4's "half-step motion for menace": a 2–3 note chord voicing where the internal clash IS the whole chord.** Southside/Future-style dark synth trap: a D♯m triad with its top note pulled a semitone (F♯→F) starts the darkness before any processing; the next chord strips down to JUST the two clashing notes (F/F♯, a semitone apart); the progression resolves on a borrowed iv chord (G♯m) re-voiced with its top note dropped an octave to stay in range. Full breakdown on [[Chord Progressions]] and [[Southside Dark Synth Trap Chain]]. Arrangement-wise: no kick for the hook's first 8 bars, a half-time counter-melody effect toggled off exactly when the kick comes in (both together read as an energy jump), and a verse signaled by bouncing the intro pad to audio and pitching it down an octave rather than writing new material. → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — loner, [how southside makes dark synth trap beats for future from scratch (dark melody music theory)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TVjyuF5-xUo)

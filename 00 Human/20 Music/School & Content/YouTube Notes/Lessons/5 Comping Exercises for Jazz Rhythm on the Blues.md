# 5 Comping Exercises for Jazz Rhythm on the Blues

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6U10Q1T1w9c)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6U10Q1T1w9c)

## Synopsis

Jens Larsen's core thesis: "rhythm is much more important than the notes" in comping. Five specific comping rhythm patterns, each drilled first on a single shell voicing (no chord-quality distraction) and then taken through a full F blues using drop-2 voicings. The method for internalizing each: metronome on 2-and-4, isolate the rhythm alone before adding real voicings, then apply it to the full progression.

## What's taught

**Practice method (applies to all five):** learn each rhythm on ONE static chord/shell voicing first — the point is hearing and feeling the rhythm independent of harmonic content — then move it through the blues changes. Start with shells (root-3rd-7th only) if the full drop-2 voicings are too much at once; add real voicings once the rhythm is solid.

**The five rhythms:**
1. **Charleston rhythm** — hit on beat 1, then on the "and" of 2. The foundational reference groove; practice against a metronome clicking 2 and 4 (or all four beats if 2-and-4 is too hard at first).
2. **Push/anticipation rhythm** — state the chord on beat 1, then a longer sustained chord on the "and" of 3 that pulls the harmony forward into the next bar's chord change — creates forward momentum that the Charleston rhythm (which "just sits there") doesn't provide.
3. **Downbeat-anchored, soloist-friendly rhythm** — mostly straight downbeats, tied together by a simple 2-note back-and-forth melody line rather than by the rhythm alone (melody makes the rhythm easier to remember/internalize). Deliberately unobtrusive — safe to comp behind a soloist without stepping on their phrasing, since it mostly avoids syncopation.
4. **Off-the-one rhythm** — hits on the "and" of 1 and on beat 3, deliberately skipping beat 1 entirely. Framed as an entry point into offbeat comping generally; namechecks Red Garland's more extreme habit of anticipating harmony on the "and" of 2 and "and" of 4 (playing the next bar's chord early) as a further destination once this rhythm is comfortable.
5. **Response rhythm** — hits on beat 2, beat 3, and the "and" of 3. Designed as a call-and-response reply to a soloist's phrase that resolves on the "and" of 4 or on beat 1 — comping that answers rather than just accompanies. Explicit caution: don't over-elaborate a response rhythm, since a comping instrument's job is to support the soloist, not compete for attention ("an easy way to get fired").

**Voicings used throughout:** all five rhythms are demonstrated with drop-2 voicings over the F blues changes (F7, B♭7, B°, A half-diminished/D7, Gm7, C7alt turnaround) — cross-references Jens Larsen's own drop-2 material, matching this vault's [[Chord Voicings]] drop-2 coverage. When a chord tone is used as a melody note on top (e.g. F13 instead of bare F7), it's treated as an interchangeable color choice depending on context, not a fixed requirement.

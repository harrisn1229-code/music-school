# What is Mid Side Processing? (and how to use it during mastering)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z2zGgchC3bM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z2zGgchC3bM)

## Synopsis

Sage Audio explains the underlying mechanism of mid-side processing (why duplicating a signal and phase-inverting one copy creates a "side" image), how it shows up at tracking (MS mic technique), mixing, and mastering, and walks several specific mid-side EQ/compression/saturation moves for widening or narrowing a master. Most of the individual mid-side EQ recipes here overlap with deeper existing entries on [[Width and Stereo]] (side high-shelf, side HPF + mid boost for mono low end, vocal-carve EQ at 2-2.5 kHz) — this video's distinct contributions are the foundational phase-inversion mechanism itself, a mid-side compression widening technique, mid-side expansion/saturation isolated to one image, and a new specific frequency (~700 Hz side = guitar body).

## Techniques

- **The core mid-side mechanism: duplicate a signal to both channels, then phase-invert one copy.** An identical signal on both channels reads as centered (0°); inverting one channel's phase doesn't just cancel it out — panned appropriately, it produces the "side" image, spread to the full 180° field rather than capped at the normal 90° stereo field a pan pot alone reaches. This is the mechanism underneath every mid-side EQ/compression/saturation move already logged on [[Width and Stereo]], stated explicitly here for the first time in the vault. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Phase Alignment]]
- **Mid-side compression as a width tool: compress mid and side as separate channels, and the mid usually gets hit harder automatically.** Because the mid channel typically carries more signal energy than the side, mid-side compression naturally applies more gain reduction to the mid than the side — resulting in a relatively quieter mid and louder side, which reads as increased stereo width as a side effect of normal compression rather than a dedicated widening move. → [[Compression]] (effect), [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Mid-side expansion and mid-side saturation, each isolated to one image only.** Same logic as mid-side EQ/compression, applied to expansion and saturation specifically — e.g. saturating only the side image adds harmonic character/width without coloring the centered mid content (or vice versa), useful when a full-stereo saturation pass would over-color the mono-compatible core of the mix. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Width and Stereo]]
- **A new specific mid-side EQ target: ~700 Hz on the side image carries a lot of guitar body — boosting it makes guitars read bigger/wider.** Distinct frequency from [[Width and Stereo]]'s existing 7 kHz side-shelf and 80 Hz/2-2.5 kHz mid/side vocal-and-low-end recipes — fills in a guitar-specific slot in the same mid-side EQ toolkit. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[EQ]] (effect)
- **Delay-based stereo imaging is mechanically the same phase-cancellation principle as mid-side processing, just achieved by delaying one channel instead of inverting its phase.** Both push a signal beyond the normal 90° stereo field into the full 180° field via inter-channel phase difference — consistent with [[Width and Stereo]]'s existing delay-based widening entry, now explicitly tied to the same underlying mechanism as mid-side EQ. → [[Width and Stereo]]

# Roland Jupiter-8

## What it is
Roland's analog flagship — eight voices, two VCOs each, and the luxury polysynth of the early MTV era. Where the Juno was the people's Roland, the Jupiter-8 was the superstar's: huge, glossy, unmistakably expensive-sounding.

## What makes the sound
- **Two VCOs per voice with cross-modulation and sync** — real analog oscillators (drift and all) that detune into enormous stereo-wide pads and screaming sync leads a Juno physically can't make.
- **The Roland "sheen"**: the JP-8's filter (switchable 12/24 dB) is smooth and polite rather than aggressive — patches come out *polished*, sitting in a mix like they've already been produced.
- **The arpeggiator** through those two-osc voices is the early-80s new-wave signature — "Hungry Like the Wolf" energy is a Jupiter arp with the hold pedal down.
- Split/layer modes (bass + lead simultaneously) made it a one-synth arrangement machine on stage.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[DUNE 3]] and [[Serum 2]] cover the two-osc-detune + sync architecture well (use a gentle 24 dB filter, slow chorus/ensemble); [[Drift]] for lighter stock takes; [[Omnisphere]] ships sampled Jupiter-8 sources.
- **Paid:** TAL-J-8 (reference-grade), Arturia Jup-8 V, Roland Cloud Jupiter-8.
- **Free:** no serious free emulation — the DUNE/Serum recipe is the path.

## Used on
- Overmono — in their studio gear list (no specific technique given). → [[TN 187 Overmono - Gear List]] — Tape Notes
- **Single-note accent role: "Give Me a Sign" preset from the "Layers" Analog Lab bank, used for exactly ONE note across an entire Rhodes-led soulful sample, then pushed as the build's most heavily processed element (heavy reverb, mix 81/heavy decay, → [[Cassette]] Crushed preset) "to make it really stand out within the mix."** A deliberately opposite role to the Mike Dean layered-pad use below — here the Jupiter-8 does the least musical work (one note) but gets the most extreme processing, because its job is a single attention-grabbing hit, not a sustained texture. → [[Cassette]], [[Reverb]], [[Soulful Sample Stacking Chain]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — loner, [use this easy technique to take your samples to the next level!? (making a soulful sample)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vN0fgGDxp6E)
- Mike Dean — CS-80 + Jupiter-8 layered pads are "the core of the Mike Dean sound," heard across Travis Scott's *Astroworld*, "Pick Up the Phone," and the "Goosebumps" remix; digital-emulation recipe (Arturia Jup-8 V default patch: sawtooth + rectangle wave, wide Fine Tune, chorus/reverb/overdrive, modulated chorus + cutoff, layered with CS-80 V4 "Classic Strings") → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Layering and Stacking]] — loner, [mike dean's secret 'astroworld' synth techniques!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2bJGXDlSFdY)
- Duran Duran — *Rio* era: Nick Rhodes' Jupiter-8 arps and stabs ("Hungry Like the Wolf", "Rio") are the canonical documented showcase.
- Genre/era: early-80s new wave/synthpop luxury lane, Italo, and the [[Synthwave]] revival's "expensive pad" register → [[BED Slot]], [[LEAD Slot]].

# 6 Ed Sheeran Songs That 'Rip Off' Other Artists

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_V4ktmwIFAw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_V4ktmwIFAw)

## Synopsis

A third entry in this batch's copyright/influence mini-series (after [[18 Songs That 'Rip Off' Other Hits]] and [[7 Songs That 'Rip Off' Other Tunes]]), focused entirely on Ed Sheeran — case studies spanning legitimate credited interpolation through to a settled lawsuit, used to reinforce the "melody is what actually distinguishes a song, not chords/rhythm" principle from the earlier videos.

## Techniques

- **The chords-and-rhythm-aren't-ownable principle, restated with a stronger worked example than the earlier videos in this series**: Sheeran's "Thinking Out Loud" vs. Marvin Gaye's "Let's Get It On" share both an identical chord progression AND the same rhythm applied to it (strong enough that Sheeran himself medleyed the two live before any lawsuit existed) — but the video argues Sheeran is "well within his rights," because chords and rhythm are basic building blocks, and the actual melodies remain "different, distinct, and unique." Reinforces [[18 Songs That 'Rip Off' Other Hits]]'s framing: melody, not chords, is the right thing to judge similarity by.
- **A same-writing-session case study in intentional, credited interpolation done right**: Sheeran openly discusses intentionally interpolating TLC's "No Scrubs" into "Shape of You"'s chorus hook (wanted an R&B feel) — the "No Scrubs" writers are credited as a direct result. Same session produced "Strip That Down" (credited borrow from Shaggy's "It Wasn't Me," which itself sampled an earlier song whose writers are ALSO credited on Sheeran's track even though the actually-sampled element doesn't appear in it — a real-world illustration of how a sample's own upstream credits can propagate forward) and "Don't" (uncredited-in-video but described as clearly, intentionally lifting from Lucy Pearl's "Don't Mess With My Man").
- **A borderline case the video explicitly declines to call plagiarism**: a Sami Switch claim that part of "Shape of You" was lifted from his song "Oh Why" — video's own judgment: the shared hook is "a typical climb up the minor pentatonic scale" with generic lyrics, i.e. not distinctive enough to establish ownership, illustrating the same "is this a genre-common building block or a real unique lift" test used in [[7 Songs That 'Rip Off' Other Tunes]] for Uptown Funk.
- **A genuinely settled, undisclosed-terms lawsuit case study, discovered via social media rather than legal monitoring**: "The Rest of Our Life" (Sheeran-cowritten, recorded by Faith Hill/Tim McGraw) vs. Jasmine Rae's "When I Found You" — near-identical piano intro, matching key/tempo/time-signature/style, and similarly-phrased verse melody; the original writers only became aware of the similarity via a fan's tweet, then sued and settled for an undisclosed sum (apparently royalties-for-cash, not ongoing writing credit).
- **Video's own self-correction note worth preserving as a methodology caution**: the uploader's pinned description flags that the video itself "went too heavy with the 'plagiarism' framing" on some examples (specifically "Shape of You" vs. "No Scrubs," which the description now reframes as more likely a standard cleared interpolation than plagiarism) — a reminder that even a careful source can overstate a legal conclusion in the video itself, and the surrounding context (description, later corrections) is worth checking, not just the transcript.

# How "Cold Heart" was made from 4 classic Elton John songs

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t1ihDf0OkIQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t1ihDf0OkIQ)

## Synopsis

David Bennett reverse-engineers Elton John/Dua Lipa's "Cold Heart" as a 4-song medley built from archival Elton John recordings (Sacrifice, Rocket Man, Kiss the Bride, Where's the Shoorah) rather than new performances — reharmonized, retuned, and in one case vari-sped so that pitch and tempo both landed on the destination values in a single move.

## Techniques

- **Reusing an original vocal performance under an entirely new chord progression**: Sacrifice's 1989 vocal take is used unaltered, but its original I-IV-2-5 progression in D♭ major is replaced with a new B♭ minor (its relative minor) progression looped throughout Cold Heart — same notes, same performance, different harmonic context and mood.
- **Correcting an unintentional tuning artifact when reusing archival material**: the original Sacrifice master is ~50 cents flat (between D♭ and C major) due to a tape-speed shift after recording; the Cold Heart version is pitch-corrected back to concert D♭ major.
- **Matching a second sample's key AND tempo by transposition + time adjustment**: Rocket Man's chorus is transposed up a minor 3rd to match Sacrifice's key, reharmonized against the same B♭ minor loop (replacing its original I-IV progression), and slowed ~20 BPM to Cold Heart's 116 BPM tempo.
- **A single vari-speed move solving both key and tempo at once**: the "Where's the Shoorah" choir sample (E♭ major, 130 BPM) drops to D♭ major (Cold Heart's actual key) automatically when slowed to 116 BPM via vari-speed, since pitch and tempo are coupled when speed-shifting rather than independently time-stretched — described as a "really amazing coincidence" the producers likely found by testing which archival tracks could vari-speed cleanly into the target key/tempo.

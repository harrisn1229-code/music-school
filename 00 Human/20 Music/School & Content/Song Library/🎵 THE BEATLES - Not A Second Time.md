# Not A Second Time — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Verse (an odd **7 bars**, 2+2+3) · Verse (variant) · Refrain (10, in short uneven phrases) · Refrain (guitar solo) · Verse · Verse (variant) · Refrain · fade. No intro — the drums only enter at bar 5 of the first verse, which makes the opening half-verse feel retroactively like one.

Nominally G major, but the song keeps tipping toward E minor, and Pollack reads that as programmatic: the lyric claims unbendable resolve and the harmony does not believe it. The middle section is a **refrain, not a bridge** — it carries the hook and fulfils the verse rather than contrasting with it.

The best lesson here is how much work **one chord in one bar** does. The two verse versions are identical except for bar 6: with I there the verse feels closed at six bars and bar 7's V is an appendix; swap that bar to ii and the V stays open, so the last three bars build an expectation the refrain then satisfies.

**No V chord appears in the refrain at all.** vii° stands in for it, and because a diminished triad spells as vii of G *or* of E minor, it smooths the [[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]] when E minor lands in bar 9 where G was expected — and, unusually, the deception is never corrected. The persistent I → vi motion in the verses is the same [[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]] ambiguity at lower intensity. The tune is heavily pentatonic, with F♯ used as a 9–8 appoggiatura over the vi chord ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]). John is double-tracked with **no other backing voice at all**, and the outro simply vamps I → vi into the fade.

## Synopsis

A hands-on hardware maintenance video with a repair technician: memory-retention batteries in vintage programmable synths and drum machines, why they're a real risk (leaking, corroding circuit boards), and a live walkthrough replacing one (a Yamaha DX7) — testing, desoldering, cleaning corrosion, and correct polarity/solder order. ## What's taught

- **Every programmable synth or drum machine has a small memory-retention battery keeping patch/pattern data alive when powered off** — typical lifespan cited at 5 to 30 years depending on the unit. When it dies, the usual symptom is lost patches on power-up, and the usual fix is "just replace the battery and reload."
- **Some units are known leakers and deserve proactive inspection before failure** — the TR-808's battery specifically sits on the bottom of the case, directly over the circuit board, and is called out as leaking "all the time," corroding traces underneath it. Worth opening and inspecting on any TR-808-era gear even before symptoms appear, rather than waiting for a dead battery to prompt a look.
- **Test with a $5 multimeter before assuming a battery's condition** — check voltage at the battery terminals directly rather than guessing from symptoms alone; a battery marked "rechargeable" should be left to a technician (risk of it exploding) rather than swapped casually, though rechargeable memory batteries are uncommon in this gear.
- **Replacement procedure**: desolder the old battery (a desoldering iron/sucker, not just pulling it), clean any leaked corrosion off the board and contacts with isopropyl alcohol before testing continuity, confirm polarity by checking which terminal has the "+" marking on both the old battery and the board footprint, then solder the new battery in — **positive side first, negative side second**, specifically to avoid momentarily shorting the battery to ground, which risks it exploding if the connection isn't made fast enough.

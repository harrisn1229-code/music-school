# You Suck at Mixing No. 11: How to Make Your Own De-esser in Ableton

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y36rFBUYhEc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y36rFBUYhEc)

## Synopsis
A short, focused build: instead of reaching for Ableton's stock De-esser or a static EQ cut, Underbelly builds a de-esser from scratch out of Multiband Dynamics. The core teaching point is *why* a static EQ cut fails (it removes the frequency for the whole recording, making the voice sound unnatural) versus a dynamic solution (only ducks the problem band when it's actually loud). Short but a genuinely reusable DIY technique with exact settings.

## Techniques
- **Find the sibilant frequency by ear before doing anything else** — put a wide-Q bell on an EQ Eight, narrow the Q, and sweep it up the spectrum while the vocal plays until the "s" sound spikes painfully; here it landed around 4-5 kHz (also, not coincidentally, where ears are most sensitive to begin with). → [[EQ]]
- **Don't statically cut the sibilant band with EQ** — a static cut removes that frequency range for the *entire* recording, not just during the S sounds, and the voice starts to sound unnatural/thin as a result — the problem is dynamic (only bad during the S), so the fix needs to be dynamic too. → [[EQ]]
- **Ableton's stock De-esser plugin was rejected for the same reason** — it was heard to be turning the sound down continuously rather than only during S sounds, so instead of trusting the plugin's label, the DIY route below was built. → [[De-essing]]
- **Build a de-esser from Multiband Dynamics: isolate one band, then limit it hard** — turn off the top and bottom bands, keep only the middle band, and narrow that band's frequency range down to the sibilant zone found by ear (here ~3-7 kHz). On the downward compressor for that band, crank the ratio to something extreme like 1:∞ so it behaves like a limiter — any energy in that narrow band above threshold gets clamped, while everything else in the signal passes untouched. Widening the band's frequency range afterward opens up how much of the "s" region gets caught if the effect isn't strong enough. → [[Multiband Dynamics]], [[De-essing]]
- **The technique generalizes beyond vocal S's** — the same narrow-band-plus-extreme-ratio Multiband Dynamics trick works as a more transparent way to tame any problem frequency on any instrument than a static EQ cut, whenever the problem is only loud sometimes rather than all the time. → [[De-essing]], [[EQ]]

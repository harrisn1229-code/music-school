## Techniques

- **A pitch-bend "mistake" (Herbie Hancock's solo landing audibly sharp before he bends it back into tune) was deliberately kept in the final take rather than re-recorded** — cited as a genuine example of leaving an in-the-moment performance flaw in place because it read as musically alive, not as an error to fix.
- **A harmonized solo line panned to the opposite side and voiced in fourths/fifths against the main solo, on the same Moog modular, rather than doubling it in unison** (Keith Emerson, Tarkus) — a concrete example of the "voice a harmony interval" pattern seen elsewhere on this channel, applied at the arrangement/panning level rather than inside one patch.
- **An expression pedal on a Minimoog, controlling filter brightness with the foot while the hands stay on the pitch/mod wheels** (credited by name to George Duke's playing as the direct inspiration for adopting this setup) — a second, independent source for the same expression-pedal technique already logged from the "5 Mistakes" video; corroborating detail worth keeping both sources on.
- **Reference-listening list, worth returning to for ear-training/style study rather than patch settings**: Fred Mandel's Jupiter-8-in-unison-mode solo on Queen's "I Want to Break Free"; Herbie Hancock (Headhunters-era funk synth licks); Keith Emerson's Tarkus solo; Chick Corea's Minimoog pitch/mod-wheel mastery; Edgar Winter's "Frankenstein" ARP 2600 solo (wide interval leaps, primitive pitch bender); Joe Zawinul's Weather Report "Birdland" outro; George Duke with Stanley Clarke; Jan Hammer's guitar-influenced Minimoog phrasing (Jeff Beck/Mahavishnu Orchestra lineage).

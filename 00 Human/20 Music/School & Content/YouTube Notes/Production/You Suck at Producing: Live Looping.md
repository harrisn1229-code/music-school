# You Suck at Producing: Live Looping

## Synopsis

A viewer-requested breakdown of the Ableton Session View rig Underbelly uses for solo live looping — walking through the Computer MIDI device's five-track setup (Drums, Rhodes, Bass, Guitar, Synth) and exactly how his hardware pad/fader controller is mapped to it. Covers why the sounds were chosen (versatile, warm, and none too overbearing — a "sandwich" where no ingredient overpowers the others), the specific and slightly counterintuitive fader-to-Sends (not track volume) mapping he uses to dodge a clipping problem, click-track clips standing in for the built-in metronome, the pad-bank layout for arming/recording/overdubbing loops per track, the "red box" clip-launch grid that mimics a Launchpad/APC, and the macro-mapped FX knobs (reverb, delay, low-pass/high-pass filter, Beat Repeat) he rides live while building and breaking down a loop. Same overall live-performance domain as [[How To PERFORM with Ableton Live - Live Performance Setup]], but this one is the mechanics of *looping itself* rather than song-to-song set structure.

## Techniques

- **Five-track live-looping palette: Drums, Rhodes, Bass, Guitar, Synth** — picked for being versatile, warm, and fresh together, "like ingredients you'd find at your local Subway." General principle for building your own rig: pick instruments you keep going back to across your own productions, and make sure no single sound is too overbearing or it drowns out the others in the loop-building "sandwich."
- **Map controller faders to track Sends, not track Volume, and set the tracks to Sends Only** — the max position of his controller's faders corresponds to roughly +6 dB on a track's actual Volume fader, which caused clipping when mapped directly. Fix: map the faders to each track's **Send** level instead of Volume, and switch each track's output to **Sends Only** — this routes the fader control through a range that doesn't clip while still giving full level control over each element in the loop.
- **Two more faders for filter cutoff and global BPM** — a second-to-last fader is mapped to the low-pass filter cutoff on his synth for "wub wub" sweeps; the last fader on the controller is mapped to **global BPM**, letting him ride tempo live during a set.
- **Click-track clips replace Live's built-in metronome for a live-looping session** — instead of the standard metronome tick, four simple click-sound clips sit on the drum track (three in 4/4, one in 6/4 for switching feel) and get triggered like any other clip; the built-in metronome is switched off entirely in favor of these, so the "beep" doesn't have to compete with the sound of an actual loop starting.
- **Overdub a drum loop via a pad mapped to Live's Overdub button** — workflow: press play on a click-track clip, switch to a second pad bank containing macro/transport controls, and hit the pad mapped to **Overdub** — this lets him record new drum hits on top of the still-playing click track without stopping playback, building the loop hit by hit rather than recording one full pass.
- **Pad-bank navigation stands in for a full Launchpad/APC grid on a small controller** — his Computer MIDI device shows a red highlighted box around the first four clip slots that mirrors what a Launchpad/APC would show; a set of macro-mapped "pad bank" buttons moves that box around the session grid, so a compact 16-pad controller can still reach every track's clips (drums, keys, bass, guitar, synth) by switching which 4×4 region is currently active, rather than needing a full-size grid controller.
- **Left-to-right build order across the rig: drums → Rhodes → bass → guitar → synth** — his usual live-looping performance order, arming each track in turn via a controller button mapped to that track's Arm, recording a short phrase, and triggering playback before moving to the next instrument in the chain.
- **Macro-mapped FX knobs ridden live during the loop build/breakdown: reverb, delay, low-pass/high-pass filter, and Beat Repeat's Grid + Pitch Decay** — separate knobs bring in reverb and delay sends, sweep a low-pass then a high-pass filter, and (via a dedicated pad bank) drive **Beat Repeat**'s Grid (repeat rate — faster/slower) and Pitch Decay (falling-pitch stutter) for a build moment, finishing with a manual fade-out on the loop. → [[Beat Repeat]] — same Grid/Pitch Decay mapping already documented on the device's own note from a separate, more detailed Beat Repeat breakdown; this video's contribution is seeing it used live, mid-performance, rather than in isolation.

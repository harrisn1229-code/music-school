# Cassette

![[Images/Cassette.png]]

**Category:** Drive/Color
**Vendor:** Wavesfactory

## What it is

Wavesfactory's vintage cassette tape simulator — four modelled cassettes, Pro/Home/Micro deck characters, and six degradation knobs: **erasures** (HF loss from re-recording), **spread** (stereo→mono), **stability** (wow/flutter speed fluctuation), **static** and **dynamic** noise, and **artifacts** (HF dips + dropouts).

## What it's used for

Adding lo-fi cassette warmth, saturation, and tape imperfection character to drums, vocals, bass, or a full mix bus.

## FX chain notes

Used as a coloration/character stage, typically after the main mix chain when a deliberately degraded/vintage texture is wanted.

Not a fixed slot — a **texture option**, printed when a part wants deliberate degradation:

- **[[1 Electric Piano Track|Electric Piano Track]]** — *behind-the-beat feel*: Player = **Home**, **Stability** up with wow depth ~15% / flutter ~10% inside its panel for tape slew/lag (the owned stand-in for Kenny's Strymon Deco move), **Static** low for a touch of floor, Mix 100% and print. | ①   | Tape Type (4 cassettes)       | Selects which of the four modelled cassettes is loaded (shown on the cassette graphic; `‹ ›` arrows either side). Each has its own frequency response, noise character and saturation curve. | Audition all four on the source before touching anything else — the tape choice moves more than any knob here. | —                       |
| ②   | Player (Pro / Home / Micro)   | Deck-quality model. Sets the baseline mechanical quality and bandwidth: Pro = tight/hi-fi transport, Micro = cheapest/most degraded. | ③   | Input                         | Drive into the tape stage — more input = more saturation and tape compression.                                                         | Push for audible tape drive on drums/bass; keep low for clean warmth.                             | —                       |
| ④   | Erasures                      | Emulates degradation from a cassette being recorded over many times — progressive **high-frequency loss**, so a duller sound.           | The "this tape has been reused a hundred times" control. A little takes the sheen off a bright synth or hat without an EQ move. | —                       |
| ⑤   | Spread                        | Stereo width, from 100% stereo down to fully mono.                                                                                     | Pull toward mono to glue a too-wide stereo synth into a cohesive tape image — cassette crosstalk narrows naturally. | —                       |
| ⑥   | Stability                     | Tape **speed fluctuation**. Its panel carries depth + speed sliders for **wow** (slow drift) and **flutter** (fast jitter), plus a randomness slider. | A little wow on sustained keys/pads sells "tape" instantly; keep flutter subtle or it reads as broken rather than vintage. | —                       |
| ⑦   | Static (noise)                | Steady tape **hiss** — a constant noise floor whose character follows the selected tape and deck.                                       | Low for character; push it for an intro/lo-fi section then gate or fade it back out.              | —                       |
| ⑧   | Dynamic (noise)               | Signal-dependent noise — slightly distorts and adds noise **in proportion to the incoming amplitude**, unlike Static's constant floor.  | This is the one that makes the noise feel "played" rather than laid over the top — it breathes with the performance. | —                       |
| ⑨   | Artifacts                     | Adds **dips in the high-frequency spectrum**; its panel has speed + depth sliders for **degradation** (slow HF dips) and **dropouts** (fast dips). | Sparingly. A few percent of dropouts is the difference between "tape plugin" and "actual worn cassette". | —                       |
| ⑩   | Output                        | Makeup level after the tape stage.                                                                                                     | Level-match against bypass so the A/B is about texture, not loudness.                             | —                       |

# Don't Pass Me By — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Three chords, one per four bars, no modulation anywhere — which makes this a case study in **rescuing a harmonically inert song with phrase lengths and arrangement**. Ringo's first released original, though it existed at least in part four years earlier, when a BBC broadcast suggests it was headed for a bluesy up-tempo treatment rather than the country arrangement it eventually got.

**Form.** Deliberately oversized sections ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]). The **verse runs 19 bars** — a prime number — in four lyric phrases, the last of which takes **seven bars**; the third is also a bar short against the pattern. The **refrain runs 24 bars** in six even phrases, and the final refrain adds one extra bar at the end of its third phrase, echoing the verse's missing bar in the same position. An interlude stops the beat dead for a couple of seconds. That silence exists because the song ends with **two refrains back to back**, a boredom risk; the surviving alternative version drops a verse reprise in between instead, and it drags.

**Harmony.** I, IV and V, changing once every four bars. The **harmonic rhythm is slow enough to be a liability**, and the asymmetric seven-bar phrase at the end of the verse is what keeps the song moving ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). The outro's apparent plagal cadence is better heard as a **double appoggiatura resolving into the tonic** than as a cadence in its own right ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]).

**Melody.** Verse and refrain both drop a bluesy **♭3 near the end of the section** ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]) — the same placement in both, which makes it a structural signature rather than an ornament. In the refrain the vocal lines get shorter and more interjectory as the section runs, so the gaps get bigger and the fiddle obbligato takes over the space.

**Arrangement.** Thick to the point of muddy and unusually unvaried for a Beatles record; the country fiddle is the one inspired element, and both ends are bracketed by unstructured playing.

# The Key To Understanding A Jazz Blues

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rFrDMQYo348)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rFrDMQYo348)

## Synopsis
Jack Ruch's short, focused lesson on the single chord sequence that separates a **jazz blues** from a plain **1-4-5 blues**: the **1-6-2-5** turnaround that replaces (or gets inserted into) the tonic bars of a standard 12-bar form. Rather than teaching the whole form at once, Ruch isolates just the 1-6-2-5 section, builds one worked melodic line over it phrase by phrase (chord by chord, explaining the reasoning behind every note choice), and then drops that line into a full 12-bar solo over a B♭ blues so it's heard in context. The teaching approach itself is the most reusable thing here as much as the specific line: isolate the hard four-bar section of a longer form, build vocabulary over just that, then reintegrate. Harmonically the video is a compact, concrete worked example of ideas the vault's Theory strand already has pages for — the VI7 turnaround, diatonic I→iii substitution, and guide-tone/chord-tone targeting — which is why most of this enrichment lands as sourced examples on those existing notes rather than as new pages.

## What's taught

- **What makes a jazz blues a jazz blues:** same 12-bar skeleton as any blues (start on I, move to IV in bar 5), but a jazz blues layers substitutions on top — specifically the **1-6-2-5** progression is the chord sequence that most distinguishes it from a "typical 1-4-5 blues" (Ruch's examples of the plain style: Freddy King, Otis Rush) — vs. the jazz style (Wes Montgomery, Grant Green). → [[Blues Form and Harmony]], [[Chord Progressions]]
- **The I chord in this section is usually not a dominant 7th.** Where a straight blues would keep I7, the jazz-blues 1-6-2-5 typically plays the **I chord as a major 7** (B♭maj7, not B♭7) — the dominant-as-home-sound convention of the plain blues form is suspended for this section. *(general framing, ~2:00)* → [[Blues Form and Harmony]] § *Why every chord is a dominant 7th*
- **The I→iii-7 substitution, worked concretely:** instead of (or superimposed over) B♭maj7, play a **D minor triad/D minor 7 arpeggio** — the iii chord substituting for I, a textbook [[Chord Substitution]] § *Diatonic substitution* move (D minor shares two of B♭major's three triad tones) heard as an actual line rather than described abstractly. Ruch also names two more voicing options for the same bar that show up "depending on the tune or the artist": a **split bar — two beats of B♭7, two beats of Dm7** (I7 then its iii substitute, back to back) — or just one or the other held for the whole bar. → [[Chord Substitution]], [[Turnarounds]]
- **The melodic formula for outlining an arpeggio: hit the top note first, come down through the arpeggio, then climb back up.** Explicitly named as a movable shape you can reposition anywhere within any arpeggio, not a fixed lick — over the D minor arpeggio here it lands on a C on top (the b7 of Dm7 / the 9th of B♭maj9), i.e., it's simultaneously outlining Dm7 *and* B♭maj9 since they share the same four notes. *(~3:00–4:00)*
- **"Arpeggio up, scale down" as a second reusable formula:** after outlining the arpeggio, descend through the parent scale (B♭ major / D minor, same notes) rather than the arpeggio itself — climb the chord tones, come down the full scale. Also named as a shape to move around at will, not tied to this one spot. *(~4:00)*
- **Targeting chord tones on strong beats when the harmony changes — named explicitly as "a really key component to outlining changes."** As the progression moves to the VI7 chord (G7), the line comes down and lands on the **major 3rd of G7 (B natural) squarely on the downbeat** of the chord change. This is the guide-tone-targeting principle stated as a rule of thumb, not just demonstrated. → [[Guide Tones]], [[Bebop Vocabulary]] § *Target the guide tones*
- **The altered/bebop sound: leap to the ♭9, then chromatic-walk to the root.** Over the same G7, right after landing the 3rd, the line jumps up to the **♭9 (A♭)** — Ruch calls this out by name as what gives the line "a really cool altered, very jazz-bebop type of sound" — then resolves with **three consecutive chromatic notes walking up to the root**. → [[Bebop Vocabulary]] § *Approach notes*, [[Altered Chords]]
- **The line implies the full harmony with no rhythm section underneath it** — Ruch plays this whole passage solo and points out that the line itself carries all the harmonic information of the chord changes without anything comping underneath. Direct real-world demonstration of the guide-tone principle that a single melodic voice can imply a full progression. → [[Guide Tones]]
- **Chromatic color tones over the ii chord (Cm7):** a descending chromatic passing tone from the **5th down to the ♭5** purely for color (not a chord tone, just connective tissue), followed by a move up to the **9th** of Cm7 — framed as "you could play strictly chord tones... but adding the flat nine or the regular nine gives these chords their own flavor," i.e., tensions as deliberate color choices, not accidents. *(~6:30–7:30)*
- **Voice-leading into the V chord (F7):** the line resolves down to the **3rd of F7** with clean, stepwise voice leading, then a falling altered-sound phrase carries it back to B♭ (I) to close the turnaround/resolve to the top of the form. *(~7:30–8:00)*
- **Practice/vocabulary-building method (the video's real throughline):** isolate one tricky four-bar section of a longer progression (here, the 1-6-2-5) rather than trying to solo the whole 12-bar form at once; build and internalize one line over just that section; only then tie it into a full chorus so the isolated vocabulary connects with everything else you already play over the rest of the form. Explicitly framed as the general practice approach for any longer progression, not specific to this tune. *(~1:00–1:30, restated ~13:30)*
- **Full 12-bar demonstration:** after the worked line is established, Ruch plays a complete 12-bar solo over a B♭ blues backing track, using typical swing/jazz-blues phrasing through the main part of the form and dropping the worked 1-6-2-5 line in at the matching bars — showing the isolated vocabulary reintegrated into a real solo. *(~13:30–14:40, tabs/backing track gated behind Ruch's Patreon, not in the free video)*

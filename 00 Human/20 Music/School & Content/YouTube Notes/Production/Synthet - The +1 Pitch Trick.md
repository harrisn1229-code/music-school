# Synthet - The +1 Pitch Trick

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qLjI298_yhk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qLjI298_yhk)

## Synopsis

A slide-note embellishment lesson built around one move: drop a note or chord a semitone, then add a slide back up to pitch — a small, controllable "jazzy" scoop applicable to chords, melodies, drum fills, and reversed samples alike. Includes a caution that heavy auto-tune retuning can strip this kind of expressive slide out of a vocal take, and closes with a separate, bigger pitch move — transposing an entire song up a semitone at the very end for a "spicy key change."

## Techniques

- **Semitone-down-then-slide-up scoop** — shift a chord or note down one semitone, then add a slide note bending it back up to the original pitch; adjust the slide's length to taste. A small, cheap way to add "jazzy" variation to a repeated chord or the last chord of a phrase. → [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Works on melodies, drum fills, and reversed samples too** — the same down-a-semitone-then-slide-up move isn't chord-specific; applied to a melody it's a spice move, and on a reversed sample, pitching it up from a semitone below is described as "captivating." A semitone is usually sufficient — don't overdo the interval.
- **Heavy auto-tune retuning can erase this kind of expressive glide** — sung slides/bends get quantized away under aggressive retune settings; be mindful when auto-tuning a take that had an intentional scoop or bend in the performance.
- **End-of-song semitone key change** — pitch the entire final section up one semitone for a "spicy" late key change; workflow tip: right-click the pattern/clip you want to change and use Transpose rather than manually re-recording or re-programming it. → [[Modulation (Key Change)]]

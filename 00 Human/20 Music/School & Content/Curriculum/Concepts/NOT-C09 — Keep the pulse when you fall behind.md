# NOT-C09 — Keep the pulse when you fall behind

**Q.** You fall behind mid-piece. What do you do, and what are the two things you never do?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Keep the pulse — thin it out and rejoin at the next downbeat.** Drop notes, thin the chord, or play only the downbeats until you catch up. **Never stop and never go back.** A wrong note is a wrong note; a stop is a failure of the skill itself, because the continuity *is* what the exercise trains. "Slow down until I can play it" is the third trap — it turns the rep into a different exercise and rehearses hesitation.

## Why it matters

It's the rule that saves a performance, and the hardest habit to build. Sight reading exists for the situation where you can't fully play what's in front of you, so dropping the tempo until everything is playable removes the exact condition being trained — and builds the stop-and-fix reflex that ends a live read when there's a band who won't wait.

## Drill

Cold-read a line too fast for you, once. When it falls apart, play downbeats only and rejoin — no stopping, no second pass.

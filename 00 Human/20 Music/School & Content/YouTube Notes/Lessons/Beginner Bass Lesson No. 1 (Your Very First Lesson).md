## Synopsis

BassBuzz's actual "day one" video — the literal starting point the channel's other beginner content (and this vault's [[The BassBuzz 55-Step Bass Learning Roadmap]], Level 0–Level 1) assumes has already happened. Josh walks a total newbie through seven steps in order: gear essentials (plugging in, knobs), playing position and posture, tuning basics, plucking-hand technique, fretting-hand technique, and groove/rhythm — then stitches all of it together into one guided first "song," a heavily simplified three-note version of Radiohead's "High and Dry" on the open/2nd-fret/5th-fret E string. The format throughout is diagnose-a-mistake-then-fix-it (a cold-open gag of the host sounding terrible sets up each section), and every section ends with a short guided play-along so the tip gets practiced immediately rather than just explained. Nothing here is new *theory* by BassBuzz's own later standards — it's the deliberately-bare floor every other technique note in this vault already assumes — which makes it valuable specifically as the anchor for those assumptions: several existing notes ([[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]], [[Fretting-Hand Strength, Stretch, Stamina, and Finger Control]], [[Bass Note Muting Technique]]) document *advanced* versions of skills this video introduces at their absolute starting point, and nothing in the vault previously covered playing position, gear/knob setup, or tuning basics at all.

## What's taught

**Step 1 — Get plugged in.**
- Plug the bass into an amp with a cable via the input jack on the bass body (front or side, either end of the cable works) — practice **amplified most of the time** so you get used to hearing the bass the way an audience would, not just how it feels/sounds unamplified.

**Step 2 — Bass and amp knobs (the fast "not terrible" starting point, not tone optimization).**
- Bass knobs control **volume** (loudness) and **tone** (dark/warm vs. bright/zingy character).
- **P-Bass (Precision-style):** two knobs — one volume, one tone. Tone knob fully clockwise = brighter; fully counterclockwise = darker.
- **J-Bass (Jazz-style):** one volume per pickup, plus a shared tone knob (same clockwise/counterclockwise rule).
- **Active bass** (battery-powered electronics): knob layout varies — check the specific bass's manual. **Unplug the cable when not playing an active bass**, or the battery drains and it goes silent.
- **Amp knobs, fast starting point:** set every knob **except volume** to 12 o'clock; refine later.
- **Setting overall volume — order matters:** max (or near-max) the **bass's own volume knob first**, then creep the **amp's volume up** from there until it's as loud as you want/as your neighbors will tolerate — not the reverse. → routed to [[Bass Tone Chain Priority]] (Link 5 and Link 8) for the deeper troubleshooting version of the same two links, and the new [[Bass Playing Position, Setup, and Tuning Basics]] for the full beginner setup writeup.

**Step 3 — Playing position and posture (five tips, framed as pain/technique prevention, not just comfort).**
1. **Sitting height:** hips level with or above your knees (prevents lower-back pain); use a seat with no arms so your arms aren't boxed in.
2. **Where to rest the bass when sitting:** over the right leg is the most common default; over the left leg works for some players and can help reach higher notes, but makes the lower notes (further away) harder to reach — try both.
3. **Always play with a strap on**, sitting or standing — it stabilizes the bass so your hands are free to focus on playing. Attach to both strap buttons; run your head and strumming-side arm through. Set strap height so the bass sits in roughly the **same position** whether you're sitting or standing (pro bassists vary a lot here — this is a starting-point recommendation, not a rule).
4. **Neck angle:** angled up a bit is generally nicer than parallel to the floor for most playing; pros vary (some tilt up more, some less, some lower — lower can suit slap).
5. **Push the headstock away from you** — opens up room for the fretting arm to move comfortably, especially higher up the neck where you'd otherwise run into your own torso.

**Step 4 — Tuning basics.**
- Tune before you play — an out-of-tune bass won't match any song or other instrument.
- Tuning turns the tuning pegs, which loosen/tighten the strings.
- **You can tune by ear or with a tuner** (clip-on, plug-in, pedal, or app) — a tuner is recommended for beginners specifically because it's easier to learn and more reliable at first.
- **String names, thickest to thinnest: E A D G.** Mnemonic used in the video: "eat and drink grapes." These are the only note names needed to get started with tuning; full fretboard note-naming is deferred to a dedicated video/note (this vault: [[Bass Fretboard Note Memorization System]]).
- Video does not walk through an actual tuning session (called out as too slow/boring for this format) — points to a separate dedicated tuning resource instead (not queued this pass).

**Step 5 — Plucking-hand technique (three tips) + a guided groove drill.**
1. **Anchor your thumb** somewhere consistent so your fingers can find the strings reliably — on a low string you're not using (e.g. resting on the E or A string), or on a pickup. **With two pickups (e.g. a Jazz bass), default to anchoring on the one closer to the neck**, not the bridge pickup, for most playing.
2. **Pluck with index and middle fingers, alternating** (index-middle-index-middle…) — the standard two-finger technique, chosen as the sweet spot between one-finger (simple but slow) and three-finger (fast but hard to learn).
3. **Pull across, not up and away** — the single biggest newbie-vs-legit tell. Pulling a finger up and away from the bass gives a thin, wimpy sound; pulling *across* into the next string (or into your own thumb, if you're on the lowest/E string) gives a fuller, more solid, bassier tone — and it doubles as automatic muting, since your plucking fingers land on and damp the string you just came from.
- **Guided drill:** anchor the thumb on the pickup, pluck the open E string — index on beat 1, middle on beat 3, over a repeating 4-count, pulling across each time.
- **Adding funk via muting:** once the plain plucking pattern (pluck on 1 and 3) is solid, add a mute on beats 2 and 4 so the note doesn't just ring through to the next pluck — either touch the string with the fretting hand's fingers (without pressing down) or lay the plucking fingers back on the string; both hands work, and doing both isn't wrong either. Pattern: pluck–mute–pluck–mute. → full method: [[Bass Note Muting Technique]], [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]].

**Step 6 — Fretting-hand technique (three tips) + a guided two-note drill.**
1. **Find your hand's natural relaxed shape first, then bring the neck to it — not the other way around.** Let your fretting arm hang relaxed at your side, palm facing you: shoulders dropped, elbow near your body, wrist and fingers straight or slightly bent forward, thumb loose off to the side. Keeping that shape unchanged, bring the bass up so the neck meets the fingers. Fingers end up roughly perpendicular to the neck, though the exact angle shifts with how far your arm sits from your body.
2. **Don't force the thumb to stay pinned behind the middle finger** — common advice, but it adds unnecessary hand tension and isn't necessary. Let the thumb rest wherever it naturally wants along the back of the neck (often roughly following the fingers, or angled toward the headstock) and keep it as relaxed as possible; resist the urge to jam it into the neck for extra pressure.
3. **Press toward the end of the fret** (the side closest to the pickups, right behind the fret wire) rather than in the middle of the fret space — gives the cleanest, least buzzy note. (This is the same fix [[Fretting-Hand Strength, Stretch, Stamina, and Finger Control]]'s Exercise 1 teaches for eliminating rattle — this video is the first-lesson version of that same rule.)
- **Guided drill:** two notes on the E string — 5th fret (dot marker) and 7th fret. Pluck the 7th fret with the pinky, then the 5th fret with the index, back and forth on a 4-count. Dot markers on the fretboard are called out explicitly as visual position landmarks.

**Step 7 — Groove: feel the pulse, then play a specific rhythm against it.**
- **Step one of grooving: feel the underlying pulse** (the beat you'd naturally bob your head or tap your foot to) before playing anything — playing without first locking onto that pulse produces rhythms disconnected from what the rest of a band is doing.
- **Step two: know the specific rhythm you're aiming for.** Named note values by how many beats they last: a **whole note** = 4 beats, a **half note** = 2 beats, a **quarter note** = 1 beat.
- Cross-referenced (not detailed in this video) for more depth on pulse/drums: a separate BassBuzz "how to play bass with a drummer" video (not queued this pass). Vault's fuller theory treatment: [[Time Signatures & Note Values]].

**Putting it together — "High and Dry" (Radiohead), heavily simplified.**
- All notes played on the **E string only**, using exactly the three notes/techniques just taught: **open string** (no fretting), **2nd fret** (index finger), **5th fret** (pinky finger). Move between the 2nd and 5th fret with a **micro-shift** of the whole hand rather than a static stretch — no need to reach across a fixed hand shape.
- The same three notes get run against all three rhythms taught above, cumulatively: first as **whole notes** (very spacious — one pluck every 4 beats), then **half notes** (one pluck every 2 beats, remembering to alternate index/middle where two plucks fall close together), then **quarter notes** (one pluck per beat — the most "pushed"/active feel of the three).
- Framed explicitly as a deliberately simplified version, not the real bassline — encourages going and learning the actual full song afterward if you're a fan. **Not routed to a dedicated Library song note** — this is a pedagogical simplification built to drill rhythm values and the two frets just taught, not a real transcription of the song's actual bassline, so it stays here on the video page rather than getting a song-ref (see [[🎵 GORILLAZ - Feel Good Inc]] for what an actual transcribed-bassline song note looks like, for contrast).

# CHD-C12 — Decoding a slash chord

**Q.** Give the method for reading a slash chord you do not recognise, and the two cases where the method stops short of an answer.

> [!question]- Answer
> **1.** Take the **bass note as the root**. **2.** Relate every note of the upper chord back to it as a scale degree or extension. **3.** Name the chord from those degrees — which tells you whether it is behaving as a dominant, a major-7 type or a minor-7 type, and therefore how it wants to resolve. It stops in two cases: if the bass note **is** a tone of the upper chord there is no new chord, only an inversion (A♭/C is first-inversion A♭); and if the composite has **no 3rd and no 7th** (D/C) the reading is genuinely ambiguous and only the surrounding harmony decides.

## Why it matters

A slash symbol is a **playing instruction** — upper chord over bass note — and it looks mysterious only because it refuses to sort into a familiar category at a glance. It has to be decoded rather than read off a symbol chart. Worked: B♭/C is ♭7-9-11 over C, i.e. C7sus4(9). G/C is 9-5-maj7, a Cmaj9 with no 3rd. E/C is maj3-♯5-maj7, i.e. Cmaj7♯5. The method works identically on minor, diminished and augmented upper triads, and on four-note upper chords too.

## Drill

Run the three steps on B♭/C, A/C and G♭/C. Name each composite and say where each one wants to go.

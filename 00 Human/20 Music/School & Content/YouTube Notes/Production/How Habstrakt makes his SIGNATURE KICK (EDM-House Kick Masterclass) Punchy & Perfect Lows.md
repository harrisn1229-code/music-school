# How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK (EDM/House Kick Masterclass) [Punchy & Perfect Lows]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)

## Synopsis
Habstrakt builds his signature EDM/house kick live in Ableton, from a bare filter to a finished, mix-ready hit, answering Q&A questions from the Sol State host along the way. Where the vault's existing Kick Design content (Disclosure, Steve Duda/deadmau5) is mostly analytical — decomposing a kick into transient/body/tail, explaining room reverberation and psychoacoustic sizing tricks, and documenting frequency-separated two-layer recipes — this video is a hands-on device chain: it shows the actual Ableton devices and knob moves used to synthesize a kick from nothing (a self-oscillating filter standing in for an oscillator), add a click layer (Operator + noise + FM), reshape the transient with an EQ Three quirk that isn't about gain at all, and manage loudness/distortion into the kick's saturation stage with a resample-and-scoop workflow. The most valuable material is the self-resonating-filter-as-oscillator technique and the EQ Three transient trick — both are novel additions, not restatements of what's already documented. The video closes with a strong production-philosophy point (no universal rules, everything is contextual — pitch, EQ, and length decisions all depend on what's already in the mix) that reinforces rather than duplicates the "swap the sample, don't over-process" and "contextual decisions" threads already in the vault.

## Techniques
- **Self-oscillating filter as the kick's tone oscillator, no synth needed** — Ableton's newer MS2 filter model (the self-resonating Korg-style circuit) starts ringing its own pure sine tone once Resonance is pushed to maximum and any brief signal passes through it; after that initial trigger the filter sustains the tone on its own, so the original input source becomes disposable. Routing an envelope to the filter's **Frequency** knob then shapes that self-oscillating tone's pitch-drop and decay, functionally replacing a synth oscillator + pitch envelope with just a filter. → [[Auto Filter]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **LFO Phase offset on that same filter for stereo motion** — the filter's LFO section has its own Phase knob, which offsets the left and right channels' modulation independently for subtle stereo drift on the filter sweep itself (same underlying trick already documented on → [[Auto Filter]] from a different source). — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **Hunt for "a good note" first, then loop it before shaping the envelope** — before committing to an envelope shape, audition the self-oscillating filter/patch at different pitches until landing on one with "tons of low end," loop that as the working seed, then start sculpting the amp/pitch envelope on top. — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **Operator with a white-noise oscillator + FM on top, as the click/transient layer** — group the filter-based body with a second layer built in [[Operator]] using its noise oscillator, with FM added on top for extra edge, specifically to supply the attack transient the filter-oscillator body layer doesn't have on its own. → [[Operator]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **Freeze, resample into a fresh sampler, and re-envelope for "magic"** — duplicate and freeze the current kick chain, drop the frozen audio into another sampler instance, and apply a new envelope on top of the resample; repeating this resample-and-reshape cycle is explicitly called out as a way to stumble into results by accident rather than by direct design. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **Saturation is "always" on a kick, dialed to just short of "too hard"** — treats saturation as a default stage rather than an optional extra on every kick; pushed toward the edge of clipping for low-end weight and character, backed off from the point where it reads as obviously too distorted/hard. → [[Saturator]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **EQ Three's Freq Lo/Freq Hi knobs alone reshape the kick's transient — no gain movement required.** A happy accident (reaching for EQ Eight, grabbing EQ Three instead): with all three band gains left untouched, just sliding the Freq Lo and Freq Hi crossover-point knobs across the kick audibly changes its transient character, because moving the crossover split itself reshapes the signal even with zero boost/cut applied. → [[EQ Three]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **Whether to cut a kick's low end is fully contextual — no fixed rule.** Explicitly rejects a universal "always/never cut the lows" answer; some kicks want it, some don't, decided by ear per kick. — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **A light Limiter + freeze/resample pass to "get the hair off" before finalizing** — a final limiter stage tames residual peak spikes, then the result gets frozen/resampled to lock it in as the finished kick. → [[Limiter]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **To push a saturated kick louder without blowing up the master, scoop the ONE frequency causing the clip instead of just pulling the fader down.** Drive the kick's saturation/level as far as it'll go until it audibly clips, back off just to the edge, then resample/freeze that result and listen for the specific frequency doing the clipping — a narrow EQ cut right at that frequency (not a broad level pull-back) is what actually buys back headroom, because it removes the one offending band rather than taming the whole hit. → [[Saturator]], [[Kick Design]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **Retune the kick contextually per song section rather than leaving it fixed** — the same kick loop gets transposed (e.g. three semitones higher) in MIDI when a different section's harmony/other elements no longer support the original pitch; the kick's tuning is treated as just another arrangement variable, not a one-time setting. → [[Kick Design]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **"There are no rules, only what the context calls for."** Explicit pushback on do-you-always-X questions (always cut tails, always tune drums, etc.) — every production choice depends on what else is happening in that specific song, not on a fixed personal rule. — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)

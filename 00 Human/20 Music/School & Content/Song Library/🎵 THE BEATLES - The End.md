# The End — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** three parts, roughly 34s / 55s / 35s, which produces an ABA symmetry even though the outer parts share no material. **Part 1** — an 8-bar phrase instrumental, a 4-bar drum solo, the phrase again with a screaming double-tracked lead, then a 16-bar drum solo. **Part 2** — fourteen passes of a 4-bar frame carrying the round-robin duelling guitar solos. **Part 3** — one unusually long phrase: four bars of piano alone, eight of vocal, four of 3/4, then a final stretch of slow 4/4.

The home key **migrates**, A major for most of it and C only in the second half of part 3 — a third relation ([[POP-C11 — Chromatic mediants and pop modulation]]). Part 1's opening A is far more a dominant pointing at D than a tonic, so the phrase behaves as convergent rather than closed ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). Part 2 is a plagal I – IV frame and nothing else. Part 3 sits over a static A with a G major chord stacked in the treble against it — a **pedal point**, not a root change ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]) — then holds the two inner voices while soprano and bass descend in parallel sixths, and the F that results is the pivot: ♭VI at home, IV in C.

Rhythmically it runs **two metric modulations back to back**: to 3/4 with the quarter held constant, then back to 4/4 with the new quarter equal to a whole bar of that 3/4 — a written-in slowdown rather than a played ritard. The syncopation motif threads all three parts ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]), down to the final "make" sung just ahead of its downbeat.

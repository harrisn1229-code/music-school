# How Chromeo Makes KILLER Drums (step by step)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8VLtlIUMF4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8VLtlIUMF4)

## Synopsis
Chromeo (Dave 1 and P-Thugg) walk through how a song's drum track actually gets built, from the fast disposable skeleton used to write the song to the final programmed kit that ships. They start a session by stacking two pre-made "writing loops" — drum breaks chopped from old disco records — because when an artist is in the room you want to get the song written in minutes, not lost in production detail. But that loop combo never survives to the final record: Chromeo describe themselves as old-school hip-hop purists who consider it "kind of cheating" to ship a pre-made drum loop, partly on principle and partly because a loop other people can also find (on Splice or elsewhere) means "our job isn't done." So they rebuild the drums from scratch by combining live drums and drum samples, layering piece by piece until it's as groovy as the scratch loop but higher quality: a sampled kick with boosted low end doubled with a raw live room kick for natural "air," a sampled snare (compressed, reverbed) doubled with a live snare for texture, then isolated snare, clap, and hi-hat hits harvested directly out of the original reference loop and layered back on top. The piece that "sealed the deal" was a live, hand-played hi-hat overdub recorded fresh over the whole pattern — chasing the real, bright, noisy live hi-hat they loved on a Gap Band reference track — cleaned up with just a basic EQ pass since the raw take was dirty. The video closes with a direct before/after A/B of the old scratch loop against the finished programmed drums.

## Techniques
- **"Writing loops" as a disposable skeleton, never the final product.** With an artist in the studio, stack a couple of pre-made drum loops (chopped from old disco/funk breaks) to get a full song skeleton down in minutes — the point is speed during the write, not a finished sound; the plan going in is to gut and rebuild the drums later. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [How Chromeo Makes KILLER Drums (step by step)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8VLtlIUMF4)
- **The anti-loop purist stance: shipping a pre-made drum loop on the final record is "cheating."** Chromeo frame themselves as old-school hip-hop purists who always programmed their own drums rather than use a pre-made loop on the finished song — not a technical limitation, a deliberate identity choice, reinforced by the fact that house/dance producers looping full records is normal for that genre but isn't their lane. → [[Sample Clearance]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [How Chromeo Makes KILLER Drums (step by step)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8VLtlIUMF4)
- **"If a fan can find your drum loop on Splice, your job isn't done."** The originality bar isn't just "does it sound good" — it's whether the exact sound is sourceable by anyone else; a loop findable in a marketplace fails that bar even if it grooves, because the goal is to *provide* sounds, not reuse what's already out there. — Chromeo (Sol State), [How Chromeo Makes KILLER Drums (step by step)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8VLtlIUMF4)
- **Kick: a low-end-boosted sample doubled with a raw live room kick purely for "air."** The core kick sample was chosen specifically for having much more low end than the loop's original kick, boosted further; a live kick tracked dry in the room — which "sounds terrible" and "cheap" completely on its own — gets stacked underneath purely to add natural air/room character the sample alone doesn't have. → [[Kick Design]], [[Layering and Stacking]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [How Chromeo Makes KILLER Drums (step by step)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8VLtlIUMF4)
- **Snare: a processed sample (compression + reverb + low end) doubled with a live snare purely for texture.** Same live/sample pairing logic as the kick — a snare with compression, a touch of verb, and some low end still isn't "enough" alone, so a live-tracked snare (also "terrible" and "cheapo" in isolation) gets layered in just for texture, not for tone. → [[Snare Design]], [[Layering and Stacking]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [How Chromeo Makes KILLER Drums (step by step)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8VLtlIUMF4)
- **Harvest isolated snare, clap, and hi-hat hits straight out of the ORIGINAL reference loop, not the whole pattern.** Rather than reuse the scratch loop wholesale or throw it away, Chromeo chop single hits out of it — an isolated snare, a clap, even a sliver of hi-hat — and layer just those individual tones on top of the from-scratch kit; framed explicitly as a "treasure hunt for good single sounds," the same logic as chopping a kick from an old record. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Layering and Stacking]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [How Chromeo Makes KILLER Drums (step by step)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8VLtlIUMF4)
- **Live hand-played hi-hat overdub, tracked specifically to chase a reference record's hi-hat character.** After the built pattern still felt like it was missing something, Chromeo A/B'd against a Gap Band reference that had a real, bright, noisy live hi-hat on top of everything — so instead of reaching for another hi-hat sample, they went back into the room and hand-played a live hi-hat overdub across the whole pattern (deliberately loose/"clumsy," not quantized), then applied just a basic EQ pass to tame the dirt. This is the layer they credit with "sealing the deal" — thumpier, brighter, groovier than the fully-programmed version underneath it. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Layering and Stacking]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [How Chromeo Makes KILLER Drums (step by step)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8VLtlIUMF4)
- **Finish with a direct before/after A/B, loop vs. finished drums, bass in.** Once every layer is stacked, play the new programmed drums against the original scratch loop back-to-back (with the bass added) as the final gut-check for whether the rebuild actually beat the placeholder — the old loop reads as comparatively "clunky" once the new stack is in. — Chromeo (Sol State), [How Chromeo Makes KILLER Drums (step by step)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8VLtlIUMF4)

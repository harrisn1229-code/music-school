# VOI-C14 — A chart is a framework, not an instruction

**Q.** You are handed an unfamiliar standard ten seconds before the count-off. What do you read first, and what do you do with the chords printed on the page?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Read the form first, before a single chord** — AABA or ABAC, and how much of the chart is therefore actually new material. Then **reduce the chords to recurring named devices**: a secondary ii-V into the IV chord is "the IV chord", four bars of motion is "a turnaround". The printed harmonisation is **a starting point, not gospel** — when a chord clashes with the melody, substitute.

## Why it matters

Thirty-two bars cannot be understood as thirty-two independent decoding problems in the time available. Form plus device-matching turns an unfamiliar chart into a known shape with a few details filled in. A concrete prediction that comes free: in an AABA tune starting on the tonic, the last two bars of each A are a turnaround about ninety percent of the time.

## Drill

15 min: open a standard you do not know. Before playing anything, mark the form, circle every turnaround, and name every multi-chord passage as one device. Then comp it.

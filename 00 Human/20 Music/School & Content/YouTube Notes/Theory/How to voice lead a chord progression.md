# How to voice lead a chord progression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FNl6bb755Z4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FNl6bb755Z4)

## Synopsis

A worked, repeatable method for voice-leading any chord progression — choosing inversions that minimize note-to-note motion — demonstrated on a plain Am-F-C-G progression and on "Clocks" (Coldplay), with the rule for why the bass part is normally left out of the process.

## Techniques

- **The full algorithm**: find the note(s) shared between the current chord and the next chord in the progression; keep that shared note fixed as a pivot; move the remaining note(s) to the nearest available note of the next chord (by step, not leap). Repeat through the whole progression. You never need to identify the resulting inversion by name to do this — it's purely a "minimize the distance moved" procedure.
- **The choir analogy for why it matters**: assign each chord tone to a different singer. Root-position-only playing forces some singers into unpredictable leaps chord-to-chord; voice-led inversions let most singers hold a note or move by a single step — easier to perform AND reads as smoother, more melodic motion, since the individual voices stop sounding like they're randomly jumping around the scale.
- **The bass exception, stated as a general rule**: the lowest voice in an arrangement (piano left hand, bass part) usually does NOT get voice-led — it stays on each chord's actual root throughout, even while upper voices are inverted above it. Voice-leading the bass too would smooth its motion but lose the harmonic clarity/character the root-motion bassline provides; a full chord's identity (root vs. inversion) is generally judged from the bass regardless of what the upper voices are doing.
- **Worked case study, "Clocks" (Coldplay)**: right hand voices E♭ (1st inversion) → B♭m (2nd inversion) → B♭m → Fm (root position), each transition chosen by finding the shared tone (E♭→B♭m share B♭; B♭m→Fm share F) and moving only what has to move, by step. Playing the identical chords in strict root position "completely loses the character" despite being the same progression. Left hand, by contrast, stays on the plain root note of each chord throughout — demonstrating the bass exception directly.

# Producing The Weeknd's vocals with Illangelo

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NzSvZaxZUHM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NzSvZaxZUHM)

## Synopsis

Illangelo (Daniel Lopatin's collaborator-in-name-only — actually longtime Weeknd co-producer/engineer, credited across *Trilogy* and *After Hours*) opens up his own Ableton session for Mix with the Masters and walks through two distinct pieces of a Weeknd vocal production: first a dense, sound-design-heavy **"backgrounds" texture chain** (the reverse-reverb swell that opens the record — layered octave/dry/OTT'd takes run through a stack of modulation and space plugins), then the **lead vocal chain** from tracking through to the final EQ. The lead-vocal half is the more valuable half for this vault — it's a first-person, plugin-by-plugin breakdown of a working pop/R&B vocal chain from someone who's shaped one of the most recognizable vocal sounds in modern pop, including exact tracking-compressor settings and the actual product names in his mixing chain (Vocal Rider, Pro-G, DeBreath, an Eiosis de-esser, Pro-L 2, Pro-MB, and a final "Air band" EQ he calls his favorite plugin). Recurring theme throughout: many small, individually-subtle stages stacked in series rather than one or two plugins doing all the work — "little bit by little bit, chipping away at it." **Transcript note:** the auto-pulled captions run clean (spoken breakdown, not garbled music) but cut off with roughly a minute of runtime left, mid-sentence, just before Illangelo names that final "Air band" EQ plugin — everything captured up to that point is reflected below; the plugin name itself is not confirmed.

## Techniques

### Backgrounds / reverse-reverb texture chain (opens the record)
- The reverse-reverb swell that opens the track is triggered from a channel inside a group Illangelo calls "backgrounds" — built from **three parallel vocal layers**: one pitched an octave up, one left completely dry, and one run through [[OTT]] pushing the mids/highs hard while reining in the lows.
- **[[OTT]]** — used on one of the three background layers specifically to push mids and highs while controlling the lows, rather than a flat multiband glue pass. → [[OTT]]
- **"Denise, The Sweeper"** — named on-screen but not identifiable as a specific branded plugin from the transcript alone (possibly an in-house nickname for an automated filter/EQ move rather than a commercial product); logged here rather than guessed onto the wishlist. Immediately followed by a second EQ layer doing low-end notching ("a little bit of notches here... I don't know exactly what this was for but it's there") plus a separate low-end band that fades back IN as the sound decays in volume, to reintroduce weight as the swell dies away.
- **[[Utility]]** — a touch of level automation drawn in specifically to help accent the swell's "rising" feeling (an automation move, not a static setting). → [[Utility]], [[Automation]]
- **ShaperBox (Cableguys, unowned)** — Mix parameter at 22%, pumping at half-beat, for rhythmic amplitude movement on the swell layer. → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Sinevibes Blend multi-chorus (unowned)** — deliberately run at just **2 voices**, despite the plugin supporting up to 8: "if you throw this down to two voices, I love it... there's something about keeping it to the simplistic nature of just two." → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Modulation]] (effect)
- **MondoMod (SoundToys, unowned)** — Illangelo's own words: "I used to use this plug-in all the time... I used this on all backgrounds, I used this on anything that was stereo information. I wanted to get a little bit more from it... I just want to hear it moving." Blend set to roughly 60–80%, used to keep stereo content in constant subtle motion rather than letting a panned sound sit static. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Modulation]] (effect), [[Width and Stereo]]
- An **Audio Effect Rack** containing a device/preset referred to only as "whirl" (unidentified — not confidently matched to any specific commercial plugin) for further sound design, feeding into [[ValhallaDelay|Valhalla Delay]].
- **[[ValhallaDelay|Valhalla Delay]]** — feeds the closing reverb stage of the backgrounds chain. → [[ValhallaDelay]]
- A very long reverb closes the whole background chain — **~19-second decay**: "long decay, 19 seconds... that's beautiful." Bypassing it on the isolated solo made the difference immediately obvious. → [[Reverb]] (effect), [[Depth and Space]]
- Chain finishes with another EQ pass (Pro-Q-family, "Q3" in his own terminology) rolling off the very top end of the whole background stack.

### Recording the lead vocal
- Tracks direct into a **UAD audio interface**, monitoring live through it so the artist (Abel/The Weeknd) hears Auto-Tune plus compression/limiting on the monitor path in real time while performing — keeps playback feeling controlled without printing heavy processing to the file.
- Of the many UAD Unison preamps available, his named favorite is the **Manley VOXBOX**: "I personally really love the VOXBOX from Manley" (all the UAD preamps are described as good — this is the reach-for). → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- Tracking-compressor recipe (a Unison-loaded compressor on the UAD interface — direct match to owned [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII]]): compressor **on**, Release **Medium or Medium-fast**, Attack **Medium or Medium-slow** (depending on the song's tempo/feel), **Threshold left alone** — level controlled entirely via **Input** drive. Target: only slight needle movement — bumps up to about **−1 dB**, peaks **−3 to −5 to −7 dB at the very most** — "a healthy movement," explicitly not the −10/−20 dB range some engineers chase.
- Reverb monitored live while tracking, with a delay running on a secondary Aux — the performance is sung against real space and repeats from the first take, not effects bolted on afterward.
- Stated reasoning for staying interface-direct rather than reaching for vintage mics/hardware compressors/preamps (which he's tried and rates highly): a full year of sessions with Abel jumped between so many different studios that a portable, consistent direct-to-UAD signal path matters more than hardware character that can't travel — "it's just best to keep everything going direct into the UAD sound card."

### Lead vocal — Ableton processing chain (in signal order)
1. **[[Utility]]** — used to manually remove one specific breath, right at the top of the chain, rather than reaching for a dedicated breath-removal plugin for it. Contrasted directly with Cubase's hotkey-based manual breath-editing workflow, which he still finds faster/cleaner but had to partly abandon adapting to Ableton. → [[Utility]], [[Vocal Comping and Takes]]
2. **[[Pro-Q 4|Pro-Q ("Q3")]]** — steep low-end roll-off ("rolling off the low end, quite high"), a boost around **1500 Hz, roughly +4 dB** (a slope/shelf move, not a cut), and a gentle top-end filter for smoothing. Run on **Linear Phase, Medium** only at the very end of finishing a record; **Natural Phase** for the other 99% of the working process — "shout out to FabFilter for creating these just great plug-ins." → [[Pro-Q 4]]
3. **Vocal Rider (Waves, unowned)** — Range maxed at **12 dB up / 12 dB down**, Center **0**, Sensitivity **maxed**, Speed **Fast**. Used specifically because tracking stays clean/uncompressed going in, so Vocal Rider real-time gain-rides the vocal into a tight range before it ever reaches The Box's compressors — likened to the singer physically moving toward/away from the mic automatically (pushed back when loud, pulled in when soft, adding intimacy without compression artifacts). Framed as the foundation the whole rest of the chain depends on: "we're just trying to get the strongest base possible for the lead vocal, because if we don't get the lead vocal super dialed in from the jump, later on the process... you're going to have more trouble." → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[RVox]]
4. **[[Pro-G]]** — used as a gate/expander to create deliberate contrast between the eventual hyper-compressed, focused vocal and audible "space in between" phrases: opens above threshold, tucks by about **12 dB** when below it — "very important part of the puzzle almost with anything you play with." Explicitly compared to Waves RVox's built-in gate (owned [[RVox]]) — Illangelo prefers splitting the job across separate dedicated plugins for more control, even though it's "a little bit more work." → [[Pro-G]], [[RVox]]
5. **DeBreath (Waves, unowned)** — dialed to **−9**, a light touch-up catching breaths the Utility pass at the top didn't fully remove. Named drawback, stated directly: adds significant plugin latency with no workaround found ("if y'all do, let me know"); kept in the chain anyway because it instantly does what used to take hours of manual breath-cutting, despite less precise breath-reintroduction than doing it by hand. → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
6. **Eiosis de-esser (Eiosis E2 Deesser, unowned)** — the strongest single endorsement in the video: "my God, man, I've tried a lot of de-essers and nothing, nothing does what this de-esser does. I think this should be almost your first choice in de-essing. It's my first choice." → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[De-essing]]
7. **[[Pro-L 2]]** — used HERE on the lead vocal track itself, not the master bus. Attack set deliberately **slow** so each phrase's initial transient energy pokes through before the limiter clamps down — peaks visibly land at the beginnings of phrases as a result. Stated philosophy: many gentler stages in series so the vocal never arrives "already clamped down." → [[Pro-L 2]], [[Limiting]] (effect)
8. **[[Pro-MB]]** — describes this multiband-balancing move as an approach he developed "many, many years ago... out of necessity" (needing sessions to sound better, faster) rather than crediting himself with the plugin itself. Most of the gain reduction happens on the low end; the top end is mostly let through except at specific moments that trigger it (wanted, not a flaw — "we want that"). Framed as dynamically balancing the vocal's spectral range in a way static EQ can't: "if you were to do this just with a standard EQ, it's not going to sound as alive as when you're doing it with something that reacts instantly in the moment as needed." → [[Pro-MB]], [[Multiband Compression]] (effect)
9. A **second, unnamed de-esser** — a light cleanup pass catching a couple of remaining syllable/sibilance peaks the Eiosis stage didn't fully address, explicitly NOT the heavy lifter ("it's not doing the heavy lifting... all these plug-ins are just doing a little bit by little bit at different moments"). A layered two-de-esser approach: one primary (Eiosis), one touch-up pass later in the chain. → [[De-essing]]
10. **[[Utility]]** feeding into a favorite EQ plugin — name not captured (the transcript cuts off before it's stated) — built around a dedicated **"Air band"** control, boosted gently around **20 kHz** (set to roughly 0.5 in this session, though he notes it's sometimes cranked to 4–6 on other records). Compared directly, unprompted, to owned [[Fresh Air]]: "I think Slate Digital might have just released something called Fresh Air... I think I might have messed with it a while back and it's fire. This one also does a similar thing." Called his overall favorite plugin — "y'all are going to love this if you don't already know about it or use it." → [[Fresh Air]], [[EQ]] (effect)

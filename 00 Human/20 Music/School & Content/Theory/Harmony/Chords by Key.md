# Chords by Key

Part of [[Music Theory]]. Diatonic chords = chords built only from the [[Keys|key's]] [[Scales|scale]] notes, stacking every-other degree (1-3-5, 2-4-6…). Same pattern in every key.

## Major keys — the pattern
Triads: **I · ii · iii · IV · V · vi · vii°**
7ths: **Imaj7 · ii7 · iii7 · IVmaj7 · V7 · vi7 · viiø7** (see [[Seventh Chords]])
Memorable chant for the quality pattern, true in every major key: **"Ma-mi-mi-Ma-Ma-mi-dim"** (major-minor-minor-major-major-minor-diminished, degrees I–VII) — [[Chord Progressions - Beginner's Guide - Part 4 (The Diatonic Secret)|Max Konyi]]

| Key | I | ii | iii | IV | V | vi | vii° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C | C | Dm | Em | F | G | Am | B° |
| G | G | Am | Bm | C | D | Em | F♯° |
| D | D | Em | F♯m | G | A | Bm | C♯° |
| A | A | Bm | C♯m | D | E | F♯m | G♯° |
| E | E | F♯m | G♯m | A | B | C♯m | D♯° |
| B | B | C♯m | D♯m | E | F♯ | G♯m | A♯° |
| F♯ | F♯ | G♯m | A♯m | B | C♯ | D♯m | E♯° |
| G♭ | G♭ | A♭m | B♭m | C♭ | D♭ | E♭m | F° |
| D♭ | D♭ | E♭m | Fm | G♭ | A♭ | B♭m | C° |
| A♭ | A♭ | B♭m | Cm | D♭ | E♭ | Fm | G° |
| E♭ | E♭ | Fm | Gm | A♭ | B♭ | Cm | D° |
| B♭ | B♭ | Cm | Dm | E♭ | F | Gm | A° |
| F | F | Gm | Am | B♭ | C | Dm | E° |

7th version of any row: I and IV become maj7, ii/iii/vi become m7, V becomes 7, vii° becomes m7♭5. (Key of C: Cmaj7 Dm7 Em7 Fmaj7 G7 Am7 Bm7♭5.)

## Minor keys — the pattern (natural minor)
Triads: **i · ii° · ♭III · iv · v · ♭VI · ♭VII**
7ths: **i7 · iiø7 · ♭IIImaj7 · iv7 · v7 · ♭VImaj7 · ♭VII7**

| Key | i | ii° | ♭III | iv | v | V* | ♭VI | ♭VII |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Am | Am | B° | C | Dm | Em | E | F | G |
| Em | Em | F♯° | G | Am | Bm | B | C | D |
| Bm | Bm | C♯° | D | Em | F♯m | F♯ | G | A |
| F♯m | F♯m | G♯° | A | Bm | C♯m | C♯ | D | E |
| C♯m | C♯m | D♯° | E | F♯m | G♯m | G♯ | A | B |
| G♯m | G♯m | A♯° | B | C♯m | D♯m | D♯ | E | F♯ |
| E♭m | E♭m | F° | G♭ | A♭m | B♭m | B♭ | C♭ | D♭ |
| B♭m | B♭m | C° | D♭ | E♭m | Fm | F | G♭ | A♭ |
| Fm | Fm | G° | A♭ | B♭m | Cm | C | D♭ | E♭ |
| Cm | Cm | D° | E♭ | Fm | Gm | G | A♭ | B♭ |
| Gm | Gm | A° | B♭ | Cm | Dm | D | E♭ | F |
| Dm | Dm | E° | F | Gm | Am | A | B♭ | C |

*V = the **borrowed major V** (from harmonic minor): not diatonic to natural minor, but used constantly because it pulls back to i harder than v. V sounds classical; v sounds modal/natural-minorish. See [[Scale Modes Reference]].

- Each minor key's chords = its relative major's chords, renumbered ([[Keys]]) — Am's C-Dm-Em-F-G are C major's I-ii-iii-IV-V
- Berklee/pop numeral style (used by drawmusic): always uppercase + quality suffix, numbered from the MAJOR scale even in minor keys — Im, ♭III, IVm, Vm, ♭VI, ♭VII. Classical style (used here): case shows quality. Both name the same chords; intervals decide quality, not the numeral case ([[Chord Naming by Context]])

- **A fast fretboard shortcut for finding all 7 diatonic triads (and their 7th-chord extensions) from a single root note on the low E string, no scale-degree counting required.** Find the key's root on the low E string, then play the same fixed shape/pattern from that fret to get the **I, IV, V** major chords. - **Why the major-key diatonic 7th-chord quality pattern (Imaj7 · ii7 · iii7 · IVmaj7 · V7 · vi7 · viiø7, the table above) is structurally forced, not arbitrary — full half-step-by-half-step derivation.** Checks whether each stacked third in the "skip a note" chord-building method straddles one of the major scale's two half-steps (3–4 and 7–1): a half-step inside the span = minor 3rd, none = major 3rd; reading the resulting 3-interval stack off the reference spelling table gives each chord's quality. Proves V is the *only* diatonic degree that can produce the major-minor-minor stack (dominant 7) and vii the only degree that can produce the minor-minor-major stack (half-diminished) — the mechanism behind this table's `7th version of any row` line. Bonus mental shortcut: any diatonic chord's 7th is one scale-degree number below its own root (7th of ii = 1, of V = 4, of vi = 5, of iii = 2). - **A practice drill for building the diatonic 7th-chord ladder off harder, non-major scales, not just major keys.** The familiar major-key derivation, worked in E♭ major: **E♭maj7 – Fm7 – Gm7 – A♭maj7 – B♭7 – Cm7 – Dø7 – E♭maj7**. Then the same stack-a-7th-on-every-degree method applied to **A♭ harmonic minor** (A♭ B♭ C♭ D♭ E♭ F♭ G): **A♭m(maj7) – B♭ø7 – C♭maj7♯5 – D♭m7 – E♭7 – F♭maj7 – Gdim7 – A♭m(maj7)**. - **Beginner walkthrough of building the major-key diatonic triads by stacking every-other note of the scale, off C major** — stacks C-E-G (I, major), D-F-A (ii, minor), E-G-B (iii, minor), F-A-C (IV, major), G-B-D (V, major), A-C-E (vi, minor), B-D-F ("wonky," vii°, diminished — noted as rarely used in popular music), landing back on C. - **A systematic guitar drill for physically mapping the whole minor-key chord ladder above, string by string.** Pick a key (worked in G minor), play the full **i · ii° · ♭III · iv · v/V · ♭VI · ♭VII** triad row tonic-to-tonic — start on the tonic, climb as high as the guitar reaches, then go below the tonic and back up, landing back on i so the ear locks onto a tonal center (the same root-to-root logic as scale practice) — using a simple 3-note **1-5-3** voicing; repeat the identical ladder off a second string (6th string, then 5th string) to see the same chords in a second neck position. Then do it again as **seventh chords** with a **1-5-7-3** (bottom-four-string) voicing, producing exactly this table's 7th row: **i7 · iiø7 · ♭IIImaj7 · iv7 · v7/V7 · ♭VImaj7 · ♭VII7** — direct transcript confirmation of the 7th-chord conversion rule already stated above. Explicitly frames the whole drill as removing "guessing and checking" from songwriting — map every option before writing so a chord choice is deliberate, not stumbled into. → [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Confirms and explains WHY the V gets played both minor (natural-minor v) and major (borrowed V)** — the major V is borrowed from the [[Scale Modes Reference|harmonic minor scale]] (raise natural minor's ♭7-relative 3rd-of-v a half step) and is specifically what makes a progression read as "functionally/traditionally minor" rather than modal — reaching a dominant 7th on that borrowed V (turning v into V7) is called out as "the true functional minor key sound." Leaving it as natural v instead reads as more modal/natural-minor. - **Circle-of-fourths cross-string run through the same 7 chords, to see options the straight up/down-the-neck version above misses** — starting from the highest playable voicing and moving root-by-root a 4th up / 5th down (iv → ♭VII → ♭III → ♭VI → ii° → i, i.e. Cm → F → B♭ → E♭ → A° → Gm in G minor) reproduces the exact chord sequence of the jazz standard **"Autumn Leaves."** Full drill and the underlying 5–1/dominant-tonic "gravity" explanation: [[Circle of Fifths]]. — Jared Borowski (Sound Guitar Lessons With Jared), [[Writing a Complete Song in a Minor Key (Part 1) - Chord Progression]]

# IMPROVE your chords with COMMON TONES and VOICE LEADING!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0fB1ZaLfB2w)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0fB1ZaLfB2w)

## Synopsis

A 19-minute Jack Ruch theory/comping lesson in two halves. The first half is a compressed rebuild of chord construction from scratch — intervals, major/minor triads, sus chords, 6th chords, 7th chords (major 7, dominant 7, minor 7, minor-major 7), and the three upper extensions (9, 11, 13) with the rule that extension numbers only apply once some kind of 7th is already present (otherwise it's an "add" chord). The real content is the second half: a single ii–V–I in C (Dm7–G7–Cmaj7) used as a fixed harmonic skeleton to demonstrate **two distinct ways of choosing extensions across a progression** — (1) **common-tone threading**, picking one note (B, D, E, or G) and voicing every chord in the progression so that note sits on top, which mechanically determines which extension each chord gets; and (2) **voice leading**, moving one voice chromatically by half step from chord to chord (descending, ascending, or both directions at once as contrary motion) instead of holding a note still. Every worked example reuses the exact same three-chord skeleton, so the video functions as a compact catalog of ~15 different ways to voice one ii–V–I, each with a stated rationale for why that top-note or moving-voice choice produces the extension it does. No tab/notation is shown on screen within the captions track (Ruch points to his Patreon for that); everything below is reconstructed from spoken chord names plus the underlying diatonic chord-tone math, which resolves consistently across every example.

## What's taught

**Foundations (skip if already fluent — covered for completeness):**
- Major triad = 1-3-5 of the major scale; minor triad = flat the 3rd a half step. Sus2 replaces the 3rd with the 2nd (D over C); sus4 replaces the 3rd with the 4th (F over C).
- 6th chords: add the 6th scale degree (A over C) to a triad → C6 / Cm6, with several fretboard voicings shown as options rather than one fixed shape.
- 7th chords: major 7 (add the 7th, B, to a major triad), dominant 7 (flatten that to B♭ — "also referred to as a dominant 7 chord"), minor 7 (minor triad + ♭7), minor-major 7 (minor triad + natural 7 — flagged as "a really interesting chord").
- **Upper extensions rule:** 9 = 2nd an octave up, 11 = 4th an octave up, 13 = 6th an octave up — but these numbers are only used once a chord already carries some form of 7th. C9 = C7 + D (C E G B♭ D); Cmaj9 = Cmaj7 + D (C E G B D). Add a 9th to a bare triad with no 7th present and it's a **C add9** instead (C E G D), not a "9" chord — same distinction already on [[Add Chords]]/[[Extended Chords]].

**The core exercise — one ii–V–I (Dm7–G7–Cmaj7), voiced ~15 different ways:**

*Common-tone threading* — pick one note and keep it on top through all three chords:
- **Common tone B:** Dm6 (B = the 6th) → G7 (B = the 3rd) → Cmaj7 (B = the major 7th). B is a chord tone of all three chords in different roles, so it threads for free with no alteration needed.
- **Common tone D:** Dm triad, D on top (root) → G7, D on top (5th) → Cmaj9, D on top (9th).
- **Common tone E:** Dm9, E on top (9th of Dm) → G13, E on top (13th of G) → Cmaj7, E on top (3rd of C — stated explicitly as "one of the basic chord tones").
- **Common tone G:** Dm11, G on top (11th of Dm) → G7♭13, G as the root → C6/9, G on top (5th) — the richest of the four, netting a Dm11, an altered-color G7, and a 6/9 chord from one held note.

*Voice leading* — instead of holding a note, move ONE voice by half step, chord to chord. Same Dm7–G7–Cmaj7 skeleton throughout, extension choice now driven by "what's a half step from the last note" rather than "what stays the same":
- **Descending E→E♭→D** (the inner-voice mirror of the "common tone G" example above, tracking a different voice through the identical three chords): Dm9 → G7♭13 → C6/9.
- **Descending A→A♭→G:** Dm7 (A on top, the 5th) → G7♭9 (A♭ = ♭9) → Cmaj7 (G on top, the 5th).
- **Descending D→D♭→C:** Dm triad (D on top, root) → G7♯11 (D♭ = ♯11, enharmonic C♯) → Cmaj7 (C on top, root).
- **Descending B→B♭→A:** Dm6 (B on top, the 6th) → G7♯9♭13 (B♭ = ♯9) → C6 or Cmaj13 (A on top, the 6th/13th).
- **Ascending A→B♭→B:** Dm7 (A on top, the 5th) → G7♯9 (B♭ = ♯9) → Cmaj7 (B on top, the major 7th).
- **Ascending D→E♭→E:** Dm triad (D on top, root) → G7♭13 (E♭ = ♭13) → Cmaj7 (E on top, the 3rd).
- **Ascending C→D♭→D:** Dm7 (C on top, the ♭7) → G7♯11 (D♭ = ♯11) → Cmaj9 (D on top, the 9th).
- **Ascending G→A♭→A:** Dm11 (G on top, the 11th) → G7♭9 (A♭ = ♭9) → C6/Cmaj13 (A on top, the 6th/13th).

*Contrary motion* — combine an ascending line with a descending line **inside the same three chords**, so two voices move in opposite directions at once (deliberately framed as the payoff/advanced move that closes the lesson):
- **Dm9 (root-5th-♭7-9, doubled 5th on top) → G♯9♭13 (played rootless) → Cmaj9**, read alternatively as a **G triad over C** (upper-structure triad — [[Upper Structure Chords]]/[[Upper Structure Triads from Seventh Chords]]). Outer voice ascends A→B♭→B while an inner voice descends E→E♭→D simultaneously — two independent chromatic lines inside one three-chord move.
- **Dm bar chord → G7♭9♭13 → Cmaj7 bar chord.** Inner voice D→E♭→E ascending, top voice A→A♭→G descending, at the same time.
- **Dm11 → G7♭9♭13 → Cmaj13 (closing example).** Top voice (the 11th of Dm) ascends G→A♭→A into the ♭9 of G7 then the 13 of Cmaj13, while a second, inner voice (the 9th of Dm) descends E→E♭→D into the ♭13 of G7 then the 9 of Cmaj13 — two contrary-motion lines at once. Ruch narrates it explicitly, tone-by-tone: "the top note of our D minor 11 chord, [the] 11, then moves to the flat 9 of G, then finally the 13 of the C chord, and the 9 within this D minor chord moves down to the flat 13 of the G altered chord and then to the 9 of the C major chord."

**Stated takeaway:** none of this is prescriptive — "it's up to you to decide what sounds good to you and what works in the context of the music you're making." The exercise's value is in having a repeatable **process** for choosing extensions (thread a common tone, or move a voice by step) rather than picking upper-structure colors arbitrarily.

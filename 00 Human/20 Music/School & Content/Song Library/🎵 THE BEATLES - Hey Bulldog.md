# Hey Bulldog — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Arranged and recorded start to finish in a single day, and it shows in the best possible way. The structural oddity worth leading with: **the title phrase never appears until the outro**, and the section everyone would call the bridge functions as a refrain — on the evidence of the music alone you'd have guessed a different title. The whole track runs on one riff and one rhythmic idea.

**Form.** Nominally the two-bridge model with the ends chopped about. There is **no final verse**: the second refrain runs straight into the extended outro, which repeats the ostinato twelve times and is the point of the song rather than an afterthought. The intro exposes the riff three times over staggered instrument entrances, a standing Beatles habit.

**Harmony.** The home key mode **flips section by section** — minor for intro, refrains and outro, Mixolydian-inflected major for the verses — with blues colour over the top of both, so even the "major" verses are full of ♭3 and ♭7 ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]). That pushes the chords to the flat side: a naturally occurring **♭VII** and a **v that appears minor rather than major** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). The minor v doesn't destroy the tonic but props it up far more weakly than a major V would. The refrain lays a **rising chromatic line in an inner voice** over static i–iv harmony — dramatic, and deliberately not grammatical, since the line doesn't change the chords underneath it.

**Melody.** The riff is the melodic engine ([[MEL-C03 — Motifs and how to develop them]]) — it opens the song, closes the refrain, colours the refrain tune, and is finally developed over a chord change in the outro. **Rhythm is where the writing lives** ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]): the refrain states one phrase three times with different scansion each time, with two repeats sharing an identical rhythm but **starting in opposite halves of the bar**.

**Arrangement.** Small band — piano, bass, drums, lead guitar — and the two success factors are relentless eighth-note piano chords and a bassline that never sits still. The stereo build is unusually deliberate and worth A/B-ing the channels. The outro's payoff is set up early: background chatter under the guitar solo, and the first bark landing during the last refrain, before the outro has even started.

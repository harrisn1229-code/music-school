# These songs have the same chords... let's swap them! (Mr Blue Sky)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lCr_RbeLUDg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lCr_RbeLUDg)

## Synopsis

First of three RipX-powered "vocal/instrumental mashup" videos (all sharing an identical generic title on YouTube — this note, and the two companion notes in this batch, are disambiguated by their most distinctive song pair). Demonstrates that when two songs share a chord progression, their vocal melodies can be swapped onto each other's instrumentals — used here as a way to *prove* a shared-progression claim by ear, across three song pairs of varying rarity.

## Techniques

- **Core premise**: a chord progression's job is to support the melody above it — so two songs built on sufficiently similar progressions can have their vocals/instrumentals cross-mashed and still sound musically coherent, which serves as an audible proof that the progressions really are close enough to be "the same."
- **Pair 1 — "Mr. Blue Sky" (ELO) / "Yesterday" (The Beatles)**: Bennett states he knows of no other song pair with this similar a progression. Crucially **not identical** — different chord order/timing in places, and different overall phrase length (Yesterday = 7 bars, Mr. Blue Sky = 6 or 12 bars depending on how you count the half-time/double-time feel) — so making the swap work required deliberately adjusting some harmony notes in the isolated vocal stems (via RipX Edit) rather than a pure drop-in swap.
- **Pair 2 — "Karma Police" (Radiohead) / "Sexy Sadie" (The Beatles)**: share the exact chord progression C–D–G–F♯ — notable specifically for the F♯ major chord (built on the raised 7th degree), an unusual chord choice rare enough that its shared presence is cited as long-standing evidence/speculation that Radiohead drew direct inspiration from "Sexy Sadie" when writing "Karma Police."
- **Pair 3 — "Boulevard of Broken Dreams" (Green Day) / "Wonderwall" (Oasis)**: both built on the **Plagal Cascade progression** (i–III–♭VII–IV in minor, already documented on [[Chord Progressions]]/[[Diatonic Modes]] from earlier videos in this batch) — Wonderwall is usually a semitone higher (F minor vs. the comparison key) and technically voices its chords with non-triadic extensions rather than plain triads, but close enough that Noel Gallagher (Oasis) has publicly and pointedly noted the resemblance to Green Day's song in a 2006 quote: *"if you listen you'll find it's exactly the same arrangement as Wonderwall — they should have the decency to wait until I'm dead before they steal my songs."* Bennett's own take: shared chord progression alone is legally insufficient for a copyright claim (progressions can't be owned), though the broader arrangement similarity (acoustic guitar texture, drum entry placement, tempo, general "feel") is a real and separate similarity beyond just the chords.
- **Production note (context, not a technique to route further)**: all mashups were built using RipX DAW's stem-isolation + pitch/timing manipulation tools (sponsor segment) — the harmonic analysis is the substantive content; the tool itself isn't vault-relevant beyond noting how the audio proof was constructed.

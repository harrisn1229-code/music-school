# 14 Songs Based On Classical Music

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iTZjYNKPnYg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iTZjYNKPnYg)

## Synopsis

David Bennett's sixth video in this series (he notes he's "starting to run out of examples") catalogs pop/rock songs that quote public-domain classical pieces, organized loosely by the mood each piece is used to evoke — weddings, funerals/gloom, and one chain of a piece being reworked, then reworked again.

## Techniques

- **Wedding-themed quotes**: Wagner's Bridal Chorus quoted outright at the 3-minute mark of OutKast's "Miss Jackson" (an adapted version also forms the song's piano hook, diverging from the original after the first bar). Mendelssohn's Wedding March quoted in Katy Perry's "Never Worn White" (intro) and 10cc's "Une Nuit à Paris" (worked into the "Honeymoon" section).
- **Funeral/gloom-themed quotes**: Chopin's Funeral March opens DJ Shadow's "Six Days" and the Minecraft soundtrack's "Death" cue, and closes out Duke Ellington's "Black and Tan Fantasy" as a dragged-out final quote.
- **Meter-adapted quote**: Rufus Wainwright's "Oh What a World" directly quotes Ravel's Boléro main theme, reworked from 3/4 into the song's 4/4.
- **A borrowing-of-a-borrowing chain**: Beethoven's 5th Symphony opening → reworked into disco by Walter Murphy as "A Fifth of Beethoven" (1976) → that disco version itself reworked again by Robin Thicke's "When I Get You Alone" (2012) — public-domain borrowing chains can go more than one hop back.
- **Why classical works so well as a source**: fully in the public domain (70 years post-composer-death in most jurisdictions) so it's legally free to draw from, and it offers a sound/idea palette genuinely outside typical pop/rock vocabulary — a "secret ingredient" for character rather than just a legal shortcut.

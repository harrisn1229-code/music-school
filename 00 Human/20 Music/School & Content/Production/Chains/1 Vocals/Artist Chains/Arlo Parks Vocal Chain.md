# Arlo Parks Vocal Chain

**Reference/vibe:** a deliberately restrained, un-fussed vocal system — a small saved insert chain ("Really Really Basic Vocal Chain") plus a handful of named, characterful parallel sends, wrapped around a lead performance built on trained, punch-in-free doubling accuracy rather than comp-and-align editing. The whole approach mirrors the record's production philosophy: keep the moving parts to the minimum that still lets the vocal feel embedded in the track rather than sitting dry and in-front.

**Signal source:** Shure SM7, used for the entire *Ambiguous Desire* album (documented, not owned — see [[Shure SM7]]) — "that's the microphone we used for the entire project... I've tried so many other mics that are fancier or whatever and I just... I'm a creature of habit."

## Chain overview

Track (SM7, full album) → doubled/tripled/quadrupled full takes, no comping, no punch-ins → insert chain "Really Really Basic Vocal Chain" (tune → EQ → compressor → slapback delay → touch-up EQ) → parallel sends ("Underwater Space" reverb, a doubler, a plate reverb, a delay automated on/off per section).

## Step 1 — Tracking: trained doubling accuracy, no comping

- Arlo sings every take **start to finish, never punching in**, and doubles/triples/quadruples the vocal by singing the whole song again from scratch rather than comping sections together — described by Baird as "extremely precise," landing in near-alignment without correction tools. The skill traces back to not knowing comping was an option as a teenager, so every home recording had to be perfect start to finish — a constraint that became a trained ability. → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]]
- On Senses, the guide vocal was recorded as a wordless freestyle over the instrumental first (no lyrics yet, just a repeating "dulled my senses" motif), with real lyrics written afterward around that emotional/lyrical center. → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]]

## Step 2 — Insert chain: "Really Really Basic Vocal Chain" (saved preset)

A small saved preset used as a starting point on most vocals:
1. **Tune** — light pitch correction, "a little tune."
2. **EQ** — a first EQ pass.
3. **Compressor** — "a little compressor."
4. **Slapback delay** — originally added just to make the tracking process feel more fun/inspiring in the room, but sometimes left on in the final mix rather than removed.
5. **A second, touch-up EQ** at the end of the chain.

## Step 3 — Parallel sends (named, characterful — not generic reverb/delay)

- **"Underwater Space"** — a named send bus described as giving the vocal a "watery texture"; several elements are routed to it at once rather than it being a single insert.
- **A doubler** — width/thickening send, separate from the literal doubled vocal takes in Step 1.
- **A plate reverb** send.
- **A delay**, automated on/off per section rather than left running throughout — turned on only where the arrangement calls for it.

## Notes / variations

- **The goal stated explicitly is embedding, not front-and-center clarity:** "I always enjoy when my vocal feels like it's kind of embedded into the tapestry of the music but also still having that definition" — a deliberate move away from the drier, more up-front vocal treatment on Arlo's earlier records, part of this album's wider electronic/nocturnal palette shift.
- **Vocal-as-percussion is a second, separate technique layered on top of this chain, not a replacement for it** — on Jetta and Heaven, short chopped slices of the SAME lead vocal are used rhythmically/percussively alongside the sung lead. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **This chain is a starting point, not a fixed formula** — the send balance and which effects are active are described as "kind of different for every song."

# How I Programmed The Synth Opening On Michael Jackson's Thriller: The Synclavier Pt.3

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D3LBdNwlH78)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D3LBdNwlH78)

## Synopsis

Part 3 of a series on the Thriller album-opening "storm door" chord (part 1 covered the Prophet-5, part 2 the Jupiter-8): the third layer underneath those two synths, plus Fender Rhodes and live brass, is a Synclavier organ patch built entirely from additive-style sine wavetable stacking rather than subtractive synthesis — meant to evoke a pipe organ's foot-pedal bass part.

## Techniques

- **Additive/wavetable octave stacking instead of a subtractive filter patch**: three separate "voices" are each built from stacked sine waves at the fundamental plus successive octaves above it (up to four octaves per voice) — directly analogous to a Hammond organ's drawbar octaves, but here every drawbar is set to a pure octave rather than mixing in thirds/fifths.
- **Small amounts of FM (frequency modulation) layered onto the sine stack** add a subtle "buzz"/brightness on top of the pure additive tone — demonstrated with FM removed vs. added, a small but audible difference in harmonic richness.
- **Slight detuning between voices for width**: one of the three sine-stack voices is tuned to 438 Hz instead of 440 Hz (about 8 cents flat), sitting an octave below the other voices — a small detune specifically chosen to add subtle beating/width without being an obviously "out of tune" chorus effect.
- **Layering role**: this Synclavier organ patch sits UNDERNEATH the Prophet-5 and Jupiter-8 (from parts 1 and 2), along with Fender Rhodes chords and a live brass section — five total layers combining to give the Thriller opening its full, powerful character. No single layer alone reads as "big"; the size comes from the stack.

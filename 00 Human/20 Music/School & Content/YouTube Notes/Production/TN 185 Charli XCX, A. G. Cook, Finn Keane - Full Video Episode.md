## Synopsis

John Kennedy sits down with Charli XCX, A.G. Cook, and Finn Keane (his first Tape Notes appearance) to break down three tracks from *Music, Fashion, Film* — "No One Lasts Forever" (feat. a spoken-word interview with David Cronenberg), "Card Declined," and "Rock Music." The through-line across all three is a deliberate pivot away from *Brat*'s hard-tuned, hyperpop-plastic vocal sound toward something rawer, more "bendy" and live-feeling — Charli explicitly frames this album as "the polar opposite" of *Brat* in approach. Production-wise the trio worked almost entirely audio-first (barely any MIDI — chopping/resampling actual recordings of guitar, bass, live drums, and a small hardware synth palette — Minimoog, PPG Wave, MS-20) inside a single studio room (Rue Boyer, Paris) with a deliberately limited gear set they refused to expand on a return session, specifically to force new textures out of constraint. Two philosophies recur throughout: (1) preserve raw/demo-quality artifacts rather than polishing them out — DI guitar takes are treated as a "fingerprint," never re-amped or re-performed once printed, and AlterBoy/tuning artifacts are left in deliberately; (2) let elements "stick out" rather than gluing everything into one sonic world (an explicit Lindsey Buckingham *Tusk* reference — home-studio and expensive-studio elements sitting in obvious contrast, wild panning, mono-to-stereo drops). "No One Lasts Forever" also documents a genuine creative-process technique: recording a long spoken-word interview (with David Cronenberg) as raw material, marking useful moments live during the take, then chopping/looping a phrase rhythmically into the track.

## Techniques

**Creative process / workflow**
- **A single room, a small hardware palette, and a rule not to add anything new on the return session.** The album's sonic identity comes almost entirely from what was physically present at Rue Boyer studio (Minimoog, PPG Wave, Roland TR-606, Vermona DRM, a handful of guitar pedals) — the trio returned weeks later and deliberately did not add new gear, treating the limitation itself as the tool for finding new textures within a fixed palette. → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]]
- **Recording a long-form spoken-word interview as raw sampling material, with live marking of usable moments.** Charli flew to Toronto and recorded an ~90-minute conversation with David Cronenberg about mortality; she texted the engineer to mark specific moments during the recording itself ("mark this, mark this") rather than relying on a post-hoc listen-through, ending up with ~100 marked moments to choose from. One short phrase ("no one lasts forever") was found to land rhythmically on the beat by chance and became the song's looped hook. → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]]
- **Two producers working in parallel Logic sessions, routing one session's output into the other's as a live sound source.** Finn: "I was plugged into the desk... using my session as an instrument" — recording one person's live session output directly into the other's project file, rather than bouncing/exporting stems back and forth. → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]], [[Session View Workflow]]
- **Treat a DI-recorded pedal take as a locked "fingerprint," never revisited.** Once a guitar part is tracked dry-to-pedal in the room, it's never re-performed or re-amped later ("we're not going back and like, oh, let's do a reverb shoot-out") — the specific imperfections of that exact take (a harmonic that catches wrong, a battery-dying pedal wobble) are treated as the character of that part of the song, not a mistake to fix. → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]], [[Analog vs Digital Workflow Philosophy]]
- **A joking extreme pitch/tempo-down reveals a usable texture.** "Card Declined" began as an unusably fast jam; slowing the whole Logic session down **−9 semitones** via Logic's Speed Change (described as "really dangerous... gonna crash" at that extreme) — done as a joke — immediately revealed a droney, Velvet Underground-adjacent character that became the actual song. The session was later fully exported/re-imported once it hit Logic's processing ceiling at that extreme a setting. → [[Tempo Changes and Rubato]]

**Vocal production**
- **Vocal moving in and out of tune across a song for a raw-vs-classic contrast.** "Rock Music" deliberately alternates a loosely/barely-tuned, more "live" vocal delivery against sections of classic hard Auto-Tune — an explicit pivot away from *Brat*'s fully hard-tuned sound, framed as wanting the vocal to feel more "bendy" and live. *(general starting point — no specific Auto-Tune retune/humanize values given, just the deliberate alternation)* → [[Vocal Tuning]]
- **Little AlterBoy's Robot/Quantize mode for static, non-harmonic backing layers.** On "Card Declined," backing vocal lines are rearranged into nonsense syllables and run through AlterBoy's robot mode to produce flat, static one-note textures that read as texture rather than real harmony — described as "not really harmonies, like static one-note things," stacked with a second, even more processed layer through [[Bitspeek]]. → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Bitspeek]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Crunched, hard-panned backing vocals promoted to the lead role at the end of a song, artifacts left in deliberately.** On "Rock Music," heavily distorted backing vocals (processed through **Waves Butch Vig Vocals** — jokingly called "this strange steampunk plugin") sit hard-panned through most of the song, then take over as the lead at the very end, turned up loud; the extra digital artifacts AlterBoy/Butch Vig introduce at that setting are deliberately left in rather than cleaned up, with only light compression for dynamic range — the philosophy stated explicitly: "we're leaning into all of that sort of extra dirt... it might be digital, it might be analog, doesn't really matter — that's what's fun about it." → **Waves Butch Vig Vocals**, [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Width and Stereo]]
- **A "ghost" vocal layer built from filtered/crushed dialogue rather than a sung part.** A snippet of Charli's own spoken interview audio (recorded alongside the Cronenberg conversation, ultimately not used as dialogue) is filtered and crushed and buried in the mix as a textural "ghost" presence rather than a lyric — sitting alongside the other "radio-filmish" textures in the second half of "No One Lasts Forever." *(general starting point — filter type/crush amount not specified)* → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]

**Sampling / resampling as production**
- **A granular/concatenative resynthesis plugin (DataMind Audio Concatenator) fed an entire studio jam session to build a new drum loop from unrelated audio.** The trio recorded a long instrumental "jam" (MS-20, an MS-20 run through a phaser, Vermona drum machine hits, guitar) with no plan to use it directly; loading the whole batch of audio files into the Concatenator and dropping a target audio file underneath it makes the plugin search across all the loaded samples for fragments similar in pitch to the target, stitching together a genuinely new drum loop built entirely out of the jam's unrelated recordings, described as having "that radio dial feeling." → **DataMind Audio Concatenator**, [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Kilohearts Multipass used to "milk" pitched content out of a resonator effect on an unrelated drum recording.** A resonator effect applied to a snare from a completely different drum session (an accidental "let's just try throwing the resonator on this" moment) produced audible pitch content; Multipass's multiband lanes are then used to isolate and emphasize two of those resonated pitches so they fade in and out independently at the song's ending. → **Kilohearts Multipass**, [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Logic's basic Loop region tool used to deliberately create off-grid, glitchy chops — and the artifact (an extra clicked syllable, a stray click each repeat) is kept, not cleaned up.** Used repeatedly across the album on both vocals and guitars (guitar loops on "Wink Wink," "Card Declined," and the looped ending of "No One Lasts Forever") — chopping a region with the stock Loop function landing slightly off the rhythmic grid on purpose, so each repeat throws in an extra syllable or audible click, treated as a texture rather than an error to quantize away. Logic's built-in time-stretch (acknowledged as "not so great") is used the same way — deliberately, for its own artifacts, on both words and guitar takes. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **A phased, drifting multi-voice vocal loop (a Steve Reich "It's Gonna Rain" tape-phasing homage), finished with a fake tape-wobble/flutter.** The looped "they are dead, they are gone, no one lasts forever" outro layers multiple copies of the same vocal loop slightly out of tempo/phase with each other so they gradually drift apart over the outro (explicitly cited: Steve Reich's tape-phase pieces); a synthesized/added tape-wobble flutter effect is applied to just one side of the stereo image, lightly panned, specifically because without it the phasing effect read as too flat — the wobble is what sells the "unspooling" feeling. A second, extreme version of the same loop concept made for the vinyl edition uses a literal locked groove (a continuously-repeating vinyl pressing) instead of a DAW loop, deliberately not tempo-locked so it throws in an extra syllable and a click on every single pass. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Layering and Stacking]]

**Guitar / pedals**
- **Gamechanger Audio Plasma Coil (Third Man Records edition) as the main lead guitar sound on "No One Lasts Forever," pushed through heavy compression to expose its ugliest detail.** Described as "the most extreme guitar sound we went for" and "the most kind of DI-sounding pure fuzz, pure guitar on the whole thing" — heavy compression is applied specifically "to bring out all the little horrible details," and the plasma pedal's own knobs are manipulated live/manually during a studio jam session (not automated) for further texture. → **Gamechanger Audio Plasma Coil**, [[Compression Staging]]
- **A deliberately minimal, one-pedal-per-sound DI guitar chain, varied per song rather than standardized.** Rather than a fixed pedalboard chain, each guitar part in the session runs through a single overdrive/fuzz pedal straight DI, with the specific pedal swapped out song to song for a different flavor of "dry" distortion: **Boss SD-1 Super OverDrive** on two of "Rock Music"'s guitar layers, a rack unit for the higher two, and the **Lovepedal Englishman** reached for specifically on parts wanting a heavier/grungier, amp-like broken-up tone — the Englishman has a switch that can simulate the amp "running out of power," deliberately used to keep the guitar sounding unpolished/first-take/"innocent." → **Boss SD-1**, **Lovepedal Englishman**, [[Analog vs Digital Workflow Philosophy]]
- **A single chopped/resampled guitar snippet becomes both a rhythmic synth-style bed AND the song's hi-hat layer.** On "Rock Music," a short piece of an old recorded guitar take is fed into **Kilohearts Phase Plant** (used purely as a sampler here, not a synth) with an aggressive LFO constantly modulating it plus an arpeggiator run through the same patch, turning a single guitar power chord into a bouncy, rhythmically alive pad; the exact same chopped guitar sample is separately run through **Cableguys ShaperBox** and blended with a crash-cymbal sample to become the track's persistent hi-hat texture — described as "strangely in sync" because both layers are cut from the same source audio. → **Kilohearts Phase Plant**, [[Shaper]] (owned ShaperBox equivalent)
- **Mono-centered verses opening into hard-panned-wide stereo guitars for a brief, deliberate contrast.** "Rock Music" keeps almost everything dead-center/mono through the verse, then two guitar layers (run through the Lovepedal Englishman) suddenly go hard left/right for a short, deliberately jarring "super wide" moment before returning to mono — a arrangement-level width contrast rather than a constant stereo image. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Chase Bliss CXM 1978's motorized faders "ridden" live during a take, played with deliberately imperfect harmonic picking.** A closing guitar/bass-register part on "Rock Music" runs through the CXM 1978 with its large motorized reverb faders being manually moved during the performance itself (not automated afterward), paired with a harmonic-pick playing technique on the guitar that sometimes catches cleanly and sometimes doesn't — the inconsistency is kept rather than re-taken, described as preserving "all those kind of details." → **Chase Bliss CXM 1978**, [[Depth and Space]]

**Synths / drums**
- **Waldorf PPG Synth (PPG Wave) deliberately mis-played for a pitch-drifting, "janky" chord progression, later run through an unusual filter + Multipass for a dissolving ending.** A. G. Cook: the PPG "is sort of drifting in sound but kind of in pitch" — playing into that instability rather than fighting it produced an unusual chord progression used as the basis for "Card Declined"'s second half; at the very end of the same song the same PPG patch is run through an unspecified filter plus **Kilohearts Multipass** to create a "chewing," dissolving morph as the part fades. → **Waldorf PPG Synth**, **Kilohearts Multipass**
- **Pairing a live-recorded drum kit take with a Vermona DRM drum machine for the first time.** "Rock Music"'s drums are a chopped/edited live kit take (deliberately kept rough/first-take feeling, not comped from many takes) layered underneath a Vermona DRM1 hit pattern — described as the first time the trio had paired live drums with the Vermona specifically; the live kit supplies character/roughness, the Vermona supplies a "classic electronic" backbone. Parallel compression/saturation on the combined drums was tried and rejected specifically because it "took away from the joy... the roughness of it." → [[Vermona DRM1]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Compression Staging]]
- **Roland TR-606 pitched down and blended under other percussion for a soft, "weird" low layer.** On "No One Lasts Forever," TR-606 hits are pitched down and mixed in quietly under the main drum part specifically for a soft, understated low-end texture rather than a rhythmic lead role; elsewhere on "Card Declined" the TR-606 is layered again with the Vermona DRM for "heavy but crunchy" character, sharing the same percussive attack quality as the Plasma Coil guitar. → [[Roland TR-606]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **A Minimoog + Korg MS-20 pairing for a closing synth-noise texture under cymbals.** On "Rock Music"'s second half, a clipped white-noise-like element (built from the Minimoog and MS-20 together — one carrying a low layer, the other the noise character) is timed to fill the space specifically when the cymbals come in, rather than running continuously. *(general starting point — no specific patch settings given)* → [[Minimoog Model D]], [[Korg MS-20]], [[Layering and Stacking]]

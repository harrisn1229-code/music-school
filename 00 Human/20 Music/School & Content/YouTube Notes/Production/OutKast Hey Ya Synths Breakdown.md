# OutKast "Hey Ya" Synths

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e1UPLuyIfow)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e1UPLuyIfow)

## Synopsis

The two Juno-60 synth layers from "Hey Ya" — a tuba-like synth bass (played alongside a separate real electric bass) and a short, breathy melodic counter-line — plus a genuinely useful arrangement observation about the song's irregular bar structure.

## Techniques

- **An odd bar-count phrase (5 bars of 4/4, then a 2-bar phrase) instead of a clean 4/8-bar loop** keeps the groove feeling forward-moving rather than settling into predictable repetition — worth trying deliberately when a section feels too "loopable."
- **Tuba emulation: a slow filter-envelope attack for the instrument's characteristic "lip" onset, requiring the player to anticipate the beat** — the same way a real tuba player has to push their timing slightly ahead so the sound actually "blooms" ON the beat; a slow attack alone doesn't sound right if played exactly on the grid. Sawtooth + a second oscillator on a narrow pulse wave (not detuned/chorused) filled out the tone better than chorusing did, when A/B'd directly.
- **A breath/noise layer on the melodic patch contributes character disproportionate to how audible it is** — barely perceptible in isolation, but removing it measurably flattens the sound's personality; worth checking as an early diagnostic step ("what's the very first thing I notice?") when ear-matching an unfamiliar patch.
- **Deliberate rhythmic counterpoint between bass and melody layers** — the melodic synth line is written to cross the bass part's rhythm rather than lock to it, adding bounce against an already-rhythmic vocal.
- **Attack time alone changes an instrument's character from "talking" to "singing"** — the same patch read as more percussive/spoken with a faster attack, and more melodic/sustained with a slower one, demonstrated as the single control that made the difference.

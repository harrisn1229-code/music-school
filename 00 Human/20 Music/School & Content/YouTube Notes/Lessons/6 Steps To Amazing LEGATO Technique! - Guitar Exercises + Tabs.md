# 6 Steps To Amazing LEGATO Technique! - Guitar Exercises + Tabs

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8a5yZHmRVxA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8a5yZHmRVxA)

## Synopsis

Six fretting-hand mistakes/fixes for legato (hammer-ons/pull-offs), each paired with a short exercise, building to applying the fixed technique over actual licks/backing tracks rather than stopping at drills.

## Techniques

1. **Don't over-press fingers you're not using.** A common bad habit: keeping the index (or middle) finger pressed down on its fretted note for the whole exercise even when it's not actively hammering/pulling. Lift a finger slightly the instant it isn't needed — the alternative is a stiff, uneconomical motion. Fix drilled on all fingers equally, not just the strong ones.
2. **Practice on a "sandwiched" string, not just the outer strings.** The same finger-independence exercise gets meaningfully harder (and more representative of real playing) moved from the low E or high E string to a middle string (e.g. G) where fingers must avoid ringing the strings on both sides — especially audible under distortion.
3. **Drill shape-to-shape transitions, not just one position.** Most finger-independence workouts sit in one fixed position; real legato lines require switching scale/arpeggio shapes mid-phrase without an audible seam. Practice connecting two shapes deliberately so the transition disappears rather than sounding like "two separate shapes."
4. **Fret near the fingernail, not the fleshy fingertip pad.** Contacting the string closer to the nail (not the pad) makes it easier to avoid touching neighboring strings and easier to build speed/cleanliness. Named as the fix for "flat fingers."
5. **Keep the wrist angle constant across all six strings.** The biggest tip, in the video's own words: don't change wrist angle when moving between strings on a full-fretboard legato line — move the whole hand up/down instead of rotating the wrist per string. A constantly-changing wrist angle produces inconsistent results (works "by accident" some takes, fails others) and makes it hard to diagnose why a lick sometimes works and sometimes doesn't.
6. **End every practice session applying the technique to real licks/improv over a backing track**, not just isolated drills — keeps the fixed mechanics from sounding like "practicing" once used musically.

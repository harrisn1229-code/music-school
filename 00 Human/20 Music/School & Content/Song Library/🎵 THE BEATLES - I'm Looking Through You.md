# I'm Looking Through You — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Folk rock with a light blues coating, and the analytical interest is almost entirely **rhythmic-metric**: the words are scanned off-centre against four-square phrasing, and the verse is a genuine puzzle — Pollack cannot settle where the downbeat of each phrase falls, and lays out both parses with their costs. Parsing the downbeat just before the first word gives clean AABA' poetry and sensible chords at the cost of an awkward meta-syncopation; parsing that bar as a pickup removes the syncopation but starts every phrase on an odd chord and worsens the final elision. **Neither is free.**

**Form.** Textbook layout — two bridges with one verse between them. A 2-bar intro cell played three times plus a tag; four sung verse phrases in AABA′ with the last eliding into a vamping connector that reuses the intro cell; a clean 4 + 4 bridge; and an outro on the same vamp.

**Harmony.** An unusual key for them, likely written a few frets down and capo'd up. Two things carry it: the **deceptive move to vi at the start of the third phrase** ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]), the major key wilting into its relative minor, which is the monotony-breaker; and a harmonic rhythm unusually elastic for the Beatles at this point ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]), which is part of why the metric parse is hard. **4–3 suspensions unify the song** ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]) — quick and buried at the end of the first two verse phrases, huge and exposed at the end of the bridge. The intro's opening six-four is the same idea in disguise: better heard as a dual appoggiatura on the V chord than as a root-position tonic.

**Melody.** Diatonic major almost throughout, decorated with appoggiaturas; the only chromatic colour is bluesy ♭3 at each verse climax and ♭7 in the riffs trailing it. A **fixed bass lick recurs under every I–IV–ii**, descending against a rising tune — once you hear it, it works as a subliminal hook.

**Arrangement.** No drum kit; percussion is thigh slaps and handclaps plus tambourine, and the **tambourine part is scheduled, not sprayed** — deliberately silent under all four sung verse phrases. The lead is double-tracked everywhere except the outro, and dropping to single-tracking there buys a late jump in intimacy for nothing.

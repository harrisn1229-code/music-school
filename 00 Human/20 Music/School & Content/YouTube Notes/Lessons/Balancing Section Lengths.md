# Balancing Section Lengths | You Suck at Producing #60

## Synopsis

A genre-comparison lesson on arrangement section lengths, cross-checking real tracks rather than asserting rules abstractly. House music (Valentino Khan, AC Slater, Shiba San) is shown sticking rigidly to 16-bar sections driven by DJ-mixing needs, with fills landing at the same relative points across otherwise-unrelated tracks. Pop music (Charlie Puth, Dua Lipa, Cardi B) instead holds consistent PROPORTIONS between sections (intro ~4 bars, verse/pre-chorus/chorus ~8) while the chorus itself grows longer on each repeat within a song — a "bigger dose each time" hook-delivery principle. Closes with a Billie Eilish example that deliberately breaks both conventions, framed as only reading as intentional because the listener/producer already knows the rules being violated.

## Techniques

- **House: strict 16-bar sections, driven by DJ-mixing/dance-floor needs** — cross-genre comparison confirms 16-bar intro/build/drop sections as a near-universal house convention, with fills consistently landing halfway through a section and again right before the drop; the practical upshot is that with length effectively fixed, the real per-section decision is just energy up or down. → [[House - EDM]]
- **Pop: consistent cross-song section proportions (intro ~4 bars, verse/pre-chorus/chorus ~8), but the chorus itself lengthens on each repeat** — a short, teasing chorus on first appearance, progressively longer on each subsequent chorus, longest at the song's end — delivering a bigger "dose" of the hook each time rather than repeating it identically. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Deliberately break section-length convention only once the convention is understood** — an intentionally unconventional structure (rapid verse/drop cycling, an odd 3-bar buildup instead of 4/8, stacked hook sections) reads as a considered choice specifically because it violates a well-established, recognizable pattern — not because it follows no pattern at all. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

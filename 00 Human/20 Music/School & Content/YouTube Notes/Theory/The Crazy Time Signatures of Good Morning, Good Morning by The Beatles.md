# The Crazy Time Signatures of Good Morning, Good Morning by The Beatles

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gBCPoiaqLjI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gBCPoiaqLjI)

## Synopsis

A meter analysis of a Beatles verse so metrically irregular that no single "correct" time-signature transcription exists — multiple published transcriptions of the song disagree on how to bar it, one demonstrably wrong (miscounting beats into an undersized bar). Verse 2 adds a further wrinkle: a shift from simple to compound time.

## Techniques

- **When no instrument clearly accents a beat, use CHORD CHANGES as the accent cue to determine bar groupings — a practical method for barring genuinely ambiguous meter.** In "Good Morning, Good Morning," neither the drums (Ringo marks every beat evenly with the snare, no backbeat pattern) nor the guitar (staccato chords on every beat, no emphasis) gives away where the "strong" beats are. The one place the song DOES accent a beat is wherever a chord changes — an on-beat chord change reads as a downbeat, and that's the actual basis the video uses to derive its preferred bar groupings (verse line 1: 3/4+5/4+2/4; line 3: 5/4+4/4 rather than the "obvious" three bars of 3/4 the raw beat count would suggest). General principle: when rhythm alone won't reveal the bar structure, harmonic rhythm (WHEN chords change) can substitute as the accent signal.
- **A "there is no single correct transcription" case, stated explicitly rather than glossed over**: multiple real published transcriptions of this song disagree on how to group its beats into bars, and the video treats this as legitimate (several valid options exist) rather than pretending one is definitively right — while also flagging one transcription's genuine error (a bar with more beats than its own time signature allows). Useful precedent for treating irregular-meter notation as a communication choice, not a single ground truth, matching the same principle already documented for "Pyramid Song" in [[Syncopation and Metric Displacement]].
- **Simple time → compound time as a distinct category of meter change, separate from "different number of beats per bar."** Verse 2 shifts from a series of simple-time bars (each beat divides into 2) to 12/8 (compound time, each of 4 beats divides into 3) — NOT a change in how many beats are in the bar (both feel like 4 strong beats), but a change in how each beat subdivides. Practically equivalent to (and often easier to transcribe/read as) 4/4 with a triplet on every beat — confirmed in the drum pattern, where the hi-hat plays consistent eighth-note triplets against kick/snare marking the 4 strong beats. The audible effect: a "shuffled" or swung 4/4 feel, distinct from the earlier verse's more abstractly-barred simple-time irregularity.
- **A songwriting-process observation worth keeping as context**: the video argues this level of metric complexity almost certainly wasn't a deliberate compositional puzzle — more likely the band followed the melody/chords as they naturally heard them and the irregular meter fell out as a side effect, only becoming apparent when someone later tried to transcribe it to sheet music.

# Super Charge Your Blues Rhythm Skills!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3QHhFNeaDCA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3QHhFNeaDCA)

## Synopsis

Jack Ruch's 33rd note in this vault ([[YouTube Notes Index]]) — a straight-ahead blues-rhythm-guitar lesson aimed at players stuck on plain Chuck Berry-style comping. In the key of G, Ruch teaches two families of movable, rootless four-note chord grips — **9th chords** and **13th chords** — for the I-IV-V blues (G9-C9-D9 / G13-C13-D13), each playable in two neck positions, then shows how to dress a bare comping pattern (accents on beat 1 and the "and" of beat 2) up into a fuller two-bar phrase with two specific fills: a beat-4 triad-resolving-to-chord-top fill, and a beat-3 pickup fill alternating two notes into the top of the phrase. The video is entirely comping-vocabulary and fretboard-navigation — no gear, no soloing, no production — and closes by demonstrating all four combinations (chord family × position) mixed freely against a backing track. Distinct from Ruch's other rhythm-guitar video already in the vault, [[Make Your Rhythm Guitar Sound Like A Record]], which layers three separate *parts* from small triad shapes; this one builds a single part from bigger extended-chord grips with fills woven directly into it.

## What's taught

- **Setup:** key of G, I-IV-V blues (G-C-D), all chord voicings **rootless** (no root note in the shape — supplied by the bass) and built on the middle four strings.
- **9th-chord family, position 1:** rootless **G9** = B F A D (3rd-♭7-9-5th); **C9** = C E B♭ D G (root-3rd-♭7-9-5th); **D9** = the C9 shape slid **up one whole step** = D F♯ C E A. A half-step chromatic passing chord connects C9 back to G9.
- **Base rhythm template:** accents on **beat 1** and the **"and" of beat 2** — play this alone against the backing track before adding any fill.
- **Fill 1 (end-of-measure, makes it a 2-bar phrase):** on **beat 4** of bar 1, a **D major triad** (G C E) resolving down to the **top of a G7 voicing** (F B D), with a **hammer-on B♭→B** (half step) into the landing. Over the C9 (IV chord), the same fill keeps the **B♭ in** rather than hammering to B, since B♭ is a chord tone of C9.
- **Fill 2 (beginning-of-measure pickup, stacks with Fill 1):** a rhythmic pickup starting on **beat 3 of the previous measure**, alternating **E and G**. Over the IV and V chords it becomes **E-G-E**, then resolves down to the **root of the current chord** (C for IV, D for V) instead of back up to G.
- **Relocating to position 2 (up the neck):** the C9/D9 shape (transposed) covers **G9** around the 10th fret; the rootless-G9 shape (transposed) then covers **C9**; sliding that up a whole step again gives **D9**. Same relay logic transposes the whole 9th-chord family to a second position. Demonstrated live: comp position 1 once through, then jump to position 2 with the fills added, mixing positions mid-phrase.
- **13th-chord family, position 1:** **G13** = F B E G (♭7-3rd-13-root); **C13** = B♭ E A C (♭7-3rd-13-root); **D13** = the C13 shape slid **up one whole step** = C F♯ B D. Stated character: 13th chords sound **brighter** than the 9th-chord family — same function, different color, "different tools in your toolbox."
- **13th-chord family, position 2:** same relay-transposition method as the 9th-chord family — the position-1 G13 shape reappears higher up the neck as the position-2 grip, paired and whole-step-slid the same way to get C13/D13 in the new position.
- **Same two fills reused** over the 13th-chord family in both positions — no new fill vocabulary needed, just the existing two fills laid over a different chord family.
- **Closing demonstration:** free comping mixing chord family, position, and fills at will over the backing track — the stated end goal is fluency moving between all four combinations live, not memorizing them as separate fixed routines.
- **Logistics (video-page-only):** tabs/notation and the backing track are available on Ruch's Patreon — external resource, not routed further.

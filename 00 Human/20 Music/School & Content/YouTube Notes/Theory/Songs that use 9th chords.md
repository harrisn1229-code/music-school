## Techniques

- **The 9th degree itself never moves between dominant 9, major 9, minor 9, or add9 — only what's BELOW it changes.** C9 (full name "C dominant 9") = C7 (dominant) + 9th. Cmaj9 = Cmaj7 + the SAME 9th note. Cm9 = Cm7 + the SAME 9th note. In every case, the "major/dominant/minor" label describes the underlying 7th-chord quality, not the 9th itself — genuinely easy to misread given the name foregrounds "9."
- **Dominant 9 as a "blues-tonality upgrade" on the tonic** — using a dominant 9 rather than a plain dominant 7 chord even ON the tonic (not just V), for extra depth in blues-derived music: "Papa's Got a Brand New Bag" (James Brown). "Play That Funky Music" (Wild Cherry) — used throughout.
- **add9 vs. 9, restated with a clean example pairing**: "Cadd9" adds ONLY the 9th to a plain triad (no 7th) — "Wonderwall" (Oasis). Reinforces the naming-trap material already logged from the companion video on [[Extended Chords]].
- **Major 9**: "Peg" (Steely Dan, opening).
- **Minor 9**: "Let's Stay Together" (Al Green) — the same example already logged from the companion video, independently confirmed here. "Dogs" (Pink Floyd, verse, strummed acoustic).
- **Both major-add9 AND minor-add9 in one arpeggiated part**: "Every Breath You Take" (The Police) — the guitar arpeggio moves between both add9 flavors within the same part.
- **The ♭9 alteration — new ground versus the companion video** (which covered ♯9 but not ♭9): flattening the 9th on a dominant chord adds extra tension for an even more satisfying resolution. "For Once in My Life" (Stevie Wonder) — D7♭9 resolving to Gm7; Bennett A/B's a plain D7 version against the actual D7♭9 Stevie plays, to demonstrate the extra payoff the ♭9 buys on the resolution.
- **♯9 ("the Hendrix chord") — reinforces the companion video with two additional Beatles examples**: "Taxman" and "Michelle" (both cited here as further Beatles uses of the dominant-♯9/Hendrix-chord sound, beyond "Michelle"'s ♭9-adjacent chord already logged from the companion video).
- **Minor-major 9 ("the James Bond chord")**: defined explicitly as a minor-major 7 chord (minor triad + major 7th) with the 9th added on top (e.g. Em(maj9) = Em(maj7) + F♯) — same chord already logged from [[Songs to recognise different chord types by ear]], reinforced here with the explicit build-it-in-two-steps explanation.

# US vs. UK music theory EXPLAINED

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ltO5r5ZZNxo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ltO5r5ZZNxo)

## Synopsis

A terminology cross-reference for the three main places US and UK music-theory vocabulary diverges — note-value names, interval-step names, and cadence names — useful as a lookup when the same underlying concept shows up under an unfamiliar label.

## Techniques

- **Note-value naming, two full systems mapped 1:1**: whole note = semibreve, half note = minim, quarter note = crotchet, eighth note = quaver, 16th note = semiquaver, 32nd = demisemiquaver, 64th = hemidemisemiquaver. The US system names each value by its literal fraction of a whole note (transparent/logical); the UK system's names are historical holdovers from an older era when the semibreve wasn't the longest note (a now-obsolete "breve" and "longa"/"maxima" existed above it) — which is why "minim" (from Latin *minimum*) now names one of the LONGER note values, a fossil of when it really was the shortest.
- **Interval-step naming**: UK "semitone" / US "half step" (both mean the same smallest standard interval); UK "tone" / US "whole step."
- **Cadence naming, a genuine 1:1 terminology map worth having as a lookup table**: UK "perfect cadence" = US "authentic cadence" (V→I); the "perfect" qualifier itself (both dialects) additionally requires root-position chords with the tonic on top when resolved — an "imperfect authentic/perfect" cadence is the same V→I without meeting that stricter voicing. UK "imperfect cadence" = US "half cadence" (ending on V, unresolved) — the US name is described in-video as clearer, since "cadence" implies resolution to most ears and "half" signals the deliberate non-resolution better than "imperfect" does. UK "interrupted cadence" = US "deceptive cadence" (V→vi).
- **Picardy third naming is actually a shared borrowing, not a US/UK split**: the UK term is borrowed directly from the French "tierce picarde" rather than being a native English coinage — a minor-key passage resolving to a MAJOR tonic chord instead of the expected minor one. → [[Picardy Third]]

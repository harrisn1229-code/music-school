## Synopsis

A tight, exercise-focused lesson from Jared (Sound Guitar Lessons) laying out a single core idea — **directional alternate picking** — and then a ladder of seven exercises that drill it into automatic use. The core idea reframes "alternate picking" away from strict down-up-down-up alternation and toward a **beat-locked default**: downstrokes on the beat, upstrokes on the offbeat, with the picking hand continuing to move through that pattern even on notes it doesn't actually pluck (rests, or notes played via slur instead of a pick attack). He opens with a strumming analogy (fluid constant motion vs. jolting stop-start motion) to motivate why a default, non-cognitive picking pattern matters, then works through seven progressively harder exercises built on a C major scale (5th position) and, for the last one, an A minor blues scale shape — each demonstrated at 100 BPM and again at double speed. The last two exercises are the payoff: full scale runs with selective slurs, showing how the beat-locked pattern survives (and simplifies) real playing once hammer-ons/pull-offs/slides start skipping strokes. Unlike Jared's practice-system videos (e.g. [[How I Organize My Guitar Practice (So I Never Want to Stop)]]), this one doesn't reference his "Practice Priority Pyramid"/spreadsheet system at all — it's a standalone, self-contained lesson, though it shares his channel's habit of a hard milestone gate (here: three clean reps in a row with a metronome).

## What's taught

The full rule system and all seven exercises are extracted in detail on the new technique note **[[Directional Alternate Picking System]]** (created from this video) — summary:

- **The rule:** pick down on beats 1/2/3/4, pick up on the offbeats ("and"s), regardless of whether a note is actually being plucked there — the hand keeps moving through rests/slurs so the next real pluck is already headed the correct direction.
- **Distinguished from "strict" alternate picking** (literal down-up-down-up alternation with no beat-anchoring) and positioned as a *default/backdrop* you fall back to, not an unbreakable rule — deliberate departures (runs of downstrokes, etc.) are fine on purpose.
- **Positioned against economy picking**, named as a separate technique Jared plans to cover in a future lesson.
- **Seven exercises**, each at 100 BPM then double-speed, using a C major scale (5th position) for 1–6 and an A minor blues scale shape for 7:
  1. Straight alternate picking baseline (`D U D U`, every note picked).
  2. `D U D –` (rest/sustain on the trailing offbeat).
  3. `D U – U` (two upstrokes in a row, strategically placed).
  4. `D – D U` (ghost-motion through a skipped downbeat).
  5. `– U D U` (rest on beat 1 — syncopation/internal pulse training).
  6. Full C major scale run with selective slurs — first "real" application, forcing skipped strokes where hammer-ons/pull-offs replace a pluck.
  7. A minor blues scale with selective slurs — same idea, more musical/"fun" capstone; worked example of sliding 8th fret → 7th fret on the 3rd string as the slur move.
- **Milestone: each exercise clean with a metronome, three times in a row, no mistakes** — the stated "leveled up" bar.

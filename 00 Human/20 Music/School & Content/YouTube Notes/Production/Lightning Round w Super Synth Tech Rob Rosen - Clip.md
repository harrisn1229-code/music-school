## Synopsis

A rapid-fire Q&A between synth tech Rob Rosen and composer Anthony Marinelli — opinions, not technique, on which vintage synths are best-sounding, most reliable, most overrated, best value, etc. The one genuinely actionable technique (running guitar pedals on synths, with specific pedal recommendations) is routed to [[Modulation]]; everything else is kept here as a reference record of two experienced players' gear opinions rather than forced into individual gear notes.

## Techniques

Mostly opinion/ranking, not technique — Rob Rosen's rapid-fire answers, for reference:
- Easiest to program: Juno-106. Never breaks: OB-8. Most unique-sounding: Memorymoog. Best bass: ARP 2600. Best string synth: Solina. Most expressive: CS-80 ("no questions asked"). Most expensive: Moog Modular or an ARP 2500. Hardest to repair: CS-80 and the Oberheim Matrix/Quadra series. Best reissue: OB-X8. Most overrated: PPG Wave (his own minority opinion). Best under-the-radar: Roland System-100M (over the better-known 700 series). Desert-island synth: Oberheim OB-8 ("Voice" — the Oberheim Eight Voice). Best bang-for-buck: Trident synth, or a Jupiter-8 relative to its ~$25k price vs. a Trident's ~$6,500. Favorite virtual synth: Omnisphere. Never wants to see again: Oberheim Matrix/Quadra series. Favorite FM/wavetable vintage synth: the Yamaha GS1, the Yamaha DX1, and the Synclavier. Cheesiest-in-a-good-way: Oberheim Matrix-12 (its stock presets). Favorite vintage drum machine: Oberheim DMX. Best-looking: the Oberheim Eight Voice ("the white tux of synths").
- Anthony Marinelli's answers: favorite synth overall: ARP 2600 (easiest to program, self-contained with built-in speakers — no engineer needed to patch in). Favorite polysynths: Jupiter-8 and Prophet-5 (reliable enough to gig with repeatedly). Desert-island synth: ARP 2600 (accepting its monophonic limits). Best-looking: the Moog Modular (childhood association with hearing *Switched-On Bach* in 1968).
- **Guitar pedal technique** (the one genuinely reusable production tip, routed in full to [[Modulation]]): run guitar pedals on synths, keeping a parallel dry signal alongside the pedal path rather than going fully wet, and don't worry about impedance-matching a synth's hotter signal into a guitar pedal — it works and sounds good regardless. Named pedals: Maestro Phase Shifter, Mu-Tron III.
- Career-history aside from Anthony: spent a long stretch focused on film scoring via the Synclavier system (FM synthesis → mono sampling → poly sampling → MIDI → direct-to-disk digital recording), including creating orchestral mock-ups for Steven Spielberg on *The Color Purple* with Quincy Jones (Jones nicknamed the mock-up process "Polaroiding," after the photos he used to take of console settings before recall was possible) — before returning to hands-on analog synth programming.

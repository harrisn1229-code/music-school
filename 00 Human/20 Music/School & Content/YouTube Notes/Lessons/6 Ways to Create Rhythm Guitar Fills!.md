# 6 Ways to Create Rhythm Guitar Fills!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Yhy_qjyBMg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Yhy_qjyBMg)

## Synopsis

A concrete, R&B/gospel-lineage lesson on decorating rhythm guitar with fills — the connective-tissue phrases played in the gaps a lead vocal or melody leaves open. Jack Ruch demonstrates six distinct interval/shape devices over one basic R&B backing track (chords **A major – B minor – F♯ minor**, all diatonic to A major, with a simple organ melody standing in for a lead vocal): fast hammer-on/pull-off "butterfly" licks (a Curtis Mayfield/Bobby Womack gospel device), three-string triad-grouping fills, diatonic double stops in 6ths (the Steve Cropper/Memphis Soul sound), 4ths, and 3rds, and single-note pentatonic lines (call-and-response, counter-melody, muted texture). Unlike a scale-running lesson, every device here is explicitly framed as chord-progression-aware — because the three chords are diatonic to one key, all six devices draw from the same A-major-scale/A-major-pentatonic note pool, so they combine freely rather than needing separate vocabulary per chord. The most transferable idea: rhythm guitar fills aren't one skill but a toolbox of six genuinely different shapes (single-note licks, three-string triads, and three flavors of double stop), each with its own idiomatic sound, and a competent rhythm player reaches for whichever one fits the moment rather than defaulting to the same lick every time.

## What's taught

- **The setup:** an R&B backing track in the key of A major — chords A, Bm, F♯m (I–ii–vi), with a simple organ melody standing in for a lead vocal. All six fill devices below are demonstrated directly against this progression and melody, so every device is really being taught as "how do I fill the space this melody leaves."
- **1 — Butterfly fills:** fast hammer-on/pull-off licks pulled from the A major pentatonic scale, played inside/around the current chord voicing. Named for the gospel/R&B lineage — **Curtis Mayfield, Bobby Womack**. Over A major: hammer from the 5th degree up to the 6th with the open high A ringing; hammer from the 9th up to the major 3rd with the 5th ringing. Same device relocated around the B minor and F♯ minor chord shapes.
- **2 — Triad-grouping fills:** uses the three-string triad groupings (top three strings / upper-middle three strings / lower-middle three strings) as fill material — either decorating a single chord or alternating between two triads for a mini-progression fill (worked: A major → E major; A major → D major). Same grouping system relocates to Bm and F♯m shapes.
- **3 — Sixths:** diatonic double stops a 6th apart (A up to F♯ in this key), moved through the A major scale under the chords — the classic **Steve Cropper / Memphis Soul** sound.
- **4 — Fourths:** diatonic double stops a 4th apart (E up to A). Two named grip pairs slide diatonically: 9th+5th (B+E) → 3rd+6th (C♯+F♯); 5th+root (E+A) → 6th+9th (F♯+B). Over the minor chord (Bm): play ♭7+minor 3rd above the chord, sliding up a whole step to root+4th.
- **5 — Thirds:** diatonic double stops a 3rd apart (A up to C♯), moved through the scale on different string sets — framed as a generally reusable device (any scale that fits a progression can be harmonized in 3rds for fills/counter-melodies), not R&B-specific.
- **6 — Single-note pentatonic lines:** straight lines from A major pentatonic (heard as F♯ minor pentatonic over the F♯m chord) played between rhythmic chord "chunks" that imply the backbeat/snare groove. Three uses named directly: call-and-response with the melody, counter-melody against it, and muted fills for percussive texture rather than melodic content.

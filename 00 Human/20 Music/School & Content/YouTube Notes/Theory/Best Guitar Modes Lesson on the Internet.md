## Synopsis

A from-scratch modes lesson built around a genuinely useful framing not stated this explicitly in the vault's existing modes material: modes are taught two different, both-valid ways — a **scalar methodology** (mode = a scale shape starting on a different fretboard position) and a **tonal center methodology** (mode = which note the underlying chord progression makes feel like "home"), and most of the internet's mode confusion comes from mixing the two without saying so. The mechanical scale-building content (major-scale-starting-on-different-degrees, the intervallic-difference table) duplicates ground the vault's [[Diatonic Modes]] page already covers thoroughly — the routing below adds only the net-new framing and the worked audio-demonstration example.

## Techniques

- **Scalar methodology.** Each mode is the same parent major scale, played starting from a different scale degree — same notes as playing the relevant [[Diatonic Modes]] "relative approach." Guitar-specific addition: physically slide to a different scale-shape position on the neck for each mode rather than just naming a different starting note — e.g. C major scale shape gives Ionian at the standard position, sliding up to the position that starts/ends on D gives the same notes read as D Dorian. **Explicitly flagged as an incomplete method** — "does not fully capture the sound of each mode" — because playing a shape without a grounding chord progression underneath doesn't establish which note actually feels like home; critics of scale-shape-only teaching are described as not wrong.
- **Tonal center methodology — the fix, demonstrated by audio A/B.** The tonal center is "the note that feels like home." Worked example: the exact same G major scale notes played over three different backing tracks produce three different perceived modes purely from the chord progression underneath — over a G-rooted (Ionian/major) track, G feels like home; over an E-rooted (Aeolian/minor) track, E feels like home; over an A-rooted (Dorian) track, A feels like home. **The core claim: it's the underlying chord progression's tonal center, not the notes played over it, that determines which mode is actually being heard** — identical melodic material, three different modal identities.
- **Diagnostic for identifying a mode by ear in a progression:** find which chord functions as the tonal center/home chord in a progression built from a single key's diatonic chords — that chord's root names the mode. Worked example: a D–C–G progression (all diatonic to G major) with D functioning as the tonal center reads as Mixolydian, not the "default" Ionian a plain G-major key signature would suggest.
- **Intervallic-formula table (matches [[Diatonic Modes]]'s existing color-tones section, no new information):** major = baseline; Lydian = ♯4; Mixolydian = ♭7; Dorian = ♭3 ♭7; Aeolian = ♭3 ♭6 ♭7; Phrygian = ♭2 ♭3 ♭6 ♭7; Locrian = ♭2 ♭3 ♭5 ♭6 ♭7 — framed as a "know the major scale, derive any mode" cheat sheet.

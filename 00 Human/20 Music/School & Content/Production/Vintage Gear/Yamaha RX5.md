# Yamaha RX5

## What it is
Yamaha's flagship PCM drum machine of the DX era — a big black slab with deep per-voice editing that made it the *sound designer's* drum machine of the late 80s, where most rivals were preset boxes with attitude.

## What makes the sound
- **12-bit PCM voices with real synthesis-style editing**: per-sound pitch (over a huge range), full envelopes with reverse-style decay tricks, and pan — you could mangle the ROM kit into things Yamaha never intended, which is exactly what producers did.
- **The pitch-bent RX5 toms/kicks** (detuned way down into booming, gated thuds) are its signature move — a poor man's Simmons drama from PCM.
- Cartridge slot for extra waveforms; punchy, bright, slightly brittle converters that read unmistakably "late-80s digital."
- 12 velocity-responsive keys + a serious pattern/song sequencer made it a production hub, often clocking a whole MIDI rig.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Simpler]] with RX5 samples (repitch aggressively — that's the idiom); [[kHs Bitcrush]] for the 12-bit edge.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2; vintage archives.
- **Free:** RX5 kits and cartridge dumps circulate freely.

## Used on
- Genre/era: late-80s R&B/new jack, synthpop, and hip hop productions that wanted tuned/mangled machine drums — widely used, thinly documented at the record level, so genre-level per the rule.

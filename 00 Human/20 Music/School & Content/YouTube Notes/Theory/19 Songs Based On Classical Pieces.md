# 19 Songs Based On Classical Pieces

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JE5Vb2xR06k)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JE5Vb2xR06k)

## Synopsis

David Bennett's fifth video in the classical-borrowing series, cataloging 19 more pop/rock songs derived from classical pieces — including two genuine multi-hop borrowing chains, several meter-changed lifts, four previously-uncovered Elvis Presley examples, and a real melodic-inversion case study (Rachmaninoff inverting Paganini).

## Techniques

- **Borrowing chain 1**: Chopin's Nocturne in F minor No. 1 → Blonde Redhead's "For the Damaged Coda" → self-sampled by B.o.B for "Both of Us" — three generations removed from the source.
- **Borrowing chain 2**: Nino Rota's *What Is a Youth* (deliberately neo-Elizabethan film score) → Lana Del Rey's "Old Money" (first two bars lifted, meter changed 3/4→4/4) — three stylistic generations from actual Elizabethan music.
- **Meter changes are a common default move when lifting a classical melody**: Satie's *Gymnopédie No. 1* (3/4) → Janet Jackson's "Someone to Call My Lover" (stretched to 4/4, chord order also reversed).
- **Four more Elvis songs derived from classical/light-classical sources**: "Tonight Is So Right for Love" (Offenbach's Barcarolle), "Today, Tomorrow and Forever" (Liszt's Liebesträume), "It's Now or Never" (the Neapolitan song "'O Sole Mio," relayed via a 1949 Tony Martin cover).
- **Ode to Joy reused at three different fidelity levels**: near-verbatim quote (The Seekers' "Emerald City"), transformed-but-traceable (Bright Eyes' "Road to Joy," melody lowered a step + simplified harmony), and merely similar contour rather than a real quote (The Killers' "Mr. Brightside" — flagged as probably NOT a genuine borrow, a useful calibration case for "how similar is similar enough").
- **True melodic inversion as a composition technique**: Rachmaninoff's *Rhapsody on a Theme of Paganini*, Variation 18, inverts every interval of Paganini's 5-note theme (ascend→descend and vice versa) to generate the entire variation — full mechanism now on [[Motivic Development]].

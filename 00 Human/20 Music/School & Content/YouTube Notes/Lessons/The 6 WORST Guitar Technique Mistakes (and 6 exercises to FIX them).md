# The 6 WORST Guitar Technique Mistakes (and 6 exercises to FIX them!)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oDvut9G_3Yw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oDvut9G_3Yw)

## Synopsis

Six recurring technique mistakes BERNTH identifies both in his own playing history and across his students, each paired with a fix/exercise. Substantial overlap with, and a few genuine additions to, the vault's existing [[Fretting and Picking Hand Mistakes Checklist]] — most notably a deliberate, context-dependent exception to "always use the thumb behind the neck."

## What's taught

1. **Thumb not used at all (severe beginner error)** — the thumb should actively support the fretting fingers from behind the neck; playing with no thumb contact at all is rare but genuinely crippling once corrected.
2. **Thumb-over-the-neck is fine — deliberately, contextually — for expressive bending/vibrato, even though it's "wrong" for fast technical passages.** States this as a personal, intentional switch: thumb tucked behind the neck (Italian-style) for fast/technical playing, thumb hooked over the top (Hendrix-style) specifically for expressive bends and vibrato, because the more expressive, physically freer motion is worth trading strict technique for in that context. Matches [[Fretting and Picking Hand Mistakes Checklist]]'s existing Hendrix/Italian grip split, but stated here as a real-time in-song switch rather than a fixed per-context rule.
3. **Full-arm/locked-wrist picking motion** — using the entire arm instead of the wrist for picking causes messy string transitions, poor note-level control, and blocks dynamic control; framed as one of BERNTH's own biggest historical roadblocks. Fix: deliberately unlock and isolate wrist motion via a dedicated drill.
4. **Extreme fretting-hand angle limits stretch, and it's misdiagnosed as "small hands."** Many students blame hand/finger size for failed stretches when the actual cause is the *angle* the hand approaches the fretboard at — demonstrated extending a 2-note-per-string pentatonic shape to 3-note-per-string: an extreme hand angle makes the stretch impossible, a corrected angle makes the same stretch easy with the same hand.
5. **Finger positioning: play close to the fret AND keep a curled angle.** Restates [[Fretting and Picking Hand Mistakes Checklist]]'s existing fret-proximity and curled-finger points, with an added consequence: flat (uncurled) fingers block adjacent strings, which directly prevents a clean hammer-on/pull-off "snap" motion — ties finger curl to a concrete downstream technique failure, not just tone.
6. **Hand synchronization** — the classic mismatch between right-hand picking speed and left-hand note-changes, illustrated by a real gig story: a touring drummer once called him out for not actually playing in note values (not locking to 16ths/16th-triplets), just picking "as fast as possible" without synchronization. Fix: a dedicated hand-sync drill.

Also touches string-bending pitch accuracy (play the two target pitches separately first, then bend to check tuning against a backing track) and vibrato dynamics (avoid a single fixed intensity — match vibrato width/speed to the musical context, e.g. rock lead vs. acoustic ballad).

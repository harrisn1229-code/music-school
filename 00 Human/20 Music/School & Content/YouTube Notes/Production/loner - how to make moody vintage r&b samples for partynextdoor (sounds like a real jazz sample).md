# how to make moody vintage r&b samples for partynextdoor (sounds like a real jazz sample)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wD4RzEKo7vU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wD4RzEKo7vU)

## Synopsis

A from-scratch sample-flip breakdown by loner, chasing the vintage jazzy R&B mood of PARTYNEXTDOOR's "Joy" (off *P3*). The video opens on identifying and setting up an unusual 6/8 time signature by ear (counting the drums in threes), then builds a full instrumental sample — piano, electric guitar, bass, brass — voiced with real jazz harmony (minor 9ths, minor 7ths, a major 9th with an inverted bass note) and pushed toward "gritty and dark" with layered reels/tape, compression, and EQ on every element. The drum programming is deliberately minimal (kick + snare + hats + a heavily-reverbed ride), and the arrangement is built entirely by *subtracting* elements across four 8-bar sections rather than adding new material — the same restraint-through-subtraction move logged elsewhere on this channel. The whole sample is bounced to one audio file, pitched down a semitone, and finished with EQ + compression before a short "flip" demo: Melodyning the bounce to find its bass notes, pitching the whole thing up 2–3 semitones because the bass read too low, and writing a new bass part (including glide notes) on top. Most valuable here: the concrete jazz voicings (with an explicit chord inversion explained), the 6/8 setup workflow, and the subtraction-based 4-section arrangement — all of it plainly reusable outside the R&B/jazz lane.

## Techniques

**Time signature (0:00–1:00)**
- **Identify an unusual time signature by ear from the drums, not the melody.** loner's method: listen to how the drums count — "Joy" counts in groups of three (one-two-three, one-two-three) instead of four, which is the tell for 6/8 (six counts per bar) rather than 4/4.
- **FL Studio setup:** Options → Project General Settings → change the numerator/denominator fields to 6/8. Owned-lane equivalent: Ableton's time signature is set in the same place as tempo (top of the Arrangement/Session view, or per-clip in Session View) — change the numerator/denominator fields there the same way.
- **6/8 changes the whole bounce, not just the count** — demonstrated against a metronome click: the same chords in 6/8 vs. 4/4 read as a completely different feel, before any processing is added.

**Piano — jazz chord voicings (1:00–2:20)**
- **Instrument: Teletone Audio "Postcard Piano"** — chosen deliberately for vintage character/imperfection over a clean modern piano. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Roll the bass note of the chord slightly ahead of the rest of the notes** (not block-triggered) for a natural, "bouncy" feel — a small, deliberate rhythmic offset rather than a full arpeggio. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Progression:** Bm9 (×2, with filler notes added) → C♯m7 (×2, with top-line notes added) → bars 5–7 repeat bars 1–2 (Bm9 with the same extra notes) → C♯m7 again → resolves on **Amaj9**, voiced with **A as the bass note** and **B inverted up to the top** of the chord (rather than sitting where a plain root-position voicing would put it). → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Chord Inversions]]
- **Processing:** a "Reels"-style tape emulation ("baked" preset, hiss/motor character turned down) → **Goodhertz Vulf Compressor** (owned, [[Ghz Vulf Compressor 3]]) to tighten the part up → EQ cutting low end. → [[Kramer Tape]], [[EQ Eight]]

**Electric guitar — filling the gaps (2:20–3:20)**
- **Instrument: Kontakt "Evolution Strawberry Bank"** — used to fill the empty space before/between the piano chord hits. → [[Kontakt 7]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Chain:** a valve compressor → [[Cassette]]-style crunch → a fruity delay → EQ cutting low end and harsh mid-range frequencies.
- **The last bar of the guitar part is reversed** — the audio is time-stretched slightly first, then reversed, for a unique-sounding cadence into the next section.

**Bass — Scarbee Rickenbacker (3:20–3:40)**
- **Instrument: Kontakt "Scarbee Rickenbacker"** ("scarlet rick and back a base" in the auto-caption) — kept deliberately simple/clean. → [[Kontakt 7]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Chain:** a tube pre-amp preset only — no other processing, to keep the part clean.

**Brass — sourced, not synthesized (3:40–4:10)**
- **Realistic brass is hard to fake convincingly**, so loner sources Splice loops rather than programming a brass VST.
- **Layered chops, reversed and left dry (no effects on the reversed pass itself)**, then put on ONE mixer channel together with: a bandpass EQ (cutting both low and high end) → a heavy reverb (mix pushed almost to 100%) → a fruity delay for extra space. → [[EQ]], [[Reverb]], [[Delay]] (effect)

**Drums — minimal, deliberately simple (4:10–5:30)**
- **Snare + kick on one mixer channel**, kick hitting only on the downbeat of every bar, both run through a bandpass EQ (cut low + cut high).
- **Hi-hat volume turned way down** — present but barely audible.
- **Ride hits only on the "one"**, pushed through a heavy reverb specifically to extend/lengthen the hit, plus a delay to fill space.
- **Open hat** as the final drum element.
- **Swing knob brought up to ~50%** — a habit loner says he uses a lot lately on "unique" drum feels (afrobeats, jazzy material like this one) for a more natural swing. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]

**Arrangement — subtraction across 4×8-bar sections (5:30–6:20)**
- **Bars 1–8:** every element present (bass, guitar, piano, brass).
- **Bars 9–16:** bass removed — explicitly reasoned as leaving room for whoever eventually flips the sample to add their own bass.
- **Bars 17–24:** guitar removed (bass restored) for a more spaced-out section.
- **Bars 25–32:** bass removed again, AND brass removed, for the sparsest final section.
- Pure subtraction — no new material is written per section, only elements toggled off. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

**Bounce & finishing (6:20–7:00)**
- **Bounce the entire sample to one audio file**, then **pitch the whole thing down 1 semitone**.
- **EQ:** cut real low end + a few mid-range frequencies.
- **Compressor:** tighten the sound and shave the peaks.

**Flip demo (7:00–end)**
- **Open a new project and Melodyne the bounced sample** to identify its actual bass notes (found: B♭, C, B♭, C, A♭). → [[Plugin Wishlist]] (Melodyne is the #1 tracked gap)
- **The extracted bass notes read too low**, so the ENTIRE sample gets pitched up 2–3 semitones before writing a new bass part on top.
- **New bass part includes a couple of "glidy" notes** (slides/portamento between notes) rather than plain static hits.

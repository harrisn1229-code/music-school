# Ten Daily Fretboard-Gliding Exercises

Ten short daily exercises for fretting-hand strength/coordination, picking-hand speed, chord-shape stretching, sweep picking, and away-from-the-guitar rhythm counting. Progress criterion for the sequential ones: once a run can be played 3 times through without stopping, increase the tempo.

## 1. 1-2-3-4 finger combinations (baseline)

The classic four-fret, one-finger-per-fret ascending/descending exercise. Trains finger strength and hand synchronization. Common mistakes: lifting fingers too high while ascending, letting open strings ring, not staying smooth/connected. Once the basic 1-2-3-4 order gets easy, vary the finger order (2-4-1-3, 3-2-4-1, 4-3-1-2, etc.) to keep it challenging.

## 2. Anchored-finger version of exercise 1

Same four-fret span, but anchor one finger down for the whole pattern (the ring finger is named as the hardest choice) while the others move. A harder variant of exercise 1 — same benefit, more hand stretch.

## 3. Right-hand-only 16th-note string-crossing speed drill

Left hand not used at all. Play a full bar of 16th notes on the high E string, then two groups of 16ths alternating E and B strings, then two more groups alternating E and G strings. The catch: the pattern starts on an **upstroke on the downbeat**, the reverse of the usual downstroke-on-downbeat default — deliberately trains picking-hand speed and string-crossing independent of fretting-hand demands.

## 4. The "Macroclin" two-string interval pattern

Named after Thomas Macroclin (source of exposure, not necessarily the exercise's originator). Two interleaved patterns at once: the high E string repeats the same four notes throughout, while the string underneath it cycles a 3-2-1-4 finger order using only fingers 1+3 or 2+4 (never both pairs together) — forming either a minor 3rd or a perfect 5th interval shape (exact interval only strictly true on the B/E string pair; treated loosely elsewhere for organizational purposes). A genuine hand-stretch exercise.

## 5. Satriani's staircase chord-shape drill

Build a "staircase"-shaped chord voicing, invert it, and repeat — a chord-shape/voicing exercise (not a scale run) credited to Joe Satriani, aimed at players who want musical/melodic material rather than pure shred technique. Works across different string sets too.

## 6. Paul Davids' major-7 arpeggiated chord + fixed-finger reach drill

Two parts, credited to Paul Davids. Part 1: form the same "staircase" chord shape as exercise 5 (a major 7th chord), then pick the notes individually while keeping all fingers planted on their frets — notes should sound picked one at a time, not rung together like a strum (a lighter-touch analogue of sweep picking). Part 2 (much harder): keep all fingers anchored in their original chord-shape position while only the middle and ring fingers are allowed to move to reach the exercise's other notes — genuinely difficult to keep the anchored fingers, especially the middle finger, from drifting.

## 7. Away-from-the-guitar rhythm counting

No guitar needed — just a metronome app. Practice counting "1-2-3-4" out loud in time. Easy at faster tempos (demoed ~75 BPM); much harder as the tempo drops (demoed down to 50 BPM, then 30 BPM — the metronome's floor). At very slow tempos, count the 16th-note subdivisions between beats out loud ("1-e-and-uh, 2-e-and-uh...") to stay anchored — removes "I don't have my guitar" as an excuse to skip rhythm practice.

## 8. Sweep-picked version of the Satriani staircase chord

Credited to John Petrucci's *Rock Discipline*. Take the same staircase chord shapes from exercise 5, but sweep-pick through them instead of arpeggiating with individual picking — see [[Sweep Picking Arpeggio Shapes]] for the general sweep mechanic. Caution: don't tense the shoulder to power the sweep motion — it should come from the wrist/forearm, not the shoulder.

## 9. Two-finger micro version of exercise 1, playable as trills

A reduced version of exercise 1 using only two fingers at a time instead of all four, playable either as picked notes or as trills (rapid hammer-on/pull-off alternation between the two fretted notes) — likened to a flute player's trilling technique.

## 10. Pre-playing warm-up routine

Run hands under hot water to increase blood flow before playing. Massage and squeeze the fingers. Stretch fingers backward, then make a grabbing/fist motion, repeated in both directions, for all fingers — recommended 10–15 minutes total (not rushed) to properly condition the hands and reduce injury risk. Optional callus maintenance: trim excess finger-tip skin (not the nail itself) with nail clippers on all fingers that contact the strings.

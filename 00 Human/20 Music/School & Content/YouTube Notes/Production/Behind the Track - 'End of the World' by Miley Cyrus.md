# Behind the Track｜'End of the World' by Miley Cyrus

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lc8rL_onYog)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lc8rL_onYog)

## Synopsis

Jonathan Rado and Sean Everett walk through building "End of the World" from a simple piano-vocal demo, reharmonized toward a 70s-Swedish-pop (ABBA) flavor, tracked with live drums/bass/vocals at The Village, and layered into a dense "orchestra out of one part" acoustic-guitar wall.

## What's taught

- **Reharmonize a simple demo's chords to reopen the song, then let the vocal melody adapt to follow.** The original two-chord piano-vocal demo was given a different chord progression in a "classic 70s Swedish" (ABBA) style by a co-writing team, which changed the song's character and effectively became the chorus hook; the vocal melody was subsequently adjusted to track the new harmony rather than the chords being fit under the original melody.
- **Reference points named explicitly for drum sound, borrowed from specific classic records.** Drum tracking aimed at the sound of Pink Floyd's *The Wall* and Fleetwood Mac's *Rumours* — named as concrete sonic targets guiding mic/room choices during tracking, not just a vague "vintage" direction.
- **Groove and feel come from the player, not the notes — demonstrated by swapping bass players on an identical part.** A programmed/demo bass part (straight 8th notes) had been sitting in the mix for a long time and "sounded good"; once a specific session bassist (Pino) played the exact same 8th-note part, the entire groove of the song shifted for the better — same notes, same rhythm, different feel entirely from touch/timing nuance the player brings that can't be notated or easily explained.
- **Build "an orchestra out of one part" by stacking many performances of the same acoustic guitar part on top of each other**, explicitly modeled on George Harrison's layered-acoustic technique — many acoustic guitar takes of the same motif combined into one composite, denser-than-any-single-take acoustic texture, with the piano motif acting as the anchor everything else follows.
- **Deliberate emotional juxtaposition, again**: a lyrically sad song paired with music that doesn't read as sad — the instrumental is described as intentionally "fighting against" the song's emotional content rather than reinforcing it.
- **Composition-by-iteration philosophy**: a melody is genuinely malleable — different chords, a different beat, a different tempo can all be tried under the same melodic line until something "feels correct," rather than assuming the first harmonic/rhythmic setting is final.
- **"Make it harder for yourself on purpose" as a stated production philosophy**: deliberately using unfamiliar gear or extra processing steps (e.g. routing through tape) specifically because the friction and limitations of doing it the harder way bake real, audible character into a track — treating a technical inconvenience as a source of usable character rather than something to streamline away.

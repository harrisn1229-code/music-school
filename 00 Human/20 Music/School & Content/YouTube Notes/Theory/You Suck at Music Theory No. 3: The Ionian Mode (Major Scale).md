# You Suck at Music Theory No. 3: The Ionian Mode (Major Scale)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AWcibIjEWFY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AWcibIjEWFY)

## What's taught
Underbelly builds the major (Ionian) scale from its whole/half-step formula, then stacks a triad on every degree of the scale ("leapfrog": root, skip a note, add a note, skip, add) to show WHY some of those triads sound happy, sad, or "wrong" — by literally counting the half-steps inside each stacked triad. That leads to the core rule (4+3 half-steps = major, 3+4 = minor, 3+3 = diminished, always adding up to 7 except the diminished chord's 6) and the resulting quality pattern that holds in every major key (I ii iii IV V vi vii°). The back half turns that chord palette into a method for actually writing a progression: direction, distance, and quality as three independent levers for choosing the next chord.

## Techniques
- **Build the major scale from the W W H W W W H formula, off any root** — same pattern, any starting note, same "happy" quality regardless of key. → [[Scales]]
- **Stack a triad on every scale degree by "leapfrogging"**: root, skip the next scale note, add the one after, skip, add — repeat starting from each of the 7 degrees to get the full diatonic triad set. → [[Chords]], [[Chords by Key]]
- **Chord quality is decided purely by counting half-steps inside the stacked triad**: 4 then 3 half-steps (bottom-to-middle, middle-to-top) = major; 3 then 4 = minor; 3 then 3 = diminished. Major and minor both total 7 half-steps root-to-top; diminished totals only 6 — the "odd one out," which is why the vii° chord sounds unresolved and mostly gets used only as a passing chord. → [[Chords]]
- **The resulting triad-quality pattern is identical in every major key**: I ii iii IV V vi vii° = Major minor minor Major Major minor diminished. Verified by rebuilding the same pattern starting on G instead of C. → [[Chords by Key]]
- **Writing the next chord in a progression = three independent levers**: **direction** (moving up to the next scale-degree chord reads as uplift, moving down reads as a pull-back), **distance** (a small jump like I→ii is gentle, a big jump like I→vii° is dramatic), and **quality** (major→major feels different from major→minor even across the same distance/direction). Start a progression on the scale's own root chord to establish the key, then vary these three levers deliberately. Recommended drill: write at least 4 different progressions in a key other than C using this method. → [[Chord Progressions]]

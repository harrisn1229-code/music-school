# Intermediate Rhythm Guitar Exercises - Improve Your Timing Now!

## Synopsis

A note-grouping exercise for tightening rhythm guitar timing and generating syncopated riff/comping ideas — break a chosen note value (eighth notes here) into small groups (2, 3, 4...) and accent the first note of each group.

## Techniques

- **The system:** pick a note value (eighth notes for this lesson) and mentally break the stream into groups of a chosen size — 2, 3, or 4 — accenting the first note of every group.
- **Groups of 2 and 4 are the "boring" case:** every accent lands squarely on a beat the click is already counting (quarter notes), so the result stays rhythmically predictable no matter how the notes are arranged.
- **Groups of 3 are where it gets interesting:** because 3 doesn't divide evenly into the underlying meter, the accents land *between* the straight quarter-note pulses the metronome is counting — the most basic and reliable way to generate genuine syncopation from a simple accent rule.
- **Important distinction:** groups of three eighth notes here is **not** the same thing as eighth-note triplets — triplets compress three notes into the space of two; this note-grouping system keeps the underlying eighth-note pulse constant and just changes where the accent (not the note density) falls.
- **Same system used for composing/improvising in odd meters** (7/8 etc.) — this lesson is the simpler, straight-meter application of the same note-grouping idea.
- **Worked accent example:** accenting with an octave-higher E on the 12th fret of the low E string, moving the accent point around the note stream to compare groupings by ear.

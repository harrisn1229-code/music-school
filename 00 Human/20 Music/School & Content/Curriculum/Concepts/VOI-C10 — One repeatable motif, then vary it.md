# VOI-C10 — One repeatable motif, then vary it

**Q.** Why does repeating one rhythmic idea beat inventing a new one every bar — and in what order do you introduce variation?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Repetition is what makes comping read as cohesive and intentional.** Reinventing the rhythm bar to bar reads as scattered even when every individual choice is correct. So **lock one motif first** — hold it for a full pass through the form — *then* depart from it and return. The return only lands because the motif was established as the baseline.

## Why it matters

A stable comping pattern is also what lets everyone else in the rhythm section interact with you: a drummer can catch a hit and double it, a soloist can build on a surface that stays put. Scaled up, the same idea is **riff comping** — hold one whole melodic-rhythmic figure for eight bars at a time and let the tune's own repetitions tell you when to change it.

## Drill

15 min: pick one two-hit-per-bar motif — say the "and" of 1 and beat 3 — and hold it through a whole chorus. Then add a walking bass: root on beats 1 and 3, an approach note toward the next root on 2 and 4.

# Erosion

**Category:** Drive/Color
**Vendor:** Ableton

## What it is

Ableton's stock lo-fi/degradation device — narrow-band noise or a tunable sine tone modulated onto the signal to add grit, hiss, or metallic ring. Live 12.4 added a **Blend** knob that crossfades between the noise-based and sine-based modes instead of forcing a hard either/or choice.

## What it's used for

Cheap-sounding lo-fi grit, metallic/ring-mod-adjacent texture, and subtle noise-floor character — often as a texture layer rather than a corrective effect.

## FX chain notes

Inserted directly on a track for character/texture; frequently paired with an envelope follower so the effect only appears while the source is playing, rather than sitting on constantly.

## Techniques

- **Frequency + Noise both pushed up for straightforward bass grit** — a simpler, non-envelope-followed use than the Blend/envelope-follow techniques below: turn Erosion's Frequency up and Noise up toward 100 directly on a dubstep bass patch for immediate texture/grit, no modulation routing needed. → [[Operator]], [[BASS Slot]] — You Suck at Producing, [The Most Slept On Ableton Synth for UK Dubstep](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vLsEQAxir1o)
- **Blend knob (Live 12.4) crossfades noise ↔ sine** — instead of picking one Erosion mode, Blend sweeps continuously between the noise-band character and the sine/tone character, so a patch can sit anywhere on that spectrum rather than jumping between two fixed modes — dnksaus, [delay just became an AUTO SLICER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zAjmcohzIwc)
- **Envelope-follow Erosion's own Amount and/or Blend** — map an [[Envelope Follower]] to Erosion's Amount (and optionally the new Blend knob) so the erosion character tracks the amplitude of whatever's playing into it — a level-reactive grit layer instead of a static one, useful stacked with a Wander-mode [[Echo]] pitch-warble for a moving lo-fi texture. → [[TEXTURE Slot]] — dnksaus, [delay just became an AUTO SLICER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zAjmcohzIwc)

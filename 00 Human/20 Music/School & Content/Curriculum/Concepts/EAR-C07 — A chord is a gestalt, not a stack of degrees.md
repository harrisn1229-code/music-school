# EAR-C07 — A chord is a gestalt, not a stack of degrees

**Q.** Why shouldn't you decompose a chord into its component scale degrees in real time, and what should you train instead?

> [!question]- Answer
> Because combining feeling states produces a genuinely **new, higher-level experience** — the way syllables combine into a word you hear whole rather than as separate sounds. Train **each chord type × quality × position as its own feeling state directly** (major-on-4, minor-on-6, dominant-on-♭7), the same way you trained the twelve single degrees. Reserve conscious decomposition for the cases that actually need it, like an unfamiliar voicing you're transcribing.

## Why it matters

It's the reason chord recognition doesn't get faster by getting better at intervals. The recursion runs degree → chord → progression → whole song, and each level is recognised as a gestalt rather than reconstructed from the one below.

## Drill

Play the seven diatonic triads of C in random order against a C drone. Name each by *position and quality* as one label ("minor-on-6"), not by spelling it.

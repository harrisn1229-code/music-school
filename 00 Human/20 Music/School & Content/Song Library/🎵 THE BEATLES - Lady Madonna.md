# Lady Madonna — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A slice-of-life character sketch welded onto a hard-driving semi-blues, and by 1968 the hardest-rocking thing the band had released in a while. The **fast tempo and very short verse** mean the song racks up a lot of sections for an ordinary running time, and an unusually large share of them are instrumental.

**Form.** Verses arrive in pairs everywhere except the very last one, so the eight-bar unit reads as sixteen; counting the intro — a complete instrumental run-through of the verse — the opening block is effectively a verse stated three times over. The bridge is 16 bars in four phrases, ABAC.

**Harmony.** A small vocabulary used pointedly. The **♭VI–♭VII–I cadence is the whole harmonic idea** — neither of those chords contains any of the home key's sharps, so the approach to the tonic is a wash of cross-relations ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]), and that clash is the sound rather than an error to fix. The actual V chord is **withheld from the verse entirely** and appears only at the end of each bridge. Because the home key is already soaked in the blue ♭3, the bridge can slip to the ♭III key through the parallel minor ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]), converge there, and swerve back at the last moment onto a dominant deliberately ambiguous between major and minor — which is exactly what makes the major verse feel earned when it returns. Harmonic rhythm is structural ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]): steady through the verse with a small acceleration into the cadence, then **half speed through the bridge**.

**Melody.** The tune leans on ♭3 and ♭7 ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]) while the verse simultaneously stresses the diatonic major 6th against a bassline running ♭6 underneath — friction audible long before it's explicable. The bass riff's third note is a notation puzzle Pollack leaves open, and his answer is that the ear takes it both ways at once.

**Arrangement.** Piano, drums and bass with the exotic layer supplied by **vocalising into cupped hands to fake a brass section**, joined by real saxophones. The bassline competes with the tune for attention and is then promoted to lead in the instrumental verses instead of a solo being written. The intro is piano and drums alone, and the outro hands the track back to that same reduced trio, so everything between reads as accumulation rather than clutter.

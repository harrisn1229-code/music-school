# What to Learn on Bass (in Order)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qUbdASBMkts)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qUbdASBMkts)

## Synopsis

BassBuzz's master curriculum video — the "what order to learn everything on bass" companion piece that [[Goal-Tiered Practice and the Self-Taught Bass Commandments]] and [[How to Start Learning Bass - Self Taught!]] both point at and leave for later (their Commandment 6 closer names it directly as a way to keep short-term practice goals fresh over a 6–12 month horizon). This video is that promised curriculum: **55 numbered steps grouped into five levels** (Level 0 through Level 5), each step a self-contained thing to learn, roughly in the order BassBuzz recommends learning it, from "buy a bass" all the way to jazz-fusion-shredder-level theory and technique. It's explicitly framed as an index, not a tutorial — "you could technically learn all of these things in like an hour but it'll take weeks or months to actually learn good technique," and the video itself is a curriculum map, not a lesson, with a companion free PDF (linked in the video's description, not accessible from the transcript) providing resources/exercises per step and a "mega playlist" ordering BassBuzz's own catalog to match. **The load-bearing psychological frame, repeated at every level transition:** learning bass is, in part, a fight against "the resistance" — a term borrowed explicitly from Steven Pressfield (author, *The War of Art*), personified here as an internal voice that "urges you to give up, forget about it, think you're not good enough." Each level's song-list section closes with the same beat: crushing a level's assigned songs is ammunition against the resistance, and — named as its own explicit step (**Step 21, "notice your progress"**) — most self-taught players (and even some teachers) never do this without being prompted, so it has to be a deliberate practice, not an assumption. This reframes the whole roadmap as goal-tiered in the same spirit as [[Goal-Tiered Practice and the Self-Taught Bass Commandments]]'s long/medium/short-term ladder, just laddered by **skill level** (0–5) instead of by a player's personal goal — the two frameworks are meant to be read together: this video answers "what's next," Goal-Tiered Practice answers "how to structure showing up for it."

## What's taught — the 55 steps, by level

### Level 0 — Get ready to play (steps 1–4)

- **Step 1 — Buy a bass.** Named as the one thing "most humans on earth have never done" and the single biggest determinant of whether progress happens at all. The video's own bar for choosing one is low and deliberately de-stressed: "you can just pick a bass that makes you feel good and has a pretty paint job" — a free companion PDF (linked in the video description) has fuller buying resources.
- **Step 2 — Learn how to tune.** Framed as a precondition for everything else: "if your strings aren't tuned correctly nothing you play will sound right."
- **Step 3 — Learn how to plug into your amp and attach a strap.** Named as basic enough to "train a monkey to do" — the point being it's a non-obstacle, not a skill to dwell on.

### Level 1 — Technique foundation + easy riffs (steps 4–9, practiced against songs)

Framed as the level where early confidence-building matters most, because most self-taught players quit here if a teacher (or a video series) front-loads theory/scales before any musical payoff — the video's own diagnosis of why that happens: a bad teacher "grinds hours of theory and scales before you get any musical satisfaction."

- **Step 5 — Learn the names of your open strings.** E A D G, low to high.
- **Step 6 — Learn how to pluck.** Alternate index and middle fingers, plucking across (not up off) the string, thumb anchored on a lower string for reference. → [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]] is the vault's dedicated deep-dive on this right-hand system.
- **Step 7 — Learn how to fret.** Use all four fretting fingers, press near the end of the fret (closest to the bridge side of the fret wire), fretting-hand thumb kept relaxed. → [[Fretting-Hand Strength, Stretch, Stamina, and Finger Control]].
- **Step 8 — Learn basic rhythms.** Whole notes, half notes, quarter notes, eighth notes. → [[Time Signatures & Note Values]].
- **Step 9 — Learn to read tab.** Framed as the beginner's actual notation, not sheet music — turns "this magical jumble of numbers" into a playable line.

**Songs to test the level:** "Killer" (Seal), "Walk on the Wild Side" (Lou Reed), "Running With the Devil" (Van Halen) — described as "delicious but doable diddies" chosen deliberately easy so the player gets a musical win fast, not a grind.

### Level 2 — Rhythm, listening, and the 12-note system (steps 10–21, practiced against songs)

The stated failure mode this level exists to fix: once a player starts trying fuller songs, they hit **ringing strings, plucking the wrong string, and losing the beat** — three named, specific, common beginner breakdowns.

- **Step 10 — Basic muting technique**, to tame ringing strings. → [[Bass Note Muting Technique]].
- **Step 11 — String crossing**, to get more reliably on the plucking-hand's target string. → [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]].
- **Step 12 — The finger-roll technique**, for more efficient fretting.
- **Step 13 — How the drum kit works** (basic drum anatomy/role knowledge, not drum playing).
- **Step 14 — Start actively listening to the drums while playing along**, so timing doesn't drift.
- **Step 15 — Spend real time mastering Level 1's rhythms before moving on.** Explicit warning against rushing ahead: "if you jump to the more complex rhythms in later levels too soon your rhythm will be sloppy... take the time here to master the many combos and variations of the basics."
- **Step 16 — Learn the chugging rhythm** — named as used constantly across many different bass styles.
- **Step 17 — The 12-note system**: how the musical alphabet works and maps onto the fretboard. → [[Bass Fretboard Note Memorization System]] is the vault's applied version of this exact mapping.
- **Step 18 — Basic musical intervals**: how the distance between two notes is named. → [[Intervals]].
- **Step 19 — Time signatures**: how many beats per bar. Deliberately de-stressed: "this mostly just means counting to four over and over."
- **Step 20 — Learn to read sheet music, specifically rhythm notation.** Called optional overall, but **rhythm notation specifically is important for everyone**, because tab (learned back in Level 1) shows *what* fret to play but never *when* — rhythm notation is the piece that fills that gap. → [[How to Read Sheet Music — The BIGGEST Mistake Everyone Makes Part 4]].
- **Step 21 — Notice your progress.** Called out as its own numbered step deliberately, not an afterthought: "I'd say about 100% of students miss this without being prompted, and even bass teachers are not immune... you get so focused on what you're going to learn next that you forget how far you've come." Named directly as **the best defense against the resistance** — see the Synopsis above. Ties directly to [[Goal-Tiered Practice and the Self-Taught Bass Commandments]] Commandment 3's "track progress in a simple journal" point — this step is the *reason* that journal point exists, not a separate idea.

**Songs to test the level:** "21 Guns" (Green Day), "Child In Time" (Deep Purple), "With or Without You" (U2) — named as songs that "will easily crush your spirit" without the Level 2 skills above.

### Level 3 — Core theory (steps 22–34, practiced against songs)

Framed against the "freak geniuses" straw man directly: some players really can play by pure ear/instinct with no theory, but for anyone theory-curious enough to be watching a video like this, **theory speeds up learning songs, building better basslines, and communicating with other musicians** — theory is presented as a speed multiplier, not a gate.

- **Step 22 — Memorize more note names** (beyond Level 2's basics).
- **Step 23 — The major scale.** → [[Bass Scale Shapes — Major, Minor, Pentatonic & Blues]].
- **Step 24 — The minor scale.** → [[Bass Scale Shapes — Major, Minor, Pentatonic & Blues]].
- **Step 25 — Actually make music using those scales** — explicitly named as its own step separate from learning the shapes, i.e. don't stop at shape-memorization. → [[Improvising Over a Drone Track]] and [[Bass Pentatonic Riff-Writing Recipe]] are the vault's existing "now do something with the scale" methods.
- **Step 26 — What a key and a key signature are.** → [[Keys]], [[Key Signatures]].
- **Step 27 — What chords and arpeggios are, and how they relate to scales.** → [[Chords]].
- **Step 28 — Triads, and how to play them on bass.** → [[Chords]] § *Triads (3 notes)*.
- **Step 29 — Diatonic chord progressions and Nashville numbers.** Flagged directly as **"easily the biggest point of failure if you're self-teaching"** — "it's really easy to misunderstand this material without a teacher to guide and support you." → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Chords by Key]].
- **Step 30 — (Soft course plug, listed as its own step in the video's own numbering, kept here for completeness of the count, not as an actual technique.)**
- **Step 31 — Understand the 12-bar blues song form.** Named as worth heavy focus specifically for anyone wanting to play blues. → [[Blues Form and Harmony]].
- **Step 32 — Syncopation**: emphasizing off-beat notes to make a line more exciting. → [[Syncopation and Metric Displacement]].
- **Step 33 — Fret more efficiently** (a return to/deepening of Level 2's finger-roll work at speed).
- **Step 34 — Pluck faster.** Named plainly: "which is where you pluck, but you do it... faster." Treated as a distinct trainable skill, not a byproduct of other practice.

**Songs to test the level:** "Dreams" (Fleetwood Mac), "Feel Good Inc" (Gorillaz — the vault already has a full bass breakdown of this exact song, see [[🎵 GORILLAZ - Feel Good Inc]]), "Don't Stop Believing" (Journey).

### Level 4 — Jam-ready basslines and rhythm (steps 35–41, practiced against songs)

Framed as the level that unlocks **jamming with other people confidently** — coming up with interesting basslines on the fly, and having the repertoire range to handle "most songs people would throw at you in real life."

- **Step 35 — Time signatures beyond 4/4**: specifically 3/4, 6/8, and 12/8.
- **Step 36 — Play and count triplets.**
- **Step 37 — Play a shuffle using swing eighth notes.** Named as the second essential blues-specific skill (after Step 31's 12-bar form) for anyone chasing blues playing.
- **Step 38 — The Ultimate Groove Workout** (credited to Victor Wooten): play along with a drum beat, then gradually replace the drum track with silence while continuing to keep time entirely on your own — the stated diagnostic purpose is that without a drill like this, "you'll never be sure if you can actually keep time without a metronome." → new standalone note: [[Ultimate Groove Workout — Fade the Beat]].
- **Step 39 — Roots, fifths, and octaves**: the absolute-basics vocabulary for constructing a bassline on the fly.
- **Step 40 — Walk-ups and walk-downs**, to make basslines more melodically interesting/flavorful. → [[Chromatic Bass Lines]] documents the closely related chromatic line-cliché family.
- **Step 41 — Utility scales**: major pentatonic, minor pentatonic, and the blues scale, named as covering the large majority of practical bassline-building situations. → [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]], [[Bass Scale Shapes — Major, Minor, Pentatonic & Blues]].

**Named milestone:** completing through Level 4 is called out directly as "how far my Beginner to Badass course takes you," and as the point at which "thousands of people have found this to be enough knowledge to play casual gigs, perform in the band at church" — i.e. Level 4 is presented as a real, sufficient stopping point for most working amateur bassists, not merely a waypoint to Level 5.

### Level 5 — Advanced technique, harmony, and stylistic tools (steps 42–55)

Framed as a genuinely long-haul level — "at least a year," with some sub-skills (rhythm feel, muting, note length specifically) named as a **lifelong** pursuit rather than something that finishes.

- **Step 42 — Memorize every note on the fretboard.** Directly names the mental map goal: "you should have a map just like this inside your brain." → [[Bass Fretboard Note Memorization System]] is the vault's full five-step system for exactly this.
- **Step 43 — Basic sixteenth notes**, subdividing the beat into four.
- **Step 44 — Syncopated sixteenth notes.** Named as essential across rock, pop, and metal, and explicitly **essential, not optional, for funk** — the video's own before/after example turns a flat, straight line "wrong" until it's played with the syncopated-sixteenths feel.
- **Step 45 — Advanced muting and note-length control.** Framed with unusual weight for a single roadmap bullet: **"this is 90% of what pros focus on in a recording session"** — clean starts/stops and dead silence between notes matters most specifically for recording work, and "noobs think this is easy." → [[Bass Note Muting Technique]].
- **Step 46 — Seventh chords.** Named as used "to some degree" in basically every style, and as the absolute harmonic floor for anyone wanting to play jazz. → [[Seventh Chords]].
- **Step 47 — The modes** (explicitly flagged optional, "depending on how hard you want to nerd out"). Payoff named directly: modes explain *why* chords are built and function the way they do, beyond just naming them. → [[Diatonic Modes]].
- **Step 48 — Chart-reading skills beyond sheet music and tab**: informal chord/lyric charts and Nashville-number-style scribbled charts, where a bar's worth of beats is marked with chord names for the player to improvise a bassline against. Named limitation: this notation doesn't convey rhythm/exact timing, so it only works if the player already roughly knows the song.
- **Step 49 — The pick.** Sequencing advice given directly: players chasing rock/punk specifically might want to introduce the pick as early as Level 2 or 3, but the video's general recommendation is to **get solid on fingerstyle first** — "it's easier to learn early than later, and learning both simultaneously is a mess waiting to happen." → [[Pick vs. Fingers on Bass — Tone and Technique Tradeoffs]].
- **Step 50 — Slap.** Explicitly downgraded from "vital": "it's not really on this list... it's not used on 99% of gigs, so it's not a vital part of being a solid bassist — but it is really really fun." Positioned as a stylistic specialty to add once the core levels are solid, not a core-path skill. → [[Slap Thumb Technique — Angle, Anchoring, and Muting]].
- **Step 51 — Ghost notes**: plucking a muted string to get a percussive thump rather than a pitched note, adding texture/rhythmic interest to a line without adding new pitches.
- **Step 52 — Hammer-ons and pull-offs**, for more ways to articulate a line without re-plucking every note. → [[Hammer-Ons and Pull-Offs (Bass)]].
- **Step 53 — More scale fluency**: playing the same scale (e.g. major) in multiple different shapes/positions on the neck, not just one memorized box shape.
- **Step 54 — One-string scales**: playing a full scale confined to a single string.
- **Step 55 — Scales across multiple octaves.**

**The load-bearing closing move:** Level 5's full 42–55 list is deliberately given "rapid-fire" and largely undetailed on purpose — the video frames it as a checklist for **tier-recognition**, so a player (or their teacher) can spot when Level-4-or-below material is being crowded out by Level-5 topics that don't serve the player's actual current goal. This is the exact same diagnostic [[The Five-Tier Bass Theory Curriculum]] documents from a sibling BassBuzz video, just applied to the *full skill curriculum* here rather than to theory specifically — see Related below for how the two five-level frameworks map onto each other.

## Not routed (and why)

No production/mixing/plugin content anywhere in the video — pure instrument-curriculum sequencing, so Steps 4 (plugin notes), 6 (wishlist), and 7 (chains) of the enrichment workflow have nothing to do here. Most of the 55 steps are one-line pointers to topics the video explicitly says it isn't teaching in depth ("this would be like a 30-hour video") — the vault already has dedicated, deeper technique/theory notes for the large majority of them (linked inline above); this page and the Related list above are the connective index between the roadmap and that existing material rather than a source of new settings/exercises for each individual step.

# Resolving Seven Common Guitar-Practice Debates

Brandon D'Eon's resolved positions on seven commonly-contradicted pieces of guitar advice — mostly "both sides are right depending on your goal" rather than a single winner, but concrete enough to act on.

1. **Scales needed for soloing?** Goal-dependent. Simple fills/riffs with a strong ear → not strictly required. Full-fretboard, any-key improvisation → required.
2. **Clean vs. distorted practice tone.** Split by hand: **clean tone for left-hand technique** (legato, trills — distortion masks sloppiness a clean signal exposes). **Distorted tone for right-hand technique** (alternate/sweep picking — distortion amplifies muting and noise mistakes far more harshly than clean).
3. **Slow-gradual metronome climbs vs. short fast speed bursts.** Use both, in sequence: push straight to a target tempo beyond your current plateau to break it (recruits different hand/forearm muscle patterns a comfortable tempo never activates), then grind for accuracy at that new tempo until it's clean.
4. **Learn technique through songs vs. dedicated exercises.** Technique-dependent, judged by how musical isolated reps can be. **Bending** → songs (few bending exercises are musical enough to sustain engagement). **Sweep picking** → dedicated exercises (too many specific failure modes — ringing notes, pick angle, muting, spacing, hand-sync — for incidental song-learning to catch reliably).
5. **CAGED system: tool or crutch?** His position: mostly a crutch beyond beginner level — it teaches memorized shapes rather than the underlying notes/scale-degree construction, and breaks down for voicings/positions outside the five shapes (worked example: a given Phrygian position ambiguously "fits" two different CAGED shapes at once). Useful for beginners specifically; the alternative is learning notes/construction directly. *(Note: this vault's own material — [[CAGE System Scale Shapes]], [[Positional CAGED Soloing with Matched Modes and Arpeggios]] — uses CAGED as a practical organizing tool; this is a real pedagogical disagreement worth knowing about, not an error to reconcile.)*
6. **Does theory help or hurt creativity?** Helps, with the same goal-dependent carve-out as #1 — pure-entertainment/song-playing goals don't strictly need it. "Theory kills creativity" is treated as usually post-hoc justification rather than a real trade-off.
7. **What does "phrasing" mean? (Definitional, not a real disagreement.)** Two incompatible definitions circulate: **content** (which notes/phrases you choose) vs. **delivery** (dynamics/timing/articulation of the same notes — like saying the same sentence several different ways). Worth clarifying explicitly whenever the word shows up in feedback, since the two definitions imply different practice actions.

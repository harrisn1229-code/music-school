## Techniques

- **Slice mode setup for one-shot-per-key mapping: transient algorithm + Gate trigger + lowered transient-detector sensitivity** — load a multi-hit sample (here, eight impact sounds in one clip) into [[Simpler]], switch Playback Mode to **Slice**, leave the slicing algorithm on **Transient** (places slice boundaries at the audio's amplitude peaks — first slice on MIDI note C1, subsequent slices mapped chromatically upward), set **Trigger Mode to Gate** so playback stops the instant the note releases (instead of ringing out), then turn **down the transient-detector sensitivity** until each individual hit lands on its own distinct MIDI note rather than several hits sharing one slice. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Snare Design]]
- **Texture mode's "double the length" button as the fastest way to hear granular processing engage** — with Warp mode set to **Texture** and **flux** turned all the way down, the segment-length-doubling button (halves playback speed each click while Warp stays on) demonstrates the transition from ordinary time-stretch into audible granular behavior in real time — clicking it twice compounds the effect further. → [[Granular Synthesis]], [[Simpler]] (Warp ③)
- **Grain size + flux as a joint control pair, plus transpose as a third lever** — **grain size** sets each grain's length (smaller = shorter grains, larger = longer grains); **flux** adds randomness to grain length, and higher flux values read as a "stuttery" characteristic; using grain size and flux together (rather than either alone) opens up a much wider result space, and **transposing** the warped audio on top of that is described as adding still more variation. → [[Granular Synthesis]] (documents the same grain-size/flux pair from the later Texture-mode-focused video; this is the same control pair, general-overview framing)
- **Modulate Texture-mode pitch with Simpler's built-in LFO or its pitch envelope, not just static transpose** — beyond manually transposing the warped clip, Simpler's own **LFO** or its **pitch envelope** (in the Controls tab) can modulate pitch on top of an already-texture-warped sample for continuously evolving results, rather than a fixed transposition. *(stated as a suggestion to explore further, not a specific numeric recipe)* → [[Granular Synthesis]], [[Simpler]]
- **Tones mode = Texture mode without flux, and its grain size is signal-dependent, not a fixed static value** — Tones mode drops the flux parameter entirely (no random per-grain jitter), and unlike Texture mode's grain-size knob (a static, user-set value), Tones mode derives grain size from the source signal itself — the two modes sound audibly different even on the same source for that reason, not just because flux is missing. → [[Granular Synthesis]]
- **Beats mode's "preserve" parameter: loop each transient in place, or loop against a fixed beat division (BPM-dependent)** — with Warp mode set to **Beats**, the **Preserve** parameter has two behavior families: set to **Transients**, Simpler plays and loops each detected transient in place until the next transient arrives, repeating that process through the whole sample; set to a **beat division** (quarter, eighth, 16th, 32nd note, etc.), Simpler instead loops the current audio snippet until the next beat division boundary occurs, regardless of where the actual transients fall — and because beat divisions are tempo-relative, this behavior is **BPM-dependent**, so the same setting sounds different at a different project tempo. Finer divisions (16th/32nd) produce denser, more granular-sounding results than quarter/eighth. → [[Granular Synthesis]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Beats mode's Loop parameter — three distinct per-segment looping behaviors** — governs how each segment (between transients, or between beat-division boundaries) loops internally: **Loop Back and Forth** (the default) plays a segment forward to its end, reverses back through it to a zero-crossing near the segment's middle, then plays forward again, repeating that back-and-forth cycle until the next transient/beat boundary arrives; **Loop Forward** loops the same segment but only ever plays forward (no reverse pass); **Loop Off** plays the segment once to its end and then goes silent for whatever time remains before the next boundary, rather than looping/repeating it. → [[Granular Synthesis]]
- **Beats mode's Envelope slider = a per-segment volume fade, combinable with beat division for rhythmic/glitchy results** — the **Envelope** slider applies a volume fade across each segment of audio between transients/beat boundaries: **100 = no fade** (segment plays at full level throughout), **0 = an almost instantaneous decay** (segment drops to silence almost immediately after triggering). Dialing this slider together with different beat-division settings is called out specifically as a route to rhythmic, glitchy textures — a low Envelope value on a fine beat division (16th/32nd) produces short, percussive stutters rather than a sustained loop. Transposing on top adds further variation. → [[Granular Synthesis]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Complex and Complex Pro modes sound qualitatively different from the rest, and pair especially well with Simpler's pitch envelope** — before demoing Complex/Complex Pro, revert the audio sample's warped length back to its original (un-stretched) length first — these two modes behave quite differently from Texture/Tones/Beats/Re-Pitch (phase-vocoder-style character rather than grain-based), and Simpler's built-in **pitch envelope** is called out as especially effective for shaping them further. → [[Granular Synthesis]]
- **Complex Pro's two extra sliders — Formant and Envelope — normally exist to preserve intelligibility when transposing, but can be pushed for effect instead** — Complex Pro is "very similar to Complex mode except it provides additional control via two sliders": the **Formant** slider adjusts how much the sample's formants are compensated for when the sample is transposed, and the **Envelope** slider influences the sample's spectral characteristics. In conventional use these exist to keep a transposed voice/instrument intelligible (formant-corrected pitch shifting); pushed deliberately away from "correct," they become a sound-design tool instead of a correction tool. → [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Simpler]]
- **Re-Pitch mode = the classic sampler time-stretch: pitch and time are locked together, on purpose** — Re-Pitch mode raises or lowers the sample's pitch by whatever amount is needed to produce the designated amount of time-stretching — i.e. it does *not* decouple pitch from time the way the other warp modes do; this is explicitly named as "how traditional samplers worked back in the day," useful when that locked-together, old-school-sampler character is the desired sound rather than a flaw to route around. → [[Granular Synthesis]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Region-based slicing at the maximum 64 regions, on musical/vocal content, as a granular-pad source distinct from transient slicing** — set Simpler's slicing algorithm to **Region** instead of Transient — this divides the sample into separate regions of **equal length** rather than at detected transients — and set the **Regions** parameter to its maximum value of **64** for the finest possible equal-length slicing; audition individual 64ths of the source (here, a vocal/harmonic song snippet) for one that sounds interesting on its own, then treat that single slice as the seed material for further Texture-mode warping. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]
- **A song-to-pad recipe: one Region slice → Texture-warp/stretch it out → large spacious reverb** — having isolated one promising 64th-region slice of a musical source, switch that slice's Warp mode to **Texture** and stretch it out (segment-BPM manipulation, as earlier in the video) to turn the short fragment into an evolving pad, then send it through a **large, spacious reverb** — described as sounding noticeably better with the reverb added than the dry stretched result alone. A fast, repeatable way to mine an ambient texture/pad out of any song's vocal or harmonic content. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Granular Synthesis]]

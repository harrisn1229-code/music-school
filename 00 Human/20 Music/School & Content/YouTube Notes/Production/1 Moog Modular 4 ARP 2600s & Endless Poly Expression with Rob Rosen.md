# 1 Moog Modular, 4 ARP 2600s & Endless Poly Expression with Rob Rosen

## Synopsis

A companion session to [[The future of EXPRESSIVE analog synths is HERE]] — same core rig (Josh Mattoff on Continuum/Osmose, four ARP 2600s + a Moog Modular 3C, one voice each), this time with Rob Rosen trying the Continuum for the first time and additional techniques not covered in the first session: a second CV destination on the same trigger signal, a continuous sustain-pedal swell, the Continuum's pitch-quantization "rounding" control, and an MPE arpeggiator that cycles through multiple physical synths per step.

## Techniques

- **Voice-priority allocation**: voices assign in a fixed low-priority order — voice 1 = Moog 3C, voice 2 = lower ARP, voice 3 = upper ARP, and so on.
- **Multing one trigger signal to two destinations**: the same envelope/gate signal that fires the amp envelope also feeds pulse width, so a harder touch simultaneously changes loudness/brightness AND waveform shape — compounding the expressive effect of one gesture.
- **A continuous sustain pedal**: with 0-127 range grabbing one specific held voice, lifting the foot gently (not fully) and pressing back down continuously sweeps that voice's filter/VCA in real time — a live-played swell on a held drone note.
- **Pitch quantization ("rounding") on the Continuum**: a single control dialing between fully continuous/fretless pitch bend and hard, quantized notes — turned all the way up it plays like a normal keyboard, backed off it restores full glissando behavior.
- **An MPE arpeggiator that cycles through multiple physical synths, not just notes on one instrument**: each step can fire a genuinely different hardware voice (Moog, then ARP 1, then ARP 2...), with per-note continuous expression (aftertouch octave jumps, fine pitch bend down to a quarter-semitone) still live while it runs — plus a real gotcha where a touch light enough to be inaudible can still silently trigger/advance a step.
- **Live tuning drift is real even on a well-maintained rig** — one oscillator drifted noticeably after roughly two days of stable use, requiring an on-the-fly retune mid-session.
- **Deliberately leaving resonance off on some voices while on for others** in a multi-instrument ensemble patch adds more perceived "personality" to a combined chord than uniform settings across all voices — same heterogeneity principle as assigning different physical units to different chord notes.
- **A tension/resolution framing for resonance and pitch imperfection**: a near-miss in pitch or timing that then gets corrected reads as MORE satisfying once resonance is added, likened to how a string player actively lands and corrects pitch by ear rather than starting perfectly in tune.

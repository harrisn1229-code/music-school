## Synopsis

BERNTH connects three "exotic" scales as one-note-at-a-time relatives, rather than three separate things to memorize: natural minor → harmonic minor (raise the 7th) → Phrygian dominant (harmonic minor's own 5th mode, same notes, different root — not a new scale) → Hungarian minor (harmonic minor with the 4th ALSO raised, a genuinely new/fourth note change). All three scales are already documented in the vault's [[Scale Modes Reference]] and [[Harmonic Minor Modes]]; this video's contribution is the practical one-note-change learning path connecting them.

## What's taught

- **Harmonic minor, derived as "natural minor with one note raised."** From A natural minor, raise the 7th (G→G♯) to get A harmonic minor. Stated as the fastest route to learning the scale across the neck: since only one note changes, an existing natural-minor fingering needs only that single adjustment, not a from-scratch new shape.
- **Phrygian dominant is NOT a separate scale to memorize — it's harmonic minor's own 5th mode, same pitch collection, different root.** Two equivalent ways to find it, both given: (1) as "the Phrygian scale with a major 3rd instead of minor" (E Phrygian's E-F-G-A-B-C-D becomes E-F-G♯-A-B-C-D); (2) as harmonic minor starting from its 5th degree (playing A harmonic minor starting on E gives exactly Phrygian dominant — the "5th mode of harmonic minor" relationship). Practical payoff stated directly: a player who already knows A harmonic minor across the neck can play E Phrygian dominant over an E-rooted backing WITHOUT learning any new fingering at all — just start/end phrases around the E root inside the same A harmonic minor shape already known. Named uses: modern metal and jazz, especially as the color over a minor key's dominant V7 (matches [[Harmonic Minor Modes]]'s existing borrowed-V entry).
- **Hungarian minor — a genuinely new (not modal-relative) 4th scale, built by raising harmonic minor's 4th degree as well.** From A harmonic minor (1 2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 7), raise the 4th (D→D♯) to get Hungarian minor (1 2 ♭3 ♯4 5 ♭6 7) — described as "the even darker, more mysterious cousin" of harmonic minor, with the added ♯4 creating extra dissonance against the ♭3. Unlike Phrygian dominant, this is NOT a mode of harmonic minor (different formula, not a relabeling of the same notes) — a genuine 4th color, reached by the same one-note-change learning method.
- **General practice recommendation:** don't just memorize finger positions for each of the three scales — understand and drill the relationship between them (which single note changes, and why), then apply the "octave trick" (relocate the same fingering to any root) to move each scale to other keys quickly.

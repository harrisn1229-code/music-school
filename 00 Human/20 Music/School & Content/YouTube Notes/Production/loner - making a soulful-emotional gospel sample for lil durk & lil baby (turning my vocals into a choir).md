# making a soulful/emotional gospel sample for lil durk & lil baby (turning my vocals into a choir)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nHBEE-uvq4M)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nHBEE-uvq4M)

## Synopsis

A from-scratch build in loner's recurring "recreate a hitmaker's process" catalog, this one framed as a Lil Durk/Lil Baby-style gospel-emotional sample rather than a reverse-engineer of a specific record. The build order is unusually clean and worth following as a template on its own: chords first (a simple, widely-used progression, voiced wider with one octave-displacement trick) → a two-piano-plus-Rhodes bed processed individually → the video's headline trick, a **4-layer self-recorded vocal choir** (main/low/high/mid-tone), each layer given a distinct pitch/formant/reverb treatment and bounced to one stem → a second melodic piano pass with reverb/tape processing → a B3 organ doing connective fills → the whole sample bounced into Fruity Slicer and turned into a beat with 808/hi-hat tuning and a tempo bump to 138. The vocal-stacking segment is the most valuable and most reusable part of the video — it's a compact, fully-specified recipe for turning one voice into a "choir" using only pitch/formant shifting and differentiated reverb, no external singers required, and it maps directly onto the vault's existing [[2 Ensemble Stack Group|Ensemble Stack Group]] "Wide Ensemble / Choir" variation. Auto-captions are moderately garbled on plugin names (a recurring pattern for this channel) — "Altar Boy" is a confident homophone match for [[Little AlterBoy]] (established elsewhere on this channel), "Reels" and "J37" match previously-logged AudioThing Reels / Waves J37 mentions, but "Loophole 3" (the second piano instrument, "Homes Piano" preset) does not match any plugin name in the vault or a recognizable product name — flagged low-confidence and left unresolved rather than guessed.

## Techniques

### Chords
- **Progression: vi–I–iii (repeated), then vii–I–iii–V** — stated explicitly as "pretty simple, pretty commonly used," ending the phrase on the V "which is the best way to end your chord progression." *(genre-specific — no exact key given)* → [[Chord Progressions]]
- **Widen a chord voicing by taking the MIDDLE note of each triad and moving it up an octave, leaving root and top note in place** — makes an otherwise simple triad "look a lot more complex" and read as a wider, more open voicing without changing the underlying harmony. Routed to the reusable voicing-technique home: [[Chords]]. → [[Chords]], [[Chord Progressions]]

### Chord-bed processing (piano + Rhodes)
- **EQ → tape ("Reels," bringing down hiss and turning down harshness for "a subtle tape effect") → light spring reverb → Sketch Cassette (pushed for a "really distorted and vintage" character)** — a 4-stage color chain applied early, before the instrument layering below. → [[Kramer Tape]], [[Cassette]], [[Reverb]]
- **Layer a piano under a Rhodes (Lounge Lizard) for extra depth** — bring the piano's top notes down and transpose the Rhodes up a few semitones (settled on +4) so the two don't collide in register. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Swap the main piano to a free Kontakt library patch ("Rutvik Upright," from Piano Book) and filter out a lot of its low end** specifically because it was clashing with the Rhodes underneath it — a register-carving move, not a tone complaint. → [[Kontakt 7]], [[EQ]]
- **J37 tape stage added to the Rhodes specifically** (a separate tape instance from the earlier "Reels" pass on the full chord bed) — a second, more targeted tape-coloration layer. → [[Kramer Tape]]
- **Delay added to the piano, deliberately to push it back behind the Rhodes** — the piano was originally meant to be the lead instrument, but the delay is used to "kind of drown it out" so the Rhodes takes over as the main sound; a delay-as-demotion move rather than a straightforward space effect. → [[Delay]]

### Vocal choir build (the headline technique)
- **Four distinct vocal layers recorded to fake a choir from one voice: Main, Low, High, and a fourth "middle range, different tone" layer** — stacking all four together is explicitly what reads as "a bunch of people singing." This is a from-scratch instance of the vault's own [[2 Ensemble Stack Group|Ensemble Stack Group]] "Wide Ensemble / Choir" variation (Imogen Heap / HAIM / gospel self-stacks), run with only pitch/formant + reverb differentiation rather than the full tune/align/glue chain documented there. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[2 Ensemble Stack Group|Ensemble Stack Group]]
- **Main layer: [[Little AlterBoy]] with Formant brought down "very slightly," plus a very short ambient reverb.** The lightest-touch layer, closest to a natural voice.
- **Second layer: pitch up a full octave via [[Little AlterBoy]], Formant brought down slightly, same reverb preset as the main layer.** Paired with the main layer's formant-down direction so the two blend into what the video calls a fused pair ("together those two sound like—").
- **Third layer: the octave-up + formant-down combination reads "a lot more open and high pitched," so it gets a longer, heavier reverb than the first two** — reverb length scaled to how exposed/high the layer sits, not a uniform send.
- **Fourth (low) layer: EQ first to cut low end that was "hitting really low"; then [[Decapitator]] on one of its vocal presets specifically for low-end crunch; then a heavy reverb** — the only layer where saturation is deliberately added, matched to its job of carrying weight/grit rather than pitch.
- **Bounce all four processed layers to one stem before continuing the build** — simplifies the rest of the arrangement and stops the 4-way stack from needing to be re-balanced every time something else changes.

### Second piano pass + organ
- **A second, purely melodic piano part ("Loophole 3" instrument, unresolved — preset name "Homes Piano") layered on top of the vocal-choir stem, run through a heavy reverb, then a "Reels" tape instance set to the "Excited Tape" preset.** *(instrument name low-confidence — auto-captions render it "Loophole 3," which doesn't match any known plugin; treat as an unidentified piano source rather than guessing a real product.)* → [[Kramer Tape]]
- **Octave-jump a repeating melodic phrase mid-part: first half of the line played an octave higher, second half dropped an octave lower** — described as not his normal move, kept because it "sounded really cool" in this specific build. A cheap way to add motion/contrast to a simple repeated melody without changing the notes. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **B3 organ doing short connective rolls that lead into the next chord change** — a transitional-fill role rather than a sustained pad; genre-specific gospel color. *(vendor unresolved — see Plugin Wishlist.)*

### Beat / drums
- **808 tuned up two semitones because its low note (an A) was reading too low against the chords** — a tuning fix caught by ear during the beat pass, not decided in advance. → [[Bass Design]]
- **Hi-hats shifted back (late) specifically to stop them clashing with the 808/kick** — framed as the main reason this producer habitually shifts hats at all: "the frequencies with the 808s and the kicks and the hi-hats tend to clash if you don't shift the hi-hat back slightly." A masking-driven microtiming move (transient/frequency collision), distinct from the feel-driven swing/pocket shifts documented elsewhere on this channel. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]]
- **Tempo pushed up to ~138 BPM** as the last step before wrapping the beat. *(genre-specific tempo choice — no reasoning given.)*
- **Whole processed sample bounced to one stem, then loaded into Fruity Slicer** to build the beat/drum pattern around it.

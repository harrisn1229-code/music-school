## Synopsis

Five pentatonic-spicing tricks packed into a single worked lick over the standard A minor pentatonic box, dissected trick by trick. Several overlap with content already logged elsewhere in this batch (odd-grouping/economy-picking, extended 3-note-per-string pentatonic, Dorian-in-pentatonic) — this page notes the overlaps and routes only what's genuinely new.

## What's taught

- **Trick 1 — group 16th notes in 5s (not quintuplets), combined with economy picking to accent each group.** Clarifies a common misconception directly: grouping plain 16th notes into 5s is NOT the same as playing 16th-note quintuplets (quintuplets fit 5 notes in the space of 1 beat; this is regular 16th-note subdivision, just grouped/accented in 5s across the bar rather than the usual 4s). Economy picking (two downstrokes in a row at the group boundary, rather than strict alternation) both accents the first note of each 5-group for time-keeping and eases the string transition. (Closely related to this batch's own [[Syncopation and Metric Displacement]] bullet on 5s/7s grouping + the same economy-picking consequence, from a different BERNTH video — the quintuplet-vs-grouped-16ths distinction is the one point not already logged there.)
- **Trick 2 — an octave-arpeggio shape combining tapping with hammer-ons/pull-offs**, built from the pentatonic box: fret a low chord tone, tap the perfect 5th with the picking hand, then walk back down via pull-offs — moved through successive octaves using the same shape. Flagged as physically demanding (strong fretting-hand hammer-ons/pull-offs plus stretches, with a premium on clean string transitions/no unwanted noise).
- **Trick 3 — extend 2-note-per-string pentatonic to 3-note-per-string, including a deliberate note repetition and a blue note.** Adds a repeated note within one string's group (audibly unusual at speed) and inserts the blue note (♭5) for extra color — the same "extended pentatonic" family of tricks already logged from this batch's [[6 Life-Changing Guitar Music Theory HACKS!]] and [[5 EASY METAL GUITAR SOLO TRICKS (that sound advanced!)]], this time with the blue-note addition specifically.
- **Trick 4 — Dorian-in-pentatonic: swap the ♭7 for the major 6th (F♯) inside an A minor pentatonic box** to close a phrase with a modal-flavored voicing. Matches this batch's already-logged confirmations of the same swap in [[Dorian from the Minor Pentatonic Box]] — not re-logged again here to avoid a fourth duplicate entry.
- **Trick 5 — find an arpeggio shape hidden inside the pentatonic box for sweep picking**, using a ring-finger-then-index-finger roll to keep the swept notes individually audible rather than blurring into a rake. A variant finger-rolling technique alongside [[Sweep Picking Arpeggio Shapes]]'s existing middle-finger-roll method for the same separation problem.

No production/gear content.

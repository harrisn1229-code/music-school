# 5 Lines To Get You Playing The Diminished Scale

## Synopsis
Jack Ruch teaches the half–whole (dominant/"double") diminished scale through five worked guitar lick examples, all played over the same **Dm7 → G13♭9 → Cmaj7** (a 251 in C major) so the scale's function stays fixed while the licks vary — a deliberate teaching choice that lets each example isolate one specific way of drawing on the scale (straight scale run, register shift, an angular scale-fragment position, the "double diminished" two-arpeggios-a-half-step-apart framing, and triads pulled out of the scale) rather than mixing devices. He opens by naming the scale's construction (G half–whole diminished: G-A♭-B♭-B-D♭-D-E-F, alternating half/whole steps starting with a half step), its symmetry (transposable in minor thirds, same note collection every three frets), and its home use — Robin Ford-style, resolving the I chord of a blues into the IV via a 251 — before working through the five licks. Every lick shares the same shape of idea: outline the Dm7 (the ii), use a chromatic enclosure to land precisely on the G7's 3rd (or another chord tone) in time with the chord change, spend a beat or two inside the diminished/altered color, then resolve onto a clean chord tone of the Cmaj7 target exactly as that chord arrives — tension-and-release is stated explicitly as "the whole name of the game." Licks 4 and 5 are the most conceptually dense: lick 4 reframes the scale as two diminished-7 arpeggios a half step apart (the "double diminished" scale) and just alternates between them; lick 5 pulls major triads out of the scale (G major itself, plus B♭, D♭, and E major a minor third apart each, all present in the same eight notes) and weaves between the E major and B♭ major triads for their ♭9/13 and ♯9/♭7/5 colors over G7. The most valuable single idea in the video isn't any one lick but the underlying principle that every lick teaches: land on the diminished/altered color deliberately, but always resolve it onto a chord tone exactly as the chord changes — the licks are just five different doors into that same discipline.

## What's taught
- **The G half–whole (dominant) diminished scale, constructed live: start on the root, then alternate half step / whole step / half step / whole step… all the way up.** Notes: **G, A♭, B♭, B, D♭, D, E, F**, back to G — an 8-note scale (as opposed to the whole–half "fully diminished"/vii° scale used over a diminished chord itself — this is the dominant-function version, played over a **7th** chord, not a °7 chord). (~0:40–1:10) → [[Diminished Chords]], [[Scale Modes Reference]]
- **The scale is symmetrical — the same 8 notes repeat if you start it a minor third (3 frets) higher or lower**, so any shape learned here transposes freely up/down in minor thirds without relearning new notes. (~1:10–1:25) → [[Diminished Chords]]
- **The scale's signature altered-dominant chord: G13♭9**, used as the V of a **Dm7 → G13♭9 → Cmaj7** 251 — all five lick examples are played over exactly this progression, with the half–whole diminished scale played specifically over the G13♭9 bar. (~1:25–1:55) → [[Altered Chords]], [[The ii-V-I]]
- **Home context for the whole scale: getting from the I chord to the IV chord of a blues via a 251**, the same device Ruch teaches elsewhere on his channel (name-checks Robin Ford as a player who uses this scale this way) — play the 251 (ii–V–I of the IV) with the half–whole diminished scale over the V, landing on the IV. (~0:10–0:35, restated ~14:20–14:40) → [[Blues Form and Harmony]], [[The ii-V-I]]
- **Lick 1 — straight scale run.** Outline Dm7 (the ii) from a D minor shape, then a **chromatic enclosure** (a note above the target, then two chromatic notes below it) lands precisely on **B, the 3rd of G7**, exactly in time with the chord change; jump to the 5th (straight out of a G major triad); then descend through the half–whole diminished scale and land on **E, the 3rd of Cmaj7**, again exactly as that chord arrives. Framed as the simplest of the five — pure scale-tone descent with two precisely-timed chord-tone landings bookending it. (~2:00–3:40) → [[Chord-Tone Soloing]], [[Enclosures Through the Scale]]
- **Lick 2 — same shape, lower register.** Identical Dm7 pickup and chromatic enclosure into G7's 3rd, but played down an octave; ascends up through the scale instead of descending, and resolves onto **G, the 5th of Cmaj7**. The point of the register shift: a different spot on the neck opens different fingering/note options within the same scale even though the harmonic content is identical. (~3:45–5:10)
- **Lick 3 — an angular, "stretched-out" position across the fretboard**, using ghost-noted pivot tones for rhythmic texture rather than clean articulation. Descends through the diminished scale using this note-function sequence over the G7: **♭5 → ♯9/♭9 → major 3rd → ♭9 → root → ♭7 → 5th → 6th**, resolving on the **3rd of Cmaj7**. The pivot/ghost notes aren't struck hard — they exist to add rhythmic push through the phrase, not to be heard as clean melodic notes. (~5:15–7:00)
- **Lick 4 — the "double diminished" framing: the half–whole scale IS two diminished-7 arpeggios a half step apart.** Within G half–whole diminished sits a **G diminished-7 arpeggio** and, a half step up, an **A♭ diminished-7 arpeggio** — both move in minor thirds like any dim7 arpeggio. This lick plays the same Dm7 pickup, then goes **up the A♭ dim7 arpeggio, down the G dim7 arpeggio**, resolving onto the **major 7th of Cmaj7**. Named color tones hit along the way: the **♭5** and the **♯11** of G7 — described as "tense, colorful, outside notes." (~7:05–9:15) → [[Diminished Chords]], [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]]
- **Lick 5 — triads pulled directly out of the scale.** Because the scale is symmetric, any triad found in it recurs a minor third higher/lower — the scale's own **G major** triad (root-3-5) means it also contains **B♭ major, D♭ major, and E major** triads, all four spaced a minor third apart. Ruch names the specific colors each contributes over G7: **E major triad → ♭9 and major 13** on G7; **B♭ major triad → ♯9, ♭7, and 5** on G7. The lick: Dm7 pickup starting on E→F, chromatic walk to the 3rd of G7, then the **E major triad** shape, down a half step into the **B♭ major triad** shape, then up a half step to land on the **major 7th of Cmaj7** to resolve. (~9:20–13:30) → [[Triads as a Fretboard Map]], [[Altered Chords]]
- **Practice framing across all five licks: learn them note-for-note first to get the scale under the fingers and internalize the phrasing, then deliberately vary the lines/phrasing once the shapes are comfortable** — the licks are a gateway into the sound, not a fixed vocabulary to reproduce verbatim. Same "learn it, then make it your own" framing Ruch uses elsewhere. (~2:55–3:15, restated ~13:35–13:55) → [[Solo Analysis and Concept Extraction]]

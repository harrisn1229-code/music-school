# Dance Pop (Dua Lipa ↔ Charli XCX)

**What makes it the genre:** pop songwriting welded to club engineering — a four-on-floor (or electro) engine under radio structure, the vocal as a *rhythm instrument*, and production that's danceable at 2 AM but hooky in the car. Two poles: **nu-disco polish** (Dua) and **brat-club abrasion** (Charli); most tracks live on the line between them.

## Pole A — Nu-Disco / Disco-Pop (the Dua lane)

1. **The rhythm section is 1979 with 2020s low end:** four-on-floor kick + real-feel disco groove — octave-bouncing bassline (live-played or [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]]), tight funk guitar chanks ([[1 Electric Guitar DI Track|Electric Guitar DI Track]] clean-funk variation, panned, dry), strings/brass stabs as hooks.
2. **The pocket is played, not just ducked:** the bass *avoids* the kick (offbeat octave pattern) so sidechain stays light ([[Kickstart 2]] shallow) — bounce over pump.
3. **Percussion layers:** open hat on the offbeat (the disco engine), congas/tambourine loops low in the mix, big clap+snare stack ([[2 Drums Hats Group|Drums Hats Group]] doing real work).
4. **Vocal:** [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]] — smooth opto power, low-mids soothed, rhythmic consonants, chorus stacks hard-panned. Everything serves the groove grid.
5. **Arrangement:** pre-chorus *lifts* (filter opens, stack widens), post-chorus instrumental hook (the "Don't Start Now" bass moment), disco strings answering vocal lines.
6. **Mix:** bright, punchy, controlled — [[Modern Pop]] presentation over [[House - EDM]] energy management. Master −8 to −9 LUFS.

## Pole B — Hyperpop / Brat-Club (the Charli lane)

1. **Minimalism + violence:** few elements, each extreme — one distorted bass/synth riff ([[Serum 2]] through [[Decapitator]]), a drum machine pattern, the voice. **A.G. Cook's move: mute things; abrupt full silences are arrangement events.**
2. **The voice is the synth:** [[Charli XCX Vocal Chain]] — retune 0, formant automation, insert distortion, clone stacks. Spoken verses vs hard-tuned hooks = the dynamic.
3. **Drums:** electro/club patterns (not always four-on-floor), clipped hard ([[Gold Clip Track]]), zero room — the "expensive cheap" drum machine aesthetic.
4. **Sound design over chords:** 1–2 chord loops; interest comes from timbre automation (filter, formant, bitcrush [[Decimort 2]]) and structural stunts (beat drops out, pitch bends, tape stops).
5. **Bass and synth share one lane:** the riff IS the bass IS the hook — mono, distorted, sidechained hard when the kick needs room.
6. **Mix:** aggressively loud and dry (−7 to −8 LUFS), top end glassy, transients clipped — polish through abrasion, never through reverb.

## Shared DNA (what to keep constant across both)
- Vocal always danceable: tuned, flattened dynamics, consonants on the grid.
- The **8-bar contract**: something changes every 8 bars (mute, add, filter, stunt) — dance pop dies in static loops.
- Groove test: kick + bass + vocal alone must already move ([[House - EDM]] rule).
- Contrast engineering from [[Modern Pop]]: intimate/dry verse elements vs. huge chorus blooms.

## Arrangement

**Section map** — both poles run pop structure on a 16-bar club grid.

| Section | Bars | Dua lane | Charli lane |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 8–16 | filtered groove, no vocal | the one riff, alone |
| Verse | 16 | bass + guitar chank + drums, vocal dry | riff + drum machine + spoken vocal |
| Pre-chorus | 8 | filter opens, stack widens | *skipped* — or a hard silence |
| Chorus | 16 | full: strings/brass stabs, stacked vocal, open hats | riff distorted harder, cloned vocal stacks |
| Post-chorus | 8 | **instrumental hook** (the *"Don't Start Now"* bass moment) | a stunt: tape stop, pitch drop, total mute |
| Bridge | 8 | strip to bass + vocal | full silence, then everything |

**Entrances & exits**
- **The 8-bar contract** (see Shared DNA): something mutes, adds, filters or breaks every 8 bars. Static loops kill this genre faster than bad sounds.
- Dua lane: the open hat on the offbeat is the engine — when it enters, the section is "on."
- Charli lane: **A.G. Cook's move is muting.** An abrupt full silence is a real arrangement event, used where the Dua lane would put a riser.
- The post-chorus instrumental hook is close to mandatory in the disco lane, and it's where the bassline gets to be the lead.

**The contrast move — the filter, or the silence.** Two poles, two mechanisms. Nu-disco opens a filter and widens a stack into the chorus. Brat-club cuts to nothing and slams back. Both are the same idea: the section before the payoff has to be *less*.

**Density rule: Dua 6–7, Charli 2–4.** That gap is the clearest measurable difference between the poles. If a "brat" track has seven things going, it's a disco-pop record wearing distortion.

## Session recipes
- **Disco-pop:** 110–124 BPM · start with the *bassline + kick* (not chords) · cut the funk guitar early (it defines the pocket) · vocal rhythm written against the bass.
- **Brat-club:** 120–140 BPM · start with ONE sound (distorted riff or drum loop) + vocal immediately · resist adding — automate and mute instead · save one stunt (silence, pitch-drop, self-oscillating delay) per section.

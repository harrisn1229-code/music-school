A short Lennon fragment in the **Abbey Road medley**, segueing directly into Polythene Pam. Character-sketch lyric, punchy and brief — an example of the medley's 'fragments as movements' approach.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Mean Mr Mustard]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #186.*

**Form.** A deliberate fragment — intro plus two verses, no chorus, no ending — and his argument is that this is form serving function: after two substantial self-contained songs the medley needs its pace back, so this one couples tightly at both ends and gets out. Even at throwaway length it finds room for a **14-bar verse structured 4 + 6 + 4**: the first phrase composed straight through over a sequenced motif, the middle phrase tiled A–B–A, the last tiled C–C. Verse′ is musically identical apart from a meter shift in its third phrase. There is barely an intro — two beats of snare with a grace-note roll, borrowed from the back half of the last bar of "Sun King," so the two tracks join without a gap.

**Harmony.** Four chords only: `I (4 bars) · V – ♭VII – V (6 bars) · I – ♭VI – V (×2)`. Both ♭VI and ♭VII are borrowed from the parallel natural minor ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). In the middle phrase the bass makes a syncopated chromatic climb and descent — B–C–C♯–D up, D–C♯–C–B back — that lands the D and B chords **just before their downbeats**, spotlighting the cross relations rather than smoothing them; the third phrase pushes the same idea further with ♭VI. The exit is the tightest transition on the album: the chromatic bass lick lands on D, which becomes the ♭VII of the double plagal opening "Polythene Pam."

**Notable.** The tempo is essentially identical to "Sun King" and you cannot hear it, because the backbeat changes from lazy flow to a lumbering march. In verse′ the eighth-note pulse is held constant while the meter shifts from 4/4 to 6/8 for the final four bars, so chords now change every three eighths instead of four ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]) — a local acceleration that becomes the new half-bar for the following track. The melody is almost entirely one motif, a step down followed by a third down ([[MEL-C03 — Motifs and how to develop them]]), sequenced downward and then relaunched from higher scale positions.

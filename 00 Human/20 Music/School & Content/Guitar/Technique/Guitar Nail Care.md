# Guitar Nail Care

Maintenance for players who use their right-hand nails for tone — classical/nylon-string and fingerstyle playing — where a cracked or chipped nail mid-project is a real problem, not just cosmetic.

## Fixing a chip or crack without wrecking the nail

- **Daily use is what prevents chips in the first place.** Since starting a strict daily-practice rebuild at the beginning of the year, Jared reports **zero nail issues** — the mechanism, per him, is simply playing (and polishing) every single day rather than intermittently; he keeps both a classical and an electric guitar within reach so both hands stay in daily use, and consistent wear avoids the small imperfections that build up when nails go days between sessions. Superglue-the-dead-part remains the fallback for when a chip does happen. — Jared, [[How I Organize My Guitar Practice (So I Never Want to Stop)]]
- **Superglue only the dead part, never the flesh.** The white free edge of the nail is dead tissue; only glue that part — never let glue touch the live nail bed/cuticle underneath, which is the actual health risk people warn about with nail products. — Jared, [[This might ruin my channel Practice Log No. 1|Practice Log #1]]
- **Only works on a partial crack.** The technique only holds a chip/split that hasn't fully broken off — a beginning slice or an overlapping crack you could still peel back, not a nail that's already snapped clean through. — Jared, [[This might ruin my channel Practice Log No. 1|Practice Log #1]]
- **Buff with 500-grit classical guitar nail paper.** After the glue sets, polish the repaired tip with nail paper at 500 grit (sold specifically as classical-guitar nail paper) — smooths the glue bump, and the reinforced tip ends up sounding thicker/fuller than the bare nail, not just repaired. — Jared, [[This might ruin my channel Practice Log No. 1|Practice Log #1]]
- **Nightly cuticle oil for the *next* nail, not the current one.** "Healthy Hoof" cuticle product, worked in around the cuticle every night before bed — the logic is it feeds the nail bed so nail that's still growing in comes out stronger; it takes weeks to show since it's not affecting the nail you already have. Unverified/anecdotal (Jared calls it possibly "snake oil" but is trying it). — Jared, [[This might ruin my channel Practice Log No. 1|Practice Log #1]]

## Why this matters mid-project

Jared's context: he plays without nails for long stretches (weak/floppy nails historically), but wants full natural nail tone for nylon-string/fingerpicking work and can't afford to lose 1-2 weeks waiting for a nail to regrow when he's mid-recording. The glue-and-buff fix bridges that gap without the long-term nail damage that comes from more aggressive nail products (acrylics, constant gluing over live tissue).

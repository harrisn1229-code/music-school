# The ONLY Modular Oberheim 8 Voice w/Rob Rosen-Clip

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AWi0m9crnJM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AWi0m9crnJM)

## Synopsis

Gear-history documentary, not a technique video — synth tech Rob Rosen tours a one-of-a-kind custom 8-voice modular Oberheim SEM system, hand-built in the 1970s by synthesist Casey Young. No usable production/synthesis technique to extract; the value here is design-credit and construction trivia around the Oberheim SEM module, honestly reflected in minimal routing.

## Techniques

Not applicable in the usual sense — this is a tour of a unique historical instrument, not an instructional video. What's documented for the record:
- **The rig**: 8 Oberheim SEM (Synthesizer Expander Module) units plus a "Digitune" tuning module, an 8-voice programmer module, two dual sequencers, keyboard-to-SEM gate/CV level-shifting circuitry, an MXR mini limiter, a Bi-Phase 2 phaser, and a Surge wave shaper — all interconnected via custom 1/4" patch bays Casey Young built himself, housed in a single wooden cabinet (cabinet woodwork was the one part he didn't build himself).
- **SEM design credits** (the one piece of genuinely reusable gear-history fact): architecture by Tom Oberheim and Jim Cooper; the oscillator section designed by Dave Rossum (later E-mu Systems founder); the filter section designed by Dennis Collins, formerly of ARP — Oberheim brought in outside specialist designers for the SEM's core circuits rather than building every section in-house.
- The system could alternately be controlled via a second, custom-modified Oberheim 8-voice unit outputting CV/gate, or run in parallel with an EMU modular polysynth system on a 4060 keyboard.

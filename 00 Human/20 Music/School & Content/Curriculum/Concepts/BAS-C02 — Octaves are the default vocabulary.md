# BAS-C02 — Octaves are the default vocabulary

**Q.** What's the default move for adding motion to a root-based line, and where do fills come from?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Jump up the octave and back**, occasionally through a passing tone — a 5th above is the classic. A strong bassline can be mainly octaves with the occasional tiny fill. For the fills: **steal the melody's rhythm** — mirror the *rhythm* of what the guitar or melody just played, not necessarily its notes, so the busyness hides inside existing phrasing. Then **record busy and mute down** — play a too-busy line through the whole loop and delete everything but the killer moments, never repeating the same fill in consecutive phrases.

## Why it matters

Octave motion is where a disco or funk line's bounce actually comes from — it mimics how a real player physically moves around the neck. And rhythm-stealing keeps added busyness cohesive instead of arbitrary.

## Drill

Take a root-per-bar line. Add octave jumps only. Then add exactly one fill, borrowed rhythmically from the keys part.

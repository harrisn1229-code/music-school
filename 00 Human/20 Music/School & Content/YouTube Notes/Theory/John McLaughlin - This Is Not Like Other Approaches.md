# John McLaughlin - This Is Not Like Other Approaches

📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rMHtLd692Fw)

## Synopsis

Jens Larsen analyzes John McLaughlin's solo on "Take the Coltrane" (F blues, from the album with Joe DeFrancesco and Elvin Jones), focused on McLaughlin's atypical approach: minimal arpeggio use, heavy chromaticism, and lines that are sometimes deliberately harmony-ambiguous rather than chord-tone-anchored — described as thinking in "a larger picture" (attributed partly to McLaughlin's Indian-classical-music influence) rather than scale-per-chord.

## What's taught

- **Chromatic sliding minor-3rds as a connective device**, used repeatedly across the solo to move between target notes rather than diatonic scale steps — creates deliberate melodic ambiguity/tension independent of the underlying chord.
- **Harmonic "pull-ahead"** — playing content that belongs to the *next* chord before the bar line actually arrives (e.g. treating an F7 bar as already G7, playing a Lydian-dominant-flavored line early) — offered as an alternate analysis to "this note doesn't fit the current chord," reframing an apparently-outside note as an anticipated resolution instead.
- **Triad substitution for a sus sound**: playing an F major triad over a G7 chord, or a B♭ major triad over C7, both effectively spelling the 7sus4 sound of that root without naming it that way.
- **Held outside notes as pure melodic tension, not harmonic**: a note that doesn't belong to the current chord/key (e.g. the ♭6 over an F blues) is picked deliberately and *held* rather than passed through quickly, then resolved later — argued to be a melodic choice (tension-and-resolution shape) rather than an attempt at a "correct" alternate scale.
- **Minimal arpeggio use as a stylistic signature** — most phrases are built from scale fragments, chord-tone landing points, and chromatic connectors rather than full arpeggios; when arpeggios do appear, they're used sparingly and often stacked as triads-over-a-different-root rather than run as lines.
- **Motif displacement within a bar** — restating the same short melodic motif but shifted to start on a different beat than its first statement, creating rhythmic variation from identical melodic material.
- **Tritone-substitution reading of a ii-V**: an F♯7→B7 pair analyzed as the tritone-substitute ii-V resolving to B♭, rather than naming the chords in F.

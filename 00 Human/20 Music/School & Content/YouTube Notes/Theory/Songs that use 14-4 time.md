# Songs that use 14/4 time

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YdRy0ZblGdE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YdRy0ZblGdE)

## Synopsis

Continues the meter series with 14/4 — technically NOT an odd meter (14 divides evenly into 2s), but still unusual in practice because real songs build it from two different smaller meters glued together rather than a uniform pulse, the same "composite meter" category already documented for 10/4 and 6/4.

## Techniques

- **14/4 as composite, not odd, meter**: 14/4, 10/4, and 6/4 all pass the "divides evenly" test that separates them from true odd meters (5/4, 7/4, 11/4), but still sound unusual because real songs alternate between two different smaller meters bar-to-bar rather than a uniform 14-beat feel. Practical transcription usually subdivides the 14 beats rather than notating one giant bar — makes the meter's internal accent structure legible to a performer.
- **4+2+4+4 grouping**: The Strokes' "Two Kinds of Happiness" (first section) and Elliott Smith's "Rose Parade" both group their 14 beats as a bar of 4/4, a bar of 2/4, then two more bars of 4/4 — the 2/4 bar functions as a deliberate "short" bar breaking up an otherwise steady 4/4 feel.
- **3-bars-4/4-then-1-bar-2/4 grouping**: Fort Minor's "Kenji" instead groups its 14 beats as three full bars of 4/4 followed by one bar of 2/4 — the practical effect is a song that sounds like straightforward 4/4 except every fourth bar gets cut in half.
- **Other 14/4 songs cited**: Tame Impala's "One More Year"; the chorus of Yes's "Roundabout"; Pearl Jam's "Get Right"; a Radiohead track transcribed in the video's captions as "Bones" (name uncertain — possible transcript mishearing of a different title; verify if this specific attribution matters).
- **A related but distinct 14/8 (not 14/4) example**: King Gizzard and the Lizard Wizard's "The Fourth Colour" divides its 14 eighth notes into four groups of three plus one group of two (4×3 + 2 = 14) — effectively 12/8 (compound quadruple) with an extra 2/8 tagged on the end, a genuinely different grouping shape from any of the 14/4 examples above despite the same top number.

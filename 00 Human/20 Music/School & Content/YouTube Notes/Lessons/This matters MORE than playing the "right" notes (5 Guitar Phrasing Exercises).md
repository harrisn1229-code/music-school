## Synopsis

Jared Borkowski (Sound Guitar Lessons) argues that phrasing — the rhythmic composition of melodic lines, and specifically *finishing complete ideas before switching things up* — matters more than note choice for making improvised lines sound like real music rather than a string of exercises. Rather than leaving that as an abstract instinct, the video gives four concrete "phrasing blueprints": fixed letter-forms (A A' B, A A A B, A A B A, A A B C) that constrain how strictly you must repeat, vary, or contrast your own material over a fixed number of measures, each demonstrated live over A minor pentatonic or a plain major scale to show the structure is scale/genre-agnostic. A fifth, closing exercise reframes the whole lesson as training toward *letting go* of conscious letter-tracking — reacting to your own playing by feel, aided by singing/humming along and by playing in octaves (a new device: octaves physically slow the line down and simplify it). This is a companion piece to the channel's other phrasing video already in the vault ([[Do this for better musical phrasing (guitar phrasing secret weapon)]], routed to [[Singing Along With Your Playing (Phrasing)]]) — that video develops the "sing along" approach in full depth; this one is the "phrasing models" approach that video's own framing referenced as a separate, not-yet-covered video. Together the two videos now cover two of that framing's three named approaches (phrasing models here; singing along there); the third (borrowing rhythms from speech/existing melodies) remains uncovered.

## What's taught

The complete blueprint extraction, with exact rules and drills, now lives on its own note since it's reusable technique rather than video-specific trivia — see [[Phrasing Blueprints]] for the full write-up of all five exercises (four fixed blueprints + the bonus sing-along/octaves exercise). Summary:

1. **[0:00–4:50] Blueprint 1 — A A' B.** Play any idea (A); repeat it near-verbatim, changing only the final note(s) (A'); repeat A exactly; close with a totally different idea (B). Demoed twice in A minor pentatonic, ~8 measures per pass.
2. **[4:50–6:44] Blueprint 2 — A A A B.** Same shape, rhythm-only (no prime) — repeat the exact rhythm three times while pitches vary freely, then close with a different rhythm.
3. **[6:44–8:22] Blueprint 3 — A A B A.** Borrowed from the AABA jazz song form. Each idea is strictly 2 measures: ~1 bar playing, ~1 bar resting/sustaining. B plays straight through the full 2 bars (filling the gap A left open), then the form closes by restating A. Demoed in plain major scale to show the structure isn't tied to any one vocabulary. Flagged by Jared as one of his favorite blueprints to work with.
4. **[8:22–9:54] Blueprint 4 — A A B C.** Like #3's setup but closes with a wholly new idea (C) instead of returning to A — no memory burden of restating the opening idea. B is allowed to open identically to A, then diverge partway through (evolve rather than contrast immediately). Jared notes the improvised demo at the very top of the video happened, unplanned, to land as exactly this blueprint.
5. **[9:54–13:00] Bonus — react by feel; sing along; try octaves.** Framed as the actual goal, not a bonus: stop consciously tracking a letter-form and just react to what you played. Sing or hum along while playing (pitch accuracy doesn't matter) — reported to add space, improve memory of what was played, and simplify lines. New device (not in the channel's other phrasing video): **playing in octaves slows you down and forces simpler phrasing**, and combines with singing along rather than replacing it. Also floats practicing phrasing over random/atonal notes specifically to prove phrasing (not note choice) is what makes something sound musical.
6. **[13:00–13:35] Outro/plug.** Free chord-tone/arpeggio vocabulary pack (12 chord types × 5 positions with matching shapes) offered as material to run these blueprints over — link in the description / soundguitarlessons.com. Next lesson flagged: a chord-melody solo arrangement of "Stella By Starlight."

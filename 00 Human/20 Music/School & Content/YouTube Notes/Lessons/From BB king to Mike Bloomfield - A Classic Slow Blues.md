# From BB king to Mike Bloomfield - A Classic Slow Blues

📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tpr9pMp5A58)

## Synopsis
Jack Ruch breaks down a two-part slow blues guitar solo in the key of C, explicitly built to teach **phrasing, note choice, and touch** rather than any single flashy lick — his stated goal is showing how much mileage comes from simple ideas played with intent. The solo is presented as a worked example: bar-by-bar across a full 12-bar form (I chord, then IV chord, then the turnaround back through V), with the lesson calling out where each phrase came from — repeatedly citing BB King and (his biggest influence, per the lesson) Mike Bloomfield, plus a nod to Albert Collins for one specific device. The most valuable material is less "here's a lick" and more "here's *why* this lick works": the pickup phrasing that anticipates each 4-bar boundary, the physical mechanics of a vocal-quality bend (delayed vibrato), picking-hand position as a deliberate tone control, and a chromatic chord-tone-targeting approach for soloing cleanly over the IV7 change. The whole thing closes with a genuine Mike Bloomfield signature lick — a toggle between the 5th and ♭5 over the I chord — framed as proof that a "signature" sound is often just a small, well-placed interval move played with conviction. No gear, amp, or production detail is discussed; this is pure playing technique.

## What's taught
- **Pickup phrasing that anticipates the downbeat, not just the note choice.** The solo's opening line is a rhythmic pickup — 5th scale degree → major 6th → root — played in the beat(s) before bar 1, with a double-stop (3rd + 5th of the C major chord) landing exactly on the downbeat that starts the 12-bar form. Called out explicitly as "a common phrasing technique in the blues": the anticipation, not the pitches, is the technique. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]], [[Blues Form and Harmony]]
- **The same pickup phrase is reused to mark the next structural boundary** (bar 5, the move to the IV chord) — restating a phrase shape at each 4-bar turn as a melodic analogue of the form's own AAB call-and-response structure. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]], [[Blues Form and Harmony]]
- **Mixing major and minor 3rd inside one phrase via a hammer-on.** Playing ♭3 up to the major 3rd (hammer-on), then continuing through the root and major 6th — used more than once in bars 1–4, the concrete physical gesture for what [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]] describes as bending/sitting between the ♭3 and natural 3rd. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]], [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- **Delayed vibrato on a bend, for a vocal-like quality.** On a big bend from the ♭7 up to the root (on the B string): bend cleanly to pitch first, hold without vibrato briefly, then slowly widen into vibrato — rather than starting the vibrato immediately on the bend. Explicitly framed as making the note sound "more vocal." → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Trilling/toggling between the ♭3 and the major 6th, held as a color over the I chord** — named as a device heard across Mike Bloomfield, BB King, and Albert Collins specifically, used twice in this solo (once in bars 1–4, again in a variant in bars 5–8). → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]], [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- **Box-position switching mid-phrase.** After the major-pentatonic-based opening material, the solo shifts up the neck into a minor pentatonic box position (a slide up to the root on the 13th fret of the B string bridges the two) for a chromatic connector lick — position changes are lick-driven, not just chord-driven. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Chromatic connector: ♭3 → major 3 → 4th → bend, then resolve back down through 4th/5th to ♭3 and root.** A self-contained three-note-plus-bend lick used to close out the first 4 bars from the new minor pentatonic box position. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Chromatic chord-tone targeting over the IV7 chord.** Over F7 (root–3rd–5th–♭7 = F, A, C, E♭), walk up chromatically into each chord tone in turn: approach note → 3rd, approach note → 5th, approach note → ♭7 — effectively playing the arpeggio with a chromatic approach note in front of each target rather than leaping directly to it. Framed as reusable for any dominant-7th chord change. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **A big bend up to the tonic pitch (C) inside the IV-chord phrase**, then a repeat of the same targeting lick, then a repeat of the ♭3/major-6th trill coming back down. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Pick-hand position as a deliberate tone control.** For the more minor-sounding, chromatically dense phrase in bars 5–8, he deliberately picks down near the bridge for a **twangier, brighter, more aggressive** sound — a touch decision independent of note choice or gear, chosen to match the harder edge of that specific lick. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **A second chromatic run — root → chromatic walk-up (♭3, major 3, 4th) → bend-and-pull-off down to ♭3 → double pull-off (4th to ♭3) → grab the ♭7 → closing bend/pull-off flourish → hammer-on to the major 3rd** — the bars 5–8 closer, explicitly called out as leaning hard on the minor 3rd before resolving up into the major 3rd, described as "such a typical thing" in this style of blues playing. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]], [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- **Turnaround phrasing (bars 9–12): a bend from the root of the V7 chord (G7) up to its 6th**, rather than a more "expected" chord-tone bend target — over the V chord, played as the fifth of the I (C) chord functioning as the root of the V7 (G7), then bent up to the 6th of that G7. A reminder that a bend's payoff note doesn't have to be a 3rd/5th/root. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **The turnaround repeats the minor-pentatonic chromatic connector and the IV-chord targeting phrase** as the progression moves G7 → F → the C7/F7/C7 turnaround, before the closing lick — reinforcing that this solo is built from a small, reused vocabulary rather than twelve bars of fresh material. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]], [[Blues Form and Harmony]]
- **Closing signature lick: toggling between the 5th and the ♭5 over the I (C) chord** — explicitly named as a genuine Mike Bloomfield signature move, and framed as portable to other chords/keys: pick a chord tone and its chromatic neighbor a half-step below and alternate. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **General teaching philosophy stated up front and demonstrated throughout:** simple ideas (a short pentatonic-based phrase, a bend, a hammer-on) deliver the "blues" sound through *phrasing, note choice, and touch* rather than technical complexity — the whole 15-minute solo is built from roughly half a dozen reused devices, not a long list of unrelated licks.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — I'm So Tired]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #138.*

**Form.** A song that swings between exhaustion and agitation, and does it with **two clichés — I–vi–IV–V in the verse, I–IV–V in the refrain — with exactly two non-cliché chords dropped into the verse to break them**. Verse-heavy with a refrain that functions as an outburst, then a complete ending rather than a fade. Verse **12 bars**, AAB, blues-shaped, harmonically **open** — every verse begins on I and ends on V or IV, never at rest. Refrain also 12 bars but in short phrases in an AABBC pattern, and harmonically **closed**, a full circle back to I. Outro is the refrain's last phrase repeated three times. The ascending bassline pickup that opens the track also restarts verse 3 straight out of the refrain, as if the outburst had not happened.

**Harmony.** `Verse: I – ♯V/III – IV – V · I – vi – IV – V · I – ♯Vaug – vi – iv`. Two deliberate cliché-busters: the **G♯ major in bar 2** of the opening phrase where the second phrase puts F♯ minor — so the two "identical" phrases are not, and the G♯→D that follows is **root motion by tritone**, close to unheard of in pop and specific to John's songs — and the **augmented triad** in bar 2 of the third phrase, which he says should not get a Roman numeral at all, being voice leading between the chords on either side ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]). The phrase then lands on **minor iv**, set up by a preceding chord that forces an F♮ cross-relation ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). `Refrain: I (4 bars) – V (4) – IV (2) – I (2)` — three chords, four bars each, a drastically slower **harmonic rhythm** than the verse's one-per-bar. That, plus a different drum pattern, is the entire mechanism behind the apparent tempo lift ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]); the tempo never changes.

**Notable.** The outro is worth studying as an energy curve ([[ARR-C05 — The energy curve — one apex, and pull back before the end]]): the closing phrase runs three times, the first two heavy with drums and organ glissandos, the third **pulled back to muttering** — the peak is not the last thing you hear.

**Against this page.** Agrees on A major. He calls the second section a refrain, where this page's `structure:` reads it as a bridge.

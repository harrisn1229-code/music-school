# RHY-C04 — Polyrhythm and hemiola

**Q.** What is a polyrhythm? What is a hemiola? How do they differ?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Polyrhythm** = two conflicting subdivisions at once — classically **3 against 2**, three evenly spaced notes in the span of two. **Hemiola** = a pattern implying a *different meter* over the prevailing one — **three groups of two against two groups of three**. Polyrhythm stacks subdivisions; hemiola *reorganizes the grouping*.

## Why it matters

Both blank, and hemiola appeared **nowhere in the vault** before 2026-08-03. Count 3-against-2 with *"nice cup of tea"* — 'nice' and 'of' are the 2; 'nice', 'cup', 'tea' are the 3. Practical hemiola: a dotted-quarter pulse over 4/4 — three eighths against a bar of eight — cycles out of phase and realigns on the downbeat every 3 bars (8 dotted quarters = 24 eighths = 3 bars).

## Drill

Clap 3-against-2 with the phrase until it locks. Then program a repeating dotted-quarter hit over a 4/4 loop at least 3 bars long and hear it drift, then land back on the downbeat of bar 4.

# how to make a 2000's r&b sample & flip it into a beat for tory lanez/bryson tiller

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sPqBmZLaDM4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sPqBmZLaDM4)

## Synopsis

loner builds a from-scratch sample deliberately styled on the **2000s R&B era** — reference tracks named on-screen are Ne-Yo's "So Sick" and T-Pain/Chris Brown's "Best Love Song" — then flips the finished sample into a modern-day R&B trap beat. The sample half stacks four elements around a simple piano bed: the piano itself (Kontakt's "Gentleman" patch, A-minor/all-white-keys chords for instant melodicism), an Analog Lab counter-melody lead, a chopped Splice string loop, and a full lead-plus-harmony vocal. The video's clearest stated principle is genre-appropriate restraint: unlike loner's usual vintage/tape-heavy treatment, this era "isn't too vintage" (2000s, not decades removed), so no tape/wear effects are added anywhere in the sample — the vocal and piano stay comparatively clean. The second half flips the finished sample into a modern beat: run it through a fresh lo-fi EQ/reverb/time-stretch pass to push it further back and distant, then build a drum pattern around it (snap, rim shot, a small hi-hat roll, and a second "short and stabby" bass layered in as a switch-up). Runs a tight, no-filler 9:25 and packs concrete settings for nearly every stage.

## Techniques

**Piano bed — Kontakt "Gentleman":**
- **Instrument:** Kontakt's "Gentleman" patch — described as "super clean, super simple."
- **Chords:** simple, melodic chords built entirely from the **A minor scale (all white keys)** — called out explicitly as "a great way to instantly get a more melodic sound" without needing to think hard about voicing choices. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Chord Progressions]]
- **Voicing detail:** the piano's **top-line notes** are singled out as doing double duty — they give the part "bounce" AND function as the actual melody, rather than the melody being a separate layer.
- 🎬 general lesson: leaning on a diatonic white-keys-only scale is a fast, repeatable way to guarantee melodic chords without hunting for voicings by ear. → [[Melody Writing]]

**Piano FX chain:**
1. **Compressor** — "really wanted to tighten the sound up and make it super together." → [[Compression]]
2. **Small room plate reverb** — a small, room-sized plate setting, kept subtle. → [[Reverb]], [[Little Plate]]
3. **Pro-Q 3** (owned-lane [[Pro-Q 4]]) — a low-end cut, plus a **dynamic EQ cut** on a specific narrow section: place a normal peak/cut band wherever you want to carve, then click the arrow → **Make Dynamic** — the band only engages once the signal crosses a threshold, "almost like compression" rather than a static, always-on cut. → [[EQ]], [[Pro-Q 4]]
- 🎬 general lesson: **make-dynamic EQ turns a static cut into a threshold-gated one** — useful when a frequency is only a problem some of the time (loud passages, specific notes) rather than throughout the whole part. → [[EQ]]
- **Deliberately no tape/vintage stage on the piano** — "normally I'd add some sort of tape effect... but these types of songs often have really clean sounds. They're not too vintage" — 2000s reference tracks get noticeably less aging/wear processing than loner's usual 60s/70s or "vintage soul" builds. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

**Counter-melody — Analog Lab:**
- **Source:** Arturia Analog Lab, "softy lead" preset from the **Layers** bank — a simple, super-basic melody played on top of the piano.
- **Chain:** simple EQ (low-end cut) → **[[Cassette]]**, "Crushed" preset (audibly distorted/crushed character) → a pretty light reverb.
- → [[Cassette]], [[EQ]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**Strings — Splice loop (VST search came up empty):**
- Couldn't find a VST string sound that "sounded really good," so pulled a **string loop from Splice**, chopped it up, and layered it on top of the piano. → [[Splice Sounds]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

**Strings, second layer — SRX Orchestra (during a vocal-free section):**
- Wanted to fill the space of a section without vocals, so opened **SRX Orchestra** (unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]), found a good string sound, and played a simple counter-melody with it. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**Lead vocal:**
1. **Pitch up an octave** (before anything else in the chain). → [[Pitch and Formant]]
2. **[[Little AlterBoy]]** — added after the octave pitch-up, no specific preset named for this stage.
3. **Pro-Q 3** (owned-lane [[Pro-Q 4]]) — cutting out highs AND lows.
4. **[[Decapitator]]** — reached for specifically **when the vocal isn't cutting through the mix enough / lacks grit**, not applied by default — "if I feel like the vocal isn't cutting through the mix enough, it doesn't have enough grit to it, then I'll throw a Decapitator on there. It just distorts it a little bit." Preset used: **"mild tube vox."** → [[Decapitator]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
5. **Reverb** — the auto-captioned **"Intensity" reverb, "vocal distance sci-fi" preset**, run almost all the way wet. (Third confirmed sighting of this unconfirmed-vendor reverb across loner's videos — see [[Plugin Wishlist]].) → [[Reverb]]
6. **A super light fruity delay** (owned-lane [[Echo]]/[[Delay]]) to close the chain. → [[Delay]]

**Harmonies:** layered underneath the lead, hit "just at the end of each four bars," processed through "pretty much exactly the same" chain as the lead vocal above. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]

**The flip — processing the finished sample for the beat:**
1. **EQ** — a band cut taking off some high end and some low end, kept restrained ("not go too crazy") — pushes the sample toward more lo-fi/distant/airy. → [[EQ]]
2. **Reverb** — kept fairly short, low end cut out of the return, size brought down a little. → [[Reverb]]
3. **Time-stretch + tempo drop** — stretch the sample and bring the tempo down, landing around **145 BPM**, specifically to slow it down for the flip. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- 🎬 general lesson: EQ → reverb → stretch/tempo-drop, in that order, is a compact 3-stage recipe for pushing an already-finished sample further back/distant before building drums under it. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

**Flip drums:**
- **Snap:** the starting element — trimmed to hit only **twice** per pattern.
- **Rim shot:** placed in between the two snap hits.
- **Hi-hat:** one small roll added, "nothing crazy."
- **Second bass (switch-up layer):** a **short and stabby** bass added as a second bass layer specifically so the pattern "switches up." → [[Bass Design]]
- 🎬 general lesson: build the flip's drum pattern starting from the snap/rim-shot pair, then layer a small hi-hat roll and a contrasting short/stabby second bass as the switch-up element, rather than programming a full kit at once. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]]

## Synopsis

A short, plainly-stated beginner-to-intermediate lesson on the **backdoor dominant chord** (♭VII7 resolving up a whole step to I) — what it is, why it works, which standards carry it, and three concrete soloing approaches over it. ## What's taught

- **What a backdoor dominant is.** Take the diatonic vii chord of a key (B in C major), flatten its root to ♭VII (B♭), and make it dominant → B♭7 resolving up a whole step to Cmaj7. Naturally carries a ♯11 (E natural in B♭7's case), so a ♯11 voicing is a safe add.
- **Why it works.** B♭7 shares a lot of tones with G7 altered (the "real" V of C, in its altered form) — both dominant sounds want to pull to the same tonic, which is why the ear accepts the substitution.
- **Where it shows up.** Named standards carrying a backdoor dominant: **Ladybird**, **Stella By Starlight**, **I Should Care**.
- **Soloing approach 1 — reframe as a foreign-key 2-5-1, resolve to the shared tones.** B♭7 is the V chord of E♭ major, so any 2-5-1 lick in E♭ works over it *as long as it's aimed at a note E♭ major and C major have in common* — the two keys share **G and D**. Practical rule: borrow E♭ ii-V-I vocabulary, target the resolution note to land on G or D rather than the "wrong-key" E♭.
- **Soloing approach 2 — read the backdoor chord as its own related ii, apply the Kansas City blues scale.** B♭7 is the V of a backdoor 2-5-1 whose ii is F minor 7 (matches [[The ii-V-I]] § *Guitar shortcut: the power-chord finder*'s backdoor-ii derivation exactly). Play the **[[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]] § Kansas City blues scale** off F minor, and resolve the blues lick to a chord tone of Cmaj7 — explicitly naming two extension targets beyond the plain chord tones: the **9th (D)** or the **13th (A)** of Cmaj7.
- **Soloing approach 3 — read the ii chord as minor-major (melodic minor), with a full practice methodology.** Reinterpret F minor 7 as **F minor-major 7** and solo with **F melodic minor** — it carries both the ♯11 color the B♭ dominant wants and the minor-ii sound. Stated practice routine, given as a repeatable sequence rather than a one-off lick:
  1. Start the scale from **every chord tone of F minor-major 7** and try to land on a chord tone or extension of Cmaj7 by the next measure (4 beats later).
  2. Once that works, **add chromatic passing notes** going both up and down the scale.
  3. Add **target notes on Cmaj7** and land on them using **enclosures**.
  4. Start from **every note of the F melodic minor scale** (not just chord tones) and find which ones lay best under the fingers on guitar.
  5. **Combine fragments of the F minor-major 7 arpeggio** with the scale-derived ideas to build fuller lines.
- **Recap framing.** Three interchangeable mental models for the same chord: a 2-5-1 in E♭ resolving to a shared tone (G or D), the Kansas City blues scale off the related ii (F minor), or F minor-major 7 (melodic minor) resolving to Cmaj7 — pick whichever fits the moment.

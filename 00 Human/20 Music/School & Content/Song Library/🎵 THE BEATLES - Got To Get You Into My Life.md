# Got To Get You Into My Life — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Paul synthesising big-band pop, blues and modal rock into something that refuses a genre label. Formally, the notable thing is that the **vamping, potentially endless coda grows out of an extra repeat of the refrain** rather than being tacked on as a new section. Analytically the meat is the verse, which is a study in three consecutive strategies for a bass line.

**Form.** A 4-bar instrumental intro vamping on I, already carrying the stacked upper extensions that will characterise the verse. The verse is a squared-off **16 bars** but internally phrased in unequal lengths. The refrain is **6 bars**, extended from an expected four because one chord is sustained across two of them — syncopation operating on harmonic rhythm rather than in the foreground ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]). The outro runs over twelve bars on I before the fade, implying a jam that continues past the end of the tape.

**Harmony.** The verse's A phrases are a sustained tonic with 7–9–11–13 stacked on it; call it an extended I or call it ♭VII and IV superimposed, it's experientially identical. The key is established by **pedal-point insistence, not by any cadence** ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]). The B phrases hold a chord above a bass walking down chromatically until the sonority becomes an implicit half-diminished seventh pointing hard at the relative minor — but the resolution is deliberately deferred to the *middle of the next phrase*. The C phrase then descends diatonically and finally lands the key unequivocally. Sing the bass line alone and the section reads as three tries at the same climb. The refrain is plain but bluesy in delivery, with ♭3 and ♭7 rubbing against the chords ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]).

**Melody.** Spiky in the verse, wide leaps across a wide range. The refrain tune is fragmentary but elides seamlessly into the instrumental rejoinder answering it — a **hocket** that reads back as one continuous line when you hum the song.

**Arrangement.** A big brassy stage band recorded close enough to distort on purpose; the mix is genuinely dirty, partly tape limits and partly the intended aesthetic. The pulse is fast, but the thrust comes from relentless triplets filling the beats plus constant syncopation.

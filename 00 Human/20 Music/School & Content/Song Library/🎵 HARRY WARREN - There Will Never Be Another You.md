# There Will Never Be Another You — Harry Warren

> A 32-bar **ABAC** standard in E♭ major, used by Jens Larsen as the full worked example for a chord-memorization-by-chunking method — the same slot-devices ([[32-Bar Song Form]]'s table) that recur across "It Could Happen to You," "But Not for Me," and other tunes in this form. Full breakdown: [[Memorizing Chord Progressions and Jazz Standards - Jazz Theory and Analysis put to use!]].

## Chords

*As analyzed, chunked.*

**A** (8 bars) — `E♭maj7 (2 bars) | Dø7 – G7 → Cm7 (2-5 to vi) | B♭m7 – E♭7 → A♭maj7 (2-5 to IV)`
**B** (8 bars) — `A♭maj7 (IV) | A♭m6/D♭7 (IV-minor) → E♭maj7 (I) | F7 – B♭7 (V7/V, "5 of 5," 2 bars) | Fm7(ii, opens back to A) `
**A** (8 bars) — repeat of the first A
**C** (8 bars) — `A♭maj7 (IV) | A♭m6/D♭7 (IV-minor) → E♭maj7 (I) | Dø7 → Gm7 (♯4°-reharmonized-as-ii, cadencing to ii) | E♭maj7 – A♭7 – Gm7 – C7 (closing turnaround-flavored progression) | B♭7 (tag, back to top)`

## Chunk map (the memorization payoff)

| Chunk | Bars | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| Tonic | A, bar 1–2 | I, 2 bars |
| 2-5 to vi | A, bar 3–4 | Dø7–G7→Cm7, one unit |
| 2-5 to IV | A, bar 5–6 | B♭m7–E♭7→A♭maj7, one unit |
| IV → IV-minor → I | B/C opening | Reused verbatim between B and C |
| V7/V | B, late | 2-bar "5 of 5," a very common ABAC-form first-half-ending device |
| ♯4°→ii minor cadence | C, mid | Historically a bare ♯4-diminished, reharmonized post-bebop into a minor ii–V — see [[Diminished Chords]] |
| Cadence to C minor | C, closing (4 bars) | Bracket-grouped as one chunk |
| 2-5-1 to the 6th degree | C, closing (4 bars) | Bracket-grouped as one chunk |
| "4-minor back to 1" | C, closing (4 bars) | Reusable shape across many standards in this form |
| "5-of-5 to 2-5" | C, closing (4 bars) | Also common at the END OF A BRIDGE in AABA tunes (Ellington-style), not unique to this form |

## Steal this

- **Chunk a chart the same way you'd memorize a phone number** — country code / area code / rest, i.e. form → cadences → small functional units — rather than a flat row of chord symbols. → [[Memorizing Jazz Standards by Harmonic Chunking]]
- **Once B and C share their opening bars, that material only needs to be learned once** — check any ABAC-shaped tune for this before assuming the second half is all-new material. → [[32-Bar Song Form]]
- **Cadences to the IV degree at the start of the B section, and IV→IV-minor→I shortly after, are recurring, position-predictable devices across this whole family of 32-bar standards** — recognize the slot, not just the one tune's chords. → [[32-Bar Song Form]]
- **A ♯4-diminished chord resolving into a minor cadence is very often a reharmonized bebop-era substitution for what used to be a bare diminished chord** — "Embraceable You" is (possibly) one of the earliest examples of this specific move. → [[Diminished Chords]]
- **When a passage looks harmonically dense, check what's on the strong beats** — here the closing turnaround reads as just I→ii with A♭7/C7 as passing embellishments, not four equally-important chords.

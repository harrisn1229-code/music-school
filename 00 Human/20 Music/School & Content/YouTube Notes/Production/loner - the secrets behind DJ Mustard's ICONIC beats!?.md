# the secrets behind DJ Mustard's ICONIC beats!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bID1-whdYB0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bID1-whdYB0)

## Synopsis

loner rebuilds three DJ Mustard production styles from scratch — the bouncy, piano-driven West Coast sound behind Roddy Ricch's "Ballin'," Mustard's darker/uptempo lane (a Don Toliver-style piano opener plus a "Parking Lot"-style horn/vocal sample flip), and the stripped-down pop-hit mode Mustard runs for artists like Rihanna and Post Malone. The connective tissue across all three is the same West Coast "Mustard bounce" drum recipe (snap, cowbell, rim, tom transition, then hi-hat + clap for the hook, spaced kick, deep synth bass or 808) and a heavy reliance on **Audimee** — an AI vocal-conversion plugin (sponsor-plugged, captioned garbled as "AI"/"order me"/"udy" throughout) used for three genuinely different jobs: converting a vocal into two different voice models plus a pitch-edited melody for an "authentic group" sound, turning a clicked-in piano melody into a realistic flute, and turning a clicked-in guitar melody into a sax — i.e. using an AI voice tool as a general instrument-timbre-transfer tool, not just a vocal effect. Two of his own paid sample products get used as sound sources (Layers Vol. 3 for a vocal one-shot, "Next Kit" for a pulsating pad one-shot). The most reusable single idea is the **West Coast bounce drum build order** — snap/cowbell/rim first, tom added specifically as a section-transition device, then hi-hat + clap layered in only when the hook needs more energy — which is a clean, repeatable arrangement recipe independent of any specific plugin.

## Techniques

**West Coast bounce drums (the throughline across all three beats):**
- **Build order, not just sound selection, defines the "Mustard bounce."** Verse/intro: snap + cowbell + rim only. Add a tom specifically as a *transition* device going into the hook (not a rhythmic element — a signal that a section is about to change). Hook: layer the snap with a clap (doubling the backbeat hit rather than swapping it), add a hi-hat specifically for rhythmic movement, add a spaced-out kick (kick sits in gaps rather than driving a dense pattern), and finish with a long, deep synth bass. *(general starting point — no BPM or exact placement given for this beat)* → [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Roland TR-808|TR-808]]
- **The darker/uptempo variant keeps the same ~100 BPM pocket but doubles the hi-hat rate** — hats played at double speed against the same tempo is what reads as "more energy/uptempo" without actually changing the BPM, closed out with a simple 808 instead of a synth bass. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **The pop-hit variant (Rihanna-style) strips the kit down to "very spaced out"** — a reverbed rim, jittery/irregular hi-hats, a basic kick, and a distorted, tinny bass carrying the hook — deliberately sparse so there's maximum room left for the vocal. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Rap Vocal Production]]

**"Ballin'"-style piano/vocal build:**
- **Layer two piano parts together, both in a major scale, for the bouncy West Coast melodic-chord identity.** Called out as ingrained DNA for West Coast production specifically — the major-scale-plus-doubled-piano combination is what gives Mustard's chords their bounce rather than a moodier minor-key read. → [[1 Electric Piano Track|Electric Piano Track]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Melody Writing]]
- **Sample source discipline: identify a sample already flipped by another artist (Summer Walker's "Body"), then flip it differently rather than copying the same treatment.** Same source sample, deliberately different arrangement/instrumentation choices from the reference flip. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Sample Clearance]]
- **A vocal one-shot (sourced from Layers Vol. 3, loner's own sample pack) + basic reverb, then run through Audimee, selecting TWO different voice models and using Audimee's pitch-editor to alter the melody on one of the two voices.** The point isn't just "change the voice" — running the same vocal through two different voice conversions with one melody deliberately altered creates a fake "group" (multiple singers) sound out of a single source take, closer to how the original sample actually sounds. → [[Plugin Wishlist]] (Audimee), [[Little AlterBoy]] (closest owned pitch/formant tool — doesn't do full voice-model conversion), [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Rap Vocal Production]]
- **Filter the AI-processed vocal before adding the next layer** — a basic but easy-to-skip step: don't just add the synth layer on top of the raw vocal-AI output, filter it first so the next layer has room. → [[Masking and Carving]]
- **A high, synthy string layer "inspired by Bud De" for a 2000s R&B vibe** — added on top of the filtered AI vocal specifically to date-stamp the track to that era's R&B sound. Source name is low-confidence (auto-caption garble); treat as a general "bright synth string stab" reference rather than a specific patch. → [[1 Synth Lead Track|Synth Lead Track]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A dedicated "switch" sound for the pre-hook/section change: a one-shot gliding pad with portamento turned up (the "Ballin'" glide sound).** Using ONE specific patch (rather than a generic pad) purely as a transition marker is the reusable idea — a single one-shot with glide, not a whole new part, signals the section is about to switch. → [[1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Rhodes/Roads electric piano + guitar accents layered in as texture**, on top of the piano/vocal/synth stack already built — arrangement principle stated directly: "Mustard beats may sound simple, but they always have different instruments coming in and out, allowing the song to develop." → [[1 Electric Piano Track|Electric Piano Track]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

**Dark/uptempo build (Don Toliver-style opener + "Parking Lot"-style sample flip):**
- **A catchy piano melody as the beat's cold-open hook**, same DNA as the "Ballin'" piano layering but written darker/more uptempo. → [[Melody Writing]], [[1 Electric Piano Track|Electric Piano Track]]
- **AI instrument-timbre-transfer, technique #1: click in a melody on a piano, drag the MIDI/audio into Audimee, and select a flute preset** — because a realistic flute isn't otherwise available, Audimee is used to convert a piano performance's timbre into flute rather than the more usual vocal-conversion job. Explicitly framed by loner as "you're not just limited to flipping vocals" — Audimee doubles as an instrument-swap tool. → [[Plugin Wishlist]] (Audimee), [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Sample flip #2 ("Parking Lot"-style): a source sample with horns AND vocals, flipped by pulling individual melodic elements out rather than using the loop whole.** Clicked in a guitar melody from the sample and converted it into a **sax** via Audimee (instrument-transfer technique #2, same tool as the flute move above) and separately kept a vocal loop from the same source sample. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Rebuild the rest of the sample's texture from owned instruments once the AI-converted lead is in place: acoustic bass, a Rhodes/Roads lick, and a vintage percussion loop.** The AI conversion handles the one element (guitar→sax) that can't be easily re-played; everything else around it is played/sourced conventionally. → [[1 Electric Piano Track|Electric Piano Track]], [[Bassline Writing]], [[Layering and Stacking]]

**Rihanna/Post Malone pop-hit build:**
- **Textured, pulsating pad chain: one-shot (from "Next Kit," another of loner's own sample products) → Reverb → guitar amp → shaper box (Cableguys ShaperBox territory) → EQ.** Running a synth/pad one-shot through a *guitar amp* simulator specifically (not just reverb/EQ) is the character-defining step — it's what turns a plain pulsating one-shot into something textured rather than clean. Owned amp-sim lane: [[Morgan Amps Suite]] (owned) — note [[Guitar Rig 7 FX]] is installed but runs in DEMO, not owned; shaper-box stage already tracked as a real gap on [[Plugin Wishlist]] (medium-impact item, Cableguys ShaperBox 3). → [[1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[EQ]] (effect), [[Reverb]] (effect)
- **Power chords — just two notes — deliberately, to keep a dark vibe without reading as melodic.** A concrete arrangement/voicing choice: dropping to a two-note (root + fifth style) voicing removes the third, which is exactly what keeps a chord from resolving into a clear major/minor "melodic" feel — useful whenever a pad needs to feel moody rather than pretty. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Chord Progressions]]
- **Second pulsating element (from the Post Malone Mustard beat): same one-shot-plus-shaper-box recipe as above, but with MORE attack and a different shaper setting.** Two pads built from the same signal-chain recipe, differentiated purely by attack character and shaper settings rather than a different source sound — a reusable "one recipe, several variants by tweaking two parameters" workflow. → [[1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Final melodic element: a vocal, reversed, chopped up, then given a bright reverb.** A simple three-step vocal-texture recipe (reverse → chop → bright reverb) used as the track's one clearly melodic element, sitting against the deliberately non-melodic power-chord pad. → [[Reverb]] (effect), [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]

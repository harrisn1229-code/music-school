# Real Love — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4, in E minor) · Verse (8) · Verse · Refrain (8, AA′BB′) · **Intro** · Verse · Refrain · Break (unsquare, extended to 5 bars, in F♯ minor) · Verse · Refrain, with the refrain's second half repeated seven times into the fade. The returning intro is a Lennon habit going back to "Thank You Girl".

The intro's `i – V6/3` is doing real work: the **first-inversion dominant** makes the phrase relaxed rather than transitive and hands the verse a bassline to descend from. Play it with V in root position and both effects vanish. The verse then shifts straight to the **parallel major**, and its opening bars resist Roman numerals altogether — a lazily voice-led filling of the distance from I to V, with one gratuitous augmented chord in bar 4. In the last two bars the bass holds E so the change above it is neighbour motion between two E major chords rather than a chord change at all ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]).

The refrain's peak chord is a **half-diminished F♯ in 6/5**, an ordinary ii6/5 altered for sentiment; its second half is then the `I – vi – IV – V` cliché completely unvarnished, and the three local oddities are what balance that predictability.

The tune is almost purely pentatonic ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]) — Lennon's standing melodic mode — and runs roughshod over the chords with **neighbour tones that are never resolved**. The clearest case is the G♯ in bar 4 of the refrain: people supply the missing resolution themselves when singing it from memory.

## Techniques

- **Sustain-and-swell-capable instruments suit melody better than instant-decay instruments.** Voice, strings (violin), and synth pads/leads can hold a note indefinitely and swell its volume over time — a decaying instrument (piano, guitar, harp) can't "bring a note back to life" once struck, it only fades. Sustained-instrument melodies read as more expressive/vocal-like; decay-instrument melodies (piano, plucked synth) still work but carry a different, more percussive-adjacent character — a factor to weigh when choosing what plays the hook. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **Melody is the "hummable" element because it's a single note at a time and relates directly to the human voice** — chords can't be hummed (multiple simultaneous notes), bass is often overlooked by untrained ears, drums can only be approximated by beatboxing — melody alone maps onto what an average listener can vocally reproduce, which is the underlying reason it dominates listener attention and recall.
- **Take deliberate time auditioning melody options rather than keeping the first idea** — try multiple melodic ideas against the same chord/bass/drum bed and consciously choose, rather than defaulting to whatever the fingers land on first; framed as worth the extra time specifically because melody carries disproportionate weight in what a listener remembers. Already implicit in [[Melody Writing]]'s "record busy, mute down" and "fixing a boring melody" sections — no new mechanic, just reinforcement of the same discipline.

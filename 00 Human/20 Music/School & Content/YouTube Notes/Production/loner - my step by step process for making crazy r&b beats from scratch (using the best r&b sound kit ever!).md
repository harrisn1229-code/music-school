## Synopsis

The launch video for loner's own sample bundle, **Layers Volume One** — five kits sold together (~$50, framed as $100+ of value): 151 one-shots processed through vintage effects/guitar pedals, 50 Arturia Analog Lab presets built for R&B sounds/textures, 50 MIDI chord progressions plus counter-melodies (labeled by key), 10 flippable full samples, and a 200+ sound drum kit — all original, "no recycled stuff." Rather than a straight product ad, loner speed-builds one full sample and one drum pattern live using only the new kit's contents, narrating each choice as he goes — a real, if fast and low-detail, workflow walkthrough rather than a deep breakdown. The through-line worth extracting isn't any single setting (there's almost no processing shown — the kit's whole pitch is that its sounds arrive pre-processed) but the *decision sequence*: one-shot → pitch it down for texture → pad underneath → lead filling melodic gaps → textural layers → bass (pitched to taste) → a chopped, key-matched vocal loop, the whole sample assembled in "around about 10, 15 minutes." The drum half follows its own build order — rim shot, layered snares, hats, percs for bounce, kick, and a new 808 pattern added *alongside* (not replacing) the sample's existing hard guitar bass. This is the vault's most direct confirmation yet of what's actually inside "Layers Volume One," a kit already referenced piecemeal across several other loner breakdowns (see [[Plugin Wishlist]]).

## Techniques

- **Layers Volume One kit contents (official, from the source): 151 vintage-effects/guitar-pedal-processed one-shots, 50 Analog Lab R&B presets, 50 MIDI chord progressions + counter-melodies (key-labeled), 10 flippable samples, and a 200+ sound original drum kit — five kits bundled for ~$50.** The direct product description, not a mention in passing — confirms and expands the fragmentary "Layers Volume 1" references already tracked from other breakdowns (e.g. the "Pray for Love" bass preset). → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Pitch one-shots DOWN deliberately for texture — "they kind of stretch out and they have a really nice texture the lower you go."** A general starting-point move for any one-shot, not tied to this kit specifically: pitching down doesn't just lower the note, it changes the character/texture of the sample. *(general starting point — no semitone amount given)* → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Build the harmonic bed as pad-under-lead, both from Analog Lab, auditioned fast: first preset tried for the "underneath the roads" pad worked immediately; the lead layer went through several presets before settling on the simplest one, specifically to fill the melodic gaps between chord hits.** Explicitly framed as needing "not much processing... this kit because the sounds are already processed" — the whole point is idea-speed, not sound design. → [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Bass one-shot pitched UP a couple of semitones mid-build when it read too low** — a same-session correct-by-ear adjustment, not a planned setting. Guitar-bass and synth-bass one-shots are available in both the one-shot kit and the drum kit. *(general starting point — "a couple" semitones, not exact)* → [[Bass Design]]
- **A key-matched vocal loop chopped so it only plays in the GAPS where the chords don't hit, then pitched back down to its original pitch after being auditioned pitched-up for a key match.** Sourced from the one-shot kit's vocal loops, screened by ear for the closest match to the sample's key (B minor); the chop-to-fill-the-gaps move keeps the loop from running continuously and colliding with the chord hits. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Textural layering pass: a perc loop from the drum kit, plus a second, separate stretched one-shot texture from the one-shot kit** — added purely for texture after the harmonic/bass/vocal layers were in, then a late Analog Lab textural accent was tried and pulled back out because "it didn't fit too well" — restraint as the last step, not every audition kept. → [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Whole sample built in "around about 10, 15 minutes" — the kit's stated core value proposition is compressing idea-to-sample time, because every sound arrives pre-processed.** Directly ties to the "minimal processing" thread running through the whole build — worth reading as a sound-selection philosophy (buy/build a pre-processed palette so writing time isn't eaten by sound design) as much as a specific technique.
- **Drum build order: rim shot first, a second snare/rim layered underneath it → hi-hat MIDI → a perc layered under one of the snares, then a second perc "just to create a little bit more bounce" → one more snare added → kick (a "textured" kick specifically chosen from the kit) → tempo dropped mid-build (settled ~134 BPM, felt "a bit too fast" before) → an 808 added as a NEW/separate pattern (Make Unique), kept ALONGSIDE the sample's existing hard guitar bass rather than replacing it, picked deliberately "subby" specifically to counteract that hard bass.** A concrete build sequence, not a single tip — each layer named in the order it was added. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Bass Design]]

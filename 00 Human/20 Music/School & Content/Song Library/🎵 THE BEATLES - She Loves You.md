# She Loves You — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (8, phrased 2+2+4, itself a variation of the refrain) · Verse (16, four equal phrases, harmonically open — starts on I, ends on V) · Verse · Refrain (8, converging on the tonic) · Verse · Refrain · Outro. Unusually, **no verse comes between the last refrain and the outro** — the song simply exits from the refrain.

Structurally this is the least adventurous thing in the early run: four-square phrasing, one key throughout, chords changing every two bars as a rule. What carries it is vocabulary. The verse's closing V never resolves to I — it hands off to the vi that opens the refrain, a **deceptive resolution** doing the work of relieving an otherwise flat scheme ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]), and the only complete V–I cadence in the song sits at the end of the refrain.

The colour is a **minor iv borrowed from the parallel minor** ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]), approached the hard way both times: jumped into from vi in the verse, forcing an E♮/E♭ cross relation, and landing right after V-of-V in the refrain, forcing two at once ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). Set up conventionally from major IV the same chord reads as sentimental barbershop.

The **added sixth** on the final chord — the one George Martin argued against — works because the descending three-note hook has already been sung over vi and V-of-V, chords where that E belongs, before arriving over a I chord where it does not. The open fifths and fourths in the two-part vocal, rather than the expected thirds, are the actual signature of the early sound.

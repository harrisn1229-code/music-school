# Zach Scholz - How To Record 70's Style Bass Tones

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c4EPtO-Jgj0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c4EPtO-Jgj0)

## Synopsis

A gear-demo video built around Zach Scholz's new 1982 TASCAM M-30 analog mixer (the same unit covered in [[Zach Scholz - Why I Added a 1980s Analog Mixer to My Modern Studio]]) — he runs a 1970s German Höfner bass through it to explore channel-strip EQ curves and the mixer's drive/fuzz character, framing the M-30 as a "fundamental base mix" stage that gets refined further in the computer afterward, since there's no specific song/mix target here. Moderate narration (623 words for 7 minutes — a fair amount is demo/listening, not talking), but the core points are concrete: EQ curves are mix-dependent (there's no universal bass EQ, only what a given mix calls for), light compression via the mixer's drive behavior, and his enthusiastic verdict that the M-30's overdriven fuzz on bass beats every fuzz pedal he's tried.

## Techniques

- **He tracks his 1970s Höfner bass (the exact violin bass Nate owns — [[Hofner B-Bass H-Series]]) through a 1982 TASCAM M-30 analog mixer** — the period-correct instrument through a period-correct mixer for genuine 70s-adjacent low-end character. → [[BASS Slot]]
- **Bass EQ has no universal answer — it's entirely mix-dependent** — his explicit example: a mix already dense in mid-range calls for scooping the bass's mids to make room, while a different mix might call for more or less low end; he demos several different EQ curves specifically because there's no target song here, not because any one curve is "correct." Directly reinforces the existing [[1 Electric Bass DI Track]] chain's rule that bass EQ should be wide, contextual carve moves, not fixed presets.
- **Split workflow: build a "fundamental" bass tone/EQ on the analog mixer first, then refine further in the computer once there's an actual mix to fit** — deliberately NOT doing 100% of the EQ work on either side; the mixer pass sets the character, the DAW pass fits it to context.
- **Compression kept light and mix-agnostic: ~5 dB gain reduction at most** — he flags that this is a "basic" starting point specifically because compression choices should really be made with a target song in mind, not blind.
- **Driving the M-30's channel strip harder breaks up/distorts the bass AND acts as a limiter on its dynamic range at the same time** — pushing the mixer input is simultaneously a saturation move and a dynamics move, not just tone shaping. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Verdict: the M-30's overdriven channel fuzz on bass beat every fuzz pedal he's tried on bass** — called it "the best and cleanest sounding fuzz," a stronger and more specific endorsement than the general "cool fuzz-bass" mention in his mixer-overview video. Reinforces that [[Hofner B-Bass H-Series|Hofner]]-through-mixer-fuzz is a real, repeatable, high-value bass-tone move if Nate ever adds an analog mixer to the chain — not owned today, so this stays wishlist-adjacent rather than an owned-gear technique.
- **Notes the TASCAM M-30 shares its preamp design with the TASCAM 388** (an 8-channel recorder that also appears by name in [[Zach Scholz - Don't Buy a Tape Machine Before Watching This]] as a worked channel-vs-track-count example) — he says the 388 is a piece of gear he's independently had his eye on, tying two separate TASCAM units together by preamp lineage.
- **Future plan floated: running a full drum mix through the same mixer** — not yet done, just stated as a next experiment on his channel.

# Drum programing with Kieron Menzies

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jKoovGB6z50)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jKoovGB6z50)

## Synopsis

Kieron Menzies walks through the session for a Lana Del Rey-style 808 hip-hop beat (built from stock in-the-box TR-808 sounds), demonstrating the exact plugin recipe he used to take generic drum-machine hits and make them sound dark, gritty, and deliberately "not in your face" — a signature of the album he was mixing. The through-line on almost every drum element is the same plugin, the Avid Lo-Fi bitcrusher/sample-rate-reducer, applied element by element (808, snare, hi-hats, vinyl samples) rather than once on a bus, plus a reverse-snare transition effect and a delay-and-reverb "pocket" trick for cymbals on the downbeat. He closes on the drum bus compression, admitting openly that he leans on plugin presets as a starting point rather than dialing from scratch.

## Techniques

- **Lo-Fi (sample-rate reduction) as the drum kit's signature darkening tool, applied per-element rather than on the bus.** Avid Lo-Fi inserted on nearly every drum channel — 808/kick, snare, open hi-hat, closed hi-hat, and the vinyl/hip-hop samples layered over the beat — each instance just lowering the sample rate (not adding the plugin's distortion stage on most of them). Goal stated directly: "everything to be kind of dark... beats there, but just not beats that are in your face." On the 808 specifically, dropping the sample rate turned a clicky, thin hit into something "really dark and gritty," especially when the plugin's own distortion stage is also engaged.
- **Reverse-snare transition effect: a sampled snare hit run through a reverse plugin (Pro Tools Audio Suite Reverse), separated out from the drum machine and given its own delay, for a "swishy," wave-like sweep into the beat.** The engineer pulled the reverse snare out of the TR-8 pattern as its own track and added a dedicated delay to it — a Lo-Fi insert sits on the channel but is bypassed/doing nothing, left over from an earlier pass.
- **Cymbal "pocket" trick: a quarter-note delay (very subtle) plus a long reverb (DVerb, pre-Valhalla era) on any downbeat cymbal hit, every time.** Stated as a standing habit, not song-specific: "When I have a cymbal on the downbeat, especially if it's a sample, I always add like a quarter note delay that's very subtle and a long reverb... that just makes the [cymbal] last a little bit longer... and it puts the cymbal into a pocket." A separate reverse-cymbal element gets a touch of compression instead, just to make it read as lasting longer.
- **Hi-hat filtering matched the same Lo-Fi sample-rate cut as the rest of the kit** — used to shave the "breathy high end" off both open and closed hi-hats so the whole kit stayed consistent with the album's dark, filtered aesthetic; bypassing it makes the hats noticeably brighter.
- **Drum bus compression built from a preset, then tweaked — an openly-admitted workflow habit, not a one-off.** An aux bus carrying the drum elements runs into what sounds like an SSL-style bus comp with a "Heavy Drum" preset loaded, described as "barely" audible on its own but doing real work: "It just kind of fattens the drums, the overall drum picture." His stated method: "I'd find a preset that's pretty close to what I like and then tweak... I'll lower the mix or something." He explicitly defends using presets: "I use presets all the time cuz, you know, plugins come with presets. Use them."
- **Sound selection note:** the goal throughout is a "clicky 808" character rather than a boomy one — a deliberately thin, sample-rate-crushed low end that reads as dark/lo-fi rather than sub-heavy.

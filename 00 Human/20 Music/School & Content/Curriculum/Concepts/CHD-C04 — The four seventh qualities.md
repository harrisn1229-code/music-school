# CHD-C04 — The four seventh qualities

**Q.** Build the four core seventh chords as a grid, and name the two more that come off a diminished triad.

> [!question]- Answer
> The grid is {major or minor triad} × {♭7 or natural 7}: **maj7** (1 3 5 7) · **dominant 7** (1 3 5 ♭7) · **minor 7** (1 ♭3 5 ♭7) · **minor-major 7** (1 ♭3 5 7). On a diminished triad: **half-diminished, m7♭5** (1 ♭3 ♭5 ♭7) and **diminished 7** (1 ♭3 ♭5 ♭♭7).

## Why it matters

The grid is the triad rule with one more third on top, so nothing here is new machinery — but the diatonic placement is worth memorising. **Dominant 7 occurs on exactly one degree of a major key (V) and half-diminished on exactly one (vii)**, so either chord spotted out of context names its parent key. A bare maj7 is ambiguous (I or IV), and a bare m7 worse (ii, iii or vi). Minor-major 7 occurs in neither major nor natural minor — it is the tonic seventh of harmonic and melodic minor — which is why it reads as chromatic and turns up mostly inside a line cliché.

## Drill

Spell all six qualities from C. Then take a diatonic triad progression and convert every chord to its diatonic seventh — the instant jazz version — and name what each one turned into.

# FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire (Ableton / Kontakt) [Twitch]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)

## Synopsis
Alec Maire opens his own Ableton sessions live on Twitch and walks through how he builds cinematic/trailer-music hits, drums, leads, and bass, with a heavy focus on one recurring move: resampling almost everything through a custom "Mick Gordon rack" he rebuilt from watching Mick Gordon's own stream. He shows a hit built from Decimator into layered Action Strikes samples, a full cinematic drum stack combining Damage, a Kontakt library called Medusa (for toms), Strike Force, and a personal pile of whoosh/bang hits from Keepforest and old bounced tracks, and a 9-layer string-stab patch built the same layered-and-resampled way. He demonstrates a velocity-to-volume dynamic-layering trick that makes a stack of samples read as a much bigger ensemble, then breaks down the Mick Gordon rack itself in detail: driving a signal through a console-emulation stage, a compressor, and a color/saturation stage while gain-matching input to output by ear and by analyzer, so the "louder" result is actually a lower-peak, denser signal. He pairs Kilohearts Spiff (added transient) into a hard clip stage for aggressive resampled character, then walks through his mastering bus (an emulated Shadow Hills-style opto+FET parallel compressor built from LA-2A and Distressor, plus Soothe and Pro-L 2) and closes with a full two-layer bass sound-design breakdown — a Diva funk patch driven through Bermuda Triangle and a Moog multi-mode filter, plus a second "solid under layer" widened with a diffuser/spread pair and reinforced with Waves LoAir and Fatso — stressing throughout that speed and finishing a track matters more than obsessing over any single sound.

## Techniques
- **Resample every layered hit through one shared saturation/analog-emulation rack instead of processing each hit separately** — a hit built from Decimator into layered Action Strikes samples gets fully resampled through the "Mick Gordon rack" (see below) so the whole stack glues into one hit rather than reading as separate processed layers; a Gate is added afterward just to stop the sub from leaking/smearing across the hit. → [[Gate]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Cinematic drum stack = Damage + a Kontakt tom library (Medusa) + Strike Force + a personal whoosh/bang hit pile, all layered together** — the full "cinematic drums" sound on this section is several full drum-design tools stacked simultaneously (Damage for one layer, a Kontakt library nicknamed "Medusa" specifically for its toms, Strike Force for another layer) plus a scattering of one-shot whoosh/bang/impact hits pulled from Keepforest sample packs and bounced-down hits from his own older tracks — no single plugin does the whole sound, the stack IS the sound. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Build and maintain a personal library of pre-bounced trailer/cinematic hits, sourced from finished tracks and sample packs, to reuse across sessions** — keeps a wide variety of previously-bounced trailer-music hits on hand (some from Keepforest packs, some literally bounced out of his own past tracks) so a new hit can be assembled from proven material instead of designed from scratch every time. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **A cinematic lead is just Serum with a pitch bend, driven through the same shared resample rack as everything else** — the lead sound on this section has no unusual synthesis; it's a plain Serum patch with a pitch-bend gesture, and its cinematic character comes almost entirely from being driven through the same Mick Gordon rack used on the drums and hits, not from the patch itself. — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **9-layer string-stab stack (strings, Hive, pink noise, more Serum) run through the same rack, then deliberately thinned back down** — a string-stab sound built from roughly nine separate layers (orchestral strings, a Hive synth layer, pink noise, another Serum layer) processed through the Mick Gordon rack the same way as the drums; on review some layers (Medusa, the sticks/no-snares layers) get muted back out because the sound was "over-layered" — more layers isn't automatically better, cut what isn't earning its place. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Velocity-to-volume dynamic layering: map a quiet dynamic sample to play LOUD instead of always quiet, to make a stack read as a much bigger ensemble.** On a multi-layer instrument where velocity only changes sample volume, deliberately playing a normally-quiet dynamic layer at a loud velocity (rather than always triggering it soft) makes the stack sound noticeably "more ensembly," "way way bigger" — demonstrated A/B on a single sustained note. The general principle: layering a quiet dynamic sample with a loud one makes the combined sound read as thicker, not just louder. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Prioritize finishing over perfecting any one sound — track where your session time actually goes.** After analyzing his own habits, he found he'd regularly burn two hours custom-building a "perfect" snare from scratch when a working sample pull would have taken minutes and sounded 95% as good — that time would have been better spent finishing the arrangement and getting every sound in the song roughed in. The framing: prioritize speed and getting what you want quickly over chasing "perfect as if made from scratch," because a finished song with a good arrangement beats one unfinished song with one perfect snare. — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **The "Mick Gordon rack": a rebuilt console-drive → compressor → color chain, run on nearly everything in the track via resampling.** Recreated from watching Mick Gordon's own livestream, the rack drives a signal into a UAD Helios-style console-preamp emulation, then a Fairchild-style compressor, then a Viridian-style color/saturation stage — described as making "a crazy" difference to anything resampled through it (demoed with the rack bypassed vs. engaged on the same trailer hit, night-and-day difference). Everything in this particular track, not just the drums, got resampled through this one rack. — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Gain-match input to output, then drive the pre-input gain right up to the edge of audible clipping and back off "just a touch," verified per-stage on an analyzer.** The core workflow for driving the console/compressor rack: match the processed output level to the unprocessed input level first (so you're judging tone, not level), then push the pre-stage's input gain up until it crosses into obviously "too clippy"/uncomfortable distortion, then pull back the smallest amount so it's "just getting shaved" — done per bend/stage of the chain individually (not once for the whole signal) for accuracy, checked against a level/analyzer meter at each step rather than trusted by ear alone. → [[Clipping and Loudness]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Add a ton of transient (Kilohearts Spiff), then clip it (a clip-control/trash-style stage) — a "god combination" that reads louder while measuring quieter on the meter.** Pushing a huge amount of extra transient into a signal with Spiff and then clipping that transient-heavy signal produces a result that visibly measures LESS peak amplitude on a level meter than the unprocessed version, yet perceptually reads as noticeably louder — because clipping brings the peaks together/closer, the ear reads density as loudness even though the numeric peak level is lower. → [[Kilohearts Spiff]], [[Clipping and Loudness]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Emulate a Shadow Hills-style parallel mastering bus with UAD LA-2A (silver) and a Distressor run more aggressively than the real hardware combo, on advice from a mix engineer who works with Skrillex and Zedd.** The master chain approximates the classic Shadow Hills Mastering Compressor pairing (an opto compressor plus a fast compressor) but pushed more aggressive than the real unit — an LA-2A silver-face emulation for the opto stage, and a Distressor emulation used specifically in parallel (dry signal dropped out, wet blended back in) per direct advice from a friend who mixes for Skrillex and Zedd, who runs his own Distressor the same way and dials it to roughly a 9 o'clock position on the blend. → [[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Master bus finishing chain: Soothe ("a bit less would do" preset, moderate settings) into Pro-L 2 — and the real key to loudness is often using FEWER, better-gain-staged instruments, not more processing.** After the LA-2A/Distressor parallel stage, the master gets a Soothe pass (specifically the "a bit less would do" preset, dialed to fairly moderate settings) and then a Pro-L 2 limiter. Explicit takeaway: a lot of loudness comes from arrangement discipline — fewer competing instruments, everything properly gain-staged and glued/limited — rather than from stacking more mastering plugins. → [[Soothe2]], [[Pro-L 2]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Bass layer 1 ("low end"): a funk synth patch driven through a heavy-distortion device and a multi-mode filter used for BOTH drive and a deliberate resonance peak.** Starting from a plain funk-style patch (originally chosen for having no real low end of its own), it's driven through a heavy analog-style distortion device (nicknamed "the Bermuda Triangle" in the stream, makes it "sound distorted as hell") and then a multi-mode filter (Moog-style) used for two jobs at once — overall drive/saturation, and a resonance peak dialed to boost one specific frequency area for character, confirmed by toggling it off and hearing the peak disappear. → [[Bass Design]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Bass layer 1 continued: flanger-as-EQ + a mild high-boost cleanup, run in two parallel plugin instances rather than one.** After the distortion/filter stage, a flanger-type device (Androsol) is used less for an obvious flange effect and more as a subtle tone/movement shaper (confirmed A/B — it reads as light distortion character rather than an audible flange when used this way), then more EQ tames the highs, split oddly across two separate instances of the same taming plugin (Fat Trader) rather than one, plus a touch of Soothe to settle remaining resonance before a MicroShift is added at the bus level purely to add a little stereo width to the whole low layer. → [[MicroShift]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Bass layer 2 ("solid under layer"): distortion for width, a diffuser + a separate stereo-spread plugin stacked together for extra dimension, then Waves LoAir to reinforce the sub and Fatso for gentle analog-style warmth.** A second, distinct bass layer runs its own distortion pass for width and a bit of grit, gets its highs boosted, then two dedicated stereo-imaging devices stacked back to back — a diffuser-style plugin (Quattro Mod, "has this really cool diffuser in it") and a separate spread plugin (Shred Spread) — for noticeably more width than either alone (demoed with/without). A light Soothe pass, then Waves LoAir specifically to reinforce sub content that the layer was otherwise light on, and finally Fatso, used mainly for its Warmth control (pushed to ~5, described as functioning "like an analog low-pass") rather than its compressor, which is only lightly tapped. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)
- **Bass bus routing: multiple buses in series — a Distressor + extra Soothe + a UAD sub-enhancer plugin on the first bus, then the full Mick Gordon chain (Helios → Fairchild → Viridian → Spiff → clip control) on the whole combined bass bus.** The two bass layers each feed into a first bus carrying a Distressor emulation, another Soothe pass, and a UAD plugin specifically used to enhance/reinforce sub content a touch — then that combined signal goes through the SAME Mick Gordon-style rack used everywhere else in the track (console-drive harmonics via a Helios-style stage → Fairchild-style compression → Viridian-style color → Spiff transient shaping → a clip-control/trash-style stage taming those transients), tying the bass's processing back to the same signature chain used on the drums, hits, lead, and strings. → [[Kilohearts Spiff]] — Alec Maire (Sol State), [FAT DRUMS MASTERCLASS with Alec Maire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_87oH2XsaqI)

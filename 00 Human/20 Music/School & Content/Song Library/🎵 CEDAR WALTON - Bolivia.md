# Bolivia — Cedar Walton

> Jazz standard, changes-only breakdown (no melody — the source video skips it for copyright reasons). Nathan Borton's approach: reduce the tune's four sections to a small set of already-known vocabulary buckets (5-1s, a tritone-sub 5-1, one long static-minor stretch, short closing 2-5s) rather than treating each chord change as a unique event. → [[How To SOLO Over Bolivia! (Cedar Walton)]]

## Chords
**Key:** no single tonic — a four-section form threading through several key centers, framed front and back by a static dominant vamp (Wikipedia describes the tune as composed "in G major" around a 16-bar G7(13) vamp).

**A section (16 bars):** a **modal vamp** — the whole section sits on **G7(13)**, Mixolydian, no chord change, no cadence.

**B section (16 bars), by function** (section numbers below match Nathan Borton's own breakdown, referenced in *Solo concepts* further down):

**Section 1:** E♭ major (tonic) → **A7 → D** (a 5-1 into the key of D)

**Section 2:** **A♭7 → G** — a tritone substitution standing in for the diatonic D7 → G (D7 is a tritone from A♭7)

**Section 3 (the long stretch, reduces almost entirely to B minor):** Bm7 → C Lydian → Bm7 → Bm7/A → A♭m7♭5 (half-diminished)

**Section 4 (closing run):** a 2-5 into F major → a tritone sub into B♭ major (also playable as a plain 2-5) → a 5-1 into Dm7

**Turnback into the closing vamp:** A7 → G7 (see *Solo concepts* below — this is the tune's trickiest single move)

**The literal chord symbols behind the above:** E♭maj7 → A13 (V7 of the next key) → Dmaj7 (I, a clean 5–1) → A♭7 (tritone sub standing in for D7, itself V of the next key) → Gmaj7 (I) → F♯7♯9 → Bm7 → C7♯11 → Bm7 → Bm7/A → A♭m7♭5 → Gm7 → C7 (a genuine **ii–V** into F) → Fmaj7 (I) → B7 (tritone sub setting up B♭) → B♭maj7 → A7 (V7, turning the form back around into the top G7 vamp). This lines up with the vault's own key-center summary above (E♭ → D → G → B minor → F/B♭/D minor). See [[The ii-V-I]], [[Chord Substitution]].

## Solo concepts by chord (Nathan Borton's breakdown)

- **Over A7 → D (section 1):** either (a) play the two key centers — E♭ major ideas, then **D♭ major ideas over both the A7 and the D** (keep the same color running through the dominant); or (b) engage the 5-1 directly — A7 ideas resolving to D major 7 ideas. → [[The ii-V-I]]
- **Over A♭7 → G (section 2):** either (a) play the actual tritone-sub progression — A♭7 ideas resolving to G major 7; or (b) ignore the sub and just play a plain 5-1 (as if it were D7 → G) — still works, since the sub shares the same V-to-I pull. → [[Chord Substitution]] § *2. Tritone substitution*
- **Over the section-3 stretch:** think **B minor** almost the whole way through — Bm7 (straight); the **C Lydian chord** with **B minor 7 pentatonic** (the two sounds overlap because B minor pentatonic sits diatonically inside the C Lydian/G major collection); Bm7 again; **Bm7/A** ("the exact same sound" as plain Bm7, just a different bass note — no vocabulary change needed); and the **A♭m7♭5** with **B melodic minor** — rule given: go up to the chord's own ♭3 (A♭'s ♭3 is B) and play melodic minor rooted there. Matches [[Scale Modes Reference]] § *Melodic Minor*'s own table exactly: the vi degree of a melodic-minor scale is a half-diminished chord, so "melodic minor off the ♭3" and "play the vi-chord's parent melodic minor" are the same fact. → [[Scale Modes Reference]]
- **Over the section-4 closing run:** short 2-5 licks and short minor-2-5 licks dropped straight in across the 2-5-into-F, the tritone-sub-into-B♭ (or its plain-2-5 fallback), and the final 5-1 into Dm7. → [[The ii-V-I]], [[Chord Substitution]]
- **Over the A7 → G7 turnback (the payoff move of the lesson):** since G7 is the V of a 2-5-1 in the key of C, its related ii is Dm7, and **a ii chord and its V are functionally the same sound** — so the whole A7 → G7 move can be reheard as **A7 → D minor, i.e. a plain 5-1**, folding an awkward-looking dominant-to-dominant move into vocabulary already built for a 5-1. → [[The ii-V-I]] § *Guitar shortcut: the power-chord finder*, [[Chord Substitution]] § *From videos*

## Steal this

- **Reduce a scary-looking tune to a handful of known sound-buckets before trying to solo it.** Four separately-intimidating sections collapse to "5-1 lines and static-minor sounds" once you stop treating every chord change as its own unique event.
- **A ii chord and its V are interchangeable soloing material** — recognizing a tricky dominant-to-dominant move (A7 → G7 here) as secretly "dominant → [V's related ii]" turns it into ordinary 5-1 vocabulary. → [[The ii-V-I]]
- **Melodic minor off a half-diminished chord's own ♭3** as a quick, no-counting way to find the extension-rich scale for a min7♭5 chord. → [[Scale Modes Reference]]
- **Categorize transcribed licks by type as you learn them** (5-1 / 2-5-1 / static-minor) so you have a ready bucket to reach into instead of re-deriving vocabulary tune-by-tune. → [[Bebop Vocabulary]]

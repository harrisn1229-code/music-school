# Can't Buy Me Love — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A standard long form carrying something that isn't standard at all — **the verse is a strict twelve-bar blues and the intro, outro and refrain are pointedly not** ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]). The verse uses only I, IV and V with a melody restricted to ♭3 and ♭7; the intro and outro lean on iii and vi with a melody that insists on the natural third. The refrain then sets the two worlds against each other in alternation.

**Form.** The intro is an unusual **6 bars** because the verse arrives early and elides it — the two bars that would square it off are simply cut, and they do get played, in the outro. Verses are strict 12-bar frames. The refrain is a square 8, four 2-bar phrases, with the lyric in an AB-AC pattern the music mirrors. An **extra bar is inserted** before the guitar-solo verse — the reverse of the elision gambit, flagging the solo and throwing the count off.

**Harmony.** The intro is *in medias res* and audacious about it: the home key isn't confirmed until bar 6, and every chord before it is minor, while the melody spells the tonic triad as literally as a bugle call. Blue-note cross-relations carry the verse ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]) and appoggiaturas supply most of the remaining spice over otherwise bare triads ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]). **The single most distinctive detail** is the bluesy ♭3 dropped into bar 3 of the refrain, surrounded by natural thirds on both sides.

**Arrangement.** Sizzling cymbals everywhere *except* the intro and outro — texture used purely to articulate form. The ensemble cuts out for an instant right after the downbeat of verse bar 10, and the silence reads as an accent. George's solo sounds improvised and isn't; the live versions prove it was worked out.

# Out of Nowhere — Johnny Green

> A tune whose real subject, per Jens Larsen's analysis, is **how the "outside" sound of E♭7 works inside G major** — it shows up twice, for two different structural reasons, and both times it's the same **sharp-4 double-diminished / German augmented sixth** sound. Two static two-bar zones (the opening G major7, and the A minor cadence chord later on) are loose enough at a medium tempo to support real modal/upper-structure triad exploration, which is what makes this tune a good vehicle for triad-based soloing practice specifically.

## Chords

- **Bars 1–2:** **G major7** (I), static — first of two loose, modal-feeling zones in the form.
- **Bars 3–4:** **B♭m7 → E♭7**, a 2-5 into A♭, resolving back to **G major7**. The E♭7 here is a **sharp-4 double-diminished chord** — Larsen's term, equivalent to the **German augmented sixth chord** — closely related to **C♯ diminished** (double-diminish the 3rd, E→E♭, and the same voicing an octave up reads as E♭7). → [[Augmented Sixth Chords]]
- **Bars 5–6:** **B minor → E7 altered**, a minor-cadence secondary dominant.
- **Bars 7–8:** **A minor** (ii) — the cadence target, and the second static/loose zone in the form.
- **Bars 9–10:** **E♭7** again — this time functioning as the **tritone substitute of A7** (the "dominant of the dominant" spot). Same chord symbol as bars 3–4, different function — described by Larsen as the tune's whole point: E♭7 working two different ways in the same key.
- **Bars 11–12:** effectively a **D-sub → D7** turnaround, closing the first half's dominant motion.
- **Second half:** repeats the same shape — G major7, the same B♭m7-E♭7 double-diminished turnaround, then G major → B minor → dominant → A minor cadence again — except the final turnaround uses a **flat-3 diminished chord** (another inversion of the same sharp-4-diminished sound) into a final 2-5 that **actually resolves to the tonic** this time (the first half only reached a half cadence).

## Steal this

- **A dominant chord's function can change on repeat — same chord symbol, different job.** E♭7 appears twice in the form: once as a sharp-4 double-diminished predominant resolving straight back to I, once as a tritone substitute for A7 (dominant of the dominant). Same sound, different structural role — worth checking for on any tune with a repeated "unusual" chord. → [[Augmented Sixth Chords]] · [[Chord Substitution]]
- **Static two-bar chords are an invitation to go modal**, at a medium tempo — the tonic G major7 and the A minor cadence chord both get the full Lydian/Lydian-augmented/augmented-scale (or melodic-minor) treatment specifically because there's time to establish the color before moving on. A fast-moving 2-5 doesn't need the same treatment — it's already interesting on its own.
- **Cross-chord half-step motif:** a triad idea built on G major7 (the B minor triad) is directly reusable a half step down on the following B♭m7-E♭7 — using a shape as a transposable motif rather than inventing new material per chord creates continuity across a phrase. → [[Triad Melodic Devices for Jazz Soloing]]
- **One-note scale-connection trick:** G major7's own scale and the G melodic minor used over the following (Lydian-dominant) C7 differ by exactly one note (B → B♭) — swap that one note in an existing line to bridge the chord change almost for free. → [[Triad Melodic Devices for Jazz Soloing]]

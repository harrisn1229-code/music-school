# Your Left Hand SUCKS? THIS Is The Solution! (Guitar Lesson)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SkYdmYyfw5s)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SkYdmYyfw5s)

## Synopsis

A pinky-finger-control workout built around a stated practice philosophy — isolate the specific weak spot rather than drilling generically — with a chromatic exercise that layers complexity one step at a time: picked, then hammer-on added, then pull-off added, then a final continuous-eighth-notes variation.

## Techniques

- **Diagnosed problem: "flying pinky."** After finishing a shape on one string and moving to the next, the pinky drifts away from the fretboard (traveling a real physical distance back in) rather than staying close — directly counterproductive to building speed, since it has to travel further each rep. Self-diagnosis method: film practice from an angle that shows the fretting hand clearly, to catch the drift visually.
- **Core practice-design principle, stated directly as a favorite:** keep an exercise as small and minimal as possible at first, then add complexity in layers only once the base version is solid — rather than starting with the full hard version.
- **Base exercise:** a 4-note A minor pattern (frets 5-7-8 on the low E string, 5-7 on the A string) picked slowly, moved chromatically up through all six strings, focused purely on keeping the pinky close to the fretboard rather than letting it drift after each string change.
- **Layer 1 — add a hammer-on.** Only 2 picking strokes total per repeated shape (one per string); the rest of each string's notes are hammered — same chromatic movement across strings.
- **Layer 2 — add a pull-off after the hammer-on.** Same shape, now including a pull-off; explicit caution against a large pull-off motion that grazes neighboring strings (a common source of unwanted noise) — start in one fixed position (E and A strings only) before moving the shape across the full neck.
- **Layer 3 (hardest variation) — add one more hammer-on to fill the remaining 8th-note rest**, producing a continuous, gapless eighth-note stream with no picking at all inside each shape beyond the first note per string.
- **Sound-quality target throughout:** every hammered/pulled note should be as audible and evenly articulated as the picked notes — the goal is that a listener can't tell which notes were picked versus legato'd, not a technically-correct-but-uneven stream.
- **Light touch, not force**, on both the hammer-ons and pull-offs — heavy pressure fights a clean, fast, effortless-sounding result.

# 18 Songs That 'Rip Off' Other Hits

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FHx3l6Iu47k)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FHx3l6Iu47k)

## Synopsis

A catalog of song pairs with audible similarities, framed through an explicit plagiarism-vs-influence spectrum: generic building blocks (I-IV-V chords, common blues progressions) can't be owned, but a deliberate melodic/structural lift that gets developed into something new is influence, not theft — with legal outcomes (or their absence) cited for most pairs. The most useful standalone concept is **interpolation**, introduced via the Portugal. The Man / "Please Mr. Postman" case.

## Techniques

- **Interpolation, defined and distinguished from sampling**: re-recording/replaying a melody (or part of one) from an existing song rather than using the original audio — Portugal. The Man's "Feel It Still" reworks the "Please Mr. Postman" melody this way and pre-cleared/credited the original writers before release, rather than treating it as a sample. → [[Sample Clearance]] (already documents the sampling-vs-interpolation legal distinction in depth; this video adds a second worked pre-clearance example).
- **The plagiarism-vs-influence test, stated as a spectrum rather than a binary**: a new song that "obviously copies the basic character or elements" of a source lands as plagiarism; a song that uses a source as a *starting point* but develops it "into something new and interesting and separate" lands as influence. Generic building blocks (I-IV-V, common blues turnarounds, "the basic blues" progression) are explicitly ruled out as ownable at all — cited defenses from Def Leppard (re: One Direction's "Midnight Memories") and The Who's Pete Townshend (re: One Direction's "Best Song Ever") both make exactly this argument.
- **A borrowed riff can itself be a chain of prior borrows, not a single original source**: "Grease"'s "Summer Nights" riff (itself echoed in One Direction's "That's What Makes You Beautiful") is built from the same generic I-IV-V blocks as, and was likely itself drawn from, an earlier song, "Hang On Sloopy" (The McCoys) — worth remembering that "who ripped off whom" chains can go back further than the two songs being compared.
- **A sourcing method some professional songwriting teams reportedly use deliberately**: splicing a recognizable fragment of a classic song into a new commercial pop song, specifically to lend it "a sense of authenticity or timelessness" and/or generate discussion/controversy that helps popularize the release — framed as a possible intentional strategy behind several One Direction examples, not just coincidental influence.

## Catalog (song pairs cited, roughly in the order discussed)

One Direction "Midnight Memories" ↔ Def Leppard "Pour Some Sugar on Me" (I-IV-V blues chords; no legal action, Def Leppard attributes similarity to production/vocals not composition) · One Direction "Best Song Ever" ↔ The Who "Baba O'Riley" (same 3 common pop chords; Pete Townshend declined to pursue) · One Direction "Live While We're Young" ↔ The Clash "Should I Stay or Should I Go" (Harry Styles admitted the riff was intentionally lifted; no lawsuit) · One Direction "That's What Makes You Beautiful" ↔ Grease's "Summer Nights" (intro riff, admitted) ↔ The McCoys "Hang On Sloopy" (Summer Nights' likely own source) · Green Day "Warning" ↔ The Kinks "Picture Book" (guitar riff) · Green Day "Brain Stew" ↔ Chicago "25 or 6 to 4" (opening riff) · Green Day "Hold On" ↔ Beatles "I Should Have Known Better" (harmonica intro) · Red Hot Chili Peppers "Californication" [named in video as "Dani California"] ↔ Tom Petty "Mary Jane's Last Dance" (Petty acknowledged, declined to sue) ↔ The Jayhawks "Waiting for the Sun" (Mary Jane's Last Dance's own likely source — the Jayhawks opened for Petty in 1992, a year before its 1993 release) · Nirvana "Come As You Are" ↔ Killing Joke "Eighties" (melodically similar riffs, both chorus-pedal-effected) · The Jam "Start!" ↔ Beatles "Taxman" (near-identical bassline, one extra note added; reused again on B-side "Liza Radley") · Beatles "Sun King" ↔ Fleetwood Mac "Albatross" (same key, similar guitar/bass/drum tone and parts — George Harrison confirmed the intent: "let's be Fleetwood Mac doing Albatross") · The Dodgy "Staying Out for the Summer" ↔ Blue Öyster Cult "(Don't Fear) The Reaper" (melodically identical opening two beats, similar guitar tone) · Bruno Mars "Treasure" ↔ Breakbot "Baby I'm Yours" (chorus melodic hook; Breakbot called it a rip-off but said they were "cool with it," citing their own borrowing habits) · Portugal. The Man "Feel It Still" ↔ The Marvelettes "Please Mr. Postman" (interpolation, pre-cleared and credited) · Lewis Capaldi "Someone You Loved" ↔ Dagny "Someday" (melodic/structural similarity, widely noted by listeners) · The Hollies "The Air That I Breathe" (Albert Hammond) ↔ Radiohead "Creep" — the one case here that actually went to court: Hammond was awarded a co-writing credit and a third of Creep's royalties, though by his account it was his publisher, not him personally, who pursued it ("all these lawsuits... I think it's silly... it shouldn't be about money").

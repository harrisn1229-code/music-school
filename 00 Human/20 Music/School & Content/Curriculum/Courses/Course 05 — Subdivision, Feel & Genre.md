# Course 05 — Subdivision, Feel & Genre

Rhythm as a set of nameable choices rather than a thing you find by feel: how a bar divides, where a part sits against the grid, and the three parameters that separate one genre's drums from another's.

**What it assumes**

- [[Course 01 — Derivation Rules]] — the floor the whole school stands on.
- [[Course 03 — Scale Construction]] — Lesson 3 compares grooves under real material, which means being able to spell what's playing over them.

**What this unlocks**

- [[Course 08 — Melody Writing]] — a melody is a rhythm before it's a set of pitches, and the course lists this one as a prerequisite for exactly that reason.
- [[Course 10 — Drum Programming & Beat Construction]] and [[Course 11 — Bass Writing & Design]] — both are this vocabulary applied inside a clip. [[Bassline Writing]] and [[Bass Design]] assume you can name a subdivision and a feel.
- Harmonic rhythm — how often chords change — is this material pointed at chords instead of drums.
- Practically: being able to name a groove is what turns it into a decision you can repeat, rather than one you got lucky with.

> [!info] Naming what you can already hear
> Most of this strand is documented in [[Time Signatures & Note Values]] and [[Rhythm and Groove]] and takes almost nothing new to learn — it takes *retrieval*, which is what the spaced repetition is for. Expect the course to feel more like recognition than instruction. The exception is hemiola in Lesson 2, which is genuinely new ground.

## The lessons

### Lesson 1 — Meter and Subdivision

**Read**
- [ ] [[Time Signatures & Note Values]] → *Note-value subdivision*, *Strong/weak beat hierarchy*, *Simple, compound & complex meter*
- [ ] [[Syncopation and Metric Displacement]] → *Syncopation*

**The material**
- Two ideas, and the second only exists because of the first: how a bar divides, and what it means to play against that division.
- **[[RHY-C01 — Compound meter is felt in dotted beats|Compound time signatures name the division, not the beat]].** In 6/8 the top number counts eighths. Divide by 3 and the real answer appears: **two dotted-quarter beats.** Six eighths written, two beats felt — and the felt half is the one you play from.
- Under any meter sits a **[[RHY-C09 — Meter ranks every position strong to weak|strong/weak hierarchy]]** — beat 1 strongest, then 3, then the backbeats, then the offbeats between them. That hierarchy is the thing that makes a placement sound "on" or "off" at all.
- **[[RHY-C02 — Syncopation|Syncopation]] is emphasis where the meter doesn't expect it.** Three forms of the same move: accent a weak position, place a note on a weak position and **tie it over** the strong one, or **rest** where the arrival is expected.
- The condition that's easy to skip: **the meter has to stay in the listener's head for syncopation to mean anything.** Remove the steady pulse and there's nothing left to play against — it just sounds arbitrary.

**Concepts**
- [[RHY-C01 — Compound meter is felt in dotted beats]]
- [[RHY-C09 — Meter ranks every position strong to weak]]
- [[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]

**Homework**
- [ ] **Review** — count a 6/8 bar out loud in six, then in two. Then do the same for 9/8 and 12/8, naming the beat count and beat value before you start.
- [ ] **Apply** (~15 min) — program a straight 16th hi-hat pattern, then make three syncopated versions with velocity alone, not moving a single note. Then loop a 6/8 groove and count it out loud in six, and again in two, while it plays.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 05 — Lesson 1 Test]]

### Lesson 2 — Feel and Microtiming

**Read**
- [ ] [[Rhythm and Groove]] → *Subdivision: swing vs triplets vs offbeat*
- [ ] [[Pocket and Microtiming]] in full
- [ ] [[Syncopation and Metric Displacement]] → *Hemiola*, *Polyrhythm*

**The material**
- Lesson 1 was where notes are allowed to land. This lesson is the gap between that grid and where they actually land — which is where almost all of "feel" lives.
- **[[RHY-C03 — Swing is a triplet with the middle note gone|Swing lengthens the first eighth of each pair and shortens the second]]**, leaning toward a triplet feel. A shuffle is that triplet with the middle note removed: long, short.
- Full triplet swing is exactly 2:1 — the long note takes two of the beat's three triplet parts, the short note one — while swing sits anywhere between straight (1:1) and that full triplet, which is why swing amount is a dial rather than a switch.
- **[[RHY-C06 — Pocket: laying back vs pushing|Pocket is where a part sits relative to the grid]]** — consistently, and on purpose. Late reads relaxed and heavy; early reads urgent and driving. The usable range is about **5–30 ms**, and past 40 it stops reading as feel and starts reading as a mistake.
- The move that actually creates pocket is elements **disagreeing**: kick and snare on the grid, hats behind. Everything equally late just sounds slow — pocket is a relationship, not an offset.
- **[[RHY-C08 — Quantize, humanize, groove templates|Quantize, humanize and groove templates]]** are the controls for that. Quantize snaps to the grid, for anything meant to be machine-tight. Humanize adds timing and velocity variation, for anything meant to read as played. Neither belongs at 100% — **50–75% strength** pulls a performance toward the grid while keeping its feel.
- A **groove template** stores the timing and velocity offsets of one piece of material so you can apply them to another. That's the payoff: lift the feel off a break you like and put it onto your own stiff MIDI.
- **[[RHY-C04 — Polyrhythm and hemiola|Polyrhythm stacks subdivisions; hemiola regroups them]].** Polyrhythm is two conflicting divisions at once — classically three against two. Hemiola is three groups of two against two groups of three: the same pulse, reorganised so a different meter is implied. Count 3-against-2 with *"nice cup of tea."*

**Concepts**
- [[RHY-C03 — Swing is a triplet with the middle note gone]]
- [[RHY-C04 — Polyrhythm and hemiola]]
- [[RHY-C06 — Pocket: laying back vs pushing]]
- [[RHY-C08 — Quantize, humanize, groove templates]]

**Homework**
- [ ] **Review** — set a swing preset to 0%, 50% and maximum on a hat pattern and name what changed. Then clap 3-against-2 with *"nice cup of tea"* until it locks without counting.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — a repeating dotted-quarter hit across a 4/4 loop three bars or longer. Let it drift out of phase and hear it realign on the downbeat every three bars — eight dotted quarters to twenty-four eighths — that's hemiola with a clock attached.
- [ ] **Apply** (~15 min) — take a hip hop loop and nudge only the weak-beat hits 10–15 ms late, leaving strong beats on the grid. Then extract the groove from a break you like and apply it to the same drums. Keep the one you'd actually use.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 05 — Lesson 2 Test]]

### Lesson 3 — Genre as Three Parameters

**Read**
- [ ] [[Genre Drum Patterns]] in full

**The material**
- **Three parameters explain nearly every genre distinction in drums: tempo, backbeat placement and hi-hat subdivision.** Kit choice and processing come after — these three decide what the pattern *is*.
- **[[RHY-C05 — Half-time|Half-time moves the backbeat to beat 3]]** instead of 2 and 4, so the groove feels half as fast at the same tempo: written at 140, felt at ~70. One moved hit does that much because the ear judges groove speed almost entirely from snare placement.
- That's the whole trick behind trap — fast enough for busy hi-hats and slow enough to rap over, at the same time.
- **[[RHY-C07 — Boom-bap vs trap|Boom-bap against trap]]** is the comparison worth knowing cold. **Boom-bap:** ~85–95 BPM, backbeat on 2 and 4, straight 8th hats with an offbeat open hat, swung and laid back, sampled drums. **Trap:** written ~130–145 but felt half-time, snare on 3, 16th hats with 32nd and triplet rolls, tight kit and loose hats.
- "Trap hats are triplets" is the usual shorthand and it misses the structure: the bed is 16ths, and the 32nds and triplets are **rolls layered on top**. And in trap the **808 is the kick and the bass at once** — one element doing two jobs, which is why it gets tuned to the key.

**Concepts**
- [[RHY-C05 — Half-time]]
- [[RHY-C07 — Boom-bap vs trap]]

**Homework**
- [ ] **Review** — take any 140 BPM beat with a snare on 2 and 4 and move the snare to beat 3 only. Nothing else changes. Listen to the tempo appear to halve, then say what the backbeat is doing.
- [ ] **Apply** (~15 min) — program eight bars of boom-bap and eight of trap from one empty clip, same kit. Change only tempo, backbeat placement and hat subdivision, then play both and confirm nothing else was needed.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 05 — Lesson 3 Test]]

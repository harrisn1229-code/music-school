# loner - i made beats WITHOUT VSTs for an entire week!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gZMSr8CjqOc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gZMSr8CjqOc)

## Synopsis

A seven-day constraint challenge: loner picks 10 sounds (bell, pad, perc, kick, bass, rim, hi-hat, snare, 808, lead) and makes an entire beat with only those, no VST instruments, adding a new gimmick each day to force fresh problem-solving once the "same 10 sounds" limitation alone stopped being hard. Day 1 is a straightforward cook-up establishing the palette (reverse, delay, a chord switch-up, layered counter-melodies). Day 2 spins a genre wheel (pop) and the standout move is turning a rim hit into a workable snare purely by processing/context rather than swapping samples. Day 3 builds the beat in reverse stage order (drums → counter-melody → chords last) with a delayed first-chord entrance and portamento glide. Day 4 spins an artist wheel (21 Savage) and runs a synth pad through a guitar amp for grit — a texture move loner calls something he'd never tried before. Day 5 is a hard per-sound time-limit round (as little as ~14 seconds per sound) — the standout finding is negative: panic under a tight timer pushed him toward the safest, most obvious choice for each sound rather than a creative one. Day 6 removes MIDI entirely — every part recorded in as live audio takes, no clicking in notes — and a botched hi-hat take gets rescued by recording it slower than tempo, then speeding the audio back up afterward. Day 7 combines several handicaps at once (random BPM, a longer 35-minute time box, "reuse one sound twice," and "one VST allowed") and is loner's favorite result of the week — the closing thesis is that moderate constraints (limited sound pool, a longer time box) sharpened creativity, while the extreme one-minute-per-sound constraint actively hurt it by inducing panic rather than experimentation. Light on hard settings (a fast, narrated edit with garbled auto-captions), so several tips below are recorded qualitatively rather than with exact numbers.

## Techniques

**Day 1 — establishing the 10-sound palette**
- **Reverse a sample as a first move to make a stock sound feel personal before any other processing.** → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Reach for a built-in delay early and often as a go-to texture/space tool across the whole challenge**, not saved for a mix-polish pass. → [[Delay]]
- **A mid-phrase chord switch-up** to keep a short loop from feeling static.
- **The pad is called out as "key for adding textures throughout the week"** — treat one flexible pad sound as the connective texture element across an entire beat/session rather than a single-purpose part. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Layer counter-melodies with the bell and the pad specifically**, on top of the main chord/lead material, to fill out the arrangement from a small sound pool. → [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Stretch a sample, then build the kick/perc/bass on top of it** as the day's finishing sequence.

**Day 2 — genre-wheel (pop): turning a rim into a snare**
- **A rim hit repurposed as a snare, discovered by trying it in context rather than by swapping in a different sample** — after getting stuck on what to use for a snare with only a rim available as a hi-hat-adjacent sound, trying the rim in the snare slot revealed it read convincingly as one. The general lesson stated on camera: manipulating/recontextualizing a sound reveals how far you can take it, beyond what the sound "is" on paper. → [[Snare Design]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Ascending chords on a bell voice for a "pop piano" read**, used as the closest available substitute when no piano/keys sound is in the limited pool — picking the sound in the kit whose character best approximates the missing timbre rather than forcing an unrelated one. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A half-time pad under a triplet-bounce key part**, mixing a slow-moving texture layer against a faster rhythmic figure in the same section.
- **When there's no dedicated riser sound available, use the pad itself as the riser/drop cue** — reach for whatever texture element is already in the palette and repurpose its behavior (a swell/rise) rather than treating "no riser" as a dead end. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Switching up the drum bounce/pattern when a beat feels stuck, rather than reworking the melodic material**, is what got Day 2 unstuck after the kick placement stalled progress.

**Day 3 — building a beat backwards + portamento**
- **Build the beat in reverse stage order: drums first, then the counter-melody, then the chords last** — inverts the usual chords/melody-first workflow specifically to force different decisions than the normal writing order produces (loner calls this his favorite beat of the week). → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Delay a chord's first entrance so it doesn't land on the downbeat with everything else** — holding the first chord back briefly (rather than pad/lead/chords all landing together) gives the entrance its own moment. *(general starting point — no exact timing given)* → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Automation]]
- **Portamento/glide added to a chord for a pitch-slide-in character**, layered in after the chord's held-back entrance above. → [[Modulation]]
- **8-way (heavily subdivided) drum-and-melody variation used deliberately as a creativity prompt** — imposing an odd rhythmic-division constraint on a section specifically to force unfamiliar phrasing choices. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]

**Day 4 — artist-wheel (21 Savage): amp-on-synth texture**
- **Run a synth pad through a guitar amp for a distorted, grittier texture — explicitly a first-time move for loner ("I've never done that before").** Used on a pad as the opening element of the beat, described afterward as "hard" and "wire"-sounding. Owned equivalent: [[Morgan Amps Suite]] — a guitar-amp-sim already documented running on non-guitar sources (a cloned vocal layer) for the same cut-through/grit purpose. → [[Morgan Amps Suite]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Half-time the amped pad** after the initial distorted pass, changing its rhythmic feel without changing the sound source.
- **Distortion on the drums for a gritty character**, matching the artist's harder-edged aesthetic rather than a clean kit sound. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Reverse AND pitch down a melody line, then layer a second copy of the same melody pitched UP an octave on top** — one source part split into two register-contrasted, oppositely-treated layers instead of doubling it plainly. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Deliberately avoiding the "obvious" genre cliché (a dark piano-led 21 Savage-style beat) by leaning on the limited sound pool instead** — the constraint itself is credited with pushing toward a less predictable arrangement than free choice would have produced.

**Day 5 — hard time limit per sound (as low as ~14 seconds)**
- **A tight per-element time box (as little as ~14–30 seconds per sound) reliably produces the safest, most obvious choice rather than a creative one — a negative finding, not a technique to copy.** Panic under the clock repeatedly pushed toward "the sound I'd normally reach for" instead of experimentation; contrasted directly against Day 7's looser 35-minute box, which didn't have this effect. Worth remembering when setting self-imposed creative constraints: a moderate time limit sharpens focus, an extreme one collapses it into autopilot.
- **Heavy delay stacked with a second delay, then pitched up, with feedback pushed, as a fast way to turn one sound into a distinct texture element under time pressure.** *(general starting point — no exact feedback/time values given, described qualitatively as "super heavy")* → [[Delay]]
- **Reverb added specifically to control a sound's low end/eat up excess low-mid energy**, not purely for space — used as a quick low-end tamer on a low synth-key part rather than reaching for EQ under time pressure.

**Day 6 — no MIDI, live audio takes only**
- **Every element recorded as a live audio take rather than clicked-in MIDI, including the hi-hats** — removes MIDI's editability/quantize safety net entirely, testing what a fully performed (not programmed) version of the constraint set sounds like. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Latency-Free Tracking]]
- **When a live-audio hi-hat take comes out too sloppy/inconsistent to use as recorded, play the pattern back at a slower tempo, record that slower take, then speed the resulting audio clip back up afterward** — the same performance becomes tighter/more consistent at a reduced tempo, and speeding it back up in post recovers a usable take that direct real-time playing couldn't produce. A practical workaround specifically for the "record only, no MIDI edit" constraint, but usable any time a live take is rhythmically shaky rather than re-recording it from scratch. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Latency-Free Tracking]]
- **Consolidate/shorten the fixed take once it's usable**, then quickly layer in rim, perc, and kick around the salvaged hi-hat pass before finishing with the 808.

**Day 7 — combined handicaps (random BPM, 35-min box, "reuse a sound twice," "one VST allowed")**
- **Random BPM generator (landed on 125) used as a starting-point constraint** rather than picking a familiar/comfortable tempo. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **"One VST allowed" handicap spent on Arturia Analog Lab, specifically its "Technicolor" preset bank, for a pad element** — the one non-owned-sound exception of the whole challenge, used deliberately on a texture/pad role rather than a lead. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **"Reuse the same sound twice" handicap applied to the 808: cloned it, then reversed the clone** — rather than reusing the sound identically twice, the duplicate gets its own processing pass (reverse) so it reads as a distinct element despite sharing a source. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **A moderate ~35-minute time box (vs. Day 5's ~14–30-second-per-sound extreme) didn't hinder creativity at all** — direct within-video evidence that constraint severity matters: a workable-but-real time limit sharpens focus without inducing the panic/autopilot effect Day 5 produced. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Finished the beat 3:40 early**, framed as proof the combined handicaps (which looked like the week's hardest challenge on paper) were the most successful day of the series.

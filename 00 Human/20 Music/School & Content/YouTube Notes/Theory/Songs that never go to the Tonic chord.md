# Songs that never go to the Tonic chord

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZcnG9zBBRxc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZcnG9zBBRxc)

## Synopsis

A survey of songs that establish a key without ever playing that key's tonic chord — the harmonic equivalent of a permanent suspended cadence — working from the simplest device (a IV–V vamp) up to fully composed progressions that dodge resolution.

## Techniques

- **The core mechanism: key is decided by melody, not chords.** A song can sit entirely on non-tonic chords and still clearly be "in" a key if the melody uses that key's scale and resolves (rests) on that key's tonic *note* — the chords just never confirm it. Example: "Jane Says" (Jane's Addiction), key of D major, chords never leave G and A (IV and V) — the melody alone establishes D as home.
- **The workhorse device: a IV–V vamp that never resolves to I.** Loop between the subdominant and dominant forever, creating "constant tension without arrival." Examples: "Sure and Certain" (Jimmy Eat World, G major, C–D loop), "Life Like This" (Kurt Vile, C major, F–G loop for 4 minutes straight).
- **A near-miss counts against the technique, not for it**: "Dreams" (Fleetwood Mac) vamps F–G (vi–VII in A minor) for most of its runtime but *does* resolve to the tonic during the instrumental bridge — explicitly flagged as not a true example, since one real resolution breaks the whole effect.
- **Beyond the simple vamp — full progressions that dodge resolution**: "One More Time" (Daft Punk, B minor) uses two separate chord loops (G–A–F♯m7, and G–D over F♯–Em7–A11) with neither ever landing on Bm. "Smile" (Wolfpack — described as the most harmonically sophisticated tonic-avoiding example found) stays in B♭ major but constantly reroutes around the B♭ chord via a developed progression.
- **Songwriting takeaway**: avoiding the tonic chord (not the tonic *key*) is a reusable tension device — it reads as "constantly traveling without arriving," useful anywhere a song wants sustained unresolved motion (a verse that never settles, a loop-based groove) rather than the release a full cadence would give.

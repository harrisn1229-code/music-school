# Modulation

> Effect-sense modulation — chorus / flanger / phaser / rotary. For changing key see [[Modulation (Key Change)]] in `20 Music/Theory/`.

General best practice for chorus / flanger / phaser / rotary and stereo movement. Specific tools: [[Chorus-Ensemble]], [[TAL-Chorus-LX]], [[kHs Flanger]], [[kHs Phaser]]. See also [[Width and Stereo]], [[Delay]].

## What it is
Time/pitch-varying effects that widen, thicken, and animate — from subtle stereo detune to full rotary swirl.

## Owned (first-call)
[[Chorus-Ensemble]] (Ableton, "Logic chorus"-style width) · [[TAL-Chorus-LX]] · [[kHs Chorus]] · [[kHs Flanger]] · [[kHs Phaser]] · [[Spectral Resonator]] (Ableton, MIDI-drivable resonant/vocoder-like texture)

- **Stage count (4/6/12) is a real, audible phaser variable — more stages means deeper, denser notch content — and a phaser's speed control can be INERTIAL (Leslie-style gradual ramp to a new rate) rather than an instant LFO-rate jump.** From a 5-pedal hardware phaser roundup: Boss PH1R = 4-stage, Mu-Tron Phaser II = 6-stage, Moogerfooger = 12-stage — different "flavors," not better/worse. The Roland Jet Phaser's speed knob ramps up/down gradually rather than snapping to a new rate, giving the modulation its own "personality" independent of what's being played — worth seeking out specifically when a static-feeling phaser/tremolo/rotary effect needs more life than a standard rate control gives. Also: CV-jack-equipped phasers (e.g. Moogerfooger) can have their onboard LFO fully replaced by external modulation; layering noise under a phased signal adds a "talking wind" character beyond the phase alone; running two identical mono phasers, one per channel, gives a true stereo phase effect. → [[Saturation and Distortion]] — Anthony Marinelli, [[TOP 5 GUITAR PEDAL PHASERS I LOVE ON SYNTHS]]
- **A specific pedal recommendation and matching method for running guitar pedals on synths, from a working synthesist's own rig — companion detail to the wet/dry-mult entry below.** Two go-to pedals named directly: the **Maestro Phase Shifter** ("it only does one thing but it does it really well") and the **Mu-Tron III** — both cited as literally his first pedals when they were new, prized for versatility across synths, clavinet, and Rhodes (also usable to intentionally overdrive/fuzz an organ signal). **Method**: keep the straight/dry signal running in parallel alongside the pedal path rather than committing fully wet — preserves the source's bite while adding the pedal's color as a layer on top, same wet/dry-mult logic as this page's other entries. **On impedance matching — deliberately skipped**: guitar pedals are designed for a guitar's signal level; a synth's output is hotter, and running it straight in without attenuating first is described as producing a usable, characterful result rather than a broken one — "I wasn't matching the impedance or anything, I was just jamming signal." → [[Saturation and Distortion]] (the deliberate-overdrive-via-pedal angle), [[Parallel Processing]] — Anthony Marinelli & Rob Rosen, [[Lightning Round w Super Synth Tech Rob Rosen - Clip]]
- **Chorus, phaser and flanger on synth bass — three distinct roles, all mult'd wet/dry rather than committed fully wet.** Chorus (MXR-style stereo unit): adds subtle warble/movement that "rounds out" a flat-sounding straight bass — audible mainly once blended back with the dry signal, easy to miss in isolation; speeding the rate up makes the movement audible on its own. Phaser (Mu-Tron Phasor II-style): best on **repeated bass notes** — the dry path carries the rhythm/fundamental, the phaser path adds a sweep over the top; needs only a little in the blend, more effective on brighter patches. Flanger: reads as making a static bass "alive"/moving; without it a repeated bass pattern can sound comparatively dead. All three follow the same wet/dry-mult logic as [[Saturation and Distortion]]'s fuzz entry and [[Delay]]'s tight-delay-thickening entry from the same source — see [[Parallel Processing]] for the mechanics. Owned lane: [[Chorus-Ensemble]]/[[TAL-Chorus-LX]]/[[kHs Chorus]] for chorus, [[kHs Phaser]] for phaser, [[kHs Flanger]] for flanger, each on a parallel send rather than the insert. → [[Bass Design]], [[Parallel Processing]] — Anthony Marinelli, [[Synth Bass on Steroids - The Power of EFX Pedals]]

- **Three modulation domains, not two — time-based modulation on a delay's repeats doesn't have its own common name the way vibrato/tremolo do** — frequency-based modulation is called vibrato, amplitude-based is called tremolo, and time-based modulation (shifting the delay time itself) is the third, usually-unnamed domain a delay plugin's modulation section can target; which domain(s) a given plugin's LFO can actually hit depends entirely on that plugin's design — some only shift time, some can hit all three. → [[Delay]], [[Echo]] — Sage Audio, [Delay Masterclass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjzowK7iurc)

- **Tremolo modulates volume, vibrato modulates pitch — the distinction that's easy to blur** — tremolo gives rapid, rhythmic volume changes (tempo-syncable, or hand-automated); vibrato gives pitch changes via an LFO on a pitch knob — gentle settings read as emotional, exaggerated settings as deliberately "wonky." A slow, subtle vibrato applied as constant pitch drift (not a fast obvious wobble) is the mechanism behind lo-fi's "slightly out of tune" character. - **Vocoder is far more than a robot-voice effect — cleanup, pitch-tracking color, noise texture, and chord-driven pads all live in the same device** — a dedicated deep-dive on Ableton's stock Vocoder covers five distinct creative uses beyond the classic vocoded-voice sound: a subtle Modulator-mode cleanup/de-washing pass, a built-in pitch-tracking oscillator for a coloring layer under any source, noise/cymbal-into-texture design (paired with a high/low splitter for per-band snare use), an External-carrier chord-driven "watery rhythm," and narrow-Bandwidth modulator-mode "bloopy swoop" sound design. Full breakdown lives on the device's own note. → [[Vocoder]] — dnksaus, [Why Vocoder is Ableton's Most Versatile Device!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v6tJJ2Y8Eos)
- **Vocoder routing recipe (the reusable one)** — on the vocal, add a **pre-fader** send to a bus (constant level, independent of the fader) and **mute that bus** (you don't hear the raw send); set the **vocoder's sidechain/side-input = that bus**; play chords on the vocoder synth. The vocal is now the modulator, the chords the carrier. Also works on a **whole drum bus** (send the drums through a vocoder) for added tonality/texture. → [[Vocoder]], [[Pitch and Formant]] — Disclosure, [Who Knew](https://youtu.be/e9d7KYEG3VM)
- **Perform the filter by hand: map cutoff + resonance to MIDI knobs** and ride them live while someone plays, then neaten — a soft synth ([[Poly6]]) breathes like a performed analog synth (LFOs can't fake the human-ness). → [[Automation]] — Disclosure, [Who Knew](https://youtu.be/e9d7KYEG3VM)
- **Double up tremolos for a Todd-Edwards choppy vocal** — Logic tremolo (straight 8ths) + **SoundToys Tremolator** (swung 16ths — reach for it *because* it has swing). Sing/track through tremolo, then re-create with automation. ⚠️ The SoundToys unit distorts like a real tremolo when overdriven — keep it out of the red. → [[Pitch and Formant]] — Disclosure, [Never Enough](https://youtu.be/lGopQwQo9Ag)
- **Momentary whole-bus flanger/ring-shifter for one beat** — automate a Logic Ring Shifter (or Valhalla ÜberMod) *only on the sustained "moooores"* so the vocal bus flanges for a second then snaps back (the Tame Impala / RHCP "Dani California" pre-solo move). → [[Automation]] — Disclosure, [Never Enough](https://youtu.be/lGopQwQo9Ag)
- **RC-20 "wobble" blended via Mix** for parallel phase character on stab chords; **RC-20 "flux"** for a per-note bit-crush wobble → [[RC-20 Retro Color]] — Disclosure, [Never Enough](https://youtu.be/lGopQwQo9Ag)
- **Mono→wide on the drop to grow a chorus without volume** — keep the verse mono, then at the drop mute the reverb and fade in a chorus + rotary (or any modulation) so it opens to full width; the ear reads "bigger" without a level jump. Fastest tool: the [[RC-20 Retro Color]] "Whip"/space knob (mono→wide in one control); or just switch on a flanger/chorus/phaser for the drop only. → [[Stereo Field]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Width Send]] — Disclosure, [Talk (with Khalid)](https://youtu.be/PBbnvElf9ac)
- **Sample-delay widening for a mono one-shot** — a short L/R sample delay (a few ms offset) spreads a mono FX/percussion hit across the [[Stereo Field]] — Disclosure, [Talk (with Khalid)](https://youtu.be/PBbnvElf9ac)
- **Chorus for stereo width on keys/vocals** — a classic wideness send ("good old Logic chorus"); on a mono vocal a light chorus/[[TAL-Chorus-LX]] send widens it a touch — Disclosure, [You've Got To Let Go](https://youtu.be/1iRPvK1gaOI) & [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8)
- **Stereo detune is chorus's cousin** — detuning L/R (or two short delays) fills the stereo field; a single synth voiced one-per-side makes a dramatic difference — Disclosure, [Looking For Love](https://youtu.be/NlJt0pCptGk)
- **Same-take doubling with formant shift = width + phasing shimmer** — pan the natural take one side and a formant-shifted copy of the *same* take the other; they phase against each other for a wide, living double — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8)

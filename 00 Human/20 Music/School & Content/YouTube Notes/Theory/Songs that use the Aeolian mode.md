# Songs that use the Aeolian mode

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Mgsn4p31kA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Mgsn4p31kA)

## Synopsis

David Bennett covers Aeolian (natural minor) — the most common mode after Ionian itself — with a focus on telling it apart from harmonic minor by ear or by looking at the chart, plus the odd, non-standardized "Aeolian cadence" term coined by a 1963 Times critic reviewing a Beatles single.

## Techniques

- **Aeolian = natural minor**, major with degrees 3, 6, 7 all lowered. Fully-committed examples: "All Along the Watchtower" (Bob Dylan), "Californication" (Red Hot Chili Peppers), "Street Spirit" (Radiohead), "Losing My Religion" (R.E.M.).
- **The v-chord test for natural vs. harmonic minor**: Aeolian's 5th-degree chord is minor (v); harmonic minor's is major (V), since the raised 7th becomes that chord's 3rd. "Ain't No Sunshine" (Bill Withers) uses Em → confirms Aeolian.
- **The direct 7th-degree test**: unaltered 7th → Aeolian ("Jolene," unaltered B); raised 7th → harmonic minor ("Back to Black," raised C♯, both melodically and in the major-A chords).
- **A song can mix both within itself**: "Sultans of Swing" (Dire Straits) switches between the raised and unaltered 7th at different points — common enough that "just minor key" is sometimes the more honest label than committing to one scale name.
- **Character difference, demonstrated A/B**: harmonic minor's raised 7th pulls strongly upward (directional, wants to resolve); Aeolian's unaltered 7th has much weaker pull (laid-back, less resolved, purely sorrowful rather than dramatic). "Losing My Religion" recomposed in harmonic minor loses its laid-back sorrow for a stricter, almost sinister edge.
- **The "Aeolian cadence"**: a term coined by Times critic William Mann reviewing the Beatles' "Not a Second Time" (1963) — describes a deceptive cadence (V→vi) as sounding Aeolian since it resolves to the mode built on the 6th degree rather than the tonic. Never became standard terminology; Lennon's own reaction was bemused ("I have no idea what Aeolian cadences are").

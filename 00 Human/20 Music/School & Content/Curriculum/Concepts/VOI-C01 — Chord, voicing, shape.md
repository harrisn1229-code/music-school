# VOI-C01 — Chord, voicing, shape

**Q.** What is the difference between a chord, a voicing and a shape — and why is the choice of voicing harmonic rather than cosmetic?

> [!question]- Answer
> A **chord** is the abstract pitch content (C major = C-E-G, any order, any octave). A **voicing** is the specific order, spacing and register those notes are put in. A **shape** is one physical fingering that realises a voicing. The voicing is harmonic because **register, spacing and doubling change what the ear hears the chord doing** — the same pitch classes, arranged differently, can stop functioning the same way.

## Why it matters

This is the distinction the whole strand rests on, and it is the one a chord chart hides. A chart says "C" and stops; everything after that is a decision, and it is the decision that determines whether the chord supports a vocal or buries it. Pedler's forty pages on one Beatles chord land here: the sonority is an **ensemble effect no single instrument can play**, so no chord symbol can name it and no guitarist can reproduce it.

## Drill

Play one chord three ways on guitar — close and low, spread across the neck, and up on the top three strings. Same chord every time. Say out loud what each one is now fit for.

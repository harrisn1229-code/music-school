# My Favorite Mastering Chain - Why Simple is Better

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bLVMsAwjRPw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bLVMsAwjRPw)

## Synopsis

Sage Audio's Jonathan (the same channel behind [[How to Setup Your Mastering Chain]], [[The Only Music Mastering Video You’ll Ever Need]], [[Mastering Compression Settings]], and the rest of the chain's Sage Audio sourcing on [[Master Chain]]) breaks from the studio's usual all-analog, research-heavy approach to share a personal 7-processor mastering chain he runs on his own projects and tracks he mixes for friends — explicitly framed as a "why simple is better" opinion piece, not the studio's house method. The through-line is **peak preservation**: every stage is chosen and ordered specifically to build loudness and density *without* the processors that most damage transients (EQ, saturation, brick-wall limiting) doing the heavy lifting. The most valuable material is the **maximization/upward-processing double-stack** at inserts 3–4 (a free "master in a minute" plugin plus the Eventide Omnipressor run with no compression or expansion engaged, purely for its 3rd-order-harmonic loudness boost) and the direct **Oxford Inflator-vs-Oxford-Limiter-Enhance comparison** ("Enhance is the same as the Inflator when the Inflator's Curve is set to 50, but the harmonic balance differs") — a piece of information that sharpens an existing but vaguer entry already on [[Oxford Inflator]] and [[Plugin Wishlist]]. The saturation stage (multiband [[Saturn 2]], lows-only, no oversampling needed) and the closing limiter philosophy (so much upward maximization has already happened by the final stage that the limiter — Voxengo Elephant — barely attenuates at all, ~2 dB GR at −8 LUFS integrated) both reinforce and add concrete numbers to ideas already scattered across [[Master Chain]]'s Sage Audio sourcing. Genre example is punk (more saturation/distortion is expected), but the chain is stated to generalize to most genres with subtler settings.

## Techniques

- **[~0:40] Chain philosophy — 7 processors total, ordered to protect transients.** Stated goal: an upfront, impressive, LOUD sound while affecting peaks as little as possible. The reasoning given up front: EQ and saturation both negatively affect transients in ways people don't expect (not just linear-phase EQ — *all* EQ types shift phase and affect transients to some degree), so the whole chain is built to minimize how much of that damage accumulates before the limiter.
- **[~1:00] Insert 1 — Subtractive EQ, deliberately minimal.** Two moves only: (1) a **side-image high-pass filter, 12 dB/octave**, which keeps phase rotation to a minimum while still focusing the low end into the center of the stereo image (mono-safe lows without a steeper, more phase-disruptive slope); (2) a **mid-image bell dip in the highs** to reduce sibilance — center frequency is mix-dependent, may need more than one filter, but the discipline is to only touch the spectrum where it's absolutely needed. → [[SUB Slot]], [[BASS Slot]] (via the side-image HPF's low-end centering)
- **[~1:50] Insert 2 — Multiband saturation, lows only, on Saturn 2 (owned).** Isolate the kick/bass/snare fundamentals into one band and saturate that band a fair amount; leave the high range completely unsaturated. Two stated benefits: (1) harmonics stay musical because they're only generated from kick/bass/snare, not cymbals/sibilance (which saturate into ugly distortion); (2) because only lows are saturated, there's no risk of a harmonic aliasing past the Nyquist limit, so **no oversampling is needed** — which matters because oversampling's own linear-phase filter is another transient-smearing cost, so skipping it (safely, because of the low-band-only routing) is itself part of the chain's peak-preservation goal. Genre-dependent: this example (punk) uses a fair amount of drive; other genres should run it subtler. → [[BASS Slot]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **[~3:15] Insert 3 — bx_masterdesk Classic (free, Brainworx/Plugin Alliance) for maximization — unowned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]].** Marketed as an "instant master in one minute" plugin (a category the video is skeptical of on principle) but used here for its *character*, near-default settings, as one stage in a chain rather than a one-stop master. What it does per the video: frequency response reads like classic hardware with distinctive boosts/cuts and notable high-end attenuation; subtle L/R channel variation adds a stereo "unique sound"; harmonics include a modulating-noise character similar to dithering, which reduces perceived quantization distortion; **Tone** and **Foundation** controls do mild, broad frequency-spectrum shaping; built-in maximizer and a mid-side matrix handle stereo-image alteration. Framed as sounding better as one link in a chain than as an all-in-one master.
- **[~4:20] Insert 4 — Eventide Omnipressor for pure upward maximization, compression/expansion OFF — unowned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]].** The key trick: don't engage the compression or expansion functions at all — simply inserting the plugin on the mix maximizes the overall signal by a few dB while imparting a strong 3rd-order harmonic, described as "the majority of the maximization" in this chain. Brings up quiet detail without touching peaks — the whole point of running upward processing instead of downward gain reduction. → [[Oxford Inflator]] (owned upward-processing equivalent already carries a fuller writeup of this mechanism)
- **[~6:10] Insert 5 — Final mid-side EQ, cleanup + one width move.** Four small moves in one stage: (1) a **~6 dB/octave side-image pass** to refocus low end that bled back into the sides from the upstream saturation/maximization stages; (2) a small **dip of the 3rd-order harmonic** that Saturn 2 and the Omnipressor both added, to reduce its masking effect on higher ranges and rebalance the tone; (3) a **small stereo expansion on the mid-frequency range** specifically (not a blanket side boost) — described as the most information-dense range, opening it up slightly for added perceived complexity in the side image; (4) a **high shelf for air**, bringing up lost top-end detail. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **[~7:10] Insert 6 — Sonnox Oxford Limiter, used for maximization/harmonic distortion, not limiting — unowned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]].** Direct answer to a question the video says comes up often: **the Oxford Limiter's Enhance function is the same mechanism as the Oxford Inflator with Curve set to 50** — but the two differ in harmonic balance: the Inflator's harmonics run higher in amplitude in the lows and much subtler in the highs; the Oxford Limiter's Enhance reads more balanced overall, with extra emphasis on high-frequency harmonics that "tie the high range back in to the song's root note." A genuinely new, concrete data point sharpening the existing (vaguer) Inflator-vs-Enhance comparison already on [[Oxford Inflator]] and [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Oxford Inflator]]
- **[~8:10] Insert 7 — Voxengo Elephant, final limiter, deliberately doing almost nothing.** Because so much loudness was already built via upward maximization at inserts 3–4 and 6, the final limiter barely attenuates — on this mix, up to ~2 dB of GR (often less), at an **integrated LUFS of about −8**, meaning even less limiting would have worked. Its job here is framed as "protect from overs and subtly shape some peaks," not do the loudness work. Concrete parameter notes: **channel linking kept high** to retain mono compatibility; **2x oversampling** to reduce aliasing; if any transient change is noticed, go into **attack/release/shape** and tweak for the right balance of control vs. transient retention — but almost any limiter would work in this slot since it's barely engaged. → **Voxengo Elephant** (unowned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) — cross-reference: [[Master Chain]]'s own §8 already targets a similar "max 3 dB GR" ceiling; this video supplies a worked real-mix example landing well inside that.
- **[~9:10] Genre generalization note.** This exact chain was demoed on a punk mix (where extra distortion/saturation reads as appropriate to the genre); stated to still work for most genres, just with subtler settings throughout — most relevant at insert 2 (saturation amount) and inserts 3/4/6 (how much maximization/harmonic content to dial in).

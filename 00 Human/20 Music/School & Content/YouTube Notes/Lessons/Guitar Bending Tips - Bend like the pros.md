# Guitar Bending Tips - Bend like the pros

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3rACLmS4roU)

## Synopsis

A tight, 8-minute technique lesson from Jared Borkowski (Sound Guitar Lessons With Jared) covering his top five tips for clean, professional-sounding string bends. Unlike this channel's usual theory-heavy output (quartal harmony, voice leading, reharmonization), this is pure physical execution — no scale/theory content at all. Each tip targets a specific, common failure mode: weak/unsupported bends, bends that sound out-of-tune at the attack, bends that audibly sag after the peak, bends that land on an imprecise pitch, and bends that lose grip mid-sustain because a neighboring string slips underneath the fretting finger. Two are illustrated against real recordings (Stevie Ray Vaughan's "Mary Had a Little Lamb," The Beatles' "Let It Be"), and the string-hooking tip is illustrated with a close-up photo of the fretting hand. Short, dense, and entirely actionable — every tip is a specific hand-mechanics fix, not a vague "practice more."

## What's taught

- **Tip 1 — Support the bend with as many fingers as the position allows.** Bend with the pinky → back it with fingers 1, 2, 3 all fretting the same string behind it (4 fingers total engaged). Bend with the 3rd finger → support with fingers 1 and 2. Bend with the 2nd finger → support with finger 1. A single unsupported finger can bend, but reliably only up to a half step; a full step needs the added strength of the stacked supporting fingers. *(~0:28)*
- **Tip 2 — Let the starting pitch actually sound before bending.** Common mistake: starting the bend as you pluck the string, or slightly before — the attack lands already sharp and reads as an out-of-tune note rather than an intentional bend. Practice by initiating the bend in slow motion first (to isolate a clean, in-tune starting note), then speeding up to normal bend speed while still preserving that brief true-pitch window before the pitch starts moving. *(~1:14)*
- **Tip 3 — Stop the sound at the peak of the bend; never let it audibly slip back down.** Mute with the picking (right) hand right at the peak — you can hold/sustain the bend at the peak indefinitely, the rule is just that it should never audibly release. Applies at any bend distance — full step, half step, even a quarter-step "tiny" bend. Demonstrated against Stevie Ray Vaughan's solo on "Mary Had a Little Lamb": every bend, including two-note-at-a-time bends and very small ones, stops clean at the peak with zero release. **A bend that appears to "come down" isn't always the pitch releasing** — worked example from The Beatles' "Let It Be" guitar solo: what sounds like a released bend is actually a re-pluck at the peak followed by a deliberate, controlled bend *down* — an intentional technique (bend up → pluck at the peak → bend back down), fine when chosen on purpose, but not the default behavior. *(~2:04–4:35)*
- **Tip 4 — Intonate the bend: land on the exact target pitch.** Bending is one of the few places on guitar where you have to actively listen for intonation instead of relying on frets — framed as good ear-training as well as good technique. Practice exercise: slide up to the target note first (to internalize the exact destination pitch), then bend up to that same note, alternating slide vs. bend until the bend reliably lands on the identical pitch. *(~4:47–5:36)*
- **Tip 5 — Hook the neighboring string under your fingertip so it can't slip beneath your fretting finger.** Named failure mode: while sustaining a bend (or doing bending vibrato), the adjacent string can slip in between the fretting finger and the fretboard, stealing pressure and causing the target note to sag/go flat — exactly the "coming down" flaw Tip 3 warns against, but caused by grip failure rather than an intentional release. Fix, shown via a close-up photo: catch the neighboring string on the fingertip itself so it gets pulled *up and away* from the fretboard as you bend, instead of wedging underneath. The same hooked grip doubles as the mechanism for **bending vibrato** — with the neighboring string hooked and under tension, you can oscillate the bend's width by pushing against it without losing your hold. *(~5:39–7:03)*

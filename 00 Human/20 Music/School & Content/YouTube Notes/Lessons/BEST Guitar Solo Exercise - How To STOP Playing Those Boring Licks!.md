# BEST Guitar Solo Exercise | How To STOP Playing Those Boring Licks! (Guitar Lesson)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lbSkE5I4KBY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lbSkE5I4KBY)

## Synopsis

BERNTH diagnoses two common "boring solo" failure modes — staying in one pentatonic box the whole time, and shredding fast technique in one scale without tracking the chord changes — then demonstrates the fix: accent each chord in a progression with its own basic major/minor triad shape (plus a borrowed chord-tone here and there) instead of running one scale over the whole thing. Backed by a concrete worked riff (C minor power-chord progression: Cm–F–Eb–D°7–Ab–Bb–G–F) and a daily triad-memorization drill.

## What's taught

- **Failure mode 1 — single pentatonic box.** Playing the whole solo from one minor pentatonic box position, stacking memorized licks (slides, triplet pull-off licks) inside that one box without regard for the underlying chord changes — diagnosed as instantly recognizable to an experienced listener as "one scale, one position," and creatively limiting since the player never leaves that patch of the neck.
- **Failure mode 2 — technique-first, chords-ignored.** Running fast alternate-picked/legato/sweep-picked lines confined to the parent scale (C minor) across the whole progression, showing off technique without tracking the individual chord changes underneath — sounds like "practicing licks over the track" rather than playing *with* the music.
- **The fix — triad-per-chord accenting.** For each chord in the progression, ascend through that chord's basic triad shape (root–3rd–5th) rather than running the parent scale, optionally borrowing a non-triad scale tone for color. Worked chord-by-chord over Cm–F–Eb–D°7–Ab–Bb–G–F: **Cm** — ascend C minor triad shapes, borrowing a couple of scale tones. **Eb** — Eb triad plus an added major 7th (D) for color, with a quick sweep. **D°7** (voiced as a diminished 7th rather than the diatonic D half-diminished, common in rock/metal grips) — quick diminished-7 arpeggio to accent the chromatic passing chord. **Ab** — ascending Ab major triad shapes. **G** — ascending G major triad shapes; specifically calls out that in a minor key, the V is often played major (not the diatonic minor v) for tension/resolution, so the triad choice should follow the chord actually sounding, not the parent scale's diatonic answer. Closes with a quick harmonic-minor scale run for a shredding flourish. Stated payoff: this take reads as "playing with the music," not stacked on top of or against it.
- **Daily triad memorization drill.** On the top 3 strings (G-B-E) only: ascend through all the minor triad shapes across the neck, then switch to the major triad shapes — since minor→major is a one-note change (raise the ♭3 to a natural 3, root and 5th stay put), the drill deliberately sequences minor-then-major to internalize that relationship directly. Doubles as an alternate-picking exercise (two strokes per triad note). Explicitly instructs memorizing which note is the root/3rd/5th in each shape while drilling, not just the finger pattern, so the shapes are usable by function (not just position) when soloing.

# Hey Jude — The Beatles

> Slow ballad written by Paul McCartney for Julian Lennon; builds from a quiet verse into a long, anthemic "na-na-na" outro.

## Chords
Key: **F major** throughout — a deceptively simple harmonic frame for a 7-minute song.

**Verse:** F – C – Bb – F – C – F → **I – V – IV – I – V – I**, plain root-position diatonic triads (occasionally colored as C7) — almost all of the verse's emotional weight comes from the vocal melody and the slow arrangement build, not from harmonic complexity.

**Bridge ("And any time you feel the pain..."):** Bb – F – C – Eb → **IV – I – V – ♭VII**, a brief added color: that E♭ is borrowed from F Mixolydian, sitting outside the plain major-scale palette before the verse returns → [[Borrowed Chords]]

**Coda ("Na-na-na-na"):** F – Eb – Bb – F, looped for the entire four-minute outro → **I – ♭VII – IV – I**. Beatles scholar Alan W. Pollack singles this loop out directly in his canonical analysis of the song: the flat-VII chord gives the whole coda "a decidedly modal, Mixolydian flavor," distinct from the plain-major verse — a static, orchestrally-stacked vamp rather than a progression that resolves anywhere, which is exactly what lets it ride that long without wearing out its welcome → [[Borrowed Chords]]

None of the vault's controlled-vocabulary progression tags fit cleanly here — the verse is a plain I-IV-V (not a turnaround, not axis, not the doo-wop loop), and the coda's I-♭VII-IV-I is a modal vamp built on four chords rather than the one/two-chord shape the `modal-vamp` tag is defined around — so `progressions` is left empty above rather than stretched.

## Production & Arrangement
Famous for its **four-minute coda** — the "na-na-na" outro is longer than the actual song, with a **36-piece orchestra** doubling the chords and clapping/singing along, building through repetition to a euphoric, communal fade. At 7+ minutes it broke the "singles must be short" rule.

- **Arrangement as a slow build:** solo piano+vocal → band → tambourine → orchestra → mass singalong. Each pass adds one layer.
- Warm, intimate verse; huge, roomy coda — dynamics from *addition*.

## Steal this
- **Build by addition:** start with one element (piano+voice) and add exactly one layer per section until the payoff — the [[🎵 BILLY JOEL - Vienna|Vienna]] lesson at scale.
- **A long communal coda:** ride a simple loop and stack claps/gang vocals/orchestra for a euphoric fade ([[Hand Clap Studio]], [[2 Ensemble Stack Group]]).
- **Break the length rule** if the build earns it.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Hey Jude]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #14.*

**Form.** His subject is not the length but the means of sustaining it: an **unusual binary form** — a fully composed, hymn-like song followed by a mantra-like jam on a four-bar loop, the two halves built from very plain material and complementing each other by contrasting in *kind*. Charted out, the section durations divide the running time into roughly **golden-mean proportions**, with the jam the longer half. The song half is the standard two-bridge model with one verse between and no intro at all; every section has different words, rare for this band. Verse 8 bars, four **through-composed** phrases with no internal repeat, plus one bar added rhetorically when a verse leads into a bridge. Bridge **11½ bars** despite surface AA phrasing — the first phrase stretched to five so its handoff mimics the verse-to-bridge lead-in, the second halving the F7 bar and tacking on a two-bar "na-na" tag that both returns to the verse and foreshadows the jam ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]). Jam: 18½ repeats of a closed four-bar phrase, fading mid-repeat.

**Harmony.** Purely diatonic F major in the song half — I, ii, IV, V, all root position, one chord per bar. `Verse: I – V (2 bars) – I · IV – I – V – I → I7`; the shape is closed, but the final tonic mutates into F7, **V-of-IV**, which is the entire mechanism for getting into the bridge ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). `Bridge: IV – ii – V – I → I7 (×2) · V7`, open at both ends over a Bach-ish walking bass. The jam is `I – ♭VII – IV – I`, a **double plagal cadence** ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]) whose ♭VII throws the whole second half into Mixolydian ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]) — and that modal colour, more than the repetition, is the real contrast.

**Notable.** Every appoggiatura and escape note in the first half resolves; in the jam the na-na melody's peak F sits as an **unresolved 9th** against the E♭ chord. The orchestral doubling is layered across repeats, and **the fade begins at repeat 10 with two full minutes still to run**.

**Against this page.** Agrees on F major throughout.

## Synopsis

A left-hand-only variant of the standard chromatic "spider" exercise: instead of using all four fingers in simple sequence, fingers are deliberately paired (index+ring on one string, middle+pinky on the next) while keeping the spider exercise's core no-lift, overlapping-notes discipline — a harder coordination demand than the basic version.

## What's taught

- **The paired-finger spider variant.** Base exercise unchanged (chromatic, don't lift a finger until it's needed on the next string, fingers must stay curled enough that outgoing and incoming notes briefly overlap/ring together — flat fingers block the string and kill the overlap). New twist: pair index+ring finger on one string, then middle+pinky on the next string, rather than the standard one-finger-at-a-time sequence — described as noticeably harder because the fingers "work against you" when paired this way.
- **Progression:** get the paired-finger motion solid at one fixed position/string pair first, then extend across the full exercise (multiple position changes), then reverse (descending), then move the whole thing across different sections of the neck.
- **Stated payoff:** left-hand technique is a commonly neglected area relative to picking-hand focus — framed as the top-voted lesson-wishlist topic that month, addressing a gap BERNTH names in his own earlier development (over-focused on alternate picking, under-focused on left-hand fundamentals).
- Recommended dose: ~10 minutes at the start of a practice session, to a metronome, for visible results within days.

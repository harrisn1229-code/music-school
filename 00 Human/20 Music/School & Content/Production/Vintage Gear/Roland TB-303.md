# Roland TB-303

## What it is
The Bass Line — a silver box meant to give practicing guitarists a fake bassist ([[Roland TR-606]] was its drummer). It failed completely at that job, hit pawn shops for pennies, and was then *misused* into acid house: the single most famous case of a machine's flop becoming a genre. The 303 doesn't imitate bass; it IS a sound.

## What makes the sound
- **One VCO (saw or square) into an 18 dB/oct diode-ladder lowpass** — a filter topology unlike Moog's or Roland's own synth lines: raspy, vocal, squelchy, with a resonance that *squeals* instead of blooming.
- **Accent is the magic circuit**: accented steps goose VCA level *and* filter envelope together (with resonance interplay), so a pattern breathes and barks — the "acid" articulation is accent + resonance + envelope-mod knobs ridden live.
- **Slide** (glide between tied steps) gives the drunken legato swoops; the famously awkward step sequencer produced happy-accident patterns nobody would play on a keyboard.
- Crank resonance, ride cutoff, run it into distortion → the acid scream → [[Saturation and Distortion]].

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** no dedicated 303 in the collection — closest paths: the **MS-20-style trick already in the [[Sound Selection Guide]]** (drive a resonant filter's peak into distortion), [[Analog]]/[[Serum 2]] mono saw into [[kHs Nonlinear Filter]] or [[Volcano 3]] with high resonance + [[Saturator]]; automate cutoff per-step.
- **Paid:** D16 Phoscyon 2 (the standard), AudioRealism ABL3, Roland Cloud TB-303; Behringer TD-3 in hardware (~$150).
- **Free:** Open303/JC303 (open-source, surprisingly good).

## Used on
- Overmono — "Barum" (*Pure Devotion*): run loud, dry and unapologetically classic-sounding as a deliberate anti-cliché choice — rather than disguising the familiar acid character (their normal instinct with well-known sounds), they leaned into it hard as the track's driving "call to arms" element, introduced fairly late in the arrangement. → [[TN 187 Overmono - Full Video Episode]] — Tape Notes
- Overmono — in their studio gear list (no specific technique given). → [[TN 187 Overmono - Gear List]] — Tape Notes
- Charanjit Singh — *Ten Ragas to a Disco Beat* (1982): accidental proto-acid five years early, rediscovered and canonized.
- Phuture — "Acid Tracks" (1987): the record that named acid house, built on a resonance-tortured 303.
- Genre/era: acid house/techno as a genre ([[EDM Subgenres]] acid card), 90s rave, and every "acid line" since — the 303 is arguably the most genre-defining single instrument in this library alongside the [[Roland TR-808]].

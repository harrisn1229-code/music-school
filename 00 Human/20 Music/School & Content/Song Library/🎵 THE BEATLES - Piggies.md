# Piggies — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4) · Verse (12, three equal phrases, AA′B) · Intro · Verse · Intro′ · Bridge (14, phrased 8+6) · Verse (instrumental) · Intro · Verse · Outro with a complete ending.

A run of classical mannerisms bolted to a snide lyric, and the structural item on that list is the one worth stealing: a short instrumental intro placed before **every** verse — a nineteenth-century art-song habit that makes a 2:04 track feel considerably longer than it is.

Everything stays anchored in A despite two feints away. The verse harps on I and V for six straight bars, which is exactly what makes the **ii – V-of-V** in the second phrase land ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]); that same pair then recurs at the *opposite end* of the following phrase, which is the section's rhetorical trick. The verse's last two bars slow the harmonic rhythm and fill the space with two inner voices rising in parallel tenths — four apparent chords, one actual function, a prolongation of V ([[POP-C04 — Four chords with a moving bass]]).

The altered intro before the bridge is textbook motivic transformation ([[MEL-C03 — Motifs and how to develop them]]): the same four bars with the last chord swapped from V to V-of-vi, which reads as a modulation about to happen — and four bars into the bridge you are still at home. The ending is deliberately ambiguous, two broad chords hearable as I–V in A or as a plagal IV–I in E. Harpsichord, bowed cellos and a late operatic male chorus carry the period costume.

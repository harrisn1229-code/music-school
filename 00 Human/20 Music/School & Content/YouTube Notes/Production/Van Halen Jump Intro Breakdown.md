# Van Halen "Jump" Intro Breakdown

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGofw3SBN_w)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pGofw3SBN_w)

## Synopsis

A patch recreation of Van Halen's "Jump" synth intro on an OB-X (Eddie Van Halen's original was an OB-Xa — a slightly more capable follow-up — but the sound is achievable on any 2-oscillator synth with a resonant filter and 1-2 envelope generators). Includes a real aside: Eddie Van Halen trained classically on piano before guitar, and Marinelli worked with him (on bass, due to a record-label guitar restriction) on the film *Over the Top* with Giorgio Moroder.

## Techniques

- **Two detuned sawtooth oscillators at EQUAL level is the actual key to the "Oberheim sound" here — NOT pulse-width modulation**, which is how many players assume this character is achieved. Getting the detune amount exactly right (demonstrated too little, too much, and correct) does all the work; there's no PWM in this patch at all.
- **No resonance on the filter** — the sound's character comes entirely from detune + envelope modulation depth, not a resonant filter sweep.
- **Filter envelope amount turned all the way up** (maximum ADSR-to-filter modulation depth), with only a modest initial/base filter cutoff — most of the audible "pop"/brightness comes from the envelope's modulation depth, not the base cutoff position.
- **Filter (VCF) envelope shape**: a small amount of attack time (not instant) for a touch of "bite," fairly quick decay, sustain pulled down for a short, articulate character rather than a sustained pad.
- **Amplitude (VCA) envelope shape**: essentially no attack, a little decay (mirroring the filter envelope's shape), then full sustain — the combination of a snappy filter envelope with a sustained VCA envelope is what gives the hit its "pop into a held tone" character.
- **Post-processing chain matters**: the original likely had compression, EQ, tape saturation, and reverb; the recreation used a mix of plugin and hardware processing, finishing with ValhallaRoom-style reverb — framed as a reminder that most synth patches "straight out of the box" benefit enormously from effects, and shouldn't be judged dry.

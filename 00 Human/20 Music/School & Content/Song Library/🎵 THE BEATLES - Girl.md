## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Girl]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #84.*

**Form.** The Beatles rarely modulated outright, which makes this one notable: **it changes key at every section boundary**, cycling minor / relative major / a second minor, and the key changes carry the emotional argument. **No intro at all** — it starts mid-scene, without even a cue for the opening pitch. Verse 8 bars in two parallel phrases, closed i to i, harmony varied on the second pass; refrain only 4 bars; bridge 8, built like the verse with its second phrase reharmonised to steer out. The only instrumental section is a verse placed very late.

**Harmony.** `Verse (c minor): i – V – i – iv – ♭VI – V · i – V – i – iv – i` — wholly minor, with the first phrase's ♭VI–V a feint at the relative major that the refrain makes good on. `Refrain (E♭ major): I – iii6/4 – IV6/3 – V (×2)` — the textbook reading is I–vi–IV–V, but the descending scalewise bass E♭–D–C–B♭ makes a **second-inversion G minor with a 4–3 appoggiatura** fit better; the last bar doubles as IV of F minor, which is how the bridge gets in. `Bridge (f minor): i – V – i – V · i – V – i – ♭III`, a real modulation down a fifth whose closing A♭ re-reads as IV in E♭ to hand back ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]], [[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]). The chords are simple on purpose, to keep attention on the key scheme; and the switch to the "cheerful" major works **against** the phonemes sung over it, which is the reason for the modulation.

**Notable.** The tune sits high in John's range so the delivery strains on purpose, with **augmented seconds** available from the harmonic minor ([[SCL-C02 — Harmonic and melodic minor]]). Two arrangement details he rates above the rest: **surface rhythm as leitmotif** (implied fast triplets everywhere except the bridge and final sung verse, which snap to dead-even eighths), and **stacked counter-melodies** — one added in verse 2, a second added in the instrumental verse.

**Against this page.** Agrees on C minor (this page's capo-8 note gives the same sounding key). He adds the relative E♭ major of the refrain and the F minor bridge, which the `key:` field does not carry.

# Roland TR-505

## What it is
The bottom rung of the TR ladder — a ~$300 plastic PCM box with the [[Roland TR-707|707]]'s brain, a smaller speaker-grade kit, and a rack of latin percussion borrowed from the TR-727's world. It was the drum machine you could actually afford in 1986, which made it the *bedroom* machine of early house, techno, and countless demos.

## What makes the sound
- **Even shorter, cheaper companded PCM samples than the 707** — thin, papery kick and snare, chirpy toms, plus timbale/conga/cowbell latin voices that give it a distinct flavor the 707 lacks.
- **No individual outs, no tuning** — one stereo pair, take the kit or leave it; the flat, boxy print through its converters *is* the sound.
- Full X0X-style step programming + MIDI at a toy price — the point was access, and the patterns kids wrote on it seeded scenes.
- Its cheap-and-cheerful character has made it a lo-fi/indie cult box today — the "honest little drum machine" aesthetic → [[Bedroom Pop - Indie]].

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Impulse]] with 505 samples; [[Cassette]] / [[RC-20 Retro Color]] lean into the budget-plastic vibe.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2; Samples From Mars and similar vintage-archive packs.
- **Free:** 505 kits circulate in every free drum-machine sample archive.

## Used on
- Genre/era: late-80s bedroom house/techno demos, UK bleep-era budget productions, and the modern lo-fi indie/bedroom lane that reaches for it precisely because it sounds cheap — credits are largely undocumented at the record level, so this one stays genre-level on purpose.

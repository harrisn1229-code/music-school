# DRM-C08 — A-B-A-C inside a single loop

**Q.** Why does a 4-bar loop often read as shorter than four bars, and what's the fix?

> [!question]- Answer
> Because it's one bar repeated with tiny variations, so the ear registers the repeat length rather than the bar count. Fix: structure the loop **A-B-A-C** — a simple kick pattern for A bars, a syncopated one for B, each paired with the **inverse** hi-hat treatment (strong-beat hats under the busy kick, syncopated hats under the simple kick) so the two elements constantly trade who's anchoring and who's playing around.

## Why it matters

It's the loop-scale version of the song-scale "give it real length" principle. The pattern doesn't feel like repetition until the full cycle completes — which buys you real bars for free.

## Drill

Take a 1-bar loop you like. Expand it to 4 bars as A-B-A-C with inverse hat treatments. Time how long before it bores you, versus before.

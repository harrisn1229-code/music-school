# E-mu SP-12

## What it is
The Sampling Percussion 12-bit — E-mu's follow-up to the Drumulator and the direct ancestor of the [[E-mu SP-1200]]. It fused a drum machine with *user sampling* (1.2 seconds stock, 5 with the Turbo upgrade) and handed mid-80s hip hop its first taste of chopping your own drums into the machine.

## What makes the sound
- **12-bit / 27.5 kHz sampling through SSM analog filters** — the same crunchy-digital-into-warm-analog recipe the SP-1200 perfected two years later, a touch rawer here.
- **Absurdly short sample time forced percussive thinking**: single hits, stabs, and vocal barks rather than loops — the SP-12 era sounds *punchier* and less loop-based than what the 1200's 10 seconds later allowed.
- Came with ROM drums (Drumulator lineage) *plus* your samples side by side — half drum machine, half sampler, programmable on pads with tune/decay per voice.
- Multi-pitch playback across pads previewed the chop-and-pitch vocabulary that boom bap standardized → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Decimort 2]] (12-bit vintage-sampler character — the SP family's converter grit), [[Drum Rack]]/[[Sampler]] for the workflow, [[RC-20 Retro Color]] for dust.
- **Paid:** Inphonik RX1200 (SP-1200 model covers the family sound), Rossum reissue hardware (same lineage).
- **Free:** SP-12-processed kits circulate; the [[Decimort 2]]-on-anything recipe gets you there.

## Used on
- Mantronix — Kurtis Mantronik's mid-80s electro-hip hop productions are documented SP-12 showcases.
- Genre/era: 1985–87 hip hop and electro — the bridge era between the [[Oberheim DMX]]'s ROM kits and the [[E-mu SP-1200]]'s golden-age loop chopping.

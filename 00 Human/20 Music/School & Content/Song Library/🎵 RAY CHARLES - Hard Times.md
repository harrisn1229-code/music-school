# Hard Times — Ray Charles

> A slow blues Jack Ruch's organ trio plays regularly, used as the vehicle for a layered soloing lesson: the same tune, played three times, each pass adding a harder layer of theory over the last. See [[Soloing Over Changes in 3 LEVELS!]] for the full breakdown.

## Chords
**Key:** E♭ major — a **12-bar-family jazz-blues** dressed up with several embellishments layered onto the plain I-IV-V skeleton: a **1-6 move** into the relative minor (**E♭ → G7 → Cm7**, I → V7/vi → vi), a full **major ii-V-I landing on the IV chord instead of the I** (**B♭m7 → E♭7 → A♭7**, ii-V-I of A♭), a **passing diminished chord** between IV and I (♯IV°), and a **chromatically-walked VI7 turnaround** (C7 sliding down in half steps) before the closing V (B♭7) sends the form back to the top.

## The three-level soloing map (Jack Ruch's lesson)
Same tune, same solo skeleton, three passes with increasing harmonic sophistication — see [[Soloing Over Changes in 3 LEVELS!]] for full detail on each level.

- **Level 1 — pentatonic blues foundation.** E♭ major pentatonic (two CAGED-adjacent shapes, C-shape and A-shape, rooted 6th fret/A string) for the bright/sweet sound, switching to E♭ minor pentatonic specifically at the C→B→B♭ walk-down over the passing diminished chord. Phrasing/dynamics/touch over theory. → [[Diagonal Pentatonic Blues Soloing]]
- **Level 2 — chord-tone/arpeggio outlining.** Each chord's root-3rd-5th-♭7 arpeggio, fingered with CAGED-adjacent shapes kept in one small position (the whole E♭-G7-Cm7-B♭m7 stretch stays in one ~5-fret zone), stitched together with chromatic connecting tones and blended back into Level 1's pentatonic phrasing. → [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]], [[Chord-Tone Soloing]]
- **Level 3 — the altered scale over the three functioning dominants.** G altered (over G7→Cm), E♭ altered (over the E♭7 in the 251 to A♭), and B♭ altered (over the closing V7) — same shape relocated to each root, always resolved onto a chord tone of the next chord on the downbeat. → [[Scale Modes Reference]], [[Altered Chords]]

## Steal this
- **Practice a new tune in exactly this order** — plain pentatonic/blues phrasing first (touch and space, no theory pressure), then chord-tone/arpeggio precision, then altered/outside color — rather than trying to sound "advanced" on the first pass.
- **The walk-down (C-B-B♭) over the passing diminished chord is where the minor pentatonic switch belongs** — a concrete, transposable rule: lean minor-pentatonic-bluesy specifically at a chromatic passing/diminished moment, not just "sometimes."
- **The altered scale as "dominant shell + four extensions," relocated by root** — one mental model (root-3rd-♭7 shell, add ♭9/♯9/♯11/♭13) covers all three altered-scale spots in this tune (G7, E♭7, B♭7) without re-deriving the scale each time.

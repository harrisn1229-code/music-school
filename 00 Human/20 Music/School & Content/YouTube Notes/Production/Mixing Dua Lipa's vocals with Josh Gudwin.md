# Mixing Dua Lipa's vocals with Josh Gudwin

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OW3FKauvUZo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OW3FKauvUZo)

## Synopsis

A Mix with the Masters screen-share of Josh Gudwin (Dua Lipa, Justin Bieber) mixing "Electricity," working directly in an Ableton-style session at 118 BPM. This is a **stem-handoff mix**, not a from-scratch build — Josh is explicit throughout that the producer (Mark, unnamed surname but referred to respectfully as "one of the OGs") sent him a track that already sounded good, and his stated approach is to *build off* that mix rather than replace it: keep sends and plugins the producer's team already had in place when they're working ("I kept those on because the reverb actually sounded pretty cool"), only add processing where something is genuinely uneven, and stay out of the way of a good source. The most detailed material is the lead-vocal chain — a deep, mostly-subtractive stack (EQ → EQ → compressor → light multiband resonance suppression → more EQ → narrow multiband cuts → LA-2A → C6-style subtractive multiband EQ/de-essing) applied per-track rather than as a blanket vocal-bus move, because the original vocal was delivered as one combined track and Josh's habit is to split every performance out and tailor each individually. The video's standout reusable trick is a **parallel "girth" aux** — duplicate the vocal's master aux, run a default/aggressive EQ + 1176-style compression on the duplicate, and blend it back in at roughly −33 dB for fullness without turning up the fader. Beyond vocals he covers piano (subtle mid/side + kick-sidechain mono-narrowing), guitars (an unlinked-stereo compression fix for a right-heavy signal, plus a harder-driven outro-guitar bus), bass (light-touch blend-first philosophy, Trash/Clip Control character, kick-sidechain on the drop), and drums (left almost entirely alone because the source sounds already sounded good, with Decapitator and Transient Master kept on standby for individual hits). He closes with a genuinely useful "delay throw as feel, not clarity" layering technique (tucked quarter-note + audible filtered reverb + a "half-verb blast," all dialed in then turned down further) and an automated spring-reverb swell used once, deliberately, at the bridge. Auto-captions garble a couple of product names (a reverb Josh calls "Clear" doesn't match anything sonically plausible and is logged as unidentified rather than force-matched to the owned Supertone Clear vocal-clarity tool, which does a completely different job).

## Techniques

- **Split every vocal performance onto its own track and process individually, never blanket-process a combined vocal stem.** "Originally when I got these, all the vocals were all on one track... I separate them out, EQ them differently, effect them differently on a track by track basis... each track is treated independently... I'm not like, just copying and pasting things, I'm doing minor differences between these tracks. So some need a little bit more compression, some need less compression." The whole rest of the vocal breakdown below happens per-track under this philosophy. → [[Mix Handoff]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **Base lead-vocal chain, in order: EQ (filtering) → EQ (broad) → compressor → light multiband resonance suppression → EQ again → narrow multiband cuts → LA-2A → subtractive multiband EQ/de-essing.** "A basic chain for me that I like using for vocals is an EQ, and then, an EQ feeding a compressor, and then light multiband... So after the LA-2A I like using the C6 to shape my vocal EQ-wise." → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Soothe2]], [[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]], [[Pro-MB]], [[Compression]], [[Multiband Compression]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **[[Pro-Q 4]] does the FIRST filtering pass, an unnamed SSL-style channel EQ does the broad shaping pass right after it — Pro-Q for cleanup, SSL for tone.** "My Pro-Q is doing like, basic filtering, and then the SSL is doing broad EQ." The SSL EQ move demoed: 8 kHz, roughly +0.3 dB — barely-there. Not an owned plugin (a Waves-style SSL 4000 channel strip) — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[EQ]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **A named default preset habit: an SSL-style channel compressor's "Z" preset — deliberately named to sort to the bottom of the preset list — used as his go-to always-on default: very light compression, barely audible, no EQ engaged, purely to "lift up the signal a little bit."** "Basically my ZZZZ setting... it drops down to the bottom of the list, so I use Z as my default. And my default is with the compressor in doing very light compression, barely hearing it, and it just kind of lifts up the signal a little bit. No EQ going on, but yeah, that's my SSL preset." Owned-lane stand-in for this always-on, barely-engaged, SSL-glue role: [[Glue Compressor]] set very light. → [[Glue Compressor]], [[Compression]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **A second, broader EQ pass after the compressor stage, kept deliberately small — around 2–2.5 dB.** "After all that processing, you might want to do a little bit more EQ and the Pro-Q 2 does a very good job of this. At this point, I am not doing anything more than, yeah, 2 dB here... 2.5, it looks like." *(general — Josh is eyeballing the plugin's own display, not reading off a settings sheet)* → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[EQ]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **Hunt her voice's specific "pop-out" resonance zone with narrow multiband cuts, in the 2.5–4.5 kHz range.** "When her voice hits certain frequencies, they pop out a lot, so I separate those tracks and I'm doing some multiband on these frequencies that are bothering me... I like to find the frequencies that bother me on vocals, usually between three and a half to four and a half, around there, or two and a half to four and a half. I pull those out." A concrete, artist-specific presence-zone number worth reusing as a first place to sweep-and-listen on a bright female pop vocal. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[LEAD Slot]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **[[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]] settings: Peak Reduction 3–7, Gain to taste — "for me it's between three and seven on this plug-in."** "Jump into the compressor, LA-2A, the LA-2A is a great vocal compressor to me. I love using it. It just makes my vocals for me, sound nice and even... the gain is always going to be a little louder on a plug-in, that's cool because you just get your peak reduction to where you like it... you can really push these if you want, but I don't like doing that too much." → [[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]], [[Compression]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **After the LA-2A, a multiband EQ tool doing ALL-subtractive, no-boost vocal shaping across low/mid/high — problem-frequency notches, a de-essing module, and low/mid shelving, all "very lightly."** "After the LA-2A I like using the C6 to shape my vocal EQ-wise, and I use it very lightly, hitting problem areas in my low frequencies and my high frequencies... I'll do some light de-essing work with this right here... if there's too much low end, I'll shelve it down and same thing for the mid... I'm not doing any boosting with this multiband, I'm doing all subtractive work getting rid of what I don't like." Read as McDSP C6 (unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]); owned-lane stand-in for the same multiband-subtractive-plus-de-ess job: [[Pro-MB]]. Contrast note in the same breath: [[Soothe2]] handles the *harsher* frequencies dynamically, this multiband handles static problem zones, and the SSL EQ upstream does the only *boosting* in the whole chain — three tools with three cleanly separated jobs (dynamic tame / static subtractive shape / broad boost). → [[Pro-MB]], [[Soothe2]], [[De-essing]], [[Multiband Compression]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **[[Soothe2]] settings: Mix at 38%, and a stated rule of thumb — never run a resonance-suppressor-style plugin at 100% mix on vocals.** "In this case I used Soothe, it's a great, resonant, pretty much like a resonant compressor. The more you push up, the more frequencies get pushed down... I set the mix really low, I got it on 38. These kinds of plug-ins, they're not that cool on 100, you know? Sometimes they are, but for vocals, they don't really do that for me, so I always drop the mix down." → [[Soothe2]], [[Multiband Compression]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **Sidechain-compress a lead vocal track off the kick, using a C2-style compressor — kept from the producer's stem even though its origin was unclear.** "This C2 on this track is being triggered by my kick... it's doing some side chaining on this vocal, which I'm not sure if Mark had that when he sent it to me, but it's on there now." A second instance was explicitly the producer's own choice and Josh's favorite moment in the song, kept untouched: "Mark already had that side-chaining on that part, I didn't do that, I wish I did that because that's one of my favorite parts of the song." → [[Sidechaining]], [[Compression]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **A creative reverb-drop revision reverted at the producer's request — respecting the artist/producer's call over the mixer's own preference.** "I originally had a hard reverb drop on that going into that 'all I see is you,' but Mark wanted the reverb back in, so I put it back in, it ain't no thing." A small but real reminder that mix revisions aren't always about technical correctness — sometimes the producer's creative call wins outright. → [[Mix Handoff]]
- **Kept an inherited plugin (RVox) on a "screwed hook" vocal without knowing for certain who put it there, because the sound already worked.** "This RVox was probably on there already... I'm lazy in the sense that if I want to make another change to the sound, I'll just put another plug-in on there and do that." Reinforces the "don't disturb a working chain, just add on top" habit that runs through the whole video. → [[RVox]], [[Mix Handoff]]
- **A personal default OPTO-style compressor setting for background/other vocal parts, with real numbers: mid Attack, mid Release, ~3.5 dB gain reduction, make-up gain set to roughly three-quarters of the GR amount.** "My basic vocal R compressor setting... it's OPTO, mid Attack, mid Release. I change my Release depending on the type of vocal it is... Three and a half, a little bit of make-up gain. If I'm compressing 3 dB on this R compressor, I'm boosting, you know, three-quarters of that for some make-up gain." Read as Waves R-Comp (unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]); the OPTO-character/moderate-attack-release/make-up-gain-proportional-to-GR recipe maps onto the owned optical-comp lane already covered by [[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]]. → [[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]], [[Compression]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **A FabFilter de-esser used with a plain, strong endorsement — "a fantastic de-esser, I love these guys" — on one of the vocal parts, no settings given.** → [[Pro-DS]], [[De-essing]]
- **Kept an existing plugin on an inherited vocal ("Studio D") specifically to preserve the producer's vibe rather than reprocess from scratch — the video's clearest single statement of Josh's overall mix philosophy.** "Studio D, this was already on here from Mark, so I kept it on there, again I'm not trying to take away the vibe, I'm trying to keep the vibe and build upon that." *(the plugin itself isn't identifiable with confidence from audio alone — logged qualitatively, no wishlist entry)* → [[Mix Handoff]]
- **Deliberately over-compressed a vocal part "for feel," not correctness — a stated exception to his normal light-touch habit.** "Some final compression. - **Kept the producer's original vocal reverb sends (a plate + a second, unclearly-captioned reverb) rather than rebuilding them — and ended up buying the plate plugin after mixing on the demo/trial version.** "When it comes to vocals, these guys already had some sends from Mark. I kept those on because the reverb actually sounded pretty cool and I ended up having to buy it, this Pure Plate... and this, what is it, clear? Yeah, clear, are the reverbs on this song... this Pure Plate kind of bit me in the ass because I demoed it and of course, I kept it, and then I had to buy it." The second reverb ("clear") is logged with low confidence — the owned Supertone [[Clear]] plugin is a vocal noise-reduction/clarity tool, not a reverb, so this is very likely a garbled caption for a different, unidentified reverb rather than that plugin; not routed to [[Clear]]'s note to avoid a false match. → [[UAD Pure Plate Reverb]], [[Reverb]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **A separate delay + reverb send pair also kept from the producer's engineer: an H-Delay and a Valhalla delay, praised for ease of use, plus a mid-range EQ cut (~1.5 kHz) shaping the harsh part of the return.** "So with this delay, this is also from Mark, H-Delay, Vintage Valhalla. This Valhalla is so easy to use. And then some EQ'ing on the harsh stuff, whatever midrange, what is that? 1.5 kHz?" Also sends to a Bricasti hardware reverb (unowned outboard unit — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) and a separate "hall" reverb plugin. → [[H-Delay]], [[ValhallaDelay]], [[Reverb]], [[Delay]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **A three-layer "ear candy" delay-throw send, dialed for feel rather than clarity: a quarter-note throw tucked low enough to not consciously register, a filtered reverb throw meant to be audibly heard, and a "half-verb blast" — set each, then turn the whole thing down further after listening back.** "Usually I'll tuck the quarter note so I don't really hear it, then I'll have a filtered reverb that I hear it, and then the half verb blast. And basically, once you hear these things, turn it down, listen to it again, and I usually just turn it down a little bit more. So those are more of a feel, those kinds of delay throws are just more for feeling." A reusable three-tier send-layering recipe (inaudible pulse / audible texture / big blast, then trim) for any "space that's felt more than heard" moment. → [[Delay]], [[Reverb]], [[Depth and Space]]
- **The video's headline reusable trick: a parallel "girth" aux — duplicate the vocal's master aux, filter/EQ it (default settings), hit it with an 1176-style compressor, and blend it back at roughly −33 dB for fullness/loudness without raising the main fader.** "On my lead vocals here, I'm doing some light parallel. So this is a duplicated master aux, just getting out some low and high. The basic ZZZZ function, this is like, my default for a Pro-Q2 right here. Then it's smashing an 1176, well I guess not a whole smash, but it's hitting an 1176... Then I just blend that up to taste into my mix... it's a little darker, kind of just opens it up for me. And this is down, you know, −33, so it's down there, but it's doing something. To me, it sounds better." Stated general workflow: "**That's a quick little trick if you need to get a little change in your vocal and you can't figure out what you're doing, just duplicate your aux and put some different plug-ins on there. Filter out frequencies that you don't need, and accentuate those that you do or take out the ones that are bothering you, and hit it with some compression, and turn it up or down to taste.**" Named use case here: a client revision asking for the vocal fuller/louder, solved with this instead of raw volume. The "1176" is captioned separately as "CLA-76... an older plug-in, but it still works very well" — read as Waves CLA-76, mapped to the owned [[UAD UA 1176LN]] for this technique. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[UAD UA 1176LN]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **Piano treated with very small, deliberately subtle moves, all under 2 dB total: some mid/side narrowing on the sides, light sidechain compression off the kick to pull certain frequencies more toward mono during kick hits, and a light overall EQ pass for thickness.** "This is all minor stuff, right? Taking out some sides, doing some really soft moves on here, a little bit of mid/side work on this piano, hitting it with a little bit of side chain off the kick, making it a little more mono in certain frequencies, and then doing some overall light EQ. Nothing over... nothing over 2 dB. For me it just kind of thickened him up a little bit." → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Sidechaining]], [[EQ]], [[1 Electric Piano Track]]
- **A synth-guitar hybrid tone gets only light compression control, no active EQ, and a note-worthy sound-selection observation: the source is ambiguous between guitar and synth and that's fine — "either way, it sounds pretty rad."** "Is that a guitar or is that a synth thing? Synth guitar. Either way, it sounds pretty rad. Just doing some light control with the compression, not much. That EQ is on, not doing anything." → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[1 Electric Guitar DI Track]]
- **A reusable stereo-correction move: run compression UNLINKED (dual-mono) across a guitar's L/R channels specifically because the recorded signal was right-heavy, so the compressor engages more on the loud side and evens the two channels out — instead of the usual linked-stereo default.** "So hitting it with mono, which means you know, the left and right channel operate with their own brand through this threshold, and this was a right-heavy signal, so I'm hitting the right side with a little more compression, this is evening them out ever so slightly." Plus a static EQ pass on the same track: a resonance pulled down ~2 dB, more midrange presence, reduced hiss, boosted low end. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Compression]], [[1 Electric Guitar DI Track]]
- **Outro-guitar bus hit harder than the vocal chain, wetness intentionally dropped, because it's not a vocal.** "Some Trash. And some Soothe. Yeah, so, this isn't a vocal, so I'm hitting it a little harder than I normally would, well for vocals. I dropped my wetness down just so it's hitting it like..." Trash (iZotope, unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) for drive/character, [[Soothe2]] pushed harder than its vocal-chain setting. → [[Soothe2]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[2 Guitar Group]]
- **Bass philosophy: when tracks come from a strong producer, start with blend only — add EQ/compression only if the source genuinely needs it, not by default.** "Again, when you're getting tracks from a really good producer, usually the sounds that are coming in are top-notch. The first thing I do is I blend, and then I, you know, design with the EQ and compression if need be." → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Electric Bass DI Track]]
- **Bass character stage: Trash + Clip Control for light distortion, credited to a named colleague as the source of that specific move; plus a saturation/character plugin ("Dark Essence") flagged as good for both bass AND vocals.** "The only thing I really did on this guy was throw some Trash on there, a little Clip Control. I'll just say Seth Hunter put me on to that... Dark Essence is usually good for bass and vocals. I like them on vocals." Both unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Electric Bass DI Track]]
- **Bass sidechained off the kick specifically for the drop section, ~2.5 dB gain reduction.** "And then getting hit with the side-chain on the kick. So I'm also hitting this bass with side-chain on the drop... Hitting it 2.5 dB." → [[Sidechaining]], [[Bass Design]], [[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Electric Bass DI Track]]
- **Drums left almost entirely alone because the source already sounded good — the same "don't fix what isn't broken" philosophy as the bass section, stated even more bluntly.** "I didn't do anything to the kicks. The drums I kind of left how they were because they sounded so good when I got them. Like, I just messed with the blend, I didn't change anything that didn't need to be changed." → [[Mix Handoff]], [[2 Drums Group]]
- **Light Decapitator Drive on hi-hats, with an A/B-by-listening habit across drive "styles" (algorithms) to pick a winner rather than committing to one by default.** "On the hi-hats I got a little bit of the Decapitator doing a little bit of Drive. And you know, I'll jump from all these little Styles, listening to the mix and see which wins and go with it." → [[Decapitator]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[2 Drums Group]]
- **Decapitator, Transient Designer, and Transient Master all kept loaded on toms/drum loops as standby tools, used only when a specific hit needs transient shaping — not applied as a blanket default.** "As you see on the toms and the drum loops, I have Decapitator, Transient Designer and Transient Master in case I need to do any transient love." Transient Designer (SPL, hardware/unowned) already logged on [[Plugin Wishlist]] from a separate video. → [[Decapitator]], [[Transient Master]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[2 Drums Group]]
- **The outro live-kit/live-guitar section (one combined track) runs filtering, de-essing, tape emulation/saturation "whatever feels good," into a Fatso-style tape compressor/saturator for glue.** "Hear that guitar spring, nice room, filtering, filtering, de-essing, some tape emulation, saturation, whatever feels good, do it. And this is going to this Fatso right here, and this Fatso is getting hit, and that's getting hit, and that gets blended in." Fatso (Empirical Labs-style, unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]). → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[2 Guitar Group]]
- **A spring reverb, automated and used deliberately once, for a single bridge "swirl"/swell moment that then drops out completely into the hook — a reusable "reserve creative automation for a handful of deliberate moments" philosophy.** "I did put the spring on this guy... it's automated... I was doing spring automation there... Kind of doing some reverb swells with this bridge to make it like... just to give it a little bit more life... whether you could hear that or not, it does something to the ear, and it does something to the mind, and for me, it grows that bridge part. And then it drops out completely in the hook... I like to use a Spring for those kinds of little moments. For transitions, I usually don't do much to them except balance them." Vendor unconfirmed on-screen (he even second-guesses the label himself: "Where is this Plate? It says Plate, but it's a spring.") — logged with a wishlist entry rather than force-matched to any owned plugin. → [[Reverb]], [[Automation]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Closing mix philosophy: with a track that already sounds strong from the producer, build off their mix rather than replace it — balance the uneven parts, leave the rest, and save the "add creative energy" instinct for songs that actually need it.** "With a song that already sounds good from a producer that if it came out, it wouldn't be a disaster, I like to approach my mix a little differently. I approached it with their mix in mind, I like to build off their mix and just enhance it and complement it. If there are some resonance or vocals are a little uneven, I try to balance those out, just craft the mix to where it feels good from start to finish and I don't want to change anything anymore... Sometimes I'll try to, creatively, if a song doesn't make me feel a certain type of way, I'll have to add all this stuff emotionally in the mix to make me feel good. In this case, I didn't really have to do much, just some blending, some vocal work, and just pulling it all together." Also notes the producer's own revision pass stayed almost entirely arrangement-level (timing/level tweaks), not tonal — a signal the mix had already landed. → [[Mix Handoff]]

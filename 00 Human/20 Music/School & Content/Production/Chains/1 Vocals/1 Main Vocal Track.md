# Main Vocal Track

**Role:** The lead. Center, loudest vocal element — everything else in the vocal stack supports it.

**Chain philosophy:** tune → corrective EQ → leveling comp → resonance tame → color → tone EQ → de-ess → air. Corrective before compression, saturation after compression, de-ess near the end (sibilance is shaped by everything before it).

## 1 — Recording Setup

- **Mic:** [[Neumann TLM 102]], shock mount, pop filter ~4" out, 48V on. Room: phone silenced, fan/HVAC off (or [[Gate]] at −40 dB in the monitor chain to clear what's left — Kenny's number).
- 🎬 *From video:* a singer's own mic technique can do most of the tracking compressor's job before the signal ever reaches it — Al Schmitt's vocal chain barely engaged compression at all because the singer "leans in and backs off" (proximity effect, self-managed live) — "makes my life so much easier." Worth checking for on any singer before assuming §3's CL 1B stage needs to work hard: good mic discipline at the source means lighter GR downstream, not a settings problem to chase. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]], [[UAD Neve 1073]] — Al Schmitt (Mix with the Masters), [[Recording techniques with Al Schmitt]]
- 🎬 *From video:* pick the mic to add warmth on an already-good singer, not just to correct a weak one — a distinct rationale from this note's own condenser-for-detail framing. Go-to pair: Sony C800 or Elam 251, chosen specifically "because it has warmth to it" on singers already strong enough to sing with minimal correction. Owned equivalent: [[Neumann TLM 102|TLM 102]]. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]] — Kuk Harrell, [[Radio-ready vocal effects with Kuk Harrell]]
- **Before anyone sings: lock the TEMPO and the KEY** — wrong tempo = un-loopable stems and a session reset mid-take; unknown key = no [[Auto-Tune Pro]] when someone asks. Find the key at a piano if nobody knows it — Kenny Beats, [Recording Vocals the Right Way](https://youtu.be/3Nw1d37nluo).
- **Latency:** buffer **128** (64 if the machine allows), driver error compensation to ~0 ms, [[Pro-Q 4]] in Zero Latency mode while tracking — see [[Latency-Free Tracking]]. (Monitoring through UAD Console sidesteps this; the settings matter when the monitor chain runs in Ableton.)
- **Track vocals in a separate session from the beat** — bounce the beat to a 2-track first: CPU stays fast at low buffer, and the bounce commits you ("imperfections you were gonna fix end up making the song") — Kenny Beats, [Vocal Template](https://youtu.be/X_iDENABLmk).
- **Monitoring:** [[Audio-Technica ATH-M50x]] closed-back. Interface: [[Universal Audio Apollo Twin MkII]], peaks −12 dBFS, average ~−18 ([[Gain Staging]]).
- 🎬 *From video:* put a heavy "slappy" delay on the monitor chain BEFORE the vocal is written/tracked, not after — the singer performs against the audible repeats and the delay shapes actual phrasing/syllable placement, not just space added later. Finneas on Billie Eilish's "Birds of a Feather": she "was able to sing with that while we were writing the song and have the delay to feed off of." → [[Delay]] (effect) — Finneas, [[Behind the Track - 'BIRDS OF A FEATHER' by Billie Eilish]]

## 2 — Tracking Best Practice

- **Record every take on TWO tracks at once** — the monitored FX track *and* a ghost **latency track** (zero plugins, monitor off, out to a spare hardware pair, not the master). Keep the ghost take — it lands on the grid; drag it into [[2 Main Vocal Group|Main Vocal Group]], which carries the chain — Kenny Beats, [Recording Without Latency](https://youtu.be/Gj4_IvHFn6g) → [[Latency-Free Tracking]].
- 🎬 *From video:* a related dual-channel variant with a different purpose — an "auxiliary input" setup where one channel carries the plain take (the actual keeper, what gets comped/edited) and a second channel carries a live-autotuned/processed monitor feed used only to judge in the moment whether more takes are needed, never as the edited source. Stated side benefit: the setup keeps the control room quiet during takes instead of interrupting the singer to react to each pass — see [[Vocal Mic Technique]]. → [[Latency-Free Tracking]] — Mix with the Masters (Andrés Torres and Mauricio Rengifo), [[Vocal production with Andrés Torres and Mauricio Rengifo]]
- **Mono, one lead.** Doubles/harmonies/ad-libs are their own chains ([[1 Double Vocal Track|Double Vocal Track]], [[1 Harmony Vocal Track|Harmony Vocal Track]], [[1 Backup Vocal Track|Backup Vocal Track]]) — don't stack on the lead track.
- **"Do enough takes, you can make it sound good"** — Howard Lawrence's comping confidence rule (it made SG Lewis a singer); 3–5 takes then comp, don't chase the perfect single take.
- **Different mics for different SECTIONS** — build hook-vs-verse contrast at the source: Disclosure cut a hook into a harmonica mic (crushed lo-fi) with clean upfront verses, and Lavender's released lead is an **SM-58 → Distressor → distortion** ("it sounds banging"). Owned lo-fi lane: [[Shure SM57]] → [[UAD UA 1176LN]] slammed → [[Decapitator]] — [ENERGY Release Party](https://youtu.be/fMqPuf1UVzI).
- **Playback psychology:** make the group chain slightly nicer than the headphone chain — artists hear the drag-down sounding *better* than the take felt and push harder — Kenny Beats, [New Vocal Chain 2022](https://youtu.be/-SvBNOBWJ_8).
- 🎬 *From video:* 070 Shake tracks full continuous takes and explicitly avoids comping — redoes the WHOLE take rather than punching in, aiming for "one expression and one flow" (a harder-line version of the "do enough takes" rule above: no fragment-assembly at all) → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]] — Mix with the Masters, [Behind the Track｜'Elephant' by 070 Shake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OKBT8-0l9AI)
- 🎬 *From video:* don't stop a singer to chase "the perfect take" — let them sing the same phrase repeatedly (stated as seven times) without commentary, unless there's something genuinely specific to correct. "It becomes a beautiful process if you make it something mechanical, singing that part seven times without needing to correct it, unless you need to tell them something specific." If the singer "has the magic," the right take arrives on its own; repeated generic notes are framed as worse than just letting more passes happen. Named tradeoff: this whole approach takes LONGER overall than recording to a single mixed channel, because of the extra comping pass it creates — worth it for how it treats the singer. → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]] — Mix with the Masters (Andrés Torres and Mauricio Rengifo), [[Vocal production with Andrés Torres and Mauricio Rengifo]]

## 3 — Source → Ableton (UAD Console)

Printed on the way in — conservative, because printed is forever.

### 1. [[UAD Neve 1073]] — Unison preamp
- The pop/rock vocal classic — mid-forward weight and silk. Gain into gentle color, level to ~0 VU
- **Alt:** [[UAD UA 610-B]] for rounder tube warmth on soft/ballad vocals.

### 2. [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII]] — Tracking compressor
- Ratio ~2:1–3:1 · attack **fast**, release **slow/auto** (the mkII's fixed-attack/release plus manual mode) · **2–4 dB GR** — a smooth opto *ride*, not a peak grab (Kenny's rule: "never past 7 on the needle, 10 is really pushing it")
- This is an **optical/tube leveler** going in — it rides the whole phrase like a fader and prints tube warmth + silk, where the old 1176 caught fast transients. Let the opto do the gentle glue; the fast peak-precision now lives on [[Pro-C 2]] in Ableton (§4.3).
- mkII extras worth using on the way in: **sidechain HP filter** so low-end doesn't pump the gain, and keep **wet/dry mix ~100%** (parallel is a mix-phase move, not a print move).
- 🎬 *From video:* a concrete recipe for this exact stage from a UAD-interface tracking chain — Release **Medium/Medium-fast**, Attack **Medium/Medium-slow** (song-dependent), **Threshold left alone, level controlled entirely via Input drive**, targeting only slight needle movement: bumps to ~−1 dB, peaks −3 to −7 dB at the very most ("I'm not looking for crazy −10, −20... just a healthy movement"). Monitored live with reverb + a delay on a secondary Aux while tracking, so the take is sung against real space from the first pass. → [[Latency-Free Tracking]], [[The Weeknd Vocal Chain]] — Illangelo (Mix with the Masters), [[Producing The Weeknd's vocals with Illangelo]]
### 3. [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]] — Broad EQ
- Low ATTEN 1–2 @ 60–100 Hz (rumble) · +1–2 dB @ 12 kHz air, bandwidth broad · print conservatively — tone-shaping happens in Ableton.
- 🎬 *From video:* a contrasting case where the Pultec instead **boosts** ~100 Hz hard rather than attenuating — used to put analog richness back into a voice an earlier in-the-box parallel-double stage had already thinned out, then feeds a fast-attack/fast-release 1176 downstream so the compressor reacts to the boosted bass response too (not just peaks). Only reach for this on a chain that's genuinely thinned the source first — it's compensation, not a default. → [[UAD UA 1176LN]], [[Compression Staging]], [[BASS Slot]] — Mix with the Masters (Ben Baptie), [[Julian Casablancas' vocal sound with Ben Baptie]]

**While tracking, the Ableton monitor chain is lean:** [[Gate]] (−40 to −43 dB, room noise between phrases) → light EQ → nothing latent. The "sound-good-izer" lift stays OFF until the song is done — it adds latency and character artists get used to — Kenny Beats, [Vocal Template](https://youtu.be/X_iDENABLmk).

## Prep — before any plugin (between tracking and mixing)

- **Clip-gain the comp to ~0 VU / −18 dBFS average** — pull the big volume jumps down by hand first; the compressor then works on musical dynamics, and every analog-modeled plugin gets its calibrated level.
- **Chop out ALL dead space between phrases** (keep natural breaths, never clip word edges) — every compressor downstream raises the floor of whatever you leave; uncut room noise across stacked tracks is why "the vocal feels muddy on the beat." **Editing is gain-staging** — Kenny Beats, [Recording Vocals the Right Way](https://youtu.be/3Nw1d37nluo).
- Edit pops, mouth noise, big breaths; nail timing; manual pitch surgery (Melodyne-level) *before* the chain — no plugin fixes a sloppy comp.
- Flip [[Pro-Q 4]] instances from Zero Latency to **Natural Phase** now that tracking is done — Kenny Beats, [Vocal Template](https://youtu.be/X_iDENABLmk).

## 4 — Ableton Initial Processing

**From the [[0 Vocal Foundation|Vocal Foundation]] →** the lead is the **reference the foundation was carved out of**, so its front is the block *verbatim, at neutral defaults* — §4.1–4.4 (Auto-Tune @ Retune 45–50, corrective Pro-Q, Pro-C 2 leveling, Soothe2) **are** the Vocal Foundation with nothing overridden. Where the other roles bend or decline the block, the lead is the one part that then runs the **full character/tone/air tail**: §4.5 [[Saturn 2]] color → §4.6 [[Pro-Q 4]] tone boosts → §4.7 [[Pro-DS]] finishing de-ess → §4.8 [[Gem Dopamine]] air. It gets the complete chain because everything else in the stack is built to support it.

### 1. [[Auto-Tune Pro]] — Pitch
- Key/scale: match song (④) · **Retune 45** (① — natural pop; ~50 is the proven "invisible" default, 30–35 tighter) · **Humanize 100%** (③) · **Flex-Tune 0** (②) · **input voice type set to the voice** (④)
- The retune map: **~30–40 = invisible support** ("spotting you while you bench") · **~7 = deliberately audible** (Travis/Migos/Greedo bite) · **>10–12 = full T-Pain chip**. Kenny's alternative: Humanize 0, naturalness from low retune + **Legato** style — pick ONE philosophy per song. And set the **input voice type** (Low Male/Alto/Soprano — a range switch) or the tracking misreads everything — [Auto-Tune Process](https://youtu.be/dyr6F9DLG7c)
- Pro move: scale on Chromatic, remove out-of-key notes on the keyboard (advanced view) — per-note control for borrowed notes.
- **Why:** First in chain so everything downstream reacts to corrected pitch.
- 🎬 *From video:* 070 Shake & Dave have pulled Auto-Tune back further than ever for a rawer vocal, pairing minimal correction with almost no other vocal FX ("it's really just how she sounds") → [[Vocal Tuning]] — Mix with the Masters, [Behind the Track｜'Elephant' by 070 Shake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OKBT8-0l9AI)
- 🎬 *From video:* strip tuning off ONE specific section (not the whole vocal) for a raw, spoken quality against a bigger, corrected chorus — "take the tune off of the first verse... it should just feel so raw it sounds like she's just speaking," breaths kept in on purpose → [[Vocal Tuning]] — Mix with the Masters (Benny Blanco & Selena Gomez), [Behind the Track｜'Younger And Hotter Than Me' by Selena Gomez & benny blanco](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rkJ-LaoArUY)

### 2. [[Pro-Q 4]] — Corrective EQ
- HPF 24 dB/oct @ 80–100 Hz · cut mud −2 to −4 dB @ 200–400 Hz (narrow-ish) · check 2–5 kHz for harshness/nasality
- **Why:** Cuts before compression so the comp doesn't fight rumble and mud.
- 🎬 *From video:* trim a touch of low end even on a vocal meant to stay rich — "I'm getting a little bit of a low end thing, even though I want his voice to be rich, I'm taking a little bit of that off." Richness and low-end control as two separate axes: keep the body, trim the excess underneath it. *(general starting point — no Hz/dB given)* → [[EQ]] (effect), [[BASS Slot]] — Chris Lord-Alge (Mix with the Masters), [[Chris Lord-Alge processing vocals]]
- 🎬 *From video:* aggressive, repeated narrow-Q ("tight Q's") notching by ear to dig out harsh, ear-piercing resonances — "don't be afraid... go in there and find those frequencies that are harsh and pierce into your ear, find them, and pull them out... I'm not afraid to notch multiple times if I have to, whatever it takes." Framed as a loud-playback-safety move (a spiky frequency "kills" the ear when played loud), not just tone-shaping. One problem zone named but garbled by captions — roughly **~2.2–2.8 kHz**. → [[EQ]] (effect), [[De-essing]], [[Masking and Carving]] — Mix with the Masters (Stuart White), [[Mixing vocals in the box with Stuart White]]

### 3. [[Pro-C 2]] — Leveling compression
- Style: Vocal · attack 10 ms · release auto · **3–5 dB GR** · range slider caps GR at ~5 dB so it can't overreact
- **Why:** Second-stage leveler (the [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII|CL 1B]] at tracking was stage one). The CL 1B is a slow opto that rides the *program*; Pro-C 2 set fast catches the quick transients the opto lets through — two complementary curves, which is exactly why staged compression sounds smoother than one deep comp ([[Compression Staging]]).
- 🎬 *From video:* dial compression toward a named character quality, not just level control — "that is good tense, that's exactly what I want." Worth using a felt-quality word (here: "tense," a taut/held-back energy) as the target when ratio/threshold alone doesn't capture what the compressor should do to the performance. *(qualitative — no ratio/threshold/GR given)* → [[Compression]] (effect) — Chris Lord-Alge (Mix with the Masters), [[Chris Lord-Alge processing vocals]]
- 🎬 *From video:* a lighter-touch alternate recipe from a fixed tracking-vocal chain — peak-catch only ("compressing just a little bit to kind of keep the peaks out"), immediately followed by [[RVox]] doing the actual push-the-vocal-forward job as a separate stage. Worth reaching for on a quieter/gentler take where 3–5 dB GR reads as too much. → [[RVox]] — Kuk Harrell, [[Radio-ready vocal effects with Kuk Harrell]]
- 🎬 *From video:* a distinct place to run [[Pro-L 2]] worth knowing — directly on the LEAD VOCAL TRACK, not just the master bus — with Attack deliberately set slow so each phrase's initial transient pokes through before the limiter clamps down (peaks visibly land at phrase starts as a result). Philosophy behind it: many gentle stages in series rather than one hard clamp, so the vocal never arrives "already clamped down." Sits downstream of a gate (his [[Pro-G]]) and a de-esser (unowned Eiosis E2, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) in the source chain — see [[The Weeknd Vocal Chain]] for the full ordering. → [[Pro-L 2]], [[Limiting]] (effect), [[The Weeknd Vocal Chain]] — Illangelo (Mix with the Masters), [[Producing The Weeknd's vocals with Illangelo]]

### 4. [[Soothe2]] — Resonance suppression
- **Soft mode** · default sharpness/selectivity (~3) · raise depth until clearly working, back off slightly · focus 1–6 kHz · delta mode to hear what it's removing
- **Why:** Dynamic harshness control static EQ can't do; before saturation so you're not exciting resonances.

### 5. [[Saturn 2]] — Color
- **Warm Tape or Warm Tube** (③), **single band** (①) · **Drive ~15%** (② — low) · **Mix 30%** (⑤ — 20–40% window)
- **Why:** Density + harmonics so the vocal reads on small speakers. After compression = consistent drive level.
- 🎬 *From video:* a contrasting order worth knowing — an unnamed "warmth" stage run FIRST/early instead, subtle enough that toggling it on/off is the only way to hear it: "it kind of got forward a little bit, and it gave it a little bit of warmth. It's subtle, but it's nice." One stage in a deliberately gradual, many-plugins-each-doing-a-little-thing chain rather than this note's own after-compression placement — no product/settings named. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Compression Staging]] — Mix with the Masters (Stuart White), [[Mixing vocals in the box with Stuart White]]

### 6. [[Pro-Q 4]] — Tone EQ (additive)
- Presence +1–2 dB @ 3–5 kHz · air shelf +1–2 dB @ 10 kHz+
- 🎬 *Carve, don't just boost:* the original "Help Me Lose My Mind" boosted vocal highs **+12 dB** ("psychopath stuff"). Keep this move **small (+2–3 dB), dynamic mode** so it de-esses as it lifts; lean on subtractive EQ for the rest, and get air from **[[Gem Dopamine]]** (adds highs *with* compression/de-ess) rather than a raw shelf — Disclosure, [Help Me Lose My Mind](https://youtu.be/FPgLRkgKoMU).
- **Why:** Boosts after compression/saturation — shape the finished texture.
- 🎬 *From video:* a simpler, fixed 3-band alternate recipe run identically on every singer — high shelf ~10 kHz for air, small mid bump, small third band, "all I need is just high-end." Deliberately non-diagnostic (same three moves regardless of the voice) — a fallback when there's no time to tailor per-singer. → [[EQ]] (effect) — Kuk Harrell, [[Radio-ready vocal effects with Kuk Harrell]]

### 7. [[Pro-DS]] — De-ess
- **Single Vocal mode** · split-band · sidechain filters framed on the S region (~4–8 kHz) · range 4–6 dB · audition button to hear only what's ducked
- **Why:** Late in chain — comp + saturation both push sibilance up. (Wideband + no lookahead = zero latency, safe while tracking.)
- 🎬 *From video:* "some nice, subtle DS" — a light touch, no product/frequency/amount given, logged only to confirm de-essing has a place even in a genre (rock) where the mix goal is deliberately warm/dark rather than pop-bright. → [[De-essing]] — Mix with the Masters (Stuart White), [[Mixing vocals in the box with Stuart White]]
- 🎬 *From video:* a tape machine can do this stage's job as a side effect of its own saturation/EQ character, via a named preset, rather than a dedicated de-esser — Studer A800, "Pop" setting, chosen specifically to make a background-role lead vocal thinner while de-essing it at the same time. Owned equivalent: [[Kramer Tape]] dialed toward thin/bright. → [[Kramer Tape]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Tony Maserati Vocal Chain]] — Tony Maserati (Mix with the Masters), [[Vocal chain with Tony Maserati]]
- 🎬 *From video:* a **layered two-de-esser** discipline worth adopting on a busy chain — one plugin does the heavy lifting (an unowned Eiosis E2 Deesser gets the video's single strongest endorsement: "nothing does what this de-esser does... it's my first choice" — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]), then a SECOND, lighter de-esser runs later in the chain purely as touch-up, "chipping away at any syllables or sibilances" the first stage missed — explicitly not doing the heavy lifting itself. Maps onto this note's own §7 as: [[Pro-DS]] does the primary pass, a second light [[Pro-DS]] instance (or a dynamic [[Pro-Q 4]] band) mops up after later chain stages (Saturn 2, tone EQ) have had a chance to reintroduce sibilance. Also upstream in the same source chain: a dedicated [[Pro-G]] gate creating deliberate contrast between the hyper-compressed vocal and audible space between phrases (opens above threshold, tucks ~12 dB below it) — run BEFORE this de-essing stage, not after. → [[Pro-G]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[The Weeknd Vocal Chain]] — Illangelo (Mix with the Masters), [[Producing The Weeknd's vocals with Illangelo]]

### 8. [[Gem Dopamine]] — Final air (optional)
- **Wet 30–40%** (① — how much Dolby-A-style sheen the encoder adds) · **Compression** engaged (② — the dynamic/de-ess action that keeps the added air off the S's) · **Frequency/Tone** focused on the top octave (③) · **Mix** to taste (④) · **Model** (⑤) — audition both emulated units, they're voiced differently
- **Why:** Dead last so it airs the *finished* texture. Like Fresh Air it's a **dynamic** HF enhancer rather than a static shelf, so the lift tracks the program and reads as sheen instead of harshness. The reason it's here **instead of [[Fresh Air]]**: Gem Dopamine has its own compression/de-ess stage built into the air circuit, so the enhancement self-polices sibilance — which matters at the very end of a chain where §3's compression and §4's saturation have already pushed the S's up. Fresh Air has no such stage, so it needed [[Pro-DS]] in §7 to have done a perfect job first.
- **Still skip it entirely if the [[Pro-Q 4]] air shelf in §6 already did the job** — two air stages stack into sibilance fast, dynamic or not.
- 🎬 *From video:* independent confirmation this dedicated-air-band slot is worth having — Illangelo runs [[Utility]] into an unnamed EQ (name not captured — the source transcript cuts off before he states it) built around a dedicated **"Air band"** control, boosted gently around **20 kHz**, and calls it his favorite plugin overall — "such beautiful high end frequency content in such a way that I haven't found anything else that comes close to it." Compares it directly, unprompted, to **owned [[Fresh Air]]**: "I think Slate Digital might have just released something called Fresh Air... it's fire. This one also does a similar thing." Worth auditioning [[Fresh Air]] or [[Gem Dopamine]] in this exact "final dedicated air stage after everything else" slot as the owned stand-in. → [[Fresh Air]], [[EQ]] (effect), [[The Weeknd Vocal Chain]] — Illangelo (Mix with the Masters), [[Producing The Weeknd's vocals with Illangelo]]

## 5 — Track FX (optional)

Usually nothing — the lead's character comes from §3–4, and space lives on sends. When a section needs an event:
- **Throw the last word** to an effects track: loud insert [[EchoBoy]], high end cut, subdivision per throw — Kenny Beats, [New Vocal Chain 2022](https://youtu.be/-SvBNOBWJ_8)
- **Guest-vocal lean recipe** (when someone else's processed vocal arrives): compression + mostly *dynamic* EQ (cut low mids, boost highs) + two delays (1/4 + 8th-triplet) + reverb — Disclosure's whole ENERGY-features chain — [ENERGY Release Party](https://youtu.be/fMqPuf1UVzI).
- 🎬 *From video:* a doubler as a permanent width stage on the lead itself, not a fallback — insert [[Doubler2]] (Illangelo used iZotope Vocal Doubler) directly here and automate Dry/Wet from ~50% in the song's first half up to ~90% in the second half, so the lead progressively widens/thickens over the arrangement ("3D" build) → [[Stereo Field]] — Illangelo, [How Illangelo mixes The Weeknd's vocals](https://youtube.com/shorts/fG2s168La_E?is=dN4ixQppvlexuZOz)
- 🎬 *From video:* the doubler audibility bar for this whole slot, whichever recipe above is running — never phasey/chorus-like, but load-bearing enough that removing it is audibly worse. "A little bit of kind of a doubler effect is nice if it doesn't sound phasey, if it doesn't sound like a chorus thing" — on a rock track specifically, a little doubling avoids having to turn the vocal fader up, and the target is "if you take the doubler out, it's like — what happened, why doesn't it sound as good." Keep Doubler2's Modulation Rate/Depth low enough to stay clear of that phasey/chorus line. → [[Artificial Double Tracking (ADT)]], [[Doubler2]] — Greg Wells (Mix with the Masters), [[Bringing vocals out of the speakers with Greg Wells]]
- 🎬 *From video:* a deliberately dense BRIDGE-only vocal event chain, built to contrast a simple song — pull the level down on a raw/borrowed vocal fragment, distort it ([[Saturn 2]], "I like Saturn a lot, it's cool"), add delay and reverb ([[Lair]] + others — see [[Reverb]] (effect)), then a [[Vocoder]] pass, so the vocal gets "pretty crazy"; finish by cutting everything except the vocal so it still reads as vocal, not wash → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Delay]] (effect) — Mix with the Masters (Benny Blanco & Selena Gomez), [Behind the Track｜'Younger And Hotter Than Me' by Selena Gomez & benny blanco](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rkJ-LaoArUY)

## 6 — Bus / Send FX (optional)

Routing: **Main Vocal → [[2 Main Vocal Group|Main Vocal Group]] → [[3 Vocal Bus|Vocal Bus]] → [[Master Chain]]** — "the group has the chain": comp takes live on dry tracks, the group carries the shared chain, the bus carries the finish (the final lift stays OFF until the song is done).

- **[[Reverb Send Room]]** low — body, not audible space
- 🎬 *From video:* peel this send back further than feels natural — CLA runs a vocal reverb at roughly **5% wet**, testing by ear for a "zombie vocal" (audibly-wet = too much) rather than a meter reading; also reaches for **Waves CLA Epic** (unowned — [[Plugin Wishlist]]), a combined reverb+delay unit, to run this send and the delay send below as one plugin instance instead of two. → [[Reverb]] (effect), [[Plugin Wishlist]] — Chris Lord-Alge (Mix with the Masters), [[Chris Lord-Alge processing vocals]]
- **[[Delay Send Slap]]** behind the voice — Howard's one-slap rule: single short repeat, very low, "sounds like you're in a small club"
- 🎬 *From video:* a second, independent case for slap-only (no reverb at all) as the lead's ENTIRE effect budget for a whole song — start completely bone dry, hold effects back, then bring the slap in gradually as the song progresses to build intensity rather than volume. Against tightly-picked rhythm guitar, the slap's repeat creates a second rhythmic pulse that syncopates against the guitar, tying the whole arrangement's rhythm together. → [[Delay]] (effect), [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Mix with the Masters (Ben Baptie), [[Julian Casablancas' vocal sound with Ben Baptie]]
- **[[Width Send]]** on hooks · **[[Saturation Send]]** for attitude sections
- 🎬 *From video:* a minimal three-send philosophy for the whole session — reverb (fixed medium hall, [[Pro-R 2]] best-match) + delay (fixed quarter-note ping-pong, [[H-Delay]]) + doubler ([[Doubler2]], subtle width) and nothing else routed through additional aux, specifically so the session hands off to a mix engineer with zero cleanup needed. Built live into the artist's headphone monitor while tracking, not added afterward. → [[Pro-R 2]], [[H-Delay]], [[Doubler2]], [[Mix Handoff]] — Kuk Harrell, [[Radio-ready vocal effects with Kuk Harrell]]
- 🎬 *From video:* the send-FX character philosophy this whole section is built around, from the front — a tape-style delay chosen for being inaccurate (mid-focused, rolled off top/bottom, "dreamy") with a slow modulation swim, paired with a chamber reverb dialed "dead on top" rather than clean/accurate, and — separately — a ping-pong rolled-off delay doubled up with a second independent MONO delay behind it purely for "weird depth perception." "I'm a big fan of kind of tape delay on a vocal because it's not very accurate on top or bottom — it's all sort of mid-range... I like reverbs to be sort of dead on top... I hate really clean and accurate reverbs." Owned lane: [[EchoBoy]] on a tape Echo Type with Low/High Cut pulled in and light Wow & Flutter for the delay send; [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] Chamber mode with High Shelf pulled down for the reverb send. *(qualitative — no time/feedback/decay values given)* → [[Delay]] (effect), [[Reverb]] (effect), [[Depth and Space]], [[EchoBoy]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] — Greg Wells (Mix with the Masters), [[Bringing vocals out of the speakers with Greg Wells]]
- Delay returns sidechain-duck to this vocal ([[Sidechaining]]) — tails tuck under words, bloom in gaps. This lead is the **duck key** for every vocal-lane delay return.
- **Delay rule of thumb:** glue/ambient delay → the shared group send (ducked to this lead); a specific word → the throw insert in §5 ([[EchoBoy]] on a dedicated effects track), never the whole-part send. Cohesion → group send · moment → insert.

## Variations

- **Hip hop / trap:** Retune 0–20 (hard tune), 1176-style comp harder (4–6 dB GR), drier — slap delay instead of reverb, more Saturn drive.
- **Beatles / vintage:** skip Auto-Tune or Humanize 100%, swap Saturn for [[Kramer Tape]], [[LALA]] instead of Pro-C 2, room reverb up.
- **Lo-fi / crushed:** the SM-58→Distressor lane above, or see [[Gorillaz Vocal Chain]].

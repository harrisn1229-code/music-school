# Simmons SDS-V

## What it is
Not a drum machine but a **drum synthesizer** — an analog brain triggered by Simmons' iconic hexagonal pads, played by an actual drummer. The SDS-V (SDS-5) is the "80s electronic tom" incarnate: the descending *doooom* fill that stamps a record with its decade in half a second.

## What makes the sound
- **Per-voice analog synthesis, not samples**: a swept oscillator (the pitch-drop envelope is *the* sound), a noise source, and a click/attack component, each with its own filter and envelope — kick, snare, and three toms as five little synthesizers on cards.
- **The tom voice** = triangle/sine swept downward with resonance — huge, hollow, synthetic; run through a hall reverb (or gated verb) it becomes the canonical 80s fill → pure [[TRANSIENT Slot]] drama.
- **Velocity-ish dynamics from real playing** — pads feed playing dynamics into the circuits, so it phrases like a drummer, unlike any step-programmed box of its era.
- The snare's noise/tone crack cuts like a rifle shot; the whole kit is mix-ready loud, no low-end mud.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[DS Tom]] — Ableton's DS drum synths are literally this architecture (swept osc + noise + click); [[DS Kick]]/[[DS Snare]] for the rest of the kit; [[Microtonic]] for a full synthesized-kit approach; gate a [[ValhallaVintageVerb]]/[[Ghz Megaverb 3]] tail to complete the cliché.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2 (Simmons kits), assorted SDS sample libraries.
- **Free:** SDS-V one-shots are in every 80s sample archive; the [[DS Tom]] recipe costs nothing.

## Used on
- Duran Duran — mid-80s records/tours ("The Wild Boys" era), Roger Taylor's hybrid kit.
- Rush — Neil Peart's mid-80s electronic-kit phase (*Grace Under Pressure* era) ran Simmons pads.
- Genre/era: 1982–87 pop/rock/R&B wholesale — the electronic tom fill, the [[Synthwave]] revival's favorite drum cliché to quote.

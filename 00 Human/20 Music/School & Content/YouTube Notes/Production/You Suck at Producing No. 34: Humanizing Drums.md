## Synopsis
A short, older entry in the "You Suck at Producing" catalog, delivered as a dark-comedy bit ("building a human out of organs" standing in for assembling a drum kit from disparate samples). The actual lesson is compact: individually-perfect drum samples can still sound like a pile of unrelated sounds rather than one kit, and running them all together through Drum Bus's compressor is what glues them into something that reads as one cohesive instrument.

## Techniques
- **Introduce small imperfections to individual samples before worrying about the group** — deliberately un-even spacing/timing/character on a per-element basis (framed here as giving a "person" mismatched fingers) is called out as part of what stops a drum part from sounding stiff, ahead of any bus-level glue processing.
- **A drum bus compressor is what makes disparate samples read as one kit, not the individual sample choices** — even after each element sounds fine on its own, a kit assembled from samples "out of a variety of vastly different organs" still sounds like separate pieces until they're squeezed together; enabling the compressor on Ableton's **Drum Bus** device across the whole kit is presented as the fix — described as making it sound "glued together." → [[Rhythm and Groove]] (already routed) · [[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]] ("group saturation IS the drum glue" is the fuller version of this same idea)

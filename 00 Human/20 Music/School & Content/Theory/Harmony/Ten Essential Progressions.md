# Ten Essential Progressions

Part of [[Music Theory]] → [[Chord Progressions]]. A **practice checklist**: ten progressions, to be played in all twelve keys until they're under the fingers without thinking.

```progression-songs
```

This is the companion resource to [[Joe Luegers - An Hour of Chord Progressions - Epic Ear Training]] — the thing his **technique 5** ("build your harmonic vocabulary") actually refers to. His framing:

> *"None of this really matters unless you know what chords are in what keys and have a few basic chord progressions memorized in every key… You've got to get these chords under your fingers to be able to visualize them accurately."*

The selection is his; the transpositions are mechanical. Source sheet: [Google Drive](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1qC8bK2ewSiVK_kijEQl4v0gSJ0rsThqV/edit) *(captured 2026-08-09)*.

## The ten

| # | Progression | What it drills |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **I – V – I** | The authentic cadence, bare. → [[Cadences]] |
| 2 | **I – IV – I** | The plagal cadence |
| 3 | **i – V – i** | Minor with the **borrowed major V** (from harmonic minor) |
| 4 | **i – iv – i** | Minor plagal — fully diatonic, no borrowing |
| 5 | **I – V – vi – IV** | The four-chord pop loop. → [[The Axis Progression]] |
| 6 | **I – vi – ii – V** | The turnaround. → [[Turnarounds]] |
| 7 | **I – iii – vi – V** | The descending-thirds variant |
| 8 | **I – ii7 – V7 – Imaj7** | The ii–V–I with sevenths. → [[The ii-V-I]] |
| 9 | **IVmaj7 – V7 – iii7 – vi7** | All sevenths, no tonic — the "deceptive" jazz-pop loop |
| 10 | **i – ♭VII – ♭VI – V7** | The Andalusian descent, minor |

Numbers 1–4 are cadence drills; 5–7 are the diatonic pop loops; 8–9 are the jazz vocabulary; 10 is the one modal-flavoured outlier.

## How to work it

The source sheet is a **checklist** — one tick box per progression per key, 130 boxes. That structure is the method, so keep it:

- **One key at a time, all ten progressions**, rather than one progression through all keys. Matches [[Music Theory Workbook - Enrichment|Bowman]]'s *"complete understanding of each key before you proceed to the next."*
- **Say the Roman numerals aloud while playing the chords.** The transferable thing is the function, not the shapes.
- **Then do it away from the instrument** — name the chords of #9 in D♭ with no guitar in hand. That's the level that makes ear training work, and it's the actual prerequisite the video is pointing at.
- Sharp and flat spellings of the same key (F♯/G♭, D♭/C♯) are separate rows in the source. Worth doing both once, then working from whichever spelling the music in front of you uses. → [[Key Signatures]]

> [!warning] Two errors in the source sheet
> Both found on import 2026-08-09 and corrected above:
> - **#1, C♯ major** is given as `C♯–G♯–G♯`. A I–V–I must return to the tonic: it should be **C♯–G♯–C♯**.
> - **Tab 6 is titled "I-V-ii-IV"** but its header row and every entry are **I–vi–ii–V**. The tab title is the typo, not the content.
>
> Worth knowing generally: a free downloadable chord sheet is not an authority. Everything here is derivable from [[Chords by Key]] in a few seconds, and deriving it is more useful than trusting it.

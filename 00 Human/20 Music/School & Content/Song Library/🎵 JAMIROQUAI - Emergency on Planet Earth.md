# Emergency on Planet Earth — Jamiroquai

> Title track, *Emergency on Planet Earth* (1993), bassist Stuart Zender. This note is a bass-focused breakdown of just the **intro riff** — a simple slap-and-pop roots-and-octaves pattern — as taught by [[Slap & Pop Bass - Bad vs Good Technique (Beginner - Intermediate)|BassBuzz]] as the real-music payoff for its [[Teeter-Totter Slap-Pop Technique]] lesson.

## Chords
The only stretch of this song with a chord chart consistent enough across sources to trust: the **intro**, in (likely) **G minor**: **Gm9 – B♭7/F – E♭maj9 – D7♯5♭9**
→ **i9 – III7 (1st inv.) – ♭VImaj9 – V7♯5♭9** — a genuinely jazz-funk intro voicing: extended 9ths throughout and an altered dominant (D7♯5♭9) resolving back to the Gm9 tonic, consistent with the acid-jazz genre and keyboardist Toby Smith's Rhodes writing across the album.

**The rest of the song does not have a chord chart I could verify with confidence.** The handful of tab sites offering a full verse/chorus breakdown disagree with each other on both the chords and their order — one lists a chromatically-walking Gm–Am–B♭m–Cm chorus that doesn't fit the intro's harmonic language at all, and reads like an unreliable auto-generated chart. Rather than pick one arbitrarily, this section stays scoped to the intro only, matching this page's existing "intro riff only" framing above.

## The riff

Roots and octaves throughout — a deliberately simple pattern chosen specifically as a slap-and-pop workout, not a virtuoso line. Fretting given as index (slap) + pinky (pop) unless noted:

1. **Index, 3rd fret E string** (slap) → **pinky, 5th fret D string** (pop). The D-string pop here is the harder popping-finger placement flagged throughout [[Teeter-Totter Slap-Pop Technique]] and [[Popping Technique — Thumb Up vs Thumb Down, Hook, and Minimum Viable Pressure]] — no G string to tuck against as a guide.
2. Slide the same shape down: **index, 1st fret E string** (slap) → **pinky, 3rd fret D string** (pop).
3. Move up: **index, 6th fret A string** (slap) → **pinky, 8th fret G string** (pop) — the easier G-string pop.
4. A root-only note: **pinky, 5th fret A string** — sets up the next phrase.
5. **Index, 3rd fret A string** (slap) → **pinky, 5th fret G string** (pop).
6. Up two frets: **index, 5th fret A string** (slap) → **pinky, 7th fret G string** (pop).
7. Up one more fret: **index, 6th fret A string** (slap) → **pinky, 8th fret G string** (pop).
8. Closing notes: **middle finger, 4th fret E string** — resolves back into the top of the riff for the repeat.

## Why BassBuzz uses this riff

Picked specifically as a **real-song teeter-totter workout**: it's simple (roots and octaves, no fancy fills) but still forces the popping finger onto the trickier D string early on (step 1 above), then eases into the more forgiving G-string pop for the rest of the phrase — good sequencing for a player who's just drilled the open-string E/G and E/D teeter-totter patterns and is ready to apply the motion to fretted notes in a real riff.

## Steal this

- **A roots-and-octaves riff is a legitimate slap-and-pop workout in its own right** — not every slap line needs to be flashy to be a useful drill or a useful part.
- **Sequence the harder D-string pop early in a phrase, before easing into the easier G-string pop** — mirrors how BassBuzz builds up drills generally (see [[Teeter-Totter Slap-Pop Technique]]'s open-string progression).

# You Suck at Producing - Creating Drum Beats Pt I

## Synopsis
One of the channel's earliest videos (2016) — a true absolute-beginner intro to programming a drum beat in a Drum Rack: what a beat/measure/note-value actually is (counted out loud before ever touching a piano roll), what MIDI is, and building the classic "boots and cats" pattern from a bare kick/snare/hi-hat. Largely superseded in depth by the later, more polished "You Suck at Drums" #1–#2 (2021), but the "count it out loud first" framing is a nice, simple entry point worth keeping on record.

## Techniques
- **Feel the pulse before programming it.** Simply counting out loud at a steady pace ("1 2 3 4, 1 2 3 4…") and grouping every four counts into a cycle is enough to internalize what a bar *is* before touching a piano roll — quarter notes subdivide each beat into one count, eighth notes into two ("1 and 2 and…"), and so on. → [[Time Signatures & Note Values]] (already routed)
- **MIDI holds instructions, not audio** — a MIDI clip tells the instrument (here, a Drum Rack) which notes to trigger and when; it carries no audio itself, which is why the same MIDI pattern can be re-auditioned against any drum kit.
- **Build the "boots and cats" pattern as the first practice loop** — kick on the downbeats, snare on the backbeat, and a hi-hat filling every off-beat (the "and" of each beat) — the classic mouth-drummed archetype pattern, good as a first concrete target when learning to place notes by ear/count rather than by feel alone.
- **Tempo affects how a simple pattern reads** — slowing the BPM live while the loop plays is presented as an easy way to feel how the same pattern's character changes with tempo, before committing to a final BPM.

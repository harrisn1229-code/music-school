# AUGMENTED Triad Arpeggio Guitar Shapes for Soloing with Chordal Tones

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nmfsFOiH9jc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nmfsFOiH9jc)

## Synopsis
Tenth entry in Jared Borkowski's ongoing series mapping melodic (root-3rd-5th, not scale-run) arpeggio shapes across five CAGED-derived neck positions for each of twelve chord types — this installment covers the **augmented triad** (1-3-♯5), worked entirely from a C root. Jared flags this one as the odd entry in the series: because the augmented triad is a **symmetric** structure (stacked major 3rds, so the interval pattern repeats every 4 half-steps and any of its three notes could function as the root), it has no real "home base" sound — none of the five shapes resolve or feel grounded — which makes steps 3-5 of his usual five-step ladder noticeably harder than on the other eleven chord types in the series. The payoff he states directly: mapping the augmented triad pays off whenever a **major 7♯5** chord shows up, since he reaches for an augmented-triad outline over that chord rather than learning a separate shape for it — one shape reused across situations rather than memorizing per-chord. The video is two things stacked together, same as the rest of the series: (1) five concrete fingered arpeggio shapes for C augmented, one per CAGED-family position, each demonstrated root-to-root with a recommended left-hand fingering; and (2) the same reusable five-step practice ladder run identically on every shape — map the chord tones, drill a melodic pattern off each chord tone, improvise with constant chord-tone-only notes, improvise with musical phrasing, then add non-chord tones (here: the **whole-tone scale**, generated directly from the chord tones by adding a whole step above/below each one, plus free chromatic connecting by ear). Strongest material for Nate: the five position-by-position fingerings (directly reusable for any augmented-triad or maj7♯5 vamp, or as upper-structure material over an altered dominant — see [[Augmented Chords]] and [[Altered Chords]]), the "3rd always falls on the same string as the root" fingering shortcut that generalizes across all five shapes, and the whole-tone-scale-from-chord-tones add-note recipe as a concrete, low-overhead way to color an augmented sound without needing to think in scale names.

- **Why bother mapping the augmented triad at all** — it comes in handy specifically when a **major 7♯5** chord appears: outline an augmented triad over it rather than learning a dedicated maj7♯5 shape, part of a stated broader philosophy of memorizing as few shapes as possible that still cover as many chord types as possible. [~2:10] → [[Augmented Chords]]
- **Why this chord type is harder than the rest of the series** — because an augmented triad is built from two stacked major 3rds (1-3-♯5, all major-3rd intervals), it's **symmetric**: any of its three notes could equally be heard as the root, so it has no real tonal center or "home" sound. That ambiguity is exactly why he insists on strict **root-to-root** practice for step 1 (play up to the root, pause and repeat only the root, never pause/repeat anywhere else) — it's the only way to impose a felt tonic on an otherwise rootless shape. The same ambiguity makes steps 3-5 (constant improv, musical phrasing, adding notes) noticeably harder here than on any other chord type so far in the series, since there's no grounded target to resolve phrases onto. [~3:09]
- **The five-step practice ladder, run identically on every shape/position (the reusable method, same as the rest of this series):**
  1. **Map the chord tones root-to-root.** Straight up and down the shape from root to root, pausing/repeating only on the root — pure fingering/positional mapping, no musicality yet. [~3:09]
  2. **Drill a melodic pattern off each chord tone.** From the lowest chord tone in the shape, "up to the next chord tone, then back down," off every chord tone in the shape in turn, in time.
  3. **Improvise with constant (unbroken) chord-tone-only notes.** A continuous stream of notes at any tempo, using only the shape's three chord tones, testing whether the shape is known well enough to never stop and think; slow down when it breaks down.
  4. **Improvise with musical phrasing, still chord-tones-only.** Real musical statements — an idea, space, a second idea that answers/concludes the first — explicitly flagged as the hardest step of the ladder on this particular chord type because there's no grounding root sound to phrase toward.
  5. **Add non-chord tones around the shape.** See the whole-tone recipe below — the step where the chord actually becomes useful, and where "we spend kind of years" once the chord tones are secure.
- **The whole-tone-scale-from-chord-tones recipe (the add-note technique for this chord type).** For every chord tone of the augmented triad, the available extra note is a **whole step above or below it** — doing that for all three chord tones (root, 3rd, ♯5) generates the full **whole-tone scale** built on that root. Stated explicitly as his general recommendation for step 5 on this chord type specifically (as opposed to a scale/mode name being taught directly). **Chromatic connecting is also fair game by ear** — if two notes are both established chord/whole-tone tones, connecting them with a passing chromatic note in between is fine, no rule against it. Practiced against a static backing loop (his own setup: a loop pedal cycling different voicings of a C augmented triad) so every added note is judged against real sustained harmony rather than a cappella; also suggests iReal Pro, Band-in-a-Box, or searching YouTube for an "augmented triad jam track." [~7:30, recapped at each position] → [[Augmented Chords]], [[Scale Modes Reference]] § *Whole Tone / Diminished*
- **Fingering shortcut that generalizes across all five shapes** — wherever the **first (index) finger lands on the root, the 3rd of the chord always falls on the same string**, one shape-family constant Jared calls out explicitly partway through the series (introduced on Position 3 below, but true of the whole shape set). [~13:00]
- **Position 1 — low-ish position, C root on the low string.** Cumbersome/stretchy fingering, flagged as typical of this run of "rare, altered, non-diatonic-scale" chord types in the series (same caveat as [[HALF DIMINISHED Arpeggio Guitar Shapes for Soloing with Chordal Tones]]'s awkward Position 4). Fingering: **1st finger (root) - 4th finger - 3rd finger - middle finger, then a stretch/reach for the 3rd** — reach over and under rather than shift, though Jared notes it's fine to hop/shift with the 3rd finger instead if the reach causes wrist strain; sequence continues **1st - 3rd - middle - 3rd - pinky - 1st**. The stretch is deliberate: he wants the chord-tone (arpeggio) shape to sit *inside* the same fingering the full scale forms use over that chord, rather than have two incompatible fretboard maps for chord tones vs. scale tones. This shape fits both the **augmented scale** (the 6-note symmetric scale — see [[Scale Modes Reference]] § *World & Exotic Scales*) and the **altered scale** (7th mode of melodic minor — see [[Altered Chords]]), both of which contain this arpeggio's three notes. [~3:09]
- **Position 2 — "cascading" shape.** Fingering: ascend the shape as a cascading run of **root-3rd-♯5-root**, then reach/hop over to a second 3rd on an adjacent string using the **3rd finger** as the shift finger, landing the top three strings under the **pinky and middle finger**. [~10:35]
- **Position 3.** Fingering: **1st finger (root) - 4th finger - 3rd finger - 2nd finger - 1st finger** (a cascade like Position 2), then reach the **2nd finger** over to the same fret to pivot the whole hand position, landing on **1st finger - 2nd finger** for the shift — described as a "position pivot" rather than a full shift. This is the position where the **root-and-3rd-share-a-string** rule is called out explicitly and generalized to all five shapes. [~13:00]
- **Position 4 — the "hand-buster."** Root on the 4th string under the 1st finger (so the 3rd also falls on the 4th string, per the shared-string rule); **3rd finger plays the ♯5**, **pinky plays the root** (octave up), **middle finger plays the 3rd** — flagged directly as the hardest shape physically because of the fast alternation between the 4th and 3rd fingers; recommended to take slow. [~16:27]
- **Position 5 — top of the neck, no big reach.** Root-3rd-♯5-root with fingering **3-2-1-1**: root under the 1st finger, the major 3rd taken on the **same string** via a first-finger roll (same shared-string rule again), and the ♯5 is the only note on the lowest string of the shape. [~19:55]
- **Backing-track/loop-pedal practice setup**, demonstrated live at every position: Jared plays step 5 (adding whole-tone notes) against a looped C augmented triad (varied voicings), explicitly recommending practicing the add-note step against a static backing loop of the target chord rather than a cappella. [~7:30 onward, repeated at each position]

# Linn LM-1

## What it is
Roger Linn's LM-1 Drum Computer was the **first drum machine built on samples of real drums** — a $4,995 luxury item (~500 made) that instantly made every analog rhythm box sound like a toy to 1980 ears. It's also where **quantization and programmable swing** were invented. Its most famous owner reshaped pop with it: the LM-1 is *the* Prince drum sound.

## What makes the sound
- **8-bit samples of real drums** (recorded from session drummer Art Wood's kit) stored on EPROM chips — real snare/kick/perc hits, but with the grainy, slightly crunchy edge of 8-bit companded encoding.
- **Per-voice tuning knobs** change playback clock rate — pitching a sample down also darkens and lengthens it. Prince's signature moves live here: the **pitched-down clap-rimshot** and detuned toms that sound like no acoustic kit.
- **No cymbals/hi-hat crash memory constraints**: no crash or ride (too much sample memory), so arrangements built on it feel tight and dry by construction.
- **Swing/shuffle ("timing humanize") invented here** — Linn's quantize-with-swing is the groove template every MPC and DAW swing knob descends from → [[Akai MPC60]].
- Individual outs per voice meant each drum could be processed on its own console channel — part of why LM-1 records sound so produced.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Simpler]] with LM-1 sample packs (pitch them down for the Prince trick — Simpler's classic mode repitches just like the hardware); [[RC-20 Retro Color]] or [[kHs Bitcrush]] to push the 8-bit grain harder; [[Cassette]] for era-glue.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2 (covers LM-1 and most of this worklist), Samples From Mars "LM-1 From Mars".
- **Free:** LM-1 sample packs circulate widely.

## Used on
- Prince — everywhere from *Controversy* through the *1999* / *Purple Rain* era; "When Doves Cry" is an LM-1 masterclass (that clap!). He bought one of the first units.
- The Human League — *Dare* (1981), incl. "Don't You Want Me": British synthpop adopted the LM-1 immediately.
- Genre/era: early-80s synthpop, boogie/post-disco, Minneapolis funk.

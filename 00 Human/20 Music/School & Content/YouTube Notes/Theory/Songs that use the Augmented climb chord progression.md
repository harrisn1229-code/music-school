# Songs that use the Augmented climb chord progression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ixIS64xvWSQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ixIS64xvWSQ)

## Synopsis

A companion piece to Bennett's "Something" progression video — this one is explicitly framed as its mirror image: instead of a descending line cliché from I to IV, this is an ascending one (I–Iaug–I6–I7–IV), triggered by a viewer question about Green Day's "Last Night on Earth." Same secondary-dominant mechanism, opposite direction.

## Techniques

- **The progression**: A – Aaug – A6 – A7 – D, the top voice climbing A→A♯→B→C over an otherwise-static tonic chord before resolving to IV.
- **Same secondary-dominant logic as the "Something" progression** (see [[Line Cliché]]): the dominant-7 chord reached at the top of the climb (A7 here) is the V of IV (D's own dominant), so it resolves there with the same pull a plain V chord has toward I.
- **Extension: IV → iv (minor four) → I** — heard in "Last Night on Earth" (Green Day), "505" (Arctic Monkeys), and "I Don't Feel Like Dancing" (Scissors Sisters) — same melancholic-darkening move documented on the descending version.
- **Doubling the climb**: some songs repeat the whole ascending pattern starting from the IV chord itself (IV → IVaug → IV6 → IV7 → ...) — heard in "Greatest Love of All" (Whitney Houston), "Mis-Shapes" (Pulp), and "Life on Mars?" (David Bowie, which additionally puts the climbing note in the bass too, doubling the emphasis on the chromatic line).
- **A "left turn" variant**: "For Once in My Life" (Stevie Wonder) climbs identically through the augmented and 6th chords, but resolves the dominant-7 chord to a ♭9 leading into a minor chord instead of the plain IV every other example in this batch uses — same climb, different landing.

# VOI-C08 — Spread triads, and one note per change

**Q.** What is a spread triad, and what construction rule generates every diatonic triad of a key in every inversion?

> [!question]- Answer
> A **spread** (open-position) triad is a close triad with **the middle note moved up an octave** — the same three notes reordered 1-5-3, with a string skip and a wider, harp-like spacing. The rule: **chain the diatonic triads a third at a time and move exactly one voice, by a half or whole step, at every change.** Full coverage of the key falls out as a side effect.

## Why it matters

It is voice leading turned into a generator instead of a lookup. Because the one-note rule always lands you on the *closest* voicing of the next chord, each triad quality comes back in a different inversion each time — so you cover the key without drilling inversions as a separate exercise. Watch the terminology trap: "open position" here means spread spacing, not playing near the nut.

## Drill

15 min: start on a tonic spread triad high on the neck and chain down by thirds — I, iii, V, vii°, ii, IV, vi — moving one voice per change. Close with V7 to I.

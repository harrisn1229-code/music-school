# Songs that use 6/4 time

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lf3462eZemU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lf3462eZemU)

## Synopsis

A survey of 6/4 songs (a rarer choice than 5/4 or 7/4) that turns into a genuinely deep point about time-signature notation itself: 6/4 vs. two bars of 3/4 have the identical beat count, so the real choice between them is about accent fit — and for recorded (not hand-notated) music, that choice can be a real, unresolvable ambiguity rather than an objective fact waiting to be discovered.

## Techniques

- **6/4 often feels like alternating 4/4-then-2/4 bars rather than 6 equal beats**, giving forward momentum ("Galvanize," Chemical Brothers; "Enough Space," Foo Fighters — both could be notated as alternating 4/4+2/4, but a single 6/4 signature reads more simply).
- **The same fixed beat count can genuinely change felt meter depending on where the accent lands**: Alice in Chains' "Down in a Hole" has a 12-beat guitar phrase that reads as 2 bars of 6/4 while the riff repeats alone, then re-reads as 3 bars of 4/4 once the vocal enters and the guitar stops enforcing the 6/4 feel — identical 12 beats, different meter, purely from accent placement.
- **6/4 vs. two bars of 3/4 — mathematically the same, chosen by accent fit, not arithmetic**: any 6/4 piece could be forcibly transcribed as 3/4, but if the natural strong-beat accents don't land where 3/4 would put them, that's a bad-fit choice regardless of being technically valid ("Schism," Tool, as the clean case).
- **Some songs genuinely sit on the boundary and get published different ways by different people**: "Take Me to Church" (Hozier verse) heard as 64bpm 6/4 by this analyst, published as 63bpm 3/4 on official sheet music — same recording, different chosen pulse; "Electric Feel" (MGMT) similarly gets described as either.
- **The deepest point: time-signature ambiguity is a specifically MODERN problem.** Classical composers hand-wrote their own time signature as a deliberate authorial choice onto the manuscript itself (Chopin's Nocturne Op. 9 No. 1 could have been notated 3/4 but he specifically chose 6/4 for its particular feel) — no ambiguity is possible because the composer's intent is literally on the page. Modern songwriters release *recordings*; any sheet-music transcription is usually made after the fact by a copyist guessing at the best-fit signature, not by the songwriter — meaning "what time signature is this song really in" can be a genuinely unresolvable question for recorded music in a way it never was for notated music.

## Catalog (additional named examples)

Tool, "Schism" · Soundgarden, "Black Days" · Radiohead, "Full Stop" · The Police, "Synchronicity" (fast tempo + 6/4 = frantic feel) · Dave Brubeck, "Pick Up Sticks" and "Everybody's Jumpin'" (*Time Out*, alternates 6/4 and 4/4) · Courtney Barnett, "Crippling Self-Doubt and a General Lack of Confidence" (6/4 verse contrasted against 4/4 chorus for a "relief" effect at the chorus).

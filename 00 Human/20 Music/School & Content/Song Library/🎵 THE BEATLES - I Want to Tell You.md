# I Want to Tell You — The Beatles

> Harrison's song about the frustration of ideas not translating into speech. The piano deliberately plays a clashing note against the main chord to create harmonic tension that mirrors the lyrical theme.

## Chords
**Key:** A major · George Harrison

**Verse:** A – B – E7 – A
→ **I – II7 – V7 – I** (the **B major** is a bright secondary dominant — V/V — instead of a diatonic Bm)

**"But if I seem to act unkind":** Bm – Dm6 – A – B7 – Bm – Dm6 – A
→ **ii – iv6 – I – II7 – ii – iv6 – I** (the dissonant **Dm6** is the "confusing things" tension Harrison wrote in on purpose)

## Production & Arrangement
Harrison, Revolver — opens with a **guitar fade-in** and rides a deliberately **dissonant piano stab** (a clashing E7♭9-ish note) to musically depict 'I can't get my thoughts out.' Harmony as lyric.

- **Dissonance as meaning:** use a clashing chord/note deliberately to express tension → [[Altered Chords]].
- **Fade-in intro** for an unusual entrance.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — I Want To Tell You]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #101.*

**Form.** A standard two-bridge model whose sections resist being counted. The intro is a **fade-in**, their first since "Eight Days A Week" — you become aware of the ostinato already running on the guitar's low strings, three complete iterations with staggered entrances. Verse **11 bars**, hard to parse for a second reason too: its four vocal phrases are unequal and are declaimed *across* barlines rather than into them, with a sneak reprise of the ostinato filling the gap between verses. Bridge 8 bars, much closer to square and badly needed as contrast by that point, though its three short phrases also hang over the barlines. Outro is half a varied reprise of the intro, half a counted-off fade.

**Harmony.** A major on a frugal budget. `Verse: I (3½ bars) | V-of-V (entering mid-bar 4) | V (4 bars) | I (2)` — **the chord change in the middle of bar 4 is the sophisticated move**. The guitar ostinato has I – ♭VII – IV – I embedded in it, which is where the modal tint comes from. The bridge sits on **B in the bass under everything** ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]): `Bm | B°(with B beneath) | A | B major · Bm | B° | A`, and his non-obvious call is to hear that diminished chord as **vii° rooted on G♯**, a V surrogate, despite the B underneath — he argues against assigning distinct numerals to those bars at all, since the real content is creeping chromatic motion in the inner voices.

**Notable.** The anxiety of the lyric is written into the fabric as literal dissonance: harmonic, in the piano's mechanical harping on an **E minor ninth**, and rhythmic, in the **slow triplets** of the bass ostinato grinding against the 4/4 backbeat ([[RHY-C04 — Polyrhythm and hemiola]]). The ostinato is a **saw-tooth** shape rather than an arch, starting just before the downbeat. The bridge vocal is full of 9–8 appoggiaturas echoing the piano's ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]), and backing vocals are used for emphasis on the **even-numbered** phrases — the same 2-and-4 logic as the beat.

**Against this page.** Agrees on A major.

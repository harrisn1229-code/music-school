# 97% of pop is in 4/4... let's look at the 3% that's not

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcAUcbwI8QE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcAUcbwI8QE)

## Synopsis

A data-driven survey: every top-40 best-selling US song from every year 2000–2024 (933 songs total), tallying which time signature each uses — 96.57% land in plain 4/4, and the video catalogs all 32 songs (3.43%) that don't, revealing which alternate meters actually reach commercial pop success and which essentially never do.

## Techniques

- **The actual alternate-meter breakdown, ranked by frequency**: 6/8 is by far the most common non-4/4 choice (Alicia Keys' "Fallin'," Chad Kroeger's "Hero," "I'm with You," and several more scattered across the 2000s-2020s) — felt as two main beats each divided into a triplet, "like 2/4 with triplets on each beat." Behind that: 12/8 (Lewis Capaldi's "Before You Go"), swung 3/4 (functionally the same feel as 9/8 — Alicia Keys' "If I Ain't Got You," Bailey Zimmerman's "Rock and a Hard Place" — most transcriptions notate these as 3/4 even though the swing makes them playable as 9/8), and genuine mixed meter (OutKast's "Hey Ya," a six-bar loop of 4/4-4/4-4/4-2/4-4/4-4/4; Zach Bryan's "Something in the Orange," arguably alternating 6/8/9/8 though often simplified to 3/4 in transcription "for readability" at the cost of accuracy).
- **The headline finding: zero true odd-meter songs (5/4, 7/4) EVER cracked a top-40 year-end chart across the entire 21st century so far** — despite odd-meter pop songs existing (Hozier's "From Eden," 5/4, 2014; Taylor Swift's "tolerate it" and "closure," both 5/4, 2020), none sold well enough to make a top-40 list. The pattern isn't new either: Pink Floyd's "Money" (1973, one of the best-known odd-meter hits ever) only reached #92 on the US year-end chart the year it was released — nowhere near the top 40 even at its most commercially successful.
- **A commercial-viability takeaway, stated as the practical conclusion**: IF a hit pop song strays from 4/4 at all, it will very likely land in 6/8 or 12/8, occasionally swung 3/4, occasionally true mixed meter — but essentially never in a true odd meter like 5/4 or 7/4, which appear to be structurally incompatible with mainstream chart success regardless of era.

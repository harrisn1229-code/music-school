## Synopsis

A deliberate technique+theory combo exercise: the exact same picking pattern and phrasing is repeated across four minor-scale variants (natural minor, Phrygian, Dorian, harmonic minor), each changing only one scale degree from natural minor — framed as a time-efficient practice design principle (drilling mechanical technique and characteristic-note ear training simultaneously) as much as a specific lick.

## What's taught

- **The combo-practice principle, stated as the video's actual point:** rather than practicing technique and theory as separate blocks, build technique exercises on material that also teaches something — here, keeping the fingering/picking pattern identical while cycling the underlying scale forces memorizing each mode's one characteristic note as a side effect of the mechanical reps, at no extra practice-time cost.
- **The four minor variants, each one note changed from natural minor (E-rooted throughout):**
  - Natural minor / Aeolian — the baseline, no altered degrees.
  - **Phrygian** — lower the 2nd degree (F→F♮ from F♯, i.e. the half-step-above-root color). Named as common in rock/metal for that dark half-step-above-root sound; the practical warning given is not to solo with plain natural minor over a Phrygian riff — landing on the natural 2nd against a Phrygian riff's ♭2 produces obvious unwanted tension.
  - **Dorian** — raise the 6th degree. Described as underused in rock/metal relative to how good it sounds; contrasted directly against natural minor's "sadder" minor 6th.
  - **Harmonic minor** — raise the 7th degree, the "exotic"/oriental-leaning sound already covered in an earlier dedicated lesson.
- **Mechanical/picking side: strict continuous alternate picking, no slurs.** Deliberately arranged so the same pick-hand angle serves most of the pattern by repeating certain scale positions before a string change — the same "repeat positions to keep one favorable angle" device as this batch's other alternate-picking videos, here applied specifically to make a 4-mode-cycling exercise mechanically approachable rather than adding a second layer of difficulty on top of the theory content.

# Songs that use the Phrygian Dominant scale

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-CerXEcCJcY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-CerXEcCJcY)

## Synopsis

Covers Phrygian dominant's construction and modal identity (the 5th mode of harmonic minor, same 7 notes as the *maqam Hijaz*/Andalusian scale) through a wide set of worked song examples, with a genuinely useful mechanism section on how the same chord progression reads as two different modes depending on which chord a genre treats as "home."

## Techniques

- **Construction and why it sounds exotic**: Phrygian dominant = Phrygian mode with the 3rd raised to major (instead of Phrygian's usual minor 3rd) — the resulting major-3rd-to-flat-2nd relationship is an **augmented 2nd** (3 semitones), a wider gap than the semitone/tone steps most Western scales use exclusively, and that oversized step is the main source of the "exotic" sound. Raising the 3rd also means a dominant-7th chord can be built directly on the tonic (hence the name).
- **It's the 5th mode of harmonic minor** — A harmonic minor and E Phrygian dominant use the identical 7 notes; which one you're "in" is purely a function of which note is treated as the tonal center, not a different note collection.
- **The same four chords read as two different modes depending on genre convention** — pop/rock treats the FIRST chord as tonic (harmonic-minor i-♭VII-♭VI-V reading); flamenco treats the LAST chord as tonic (Phrygian-dominant IV-III-II-I reading) — this is the Andalusian Cadence's dual-conceptualization, already thoroughly documented on [[Chord Progressions]] (flamenco origin, harmonic-rhythm variants, major-vs-minor-V ambiguity); not repeated in full here.
- **A song can pivot which of two modally-related notes is "home" mid-song, without changing pitch content at all**: "Break It to Me" (Muse) — verse in B Phrygian dominant, chorus in E harmonic minor; same 7 notes throughout, B is tonic in the verse and E is tonic in the chorus. Same device in the traditional tune "Hava Nagila," pivoting between E Phrygian dominant and A harmonic minor across sections.
- **A distinctive chord-quality set that's rare outside this scale**: Phrygian dominant is one of the only scales that naturally yields a **major tonic chord + a major ♭2 chord + a minor ♭7 chord** in the same key — cited via "Naughty Girl" and "Beautiful Liar" (Beyoncé) as pop examples of that specific triad-quality combination showing up.
- **Real-world Middle Eastern/flamenco naming and use**: known in Arabic music theory as *maqam Hijaz* and used extensively there; in flamenco as *Escala Andaluza* (Andalusian scale), the region the style originates from — Western songwriters commonly reach for Phrygian dominant specifically as a shorthand for a Middle Eastern flavor (United States of Eurasia interlude, Muse; Killing an Arab, The Cure) or a flamenco/Spanish flavor (Innuendo's flamenco middle section, Queen — itself pivoting between E and G♯ Phrygian dominant for its verse).
- **Additional cited examples** (each a single clean use, less mechanism-rich than the above): 46 & 2 (Tool, C Phrygian dominant); Dream Brother (Jeff Buckley, G Phrygian dominant).

Note: this video also covers the Phrygian-dominant-vs-double-harmonic distinction (Misirlou), the "White Rabbit" Phrygian/Phrygian-dominant switch, and "Pyramid Song"'s three-scale blend — all already documented on [[Harmonic Minor Modes]] from an earlier video ([[Songs that use the Phrygian mode]]), so not repeated here.

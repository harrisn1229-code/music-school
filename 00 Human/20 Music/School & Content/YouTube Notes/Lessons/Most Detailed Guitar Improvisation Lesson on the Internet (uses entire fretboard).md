# Most Detailed Guitar Improvisation Lesson on the Internet (uses entire fretboard)

## Synopsis

A full 52-Week Guitar Player lesson shared publicly: applying C harmonic minor to a real chord chart (Cm–Fm9–Dø7–G7(sus/♭9)–Cm(maj7)) across all five fretboard positions, cataloging exactly which scale (harmonic minor, Phrygian dominant, minor/dominant pentatonic substitutes) and which arpeggio shape works over each chord in each position. A guest player, Kyo, then teaches six harmonic-minor improvisation devices (color-tone emphasis, chromatic/bebop passing tones, augmented-triad and diminished-arpeggio symmetry, the hidden triad inside an augmented-maj7 chord, and a borrowed-dominant reharmonization trick) over two backing tracks (C minor, E minor). Closes with five common improv mistakes and a three-part practice routine.

## Techniques

- **The chart:** Cm (i, triad) → Fm9 (iv) → Dø7 → G7 (V) → Cm, repeated 3x; then Cm(maj7) (2 bars) → G7sus → G7sus♭9, in C minor at 85 BPM.
- **Position-by-position system (5 positions spanning roughly frets 2–16):** for every chord in the chart, in every position, identify which scale(s) fit (harmonic minor and its 5th-mode Phrygian dominant work over every chord in the progression; pentatonic substitutes work over some chords but clash on others — e.g. a ♮3 dominant pentatonic clashes against a chord voicing with a ♭7) and which specific arpeggio shape (named by starting scale degree, e.g. "shape 3-1" = an arpeggio starting on the 3rd, first of possibly two fingerings for that starting degree) outlines the chord cleanly. Full shape catalog assumes familiarity with the channel's separate "Improvisation Firestorm" module and isn't reproduced shape-by-shape here — the reusable takeaway is the *system* (map every chord × every position to its available scale/arpeggio options) rather than the specific fingerings, which are fretboard-position-specific.
- **Kyo's six improvisation devices** — routed in full to [[Harmonic Minor Improvisation Devices]]: emphasize each chord's color/characteristic tone; a bebop-style chromatic passing tone under the dominant; augmented-triad symmetry (repeats every major 3rd) over minor-major7 chords; diminished-arpeggio symmetry (repeats every minor 3rd) built from a dominant's 3rd, yielding a useful ♭9 tension note; the hidden major triad inside an augmented-maj7 chord (built on that chord's own 3rd); and briefly "borrowing" a dominant sound between two non-dominant chords for extra pull.
- **Five improv mistakes reviewed** (routed to [[10 Hidden Soloing Mistakes and Constraint-Based Fixes]]): staccato instead of legato playing, staying in one position too long, not using all six strings, bad rhythm from insufficient internalization (fix: know the material cold, then drill timing directly with a metronome), and botched fast chord-change transitions (fix: drill ii-V-I-style connector licks per transition, provided as a PDF in the source).
- **Practice routine:** improvise over the lesson's own backing track 3x (posting for feedback each time); repeat the entire position-mapping process from scratch on a second, differently-charted backing track, 3x; record final takes with real production care (DAW, lighting) as a "professional presentation" pass.

# How to use Polyrhythms & Changing Time Signatures in EDM

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=62kaefeplEI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=62kaefeplEI)

## What's taught
Using a real, intentionally-odd-metered track (TA Meyer's) as the working example, Underbelly reverse-engineers its shifting time signatures by ear — soloing an instrument, looping the span of a single musical idea, and counting the beats inside that loop to land on the actual bar length (10/4, 8/4, 6/4 in sequence) — then defines a polyrhythm as multiple different beat-groupings (e.g. dividing the same bar into 6 vs. into 8) occupying the same musical space simultaneously, with the ear/performer alternating attention between the two competing subdivisions across the bar.

## Techniques
- **Reading a time signature: top = beats per bar, bottom = which note value gets one beat** — 4/4 = four quarter notes per bar, 4/8 = four eighth notes, 5/8 = five eighth notes, and so on; a bassline/musical idea's own length should decide which time signature actually fits it, rather than defaulting to 4/4. → [[Time Signatures & Note Values]]
- **Identify an unfamiliar/changing time signature by soloing an instrument, looping the phrase, and counting** — solo the part that most clearly states the musical idea (here, the bass), find where it starts and ends one full phrase, put a loop bracket around exactly that span (Ableton: Cmd/Ctrl+L), then count beats against the metronome inside that loop to get the actual bar length — repeat per section since the time signature can change mid-track. → [[Time Signatures & Note Values]]
- **Insert a time-signature change at a specific point in the arrangement** — right-click at the point where the meter changes and insert a new time signature (e.g. switching from 10/4 to 8/4 partway through a section), then re-verify by counting through the new section to confirm the change holds for its full length. → [[Time Signatures & Note Values]]
- **A polyrhythm = two (or more) different beat-groupings occupying the same musical space at once** — demonstrated as one rhythmic layer dividing a bar into 6 while another simultaneously divides the same bar into 8; because the two groupings don't line up evenly, following "both at once" means shifting which grouping gets the ear's attention across the bar rather than picking one. → [[Time Signatures & Note Values]]
- **How a polyrhythm is actually felt/played: accent one grouping at the start of the bar, then shift to the other grouping's pulse later in the bar** — e.g. lean into the 8-grouping's pulse for the first part of the bar, then switch attention to the 6-grouping's pulse for the back half — a deliberate alternation rather than committing to one subdivision for the whole bar. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]

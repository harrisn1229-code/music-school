# I'll Get You — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A rushed B-side Pollack rates as plain by essentially every compositional measure — which is exactly what makes it useful. With form and harmony offering nothing unusual, what's left is a set of ingredients already unmistakably theirs. The one forward-looking move is a **minor v chord in a major key**.

**Form.** The short single-bridge model. Every section enters on a pickup, ahead of its downbeat. Verse lyrics run ABA and all three verses close with the same refrain-like tag — the hook that the 4-bar intro exists to plant. The outro grows straight out of the last verse, repeating its final phrase three times with the harmonica left ringing after everything else stops.

**Harmony.** Unrelievedly in one key, roughly half a dozen chords, but an unusual number of them arrive **embellished**: added sixth on I, major seventh on IV, a 7/9 on the V-of-V. The **minor v** is why the essay earns keeping — a minor dominant inside a major home key, tilting the phrase modal and bluesy where the ear expects major ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The bridge doesn't modulate at all; it just starts away from home and converges, with a deliberately drawn-out approach to V.

**Melody.** Centred inside a single octave, with the verse tags opening space below it and the bridge opening space above. The tune is thick with **appoggiaturas**, and those leaning notes are precisely why so many chords read as embellished ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]) — write the leaning tones first and let them name the chords, and the added sixths and ninths sound motivated rather than decorative. The bridge is a clean textbook case of harmony recolouring a fixed melodic figure: the same three-note descent is sung three times over three different chords and lands differently each time.

**Arrangement.** A nondescript backing track carrying a lot of fussy detail. The harmonica plays no hook phrase but runs almost continuously, resting only through the bridge — necessarily an overdub, since John sings throughout. Handclaps appear only in the intro as stand-in percussion, and the drums don't enter until a single fill at its end.

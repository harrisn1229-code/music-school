# Guide-Tone Diads for Blues Rhythm Guitar

Part of [[Guitar Positions]]. The smallest-footprint rhythm-guitar comping vocabulary in this collection: a single **2-note "diad"** — just the **3rd and ♭7** of a dominant chord — carries the whole harmonic identity, with everything else (root, 5th) left for the bassist to cover. Where [[9th and 13th Chord Voicings for Blues Rhythm]] and [[Rootless 9th Chords and Chromatic Movement in Blues Rhythm Guitar]] work from bigger 4-note rootless grips, this page starts one layer below that — the guide-tone core those bigger voicings are built on top of — and builds it up in stages with sus4 embellishment, a kick/snare-style register-alternation technique, and a second, higher-position diad vocabulary. Same author, same "reduce the chord to its essential notes so it doesn't crowd the mix" instinct as [[Layering Rhythm Guitar Parts with Triads]], but that page stacks **three separate parts** for production; this page builds **one comping part** for playing live in a band.

## The core shape: 3rd + ♭7, nothing else

A dominant 7 chord is root-3rd-5th-♭7, but only the **3rd and ♭7** determine the chord's quality (major/minor/dominant) — change just the 3rd and the chord's whole character flips; change just the ♭7 to a major 7 and it becomes a maj7 sound. In a band context with a bass player covering the root, **the guitar only needs those two notes** to outline the chord.

Worked in a 12-bar blues in **A** (A7-D7-E7):
- **A7:** the two notes just outside the bar-chord shape — **♭7 = G, major 3rd = C♯**.
- **D7 (IV):** the *identical two-note shape* slides **down a half step** from the A7 grip — now sounding D7's 3rd (F♯) and ♭7 (C).
- **E7 (V):** the *identical shape* slides **up a half step** from the A7 grip — now sounding E7's 3rd and ♭7.
- **The whole I-IV-V cycle uses one movable shape, relocated by half steps only** — no new grip to learn per chord, just A7 → down ½ step → D7 → up ½ step → A7 → up ½ step → E7 → down ½ step → D7 → A7.

## Embellishment 1 — sus4 slide-and-raise

Layered on the core diad: **slide into the shape rhythmically**, then **raise the 3rd up a half step to the 4th** for a sus4 color before it resolves back. Works across all three chords with the same physical move, but the 3rd's string position isn't fixed — on D7 the 3rd sits on the *bottom* of the grip rather than the top, so the raise-to-4th motion has to be adapted to whichever string the 3rd is actually on for that chord. **Alternative on the A chord:** instead of the plain sus4 hit, grab a full **D-triad fragment** and **hammer on** from a lower note up into the 3rd — one more embellishment option on the same base shape, not a different chord choice.

## Embellishment 2 — high/low register alternation ("kick and snare")

A technique the source calls out as one he uses constantly: alternate **low-register notes** against **high chord stabs on the top two strings**, mimicking a kick drum (low) and snare (high) rhythmically within the same comping part.
- **Low:** the guide-tone diad in its low-register position, fretted with the **thumb wrapped over the neck** (or a plain bar-chord-shape fingering as an alternative).
- **High:** just the **top two strings of the bar-chord shape** — concretely, **5th fret of the high E and B strings** = the chord's **root and 5th**.
- **Over the IV chord specifically, substitute a full D9 chord** for the plain diad: the same dominant-7 "C-shape" grip barred up a whole step, **with the 9th added on top** — called out directly as "a super common chord to use in blues rhythm stuff." The sus4-style raised-4th embellishment layers onto this D9 grip too, same as on the plain diad.
- **Build order:** slide into the low A7 diad → jump to the high root/5th stab → back down to thumb-wrapped bass notes → repeat the D-triad hammer-on → another high chord jab → slide into the IV chord (D9 with raised-4th) → up to V → back down.

## A second diad vocabulary — 5th + major 3rd, higher position

A distinct shape used deliberately in a different neck area from everything above: the **A major triad up around the 9th/10th fret**, playing only its **two outside notes — 5th and major 3rd** (skipping the middle/root note in that particular voicing). Same rhythmic slide-in treatment as the core diad.
- **A chord:** 5th + major 3rd of A, in this higher position.
- **D chord:** the *identical interval relationship*, moved **down one string set** (not a fret-distance relocation like the core diad — a string-set shift).
- **E chord:** the *identical shape* moved **up a whole step** from the A-chord position.
- Same underlying logic as the core diad (one small movable shape relocated across I-IV-V) but a second vocabulary built from a different pair of chord tones (5th-3rd instead of 3rd-♭7), giving a genuinely different register/sound option for the same progression rather than a variation on the first shape.

## Drill — the video's own build order

1. **Bare guide-tone diads only** — 3rd+♭7 shape through the full 12-bar A7-D7-E7 form, moving by half steps, no embellishment. Lock this in first against a backing track.
2. **Add the sus4 slide-and-raise** on top of step 1, across all three chords (remembering the 3rd's position flips between chords). Optionally add the D-triad-hammer-on variant on the A chord.
3. **Add the high/low register alternation** — top-two-string root/5th chord stab against the low guide-tone diad, thumb-wrapped bass notes underneath. Swap in the full D9 grip (with raised-4th embellishment) over the IV chord.
4. **Play the full assembled part** through the whole form against a backing track, listening for lock with the drums.
5. **Learn the second (5th + major 3rd) diad vocabulary separately**, up around the 9th/10th fret — down a string set for IV, up a whole step for V — as an alternate higher-register part.
6. **Free-application stage:** apply the guide-tone-diad-plus-embellishment approach to other progressions and genres by ear — listen to the track and the other musicians in the room to find the rhythmic spaces where a small fragment fits, rather than reusing this exact arrangement verbatim.

*(General starting-point structuring of the 6-step order — the video demonstrates each layer in sequence but the explicit "practice it built up this way" framing is the standard way to internalize a layered comping part like this.)*

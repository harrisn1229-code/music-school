# Ian Kirkpatrick — Charli XCX Vocal FX Production ("Move Me")

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByPr9CMVnIg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByPr9CMVnIg)

## Synopsis

The deep-dive on the vocal FX in Charli XCX's "Move Me": how the hidden-synth-behind-a-word actually works in **PadShop** (granular, recorded live, not printed), the **mono→stereo-double dynamic trick** that makes the hook "feel more moving," why he prints reverbs so he can envelope them, a hype "woot" that's secretly a **Melodyne artifact**, the octave-up Little AlterBoy "Skrillex move," and the **double-timed-individual-words** last-chorus loop that was Charli's idea. Plus the songwriting story behind the "drop out on the one" moment.

## Techniques

- **Hidden synth from the vocal word — the full PadShop method** — drag a sung word ("move," "me") into **PadShop** (granular synth); the pad becomes the "ooh"/"ee" of the word. He **records PadShop's live output** (doesn't print a patch), so every key press plays different random grains — "10 minutes of noodling → a quarter-second I use." A more fun, different-texture alternative to reverb/delay throws. → [[PadShop]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Granular control knobs** — randomize grain count/length (e.g. 5 grains @ 8 ms vs 5 @ 100 ms), position, and "air"; randomizing position is how you get the shimmery cloud. → [[PadShop]], [[Modulation]]
- **Same trick on "Look At Her Now" (~0:54)** — the "uh-huh" + trailing gibberish throw is the same PadShop-from-the-word move. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - Look At Her Now Breakdown]]
- **Mono→stereo-double for emotional dynamics** — verse vocal is **mono, right in front of you** (intimate); the hook brings in a **stereo double**, so it opens from "just you and her" → "the group" → back. "Not much volume difference — a *spatial* difference to increase contrast." → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Stereo Field]], [[Depth and Space]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **Valhalla Shimmer + LFO Tool, selectively** — Shimmer reverb on the main vocal, LFO Tool ducking it rhythmically for a "subtle secret rhythmic guy" in one spot. → [[Reverb]], [[LFO Tool]]
- **Print reverbs so you can chop/envelope them** — two reverbs run most of the song; printing lets him **fade the verb out then back in** on a lyric ("something about the way you…") to "nudge your focus" before you realize you left the reverb world. Also dodges CPU (AdaptiVerb in HQ mode is a hog). → [[Reverb]], [[Automation]], [[Depth and Space]]
- **The "woot" is a Melodyne artifact** — while tuning, Dua/the singer's voice squeaked on a held note; he stretched the **Melodyne artifact** and it became the song's hype noise — "you'd never guess where it came from." Keep the happy accident. → [[Melodyne]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Print effects, use only where there's room** — a "speakerphone" echo printed and placed only in the one spot the arrangement had space for it. → [[Delay]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Octave-up Little AlterBoy "Skrillex move"** — on the last hook, **Little AlterBoy: formant unchanged, pitch +1 octave, drive maxed** — an aggressive octave-up vocal layer as a chorus-escalation element (each chorus adds one more). → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Double-time *individual words* (the loop-the-last-chorus idea)** — Charli's idea to loop the final chorus; he double-timed each word separately ("oh-my-body-tight"), including on the doubles, using a **Cubase 30-second/note trick** — energetic, experimental, and it stuck. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **A vocal chop running throughout** — random pieces of the vocal chopped as a recurring texture. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Songwriting move: "drop out on the one, restart the loop on the snare"** — cutting everything on the downbeat then re-entering the drum loop on the snare; he watched a live crowd react exactly as he'd imagined — the arrangement instinct validated. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

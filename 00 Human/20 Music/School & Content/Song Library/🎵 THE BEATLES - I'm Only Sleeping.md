## Production & Arrangement
Revolver (1966). The first Beatles use of a **backwards lead guitar** — Harrison worked out a solo, then Martin/Emerick recorded it note-for-note *reversed* onto tape for that unmistakable sucking, dreamlike swell. The drowsy vocal is lightly **vari-sped** for a heavy-lidded droop.

## Steal this
- **Backwards guitar/instrument:** write a part, reverse the audio ([[kHs Reverser]] / reverse-render) for a dreamlike sucking swell.
- **Vari-speed the vocal down** slightly for a drowsy, heavy feel ([[Little AlterBoy]]).
- Languid, behind-the-beat feel = the arrangement's mood.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — I'm Only Sleeping]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #94.*

**Form.** A song where **gesture outranks content** — his own distinction: content is the care spent on tune, chords and form; gesture is the disorienting effect of jamming recognisable clichés together and coating them in studio processing. Here that is a boozy jazz ride-beat, a patter-song tune, cooing forties backing vocals and a backwards guitar in one room. The structural feature is an elision: the bridge runs into the following verse so smoothly that **the verse's first phrase is initially heard as the bridge's second phrase**, which retroactively makes the bridge feel like a fragment rather than a section. Verse an odd **9 bars** despite clean AA′ phrasing ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]); refrain **6 bars** and not remotely binary inside; refrains before each bridge get two extra bars of a time-stopping i-chord vamp; the guitar solo fills only the 5-bar A′ half of a verse.

**Harmony.** `Verse: i – iv – III – VI – III – V · i – iv – III – VI (2) – iv` · `Refrain: III – iv – v – iv – VI(maj7, 2) · i (2)` · `Bridge: vii – V-of-iv → (in a minor) iv – V – i – VI`. Both verse phrases end open but differently — phrase 1 stops on **V**, which asks for a repeat; phrase 2 ends **VI → iv**, which asks for something new. The refrain runs a **chord stream** (stepwise root motion III–iv–v–iv) they had not used since the early singles, and that plus the fat major seventh is where the jazzy air comes from. Harmonically the song is all feint and no commitment ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]): it gestures toward the relative major without completing a modulation, and the bridge's tentative move to A minor is what pays for the elision.

**Notable.** The backing track was cut normally and **slowed on playback** while John's vocal was **sped up**. The backwards guitar does not appear until verse 2. Two tiny bass passing notes — a B between C and A minor, a G between A minor and F — quietly unify the track.

**Against this page.** He reads the record in **E-flat minor**, because backing track and vocal were varispeeded in opposite directions, and then discusses everything in E minor for legibility — which is the key this page uses. Same analysis, different reference pitch.

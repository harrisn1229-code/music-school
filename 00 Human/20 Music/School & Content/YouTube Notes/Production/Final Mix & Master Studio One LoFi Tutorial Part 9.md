# Final Mix & Master | Studio One LoFi Tutorial | Part 9

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OKUCuyLBSg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OKUCuyLBSg)

## Synopsis

The finale of the [[Make a Chill LoFi Beat in Studio One Part 1|lo-fi build-along series]] — final mix pass plus a deliberately simple mastering chain on the same beat built across Parts 1–7. Max frames the whole video around one idea: lo-fi doesn't need (and shouldn't get) a heavy mixing/mastering treatment — "the nice thing about lo-fi is you don't have to do so much with all your EQ and all the fancy mixing techniques 'cause it's meant to be low-fi... it doesn't have to be perfect, it's kind of nice." The mix half covers glue sidechain compression on the whole instrument bus (kicked from the kick, dialed to be felt not heard), a final low-end-cleanup pass instrument by instrument, and a quiet-volume balance check. The mastering half is a short four-stage chain — cleanup EQ, gentle glue compression with a self-sidechain filter, then a limiter — with concrete numbers throughout, plus a true-peak/intersample-peak safety margin for MP3 export. Closes with a short arrangement note (bring elements in/out, ideally a B-section) that's the video's only unfinished thread, left for viewers to do themselves.

## Techniques

- **Glue-sidechain the whole instrument bus off the kick, dialed to be felt not heard.** Route every non-drum instrument plus every reverb that isn't on the drums into a shared "instrument bus," put a standard bus compressor on it, then send the kick's own compressor sidechain input to that bus's compressor instance — switch the sidechain mode to **external/pre-fader ("preader")** so the duck amount stays constant even if the kick's own fader gets pulled down later (the send is independent of the kick's actual output level). Compressor setup for sidechain duty: **knee 0, high ratio** (doesn't matter much exactly how high), **attack instant, release ~300 ms**. Dial the threshold by ear with playback running: pull it down to an extreme first to hear the mechanism clearly (Max demos ~26 dB of reduction), then back off to the target — **6–9 dB GR**, explicitly *not* meant to be noticed as "a sidechain happening," just felt as cohesion. → [[Sidechaining]], [[2 Inst-Synth Group]]
- **Final low-end cleanup pass, instrument by instrument, once the arrangement is otherwise settled.** Go through every non-bass, non-kick element and pull out sub content it doesn't need, adjusting how much by how exposed that instrument's low end actually is: piano got more low cut than its existing highpass (it had some already, but not enough — cutting slightly into usable low-mid was accepted as the tradeoff); guitar could take a much bigger cut since its two primary notes in this part live well above the sub zone; the lead synth and its reverse-hit swell were already handled (see [[Retro Lead Synth Studio One LoFi Tutorial Part 7|Part 7]]'s inline EQ/reverb work); the bass — which owns this range — got only a token cut, ~20 Hz at 24 dB/oct, purely to remove inaudible super-low mud, not to shape its actual low end; drums were already handled on the drum bus's own top/bottom Pro-Q cut. The rule underneath: cut the loser's low end hard, leave the low-end owner (bass/kick) almost untouched. → [[Masking and Carving]]
- **Balance-check by ear at whisper volume, then hold every fader to ~0.5 dB of "done."** After the low-end pass, do a general glance-over of the whole mix (nothing formal) listening specifically for anything poking out — demoed on the hi-hats, where only one of two hats was audible in the mix even though both existed, caught by ear and fixed with a gain bump on the buried one. The core method: **listen quiet, not loud** — a mix that "sounds awesome" cranked can still be badly balanced; playing it back small is what actually reveals what pokes out or disappears. Once a mix is genuinely balanced, individual fader nudges of more than about **±0.5 dB** should already sound wrong in either direction — that's the target precision for a "done" balance pass, not a one-shot level-setting exercise. → [[Referencing and Translation]]
- **Mastering EQ stage — corrective only, deliberately minimal for the genre.** A Pro-EQ-type EQ on the master: a highpass sweeping out everything at **20 Hz and below** ("just in case anything got caught" — not expected to do much), plus a small dip around **200–250 Hz** to keep the low-mid range from getting congested once everything sums. Explicitly framed as a cleanup pass, not tone-shaping — lo-fi's whole appeal is that it doesn't need the "fancy mixing techniques" a cleaner genre would call for here. → [[Master Chain]]
- **Master bus glue compressor with a self-sidechain highpass filter — the key move that lets it squeeze everything except the kick.** Settings: **ratio 2:1, knee default, attack ~100 ms, release ~100 ms** (both slow enough that individual transients pass through untouched — "we don't want to clamp down on every little transient"), targeting only **2–3 dB GR max** on the loudest hits. Problem without more: the kick dominates and single-handedly triggers most of the gain reduction. Fix — **not** an external sidechain from another track, but filtering the compressor's *own* detector input: enable sidechain filtering and highpass it at **~70 Hz**, so everything below 70 Hz (the kick's dominant energy) stops reaching the detector while everything above still does. Net result: the kick still triggers *some* reduction (via its higher-frequency content and neighboring elements), but no longer dominates the compressor by itself — landed around **~2 dB GR** in the final setting. → [[Master Chain]], [[Compression]], [[Sidechaining]]
- **Limiter last, always — with a true-peak safety margin built in for MP3 export.** Push the limiter's **input gain up** until gain reduction on the loudest hits (the kicks) lands around **2–3 dB**, matching the target already set by the compressor stage. Then pull the **output ceiling back an extra ~1 dB** below 0 dBTP as insurance against **intersample peaks** — the true-peak overs that MP3 (lossy) conversion can introduce even when a file measures clean at 0 dBFS on a sample-peak meter. Judge the result qualitatively, not just by the meter: "I don't want to clip much more than three [dB] off the top of my peaks, because I put those peaks there — that's where I want them to be." → [[Master Chain]], [[Limiting]], [[Clipping and Loudness]]
- **Iterative kick-trim-then-compensate as the final loudness balancing move.** Since the kick is what's pushing the limiter hardest, pull the kick's own fader down slightly (~1–1.5 dB) rather than fighting it purely at the limiter, listen back, and if it still feels right, add a small amount of makeup back at the limiter's input (~1 dB) to recover the lost loudness — a two-knob back-and-forth (source level vs. limiter drive) rather than a single "crank the limiter" move, done by ear until the loudest sections read consistently a couple dB below where the kick alone would otherwise peak. → [[Master Chain]]
- **Close with an unfinished arrangement thread, left as viewer homework.** The only thing left after the mix/master pass is arrangement — bringing elements in and out over time rather than looping one part statically. Max flags explicitly that a real song version of this beat would want a B-section to alternate against the A-part, rather than looping a single section for a full 3-minute (or, "since it's lo-fi," even 1-minute) track — a genre-specific arrangement note, not a mixing/mastering technique, and not built out on camera in this video.

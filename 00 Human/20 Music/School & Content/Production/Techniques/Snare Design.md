# Snare Design

Building a snare/clap from scratch out of synths and layered effects instead of only auditioning samples — the snare-specific sibling of [[Kick Design]]. A snare (or clapper) is really just "any somewhat tonal sound with a punchy or snappy transient" — tonal dubstep-style snares that emulate a real drum sit at one end, pitched-up percussion sounds at the other, and real percussion one-shots (found or foleyed) in between. Straddles [[MIDS Slot]] (body, ~160–250 Hz) and [[TRANSIENT Slot]] (click/crack). Companion to [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]] (the mixing chain for a chosen/finished snare), [[Layering and Stacking]], and the [[Sound Palette]].

## Anatomy of a synth snare *(general starting points — tune by ear)*
| Part | What it is | Starting point |
|---|---|---|
| Body | A fixed-frequency oscillator ([[Operator]]) → [[MIDS Slot]] | Frequency macro-mapped ~160–250 Hz; Re-trigger on, Voices = 1; Sustain + Decay down, Initial + Attack up |
| Punch | A pitch envelope on the body oscillator | Peak ≈ 48, Decay brought down — reads as percussive rather than a pitch-sweep; FM the oscillator for a "perky" character |
| Tail | A noise oscillator ([[Operator]]) + reverb, EQ'd to stay out of the body's way | Map the reverb's Decay (kept short) and Size to macros; EQ out the low end + trim harmonics so the body still punches through |
| Extra transient / click | A second noise layer, harmonics pushed up, often with an initial click *before* the main hit for tension (the Jack Ü habit) | Short, bright, < 10 ms read |
| Texture | [[Shifter]] (chorus/frequency-shift or fine-tune, macro-mapped) and/or [[Corpus]] (physical-modeling resonance) layered under or over the built snare | Cap decay so texture layers don't ring out too long |
| Punchy resonance | A narrow EQ peak around ~200 Hz | Narrow Q = more ring; map Gain + Q to a macro (floor at 0) for controlled variation |

## Building the "snare starter" rack
- Build body → tail → extra-transient → texture as described above, group the whole thing into a rack, and save it as a reusable **"snare starter."** Keep macro-mapping every stage (frequency, pitch-envelope peak, reverb decay/size, EQ gain/Q, texture dry/wet) so the saved rack is a combinatorial starting point, not a single fixed sound — every future snare becomes a variation on the rack instead of a from-scratch build. → [[Randomized Rack Design]]
- **Resample to lock in a keeper.** Because a macro-mapped rack like this is randomizable, highlight a short section (roughly half a bar), Command+L to loop it, add a new audio track, and continuously resample the snare rack while nudging macros — Live 12.2's take-lane dropdown (or Enter on a stacked take) makes it fast to audition and keep the best pass. → [[Randomized Rack Design]]

## Layering and texture options
- **Metallic (SOPHIE/umu-style) snares via [[Grain Delay]].** Grain Delay samples small snippets of the incoming signal (snippet size set by its Frequency knob); with Time at zero, turning up **Spray** randomly and differently offsets the left/right timing on every grain cycle — the result reads as metallic, and the effect gets more varied (and "cooler") the more Feedback and Frequency get pushed. This is a texture layer over an existing plain snare, not a replacement for the body/tail build above.
- **Foley/percussion browser layering.** Filter the Samples browser for **One-Shot** + **Foley** or **Percussion** tags and save the search for reuse — a fast way to build a personal library of raw layering material (clicks, snaps, splashes) instead of hunting fresh each time. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **A pitched-up impact one-shot as a percussive layer.** Filter Samples → One-Shot → "impact" for something big and booming, then pitch it up ~4 octaves (same logic as pitching a dubstep snare up into percussion territory) via [[Simpler]]; chain [[Drum Bus]] → [[Pro-Q 4]] (light expansion/compression using its dynamic-band Attack/Release) for a perky, boomy hit. Some results read closer to a hi-hat than a snare — keep that in mind and pick accordingly.
- **Spectral transient shaping (unowned).** [[Kilohearts Spiff]] (see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) is a spectral transient shaper with a **Delta** mode (hear only what's being boosted vs. dry) — another route to a perky snare; layering its noisier results under a plain snare can make good clap/snare hybrids.
- **Reverb-based tails don't have to be noise** — a short, tight reverb (small Size) can stand in for the tail layer entirely, e.g. a "water splash" character instead of a noise burst.

## From books

- **Four-move snare EQ starting point — and the 200 Hz conflict worth resolving by ear.** Low cut **100–200 Hz** ("more if necessary") to get the snare out of the [[BASS Slot]] and off the kick · low shelf **200–300 Hz** for "umph"/body (top of the [[BASS Slot]] into the [[BED Slot]]) · boost **5–7 kHz** for crisp/crack (upper [[LEAD Slot]] into the [[AIR Slot]]) · high cut **15–20 kHz** ("remove harsh freq"). Note the first two moves butt directly against each other: the HPF's upper bound (200 Hz) is the shelf boost's lower bound, and [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track]] actually **boosts +1–3 dB @ 200–250 Hz** for exactly that thwack — so high-pass *below* wherever this snare's body actually sits rather than at a fixed 200 Hz, or you cut the body you're about to boost. The 5–7 kHz crack is the brighter half of the chain's existing "crack/presence 3–6 kHz." *The 15–20 kHz high cut is a headroom move, not a harshness fix — real snare harshness is 2–5 kHz and is better handled dynamically ([[Soothe2]], [[De-essing]]); compare Lophiile's "EQ out the papery top of a saturated snare" already noted on the track chain.* → [[EQ]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]] — Whipped Cream Sounds, [[Drum Mixing EQ Guide - Enrichment|Drum Mixing EQ Guide]]

## From videos

- **Space the snare out to once per bar (instead of the usual every-other-beat density) specifically to create rhythmic CONTRAST against a fast hi-hat pattern, then layer it with a separate ambient percussive hit (its own reverb) to help it sit in the mix.** Reverse-engineering Brent Faiyaz's own "kind of weird" snare spacing on *Mercedes*: rather than the expected frequent hits, the snare lands only once per bar — deliberately playing against fast-moving hi-hats above it for a fast/slow contrast. loner's general recommendation, stated directly on camera: **"I'd really recommend layering your snares like this especially if you're finding it hard to get the snare to sit in the mix nicely"** — pairing the snare with a distinct ambient-percussion hit (separately reverbed) rather than relying on EQ/compression alone to carve its space. → [[Reverb]], [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Brent Faiyaz Ambient Synth Chain]] — loner, [how to make ambient synth beats for brent faiyaz (r&b music theory trick!?)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ugup5iVzGYY)
- **A stick sound layered underneath a snare/rim, kept nearly inaudible on its own, purely to add depth rather than to be consciously heard.** "You can hardly even hear that, but it just adds a little bit more depth to the snare" — one more confirmation of below-the-threshold-of-audibility layering as a legitimate, deliberate mixing move. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Frank Ocean Chill Ambient Chain]] — loner, [how to make chill ambient beats for frank ocean (sad/melodic chord technique)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=whI75NJcom8)
- **A rim hit repurposed as a snare, discovered by trying it in the snare slot rather than swapping in a different sample.** In a 10-sound constraint challenge with no extra samples allowed, getting stuck on what to use for a snare — with only a rim already used elsewhere in the kit — was solved by trying the rim in the snare's place; it read convincingly. The general lesson stated on camera: manipulating/recontextualizing a sound (not just processing it) reveals how far it can be taken beyond its "default" role. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — loner, [i made beats WITHOUT VSTs for an entire week!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gZMSr8CjqOc)
- **Replace every other snare hit with an ambient percussion sound for a less mechanical R&B backbeat.** Instead of a straight repeated snare pattern, alternate: snare, ambient-perc-hit-in-place-of-snare, snare, ambient-perc-hit, etc. — breaks up the predictability of a repeated snare sample cheaply, without needing a second full snare sample. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — loner, [these sampling secrets will instantly elevate your beats!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_97dAWKhLv0)
- **Add a Hold stage to the amp envelope (~30–50 ms) before Decay** so the decay only shapes the tail, not the initial transient — a small drum-envelope habit that applies equally to synth snares as kicks. → [[Kick Design]] — Max Konyi, [Synthwave in Studio One with Stock Instruments | Weekly Challenge #9](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IA61_2JAd84)
- **Duplicate a snare to a new pad, drench it 100% wet in a specific convolution IR (e.g. "Springs"), then apply that verb-drenched duplicate to only SOME hits for per-hit variation** — copy a snare sample to a new Drum Rack slot (Option/Alt-drag on Mac, Ctrl-drag on Windows), name it distinctly ("snare verb"), and put a 100%-Dry/Wet Hybrid Reverb on it with Blend fully to the **Convolution** side, choosing an evocative built-in IR ("Springs") rather than the algorithmic half — then in the clip, swap only alternating/selected snare hits over to that verb-drenched pad instead of applying the effect to every hit, so dry and reverb-drenched snares trade off across the pattern for texture variety. → [[Hybrid Reverb]] — You Suck at Producing (Underbelly ft. Arbor), [how to turn simple lofi beats into FULL SONGS ft. @prodarbour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VOMJUqx_rm0)
- **A full SOPHIE-style metallic-snare build order: Operator body → noise tail → Phaser-Flanger → Grain Delay → Drum Bus** — a dedicated metallic-snare pass that extends the plain body/tail recipe above with a specific delay-stacking chain for the metal character. Body: fixed-frequency Operator at **200 Hz**, Sustain down, one voice, Retrigger on, pitch-envelope click — same as above, plus a new refinement: draw extra harmonic bars onto the oscillator and set **Repeat to 1** for a brighter body (see [[Operator]]'s drawn-harmonics technique, applied here to a snare specifically). Tail: a second, looped noise oscillator turned fully up. Metal stage 1 — **[[Phaser-Flanger]]** as a self-modulating auto-flange: oscillating frequency pushed into flange range, Feedback maxed, driven by its own envelope follower (quick attack, ~0.5 short release), LFO waveform set to **Sample & Hold**, **Phase ≈ 14** for a slight L/R time offset, Warmth maxed, some dry snare bled through. Metal stage 2 — **[[Grain Delay]]**: Feedback maxed, short delay time, Spray on — chaining two different delay-family devices (flanger + granular delay) compounds two distinct metallic characters into one hit. Tame stage — **[[Drum Bus]]**: Transients pulled fully down, Crunch driven, Damping maxed, to pull the bright ringing result back into a drum-shaped hit. Optional finishing: resample while still tweaking, an optional compressed-room reverb, and/or a resonator pass (possibly [[Corpus]], not confidently identified in the audio) for a tonal "color snare." The channel's own explicit thesis: **"if you want to make anything metal, just use delays"** — a flanger and a Grain Delay are both forms of delay. → [[Operator]], [[Phaser-Flanger]], [[Grain Delay]], [[Drum Bus]], [[Corpus]] — dnksaus, [How to Metallic Snares like SOPHIE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MuD_Rq04tic)
- **Snare Drum of Fire: a metallic-snare rack used as a capture tool, not a finished sound** — a Dank Sauces Rack Pack rack for recording metallic snares; the workflow is to build the rack, record with a scratch MIDI pattern to hear it working, then delete that MIDI, drop in your own favorite snare sample, align it to the grid/transient, and adjust the transients from there — the rack shapes whatever snare sample gets fed into it rather than generating the sound from nothing. Sibling to the dedicated metallic-snare [[Grain Delay]] technique above. → [[Grain Delay]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — dnksaus, [Dank Sauces Rack Pack - Demoing Every Rack!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xuKc_a6xxjk)
- **Vocode a noise/hat layer into a "polarized" per-band snare texture** — split a synth snare's noise/texture layer with a high/low splitter first, then vocode just one band (Formant up, Release tuned toward a ride-cymbal-like character, Depth/Brightness adjusted) so the vocoding only colors part of the hit rather than the whole thing. → [[Vocoder]] — dnksaus, [Why Vocoder is Ableton's Most Versatile Device!!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v6tJJ2Y8Eos)
- **Reinvent any drum hit as a new snare: kill Warp, triple the pitch** — take an existing one-shot (drum or otherwise), turn off Warp mode entirely and multiply the playback pitch by roughly 3x — reliably turns an unrelated hit into a usable brand-new snare sound, framed as part of an ongoing personal habit of making custom snares this way. Fully randomizable in rack form. → [[Randomized Rack Design]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — dnksaus, [Instrument Racks + Dank Sauces Updates](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kd16qh8_h4g)
- **A clap-variation-generator instrument (unowned) as a resample source** — "Palmas," a free Max for Live instrument, generates a different clap variation on every trigger (pitch, width, a "hand count" control that spreads hits in time like a strumming effect, and a minimum-velocity knob) — worth resampling a good pass rather than leaving it live; useful for build-ups or layering under the claps/snares built with the techniques below. → [[Plugin Wishlist]] — dnksaus, [10 Devices You Should Know About (Max For Live)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B0i_S4jbhso)
- **Build a synth snare from scratch: Operator body + pitch envelope, noise tail + reverb, extra transient layer, then texture** — the full macro-mapped signal chain documented above, saved as a reusable "snare starter" rack. → [[Randomized Rack Design]] — dnksaus, [craft UNIQUE snares](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WeHN9GPokMA)
- **Metallic snares via Grain Delay's Spray** — short delay time (near zero) + random per-cycle L/R offset (Spray) reads as metallic; push Feedback/Frequency for more variety — "the randomness is what makes the metal sound cooler." → [[Grain Delay]] — dnksaus, [craft UNIQUE snares](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WeHN9GPokMA)
- **Corpus as a splinky texture layer under an existing snare** — map most macros except Width/L-R/Spread, keep Dry/Wet mapped, optionally add an LFO for a "splinky" modulated character, and cap decay so it doesn't ring out. → [[Corpus]] — dnksaus, [craft UNIQUE snares](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WeHN9GPokMA)
- **Ask "does this snare slap?" as a blunt gut-check, played at full volume (Zedd)** — for genres that want a big snare, play the snare alone at full volume and force a direct yes/no answer instead of second-guessing it through endless EQ/compression tweaks. — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Snare chain: fast/aggressive distortion for transient beef-up, then an EQ that cuts low-end artifacts and boosts the frequencies that make it stand out.** The snare gets hit with fast distortion first, then an EQ pass that cuts unwanted low-end artifacts and boosts the parts of the snare that actually stand out — the sample itself is doing most of the work, this is a light touch on top. → [[EQ]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (18 HUGE Music Production Tips & Tricks) [part 2]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99hrrt4tYko)

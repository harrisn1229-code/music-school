# Songs that use the Phrygian mode

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-DzGlzdbkDI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-DzGlzdbkDI)

## Synopsis

David Bennett defines Phrygian as natural minor with a lowered 2nd degree — the one note that gives it an extra layer of darkness/exoticism beyond plain Aeolian — then surveys where that sound lands naturally (metal, hip-hop), a chord-substitution trick for resolving a Phrygian cadence more strongly, the genuine ambiguity between Phrygian and its parent major scale when a song doesn't clearly state its tonic, and the difference between Phrygian and the closely related but distinct Phrygian dominant/double harmonic scales used in Arabic and flamenco-adjacent music.

## Techniques

- **The lowered 2nd is the whole story.** Phrygian = natural minor with scale degree 2 lowered a half-step — the note to listen for/place deliberately. Natural home in metal (Metallica's "Wherever I May Roam," "Harvester of Sorrow") and hip-hop (Snoop Dogg's "Gin and Juice" — i–♭II vamp; Kendrick Lamar's "Humble" — one altered degree in a minimal riff carries the whole dark quality).
- **Tonic ambiguity is a real, not just theoretical, disagreement** — "New Person, Same Old Mistakes" (Tame Impala) uses only A♭ major's notes; listeners split on whether the tonic reads as C (C Phrygian) or A♭ (A♭ major). Same notes, same sound, different felt "home" — the ambiguity itself is part of the modal effect.
- **Cadence-strengthening chord substitution**: "London Calling" (The Clash) sits in E Phrygian but resolves its hook via a borrowed **D major** (not diatonic Phrygian's D minor) moving to E minor — the major D's F♯ pulls up one semitone to E minor's 3rd, a stronger resolution than diatonic D minor's whole-step pull.
- **Quarter-tone caveat** — Arabic/Middle Eastern music's equivalent lowered 2nd is often a genuine quarter-tone, not the West's fixed semitone, so calling that material "Phrygian" is an approximation (example: "Hadi Kedra," Nass El Ghiwane, sampled by The Chemical Brothers for "Galvanize").
- **Phrygian ≠ Phrygian dominant ≠ double harmonic** — all three share the exotic lowered-2nd flavor but differ in 3rd/7th degree: Phrygian dominant (major 3rd) heard in "White Rabbit" (Jefferson Airplane, swings between Phrygian/Phrygian-dominant) and "Pyramid Song" (Radiohead, blends Phrygian/Phrygian-dominant/Aeolian); double harmonic/Hijaz (major 3rd + raised 7th) is what "Misirlou" actually uses, despite being commonly mislabeled Phrygian.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Wait]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #87.*

**Form.** An orphan from the *Help!* sessions pulled back out to fill *Rubber Soul*, and his argument is that **you cannot actually hear the seam** — itself an argument against treating those two albums as separated by a great divide. Formally it is dense for a filler track. It opens on an **off-beat vocal pickup** with no intro and no instrumental downbeat to cue the singer, and it has no real outro either — it just rhetorically stops, the last verse's final phrase dropping into slow motion. The main section is a **14-bar verse/refrain hybrid**, splitting into a 6-bar "verse" (3 + 3) and an 8-bar "refrain" (4 + 4); he says it is tempting to call these two separate sections but that over-reads what you actually hear. The bridge runs two roughly parallel phrases differing in instrumentation and final chord.

**Harmony.** `Verse (f♯ minor): i7 – B6/4 – b6/4 – i · V – i (×2)` — but the Roman numerals are misleading, because **F♯ is held as a pedal under all four chords** with a descending inner line E – D♯ – D♮ – C♯ above it, so the ear hears one stretched i-to-V move rather than a progression ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]). `Refrain (A major): I – IV · I – V-of-f♯ → i` — an abrupt, unhedged modulation to the relative major, then a clean pivot home ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]). `Bridge: ii – V – I – vi · ii – V – I(=♭III of f♯) – V(4–3)`, whose key is **genuinely undecidable and better left that way** ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]): read it in A and the B minor is ii; read it in F♯ minor and the E major becomes V-of-♭III. The ambiguity is the effect.

**Notable.** Syncopated throughout, sharpened by the non-square 3 + 3 phrasing and a harmonic rhythm that lands **off the beat almost constantly** ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]); against all that motion the lyric layout is rigidly symmetrical, ABCACBA. The **percussion is staged phrase by phrase** — tambourine, then maracas in even eighths, then the full kit on a drum roll with the tambourine switching to match.

**Against this page.** Pollack reads it in **F♯ minor**, which is this page's sounding pitch (E minor shapes, capo 2). That does not match the `key:` field here, which says C major. Nothing above has been changed.

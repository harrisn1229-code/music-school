## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — I've Got A Feeling]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #173.*

**Form.** A loose bluesy groove hiding a genuinely unusual formal experiment: **two separate songs, alternated and then stacked on top of each other**. He files it under *quodlibet* rather than medley and reaches for two Broadway precedents, partly to note that both do it better — his verdict is that the idea outruns the material, because Paul's half and John's half don't contrast enough for the superimposition to pay off. The 24-bar verse is laid out as six four-bar units but words and tune cut across that into an **8 + 12 + 4** phrasing, and that asymmetry is what keeps a very static section from feeling like a loop. The bridge is a compressed, rhetorical **10 bars**. The stacked section runs only 16 bars, not 24 — his stated principle in miniature: when a section has already been heard many times, **condense the late repeats**.

**Harmony.** Four chords plus a chromatic device. `Intro / verse vamp: I with IV6/4 decoration, alternating bars` · `Verse (24): [vamp ×4] · I7 – I7 – V–♭VII – IV · I – IV – I – IV` · `Verse′ (24): I – IV – I – IV, ×6` · `Bridge (10): V (2) – ♭VII (2) · IV–IV6/3 – ♭VII–G♯dim7 · I (4)` · `Outro (8): I – Bdim–Cdim – C♯dim – Cdim–Bdim · [vamp ×4]`. He insists the alternating IV6/4 in the vamp is **decoration inside a harmonic envelope on I**, not real root motion — the same call he makes for the slinking diminished chords over their pedal ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]) — and the **V → ♭VII** move used in both verse and bridge drags a cross relation along with it ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]).

**Notable.** The most instructive small thing: **Paul is precise about where he breaks the pedal**, dropping the actual root under the IV chord only at chosen spots, so the vamp reads as static while the bass is being edited underneath. Entrances are staged across a long span — two guitars only through bar 8, drums at bar 9, bass not until bar 17, piano at bar 20 ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]).

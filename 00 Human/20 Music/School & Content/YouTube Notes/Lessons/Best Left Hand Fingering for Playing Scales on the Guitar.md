# Best Left Hand Fingering for Playing Scales on the Guitar (how to shift positions)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NbbfDUp62KQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NbbfDUp62KQ)

## Synopsis

Jared Borkowski names and demonstrates the **"inchworm technique"** — a specific left-hand fingering discipline for the three of the five major-scale forms/positions (CAGED-style) that shift position mid-shape, replacing an awkward reach or jump with a contract-then-expand finger movement.

## What's taught

- **The five major scale forms are all the same scale in five neck positions** — the standard CAGED-derived movable shapes, each sharing the same root note pitch class, covering the full fretboard between them.
- **Position is defined by first-finger fret placement** — "first position" means the 1st finger sits on the 1st fret, etc.; two of the five scale forms stay entirely within one position (no shifting required), while three shift position partway through the shape and then return.
- **The inchworm technique, named and demonstrated for the position-shifting shapes:** instead of reaching the 3rd finger across an awkward stretch, or jumping the whole hand to the new position, play the note just before the string change with the **4th finger** instead of the 3rd — this "contracts" the hand (fingers crunch together), and the next string's notes then "expand" back out to normal spacing in the new position. Named for the physical analogy: like an inchworm, the hand briefly contracts, then extends one step further in the direction of travel, rather than stretching or leaping.
- **Rationale: minimize tension and reaching, even when a stretch or shift is technically "doable."** Explicitly framed as a discipline of efficiency, not a fix for something unplayable — a reach or shift might feel fine at slow tempo, but the inchworm's job is conserving energy and minimizing tension as a *habit*, so the efficiency is already automatic when speed or difficulty actually demands it.
- **Applies symmetrically ascending and descending** — the same contract/expand logic runs in reverse when descending back through a position-shifting scale form (contract with the 4th finger on the way back across the shift point, rather than stretching the 1st or 2nd finger backward).

# Pat Martino's Dimensional Planes and Parental Forms

Part of [[Jazz Harmony]] and [[Chord Substitution]]. Pat Martino's system for stacking multiple substitute sounds over a **single static chord**, taught here via Nathan Borton's breakdown of Martino's instructional video *Quantum Guitar*. Two layers: **"dimensional planes"** is the counting framework (how many distinct sounds you're playing over one chord at once), and **"parental forms"** is the generative engine (two symmetric source shapes — diminished and augmented — that each spawn a family of substitute chords a minor third/major third apart). Distinct from, but overlapping heavily with, the vault's existing diminished-substitution material on [[Diminished Chords]] (explicitly name-checked in the source as "very similar to Barry Harris's method") and the triad-superimposition devices on [[Triad Melodic Devices for Jazz Soloing]] — this page's contribution is Martino's specific naming/counting system and the **combined diminished+augmented hybrid shape** he builds from it.

## The dimensional-plane framework — how many sounds over one chord
Worked entirely over a static **A7**:
1. **One-dimensional plane** — play only the sound of the chord itself: the A7 arpeggio, or vocabulary/language built from the A7 sound. The baseline case, one sound over one chord.
2. **Two-dimensional plane** — add a second sound: play the **ii chord over the V** (a Martino-favorite substitution) — **E minor 7 over A7** — either as a straight Em7 arpeggio or as language built from an E minor sound, layered against the underlying A7. → [[Chord Substitution]] §4 *Interpolating a ii* is the closest documented analogue (though that entry comps the ii chord itself; this is soloing the ii's *sound* over the still-sounding V, closer in spirit to §*"2 equals 5, 5 equals 2"* two entries below it on that page).
3. **Three-dimensional plane** — add a third sound: the **tritone substitute of A7, which is E♭7** (arpeggio or E♭-flavored language — direct application of [[Chord Substitution]] §2 tritone substitution), *plus* that E♭7's own **ii chord, B♭ minor**, giving a fourth optional layer within the "three sounds" count (A7 itself, Em7/A7, E♭7/A7, B♭m/A7).
4. **Four-(or-more-)dimensional plane** — the payoff level: **four or more distinct sounds** stacked over the one static chord, generated systematically via the **parental forms** below rather than picked one at a time by ear.

## The parental forms — two symmetric shapes that generate whole chord families
Both forms exploit the fact that a **diminished 7th chord** and an **augmented triad** are symmetrical (repeat in minor thirds and major thirds respectively — see [[Diminished Chords]] and [[Augmented Chords]]), so altering one note of the shape by a half step produces a *different, usable* chord, and doing this systematically to every note of the symmetric shape produces a whole family of chords that all substitute for each other.

### Diminished form — four dominant chords from one diminished chord (Barry-Harris-adjacent)
**Mechanism:** take a diminished 7th chord (worked example: **G diminished** — G, B♭, D♭, E), lower the **top note by a half step**. G° with the top note (E) lowered a half step becomes **E♭ dominant 7, inverted with the ♭7 in the bass** (G-B♭-D♭-E♭ reread as an E♭7 chord voicing). Repeat the "lower a note by a half step" operation on **each of the diminished chord's four notes in turn** and you get **four different dominant 7 chords**, each a minor third apart (matching G°'s own symmetry) — all four work as substitutes over the same target dominant. **Each of those four dominant chords can also be played as a minor chord instead** (a rootless/reinterpreted reading — Martino does this directly), doubling the usable substitute count to eight sounds (four dominant, four minor) all derivable from one diminished shape. **Direct cross-reference:** this is the same "any diminished chord built off the 3rd/5th/♭7/𝄫7 of a dominant gives a rootless dominant 7♭9" mechanism already documented in depth on [[Diminished Chords]] § *From videos* (Barry Harris's "tonic diminished," the general dim-as-dominant-substitute rule, and the eight-triad-pair derivation on [[Upper Structure Triads from Seventh Chords]]) — Martino's framing just names the "lower one note at a time" generative process explicitly and stacks it with the augmented form below, which those entries don't do.

### Augmented form — major and minor triads from one augmented triad
**Mechanism:** take an augmented triad. **Lowering each note by a half step in turn** produces a family of **major** triads; **raising each note by a half step in turn** produces a family of **minor** triads. Playing all these derived triads together (over the same target) creates the composite "augmented sound." Martino doesn't go deep on this form in *Quantum Guitar* per the source, but the mechanism mirrors the diminished form exactly — one note altered at a time around a symmetric shape, generating a family of usable substitute triads a major third apart (matching the augmented triad's own symmetry). *(General starting point — the augmented form gets noticeably less worked-example detail in the source than the diminished form; treat the mechanism as confirmed but the practice depth as thinner.)*

### Combining both forms — the hybrid "A7♯5" shape and why it unlocks both families at once
Martino's actual discovery: take **one** chord shape and apply **both** operations to it — lower the top note (diminished-form move) **and** raise the next note down (augmented-form move) on the *same* voicing. Worked from a G° starting shape: lowering the top note and raising the next note down produces **an A triad over a G bass** — which reads as the same sound as **A7♯5** (i.e. a G-rooted voicing that's simultaneously readable as an inverted A7♯5). **Why this shape is special:** because it was built out of the diminished form, it inherits that form's **minor-third transposability** — the identical shape/line works over **A7♯5, C7♯5, E♭7♯5, and G♭7♯5** (all a minor third apart) and their **respective minor-chord reinterpretations** (per the diminished-form doubling above). But because the shape is *also* an augmented triad with a bass note (A augmented triad over G), it simultaneously inherits the **augmented form's major-third-related triad family** — so at *each* of those four diminished-form target chords, you additionally get a *different* augmented-derived triad to pull extra major/minor color from:
- **A7♯5** — pull from the **A augmented** parental form; worked with **F major triad** (derived by lowering a note of the A augmented triad).
- **C7♯5** — pull from **C augmented**'s family; worked starting on **D♭ minor** (the diminished-form-doubled minor reading), or alternatively **A♭ major triad** (derived from the C augmented form).
- **G♭7♯5** — pull from **G♭ augmented**'s family; worked starting on **B♭ minor** again, adding in a **D triad** (lowering the last note of the shape).

**Worked line:** a bebop-vocabulary line outlining an **E minor 7 sound**, originally played over **A7♯5** (since E minor comes from that G°-derived diminished-form family). Because the line's source chord belongs to the diminished form, the identical line also works untransposed-in-shape over **G minor, B♭ minor, and D♭ minor** — the diminished form's other three minor-third-related targets. Layering the augmented form on top, the *same* line can additionally be paired with the **F major triad** (from A7♯5's own augmented-family member) for an even richer combined color — demonstrating the "four-dimensional-plane-and-beyond" payoff directly: one memorized line, reused verbatim across four harmonically-related dominant-♯5 targets, each with its own extra augmented-triad color layered on top.

## Practice recommendation (stated directly in the source)
- Start narrow: pick **one** sound from the diminished form and **one** from the augmented form and get comfortable with just those two before trying to stack more. Concretely suggested pairing: the **B♭ minor 7 sound over A7** (diminished form — "easy to think about and sounds great") plus the **F major triad over A7** (augmented form).
- **Guitar-shape mnemonic for finding the augmented-form triad fast:** on an **A7♯5** chord shape, the **top note is the ♯5 (F)** — so the corresponding augmented-form major triad is simply "a major triad built with that same top note (F) as its top voice," letting you locate the substitute triad directly from the chord grip you already know rather than deriving it from scratch each time.
- **Practice in small fragments, then return for more** — stated as "the ideal way to practice this method": don't try to run the full four-or-more-dimensional stack at once: build up from one-dimensional, confirm each new layer sounds good in isolation, then combine.
- The method generalizes past dominant chords — "not only over dominant chords but any type of chord" — the worked examples here are all dominant/♯5 because that's what *Quantum Guitar* covers, but the diminished/augmented parental-form generative process applies to any chord quality.

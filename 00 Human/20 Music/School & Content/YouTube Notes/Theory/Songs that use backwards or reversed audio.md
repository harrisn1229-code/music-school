# Songs that use backwards or reversed audio

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6g_dbZPg4Rw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6g_dbZPg4Rw)

## Synopsis

David Bennett traces backmasking/reversed audio from the Beatles' origination of the technique through its "hidden Satanic message" moral-panic era to modern reversed-guitar-solo lineages, plus one case (Radiohead) where a reversed take became the seed for an entirely different, fully-realized song.

## Techniques

- **Reversing a whole take can generate a new song, not just a texture effect**: Radiohead reversed a stuck "I Will" take during the Kid A sessions, preferred the reversed melody, and built "Like Spinning Plates" from it — the released "I Will" (finished later for Hail to the Thief) traces audibly back to "Like Spinning Plates" when played backwards, even though it isn't literally the same take.
- **Reversed guitar solo lineage**: Jimi Hendrix ("Castles Made of Sand") → Red Hot Chili Peppers (cited as influence, own reverse solo on "Give It Away") → Oasis ("The Masterplan," "Do You Know What I Mean?," citing the Beatles), though Blur got there first in Britpop ("There's No Other Way"). Beatles origin: George Harrison's "I'm Only Sleeping" solo (Revolver) — performed normally then physically reversed, composed so the reversed version is the intended musical result.
- **Backmasking's "hidden message" reputation is cultural history, not a technique**: "Revolution 9"'s ambiguous backwards phrase seeded the "Paul is dead" myth and a wave of 1980s moral-panic legislation attempts (Stairway to Heaven, ELO's Eldorado both targeted); the backlash ironically inspired some bands (Pink Floyd's "Empty Spaces") to deliberately hide real messages backwards once the myth existed.
- Origin: "Rain" (1966) is the first backmasked vocal on a pop/rock record — John Lennon wanted the whole song reversed, McCartney/George Martin talked him down to one segment; the same sessions produced the reversed sitar texture on "Tomorrow Never Knows."

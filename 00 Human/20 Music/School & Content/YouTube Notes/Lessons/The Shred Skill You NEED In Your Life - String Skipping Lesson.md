# The Shred Skill You NEED In Your Life - String Skipping Lesson

## Synopsis

A workout on string skipping — deliberately picking non-adjacent strings (e.g. low E and D, leaving A untouched) instead of the default adjacent-string-pair shape most shred licks fall into — framed as the fix for licks starting to feel repetitive. The bulk of the lesson is the picking-angle problem this creates at the skip itself.

## Techniques

- **The core idea:** escape constant adjacent-string-pair phrasing by skipping a string in the middle of a lick, opening up lick variety beyond the usual ascending/descending two-string shapes.
- **The picking-angle problem:** switching from a downstroke (lower string) to an upstroke (skipped-to higher string) is comparatively easy; the harder direction is switching back — upstroke on the higher string into a downstroke on the lower string — because the picking hand has to travel the same distance in reverse, and unwanted noise/pull-off artifacts creep in easily.
- **Practice fix:** slow down specifically at the direction-switch point with a metronome, since timing problems concentrate there (bigger hand travel = easier to speed up or drag exactly at the skip), not across the phrase generally.
- **Worked example:** a descending run through E natural minor/Aeolian (same note set as A minor/C major, F raised to F♯), with a theory twist at the end shifting to E Phrygian (F♯ lowered back to F, landing on the same C major/A minor notes — readable as C Ionian's 3rd mode or A Aeolian's 5th). Moved through multiple neck positions as the actual workout.

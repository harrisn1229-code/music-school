## Synopsis

Applies mode theory to chord-building rather than soloing: BERNTH derives the full diatonic 7th-chord set for A Dorian side by side with A natural minor, shows a one-note voicing trick for getting an instant Dorian color on a plain minor chord, and compares the same progression (i-♭VI-♭III-♭VII) harmonized both ways. Confirms and extends the vault's existing [[Modal Pop]] "Dorian and the major IV" principle with a full chord table and a direct voicing trick.

## What's taught

- **Full diatonic 7th-chord derivation for A Dorian, compared chord-by-chord against A natural minor:** Am7 - Bm7 - Cmaj7 - D7 - Em7 - F♯m7♭5 - Gmaj7 (vs. natural minor's Am - B°/Bm7♭5 - C - Dm/Dm7 - Em/Em7 - F/Fmaj7 - G/G7 on the same degrees). The single raised note (F→F♯, Dorian's characteristic 6th) changes the ii chord's quality (from half-diminished-adjacent to a clean minor 7) and — the headline change — turns iv from minor (Dm) into a **major/dominant IV (D7)**, matching [[Modal Pop]]'s existing "Dorian and the major IV" principle from the harmonization-table side rather than the two-vamp-comparison side.
- **Instant-Dorian voicing trick: stack the characteristic 6th directly onto a plain tonic minor triad/power chord.** Rather than looking up a "Dorian chord" shape, take the ordinary A minor triad (A-C, or A-C-E) and add F♯ on top — the raised 6th alone is enough to read as Dorian-colored, without needing the full 7-chord vocabulary.
- **Worked progression comparison: i-♭VI-♭III-♭VII, voiced in plain triads for natural minor vs. full 7th chords for Dorian**, played back to back to demonstrate how drastically different the same roman-numeral progression sounds once every chord picks up Dorian's raised 6th where applicable — framed as a practical songwriting choice (which mood/atmosphere is wanted), not a right/wrong distinction.
- **Homework generalization:** the same derive-the-diatonic-chords-and-compare method applies to any other mode, not just Dorian — write a short piece using both the mode's scale (for melody) and its own diatonic chord set (for harmony) as a practice assignment.

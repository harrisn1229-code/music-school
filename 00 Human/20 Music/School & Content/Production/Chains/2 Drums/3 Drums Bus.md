# Drums Bus

**Role:** The drum section's final glue + loudness stage. Your current working chain (Bus_Drums export) kept as default — API glue into tape into density into color into a catch-limiter is a pro-standard structure.

## Chain overview
Glue comp → tape → density → color → peak catch. Compressor first so everything downstream saturates a *steady* signal; limiter last purely as a peak net.

## Chain

### 1. [[API-2500]] — Glue compression
- 🎬 *From video:* build a four-aux parallel-send system off this bus — after crushing it hard through comp/limiting, rebuild ONLY what got lost via dedicated sends rather than backing off the crush. Jaycen Joshua: "with these four, I'm making up what I'm losing by hitting the compression and the limiter and the Black Box super, super hard." Aux 1 ("Transformer") = [[Decapitator]] (dark, mid-aggressive) → an SSL-style "Cross-Eyed" comp (Attack 30, Release 1, Ratio 2:1, ~8 dB GR — see [[Glue Compressor]]) → low end stripped, top-end "spark" only. Aux 2 = an MV2-style low-level enhancer as a front-to-back attention effect (unowned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]). Aux 3 ("Transformer 2," on the kick specifically) = same Decapitator+comp recipe with Decapitator's Style E (Chandler-style) for a deliberately blown-out, Fruity-Loops-style hip-hop kick character — subtle here, pushable to full aggression on harder records. → [[Decapitator]], [[Glue Compressor]], [[Parallel Comp Send]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — Mix with the Masters (Jaycen Joshua), [Jaycen Joshua on creative drum mixing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BELkgU5j2ww)
- 🎬 *From video:* a named alternative bus compressor reached for specifically to add impact rather than just glue: **UA 33609**, driven for obvious "extra sauce" that "really makes him hit" — a heavier-drive, presence-forward use distinct from this stage's default subtler glue role. Bypass-checked against the raw signal. Unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]; owned equivalents in the same energetic-drum-bus-comp role are this chain's own API-2500 (pushed harder) or [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII]] — Mix with the Masters (Marcella Araica), [[Printing stems through analog equipment with Marcella Araica]]
- 🎬 *From video:* a contrasting light-touch alternative worth trying on a subtler mix: Jaycen Joshua runs his own drum bus through a **Shadow Hills Mastering Compressor** (plugin, A/B'd against hardware and preferred for automation flexibility) barely engaged — "I just kiss it," relying on the unit's character rather than gain reduction. Owned equivalents in the same subtle-glue role: [[Glue Compressor]] or [[Solid Bus Comp]], driven for tone with GR kept minimal. See [[Plugin Wishlist]] for the Shadow Hills entry — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters), [Jaycen Joshua's drum bus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LY-vU5MwBvs)
- 🎬 *From video:* on "My Hi"'s drum bus, the API-2500 was pushed for a **brutal transient slam** at the entry point — driven hard enough that it's audibly aggressive on its own, then dialed back via the plugin's own mix knob rather than reducing the ratio/threshold (drive the circuit hard for its character, use Mix to tame the amount) — Disclosure, [First Twitch Stream Ever (Production Tutorial)](https://youtu.be/IObzp3OugtY)
- 🎬 *From video:* a SEPARATE shared parallel "kick-snare crush" send off this bus, downstream of this stage's own glue — a DBX-160-style compressor at **~4:1–4.5:1**, transient-triggered, not much GR, adding length and reshaping the attack rather than obviously squashing; stacked with two further parallel stereo drum-bus compressors (a Fatso-style unit for peak limiting, and a Fairchild-670-style "drum crush" run as two mono instances deliberately UNLINKED left/right) — full recipe on [[Parallel Processing]] — Mix with the Masters (Andrew Scheps), [[Mix buss & drum processing with Andrew Scheps]]
- 🎬 *From video:* an automated "drums dirt" push send off this bus (high-pass EQ → lo-fi/downsample → aggressive multiband clipper → an EQ cutting the mid-range mess that stage creates), muted everywhere except transitions and choruses — beefs up the whole kit in the moment without just raising the fader; room-mic tracks get the same automated in/out treatment for the same sections — Mix with the Masters (Andrew Scheps), [[Mix buss & drum processing with Andrew Scheps]] → [[Redux]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Automation]]
- **Ratio 4:1** (②) · **Attack 30 ms** (③ — slowest step, the punch dial: slower = bigger transients) · **Release: click the knob to its final position for variable/Auto release** (④ — recovers faster after light compression, slower after deep hits; ~120 ms if set fixed) · **2.5 dB GR** on the beat
- Tone section: **Thrust = Loud** (⑤ — max low-freq detector filtering so the kick stops pumping the whole bus; drop to Med only if it thins the kick) · **Knee = Hard** (⑧ — "the 2500 sound") · **Type = New** (⑥ — feed-forward, snappy/modern punch; **Old** = warmer, laid-back vintage — pick per song)
- **Link 100%** (⑦ — unlinked, a hard left-side tom compresses only the left channel and shoves your centered kick/snare toward the right)
- **Mix 100%** (⑨) for straight glue; drop to **~75%** for the parallel transient-slam move (drive the circuit hard for character, tame the amount with Mix, not the ratio/threshold) · **Output** (⑩) to make up the 2–3 dB back to unity
- Output gain up until the drums fill the same space as bypassed, just tighter (or flip the auto-makeup switch)
- **Why:** The API-2500 is *the* drum bus compressor; Thrust is its superpower — without it, every kick hit ducks the snare/hats. The classic drum recipe: slow attack + fast release + aggressive ratio.
- **Alt use — one-plugin parallel:** high ratio, fast attack, fast release, slam it, then blend with the **mix knob** — instant "energy giver" without a separate crush send.
- 🎬 *From video:* complement bus glue by **faking hard compression at the source** — nudge a clap/hat ~10–13 ms *ahead* of the kick so its transient pokes through before the body (a comp always lets the transient through first). Don't quantize it — in-grid it sounds soft — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8) & [Waterfall](https://youtu.be/sL5CG444qwQ).

### 2. [[Kramer Tape]] — Tape stage
- 🎬 *From video:* on a heavy 4-on-the-floor drum bus, tape saturation is what "squashes the hi-hats in a lovely way" every time the kick hits — a GP9-style tape setting with lows rolled off, driven hard for squelch on the hats/percussion specifically, backed off if it starts losing overall dynamic range — Disclosure, [First Twitch Stream Ever (Production Tutorial)](https://youtu.be/IObzp3OugtY)
- Settings: **15 ips** (④) · **Flux driven hard** (① — the squelch) · **HP filter rolling off lows** (⑧ — GP9-style, keep the drive on mids/hats) · **Playback to unity** (③). **Flux is the real drive knob** — more flux = more tape compression/saturation at the same output. Or unlink record/playback and push record level (turn playback down to match).
- Speed: **15 ips** = tighter lows + lifted highs (default) · 7.5 ips = fatter lows, duller top (lo-fi/vintage drums) · bias Over = a little extra top
- A touch of wow/flutter + noise only if you *want* audible vintage; zero for clean glue
- **Why:** Tape after compression is the analog-record order (console comp → tape machine). Soft-clips the drum transients the API let through — free peak control that sounds like glue, not limiting.

### 3. [[Oxford Inflator]] — Density
- Effect 20–35%
- **Why:** Raises perceived loudness between the peaks — drums feel bigger at the same meter reading.
- 🎬 *From video:* Disclosure lean on Inflator (or the Oxford Limiter) here for a **forward push** even when it's barely engaging — the slight distortion is the point; the bus ends up reading *louder and fuller while the meter reads lower* — Disclosure, [Waterfall](https://youtu.be/sL5CG444qwQ) & [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8).

### 4. [[Saturn 2]] — Final color
- Gentle, tape/tube, single band or subtle multiband
- **Why:** Last coat of harmonic paint on the finished dynamics — by this point it's seasoning, not cooking.
- 🎬 *From video:* think of stages 2–4 as **stacked gentle saturation across the whole bus** (tape → density → tube), each adding ~10% harmonics — the sum tames the transients that would push into the red, so the drums sound bigger at a *lower* meter. A touch of de-esser on the bus also tames harsh hi-hats — Disclosure, [Waterfall](https://youtu.be/sL5CG444qwQ).

### 5. [[Ozone 9 Maximizer]] — Peak catch
- IRC IV · threshold for **1–2 dB GR on hits only** · ceiling −0.3 dB relative
- **Why:** A safety net so drum peaks don't eat the master's headroom — NOT a loudness stage; if it's working hard, back off upstream.

## Meters & gain staging
Your export shows the full meter sandwich (Utility + Swiss Army + mvMeter at both ends) — drums are *the* place for the "average around 0 VU" rule since peaks fly far above average.

## Variations
- **Trap knock:** API attack faster (10–15 ms), [[Gold Clip]] instead of Maximizer (Threshold to shave ~3–4 dB of transient, Softness low/hard, Oversampling on, Mix 100%) — clipped transients are the modern knock sound.
- **Big live rock:** more Kramer drive, API release slower, blend [[Parallel Comp Send]] up underneath.

General tips pulled from production breakdowns (via `/music-enrich`), newest-first.

- **"My Hi" drum bus chain, in order:** [[API-2500]] (hard transient slam, tamed via Mix knob) → tape saturation (GP9-style setting, lows cut, driven for hi-hat squelch) → [[Oxford Inflator]] (touching red, clip button toggled off/on for extra flavor) → subtle master-style EQ cut (a few unwanted frequencies removed) → a limiter for a few dB of natural-sounding overdrive, leaving headroom — the throughline is **stacked saturation across multiple stages** rather than one heavy hit anywhere — Disclosure, [First Twitch Stream Ever (Production Tutorial)](https://youtu.be/IObzp3OugtY)
- **A/B every saturation stage at once, not one at a time** — Disclosure's live sound engineer builds confidence in a "does-nothing-alone" plugin like [[Oxford Inflator]] by bypassing every instance across the whole session simultaneously and toggling back in — the cumulative difference (drums + bass + vocals + master all inflating together) is obvious even when any single instance in isolation seems to do nothing — Disclosure, [First Twitch Stream Ever (Production Tutorial)](https://youtu.be/IObzp3OugtY)
- **Send the whole kit through reverb** (an "H-Verb across the drum bus" signature) for space/glue — feels dead without it; pair with EQ over [[Soothe2]] here because EQ is more transparent and lets you shape transients — Disclosure, [Deeper (with Leon Thomas)](https://youtu.be/SM0FZ58ckVw)
- **Stack gentle saturation across the whole bus** (tape + tube + a third), each ~10% harmonics — reads louder/fuller while the meter reads *lower* (6.9 vs 8); tame the transients that would sum into the red — Disclosure, [Waterfall](https://youtu.be/sL5CG444qwQ)
- **[[Oxford Inflator]]/Oxford Limiter on the drum bus** for a forward-push distortion, barely engaging (safety) — Disclosure, [Waterfall](https://youtu.be/sL5CG444qwQ) & [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8)

# PadShop

**Category:** Granular / Wavetable Synth
**Vendor:** Steinberg

## What it is

Steinberg's granular synthesizer. Loads any sample and plays it back as clouds of grains — you control grain length, count, position, and randomization to turn a sound into an evolving, playable instrument. **Runs as a VST outside Cubase** (confirmed available in Ableton), which matters because it's the engine behind several of Ian Kirkpatrick's signature moves.

## What it's used for

The go-to for **hiding synths in vocals** and for granular ear-candy — turning a spoken word, an acapella, or a percussion hit into a pad, a crash, or a subliminal texture that fuses with the vocal.

## Techniques

- **Hide a synth behind a vocal word — make the patch *from* the word** — drag the sung word ("move," "me") into PadShop and build a pad from it; the pad becomes the vowel ("ooh"/"ee"), so playing the note under the word accentuates it without sounding like a separate synth. Record PadShop's live output (don't print a patch) so every key gives different random grains — "10 minutes of noodling → a quarter-second I use." → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Hiding Synths pt 2](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/U6R0cfwdwqU) / [Charli Vocal FX](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ByPr9CMVnIg)
- **The "explosion" crash** — drop an acapella in, grab a ~32 ms grain; **0% random** loops that spot; **duration** stacks 2/3/4 loops; turn **random up** and after each loop it jumps to a new position, fanning into a stereo cloud "explosion of all the vocals." A cymbal/impact replacement. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Stereo Field]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Ear Candy Adventures](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZLgI56ZONJo)
- **Playable instrument from percussion** — load percussive/drum samples; randomized grain + spread + a slight glide makes each key an interesting new sound. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Produce a Track pt2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AjcENoa_TBk)
- **Rhythmic tempo-agitator** — a PadShop layer fed by a vocal adds rhythm/motion to a slow half-time track. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Move Me Synths](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RXm50CdQ9-o)

## Key Parameters

| Parameter | What it does | Artist / record example |
|---|---|---|
| Sample / grain source | The audio the grains are drawn from | A vocal word ("move"/"me") — Ian Kirkpatrick, Charli "Move Me" |
| Grain duration | Length of each grain (e.g. 8–100 ms) | ~32 ms grains for the "explosion" crash |
| Number / density | How many grains play at once | 5 grains stacked; 2/3/4 loop repeats |
| Random (position/pitch/air) | Scatters grains for stereo clouds | Randomize position → the shimmering "explosion" |
| Filter / env | Standard subtractive shaping | — |

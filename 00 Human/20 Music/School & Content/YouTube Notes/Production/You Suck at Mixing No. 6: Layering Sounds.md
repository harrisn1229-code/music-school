# You Suck at Mixing No. 6: Layering Sounds

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgXB2t0tNHU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DgXB2t0tNHU)

## Synopsis
A compact demonstration of layering discipline across three different sources — a stacked synth, a two-layer kick, and a two-layer bass — all built on the same core idea: layers need to be genuinely distinct from each other before they're worth stacking, and once they are, one layer should be picked to "own" the low end while the rest get carved out of it.

## Techniques
- **A duplicate layer with no distinction is dead weight — differentiate it first** — when a new layer sounds functionally identical to an existing one, the fix isn't EQ, it's making the layer actually different: an octave shift, a different oscillator/waveform character (brighter or duller). Only once each layer plays a genuinely distinct role is it worth keeping. → [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Pick which layer owns the low end, then carve everyone else out of it** — on a 3-layer synth stack (a soft round foundation layer, a brighter layer, and a distinct third layer), the foundation layer was chosen to own the low end, so both of the other layers got EQ'd to cut their low end out of the way; the brighter layer also lost some of its own top end so it wouldn't step on the third layer's space. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[EQ]]
- **The same ownership rule applies to two layers of the SAME instrument, not just different instruments** — with two kick layers (a clean kick and a punchier second kick), both carried real low end. Reading where each one's low end actually protrudes (around 55 Hz for the dominant layer here), the one chosen to own the low end stayed untouched while the other layer's low end got cut out. → [[Kick Design]]
- **A sub-bass layer + an upper/harmony bass layer needs the same low-end triage** — the upper bass layer (which had a synth-lead character to it) carried noticeably more low end than it should relative to the dedicated sub layer sitting underneath it; carving that excess out of the upper layer cleaned the pair up without touching the sub layer itself. → [[Bass Design]]

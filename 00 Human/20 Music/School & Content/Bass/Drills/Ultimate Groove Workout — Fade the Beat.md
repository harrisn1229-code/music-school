# Ultimate Groove Workout — Fade the Beat

A timekeeping-verification drill BassBuzz credits to **Victor Wooten**: the only real way to find out whether you can actually hold a groove on your own, rather than merely riding a drum track's timing without noticing.

## The drill

1. Play a bassline (a groove, a riff, a walking line — anything with a steady pulse) along with a drum beat or drum track, locked in as usual.
2. **Gradually fade the drum track out**, replacing it with silence, while continuing to play the exact same line at the exact same tempo entirely from internal timekeeping.
3. The test is whether the groove holds — tempo doesn't drift, the pocket doesn't wander — once the external reference is gone.

## Why it works

Most practice happens *with* a drum track or metronome running the whole time, which never actually proves independent timekeeping — a player can be quietly following the click rather than generating the pulse themselves, and never find out until it matters (a gig with a drummer who pushes/pulls, a click-free section, an in-ear failure). BassBuzz's own framing: **"if you don't practice something like this, you'll never be sure if you can actually keep time without a metronome."** Removing the reference mid-exercise, rather than just practicing unaccompanied from the start, is the point — it isolates the specific skill of *holding* a groove once support disappears, not just starting one.

## Applying it

- Start with a simple, well-known line (a groove already comfortable at full tempo with the drum track) so the drill tests timekeeping specifically, not the line itself.
- Fade the drums out gradually rather than cutting them abruptly — the gradual version tests the transition itself (do you drift the moment the anchor weakens, or only once it's fully gone), which is a more realistic analog to a live pocket loosening under a drummer's own timing than an instant on/off cut.
- Bring the drum track back in after a stretch of silence to check whether you've drifted — this is the actual diagnostic moment: landing back in sync (or close to it) is the pass condition; audible drift is the thing to work down over repeated reps.
- Works as a standalone drill (loop a beat, fade it manually) or informally any time a drum track/metronome is already running in a practice session — periodically drop it out and check back in.

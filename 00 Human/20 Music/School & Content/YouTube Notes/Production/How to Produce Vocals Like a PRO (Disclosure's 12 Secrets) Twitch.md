# How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets) [Twitch]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)

## Synopsis

A Sol State highlight-reel compilation pulling vocal-chain clips from at least two different Disclosure Twitch sessions: a screen-share walkthrough of the full lead-vocal chain on "Lavender" (feat. Channel Tres) — compression into deliberate clipping into Decapitator into EQ into de-essing into a harshness-taming plugin into bit-crushing, then a bus chain of saturation, EQ, and heavy sidechain — plus a shorter pass over Khalid's "Talk" vocal (gem dopamine brightness, 4ths/5ths harmonies). Two segments overlap with fuller existing breakdowns already in the vault (the whisper-track technique and the Khalid "Talk" chain), but the Lavender/Channel Tres chain and the room-distance recording technique are new, previously undocumented material. Note: this video's title claims "12 Secrets" but the transcript doesn't number them — treat the count as marketing, not a literal list length.

## Techniques

- **Deliberately clip the vocal into the red after heavy compression, "in a nice way"** — after compressing the lead hard enough to sound "poppy and stabby," the signal is pushed further until it's visibly hitting the red and distorting, treated as a tone choice rather than an error to gain-stage away. → [[Compression]], [[Clipping and Loudness]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Decapitator driven hard but only at half the drive control** on the same lead vocal, stacked after the clipping stage — the "half" ceiling is called out explicitly, implying more headroom was deliberately left on the table for the bus stage later in the chain. → [[Decapitator]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Cut a lot of low end because the vocalist "has the lowest voice in the world"** — an EQ move sized to the specific singer's register (Channel Tres), not a generic vocal-EQ default; paired later with recording him on an SM7 specifically because his voice doesn't need the lows captured in the first place. → [[EQ]], [[Vocal Mic Technique]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Light de-essing (-4 to -6 dB) followed by a second, parallel harshness-taming plugin blended at 80% wet/dry** — after a modest de-esser, a second plugin (heard as "SUV" in captions, almost certainly a mis-transcription of Soothe) runs in parallel at 80% mix, doing double duty: extra de-essing plus general harshness reduction, demonstrated dry-vs-wet on the same phrase. → [[De-essing]], [[Soothe2]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Bit-crush the lead vocal as the last stage of the individual chain, purely for a lo-fi top-end taste** — described as "giving it a slightly lo-fi" character by taking off a bit of top, not a rhythmic or glitch effect — the last processing step before the signal joins the vocal bus. → [[kHs Bitcrush]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Bus-stage chain once vocal layers stack: saturation → EQ boosting high-mids → EQ controlling low-mids/lows again** — a single vocal can carry more low end than a full stack; as layers pile up the same low-mid/low content that was fine solo turns "boomy," so it gets cut a second time at the bus, after already being cut once per-track. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Push the low-end cut deliberately far to land on a "low-fi radio speaker" aesthetic** — not just cleanup: the goal state is described as sounding like a small speaker, matched to why the vocal was tracked on an SM7 in the first place (a deep voice doesn't need its lows captured, so committing to that at the mic saves fighting it later in the mix). → [[Vocal Mic Technique]], [[EQ]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Run two different delay types on the same vocal simultaneously — one rhythmic, one a quick slap/echo** — layered rather than choosing one; the rhythmic delay and the fast slap-echo do different jobs at once on the same line. → [[Delay]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Use a very short, quiet reverb (Logic's Space Designer) just to place the voice in a space, not to be heard as reverb** — explicitly framed as "not really giving it a reverb sound," just a minor placement move; on the reverb Q&A later, the same small-room presets (roughly 0.3–0.5 sec) are recommended generally for putting a vocal "in a box" like a small hallway, specifically because Space Designer's short settings hold up even though its longer tails aren't considered high quality — it was the only reverb used across the whole "Settle" album because it came free with Logic. → [[Reverb]], [[Depth and Space]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Light sidechain compression from the kick on the lead vocal (~30% wet/dry) even before the wider stacking/glue use later in the same session** — a small, subtle amount applied early on the individual vocal chain, separate from the heavier "glue" sidechain described below. → [[Sidechaining]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Rotary speaker (Roto) + chorus on ad-lib layers, recorded as fast, in-the-moment passes** — the ad-libs (including an in-joke French line) are described as mostly improvised on the spot in a handful of takes, then given roto + chorus rather than the main chain, to sit distinctly behind the lead. → [[Modulation]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Four takes per harmony part, mixed hard-pan and center-pan pairs to widen the stereo image** — the default doubling approach is one take slightly left/slightly right plus one take hard-left/hard-right; on this section they lean toward more hard-panned pairs than centered pairs, explicitly as a stereo-width move rather than a pitch/tone one. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Width and Stereo]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Heavy vocal-bus sidechain from the kick is described as "glue"** — demonstrated with a bypass A/B, the pumping is framed as making the kick feel "in control of the mix" rather than as an obvious pumping effect; called out as a defining habit of their vocal mixing at this career stage. → [[Sidechaining]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Track the vocal at a physical distance from the mic on purpose, to capture the room's own reverb and spill as texture** — mic set up at one end of the room, singer at the other, gain pushed up to catch spill and hiss rather than a close, dry signal; framed as a technique Jimmy Napes also uses on Sam Smith (moving around the room while tracking BVs to capture different room textures — cited as the reason a Sam Smith chorus, e.g. "Stay With Me," reads as a "choir"), and tied to the broader habit of some artists deliberately tracking vocals in a bathroom for its natural reverb. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Depth and Space]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Whisper-track double under a lead vocal, even recorded with major-name singers, for extra "air"** — same technique already documented in depth from the "Where Angels Fear To Tread" breakdown (whisper the lyrics along with the take, high-passed to just the air band); this clip adds the specific anecdote of asking artists like Mary J. Blige to record a whisper pass despite her being a "pop star," muting everything else so only her breath layer plays. → [[Disclosure - Listening Sesh + 808 Jam + Where Angels Fear To Tread Breakdown|Where Angels Fear To Tread Breakdown]] (fuller technical chain already documented there) — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **Gem Dopamine for treble/air on Khalid's "Talk" vocal, plus a 4ths/5ths harmony voicing** — same technique already documented in full in the dedicated "'Talk' with Khalid" breakdown (Oxford Inflator → EQ → Gem Dopamine → Vocal Rider chain, fourths/fifths described there as "medieval" and Khalid's signature interval choice); this clip is the shorter version of the same demo. → [[Disclosure - 'Talk' with Khalid Twitch Breakdown|'Talk' with Khalid Twitch Breakdown]] (fuller technical chain already documented there) — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)
- **In passing: wishing Gem Dopamine had existed yet for an earlier vocal** — a brief aside (song referred to only as sounding like "Quad's vocal" in the transcript, unclear which session) about not having had the plugin available at the time; too vague to route as its own technique, included for completeness. — Disclosure (Sol State), [How to Produce Vocals Like a PRO (Disclosure's 12 Secrets)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IwKZcTI3428)

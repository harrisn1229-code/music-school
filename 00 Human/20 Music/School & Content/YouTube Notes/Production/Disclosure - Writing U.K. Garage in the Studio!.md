# Disclosure - Writing U.K. Garage in the Studio!

Short (6 min) fly-on-the-wall clip of Guy and Howard flipping a soul/EP sample into a UK-garage bootleg live in the studio. Not a deep technical teardown — its value is the **genre-aesthetic philosophy**: UK garage *wants* imperfection. Deliberately-too-loud "bad" hats, slightly out-of-tune vocals, and a bedroom-grade MPC saturation across the whole thing are the point, not mistakes to fix. The one concrete sound choice is the electric-piano source (Keyscape's Greg Phillinganes EP). Captions transcript — low technical density, but the mindset tips are clean.

## Techniques
- **UK-garage aesthetic = embrace lo-fi imperfection, don't polish it out.** The upfront hi-hats are mixed "bad" — too loud, not "good"-sounding — "but in a very old-school garage kind of way… they would be that loud in the original tune if it was made ages ago." Same call on the vocals: left slightly **out of tune** because it reads as *garagey*, not wrong. The rule: when a flaw serves the genre's era/vibe, leave it. → [[House - EDM]] / [[EDM Subgenres]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].
- **Whole-track MPC/Akai bus saturation for "bedroom producer" character** — run the finished loop through an **Akai/MPC-style saturator** ([[Decimort 2]]-type sampler grunge) "so it sounds like it's being made through some crappy MPC in someone's bedroom." Lo-fi glue that unifies a chopped-sample flip. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[TEXTURE Slot]].
- **Electric-piano source: [[Keyscape]] "Greg Phillinganes" EP** — the go-to Rhodes/electric-piano hits for the garage bootleg's chords. → [[BED Slot]] / [[MIDS Slot]].
- **Flip a well-known soul/vocal sample into a garage bootleg** — chop a recognizable EP + vocal (millions of views), keep it "cheesy but hard enough to not be cheesy," aim for a club-tune bridge between eras ("could drop it in Wetherspoons and it'll go, like Sweet Like Chocolate"). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].

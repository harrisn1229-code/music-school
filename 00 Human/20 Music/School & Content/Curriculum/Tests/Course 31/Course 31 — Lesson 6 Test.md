> **Q1.** **The mode's own character · quartal voicings · pedal points and drones · rhythm, dynamics and texture**
>
> **Q2.** **Modal jazz holds one mode for many bars; interchange borrows a chord from a parallel mode and returns**
> One is a static approach to form, the other a chromatic spice inside functional harmony.
>
> **Q3.** **16 bars of D Dorian, 8 of E♭ Dorian, 8 more of D Dorian**
> *Also accepted:* AABA with the B section a semitone up
>
> **Q4.** **A loop is harmony that is not going anywhere — the same situation modal jazz was written for**
> *Rubric (5):* says a loop or vamp is static harmony, the same situation as So What (2) · says the interest has to come from melody, voicing, texture or rhythm instead (2) · names the arrangement consequence — one new element reads as a whole new section (1)
> A four-bar loop on Fmaj7, or a two-chord vamp, is not a simplified progression. It is a deliberate refusal to move, which is exactly the condition modal jazz set itself, and everything it developed is a worked answer to the resulting problem: commit to a characteristic note, voice in fourths so the chord does not get tiring, hold a pedal, and let rhythm and density mark the form. The arrangement consequence is the most immediately usable part — with no chord change available to signal a section, one new element does the job on its own.
>
> **Q5.** **Dorian natural 6 · Lydian ♯4 · Mixolydian ♭7 · Phrygian ♭2**
>
> **Q6.** **The music collapses into plain major or minor**
> A loop that never plays the ♯4 is not Lydian, it is major with a Lydian label.
>
> **Q7.** **B**
> Hit B and it is Dorian; dodge B and it is D minor.
>
> **Q8.** **E A D G B — three perfect fourths with a major third on top**
>
> **Q9.** **A stack of thirds names a chord and so implies a function — fourths sound open and unassigned**
> Function is exactly what this music is avoiding, which is where the hollow, floating sound comes from.
>
> **Q10.** **The shell drops the 5th and keeps the 3rd and 7th; the quartal voicing avoids the 3rd altogether**
> *Rubric (5):* says the shell keeps the 3rd and 7th and drops the 5th and doubled root (1) · says the quartal voicing avoids the 3rd, so it states no quality (2) · says the choice follows whether you want the chord's function stated or deliberately withheld (2)
> Both are sparse, and both free the register above the voicing, but they withhold different things. The shell drops the notes that carry no information and keeps the two that do, so the harmony is fully stated in three notes — right when the progression is moving and the listener needs to hear it move. The quartal voicing withholds the 3rd, so nothing commits to major or minor and no function is implied — right when the harmony is deliberately holding still and a stated chord would get tiring over sixteen bars. Moving progression, shell; static vamp, fourths.

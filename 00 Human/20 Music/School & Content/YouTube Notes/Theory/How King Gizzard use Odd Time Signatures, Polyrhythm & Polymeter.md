# How King Gizzard use Odd Time Signatures, Polyrhythm & Polymeter

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tbHuhxCXFl8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tbHuhxCXFl8)

## Synopsis

A track-by-track rhythmic analysis of King Gizzard and the Lizard Wizard's 2017 album *Polygondwanaland* — every track uses an odd or unusual time signature, and several layer polyrhythms or polymeters on top, making the album a dense worked catalog of the difference between the two techniques.

## Techniques

- **Polymeter vs. polyrhythm, stated precisely (the two are often conflated).** Polyrhythm: two different subdivisions of the SAME bar at the SAME tempo (e.g. 4 beats and 3 beats fit into one bar simultaneously, effectively 4/4 and 3/4 "happening at once" but synced every bar). Polymeter: two different time signatures layered at the SAME underlying tempo but NOT resynced every bar, so the two parts drift out of phase with each other until they eventually realign after a full cycle — described in the video as a 7-against-4 polymeter (a fast 7/8 pulse against a 4/4 drum layer), the drift itself becoming the source of interest as the two parts interact differently on each pass.
- **Metric modulation via mid-song time-signature changes**: "Crumbling Castle" opens in 7/8, shifts to 4/4, then to 5/8 — layered rather than simple: mid-song a 6/8-feeling part briefly cuts to 4/4 and the tempo reads as slowed even though the actual BPM never changes, because the felt "main pulse" subdivision changed (dotted-8th pulse in 6/8 vs. quarter-note pulse in 4/4).
- **Using stem separation (Moises) as an analysis tool**: isolating individual instrument stems from a mix makes it far easier to identify which instrument is carrying which layer of a polyrhythm/polymeter, and to slow playback for transcription without losing clarity on a specific fast part.
- **5/8 grouped further into 5-bar phrases**: one track doesn't just sit in 5/8, it groups those 5/8 bars into larger 5-bar phrase units — a second layer of "5" nested inside the first.
- **Polymeter used as a structural pivot, not just a texture**: one track begins with a 5/8 synth ostinato, then the drums layer an 11/8 pattern OVER it (an 11-against-5 polymeter) — eventually the 11/8 layer takes over as the dominant felt meter entirely, with the original 5/8 fading out, so the polymeter functions as a gradual meter-change device rather than a fixed simultaneous layering.
- **Ending on an odd-meter release valve**: after establishing dense mixed-meter material across the album, the closing sections deliberately introduce a comparatively simple fast 7/8 as a unifying transition device between tracks.

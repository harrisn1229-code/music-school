# Beatles songs to help you recognise chords by ear

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OZwZPgXQx2g)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OZwZPgXQx2g)

## Synopsis

A chord-by-scale-degree ear-training reference: for each diatonic chord (I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi in major; i, VI in minor; plus a few chromatic/borrowed chords and chord-type examples), one Beatles song is given as the go-to sound-memory anchor, with the chord's characteristic function described in plain listening terms.

## What's taught

- **I (tonic)** — complete rest/resolution, "nowhere else to go." *Let It Be* ends squarely on I.
- **V (dominant)** — pulls hard back to I, especially as a dominant 7th. *Yellow Submarine* and *All Together Now* both lean on a I→V(7) move with that pull audible.
- **IV** — a "diluted" version of V's pull toward home, less urgent. *Love Me Do*'s verse is built on I→IV.
- **ii (major key)** — minor by default (diatonic harmonization of the 2nd degree). *Don't Let Me Down*: I (E) → ii (F♯m).
- **iv (borrowed, minor four in a major key)** — swapping the diatonic major IV for its minor version. *In My Life*: IV (D major) → iv (D minor) → I, described as "the sun going behind a cloud."
- **vi** — an alternative resting point to I; still resolved-feeling but with a darker, minor edge. *Happiness Is a Warm Gun*'s ending uses the "doo-wop/50s changes" progression (I–vi–IV–V), landing on vi (Am) as part of that classic loop.
- **iii** — a "halfway house" between tension and resolution, neither fully at rest nor tense. *Help!*: iii = C♯m in the key of A.
- **♭VI (borrowed flat-six)** — major chord built on the lowered 6th degree. *It Won't Be Long*'s verse: I (E) → ♭VI (C major) → I.
- **♭VII (borrowed flat-seven)** — chromatic, outside the key. *Hey Jude*: I (F) → ♭VII (E♭).
- **♭III (borrowed flat-three)** — *Magical Mystery Tour*'s middle section: I (E) → ♭III (G major) → IV (A).
- **II (major two, borrowed)** — swapping ii's normally-minor quality for major. *Eight Days a Week*, in D: normally ii = Em, here it's E major instead, described as adding "undeniable sunshine."
- **Minor-key i** — same fully-resolved quality as major I, but with a darker/more solemn color. *While My Guitar Gently Weeps* opens on a solid A minor tonic.
- **Minor-key VI** — momentary brightness (a major chord) inside a minor key, wants to resolve home but isn't in a hurry. *Eleanor Rigby*: "All the lonely..." lands on VI.
- **Chord-type (not scale-degree) examples**: augmented triad — the opening chord of *Oh! Darling* (an E augmented triad, built on the 5th degree, resolving to I) demonstrates the augmented sound in isolation; **6th chord** — the opening of *Fool on the Hill* (voiced as Bm7/D, respelled as a D6 chord); **sus4** — the ending of *For No One* (Gsus4 → G major), a clean example of suspension-then-release.

# Film & TV themes to identify melodic intervals

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JftZNTUJTJc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JftZNTUJTJc)

## Synopsis

A third interval song-anchor set (see also [[Songs that will help you identify ascending intervals]] and [[Songs that will help you identify descending intervals]]), this time drawn entirely from film and TV themes — chosen because they tend to be widely recognized even by non-fans, and because instrumental themes isolate the interval more cleanly than song lyrics/vocal melody often do. Multiple examples given per interval throughout. Closes with a live 5-interval quiz using the Auralia app.

## Techniques

- **Minor 2nd** — *Jaws* theme (E→F); *Jurassic Park* theme (B♭↔A, pivoting).
- **Major 2nd** — "Eye of the Tiger"/*Rocky* theme (C↔B♭, both directions).
- **Minor 3rd** — *Beverly Hills Cop* ("Axel F") theme (F↔A♭, both directions).
- **Major 3rd** — "Do-Re-Mi" (*The Sound of Music*) — notably a song that is itself about ear training/interval recognition.
- **Perfect 4th** — Hedwig's Theme (*Harry Potter*), B→E; *The Good, the Bad and the Ugly* theme (A↔D).
- **Tritone** — *The Simpsons* theme (C→F♯); "Maria" (*West Side Story*, D♭→G, resolving up one more note afterward).
- **Perfect 5th (the most heavily-represented interval in the video — described as "the perfect interval" for movie themes, bold/resolute/non-tense)** — Star Wars main theme (B♭→F); *E.T.* theme (C→G, repeated a tone up as D→A); Superman theme (G→C, and again C→G in the main melody); *Top Gun* theme (C→G, same composer as *E.T.*, Harold Faltermeyer — auto-caption garbled this to "Formier"/"a web"); *Game of Thrones* theme (G→C); one further unnamed fifth (F→B♭).
- **Minor 6th** — "The Entertainer" (Scott Joplin, famously used in *The Sting*), E↔C.
- **Major 6th** — Leia's Theme (Star Wars), A→F♯.
- **Minor 7th** — Star Trek theme ("Where No Man Has Gone Before"), F→E♭; "Somewhere" (*West Side Story*), B♭→A♭.
- **Major 7th** — flagged as rare to find a clean isolated example (a wide, "unwieldy" interval to sing). "Pure Imagination" (*Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory*), D♭→C.
- **Octave** — "Somewhere Over the Rainbow" (tonic→tonic); "Singin' in the Rain" (C→C, this time from the 5th degree of F major rather than the tonic).
- **Bonus — minor 9th** — one semitone wider than an octave, meaningfully less dissonant-sounding than the plain minor 2nd despite the same pitch classes (same point made in [[Songs that will help you identify ascending intervals]]'s "Killing in the Name" example). *Doctor Who* theme opening.

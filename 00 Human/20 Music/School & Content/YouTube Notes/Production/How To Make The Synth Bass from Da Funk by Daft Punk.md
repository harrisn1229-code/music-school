# How To Make The Synth Bass from "Da Funk" by Daft Punk

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OFUqAhJ0OxY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OFUqAhJ0OxY)

## Synopsis

An MS-20 recreation of the "Da Funk" lead-bass shred, run through a 1985 Boss Super OverDrive for grit — plus a genuinely useful arrangement-level insight: this "bass" is deliberately NOT low-end-heavy, because the song reserves a separate, actual low bass for a different section.

> [!note] Source conflict, left unresolved on purpose
> This channel's own "Top 20 Pop Synth Melodies" video states the same "Da Funk" sound is "a Roland TB-303 run through a Boss DS-1 distortion pedal," while THIS dedicated breakdown uses an MS-20 through a Boss Super OverDrive and says "I'm pretty sure the sound was made on the MS-20." Both are the same creator, on different videos, giving different answers — flagged honestly rather than picking one as correct. See [[Top 20 Pop Synth Melodies]] for the other claim.

## Techniques

- **A high-pass and low-pass filter run in SERIES (band-pass via two filters, not a dedicated band-pass mode) to carve out a "middle-only" shred with no real low end and limited highs — deliberately, because the arrangement doesn't need this sound to carry bass weight.** Low-pass closed, swept open by an envelope (medium decay, slight attack softening); high-pass then rolls off what little low end remains, landing "in the middle of the sound." Same series-filter concept already logged from other patches on this channel (see [[Filters and Resonance]]) — a second confirmed instance of deliberately running two filter types together rather than choosing one.
- **Two DIFFERENT bass sounds used in different sections of the same song, one for character/melody (this shred patch, thin on actual bass), one for low-end weight (a separate "pumping" bass elsewhere in the arrangement) — rather than one bass patch trying to do both jobs at once.** Explicitly framed as the payoff: a character-forward, hummable "bass" that isn't really bass-heavy can coexist with a genuinely low, weighty bass part, as long as they're not both trying to fill the same role at the same time in the arrangement.
- **A specific vintage overdrive pedal (Boss Super OverDrive, dated to September 1985) used for a deliberately GENTLE distortion — "not heavy distortion, but makes the sound shred"** — audible mainly at the tail of notes rather than as an all-over fuzz; a reminder that "add distortion" covers a wide range from barely-there edge to full fuzz, and this patch sits at the gentle end.
- **Restates the 4-parameter analysis framework (pitch↔oscillators, timbre↔filters, duration↔envelope generators, amplitude↔loudness) as the explicit lens used to identify the source synth from listening alone**, same method as [[How I break down ANY sound]] and [[Synth Patching and Editing Method]].

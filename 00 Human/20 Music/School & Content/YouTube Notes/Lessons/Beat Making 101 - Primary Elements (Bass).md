# Beat Making 101 - Primary Elements (Bass)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n_YVmu4bvgE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n_YVmu4bvgE)

## Synopsis

Beginner-level overview of what bass is (the low end of the frequency spectrum, and the low melodic line that reinforces the chords), part of the [[Beat Making 101 - Elements of a Beat|Beat Making 101]] series. Mostly definitional/ear-training content already covered at greater depth by [[Bassline Writing]] and [[Bass Design]], but it does carry one genuinely useful, specific rule worth cross-linking: never run two distinct basslines simultaneously.

## Techniques

- **Never run two DIFFERENT basslines simultaneously — they clash and muddy the mix.** Two instruments playing the *same* bassline together is fine (a doubling/unison move, same as chords or melody can be doubled), but two genuinely different low-end melodic lines competing at once creates confusion and mud rather than richness — unlike drums/percussion or chord layers, which tolerate multiple simultaneous parts more forgivingly. When writing a track, commit to one bassline. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Bass instrument options:** electric or acoustic bass guitar, double bass (orchestral/jazz upright), or a synthesizer (the most common source for programmed/electronic tracks) — a synth can generate convincing sub/bass tones easily and is the default go-to for in-the-box beat-making.
- **Small speakers can't reproduce true sub-bass — train your bass-hearing on real monitoring.** Phone/laptop speakers reproduce a bass instrument's higher harmonic content but not its actual low fundamental; developing the ability to consciously isolate and hear bass in a mix (deliberately tuning out the more attention-grabbing melody/lead on top) requires decent headphones or monitors, ideally with a subwoofer, and is a trainable listening skill worth practicing on reference tracks. → [[Referencing and Translation]]

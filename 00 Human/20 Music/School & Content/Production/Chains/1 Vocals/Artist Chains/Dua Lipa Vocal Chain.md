# Dua Lipa Vocal Chain

**Reference/vibe:** the disco-pop powerhouse — a naturally dark, low-mid-rich alto kept punchy and rhythmic over a busy dance floor. The documented chain (Ian Kirkpatrick, "Don't Start Now") is instructive: **many of her vocals are cut on a handheld Shure SM7B** — close cousin of your [[Shure SM57]] — with the character coming from smooth opto compression and *taming her prominent low mids* so the voice cuts without shouting.
**Signal source:** [[Shure SM57]] handheld (yes, really — the movement/energy in the performance beats mic technique here), or TLM for the balladry.

## Step 1 — Record (UAD Console)
1. **[[UAD Neve 1073]] (Unison)** — solid gain (a dynamic needs it), clean
2. **UAD Teletronix LA-2A** — 2–3 dB smooth (the LA-3A in Kirkpatrick's chain is this family — opto glue)
3. **UAD Pultec EQP-1A** — low ATTEN @ 60–100 Hz (handheld rumble), nothing else

## Chain overview
Sweep the subs → smooth opto → soothe the low mids → tune tight-ish → bright rhythmic finish. The dance-pop insight: the vocal is a *rhythm instrument* — consonants land on the grid, dynamics flattened enough to ride the groove.

## Step 2 — Process (Ableton)

### 1. [[Pro-Q 4]] — Clean sweep
- HPF ~30–40 Hz first (the documented first move — the comp shouldn't react to sub junk), then a real HPF ~90 · check 200–400 Hz but *don't* gut it yet (next stage handles it dynamically).

### 2. [[LALA]] — The smooth engine
- **3–5 dB GR** constant — the LA-3A-style always-on silk that keeps power without edge.

### 3. [[Soothe2]] — The documented trick
- **Pre-emphasis centered on the LOW MIDS (200–500 Hz)**, soft mode — her body/power lives there and overpowers the track; soothe ducks it only when it blooms. (Most vocal soothe targets 2–6 kHz — this is the opposite move, and it's the Dua-specific one.)

### 4. [[Auto-Tune Pro]] — Tight pop
- Retune 20–35, Humanize 50 — locked enough to be rhythmic, not robotic.

### 5. [[Pro-C 2]] — Rhythm flatten
- 4:1, 2–4 dB — dance vocals sit *still* on top of a pumping track.

### 6. [[Pro-Q 4]] — Finish
- Presence +1–2 @ 3–4 kHz · air +1–2 @ 12 kHz · the low mids are already handled — resist cutting them statically.

## Step 3 — Effects (sends)
- **Tight and rhythmic, not washy:** [[Delay Send Other]] 1/8 synced, ducked hard (the delay pumps with the track) · [[Reverb Send Room]] short/bright · plate bloom only on pre-chorus/breakdown moments.
- Stacks: chorus doubles hard L/R tuned tight, low octave tucked under hooks (the disco thickener), ad-lib answers drenched while the lead stays dry.
- Send the lead through the mix's **pump** lightly (a touch of [[Kickstart 2]] on the vocal bus in four-on-floor sections glues her to the groove — subtle).

## Vocal arrangement

| Section | Lead | Behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Verse | single, dry-ish, mono-ish, rhythmic | nothing |
| Pre-chorus | single, widening | one double, stack starting to open |
| Chorus | lead + doubles **hard-panned in fours** | harmonies inside the stack, ad-lib answers wide |
| Post-chorus | out — the instrumental hook takes over | — |

**The move — the stack widens into the chorus.** Verse mono, chorus in fours: that width jump *is* the lift, not a fader. Pair it with the filter opening on the track (see [[Dance Pop (Dua Lipa, Charli XCX)]] § Arrangement) and the chorus lands with no level change at all.

**Everything serves the groove grid.** Consonants land on the beat — the vocal is a rhythm instrument first, which is why the stacks can be this dense without turning to mush.

**Stack count: 1 → 4 + harmonies.**

## Notes / variations
- **Future Nostalgia lane:** brighter, more air, funk-guitar-like rhythmic phrasing.
- **Houdini/dance lane:** harder tune, more delay throws, vocal chops as instruments ([[House - EDM]] vocal rules).

## From videos — Ian Kirkpatrick streams

The producer's-eye view of her records (New Rules, Don't Start Now, Fever, Pretty Please). Signal front-end: **SM7 → BAE 1084 → (rarely a Distressor) → Burl B2 Bomber** converter/clock. He tracks *in the session* — often her **demo take is the record** (Pretty Please, Fever). Newest-first:

- **Half-speed vocal tracking** — keep a copy at half speed, have her sing to it, then speed up so vibratos come out fast and tight; also the "weird" double-time parts (sing the rhythm at half, Cubase speeds to double). → [[Pitch and Formant]] — [New Rules (25 Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iHSmH57_wY8) · [Fever](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0cG4_vxe1tY)
- **Auto-Tune on while tracking (speed), then Melodyne everything after** — light AT to keep it usable, real tuning in Melodyne (tune only what needs it, chop notes first, average-line, some notes slammed hard for effect). → [[Auto-Tune Pro]], [[Melodyne]] — [New Rules](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iHSmH57_wY8) · [SLDMML](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IRQC25nu_s8)
- **Lead chain (chopped-vocal version):** Clean-Sweep EQ (kill 22–25 Hz so the comp acts on audible signal) → **UAD 1176 all-buttons "smash"** → responsible OTT → **Pultec boost 10k *into* the de-esser** → de-ess. Personality = chop + smash. → [[UAD UA 1176LN]], [[OTT]], [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]], [[De-essing]] — [HIT Vocal Chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LMjkfMIhXdA)
- **Reverb-width automation 30%→100%** across the song; **print reverbs to envelope/cut them**; print-mono-compress the verb for dense ambience. → [[Reverb]], [[Width and Stereo]] — [Vocal Production for Dua Lipa](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0cG4_vxe1tY)
- **Background-vocal group:** ~60% wet, heavy GR (want them far), EQ boost 16k / dip 700 / atten 100, then Avox Sybil de-ess; de-ess BVs hard for center definition; **separate the breaths** to a high-passed track. → [[2 Backup Vocals Group]], [[De-essing]] — [New Rules](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iHSmH57_wY8) · [Fever](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0cG4_vxe1tY)
- **Hide a synth (PadShop from the vocal word) behind a standout lyric** instead of a reverb throw; chop tiny vocal fragments to a dedicated echo track. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[PadShop]] — [Hiding Synths](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/9Eza4jngazI)

## From videos — Josh Gudwin (mix engineer, "Electricity")

Mix engineer's-eye view (not the producer/writer above) of a real stem-handoff mix — Josh Gudwin working from a track a producer ("Mark") sent him already sounding good, at 118 BPM. Stated approach throughout: build off the existing mix rather than replace it, keep the producer's engineer's sends/plugins where they already work, and split every vocal onto its own track for individual (not blanket-bus) treatment. Newest-first:

- **Per-track vocal chain, in order: [[Pro-Q 4]] (filtering) → an SSL-style channel EQ (broad, unowned — [[Plugin Wishlist]]) → an SSL-style channel comp's "Z" default preset (very light, barely audible, no EQ — owned stand-in [[Glue Compressor]]) → [[Soothe2]] (Mix 38%, never run a resonance-suppressor plugin at 100% on vocals) → [[Pro-Q 4]] again (~2–2.5 dB broad) → narrow multiband problem-frequency cuts at 2.5–4.5 kHz → [[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]] (Peak Reduction 3–7, Gain to taste) → a subtractive-only multiband EQ + de-esser (McDSP C6, unowned — owned stand-in [[Pro-MB]]) for low/high shelving and problem-frequency notches.** Every vocal performance is on its own track and gets its own version of this chain, tailored — "some need a little bit more compression, some need less compression" — never copy-pasted wholesale. The C6-style stage is deliberately all-subtractive (no boosts); the only boost anywhere in the chain is the small SSL EQ move upstream. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Soothe2]], [[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]], [[Pro-MB]], [[Glue Compressor]], [[Compression]], [[Multiband Compression]], [[De-essing]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — Mix with the Masters, [[Mixing Dua Lipa's vocals with Josh Gudwin]]
- **A parallel "girth" aux for a fuller/louder vocal revision without raising the fader: duplicate the vocal's master aux, EQ it with a default filter pass (cut a little low and high), hit it with an 1176-style compressor ("smashing" but not fully), and blend the duplicate back in at roughly −33 dB.** "That's a quick little trick if you need to get a little change in your vocal and you can't figure out what you're doing, just duplicate your aux and put some different plug-ins on there. Filter out frequencies that you don't need, and accentuate those that you do or take out the ones that are bothering you, and hit it with some compression, and turn it up or down to taste." The 1176 stage is captioned as "CLA-76" — mapped to owned [[UAD UA 1176LN]]. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[UAD UA 1176LN]], [[Parallel Processing]] — Mix with the Masters, [[Mixing Dua Lipa's vocals with Josh Gudwin]]
- **A personal default OPTO compressor recipe for background/other vocal parts: mid Attack, mid Release, ~3.5 dB GR, make-up gain set to roughly three-quarters of the GR amount.** Read as Waves R-Comp (unowned — [[Plugin Wishlist]]); owned-lane stand-in [[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]]. → [[UAD Teletronix LA-2A]], [[Compression]] — Mix with the Masters, [[Mixing Dua Lipa's vocals with Josh Gudwin]]
- **Kick-triggered sidechain compression (a C2-style plugin) on a lead vocal track, kept from the producer's stem even though its origin was unclear** — "not sure if Mark had it... but it's on there now." A separate sidechain move on the a cappella hook section WAS the producer's own choice, kept untouched and called out as one of the mixer's favorite moments in the song. → [[Sidechaining]] — Mix with the Masters, [[Mixing Dua Lipa's vocals with Josh Gudwin]]
- **Kept the producer's original vocal reverb + delay sends rather than rebuilding them: [[UAD Pure Plate Reverb]] (demoed, then had to buy after mixing on it) plus a second unidentified reverb, and an [[H-Delay]] + [[ValhallaDelay]] pair with a ~1.5 kHz EQ cut on the harsh part of the return.** Also sends to a Bricasti hardware reverb (unowned — [[Plugin Wishlist]]) and a separate hall reverb. A three-layer "ear candy" delay-throw send (tucked quarter-note + audible filtered reverb + a "half-verb blast," then trimmed further after listening) is dialed for feel, not clarity. → [[UAD Pure Plate Reverb]], [[H-Delay]], [[ValhallaDelay]], [[Reverb]], [[Delay]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — Mix with the Masters, [[Mixing Dua Lipa's vocals with Josh Gudwin]]
- **Inherited plugins kept without knowing their origin, purely because the sound already worked — RVox on a "screwed hook" vocal, and a "Studio D" stage kept explicitly "not trying to take away the vibe, I'm trying to keep the vibe and build upon that."** The clearest single statement of this mixer's overall philosophy: build off a good producer mix rather than replace it. → [[RVox]], [[Mix Handoff]] — Mix with the Masters, [[Mixing Dua Lipa's vocals with Josh Gudwin]]

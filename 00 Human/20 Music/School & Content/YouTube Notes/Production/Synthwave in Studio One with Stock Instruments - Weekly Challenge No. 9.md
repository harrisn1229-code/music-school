# Synthwave in Studio One with Stock Instruments | Weekly Challenge No. 9

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IA61_2JAd84)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IA61_2JAd84)

## Synopsis

Weekly Challenge #9: a live, 2-hour synthwave build in **Studio One** (a different DAW than Nate's Ableton Live — techniques below are DAW-agnostic), self-described as Max's first synthwave attempt. He goes fast (140 BPM, outrun/vaporwave-adjacent), building drums → bass pluck → chords/passing-chord progression → toms → a phaser-heavy intro bass synth → a saw lead with LFO-modulated pitch and flanger, finishing with a B-section that duplicates and "beefs up" the bass and kick for an energy lift. It's a useful companion to the vault's already-detailed [[Synthwave]] playbook — this session adds concrete drum-envelope, distortion-cleanup, intro-transition, and melody-writing details the existing page didn't have, plus reinforces several pillars (quantized rigidity, chorus/distortion-forward synths, big reverb) from an independent source.

## Techniques

**Drums**
- **Use the envelope's Hold stage (~30–50 ms) so Decay only shapes the tail, not the initial body** — when designing a drum's volume envelope, add a short Hold time before Decay begins so the decay curve can't eat into the transient/body of the hit — Decay ends up shaping only what happens *after* the important initial punch. → [[Kick Design]], [[Snare Design]]
- **Sometimes skip the kick on every beat** — a deliberate genre variation named but not fully committed to in this session; synthwave doesn't always require a kick on every beat, worth trying as a texture/groove option.
- **Compress the drum elements together for glue**, then audition room reverb on the kit — tried and explicitly rejected in this pass ("not working like I'm imagining") — a useful documented negative result: room reverb on a synthwave kick doesn't automatically work, worth dialing carefully rather than assuming.
- **Beef up weak toms with distortion** — toms that read "hella weak" get fixed with a distortion stage rather than EQ/level alone, for a fatter, more aggressive tom hit ("Michael Jackson track" character namechecked).
- **Duplicate and "beef up" the bass and kick for a B-section energy lift** — reprising the same bass/kick parts with a heavier/bigger treatment (rather than new parts) is enough to read as a structural energy jump. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

**Synths — bass pluck & chords**
- **Mod wheel mapped to filter cutoff** on the bass pluck patch, for a performable brightness control.
- **"Red light" (Studio One's stock distortion, → Ableton [[Saturator]]/[[Overdrive]]) generates unwanted low-frequency content that needs to be EQ'd back out** — a specific, actionable caution: driving distortion on a bass/low element often adds new low-end junk below what was there before; follow with an EQ cut to remove it rather than assuming the distortion stage is "done." → [[EQ]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **A little sidechaining locks a bass pluck into the mix even when it "could be done" as-is** — noted as the one thing separating an already-good bass tone from a mix-ready one.
- **Global saturation on the entire track/bus ("red light on the entire track")** — pushing the stock distortion device across a full track/group, not just individual elements, for overall grit.
- **Develop a chord progression by playing through a basic patch first, without committing to that patch as the final sound** — use a simple placeholder patch purely to audition and refine the progression itself; the sound design comes after the harmony is settled.
- **Passing chords in a synthwave progression** — theory reference to a full passing-chords stream; applied here as connective chords between the main progression's changes for smoother chromatic motion. → [[Chord Progressions]]
- **A "four-sus" (IVm7sus, i.e. the tonic chord voiced over the IV bass note) as an alternate cadence chord** — tried as a substitute for a plain IV major at a progression's turnaround; described as "kind of synth-wavy" even though not fully committed to for this track — a concrete example of a sus-over-non-root voicing worth filing as a synthwave-flavored cadence option. → [[Suspended Chords]]
- **A dissonant minor-major 9 chord as a deliberately unstable ending chord** — closes the progression on a "super dissonant" altered color rather than resolving cleanly, for a lingering, uneasy finish.

**Intro & transitions**
- **A "massive phaser" bass synth built specifically as an intro texture**, distinct from the main bass patch — designed as its own intro-only sound with heavy phaser character. → [[Phaser-Flanger]]
- **Layer a rhythmic kick element under a long sustained intro note**, and swing the last beat of the phrase hard, for a driving pulse under an otherwise static pad/drone.
- **Reverse reverb + a big low-frequency cut ("remove a whole bunch of frequencies")** as an intro transition device — combining a reverse-reverb swell with aggressive low-end removal on the same element for a rising, thinning transition into the beat. → [[Reverb]]
- **Steal a drum fill from elsewhere in the arrangement to reuse in the intro** — rather than programming a new fill, copy an existing fill from later in the track for a transition hit, plus offbeat hi-hats for extra motion into the drop.
- **Filter sweep over the ENTIRE intro section**, not just one element — an arrangement-wide filtered-intro move (already documented generally on [[Arrangement and Song Form]]'s filtered-intros entry; this is a synthwave-specific confirmation). → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Auto Filter]]

**Lead & melody**
- **Saw-wave lead with a slow LFO-modulated pitch** — LFO1 routed to oscillator 1's pitch for a slowly wavering, "alive" lead tone, plus a flanger for extra movement and width. → [[Modulation]], [[Phaser-Flanger]]
- **A large reverb send is essential on the lead** — explicitly needed way more reverb than first applied; the lead reads thin/dry without a generous verb.
- **Write melody rhythm around what the ear already grabbed from another part** — on the first beat, a prominent rhythmic hit from another element ("bing bo") is already going to grab the ear, so the melody deliberately avoids placing a note exactly there — an "intersecting rhythms" approach: think about what's rhythmically already occupied before placing melody notes, not just which notes to play. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **Melody-writing checklist used live: rhythm placement relative to other parts, register/octave choice, chord-tone alignment with the underlying harmony, then instinct/feel for the rest** — a concrete, stated multi-factor process for writing a lead line over changes.
- **A big pitch bend on the lead, and harmonizing a phrase rather than leaving it single-line** — both treated as near-mandatory finishing touches ("would be criminal not to") once the core melody is in place.

## Sound/tone selection

- **Studio One stock "guy"/bass pluck patch** as the starting bass-pluck sound, refined via mod wheel filter mapping and distortion.
- **Basic placeholder chord patch** used only to develop the progression, swapped for a real patch afterward.
- **Massive-phaser intro bass** as a distinct, purpose-built intro-only texture separate from the main bassline.
- **Saw-wave lead + slow pitch LFO + flanger + big reverb** as the genre's lead-synth recipe from this session.

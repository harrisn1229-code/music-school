## Synopsis
An early, fundamentals-focused video on Ableton's warping engine — how to anchor Live's tempo detection to a song's actual first downbeat, verify the detected BPM is correct both by ear (metronome overlay) and by eye (beat markers lining up with transients throughout the whole song, not just the start), and how to pick a smoother warp algorithm when pushing the tempo far from the original. Not drum-specific, but a genuinely useful piece of core Ableton workflow that several of this batch's other techniques (foley warping, sample speed-matching) build on top of.

## Techniques
- **Set the start marker to the exact first downbeat/kick before detecting BPM** — find the first kick drum's transient in the waveform, move the play marker right up against it, and make sure the clip's start point lines up exactly with that hit.
- **"Warp From Here (Straight)" locks Ableton's BPM detection to that anchor point** — right-click (Ctrl-click on Mac) the play marker at the exact start of the first downbeat and choose Warp From Here (Straight) rather than letting Ableton guess the downbeat location on its own.
- **Verify the detected BPM two ways: by ear, then by eye** — turn on the metronome and listen for it to line up with the track's actual drum hits; separately, check visually that every numbered bar/beat marker running along the top of the waveform lines up with a transient bulge all the way through the song, not just at the very start (drift later in the song means the detection isn't actually locked in).
- **Switch the warping algorithm from Beats to Complex for extreme tempo changes** — the default Beats algorithm reads noticeably jittery/uneven when the tempo is pushed far from the original (very sped-up or very slowed-down); switching to the Complex algorithm produces a visibly and audibly smoother result at those extremes.

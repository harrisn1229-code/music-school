# how i made the sample that got me a platinum song!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6aHjhniTOnk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6aHjhniTOnk)

## Synopsis

loner rebuilds, step-by-step, a 2019 sample-pack loop that — unknown to him until he stumbled on his own name on Genius — was bought and flipped into "Trouble Now" by Prof Jam & Benji Price, a song that went platinum in Portugal; he only found out because a stranger's production carried 5,000 Genius views under his credit. The breakdown itself is a compact, complete signal chain rather than a single trick: a C♯ minor nylon-guitar chord progression (built with a deliberate semitone-clash tension note and a repeated shape re-voiced two octaves up) run through a vintage/lossy-codec coloration chain, doubled by an Arturia Analog Lab piano playing the identical chords through a near-identical chain, topped with a Little AlterBoy "Double Rainbow" vocal-chop pass (pitch up, formant down, filtered, heavy reverb, panning tremolo) reused a second time with different notes for a late switch-up, a synth bass ("Bass Bass," also from the Prophet model inside Analog Lab) that enters only in that switch-up section, and a barely-there band-pass-EQ'd/tremolo'd acoustic hi-hat/open-hat percussion layer for texture. The video is as much a lesson in **sound selection** (a deliberately "vintage Mexican/Latin" palette: nylon guitar, detuned piano, vocal, light percussion) and **arrangement** (hold the piano back for a mid-track lift, slow down at the end into vocal chops + bass) as it is a plugin walkthrough, and closes on the same message it opens with: put unreleased samples out into the world, because you never know who ends up using one.

## Techniques

**Chords / theory:**
- **A four-chord loop in C♯ minor: i (C♯m, with an added D♯ a semitone above the chord's E for deliberate dissonance/tension) → i again (identical C♯m shape, but re-voiced two full octaves higher for variety) → iv (F♯m) → V (G♯ major, voiced differently again, described as making the loop "a little bit more melodic").** Functionally T–T–SD–D in the natural/harmonic-minor sense (the V is a borrowed major triad resolving toward the tonic) — a second real-world confirmation of the "voice two notes a semitone apart for tension" trick and the "repeat an identical chord shape but re-voice it an octave up for variety" trick, both already logged on [[Chord Progressions]]/[[Chord Inversions]] from an earlier loner video. → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Chord Inversions]]
- **Extra connective notes added on top of the base progression PLUS deliberately uneven strum timing within a chord (e.g. the D♯ note hitting fractionally before the other two notes of the chord) is what reads as "realistic," not the notes/velocities alone.** Direct, concrete demonstration for [[Pocket and Microtiming]]'s "laying back"/humanization concept — stagger a chord's note-on times by a few ms instead of hitting all notes simultaneously. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Notes deliberately hit early/slightly off-grid on the piano layer, called out explicitly as "what shows that it's a really old sample."** A second, independent confirmation (alongside the guitar strum-timing trick above) that micro-timing looseness — not EQ or noise — is what reads as "vintage"/"sampled from a record" rather than programmed. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]]

**Guitar (source instrument):**
- **Nylon-string guitar VST as the harmonic foundation of a "super latin and guitar type" sample era (2019) — original source library discontinued/no longer installed ("Evolution Nylon Strings," auto-caption garbled), currently substituted with a different owned nylon-guitar instrument (name also garbled in auto-captions).** Sound-selection lesson: committing to a period-specific instrument character (nylon strings, not steel-string or electric) is what sets the whole track's vintage-Latin identity before any processing is applied. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Guitar FX chain: a reverb ("Vintage Verb," likely [[ValhallaVintageVerb]]) → a tape/vintage-coloration stage (auto-caption "Reels," "Master Vintage" preset — exact plugin unresolved, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) → [[Pro-C 2|Pro-C2]] (light touch, "just to tighten it up a bit") → a low-cut EQ ("loca eq," near-certain caption garble of "low-cut EQ") → [[Ghz Lossy 3|Goodhertz Lossy]], "Packet Loss" preset for the final vintage/lossy-codec degradation ("takes away the quality of it").** A five-stage color chain: reverb → tape/vintage color → glue compression → tonal cleanup → codec-degradation finish. → [[Reverb]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[EQ]], [[Ghz Lossy 3]], [[Pro-C 2]]

**Piano (doubling layer):**
- **Arturia Analog Lab 4, a piano preset, playing the EXACT same chord progression as the guitar — "pretty similar processing to the guitar": the same "Reels"/master-preset vintage-color stage → Pro-C2 → an EQ (cut low end, plus a notch on "a really harsh frequency") → a short vintage reverb.** Explicit self-observation that his processing chain "hasn't changed too much" over the years — same basic recipe, different specific plugins. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[EQ]], [[Pro-C 2]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**Vocal chops:**
- **Sourced from Output Arcade back in 2019 (unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]); today's preference has shifted to Splice or his own recorded vocals for the same role.** A dated-tool caveat worth remembering when reverse-engineering older breakdowns from this channel — the tool named on screen may not be the producer's current pick. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **[[Little AlterBoy]], "Double Rainbow" preset: pitch shifted all the way up, formant brought all the way down, filtered (cut some high end, cut some low end), a heavier reverb than the guitar/piano chains got, and a tremolo control panning the result hard side-to-side.** A fully-specified, six-stage vocal-chop recipe — pitch/formant extreme, filter, reverb, auto-pan tremolo. → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Reverb]], [[Modulation]]
- **Same vocal preset/chain REUSED for a late arrangement switch-up, just swapping which notes/melody it plays — not a new sound, a new part on the same processed voice.** Paired with removing some of the guitar's notes in that same section to make room. → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]

**Synth bass:**
- **"Bass Bass" preset, from the Prophet model inside Arturia Analog Lab, added only in the late switch-up section alongside the vocal-chop change** — the bass isn't present for the whole track, it's introduced as part of the same structural pivot as the vocal switch-up. → [[Bass Design]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**Percussion (texture layer):**
- **Two acoustic-sounding hi-hats + one open hat, processed with nothing but a band-pass EQ and a Goodhertz tremolo control — deliberately kept "very very subtle," described as adding "a little bit of texture underneath" rather than driving the groove.** A minimal, two-device percussion-texture recipe: band-pass to carve a narrow slice out of the way of everything else, tremolo for movement instead of a rhythmic pattern change. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[EQ]], [[Modulation]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]

**Arrangement / structure:**
- **Start with every element EXCEPT the piano, then bring the piano in partway through purely to create a lift/sense of movement, then slow the whole thing down toward the end by leaning on the vocal chops + synth bass as the closing texture.** A three-phase, subtraction-then-addition structure built from the same handful of layers rather than new content. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

**Master bus:**
- **A "Reels"/vintage-coloration stage again on the master (same "Master Vintage"-family preset as the guitar/piano chains), with the hiss level deliberately boosted "because I wanted that texture in the background," finished by pitching the WHOLE sample down three semitones with a pitch-shift tool ("Sound Shifter" — likely [[Plugin Wishlist|Waves SoundShifter]]).** Two separate master-bus moves: an intentional noise-floor boost for era-glue texture, and a final key-lowering pitch shift applied to the finished bounce. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**Sound selection (the video's stated core lesson):**
- **A deliberately "vintage Mexican/Latin" sonic palette assembled from ordinary role choices, not exotic sources: a nylon guitar, a detuned/vintage-toned piano, a vocal, and light percussion — "it all fits well together and creates that vibe."** The takeaway stated on screen: sound selection (which instrument/timbre you reach for) did more work here than any individual processing chain. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **The origin story itself is a reusable lesson: an unreleased/pack-sold sample from 2019 was found and flipped by a completely unrelated producer into a platinum-certified song (Portugal) years later, discovered by loner only by chance while checking his own Genius credits page.** Explicit takeaway — put samples out into the world (packs, free kits, community trades) because you never know who downloads and uses one; the value of a sample isn't capped by what you personally do with it.

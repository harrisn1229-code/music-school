# Eleanor Rigby — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

The whole song is one gesture — an **anxious sigh**, a sharp intake of breath let out in slow motion — and Pollack's point is that it operates at every level at once: foreground rhythm, harmonic rhythm, phrase lengths, melodic contour. The music is heavily syncopated but never bounces, because the syncopation enervates rather than propels. The string-quartet element and the bluesy, boxy pop element are not separable layers; they're fused past the point where you can pull them apart.

**Form.** Compact and cyclic. The intro is a **great non-I opening** — it starts on VI at what already sounds like peak tension, climbs a little further, then unwinds — and the bridge is simply that intro repeated. The verse is an odd **5 bars parsed 1 + 3 + 1**, with off-beat vocal phrasing set against a march-beat accompaniment.

**Harmony.** Extremely spare: essentially i, VI and iv, with a major IV implied as a passing chord over the drone — the Dorian brightening inside a minor key ([[POP-C10 — Dorian IV vs Aeolian iv]]). The home key is asserted plagally and modally rather than by any dominant cadence, and the tonic gets so much drone-like airtime that "progression" is the wrong word for most of it ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]). The refrain is structurally one sustained tonic chord with a **descending chromatic inner line** doing all the work; sing that inner voice along with the record and it's obvious. Pollack is firm about not over-labelling — you could name a seventh here and an inversion there, but those are linear motion against a background, not structural chords ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]).

**Melody.** Dorian, an uncommon mode for them. The sighing shows up as contour: voice and accompaniment peak and unwind **out of phase with each other**. The register plan is strict ([[MEL-C05 — Register and intensity]]) — the refrain's first phrase tops out lower than its second, and neither upstages the song's true ceiling, which sits in the intro.

**Arrangement.** Eight players but written in four real parts, each doubled. Short choppy down-bows near the frog, **no vibrato**, close-miked; the one softening move is retreating from stark quarter notes into eighths.

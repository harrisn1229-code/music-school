## Synopsis

### Sample stacking (the headline technique)

- **Definition, stated directly on camera: "you grab samples and you put them on top of your own samples."** Framed as a technique with no single fixed workflow — you can start from your own written sample and add outside samples as accents/melodies/textures on top (this video's approach), OR start from an outside sample (e.g. "a simple guitar chord progression") and build your own material on top of THAT instead — the direction is interchangeable. The stated reason to reach for it: for hard-to-replicate realistic instruments (violins, strings, brass, guitars), sitting for ages trying to compose a convincing melody and process a VST into a realistic tone is slower and often worse than sourcing a real recorded performance and shaping it around what you've already built. Direct thesis, stated as the video's closing line: "you don't have to do everything yourself... using tools around you is a great way to not only get better but to improve your workflow, work faster, work smarter." → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Soulful Sample Stacking Chain]]
- **Stack 1 — a perc loop, placed underneath the existing sample purely to "give it a little bit more."** No source/pack named. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Soulful Sample Stacking Chain]]
- **Stack 2 (first MELODIC stack) — a Splice string loop, chosen specifically to fake realistic violin/string/brass content instead of composing and processing it from a VST.** Workflow: search Splice → chop the wanted part → reverse it → confirm it's in-key → time-stretch to tempo. Chain: **Cassette, "Lo-Fi Drums" preset** (used here as a filtering/character stage on a melodic string layer, not on drums — a cross-role reuse) → **heavy reverb** (to "drown it out") → a **medium-sized delay** (source names FL Studio's "Fruity Delay" — owned-lane equivalent: [[Delay]]/[[Echo]]) → pushed to mono, for its own pocket in the mix. → [[Cassette]], [[Reverb]], [[Delay]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Soulful Sample Stacking Chain]]
- **Stack 3 (loner's stated favorite of the video) — a "wire guitar" Splice sample, searched by name, auditioned until a melody fit, then dropped in, pitched, and time-stretched to key/tempo.** Same hard-to-replicate-with-VSTs logic as the strings above. Chain: **reverb** (to drown it out a bit) → **EQ** (cutting some lows and specific mid-range frequencies described as "kind of a bit annoying"). → [[EQ]], [[Reverb]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Soulful Sample Stacking Chain]]
- **Stack 4 — a brass sample, added because the build "just needed one more thing," reversed (pitch kept intact, not reversed-and-repitched) for a different texture than a straight reverse.** Chain: **half-time** device → **vintage reverb** at a 100% wet mix ("really drowned out") → panned hard to one side (the far side isn't named in the transcript, cut off mid-sentence — "I've also panned it quite far to the—"). → [[Reverb]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Soulful Sample Stacking Chain]]

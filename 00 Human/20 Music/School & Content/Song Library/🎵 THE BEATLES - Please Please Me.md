# Please Please Me — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4 bars over a static I, entering on beat 4) · Verse (16, four even phrases grouped 2+2) · Verse · Bridge (**10 bars, phrased 4+3+3**) · Verse · Outro with a complete ending. Compact single-bridge model, the same economy as "No Reply" and "Day Tripper" — and the cold ending matters, because a fade would have implied endless begging rather than a decision.

Pollack reads the song as **one gesture repeated at every scale**: push forward, then catch yourself and back off. Mostly I–IV–V with ii and vi, plus the two imports that give it bite — **♭III and ♭VI borrowed from parallel E minor** ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The verse's first phrase speeds the harmonic rhythm up on an offbeat, three major chords stepping upward, the first of them foreign — a G against a field of G♯s ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]) — and phrase 2 restores order by being nearly identical but closing on I. Phrase 3, the one with hard syncopation on every 2-and, is ironically **the only phrase where the harmony sits still for four bars**: the agitation is purely rhythmic ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]).

The bridge's second phrase drops its expected fourth bar and runs straight into the third ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]). The outro is the payoff for that planted G — I – V/♭VI – ♭VI – V – I; play it with the diatonic chords instead and it collapses into squareness. In verse phrase 3 lead and backing vocals interlock so their fragments assemble into one continuous line.

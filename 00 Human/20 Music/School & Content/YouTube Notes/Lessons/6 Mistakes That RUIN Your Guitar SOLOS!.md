# 6 Mistakes That RUIN Your Guitar SOLOS!

## Synopsis

A second, mostly-distinct BERNTH "solo mistakes" video (compare [[The 6 WORST Guitar Solo MISTAKES! (Fix These Bad Habits TODAY)]], already routed to the same target notes). Six mistakes/fixes: plain scale-run phrasing, no rests, single-variety string bending, motif-less lines, monotone rhythm, and staying only in basic scales.

## What's taught

- **Mistake 1 — plain up/down scale phrasing; fix — replay a known lick starting from a different note each time.** Generalizes to the whole existing lick library, not just one phrase.
- **Mistake 2 — no rests; fix — "sing it in your head."** Imagine/hum the phrase as a vocalist would, and rests land naturally where a singer needs to breathe — offered as the actual mechanism behind a vague "play something more melodic" note from a producer or bandmate.
- **Mistake 3 — relying on one bend variation only; fix — combine bend types in one phrase.** Named variations: bend-and-release, pre-bend, whole-tone/half-tone bends, unison bends, and a bend on top of an already-bent note, plus vibrato. Full list → [[String Bending Technique]].
- **Mistake 4 — lines that go nowhere; fix — build a rhythmic motif.** A short rhythmic idea stated, developed across following bars, and resolved at the end of the lick — demoed in a rock/metal harmonic-minor context, explicitly not a blues/jazz-exclusive device.
- **Mistake 5 — monotone rhythm across a long line; fix — switch subdivisions mid-line.** Worked example alternates 16th notes, eighth-note triplets, and 16th-note triplets within one otherwise-simple scalar line — rhythmic unpredictability alone makes it read as interesting.
- **Mistake 6 — staying only in pentatonic/natural major/minor scales; fix — mix in exotic scale variations**, especially over harmonically open material (basic power-chord riffs, low-string rhythm patterns) where there's room to experiment without clashing.

# Saturation Send

**Role:** Parallel dirt — a filthy copy blended under clean sources for density and attitude. Same parallel logic as [[Parallel Comp Send]]: keep the clean signal intact, add controlled wreckage underneath.

**Why parallel instead of insert saturation:** an insert saturator has one drive knob per source; a saturation *send* gives every track a blendable dirt knob (the send level) into one consistent flavor — and the dirt can be EQ'd separately from the source.

## Signal chain

### 1. [[Decapitator]] — The dirt
- **Style E** (① — Chandler/EMI TG, aggressive but keeps low-end weight + airy top; the parallel-attitude default) · **Drive 6.0** (② — genuinely dirty; subtle defeats the purpose on a parallel path) · **Low Cut 150 Hz** (⑥ — keep flabby lows out of the saturation circuit) · **Tone slightly Bright, ~1 o'clock** (⑦) · **High Cut 10 kHz** (⑧ — tame fizz at the source) · **Thump off · Steep off** · **Auto on** (⑩ — output stays level-matched) · **Mix 100%** (⑪ — the send level is the parallel blend, so the plugin runs full wet)
- **Punish (③): off by default — punch it in for a hook / rap-verse attitude moment**, then print the result. Alt **Style P** (Culture Vulture pentode) for thicker, odd-harmonic grit.

### 2. [[Pro-Q 4]] — Shape the dirt
- **HPF 200–300 Hz** (saturated lows = instant mud) · LPF 6–8 kHz (fizz control)
- **Why:** Mid-focused distortion is what reads as "attitude"; full-range distortion reads as "broken."

## Usage
- **Lead vocal:** blend under for hooks/rap verses — density that EQ can't buy.
- **Bass:** parallel grit lets the Hofner keep its clean low end while the mids snarl.
- **Snare/full drums:** an alternative flavor to the 1176 crush — try both, pick per song.

## Variations
- **Lo-fi flavor:** swap Decapitator for [[RC-20 Retro Color]] (adds wobble/noise, not just drive).
- **Tape flavor:** [[Kramer Tape]] slammed (input hot) — softer, rounder dirt for the Beatles lane.

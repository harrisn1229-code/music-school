# EchoBoy

![[Images/EchoBoy.png]]

**Category:** Reverb/Delay
**Vendor:** Soundtoys

## What it is

Soundtoys' flagship delay plugin modeling dozens of classic echo unit types (tape, analog, digital, and more) in one plugin.

## What it's used for

The primary creative delay across a huge range of styles — from clean digital slap-back to warped tape echo.

## FX chain notes

Used on a send/return, or inserted for a dedicated delay effect on vocals, guitars, and synths.

## Techniques

- **Switched to this device from stock Ableton delay specifically for its stereo image, not for any parameter it exposes.** Kevin Parker (Tame Impala), on *Deadbeat*: his prior two albums (*Currents*, *The Slow Rush*) used Ableton's stock delay/echo for vocals; this one switched to EchoBoy across most tracks, citing "an interesting stereo image that I can't quite put a finger on… it's just the way you want your vocal to sound" — a qualitative, ear-led reason rather than a specific setting. → [[Tame Impala Vocal Chain]], [[Stereo Field]] — Kevin Parker (Tape Notes), [[TN 188 Tame Impala - Full Video Episode (Part 1)]]
- **Two synced instances stacked for a guitar-solo "arrival" moment: an eighth-note repeat alongside a longer, roughly-quarter-note repeat, run simultaneously with a touch of reverb underneath both.** The eighth keeps the part moving while the longer repeat gives it size; run together rather than swapped between, the pair reads as one bigger effect than either alone. Recipe: two EchoBoy instances at synced 1/8 and 1/4 note divisions, feeding a shared reverb send. → [[Delay]] (effect), [[Reverb]] (effect), [[LEAD Slot]] — Mix with the Masters (Chris Lord-Alge), [[Mixing Muse Guitars - Chris Lord-Alge]]
- **Cross-wire the stereo feedback path — left channel's repeats feed back into the right side and vice versa — for a genuinely bouncing stereo delay instead of two independent per-channel repeat trails.** "In the Echo Boy, you can cross-balance the feedback mix so that I can have feedback from the right feeding back into the left and vice versa." The built-in Ping-Pong/multi-tap routing (⑧) is the modern one-knob equivalent of manually cross-patching feedback L↔R on a stereo delay unit. → [[Stereo Field]], [[Delay]] (effect) — Mix with the Masters (Andy Wallace), [[Mixing Avenged Sevenfold - Andy Wallace]]
- **A subtle, roughly-quarter-note tape echo left running under a full drum bus reads as "dirty quality" and "air" rather than an audible discrete repeat — deliberately kept below the threshold of being consciously heard as an echo.** Set loosely by ear to a beat division, then adjusted from there, embracing hardware drift rather than dialing an exact value: "sometimes you might come across an echo that's just kind of random and it gives you an idea for a different sort of flavor than you might have intended." Confirms the "subtle enough to disappear into the source" pattern already logged below (Greg Wells' vocal delay entries), here applied to a full drum bus via a reamp send/return rather than a vocal insert. → [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[Depth and Space]] — Mix with the Masters (Russell Elevado), [[Tape Echo on D'Angelo's Drums - Russell Elevado]]
- **Second confirmed "big fan of the Space Echo" mention, this time run "all over" a finished lead vocal rather than on an instrument — a favorite, default-reach tape-echo character rather than an occasional special-case effect.** Atticus Ross, on a Trent Reznor lead vocal: "obviously I'm a big fan of the Space Echo — you can hear it all over the vocal." No repeat/feedback/mix values given (qualitative only), consistent with the other Space Echo entries logged on this page and [[Vintage Gear Worklist]] — this unit still isn't owned as hardware; EchoBoy's Echo Type selector (tape-character modes) remains the closest owned lane. → [[Vintage Gear Worklist]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — Mix with the Masters (Atticus Ross), [[Atticus Ross' Production Process]]
- **Best-match owned lane for a real Space Echo unit: an electric sitar run through a tape-echo/spring-reverb hardware pairing became a song's actual backbone specifically because it sounded "special and odd," not because it was harmonically necessary.** Jack Antonoff, building Lana Del Rey's "The Greatest": electric sitar → Roland-style Space Echo (tape delay + built-in spring reverb) — "that just kind of made it a little special and odd... that kind of became the backbone." No specific time/repeat/reverb-mix values given (qualitative description only). This unit isn't owned as hardware; EchoBoy's Echo Type selector (tape-character modes) is the closest owned lane for the delay half of the pairing — pair with a spring-style reverb (e.g. [[Ghz DC19 3]]'s spring section, used elsewhere in this vault for the same "spring makes anything sound like a guitar" coloration) to approximate the full Space Echo character. → [[Vintage Gear Worklist]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — Mix with the Masters (Jack Antonoff), [[How Jack Antonoff creates songs from small ideas]]
- **Tape delay chosen for a vocal specifically BECAUSE it's inaccurate — mid-focused, rolled off top and bottom, with a slow modulation "swim" rather than a fast warble.** Greg Wells: "I'm a big fan of kind of tape delay on a vocal because it's not very accurate on top or bottom — it's all sort of mid-range, just like weird kind of dreamy Echo Reflections. I like a little bit of modulation on that delay, just a bit, so it swims a little bit... slow, not fast." Owned-lane recipe: Echo Type set to a tape mode, Low Cut/High Cut pulled in from both ends to narrow the repeats toward mid-range, a small amount of Wow & Flutter for the slow swim (not pushed hard enough to warble). Separately, he layers a ping-pong, rolled-off delay together with a second independent MONO delay behind it purely for "weird depth perception" the listener isn't meant to consciously register — a second EchoBoy instance run dry/unsynced behind the ping-pong one is the owned-lane read on that pairing. *(qualitative — no time/feedback/mod-rate values given)* → [[Delay]] (effect), [[Depth and Space]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]] — Greg Wells (Mix with the Masters), [[Bringing vocals out of the speakers with Greg Wells]]
- **Automate EchoBoy on a stack of hi-hat overdubs to fake a drum roll/fill filling a scene-change transition, kept deliberately low in the mix rather than a featured hit.** Going into the bridge of a song ("definitely sort of a scene change"), Peter Katis built a fill from hi-hat overdubs run through **EchoBoy with automated parameters** — the automation is what turns plain hi-hat hits into "almost like a little drum roll." Explicitly NOT a part he or the band loved on its own merits — "I don't love it either, but something has to happen" — because the section had zero percussive elements otherwise and "the song just fell completely flat" without it; the fix is functional (fill a structural gap) rather than decorative, and it's mixed low specifically so it reads as connective tissue, not a spotlight moment, carrying the song until the vocal re-enters. *(qualitative only — no specific EchoBoy parameter/automation values given)* → [[Automation]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Peter Katis (Mix with the Masters), [Mix breakdown with Peter Katis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o4F2EO_KMLk)
- **A delay throw paired with a hall verb riding directly behind it, printed only on select verse words, not run continuously.** "I also had a delay throw in here with a reverb on it on certain lines that he said in the verse. I'm using the EchoBoy for that delay throw. Followed by a hall verb." Confirms and extends the [[Delay Send Other]] throw-track pattern (EchoBoy insert as an arrangement event) with the specific pairing: the throw doesn't stand alone — a hall reverb rides the same selected words for a bigger accent than either device gives on its own. *(qualitative only — no time/feedback values given)* → [[ValhallaRoom]], [[Delay Send Other]], [[Reverb]] — Kevin Davis (Mix with the Masters), [Mixing Vocals from 'Dancing With A Stranger' with Kevin Davis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bQIZONdZvk8)
- **Tape, eighth-note, ping-pong delay as the sole processing on a Juno-emulation bell counter-melody — called out as a repeat go-to ("I like to use this delay a lot").** Building an 80s synthwave/pop beat, a simple bell patch used only at the ends of verses gets **tape-style delay, eighth-note timing, ping-pong routing** (bounces side to side), with **modulation turned down**, **drive kept low**, and **mix pushed up quite a lot** so the wet delay signal carries most of the audible character. Owned lane: this device's Echo Type selector covers tape character, and its built-in Ping-Pong/multi-tap routing covers the bounce without an extra utility device. → [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]], [[Synthwave]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — loner, [how to make an 80's synthwave/pop beat for the weeknd.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dWhh27UFWhY)
- **One patch, every delay send** — Kenny's 2022 template runs 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, slap and 1/2 sends as the *same* EchoBoy setting varied only by note division, so every delay in the song shares one tone family — Kenny Beats, [New Vocal Chain 2022](https://youtu.be/-SvBNOBWJ_8)
- **Internal high/low cut = the return EQ** — he dropped the separate EQ after each delay send; EchoBoy's feedback-path filters do the band-limiting — Kenny Beats, [New Vocal Chain 2022](https://youtu.be/-SvBNOBWJ_8)
- **Loud insert EchoBoy as a "throw" track** — dedicated track, high end cut, subdivision changed per throw; drag a line's last word onto it for spotlight delays as arrangement events — Kenny Beats, [New Vocal Chain 2022](https://youtu.be/-SvBNOBWJ_8)
- **Delay on a driven/distorted bass** — used on the same signature "Distressor-on-full-red" bass chain (see [[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]]) to keep a heavily saturated bass from feeling static — the delay adds motion without softening the distortion character underneath — Disclosure, [First Twitch Stream Ever (Production Tutorial)](https://youtu.be/IObzp3OugtY)
- **Movement delay on sustained keys** — keeps Keyscape chords alive and moving under a vocal — Disclosure, [You've Got To Let Go](https://youtu.be/1iRPvK1gaOI)

## Manual

- 📄 [[00 Human/20 Music/School & Content/Production/Manuals/EchoBoy.pdf|Local PDF]] · [Official Soundtoys manual](https://www.soundtoys.com/wp-content/uploads/EchoBoy-Manual.pdf)

**High-level points:**
- The core of the plugin's identity is the **Echo Type selector** — it models dozens of real echo mechanisms (tape, analog bucket-brigade, digital rack delays, tube, even broken/dirty modes). Pick this first; everything else shapes it.
- **Style** shifts the chosen Echo Type cleaner ↔ dirtier/more vintage — a "how much" control, distinct from Echo Type's "which one."
- **Wow & Flutter** models the pitch instability of real tape/analog delay hardware, adding organic movement that gets more pronounced with longer delay times.
- The **filter section in the feedback path** (Low Cut/High Cut) shapes how repeats degrade over successive echoes — classic dub/tape-delay behavior where each repeat gets darker/thinner, not just quieter.
- **Saturation/Character** is baked into several Echo Types, not added on top — this is why EchoBoy sounds "alive" even at low feedback, unlike a clean digital delay with color bolted on afterward.
- Built-in **tap tempo + note-division sync** locks delay time to host tempo; **Ping-Pong/multi-tap routing** gives stereo-bouncing repeats from a single instance without an extra utility device.

## Used in my chains

My default delay — the two dedicated delay sends run on it, and it's the go-to word/phrase throw insert across vocals:

- **[[Delay Send Slap]]** — *slot 1, the slap*: Studio Tape, 110 ms unsynced, Feedback 5% (one repeat), Wow/Flutter on — the "mic in a room" thickener.
- **[[Delay Send Other]]** — *slot 1, synced repeats*: Ping-Pong, dotted 1/8, Feedback 22%, feedback filtered — fills the pockets between phrases.
- **Throw insert** on vocal tracks ([[1 Main Vocal Track|Main Vocal]], [[1 Backup Vocal Track|Backup]]/ad-libs, [[1 Harmony Vocal Track|Harmony]], stacks) — a loud insert on the last word, high end cut, subdivision per throw (Kenny's move); the specific word gets the insert, glue delay goes to the shared send.
- **[[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass]]** motion delay on a driven bass (keeps saturated bass from feeling static); texture option on [[1 Electric Piano Track|E-Piano]] and [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track|guitar amp]].

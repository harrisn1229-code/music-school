# For No One — The Beatles

> McCartney plays rented clavichord — no guitars on the track. Alan Civil of the Royal Philharmonia plays the iconic French horn solo. Lennon and Harrison did not contribute. Sparse and devastating.

## Chords
**Key:** C major as played (capo 2 → **sounds in B**)

**Verse:** C – C/B – Am – C/G – F – B♭ – C
→ **I – I/7 – vi – I/5 – IV – ♭VII – I** (a descending bass C–B–A–G, then the **B♭** borrowed ♭VII — the famous ache)

**Chorus ("And in her eyes you see nothing"):** Dm – A7 – Dm – A7 – Dm – Gsus4 – G
→ **ii – V7/ii – ii … – V** (the A7 tonicizes Dm; lands on G = V)

## Production & Arrangement
Chamber-pop at its sparsest — essentially **McCartney solo**: he plays **clavichord** and bass, Ringo light percussion, and the one guest is **Alan Civil's French horn solo** (George Martin scored it a note above the instrument's normal range for that plaintive strain). No other Beatles, no guitars.

- The **descending bass line** (C–B–A–G) + the borrowed **♭VII (B♭)** are the ache; the arrangement's emptiness is what lets them land.
- Dry, close, intimate — a baroque miniature.

## Steal this
- **Clavichord/harpsichord** timbre for baroque-pop color ([[Keyscape]] has them) over a spare arrangement.
- **A single hero solo instrument** (here French horn) scored slightly out of its comfort range for emotional strain — pick one unexpected lead voice.
- **Descending bass + ♭VII** for the ache → [[Chromatic Bass Lines]] · [[Borrowed Chords]].
- **Arrange with emptiness:** the sparseness *is* the production.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — For No One]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #99.*

**Form.** Paul borrowing from early-nineteenth-century art song, placed between "Eleanor Rigby" and "Michelle" on that axis. The form is **completely cyclic** — double verse plus bridge, three times through, with no intro, no outro and no interlude anywhere. Verse 8 bars in two 4-bar phrases, though the tune's rhythmic flow subdivides them unevenly so it never sits as square as the count implies; bridge 10 bars, an AA couplet plus a 2-bar tag that sets up the next verse, with a complete ending on its final appearance. B major is an unusual home key for the catalogue — he can only pair it with "One After 909."

**Harmony.** `Verse: I (4 bars, bass walking B – A♯ – G♯ – F♯) | IV | ♭VII | I (2)`. He argues *against* assigning a fresh numeral to each of those first four bars — the ear follows one large motion from I to IV and hears the intervening sonorities as connective tissue. The section climaxes on the downbeat of bar 5 where a D♯ in the tune turns the chord into a major seventh; the move on to ♭VII, with a buried chromatic descent G♯ – G♮ – F♯, unwinds that and adds the nostalgic tint ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). The verses establish the key with **♭VII instead of V**, and V's only appearance in the whole song is inside the bridge's pivot home. `Bridge (in C♯ minor): i | V | i (2), ×2 · i | V (6/4 → 5/3)` — a textbook classical **pivot modulation to ii** ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]), whose last bar he reads entirely as V with a **double appoggiatura** rather than as I6/4 moving to V ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]).

**Notable.** The French horn solo is his recording riddle: he reconstructs it as played onto the off-pitch slowed vocal tape and then **replayed at double speed, a full octave up** — the brass analogue of the "In My Life" keyboard solo. The horn also enters *inlaid*, two beats before its verse and running into the following bridge.

**Against this page.** Agrees: his B major is this page's sounding pitch (capo 2 off C shapes).

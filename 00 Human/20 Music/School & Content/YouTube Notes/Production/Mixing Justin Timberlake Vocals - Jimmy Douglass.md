# Mixing Justin Timberlake Vocals - Jimmy Douglass

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PpIanhvC9qw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PpIanhvC9qw)

## Synopsis

Jimmy Douglass works a Justin Timberlake lead vocal through an analog outboard chain (1176 → Pultec → hardware reverb), then layers subtle background effects (an H3000 harmonizer doubler, plus internal reverb/delay) whose whole design goal is to be inaudible as discrete effects — reading only as natural space and width.

## What's taught

- **Signal chain order: 1176 (fast attack, light "swashing"/pumping) first, THEN a Pultec-style EQ after it, cutting bottom.** The compressor comes before the EQ in this chain specifically — the Pultec's low cut is applied to the already-compressed signal, not before it.
- **Hardware reverb (an EMT/AMS "480"-class unit) dialed in iteratively, not committed on the first setting** — described mid-process as "not exactly the right one yet," a reminder that even an experienced engineer treats reverb selection as trial-based rather than a fixed go-to preset.
- **An outboard harmonizer (H3000) set to a "doubler" mode for background vocals, when available** — used specifically for its spatial/doubling character on backgrounds, reached for as a hardware-first choice when the physical unit is patched in rather than defaulting to a plugin equivalent.
- **Layer internal (in-the-box) reverb and delay alongside outboard hardware effects on the same vocal**, described candidly as "a little scrappy" but functional — not treated as a purity issue; hardware and software effects are combined pragmatically rather than kept strictly separate.
- **The core design goal: effects should be buried enough that they're not consciously heard as effects at all — just perceived as natural space/width.** Stated directly: "if you don't really listen closely, you don't hear anything at all, it just sounds like space... a natural wideness on the vocal, and that's what we like." The test for whether a reverb/delay send is right isn't "can I hear the effect," it's "does the vocal just sound wider/more three-dimensional without an identifiable processed quality."
- **A practical reason to fully commit before moving on with an all-analog outboard chain**: recalling a hardware patch setup later (the next day, a week later) is "monstrous" to rebuild exactly — a stated motivation for tweaking a setting until it's fully right in the moment rather than leaving it "close enough" and planning to revisit, since revisiting isn't cheap the way recalling a plugin preset is.

# Autumn Leaves Chords - 5 Useful ways to unlock Extensions

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EjSzPfn6vOo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EjSzPfn6vOo)

## Synopsis

Jens Larsen builds a single teaching arc out of one 8-bar phrase — the first eight bars of **Autumn Leaves** (G minor: Cm7 – F7 – B♭maj7 – E♭maj7 – Aø7 – D7 – Gm(tonic) – G7) — playing it **five times in a row**, each pass adding more color than the last, so the same worked example doubles as a live demonstration of *how* to add extensions and alterations rather than just a list of finished voicings. Pass 1 is bare Drop 2 voicings on the top string set with zero extensions or alterations, establishing the "basics" everyone should have under their fingers before decorating anything. Passes 2–4 progressively add extensions by **substituting root and/or 5th for a 9th/11th/13th while always keeping the 3rd and 7th intact** (the guide tones that define quality and function), moving from one substituted note per chord to two. Pass 5 abandons functional voice-leading almost entirely in favor of a **modal, sound-first approach** — treating each chord as its own color to be maximized rather than a step in a progression that has to keep announcing itself — including inserting an extra ii-V that doesn't belong to the tune's own harmony and substituting a chord (Am9 for Aø7) that "really doesn't belong in G minor," on purpose. Larsen is explicit throughout about *why* each substitution works (which scale degree becomes which extension, why a given extension was avoided because it clashes with the tune's own melody or functional cadence), and repeatedly ties choices back to the parent scale each chord is drawn from (the tune's own G minor/G harmonic-minor material, or an altered/diminished scale layered on top for color). The video ends on the explicit point of the whole exercise: understanding the mechanics well enough to freely choose, on a chord-by-chord basis, how far to push a static harmony's color without losing the song underneath it — and knowing that Autumn Leaves stays recognizable even under heavy alteration, which is *why* he picked it as the demo tune.

## Techniques

**Pass 1 — the basic Drop 2 voicings (no extensions, no alterations)**
- All voicings kept to the **top string set** deliberately — Larsen states this explicitly: staying high on the neck keeps the extensions clean when they get added later; low-string extensions clash with the chord's lower "basic power" and get muddy. → [[Chord Voicings]], [[Masking and Carving]]
- Progression as played: **Cm7 → F7 → B♭maj7 → E♭maj7 → Aø7 → D7 → Gm6 → G7** (back to the top of the form).
- **Voicing choice, not just a note choice:** at B♭maj7 he explicitly rejects a lower-neck B♭maj7 voicing with B♭ on top and A underneath ("not the most constant interval") and instead moves up the neck to a voicing with better top-note motion — voicing quality, not just correct notes, drives the choice.
- **Tonic-minor color choice, generalizable to any minor-key resolution:** on the "home" Gm chord (G minor is the actual key), he deliberately chooses **minor 6** over the more common jazz default of **minor 6 or minor(major7)**, judging the minor 6 "a little bit more consonant" and better as a point of rest; minor(major7) was tried and rejected as functioning better as color than as resolution. **General rule stated:** on a tonic minor chord, favor the reading that resolves the tension rather than the one that adds more of it. → [[Chords by Key]], [[Chord Substitution]]

**Pass 2 — one substitution per chord: replace the root with the 9th (or, on the half-diminished, the 3rd with the 11th)**
- **Core mechanism, stated as a general rule:** to add a 9th to any chord, take the existing voicing and **replace the root with the 9th** — same shape, one note moved a whole step up. *(general starting point for any Drop 2 shape, not tune-specific)*
- **Cm7 → Cm9** (root C → D).
- **F7 → F9** (root F → G).
- **B♭maj7 → B♭maj9** (replace the B♭ root with C).
- **E♭maj7 → E♭maj9** — flagged as a useful "aha": once the root is replaced by the 9th, the resulting notes are identical to a plain **Gm7** voicing (E♭maj9-no-root and Gm7 are the same shape) — the same "one shape, two chord identities" logic already on [[Chord Voicings]] and [[Upper Structure Triads from Seventh Chords]]. → [[Chord Voicings]], [[Upper Structure Triads from Seventh Chords]]
- **Aø7 → Aø11** (substitute the **3rd with the 11th**, not the usual root-for-9th move). **Reasoning given explicitly:** the diatonic 9 available here (B♭) sounds bad in this spot because this Aø7 functions as part of a ii-V cadence into G minor and the B♭ clashes against the bass/root motion — so he reaches for the 11 instead as a cleaner-sounding extension in a cadential context. **General lesson:** which extension is "correct" isn't fixed by the chord symbol alone — check it against the chord's function in the progression, not just its own scale. → [[Extended Chords]], [[Chord Substitution]]
- **D7 → D7♭9♭13** (root → ♭9, 5th → ♭13) — the first altered-dominant color in the sequence, resolving into the tonic.
- **Gm6 → Gm6/9** (root G → A, the 9th) — same shape, same substitution logic applied to the tonic-minor voicing from Pass 1.
- **G7 → G7♭9♭13** (root → ♭9, 5th → ♭13) — mirrors the D7 treatment above, taking the progression back to the top of the form with matching color.

**Pass 3 — two substitutions per chord (root AND 5th), keeping the 3rd and 7th fixed as the anchor**
- **Core mechanism, generalized from Pass 2:** once comfortable substituting the root for the 9th alone, the same idea extends to **also substituting the 5th** — for 4-note chords, always protect the 3rd and 7th (the guide tones that define major/minor and dominant/major-7 identity) and treat root and 5th as the two "free" slots for color. → [[Extended Chords]], [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Cm7 → Cm11** (root → 9th as before, **plus** 5th → 11th) — called out as "a bit of a stretch" fingering-wise but musically the natural next step from Cm9.
- ⚠️ *Caption uncertainty flagged:* the transcript names the next altered-dominant voicing as "**D7♭9,13**" in a sentence that immediately follows the Cm11 discussion and precedes "when I'm moving from C minor 7 to F7" — since the progression's actual chord at that point is **F7**, not D7, and Larsen explicitly frames it via "contrary motion... when I'm moving from C minor 7 to F7," this is very likely a caption mis-transcription of an altered **F7** voicing (a "diminished-sounding dominant," his own phrase for it), not a literal D7. Treat the "D7♭9,13" chord name at this specific spot as unreliable; the underlying technique — building an altered dominant with a diminished-scale flavor for a ii→V move, driven by contrary motion between two inner voices — is solid.
- **B♭maj7 → B♭maj7(9,13)** — 5th substituted for the 13, added on top of the 9th already established in Pass 2.
- **E♭maj7 → E♭maj9** (same as Pass 2 — no further extension added here this pass).
- **Aø7 → Aø(nat9)** — this time substitutes the **3rd with a natural 9** (B natural) instead of the 11th used in Pass 2, deliberately reading it out of **C melodic minor** for a more altered color — Larsen flags this move as clashing with the tune's own melody (which has a C at this point), a conscious trade of melodic accuracy for harmonic color when playing solo/comping rather than under a fixed melody.
- **D7 → D7♭9♭5** — sourced from the **altered scale**, though Larsen notes the same notes are also derivable from the **diminished (half-whole) scale**; he picks the altered reading as the better fit for the harmonic context even though both are technically valid. → [[Altered Chords]], [[Diminished Chords]]
- **Gm(maj7) → Gm(maj6/13)** — resolves to G minor-major (not minor 6 this time) with a 6th/13th added on top, a brighter/more dissonant tonic-minor color than Pass 1's plain minor 6.
- **G7 → G7♯9♭5** — also sourced from the **altered scale**, closing the phrase with maximum color before the repeat.

**Pass 4 — Drop 2 shapes re-derived from upper-structure triads (same extension logic, different physical route to it)**
- **Cm7 → Cm9(11)**: voiced as an **upper-structure triad over the chord's 3rd** — the E♭ (3rd of Cm) carries a **B♭ major triad** stacked above it (2nd inversion), which supplies the 9 and 11 as byproducts of the triad shape rather than as individually chosen substitutions. → [[Upper Structure Triads from Seventh Chords]]
- **F7 → F7♭13♭9** (Drop 2 shape).
- **B♭maj7 → B♭maj7♯11** — substitutes the **5th for a ♯11**, explicitly called out as **"a Lydian sound"** and one of Larsen's own favorite voicings on this chord ("one of my favorite voicings... that I really use a lot"). He's explicit that this genuinely alters the song's own sound at this point ("there's no shadow of Autumn Leaves at this point") — offered as a deliberate color choice, not a "correct" substitution. → [[Extended Chords]]
- **E♭maj7 → E♭maj7(9,13)**.
- **Aø7 → Aø(nat9,11)** — this pass combines BOTH substitutions used separately in Passes 2–3: root → natural 9 AND 3rd → 11, at the same time.
- **D7 → D7alt (♭5,♯9)** — explicitly labeled "altered D7" this time (as opposed to the ambiguous altered/diminished framing in Pass 3).
- **Gm(maj7), no extensions** — a deliberate step BACK from Pass 3's added color: plain minor-major triad-plus-7th with **chord tones voiced close together** for a dissonant cluster effect, chosen on purpose over adding another extension — "instead of using an extension I'm just putting some chord tones next to each other" for interest.
- **G7 → G7♭9♭5**, called out as a **tritone substitution in disguise**: this exact voicing's notes are enharmonically identical to a **D♭ triad/shape**, so it can be understood (and played) as either a G7♭9♭5 or as its tritone substitute D♭7 — "you can of course also play it as that." → [[Chord Substitution]] § *Tritone substitution*

**Pass 5 — modal/color-first approach: treat each chord as its own sound rather than a step in a functional progression**
- **Framing, stated directly:** the first four passes stayed focused on keeping the harmony moving with functional voice-leading between chords; Pass 5 shifts to a **modal approach** — consider each chord mostly in isolation as its own color, worrying less about how it connects to the next chord — the same treatment Larsen attributes to **late-period Herbie Hancock / Tony Williams / Ron Carter (Miles Davis Quintet)** playing standards. → [[Modal Jazz]]
- **Cm7 → Cm(11,13)** (root → 13, 5th → 11 — both substitutions, same mechanism as Pass 3/4 but on a fresh chord pairing).
- **F7 → F7alt, deliberately avoiding the chord's own 3rd (A natural)** — the functional reason given is striking: the 3rd of F7 is the note that would normally signal "we've arrived at the V of the ii-V," so *omitting* it and loading the chord with alterations instead **delays that functional signal** on purpose while still keeping enough dominant color that the ear tracks forward motion. A rare case of a chord-tone OMISSION being the deliberate move, not an extension addition. → [[Chord Substitution]]
- **B♭maj7 → incomplete B♭maj7♯11(9)** (root, ♯11, major 7, 9 — no 5th at all) resolving up a half-step-and-more into an implied **C7(13)/B♭** slash chord — a genuinely unusual resolution target for this spot in the tune. → [[Slash Chords]]
- **E♭maj7 → E♭maj9** (plain, deliberately un-fancy — "just to make the whole thing breathe a little bit" after several dense chords in a row; a reminder that density itself is a choice, not a default to maximize every time).
- **Reharmonization — an inserted extra ii-V that doesn't belong to the tune's own changes:** in place of resolving straight toward Aø7, Larsen inserts **B♭m7(11) → E♭7(9)** as an extra ii-V borrowed into the space, deliberately taking the harmony "a little further away" from the original tune before pulling back. → [[Chord Substitution]], [[Chord Progressions]]
- **Chord substitution, not just extension — Aø7 replaced outright with Am9**, a chord Larsen states explicitly **"really doesn't belong in G minor"** — kept anyway, on purpose, as the point of the modal/color-first pass: prioritizing "is this a good sound" over "is this the diatonically correct chord." → [[Chord Substitution]]
- **D7 → D7♭13♯9**, pulling the harmony back home toward G minor after the outside excursion.
- **Gm(tonic) → Gm6/9♯11, sourced from D harmonic minor** — flagged as an unusual, rarely-heard tonic-minor voicing, deliberately chosen over the more familiar minor 6 or minor(maj7) defaults used in earlier passes, specifically to create a moment of surprise at the point where the ear expects the most "resolved," least surprising chord of the whole phrase. → [[Scale Modes Reference]], [[Chords by Key]]
- **G7 → G7♯9(natural 5) via an upper-structure triad-over-bass: a B♭ major triad, 1st inversion, played over a B bass note.** Larsen identifies this explicitly as an **upper structure** (his own term) rather than a "regular" altered-dominant voicing, and notes the natural (not altered) 5th points the sound toward the **diminished** family rather than the altered scale. → [[Upper Structure Chords]], [[Diminished Chords]]

**General framing points (apply across all five passes)**
- **Practical reason Autumn Leaves specifically was chosen as the demo tune:** it stays recognizable even under Pass 5's heaviest alteration — Larsen states some tunes would become unrecognizable under this much reharmonization, and picking a tune that survives it is what makes the exercise instructive rather than just abstract.
- **Stated practice/teaching goal:** work through a familiar tune's changes multiple times at increasing color levels rather than jumping straight to maximal alteration — the point is to feel the mechanism (which note becomes which extension, and why) at each step, not to memorize five finished voicing sets.

# How Pokemon 8-bit music was inspired by classical music

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WyvfKFVRqVs)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WyvfKFVRqVs)

## Synopsis

How composer Junichi Masuda wrote the original Pokémon Game Boy soundtrack within a genuinely severe hardware limit (3 usable pitched voices) by directly studying Bach's counterpoint, plus the specific altered-scale choices behind the soundtrack's more unsettling cues (Lydian ♭2, harmonic major).

## Techniques

- **The hardware constraint, precisely**: the Game Boy sound chip has 4 voices total — 2 pulse-wave only, 1 flexible/programmable waveform, 1 noise-only (percussion/SFX, can't play pitches) — leaving exactly 3 usable pitched voices, just enough for a bare triad.
- **Counterpoint (independent melodic voices that combine into implied harmony) directly solves the "only 3 voices, need it to sound like more" problem — and Bach's 2- and 3-part inventions were Masuda's literal study material.** Contrasted explicitly with homophony (a melody + accompanying chords, perceived as one blended texture) — counterpoint instead gives each voice its own melodic identity; stacked, the voices imply a chord progression despite no single voice ever playing a chord. Masuda studied Bach's 2/3-part inventions specifically because they're "intricate, engaging, impressive" while never exceeding 2-3 independent lines — a direct historical precedent for exactly his hardware ceiling, in Masuda's own words: "that limitation can lead to some of the best music." → [[Canon and Fugue]], [[Species Counterpoint]]
- **Arpeggiating a chord across one voice, rather than stacking it across multiple voices, is a concrete voice-economy technique for any severely limited polyphony (chiptune, a small ensemble, a sparse arrangement).** Worked in the "Pallet Town" theme: the 2nd voice switches from a countermelody to an arpeggiated chord figure — playing a chord's notes one at a time in a single voice rather than stacking them across all 3 available voices, freeing the other 2 voices to keep doing independent melodic work instead of being consumed by one harmony.
- **Two altered scales used deliberately for "unsettling"/scary game areas, each named and mechanism-explained**: **Lydian ♭2** (raised 4th + flattened 2nd together — Lavender Town, the "Pokémon graveyard" area) — an unusual, specifically unnatural-sounding combination cited as the actual (non-supernatural) explanation for the "Lavender Town Syndrome" urban myth about the music causing headaches. **Harmonic major** (major scale with a lowered 6th degree) — Mt. Moon theme, with the lowered-6th motif restated at a new pitch center (B, then shifted to E) followed by a chromatic descending line, described as "spiraling" — Masuda names Stravinsky (*Rite of Spring*, *The Firebird*) and Shostakovich (Symphonies 5 and 7) as the direct influence behind this more chromatic, later-20th-century-classical-flavored material, distinct from the Bach influence behind the contrapuntal writing. → [[Scale Modes Reference]]
- **A chiptune-specific production discipline worth generalizing: deliberately programming expressive dynamics (volume/articulation/tone changes) into a fundamentally mechanical, note-by-note-entered medium**, rather than letting the hardware's rigid entry process default to a flat, "generated" feel — cited as part of why the soundtrack reads as "played by musicians" rather than "generated by a microchip" despite being hand-programmed rather than performed.

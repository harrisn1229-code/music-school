# 8 Songs That 'Rip Off' Other Songs

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4dRzebxu3XQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4dRzebxu3XQ)

## Synopsis

Another entry in Bennett's recurring "rip off" series — 8 more melody/riff-lift cases (as distinct from sampling), pitch-shifted side-by-side comparisons, and the legal-vs-brushed-off inconsistency in how the source artists responded.

## Techniques

- **Case-by-case borrowing catalog**: Bruce Springsteen's "Outlaw Pete" (2009) lifts its main hook melody from Kiss's 1979 disco-rock song "I Was Made for Lovin' You" — confirmed by Kiss themselves, who chose not to sue ("we like Springsteen, we don't sue him"). Huey Lewis and the News's "I Want a New Drug" (1983) is the well-known case that DID end in a settled lawsuit against Ray Parker Jr.'s "Ghostbusters."
- **Comparison method, reusable for spotting a lift yourself**: pitch-shift the two songs into the same key before comparing — surface-level differences (tempo, genre, key) can mask a melody that's structurally identical underneath.
- **The legal-inconsistency point (repeated across this series' videos)**: whether a source artist sues appears to depend more on relationship/goodwill/publicity calculus than any consistent legal principle — the same artist (Kiss) sues some borrowers and not others for comparably close lifts.

# Synthet - The DELETE Trick

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0MW6gJ_G1xk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0MW6gJ_G1xk)

## Synopsis

A short case for subtraction over addition: once a groove is working, removing elements creates suspense and makes the remaining parts read as "more" (the brain can't process too many simultaneous elements), and a big reverb can help sell an emptied-out moment as intentional rather than accidental. The closing advice is a workflow habit — try silencing sections *before* spending hours adding new layers to fix a part that isn't working.

## Techniques

- **Removing elements makes what's left read as more** — once a good groove exists, try taking things away rather than continuing to add; less clutter lets the remaining parts stand out, since a listener's attention can't track too many simultaneous elements at once. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Silence as suspense, not just absence** — a deliberately emptied section builds anticipation for the next full part to land, functioning as its own arrangement device rather than a gap to fill.
- **A big reverb sells an emptied section as intentional** — dropping most elements out but leaving a wash of reverb reads as a deliberate "breathing space" moment rather than a mix mistake.
- **Workflow habit: try silencing before adding more** — when a section "isn't working," the instinct is to keep layering fixes on top; try muting elements first and see if subtraction solves it before spending hours on additional processing/parts.
- **Mute tool shortcut:** press T to open the mute tool for quick experimentation with silencing sections.

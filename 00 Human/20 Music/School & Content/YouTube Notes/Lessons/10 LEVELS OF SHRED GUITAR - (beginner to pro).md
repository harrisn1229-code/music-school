# 10 LEVELS OF SHRED GUITAR - (beginner to pro)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gH1W-ES3sUA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gH1W-ES3sUA)

## Synopsis

A genuine beginner-to-advanced shred curriculum ladder, each level explicitly building on the one before rather than being a standalone trick — useful as a reference progression, not just a single workout.

## Techniques

- **Level 1 — alternate picking basics to a metronome**, hands synchronized, groups of 4 notes.
- **Level 2 — same idea, explicitly contrasted against the anti-pattern of just picking as fast as possible and hoping the fretting hand keeps up** (unsynchronized "pushing" is called out directly as not a shortcut to clean speed).
- **Level 3 — transition every 2 notes instead of 4**, less thinking time per group but "easier than it looks"; picking hand stays relaxed, minimal motion.
- **Level 4 — sync every single picking stroke to a different note.** Named as the actual key unlock for shredding and the first real technique wall — up to this point the fretting hand could mostly "chill" while the picking hand did the work; from here everything must lock together. Worth spending real time here rather than rushing past it.
- **Level 5 — clean string transitions (2-string, e.g. B→E) with the goal of the transition being inaudible** to a listener — no stuck pick, no unwanted noise at the string change.
- **Level 6 — full 6-string ascending scale**, same clean-transition goal scaled up; minimize both picking- and fretting-hand movement, keep the fretting wrist still for consistency.
- **Level 7 — combine mixed note values, odd time signatures, string skipping, and 4-notes-per-string phrases** — framed as recombining the existing foundation, not new material.
- **Level 8 — blend legato, alternate picking, and sweep picking within one passage while deliberately preserving each technique's distinct tonal character** (alternate = fast/aggressive, sweep = cascading, legato = fluid/effortless) rather than smoothing them into one uniform sound.
- **Level 9 — advanced techniques used musically, not as flash**: multi-finger/double tapping, sweep-picking finger rolls for arpeggios — explicit framing that these should serve real musical lines, not just "chromatic nonsense" eye candy.

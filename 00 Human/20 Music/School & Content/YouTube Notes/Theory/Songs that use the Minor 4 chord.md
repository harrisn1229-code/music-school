# Songs that use the Minor 4 chord

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tStINGVUbWo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tStINGVUbWo)

## Synopsis

A full treatment of the borrowed minor-iv chord in a major key (modal interchange/mixture) — what it is, the minor plagal cadence it produces when resolving to I, the common IV→iv→I "extended" form heard constantly in pop, and a voice-leading explanation of *why* the resolution feels so emotionally loaded.

## Techniques

- **What it is**: in a major key, replace the normally-major IV chord with its minor version (e.g., in C major, play Fm instead of F) — an instance of **modal interchange/modal mixture**, borrowing a chord from the parallel minor key into a major-key context.
- **Minor plagal cadence**: the iv→I resolution (as opposed to the regular IV→I plagal/"amen" cadence) — bittersweet, melancholic. Example: the closing "nothing really matters" moment of "Bohemian Rhapsody" (Queen), A♭m→E♭ in E♭ major. "Nowhere Man" (The Beatles) in E major.
- **Extended form — IV → iv → I**: by far the most common real-world pattern — start on the regular major IV, immediately darken it to minor iv, then resolve to I. Named examples: "Wake Me Up When September Ends" (Green Day), "Creep" (Radiohead — the *other* Creep reference in this batch, TLC's "Waterfalls"-family "Perfect"), "Don't Look Back in Anger" (Oasis), "Show Me Love," "Who Do You Think You Are."
- **Alternate resolution target — iv→vi instead of iv→I**: a darker, less common variant (minor chord resolving to minor chord). Examples: "Between the Bars" (Elliott Smith), "Where Is My Mind" (Pixies — see also the dedicated "Where Is My Mind" progression video in this same batch, though that covers a *different* progression from the same song's harmonic universe).
- **iv without the major IV setup**: some songs use only i→iv (skip the major-IV half entirely) — see Bennett's separate Mixolydian-♭6/Dorian-adjacent video for more examples of this bare form; noted here as related but out of scope for this video specifically.
- **Why the resolution is so satisfying — voice-leading explanation**: compare Csus4→C (the 4th/F resolving down a step to the 3rd/E, a classic suspension release) to Fm→C — the same F→E motion is present, but Fm *also* moves A♭→G in parallel (the minor 3rd of Fm resolving down to the major 3rd of C), doubling up the semitone-down resolution motion. Two voices moving in parallel semitone resolution, both landing on chord tones of the same tonic, is what gives the minor plagal cadence its "lush, longing" quality — a "supercharged" version of a plain sus4 resolution.

# How "The Cave" Changed Kenny Beats Career (+ Producer Advice)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23lVC_G4oOM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23lVC_G4oOM)

## Synopsis

Kenny Beats traces his breakout back to "The Cave" — a cheap ($a few hundred a month), always-available room that doubled as a hangout spot and a studio, which is what actually built his catalog and his network, not beat quality or pricing strategy. He walks through the real mechanics of that era (free, unlimited time, open door to anyone regardless of status), then moves into mindset advice: fighting through session anxiety because the reward outweighs it, what watching Lauryn Hill work in the studio taught him about simplicity and emotional content over technical complexity, how to cook up in front of an artist without stumbling and killing the room's confidence, and a hard rule for splitting credit 50/50 with collaborators to build trust and reputation over time.

## Techniques

- **The Cave was the hangout AND the studio, on purpose** — a cheap year-round space that blurred the line between "come chill" and "come make music," so people who showed up to hang ended up recording, and people who showed up to record ended up staying — that dual identity is what made the room a destination rather than a booking. — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [How "The Cave" Changed Kenny Beats Career (+ Producer Advice)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23lVC_G4oOM)
- **The real answer to "how did you work with so many people" is time given away for free, not beat quality** — no fixed price on beats, no cap on session length, anyone could use the space for as long as they wanted; Kenny is explicit that "the answer is not I make the best beats... the answer is I spent the most time" letting people use the spot. — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [How "The Cave" Changed Kenny Beats Career (+ Producer Advice)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23lVC_G4oOM)
- **The room worked as a therapy couch as much as a recording booth, open to everyone regardless of status** — rich, signed, or brand new, it didn't matter; "you come work for free" was the entry point for every kind of artist, and that indiscriminate openness is what let the catalog and the reputation compound. — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [How "The Cave" Changed Kenny Beats Career (+ Producer Advice)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23lVC_G4oOM)
- **Session anxiety doesn't go away — you work through it because the payoff is worth it** — panic attacks and fear of rejection in the room are common even among artists people assume have it figured out; the reward of making something you're proud of for the rest of your life is what makes facing that fear worth it every time, not the absence of the fear. — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [How "The Cave" Changed Kenny Beats Career (+ Producer Advice)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23lVC_G4oOM)
- **Watching Lauryn Hill work non-final takes reframed what a "moment" in the studio is worth** — seeing her walk through unfinished ideas, not a final performance, gave Kenny chills and reinforced that raw process — not just the finished product — is often the actual payoff of the job. — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [How "The Cave" Changed Kenny Beats Career (+ Producer Advice)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23lVC_G4oOM)
- **Simplicity carries more emotional weight than complexity when it's full of soul** — citing the idea that "people who play easy music are usually full of soul," Kenny argues a simple arpeggio with simple chords can hit harder than something technically dense; minimalism takes more courage to be emotionally intense than layering more parts does. — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [How "The Cave" Changed Kenny Beats Career (+ Producer Advice)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23lVC_G4oOM)
- **Don't stumble through presets or plug-ins live in front of an artist — it can anchor the whole session on a bad first impression** — his cook-up advice: pull up something that's already hot, put drums on it, flip it a couple times, get to a finished high-quality idea fast; fumbling through sounds live risks the room jumping to the conclusion that the first weak thing you play is "the beat." — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [How "The Cave" Changed Kenny Beats Career (+ Producer Advice)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23lVC_G4oOM)
- **Split credit 50/50 with collaborators as a default, even when you feel like you did more** — musicians and co-producers routinely get cut out of credit; Kenny's rule is to offer an even split with everyone who worked on a track, distinguishing parts played before a song is written (co-production) from parts added after it's already written (a guest feature-style contribution) — generosity with credit compounds into people going hard for you later. — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [How "The Cave" Changed Kenny Beats Career (+ Producer Advice)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=23lVC_G4oOM)

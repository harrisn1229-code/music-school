## Production & Arrangement
A 50s doo-wop/R&B ballad pastiche, and a famous **performance-capture** story: McCartney came in **early each morning for about a week and sang it once, fresh**, deliberately wearing his voice down to get that raw, cracked, screaming quality — he wanted it to sound like he'd been performing it on stage for weeks. The lesson is that *the performance* is the production.

- Heavily compressed, roomy, vintage-voiced mix to match the pastiche.
- Restrained band under a huge lead vocal.

## Steal this
- **Wear the voice in:** to get a raw/worn lead, re-sing it fresh over multiple days rather than comping a rested take — capture fatigue on purpose.
- **Genre-pastiche production:** commit fully to an era's mix (heavy comp, room, vintage EQ — [[UAD UA 1176LN]], [[Kramer Tape]]).
- **Performance over perfection:** a cracked, committed take beats a clean one.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Oh! Darling]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #180.*

**Form.** A hybrid: the emotional weight of slow heavy blues grafted onto the **form and chord vocabulary of a wall-climbing doo-wop ballad**. For all the blues surface the architecture is the standard pop two-bridge model with one verse between, and **not one section matches a twelve-bar frame**. Slow ternary meter is genuinely rare in their catalogue ([[RHY-C01 — Compound meter is felt in dotted beats]]). **Effectively no intro and no outro** — both are a single gesture. Inside the eight-bar verse the lyric scans **4 + 1 + 3**, cutting across an otherwise four-square section; the bridge is a plain 2 × 4.

**Harmony.** `Intro: V+ (E augmented, entering on beat 3)` · `Verse (8): I – V – vi – IV · ii–V · ii–V – I–IV – I–V` · `Bridge (8): IV – ♭VI – I (2) · V/V (2) – V–♭VI – V` · `Outro: … ♭II7 – I7`. Spell the intro chord E – G♯ – B♯, not with an E♭ — the B♯ is there to push up into the C♯ of the A chord. Both sections **accelerate their harmonic rhythm toward the end**, and the transitions are handled two different ways: verse 1 ends on V to motivate the repeat, while verses running into a bridge sustain the A chord and let **I re-read as V-of-IV**. The chord worth the price of admission is the bridge's "F7": written with **D♯ on top rather than E♭** it becomes a **German augmented sixth**, which he derives as an altered IV rather than a ♭VI ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]) — both outer voices resolve chromatically outward onto the same note.

**Notable.** Blues colour comes from heavy stress on ♭3 and ♭7, and **scale degree 4 is almost banished** — absent from the verse entirely. The best study material is one two-note leaning figure, F♯ → E, introduced in bar 1 and reused over four different chords so it reads as a different dissonance each time ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]). The detail worth writing down: the backing vocals use similar material in all four verses but **enter on a different bar each time — 4, then 2, then 3, then 1**.

**Against this page.** Agrees on A major and on the 12/8 doo-wop feel.

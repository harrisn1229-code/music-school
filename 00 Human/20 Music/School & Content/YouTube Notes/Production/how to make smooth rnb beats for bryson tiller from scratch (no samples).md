# how to make smooth rnb beats for bryson tiller from scratch (no samples)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZMB_sqF_Y3Q)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZMB_sqF_Y3Q)

## Synopsis

An early (2020) loner breakdown — built around Bryson Tiller's album drop that week — with a title that undersells what's actually being demonstrated: this is a **"fake sample" recipe**, building a full '90s-R&B-style chord bed entirely from layered VST electric pianos and processing it hard enough to sound genuinely sampled, rather than sampling a real record. Three electric-piano/synth layers (two Lounge Lizard Rhodes instances plus a Wurlitzer V2) each get a *different* processing recipe — tape/chorus/Juno-layered heaviest on layer one, light reverb/delay/EQ on the already-watery Wurlitzer, EQ + mid-side widening on the third — then a wordless four-bar vocal chop gets the classic loner AlterBoy pitch-up/formant-down treatment (here mis-captioned "Autotune boy," joining a long list of AlterBoy homophones already tracked on this vault) before the whole vocal bus gets a deliberate "sampled from a record" EQ/reverb/tremolo pass. The tempo trick is the sharpest single idea in the video: build the chord bed at 80 BPM (near the reference track's felt tempo), then Warp-stretch the whole thing up to 135 BPM once the arrangement is written, rather than composing at the target tempo from the start. Drums are simple but deliberate — a shortened, pitched-down, timing-shifted hi-hat for bounce; a spaced-out kick that leans on the hats/perc for energy instead of competing with them; and an 808 that follows the chord roots with extra short ghost notes layered in so it never locks 1:1 with the kick. The arrangement itself is a clean subtraction/addition structure (reverse-keys intro → sparse-then-full verse → vocals-out hook with a pitched-up instrumental standing in for the vocal). A tight, information-dense 11:34 with concrete settings for nearly every stage despite its age.

## Techniques

**Chord bed — three independently-processed electric-piano layers (the "fake sample" trick):**
- **Layer 1 — Lounge Lizard Rhodes, basic 4-chord progression, the heaviest-processed layer of the three.** Chain: [[Cassette]] (tape/lo-fi coloration) → **chorus** ([[TAL-Chorus-LX]], owned-lane match) → a heavy reverb → a heavy delay → EQ (cut the lows and some of the mids) → a **Juno emulation, run "quite heavy"** — called out on-screen as "actually a free plugin, so definitely go get that" (unowned; owned stand-in is [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]], see [[Plugin Wishlist]]). → [[BED Slot]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Layer 2 — Wurlitzer V2 (Arturia Wurli V), a "watery"/already-washed-out patch, kept purely as background texture.** Lighter chain than layer 1 since the source is already soft: reverb, delay, EQ. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[BED Slot]]
- **Layer 3 — a SECOND Lounge Lizard instance, added specifically "for more depth," running the same chord pattern transposed to each different key of the progression.** Chain: EQ → **mid-sides** ([[Ghz Midside 3]], owned-lane match) — "makes it wider." → [[Width and Stereo]], [[BED Slot]]
- 🎬 general lesson: **stack multiple instances of the same source instrument (here two separate Lounge Lizard loads) rather than one layer doing everything** — each instance gets its own distinct processing chain and register/transposition, so three "Rhodes" reads as one lush, wide chord bed instead of three duplicate parts. → [[Layering and Stacking]]

**Vocal chop (used as a texture/sample element, not a lead vocal):**
- Recorded a **4-bar wordless vocal run** over the chords, deliberately using nonsense/placeholder lyrics ("I titled it 'Please Me the FOP'... just going to use that to kind of be some stupid lyrics around") since the vocal's job is texture, not a real topline.
- Chain: **[[Little AlterBoy]]** ("Autotune boy" in the auto-captions — the same recurring AlterBoy homophone already tracked across this channel's other videos) — pitch shifted **all the way up**, Formant brought **down a little** → EQ (cut the lows AND the highs) → a reverb specifically "to kind of drown it out, space it out." → [[Pitch and Formant]], [[LEAD Slot]]
- **Harmony/chop layer:** a second pass of "little vocal chops" layered behind the main vocal run, "same stuff really" — AlterBoy → reverb → delay. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]

**Consolidation — bounce before you manipulate:**
- **Bounced the two vocal stems into one stem, and the three keys layers into one stem**, explicitly "so it's easier to... manipulate and reverse and do all that kind of stuff to it" — consolidating a multi-layer stack down to one or two stems BEFORE reversing/chopping/stretching, rather than trying to keep every layer manipulable independently. → [[Layering and Stacking]]

**Vocal bus — a deliberate "sampled from a record" finishing pass:**
- New mixer channel for the consolidated vocal stem. Cut out **a lot of the frequencies** (heavy EQ, both highs and lows) → reverb → **tremolo panning the result side to side** ([[Ghz Trem Control 3]], owned-lane match).
- 🎬 general lesson, stated directly on-screen: **"if you're sampling a '90s R&B record, you're going to have to cut loads of the highs out and loads of the lows out"** — heavy dual-end EQ cut + reverb + tremolo is presented as a specific recipe for making a from-scratch part read as if it were actually lifted off a period record, not just "vintage-flavored." Distinct from (and more extreme than) the restraint-based "match the age of the processing to the reference era" lesson already logged on [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] for a *2000s*-era build — this is the '90s-and-earlier version of the same judgment call, where heavy dual-end EQ cutting IS the era-matching move rather than something to hold back on. → [[EQ]], [[Reverb]], [[Modulation]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

**Tempo trick — write slow, stretch up:**
- Reference track: Bryson Tiller's *Always Forever*, "a lot higher tempo than this." The chord bed + vocal were written and arranged at **80 BPM**.
- Once the arrangement was locked, both the keys and vocal clips were switched to **Stretch/Warp mode**, then the project tempo was bumped to **135 BPM** — the whole bed speeds up via time-stretch rather than being composed at the final tempo from the start. Stated payoff: "it's easy to get a good bounce with the drums" once the material is already stretched into the faster pocket. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

**Drums:**
- Palette goal: **"tight, sharp, punchy"** — a rim shot + hi-hats as the core kit sounds.
- **Hi-hat bounce recipe:** the source one-shot was too long/loose, so (1) shorten the hit's tail/decay drastically (pushed nearly to max on the decay/"out" control), (2) pitch it down, and (3) nudge the note's timing off-grid (roughly half the available shift range) — the combination of a much shorter, pitched-down hit plus a deliberate timing offset is what produces the "cool bounce," not any one move alone. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Percussion:** four separate percussion layers stacked in — called out as **"where I'm getting most of the bounce from"** in the finished drum pattern, ahead of the hats. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Kick:** deliberately **spaced out/sparse**, reasoned explicitly against the hats+perc already supplying the bounce/energy rather than needing the kick to carry it too. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **808:** follows the **bass notes of the chord progression**, plus extra **short "ghost" notes layered in between the main 808 hits** specifically so "it's not just like hitting directly with the kick every time" — a deliberate rhythmic-disagreement move between kick and 808 rather than a locked 1:1 pairing. → [[Bass Design]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[SUB Slot]]
- **Lead:** Arturia Analog Lab, a **triangle-wave lead** patch, cutoff **brought down** because the raw patch "had way too many high frequencies" — described as "a very classic R&B" lead tone once darkened. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[LEAD Slot]]
- **Riser:** a percussion one-shot pitched **all the way down**, time-shifted, and **reversed** — used as a subtle riser building from the bridge into the main hook. → [[TEXTURE Slot]]
- **Reverse-keys section:** built by chopping the consolidated keys sample and reversing the chop directly (not a separate reverse instrument). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

**Arrangement:**
- **Intro:** reverse keys + bass + lead.
- **Verse:** starts sparse (keys only, no bass) → builds through the verse toward full instrumentation (adds vocals, reverse keys, lead, bass, and the perc riser) by the end.
- **Hook:** vocals pulled OUT entirely; 808 comes back in; and — the hook's one distinguishing move — the **key sample itself is bounced to audio and pitched all the way up**, standing in for the vocal as the hook's melodic variation instead of repeating the verse's vocal chop. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- 🎬 general lesson: **swapping the vocal for a pitched-up instrumental restatement of an existing melodic element is a cheap way to vary the hook** without writing new material or re-recording — the same subtraction/substitution logic already well-documented on [[Arrangement and Song Form]] from this channel, applied here with a substitution (pitched instrumental for vocal) rather than pure removal.

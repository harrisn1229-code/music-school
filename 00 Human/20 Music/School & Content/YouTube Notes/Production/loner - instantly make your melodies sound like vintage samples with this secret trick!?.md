## Synopsis

A full build-a-sample-from-scratch breakdown from loner, structured in two halves: first he layers up an "emotional, vintage" flip element by element (piano, an Analog Lab arpeggiated lead, a 3-take vocal stack, layered strings, a pluck), then — the video's actual hook — he shows the processing trick that turns those clean, freshly-recorded elements into something that reads as an actual chopped-up vintage sample rather than a modern recording. The headline trick is a recursive chop → pitch-shift → half-time bounce loop run through a slicer (FL Studio's Fruity Slicer in the source; Ableton's Simpler/Sampler "Slice to New MIDI Track" is the direct owned equivalent), applied two or three times in a row to the same source material so each pass compounds on the last. Along the way there's a real amount of concrete, reusable technique: a specific piano chain, a mirror-arpeggiation trick credited to a named collaborator (Louis of Loophole), deliberate silence-chopping, an asymmetric reverse edit, and a genuinely interesting 3-take vocal stack where the three takes are NOT identical copies but each carries a distinct role (one dry, one carrying the heavy delay, one the "default"). ## Techniques

- **Recursive chop → pitch-shift → half-time bounce loop turns one vocal phrase into three distinct "vintage sample" textures — the video's central trick and its title's "secret."** Starting from a vocal phrase trimmed with no tail (exactly filling the loop), open it in a slicer (source used FL Studio's **Fruity Slicer**; the direct Ableton-native path is **Simpler/Sampler's "Slice to New MIDI Track"**, already documented on [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]) on a fresh instrument track: pitch the sliced instrument **up a full octave**, raise the attack, then re-chop/reorder/reverse individual slices at the original tempo — this alone leaves more room for other elements while keeping the vocal's texture/feeling. Bounce that result to one audio track, then **half-time it** (stretches the chopped material and completely changes its texture — "that sounds crazy") for **sample 1**. Repeat the whole loop on the sample-1 bounce — a new slicer instance, this time pitched **down 7 semitones** ("the lower you pitch it, the better it sounds, I think") — then half-time again for **sample 2**. For **sample 3**, skip the pitch move and instead **double the underlying MIDI notes' tempo** before half-timing. Each pass compounds on the last; the payoff is three genuinely different-sounding textures from one source phrase. Owned equivalents: [[Simpler]]/[[Sampler]] Slice to New MIDI Track for the chop step, Warp-mode time-stretching (or doubling clip length and re-warping) for the half-time step. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Arpeggiated, strummed-out piano chords plus a separate top-line "run-down" melody, for a more natural, less static feel straight away.** Both elements are cited as the very first thing added — arpeggiation over block chords, and a small descending melodic run layered above the chords for extra flavor and ear interest before any processing happens. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **A named "go-to piano chain": Waves King's Microphones (character mic emulation) → a Waves EQ cutting low/boosting high → a second Waves EQ → a short, very light reverb.** Stated explicitly as his default/repeatable piano chain, not a one-off — no owned equivalent for the mic-character-emulation stage specifically. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[EQ]], [[Reverb]]
- **A "dirty lead" patch (Arturia Analog Lab, Impulse bank) arpeggiated with the SAME notes as the piano chords but run in the opposite (descending) direction** — a mirror-image arpeggiation move credited directly on screen to Louis of Loophole (see also [[flipping randomly generated samples into the hardest beats!? w theloopholeco]], a separate loner/Loophole collab already in the vault) — Loophole also runs a paid sound-design course, name-dropped as a resource. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **3-effect lead chain: Cassette's "crush" preset → a local EQ → two ValhallaDelay instances at different note subdivisions (16th notes + 8th notes) layered together**, for a busier, dirtier repeat pattern on the Analog Lab lead than one clean delay would give. → [[Cassette]], [[ValhallaDelay]]
- **Chop deliberately around silence — even tiny gaps read as "sample," and duplicating a note adds a choppier feel.** On the piano part, small silences were cut into the arpeggiated chords at several points (including right before the end), and one note was duplicated in place to break up the rhythm further — framed as one of the core moves for making a freshly-recorded part sound like an actual chopped-up vintage sample rather than a clean live take. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Cut a synth lead's opening bars, then reverse just the tail so it swells INTO the loop restart instead of playing straight through.** The first part of the Analog Lab lead was removed entirely to leave room for other elements, but the ending was kept and reversed so it builds/swells right up to where the loop repeats — a deliberate asymmetric edit (cut the head, reverse the tail), not a whole-part reverse. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **A 3-take vocal stack (dry center + hard-panned left/right) built for an "emotional, vintage" vibe, where the takes are NOT identical copies — each earns a distinct role.** All three share a base chain — light Auto-Tune, a light formant/pitch-type effect (name unclear in captions, "Ultra Boy" — possibly [[Little AlterBoy]] given the light-touch role), a very light [[ValhallaVintageVerb|Valhalla Vintage Verb]], [[Decapitator]] "just to bring it out a little bit," and an EQ pass (cut low, boost high) — but the **right-panned take alone carries a heavy delay** plus one more effect unclear in captions ("a clever"), flagged in the video itself as important to a later trick, while the **left-panned take stays drier, with no reverb**, for contrast against the other two. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Auto-Tune Pro]], [[Decapitator]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]]
- **Spitfire Audio Augmented Strings' "Space Granules" preset layered with a second, different Augmented Strings preset**, for a string texture layer in the flip — two presets from the same instrument stacked rather than one string patch doing the whole job. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **A pluck (from an unnamed sample kit, auto-captioned "5ive and XYZ kit" — vendor unconfirmed) pushed through a super-heavy full-mix reverb + heavy delay + EQ (cut low, cut high), voiced by copying the progression's first chord and re-voicing it slightly differently** rather than writing a new pluck line from scratch — reusing harmony already established in the piano part, just recoloured for a different instrument. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]

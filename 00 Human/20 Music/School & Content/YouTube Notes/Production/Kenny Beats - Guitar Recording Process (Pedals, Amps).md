# Kenny Beats — Guitar Recording Process (Pedals, Amps)

![](https://youtu.be/VruZ-A0_aZk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/VruZ-A0_aZk)

## Synopsis

Kenny walks his studio answering "explain your guitar chain": the answer is **there is no fixed chain — it's a decision tree run fresh every time** (what guitar, what's working in the room, what the song wants, how fast he needs to move). The valuable part is the tree itself plus his current defaults: real low-watt amp or DI-through-a-preamp over amp sims lately, a small Fender as the most-recorded amp, and a tour of the pedals he actually reaches for (with which ones are sleepers). Directly relevant to [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track|Electric Guitar Amp Track]] / [[1 Electric Guitar DI Track|Electric Guitar DI Track]] parts 1–3.

## Techniques

- **Run the decision tree before every guitar take** — what guitar is available/working, what the song wants, then: DI + amp-sim plugins for speed? real amp? pedals → DI? pedals → amp? one mic or two mics balanced? which mic — RE20, [[Shure SM57]], Royer 121, Royer pair? Choose consciously each time instead of defaulting. → [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track|Electric Guitar Amp Track]], [[1 Electric Guitar DI Track|Electric Guitar DI Track]]
- **Current lane: no amp sims** — lately skips Guitar Rig / Neural DSP entirely ([[Guitar Rig 7 FX]] / [[Archetype- John Mayer X]] are the owned equivalents when speed wins); either records a real amp or goes **DI through a preamp or an old mixer** into the computer, then EQs in the box. Character on the way in, surgery in the DAW. → [[1 Electric Guitar DI Track|Electric Guitar DI Track]]
- **Most-recorded amp = 1964/65 Fender Blackface Vibro Champ** — tiny, low watts, no reverb, "even at a really low volume just sounds so good." The lesson: a small vintage-voiced Fender driven quietly beats a big amp for recording (Nate's [[Fender Blues Junior]] is the same lane). → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Peavey Decade for distorted tones** — the cheap solid-state sleeper for dirt sounds. *(alt-amp color, not a purchase prompt)*
- **Fuzz DI, no amp** — Swollen Pickle (big muff-style fuzz, Nate's [[Big Muff Pi]] is the same family) straight into the preamp with no amp at all "sounds amazing." → [[1 Electric Guitar DI Track|Electric Guitar DI Track]]
- **Tape/echo boxes on guitar** — Fulltone Tube Tape Echo + Binson Echorec + Space Echo on top of the amp, Sansui spring reverb; hardware echo as part of the tone, not a mix send. Owned equivalents: [[EchoBoy]] (tape mode), [[ValhallaVintageVerb]], the [[Sony TC-630]] print loop. → [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track|Electric Guitar Amp Track]]
- **Chase Bliss Generation Loss = "the one best pedal ever"** — degraded-tape character; he saw AJ Hall run it on **drums** and it sounded amazing. Owned equivalent: [[RC-20 Retro Color]] / [[Sony TC-630]]. → [[1 Drums FX Track|Drums FX Track]]
- **Mu-Tron envelope filter on bass** — the funky auto-wah; he uses it **way less drastic** than the classic quack — subtle envelope movement, "just about the best on bass." Owned approximation: [[Envelope Follower]] → filter, or wah pedal played gently. → [[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Electric Bass DI Track]]
- **MXR Dyna Comp — "very very slept on"** — a little compressor pedal in front of the chain for squash/sustain before dirt. Owned equivalent: a [[Compressor]]/[[FETish]] first-in-chain on DI takes.
- **MXR Uni-Vibe — the sleeper mod pedal** — "a very unique sound"; chorus/phase hybrid wobble. Owned equivalent: [[Chorus-Ensemble]] or [[ValhallaSpaceModulator]].
- **Keep a grab-and-go pedalboard on the floor** — a standing board (Strymon Sunset, mini wah, Uni-Vibe, Chase Bliss Habit, Mu-Tron) just so guitar ideas get captured fast with character already printed; a second board feeds all the keyboards too — **pedals aren't guitar-only**. → [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track|Electric Guitar Amp Track]], [[2 Inst-Synth Group|Keys & Synths]] lane
- **Weird pedals as idea generators** — Chase Bliss Habit (echo/looper) "I end up doing shit every time I never would have thought of"; Fjord Fuzz (Unknown Mortal Orchestra's boxes) for fuzz variants with octave/envelope surprises. The point: one chaos box in the chain writes parts for you.

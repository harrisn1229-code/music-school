# Old Brown Shoe — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4-bar vamp on I7) · Verse (16) · Verse · Bridge (12, AAB) · Verse (**instrumental**) · Bridge · Verse · Outro (a single reprise of the verse's last phrase, then 20-plus bars of I7 vamping to the fade). The two-bridge model with an instrumental middle verse is a shape Pollack cannot find anywhere else in the official catalogue.

Nominally C major, but the verse habitually slides into the relative minor and has to be hauled back: its last phrase reads in A minor as VI – V – i, a pivot that dies too quickly to really call a modulation ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]). Harmonic rhythm starts very slow and **accelerates across the final two phrases** ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]).

The ♭VI gets unusual prominence and makes a hard cross-relation with the A naturals in the IV chords on either side of it ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]) — Harrison's habitual wilting-downward gesture, as in "If I Needed Someone" and "While My Guitar Gently Weeps". The bridge revolves entirely around V, and that is what asserts C major as home at all.

The sharpest detail: a **C is held as a pedal point in the treble rather than the bass** across the verse ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]), and that pedal is the reason the E chord has to be augmented — the C occupies the slot where a conventional B would sit. Jangle piano, organ, lead guitar and a very heavy bass make an uncanny composite at an extreme up-tempo full of triplets.

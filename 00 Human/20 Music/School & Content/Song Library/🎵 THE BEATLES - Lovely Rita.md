# Lovely Rita — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

The first properly rock-shaped thing on the album in a long while, placed as relief after the Indian, vaudeville and music-hall detours. The analytical interest is that **root motion by fourths and fifths does nearly all the work**: the verse runs the "Hey Jude" progression, and the bridge sets off down an actual non-diatonic circle of fifths, stretching the key almost to breaking before abandoning it.

**Form.** Pollack flags the section *labels* as arguable rather than the boundaries — what he calls the verse could be a refrain, and what he calls the bridge could be the verse. The intro is 8 measures thickened by staggered entrances; the bridge runs **14 measures where 16 is what you expect**; the outro takes roughly a third of the record.

**Harmony.** The intro converges on I; both verse phrases converge on **V** instead, and that change of target is what separates the sections despite a shared vocabulary. The bridge is the real event: a *diatonic* circle of fifths would stay inside the key and return home in seven chords, while the *non-diatonic* version ignores the key signature, makes every chord major, and needs twelve. The bridge starts down that road, bails out at **♭III**, jumps back to I, and leaves chromatic false relations in its wake ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]], [[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]) — three or four chords out is enough to unmoor the key, and the abrupt snap home is the effect. Two spots are prolongations rather than progressions: the verse before the piano solo is an envelope around V dressed in ninths, elevenths and thirteenths that don't change its function, and the outro's long vamp boils down to **i–iv–i in the relative minor**, so the song ends in a key it never established ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]).

**Arrangement.** Conservative on paper and then covered wall to wall in effects: ADT set so the lead vocal reads as a peculiar *single* rather than a normal double, stray spoken remarks left on the backing track, an electronically altered kazoo, distant pristine backing vocals. **Maracas are held back until the second phrase of each bridge** — one percussion colour reserved for half a section rather than a new part written. The piano solo is very likely George Martin using the "In My Life" method, played an octave down at half speed.

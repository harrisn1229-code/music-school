# Stella By Starlight — Victor Young

> A jazz standard famous for sounding harmonically "mysterious" — it doesn't reach its own home key (B♭ major) until bar 9, and its opening move (a chord that seems to resolve nowhere sensible) is one of the most commonly reharmonized passages in the standard repertoire. Jens Larsen's full harmonic breakdown demystifies it: almost the entire tune is a chain of secondary/auxiliary dominants, backdoor cadences, and one reharmonized diminished chord, all still inside a single key — with only **one genuine modulation** in the whole form. Full worked breakdown: [[Analyzing a Standard - Stella By Starlight - Functional Harmony in Jazz -  Jazz Guitar Lesson]].

## The opening — reharmonized diminished, not a broken cadence

The tune's famous opening (Em7♭5–A7 resolving into Cm7–F7) *looks* non-functional — there's no real dominant-to-target relationship between A7 and Cm7 on paper. It's actually a **reharmonization of a ♯4° diminished chord** (E° in the key of B♭), the same "♯4°/subdominant diminished, reharmonized as a ii-V" device documented at length on [[Diminished Chords]] — Larsen names this exact tune there as one of the standards sharing the device, alongside "I Remember You" (identical cadence, identical melody-scale-degree: the leading tone of the key sitting in the melody). Practical improvising fix: don't chase the theory while soloing — target the notes A7 naturally resolves to (D or F; F also happens to be the tune's own melody note over the following Cm7).

## Chords

*Chord-function map, as analyzed — roman numerals in B♭.*

**Opening statement (auxiliary-cadence chain, never really leaving B♭ despite sounding unmoored):**
`Em7♭5–A7 (♯4° reharmonized, cadences "in D minor") → Cm7 (ii) – F7 (V) → Fm7–B♭7 (auxiliary ii-V into IV, decorating an already-diatonic chord) → E♭maj7 (IV) – A♭7 (backdoor dominant, ♭VII7) → B♭maj7 (I, finally arrives at bar 9) – Dm7 (iii) → [repeat of the opening ♯4° device] → Cm7♭5 (ii, borrowed half-diminished from the parallel minor) – F7 (V)`

**The one real modulation, just before the bridge:** `Fm (iv) → B♭m7 (heard as iv of the visiting key) → E♭7 → F major (brief true tonic, up a 5th from B♭)` — falls back toward B♭/D minor almost immediately after arriving.

**Bridge:** `G7 (looks augmented on the chart, but the melody's natural 5th confirms it's a plain auxiliary dominant to ii, not whole-tone) → Cm7 (ii, plain subdominant, not part of a ii-V) → E♭m6/A♭7 (iv-minor "backdoor," the way home from a bare subdominant)`

**Closing sequence (last A):** `Em7♭5–A7 → Dø7–D7 → Cm7♭5–F7 (a single ii-V shape restated as a descending sequence before the true resolution) → Cm7♭5–F7♭9 (final cadence, both chords borrowed from the parallel minor, B♭ minor) → B♭maj7 (I)`

## Steal this

- **When the melody's own final note doesn't confirm the key, fall back on the key signature + final chord** — Stella is one of the rare standards whose melody doesn't end on the tonic note, so the key has to be confirmed from the last chord (B♭maj7) and the key signature instead. Extends the "confirm key from the ending" method already on [[🎵 JEROME KERN - All The Things You Are]] one step further. → [[Keys]], [[Cadences]]
- **A tune can sound harmonically "busy" and mysterious purely from stacked secondary/auxiliary dominants, without ever actually leaving its home key.** Before concluding a tune "has no key," check whether an unfamiliar chord is a real modulation or just a decorated approach to a diatonic chord you already know. → [[Secondary Dominants]], [[Chord Progressions]]
- **The sharp-4 diminished device isn't just a passing chord here — it also works as a genuine modulation tool, cadencing up a major 3rd into a new key.** Cited elsewhere doing exactly that: "I'm Old Fashioned," "I Love You" (Cole Porter). → [[Modulation (Key Change)]], [[Diminished Chords]]
- **A iv-minor chord can pivot a brief modulation up to the fifth degree** — an unusual but real device (B♭ → F major here), also used in "My Foolish Heart." → [[Modulation (Key Change)]]
- **Don't trust an "augmented"-looking chord symbol on sight — check the melody before reaching for whole-tone.** The bridge's G7 looks written for a whole-tone/altered treatment, but the melody's plain natural 5th confirms it's just a secondary dominant into a minor ii chord, soloed with harmonic minor instead. → [[Scale Modes Reference]], [[Secondary Dominants]]
- **Turn a single ii-V into a descending sequence to prolong the arrival before resolving** — reusable in original writing/reharmonization. → [[Chord Progressions]]

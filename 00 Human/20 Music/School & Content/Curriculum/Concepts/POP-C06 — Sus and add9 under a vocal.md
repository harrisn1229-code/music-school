# POP-C06 — Sus and add9 under a vocal

**Q.** Why voice pads as sus2/sus4/add9 rather than plain triads under a vocal? And what does a topline landing on the 9th add?

> [!question]- Answer
> Sus and add9 voicings **remove or blur the 3rd** — the note defining major or minor — so the pad stops colliding with the topline and lets the vocal define the harmony. A **topline on the 9th** adds colour without changing function: the 9th isn't in the triad, so it sounds open and floating rather than settled.

## Why it matters

Both blank. These are the two voicing decisions that matter most in loop-based music, because the same voicing is heard dozens of times. A plain triad under a busy vocal fights it.

## Drill

Take a pad under a vocal. Voice it as a triad, then sus2, then add9. Note which one you stop noticing.

# 4 Useful Chord Progressions You Should Know

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pejqyjTuJHo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pejqyjTuJHo)

## Synopsis

Four more named progressions, each tied to a signature song, framed around the emotional character each one evokes — a Picardy-third uplift trick, a jazzy circle-of-fifths soul progression, a relaxed closed loop, and the progression at the center of the 2023 "Thinking Out Loud" vs. "Let's Get It On" lawsuit.

## Techniques

- **"Kiss From a Rose" progression — ♭VI–♭VII–I major** (E♭–F–G in G) — sets up like a minor-key progression, then resolves to the MAJOR tonic instead of the expected minor one: a **Picardy third**. Also pure parallel motion (three major triads, each a whole tone apart, every note moving together) for a bold, striking sound. Starting the same three chords on I instead of ♭VI is the "Super Mario Cadence" variant. Song: "Kiss from a Rose" (Seal); also used in the Super Mario games.
- **"Isn't She Lovely" progression — vi–II–V–I, extended** (Am7–D9–G11 or G9sus4–C) — a circle-of-fifths root movement (each chord a 5th from the previous) dressed up with upper-structure extensions for a soulful, jazzy pull to the tonic. Song: "Isn't She Lovely" (Stevie Wonder).
- **"What's Up" progression — I–ii–IV–I** (C–Dm–F–C) — a closed, laid-back loop; it climbs I→ii→IV but never touches V (the chord with real pull home), so ii and IV read as unhurried rather than urgent — relaxed character despite still resolving cleanly. Song: "What's Up" (4 Non Blondes).
- **"Let's Get It On" progression — I–iii–IV–V** (E♭–Gm–A♭–B♭7) — the progression at the center of the 2023 Ed Sheeran "Thinking Out Loud" lawsuit; noted explicitly on-camera that Sheeran's actual chord progression differs from this one despite the claimed similarity. Song: "Let's Get It On" (Marvin Gaye).

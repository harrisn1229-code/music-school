# Songs that use the "She's Electric" chord progression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mUYgS4IP_TU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mUYgS4IP_TU)

## Synopsis

A close relative of the Axis Progression — same 4-chord shape, but the usual V chord is replaced with a secondary dominant of vi, giving it extra forward pull. Bennett calls it "the secondary dominant Axis progression."

## Techniques

- **The progression: I–III–vi–IV** (C–E–Am–F, spoken as "1-3-6-4") — compare the plain Axis Progression's I–V–vi–IV.
- **III functions as V of vi** (a secondary dominant) rather than a plain V — E major (borrowed, chromatic — E minor would be diatonic) pulls specifically toward vi (Am) with real dominant force, adding drive in the first two chords that the plain Axis progression's I→V doesn't have in quite the same way.
- **The borrowed chromatic note (G♯, from E major) creates a rising line** G→G♯→A gluing the first three chords together.
- **Common variation 1**: IV→iv, a melancholy minor-plagal tag at the end — heard in Green Day's "Murder City" and Billie Eilish's "Happier Than Ever."
- **Common variation 2**: vi→V→IV, stepping down through an extra chord instead of jumping straight to IV — heard in David Bowie's "Lady Stardust."
- **Song**: "She's Electric" (Oasis).

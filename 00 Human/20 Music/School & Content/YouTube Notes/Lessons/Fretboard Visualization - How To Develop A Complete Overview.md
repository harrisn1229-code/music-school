# Fretboard Visualization - How To Develop A Complete Overview

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U57VYYC-PMs)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U57VYYC-PMs)

## Synopsis
Jens Larsen lays out a four-step practice method for building a genuine fretboard overview — not by drilling more scales and arpeggios in isolation, but by taking one song you already know cold and forcing yourself to improvise the *entire* form inside one general area of the neck. The core claim is counterintuitive and stated as the video's whole thesis: restricting yourself to one position is one of the best ways to eventually become free across the whole neck, because the restriction forces you to find genuinely practical, playable solutions with material you already have, rather than reaching for a habitual lick or blaming a gap in your scale/arpeggio knowledge. He demonstrates the method using **Lady Bird** (chosen for its short 16-bar form, letting him run full choruses quickly) around a neck area centered near C, and walks through choosing the song, prepping the position, playing and troubleshooting the changes in real time (including a concrete substitution decision on the A♭maj7→Am7 turnaround), and finally a set of restricted-material solo passes (chord-tones-only, scale-only, third-of-chord-only, pentatonic-only) designed to force new vocabulary out of the same confined area. Short (10 minutes) and dense — no wasted material, structured as a repeatable four-step process rather than a one-off demonstration. This is a companion/prerequisite video to Larsen's separate "system for visualizing the fretboard" (referenced at the end but not yet in this vault as its own note) — this video is the *drill*, that one is presumably the *organizing framework* the drill feeds into.

## What's taught

The full four-step process, worked step by step with concrete Lady Bird substitutions, has been extracted into its own drillable practice note: **[[One-Position Fretboard Overview Drill]]**. Summary of the four steps:

1. **Choose the song and the position** — a tune you already know by heart, and a general neck *area* (not a rigid scale-position box) suited to its key. Lady Bird, around C.
2. **Prep the position before improvising** — play the song's chord voicings through the full progression confined to the area first, plus a short generic drill (Larsen namechecks "the Barry Harris exercise") to get oriented. Meant to become a recurring habit across many songs over time, not a one-off warm-up.
3. **Play the song in the position and troubleshoot** — solo the full form in the area, notice which chords/transitions are difficult, and for the hard ones, inventory every available option (arpeggio, related minor arpeggio, triads, pentatonic) rather than assuming you need to "practice the arpeggio more." Worked live on Lady Bird's A♭maj7→Am7 turnaround: when the literal A♭maj7 arpeggio doesn't sit well in the position, sub in an F minor or C minor arpeggio instead (both relative-minor-family substitutes for the same Maj7 sound).
4. **Build new material inside the constraint** — a rotation of restricted solo passes over the same form, still confined to the position: chord-tones only, scale-movement only, starting every arpeggio from the 3rd instead of the root, and pentatonic-only. Each restriction removes the usual melodic default and forces new solutions.

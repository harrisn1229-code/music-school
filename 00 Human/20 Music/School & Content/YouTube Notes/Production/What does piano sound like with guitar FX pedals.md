# What does piano sound like with guitar FX pedals?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21l6OG-91Bc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21l6OG-91Bc)

## Synopsis

A hands-on demo running a Nord piano's grand-piano sound through three classic guitar pedals — DigiTech Whammy, Big Muff fuzz, Vox wah — with real settings, notable users, and a specific technique fix for making distortion work on a dense chordal instrument.

## Techniques

- **Whammy pedal on piano — interval choice matters a lot.** Set to an octave, the pedal shift is dramatic even from small pedal movement, since the top/bottom positions land back on the same chord — described as the "musical, easy to deal with" setting. Dropping the interval to a major 2nd instead allows much subtler, more controllable pitch movement for expressive nudges rather than big jumps. The "dive bomb" mode drops pitch 5-6 octaves for a dying-machine effect. Notable users on guitar: Tom Morello (Rage Against the Machine), Johnny Greenwood (Radiohead).
- **Fuzz (Big Muff) on piano needs a technique change, not just a settings change.** Distortion emphasizes overtones, and a piano's full-chord sound already has a dense overtone stack from multiple simultaneous notes — running that through fuzz produces excessive top-end fuzziness/mud. **Fix: play fewer notes at once** — alternate single notes between left and right hand rather than playing full chords, which keeps the distorted signal legible instead of collapsing into noise. A directly transferable lesson for any dense/chordal source going into heavy distortion, not just piano.

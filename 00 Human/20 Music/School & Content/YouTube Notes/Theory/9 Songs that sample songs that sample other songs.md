# 9 Songs that sample songs that sample other songs

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j2W7HTLrvkQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j2W7HTLrvkQ)

## Synopsis

Multi-hop sampling chains — a hit built on a sample of a song that is itself a sample of a still-earlier song — extending Bennett's regular sampling coverage with 9 documented three-generation lineages.

## Techniques

- **Multi-hop sample chains, documented**: Jay-Z & T.I.'s "Swagger Like Us" ← M.I.A.'s "Paper Planes" ← The Clash's "Straight to Hell" (1982). Lady Gaga's "Poker Face" ← Boney M.'s "Ma Baker" (1977) ← the traditional folk song "Sina" (specifically Muhammad's 1975 version). Jeremih's "Don't Tell 'Em" ← Snap!'s "Rhythm Is a Dancer" (1992) ← Nu Cleus's "Automan" (1984) — with a **production technique embedded in the middle hop**: Snap! significantly slowed the "Automan" sample, which lowered its pitch as a direct consequence (a reminder that time-stretching/slowing a sample changes its pitch unless pitch-locked). Kanye West's "Stronger" ← Daft Punk's "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger" (2001) ← Edwin Birdsong's "Cola Bottle Baby" (1979).
- **Reusable framing for tracing a sample's lineage**: a sample's "source" is often itself a sample — worth checking whether the thing you're about to clear or sample-flip has an earlier generation behind it, both for crate-digging inspiration and for understanding the full clearance chain.

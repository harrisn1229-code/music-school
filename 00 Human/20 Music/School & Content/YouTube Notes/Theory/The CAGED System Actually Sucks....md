# The CAGED System Actually Sucks...

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QT44Vye-uSY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QT44Vye-uSY)

## Synopsis

A fast, opinionated CAGED primer that doubles as a critique: the standard "same chord, different shape, highest fret of one shape becomes the lowest fret of the next" continuity rule works cleanly for major chords but breaks down for minor chords, where some of the predicted shapes are either physically awkward or don't actually follow the rule. D'Eon's recommended fix is to bypass CAGED-as-crutch entirely and locate a chord's actual notes on the neck instead.

## Techniques

- **The core CAGED idea, stated minimally:** each of the 5 open-chord shapes (C, A, G, E, D) can be slid up the neck to produce the same-named chord at a higher fret — e.g. a C-shaped chord slid up 2 frets becomes a D chord.
- **Chaining shapes for a chord's other voicings:** applying all 5 shapes to one root (worked on A) produces 5 different voicings of that same chord up the neck; the rule of thumb some teachers use is "the highest fret of the previous shape becomes the lowest fret of the next."
- **Where it breaks: minor chords.** Applied to E minor, the E→D shape transition follows the rule, but the predicted C-shape E minor voicing is awkward to play and doesn't cleanly follow the continuity rule either; the G-shape E minor voicing is impractical enough that it collapses back toward playing something closer to the E shape instead.
- **Takeaway/recommendation:** CAGED is fine as an entry point but shouldn't be relied on for finding alternate voicings of a chord, especially minor chords. Instead, **know the notes that make up the chord** (e.g. F = F, A, C) and find those specific notes anywhere on the neck directly — a chord-tone-first approach rather than a shape-based one.

# This Chord Progression Technique Melts Minds

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y14WHifBvE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Y14WHifBvE)

## Synopsis
Max transcribes and analyzes a chord passage from Dirty Loops' "Next to You," extracts the underlying technique, then writes and produces a short original track built the same way. The core device: a single melody note (the tonic) is held fixed across a whole run of chords while the harmony underneath moves freely — including through non-diatonic, borrowed, and functional/approach chords — creating tension purely from the *changing relationship* between a static top note and a moving harmonic floor. This is a compositional technique note more than a reference-fact video; the transcription/analysis workflow itself is also worth capturing.

## What's taught
- **The core technique: hold the melody's tonic note fixed while the chords underneath change freely (including non-diatonic ones).** The listener's ear locks onto the static top note as "home," which frees the harmony below to wander through borrowed and functional chords without ever losing the sense of key — the fixed note keeps re-anchoring the ear. Described by Max as the reason the passage sounds simultaneously adventurous and completely coherent. This is a compositional application of the **pedal tone** concept already in [[Non-Harmonic Tones]] (a note that stays fixed while functioning as a chord tone under some chords and a non-chord tone under others) — but applied to the *melody/top voice*, where the vault's existing coverage (in [[Loop-Based Harmony]]) only documents the *bass*-pedal case. → [[Non-Harmonic Tones]], [[Loop-Based Harmony]]
- **A transcription workflow for extracting a technique from a real record**: (1) find the fixed/held note in the melody first (the "anchor" — establishes what has to stay constant), (2) transcribe the bassline next, especially useful when chords move fast and are harmonically complex — the bass narrows down the possible chord qualities before you have to guess them by ear, (3) only then fill in the full chord qualities/voicings by ear, using the top note and bass as a scaffold. Worth reusing as a general ear-transcription method beyond this specific technique. → [[Sight Reading]]
- **Modal interchange isn't limited to major/minor parallel borrowing** — Max explicitly notes the same static-melody trick works borrowing from any parallel mode (Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, not just the parallel minor), reinforcing [[Borrowed Chords]]'s framing that modal interchange sources can be any parallel mode, not only the obvious major↔minor pair.
- **Deliberately alternate root-position and inverted chords across a progression for bass-line interest** — flagged as a general principle, not just incidental to this passage: if every chord in a progression is in root position the bassline is "strong and powerful" but monotonous; switching between root position and inversions (especially 1st/3rd inversion) gives the bass notes a different character chord to chord. → [[Chord Inversions]]
- **Functional (dominant/tritone-sub) chords give a progression "linear, destination-oriented" quality even inside an otherwise wandering, modally-interchanged passage** — the analyzed passage includes a genuine V7 (B7 → E) and its tritone substitute treated as an augmented-6th-style chord (a ♭7 dominant with a ♯11, resolving smoothly home); mixing functional chords into an otherwise-freely-borrowing progression keeps it feeling directional rather than random. → [[Secondary Dominants]], [[Augmented Sixth Chords]]
- **Parallel-tenths voice leading between the bass and an inner voice** creates a smooth, consistently-consonant backbone under a harmonically busy passage — Max identifies the passage's inner-voice line moving in parallel 10ths against the bass for several chords in a row, all minor 10ths, as the glue holding the fast harmonic motion together audibly. → [[Part-Writing]]
- **Production takeaway — extract and spotlight inner voices as separate parts.** After voicing the chords, Max goes back into the chord voicings, finds the most harmonically interesting individual lines buried in the inner voices, and pulls them out to three separate synths panned hard left/center/right so those lines read clearly in the mix rather than being buried inside dense block chords — a mixing/arrangement move for making a dense modal-interchange progression legible. → [[Stereo Field]], [[Chord Inversions]]
- **A held top-voice pedal can be moved to the bass for a payoff at the end of a section** — since every chord in the progression was built to work with the tonic on top, Max also tries putting that same tonic note in the BASS instead and letting the interesting upper lines move freely over it — same harmonic material, inverted role between the anchor and the moving part, used as a distinct payoff/variation near the end of his original track. → [[Loop-Based Harmony]] (pedal point), [[Chord Inversions]]

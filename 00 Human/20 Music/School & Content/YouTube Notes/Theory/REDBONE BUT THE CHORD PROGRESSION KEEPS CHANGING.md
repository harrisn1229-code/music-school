## Synopsis

Childish Gambino's *Redbone* played on guitar with the chord progression **repeatedly reharmonized** underneath the same melody. The description says only that, but it is enough: this is a reharmonization demonstration using a song most people already have memorised, which is the single best condition for hearing what reharmonization actually does.

## What to watch for

- **The melody is the control variable.** It stays put while the harmony moves — that is the whole experiment, and it is what [[Reharmonization]] describes in prose.
- **Catalogue each substitution.** Tritone sub, relative-minor swap, modal interchange, chromatic mediant — name what you hear each time the progression turns over. [[Borrowed Chords]], [[Chromatic Mediants and Pop Modulation]].
- **Which reharmonizations survive the melody and which fight it.** Some will clash against a melody note; those failures are as instructive as the successes.
- **The original for reference:** *Redbone* is a I–vi type loop in a modal-soul idiom — establish what it actually is before hearing it altered.

## Synopsis

A no-shortcuts mindset piece: real improvisational freedom requires knowing the five major-scale-mode shapes as automatically as the minor pentatonic box, which requires real repetition (his own blunt benchmark: 1,000 reps per shape, across every key), not a quick-tip video. Nearly all of the content duplicates material already routed from this batch.

## Techniques

- **Core claim: know the modes as well as the minor pentatonic box.** Most players can visualize the minor pentatonic shape instantly, with zero conscious thought; the argument is that real improvisational maturity requires the same instant-visualization fluency across the five practicable major-scale-mode shapes (seven modes, five shapes — see [[Diatonic Modes]]).
- **The benchmark: 1,000 reps per shape, across every key**, varying how the reps are done (different intervals, different orders) rather than mechanically repeating the same fixed pattern — the same reps-plus-deliberate-variation principle already documented in [[8 Problems Intermediate Guitarists Have]]'s scale-memorization fix.
- **A worked constraint-practice example**: improvise over a backing track restricted to only the D, G, and B strings, staying in one fretboard position — a concrete instance of the constraint-based practice principle already documented as Mistake 10 in [[10 Hidden Soloing Mistakes and Constraint-Based Fixes]].

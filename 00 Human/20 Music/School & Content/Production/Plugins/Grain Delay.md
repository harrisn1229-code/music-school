# Grain Delay

**Category:** Reverb/Delay
**Vendor:** Ableton

## What it is

Ableton's stock granular delay — instead of repeating the incoming signal whole like a normal delay, it samples small snippets ("grains") of it, with the grain size set by its **Frequency** knob, and can randomize the timing/pitch of those grains as they repeat.

## What it's used for

Turning a delay into a granular texture/pitch-shift tool rather than a plain echo — metallic, glitchy, or randomized repeats distinct from [[Echo]]/[[EchoBoy]]'s rhythmic-repeat role.

## FX chain notes

Inserted on a texture or drum-layering track; often run with Time near zero (so it isn't producing an audible discrete echo) purely for the grain-texture character.

## Techniques

- **Resonator via Spray instead of delay time: short delay time + high feedback makes a tonal resonance, then Spray makes that resonance's pitch drift instead of sitting static.** Same base recipe as the plain [[Delay]] resonator trick below (short time, high feedback fuses discrete repeats into a sustained tone), but Grain Delay swaps the pitch-modulation source from delay time to **Spray** — Spray adds a random amount to each grain's delay time, so the resonance's pitch continuously shifts/moves over time. **Larger Spray → lower-pitched resonance. Lower Feedback → shorter decay.** **Grain Size** sets how fast the pitch wanders — smaller grain = faster shift. Called out as "an instant fix for tracks and arrangements that sound static" — reach for it when a section feels frequency-frozen. → [[Delay]], [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — asd, [ableton sound design | GRAIN DELAY [2/2] Applications](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xUpbxrxkJiQ)
- **A randomized, per-channel Haas effect via Spray — fixes standard Haas's "lopsided" feel.** A fixed-offset [[kHs Haas|Haas]] delays one channel slightly vs. the other, which can read lopsided since one side always lags. Grain Delay's **Spray is calculated independently per channel**, so the L/R offset is randomized and different every repeat instead of one constant lag, reading more natural/less one-sided. Mechanically identical to the resonator technique above minus feedback (keep Feedback low/off). → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Stereo Field]] — asd, [ableton sound design | GRAIN DELAY [2/2] Applications](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xUpbxrxkJiQ)
- **Bouncy/bubbly/rubbery texture: subtle Pitch + high Feedback, stacking small shifts into a rising/falling result.** A small, subtle Pitch-parameter setting alone barely changes a sound, but routed through a **high Feedback** value that small shift gets **reapplied on every repeat**, stacking many tiny shifts into a progressive rise/fall rather than one static shift — reads bouncy/rubbery. Adjust Pitch in **tiny increments** to dial the bounce speed/character. Works especially well on sources with slow mid-frequency content and soft/undefined transients (demoed on an exponential kick pattern). *(exact Feedback/Pitch numbers not recoverable — the source caption cuts off mid-sentence)* → [[TEXTURE Slot]] — asd, [ableton sound design | GRAIN DELAY [2/2] Applications](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xUpbxrxkJiQ)
- **Harmonic stacking: Pitch generates the interval, Spray/Grain Size/Random Pitch/Feedback shape its character, multiple instances stack for richer combinations.** Set **Pitch** to a musical interval to generate harmony from a tonal source. **Spray** trades uniform vs. fragmented output (higher = more fragmented); at larger Spray, **Grain Size** sets how smooth/harmonically intelligible the result reads; **Random Pitch** dials subtle-to-heavy detuning; **Feedback** stacks the interval across repeats *and* alters the original's tonal quality, not just adding copies — negative intervals stacked via Feedback are especially interesting on high-pitched sources. **Stack multiple Grain Delay instances at different Pitch values** for richer harmonic combinations than one instance produces alone. — asd, [ableton sound design | GRAIN DELAY [2/2] Applications](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xUpbxrxkJiQ)
- **Strip tonal elements before granular-texturing a drum arrangement — they smear unpredictably once Pitch/Random Pitch engage.** Before running a drum arrangement through Grain Delay's randomization for a pure-texture pass, remove tonal/melodic elements first — once pitch-related parameters are engaged, any tonal content in the source gets its own pitch randomized alongside the intended texture, muddying the result. Isolate percussive/noise material for pitch-randomization passes; treat tonal layers separately. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — asd, [ableton sound design | GRAIN DELAY [2/2] Applications](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xUpbxrxkJiQ)
- **Reframe Pitch as controlling a texture's perceived physical size/sharpness, and Feedback as its density/depth, not just "pitch shift" and "repeat count."** Visualizing a texture occupying physical space reframes **Pitch** as size/sharpness (higher = smaller/sharper, lower = larger/blunter) rather than literal pitch, and **Feedback** as thickening density and adding depth (since it stacks whatever Pitch is dialed in across repeats) rather than just a repeat-count knob — a useful mental model when sound-designing toward a physical/spatial character. Sits alongside the broader frequency+time, non-linear-change model for organic textures on [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — asd, [ableton sound design | GRAIN DELAY [2/2] Applications](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xUpbxrxkJiQ)
- **Automate the delay send up and the return's Wet/Dry wetter approaching a section change, for a "wet/dry drop" transition — then pull both back to dry after landing.** Feed a lead (or other element) into a return channel running Grain Delay (paired with [[Hybrid Reverb]] after it); as the target section approaches, automate the SEND amount up (more signal reaching the return) and the return's own Wet/Dry toward wetter, so delay throws get progressively more present, then bring both back down once the new section lands — everything reads dry again in the "normal" state. Contrast axis is wet-vs-dry rather than a filter/tonal change, and it carries a stereo-width side effect (wet reads wider, dry reads more mono/narrow). Preferred over pitching/manipulating the dry sample directly because it keeps the delay's own rhythm audible through the transition. → [[Hybrid Reverb]], [[Depth and Space]], [[Width and Stereo]] — Pick Yourself, [3 transition techniques pros use instead of risers](https://youtu.be/05A0Ez0IWAE)
- **A full parameter-by-parameter breakdown: Feedback = repeat count, Pitch = semitone shift per grain, Frequency = grain size, Spray = randomized delay-time smear** — **Feedback** controls how many times the signal repeats (same as a normal delay); **Delay Time** sets the grid spacing between repeats (16th-note grid, or free it from the grid via Sync to set a raw ms value). **Pitch** shifts each grain by semitones — 12 (an octave) and 7 (a perfect fifth) read musically, small values like 3 don't. **Frequency** sets grain size — maxed out gives tiny, metallic, heavily-processed grains; turned down gives big, "whole" grains that read natural and musical. Feedback also controls how many times that pitch-shift gets reapplied to the signal — at 0% you get one clean octave-up pass; turning Feedback up stacks multiple octaves into the result for a brighter, more layered texture. A **Random** pitch option adds a wobble; **Spray** randomizes the delay *time* (not the pitch) for a smearing, de-metallizing effect — mainly needed when Frequency is high/tiny-grain; with big grains it's barely audible. The **XY** control maps any two of these parameters (e.g. Frequency + Spray, or Delay Time + Spray) to one pad for fast two-parameter sound design. → [[Delay]] — You Suck at Producing, [Making Sounds Dreamy with Ableton's Grain Delay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Noouom1jrSY)
- **Preserve the dry original in a parallel wet/dry rack, and tame excess brightness with an Auto Filter — works on percussion too, not just melodic sources** — pair Grain Delay with an [[Auto Filter]] afterward to roll off the harsh top end the granular processing adds, then group both into an Audio Effect Rack (Cmd/Ctrl+G) and split it into two parallel Chains — one "wet" chain carrying Grain Delay + Auto Filter, one "dry" chain that's just the untouched original — so the source's own high-frequency detail stays present underneath the granular treatment instead of getting filtered away entirely. The same rack works well dropped straight onto hi-hats and other percussion, not just melodic/harmonic sources — with Sync off and Delay Time set very fast, it reads as a subtle stereo/high-frequency smear rather than an audible echo. → [[Auto Filter]] — You Suck at Producing, [Making Sounds Dreamy with Ableton's Grain Delay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Noouom1jrSY)
- **Repitch a full octave up + open spray for an instant ambient-wash recipe** — drop Grain Delay on the source early in a soundscape chain, crank the pitch slider all the way up (a full octave re-pitch), turn the frequency knob down and spray up, then dial dry/wet and feedback to taste — a fast way to turn a plain vocal or dry source into part of a lush soundscape rather than the full parameter-by-parameter build. → [[Reverb]] — You Suck at Producing, [The best FX for creating LUSH Soundscapes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wysKsyJf7dg)
- **Feedback maxed, in series after a self-modulating Phaser-Flanger, for a SOPHIE-style metallic snare** — the same short-time/Spray metallic recipe below, with **Feedback pushed all the way up** for maximum grain-repeat density, chained directly after [[Phaser-Flanger]] run as a self-modulating auto-flange (envelope-follower-driven, Sample & Hold LFO) — two different delay-family devices stacked in series compound two distinct metallic characters into one hit, tamed afterward by [[Drum Bus]] (Transients down, Crunch up, Damping maxed). → [[Snare Design]], [[Phaser-Flanger]], [[Drum Bus]] — dnksaus, [How to Metallic Snares like SOPHIE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MuD_Rq04tic)
- **Layer a second XY-automation pass on top of the first via the + button** — move the XY macro pair around to modulate two parameters at once for an instant cool sound, then press the **+** button at the top of the screen to record a *second* pass of automation over a *different* parameter pair without erasing the first (e.g. a first pass on one XY combo, a second pass with Time mode's Delay Time as the X-axis and Spray as the Y-axis). **Spray** gives its own Haas-effect-like stereo widening on top of whatever else is automated; adding a random-pitch element introduces more "bubbliness." → [[Delay]] — dnksaus, [Ableton Delay Sound Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DCIEayyv1bk)
- **Dual mouse + MPE-slide control over the X/Y position while performing a texture pass** — with Dry/Wet turned down and **Spray** up (different values land on left vs. right for stereo variation), turn up the **Slide** knob to accept MPE slide data: on an MPE controller (e.g. Push 3), sliding a finger on the pad moves one axis of Grain Delay's position while the mouse simultaneously drags the other axis — e.g. mouse on the X-axis for delay time, finger-slide on the Y-axis for frequency — letting one performer control two dimensions of the grain texture live and record the result. → [[Granulator 3]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — dnksaus, [Texturing Your Tracks in Ableton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Bab-gq4E00)
- **Metallic texture via Spray, with Time near zero** — with **Time** brought down to zero, turning up **Spray** randomly (and differently each cycle) offsets the timing of the left and right channels every time a grain/frequency cycle completes — the result reads as metallic. Pitching the **Frequency** knob around changes the grain size/texture on its own even before Spray is added. Pushing **Feedback** up and varying **Frequency** further increases the variety of the result — "short delay times make it metallic, and Grain Delay makes it more random, so the metal sounds cooler." → [[Snare Design]] — dnksaus, [craft UNIQUE snares](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WeHN9GPokMA)

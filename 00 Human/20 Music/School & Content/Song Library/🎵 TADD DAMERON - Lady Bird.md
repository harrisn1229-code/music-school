# Lady Bird — Tadd Dameron

> A 16-bar C-major standard whose turnaround (Cmaj7–**Fm7**–B♭7-ish, substituting iv-minor for the expected ii chord, with the ♭VI7 resolving directly by descending major third instead of acting as a tritone sub) is the tune's most-cited feature — and, per Jens Larsen, plausibly one of the harmonic seeds behind Coltrane's "Giant Steps" major-third cycle. Full harmonic breakdown and per-chord soloing vocabulary: [[Analysis and Jazz Guitar Solo ideas for a Jazz Standard - Lady Bird - Pentatonic, Lydian Dominant]].

## Chords (16-bar form, key of C)

`Cmaj7 | Fm7 B♭7 | E♭maj7(ish, read as borrowed ♭VI rather than a true modulation) | Am7 D7 | Dm7 G7 | Cmaj7 | (turnaround:) Fm7 B♭7(→A♭7, resolving down by major 3rd, not as a tritone sub) | ...back to Cmaj7`

- Read from a spoken chord-by-chord walkthrough (captions garbled, several chord identities reconstructed from context) — treat the exact bar-by-bar spelling above as best-effort, not a verified lead sheet; verify against a real book before performance use.
- **A♭maj7 is deliberately read as a *borrowed* ♭VI chord (from C minor), not a modulation** — Larsen's stated position in an ongoing disagreement with other analysts who do hear it as a key change. Either reading is defensible; the practical payoff of the borrowed-chord reading is that it keeps the whole form anchored in one key center rather than juggling a short-lived tonicization. → [[Borrowed Chords]]
- **The turnaround substitutes Fm7 (iv minor) for the expected ii chord** of a base [[Turnarounds|1-6-2-5]] — Larsen notes the vi slot (here effectively covered by the A-minor-family material earlier in the form) is freely interchangeable with A7 or an altered/diminished A7 with the same function, and the same interchangeability holds for G7 in the closing ii-V.
- **The A♭7 in the turnaround does NOT function as a tritone substitute of D7** (its "expected" role sitting a half step above the target) — instead it resolves directly by descending major third, the same interval-cycle engine that drives "Giant Steps." Flagged explicitly as good, low-stakes preparation vocabulary before tackling Coltrane changes.

## Soloing vocabulary (chord by chord)

- **Cmaj7 (tonic):** straight C major, kept simple — avoid reaching for outside color this early in a solo. Occasional full-chorus Lydian pass as a deliberate exception, not the default.
- **Cmaj7 → Fm7:** E minor pentatonic over the Cmaj7 (its "10th line scale"), shifted up a half step to **F minor pentatonic** the instant Fm7 lands — the same movable box slides one fret, no new shape. → [[Half-Step Pentatonic Shift Over Moving Changes]]
- **B♭7 (turnaround dominant, off the iv-minor):** Lydian dominant as the default color (heard as "Fm6 with a B♭ bass," not an independent dominant); alternate device — plane a D♭maj7 arpeggio a half step above the target and resolve down into Cmaj7. → [[Scale Modes Reference]] § Lydian Dominant
- **A♭maj7 → Am7:** work out the individual guide-tone voice leading by hand rather than reaching for a scale; also colored with diminished-family and altered-scale material because the turnaround's own half-step-up-diminished character recurs through the tune. → [[Guide Tones]], [[Diminished Chords]]
  - **Confined-position practice take on this exact transition:** when the A♭maj7 arpeggio itself is awkward to finger in whatever area you're soloing in, sub in the F minor or C minor arpeggio instead (both relative-minor-family substitutes for the same Maj7 sound — F minor is A♭'s relative minor, C minor is its iii chord); pick whichever actually sits better under the hand locally rather than defaulting to "practice the A♭maj7 arpeggio more." Full four-step drill this is worked from → [[One-Position Fretboard Overview Drill]] — Jens Larsen, [[Fretboard Visualization - How To Develop A Complete Overview]]
- **Am7:** treated identically to the Cmaj7 tonic material — functionally a rootless/related tonic extension, no separate vocabulary needed.
- **Am7 → D7:** target notes NOT present in the previous chord, avoiding the bare root over the bass; the 9th of D7 is a smooth landing target.
- **D7 (V7/V):** Lydian dominant as default (matches the general "long-sitting V7/V" rule); melodic-minor pass also demonstrated (a G♯7♭5-type shape from the same parent scale). → [[Scale Modes Reference]] § Lydian Dominant
- **D7:** the **D and E major triad pair**, deliberately mixed/alternated rather than run low-to-high. → [[Triad Pairs]]
- **E7 (later in the form):** a shell voicing rooted on the 3rd (an F major-7 shell shape) as the line's orientation anchor, plus an altered-dominant pass. → [[Chord Voicings]]
- **G7 (closing turnaround dominant):** a diminished-family triad pair (not the augmented/major type used earlier) drawn from the whole-tone-adjacent dominant/diminished scale. → [[Triad Pairs]], [[Diminished Chords]]
- **Closing turnaround (C→A♭7):** the same half-step-planing device reused from the B♭7 spot — practiced by playing the line slowly in tempo, targeting one note per chord first, then filling connective material once the skeleton holds. → [[Half-Step Pentatonic Shift Over Moving Changes]]

## Steal this

- **A "10th line" pentatonic that's a half step below the next chord's own tonic pentatonic is a free half-step-shift device** — pick the color pentatonic specifically because its relative sits one fret from where you're headed, not just because it sounds good in isolation. → [[Half-Step Pentatonic Shift Over Moving Changes]]
- **Target-note practice generalizes to any unfamiliar tune: pick reachable notes in the NEXT chord and build backward, rather than reacting to the chord you're currently on.** → [[Chord-Tone Soloing]]
- **A turnaround's "surprising" chord (here, ♭VI7 resolving by descending major third instead of as a tritone sub) is worth naming as a small dose of a bigger system (Giant Steps) — learn the small version first.** → [[Chord Progressions]]

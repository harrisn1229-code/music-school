# The #1 Reason Your Basslines Don't Hit

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PnsqtmyXyEA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PnsqtmyXyEA)

## Synopsis

A more course-style, comprehensive masterclass (Minimoog) on tailoring a bass sound to fit a specific track rather than settling for a preset — the thesis being "listen to your song, find what's missing, and use your synth's resources to supply it." Covers oscillator-count/detune, resonance tradeoffs, self-feedback distortion, harmonic layering, keyboard tracking, and performance.

## Techniques

- **Single oscillator = most stable pitch.** Two identical oscillators at the same pitch phase-cancel unpredictably note to note (a repeated line reads inconsistently loud/soft); a single oscillator avoids this entirely.
- **For unison width, use an ODD oscillator count (3, not 2)** — one centered, one detuned down, one detuned up — thickens the tone without the same phase-cancellation inconsistency two oscillators produce.
- **Resonance thins the fundamental — restore it with a low-level SINE oscillator at the same pitch.** Demonstrated A/B: pushing resonance for "personality" measurably loses low end; blending in a quiet sine wave at the same octave stop brings the bass weight back without giving up the resonant character.
- **A harmonic (octave-up) oscillator adds MIDDLE fullness distinct from brightness** — added at low level, it doesn't make the patch read brighter, but audibly fills out the tone; harmonic content and perceived brightness are separable controls, worth reaching for one without the other.
- **Self-patched feedback distortion**: route the synth's own low/output signal back into its external input, then blend that returned signal in — a loop-based distortion source using only the instrument itself, no outboard pedal.
- **Keyboard tracking switches explained concretely**: fractional tracking options (1/3 and 2/3, combinable for full tracking) — off for bass so upper-register notes don't get artificially bright, matching how a real bass doesn't brighten going up the neck.
- **Decay time set to the track's tempo/subdivision leaves deliberate silence** for other elements to occupy — a quarter-note vs. half-note decay changes how much space is left between hits, not just how "thumpy" the bass feels.
- **Softened filter attack for a "funkier," more personality-laden onset**, and glide/phrasing (not just MIDI-quantized triggering) for the part to actually "talk" rather than read mechanically.

# THIS Makes Your Riffs MUCH Heavier! (Secret Power Of Dotted Notes)

## Synopsis

BERNTH teaches one rhythmic device — the dotted quarter note — as a riff-writing trick for heavier, more "bouncing" modern metal riffs, then gives a concrete four-stage practice method for internalizing it against a click rather than just following a drummer/backing track. Demoed twice (standard tuning, then 7-string in A standard) with a basic straight-eighths comparison riff to make the effect audible.

## What's taught

- **Dotted quarter note = quarter tied to an eighth (1.5 beats), equivalent to 3 eighth notes.** Using it as the primary rhythmic unit for a riff makes the heavy accents land off the beat rather than squarely on it, producing a "floating"/bouncing feel that's common in modern metal riffing — demonstrated A/B against a plain on-the-beat version of the same riff idea, which reads noticeably more basic/boring by comparison.
- **Practice method for nailing it against a click (not the drummer/backing track):** think in an eighth-note subdivision grid at all times, then progress through four stages on the same rhythm: (1) play straight eighth notes in groups of three (matching the 3-eighth-note span of a dotted quarter), (2) keep the same grouping but make the first note of each group of three an open accent and the other two dead/muted notes, (3) delete the dead notes entirely and play only the accents, (4) let the accents ring out fully as dotted quarter notes — while still mentally subdividing in eighths throughout. The point: most players rely on the drummer/track to place these accents and get lost the moment that scaffold is removed; internalizing the eighth-note grid first makes the riff tight against a click alone.
- **7-string / down-tuned demo (A standard):** the same dotted-quarter-note riff idea re-recorded on 7-string tuned to A standard for extra heaviness — same rhythmic device, different register/tuning as the "make it heavier still" lever.

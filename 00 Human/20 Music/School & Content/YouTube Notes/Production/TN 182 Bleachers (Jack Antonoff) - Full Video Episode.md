## Synopsis

Jack Antonoff of Bleachers walks through three songs from *Everyone for 10 Minutes* — **I'm Not Joking**, **We Should Talk**, and **Dancing** — plus a closing Q&A round pulling from Patreon-submitted questions. Where past Tape Notes episodes with Antonoff (see [[How Jack Antonoff creates songs from small ideas]] and [[Sonically enhancing the meaning of songs with Jack Antonoff]]) leaned toward manipulated/processed production, this one is deliberately about the opposite instinct: a nearly plugin-free live band take recorded on vacation in Rome, a mistuned vocoder hook left broken on purpose, the only Auto-Tuned vocal on the whole record kept specifically for its "trapped" quality, and — the episode's single most unusual trick — two entirely separate full recordings of the same song layered on top of each other rather than combined from one take. Companion piece to [[TN 182 Bleachers (Jack Antonoff) - Gear List]] — several of that list's bare mentions (TC Helicon, Korg VC-10, Korg M1, E-mu sampler, Roland Space Echo, Moog Model D) get real, song-specific technique here.

## Techniques

### I'm Not Joking — a near-zero-processing live band take
- **Recorded live with the touring band in a studio outside Rome**, on a trip taken alongside his wife's work travel — deliberately away from his usual locked-in home studio (Electric Lady/Tamarin) specifically to strip away his own habitual over-engineering.
- **Drums: only 4 microphones** — one kick, one top snare, one bottom snare, one overhead — proven on-camera by turning off every plugin in the session with no audible change.
- **Bass and B3 (3 mics on the Hammond) tracked the same way** — minimal/no processing, "not a tone to write home about" in isolation but working entirely in context of the full band.
- **Vocal on a Shure 50-series dynamic**, chosen specifically over a condenser or an SM58 for maximum proximity/transient feel and deliberate room bleed rather than booth isolation. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]]
- **Horns tracked with room + close mics, virtually unprocessed** — demonstrated against his usual horn move (described but NOT used here): double the horn, offset it (ideally on tape), pan hard, add a chorus/doubler to one side, automate into the bus for a "young Americans"-style thickened horn section. Real, reusable technique even though withheld on this song. → [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Backup vocal layer:** a hard-panned "choir of a man talking to himself in the mirror" — himself doing multiple character voices/registers as a gang-vocal texture, not a normal harmony stack. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **Harpsichord + clavichord bought and recorded the same day, stereo room mic, no processing** — used to bookend the song (intro + outro) as a device signaling importance, described as sounding like "a little chorus of angels." The clavichord's pitch audibly bends the harder it's struck, giving it a percussive, slightly out-of-tune character he leans into rather than fixes.
- **Album-wide device, not song-specific:** harpsichord and clavichord recur throughout *Everyone for 10 Minutes* as a deliberate "forgotten instrument" character — used sometimes straight, sometimes as a stacked reverb-drenched dreamscape, and sometimes as a direct substitute wherever he'd normally reach for celeste, glockenspiel or bass — mostly subtle/"shielded" except where a song calls for it to surface fully. Named (with the baritone guitar) as one of the two defining textural choices of the whole record. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Stated creative principle:** his best takes come from playing gear he's unfamiliar with — deliberately avoids "tasteful" playing on instruments he's mastered, arguing the golden-age synth recordings sound the way they do precisely because nobody yet knew the "correct" way to play them.

### We Should Talk — tight machine grid vs. loose human "shitstorm"
- **Foundation: Korg M1 built-in drum sounds, kept with their stock early-90s reverb character on purpose** (compared to "This Kiss"/"You're Still the One"-era Shania Twain productions) — treated as a "bad sound that becomes cool in context," plus a static/unmoving ARP synth note held underneath for tension.
- **TC Helicon deliberately left mistuned** on a doubled vocal for the hook ("We should talk") — "wasn't even set to the right tuning but it's cool." A broken-preset-as-texture choice, not something corrected afterward.
- **Loose instrumental "personality bits" layered against the tight grid:** a trace (3-string Cuban instrument, "12-string mandolin world"), doubled with a baritone 6-string played high and a second baritone doing a rhythmic pattern, a direct-DI'd Ovation acoustic-electric (praised for a Lindsey Buckingham-style tone), banjo, and Moog Model D noise — all played slightly loose/"sloppy" on purpose, explicitly to remind the listener "we're not living on this grid." → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Space Echo played as a pitched instrument:** turns the return up loud enough that the unit self-oscillates/feeds back, then manually rides the delay-time control live to tune the resulting drone to a note "not really in key but hovering around it" — a repeatable technique distinct from using the unit as a normal echo.
- **The only Auto-Tuned vocal on the entire album, kept deliberately.** Normally records a tuned scratch vocal and re-tracks it untuned for the final — here he kept the tuning because the correction artifact reads as "a soul stuck in a machine," matching the lyric's theme of being trapped in forward motion. → [[Vocal Tuning]]
- **General vocal-processing philosophy stated in this section:** TC Helicon, Korg VC-10 vocoder, and Auto-Tune are treated as interchangeable flavors of the same idea — an audibly processed, "digital and cold" voice used as an emotional device (someone "stuck" or "trapped"), not for correction or polish.

### Dancing — two complete takes layered on top of each other
- **The core trick:** recorded the entire song twice as two fully independent performances, then played both simultaneously as the final mix — not doubling one take, but layering two different complete recordings. Credits Elliott Smith's habit of re-performing (not overdubbing) guitar and vocal parts as direct inspiration, taken a step further here.
  - **V1 (minimal):** one detuned acoustic guitar + one doubled vocal take, recorded at Church Studios, London.
  - **V2 (ornate):** harpsichord, clavichord, an E-mu sampler and an unidentified Korg synth both holding sustained "stagnant" pad notes, and heavily tremolo'd electric guitars — described as "a kaleidoscope of memory."
- **Manual, fader-style automation** rides V1 and V2 in and out of each other by hand throughout, explicitly likened to riding a physical console fader rather than drawing automation curves — flagged as unusual for Pro Tools workflow.
- **Personal session color-coding convention** for navigating high-track-count sessions: bass = red, main lead vocal = blue (doubles/other takes = other shades of blue), guitars = green, ear-candy/texture tracks = yellow, older/alternate vocal takes = orange-brown.
- **A "stagnant" string part (cello + plucked-violin idea) written and played in a single unprocessed take**, then drowned in reverb — the resulting pitchiness/imperfection is treated as a feature ("blips of memory"), not corrected. → [[Reverb]]

### General mixing/production philosophy (Q&A, applies across the whole record)
- **Judge a mix outside the room.** Always leaves the studio and listens while walking or driving before calling a mix finished — the test is whether it reads as "just a song, in its best outfit" rather than "a bunch of interviewed parts."
- **Vocals can be deliberately un-glued from the track**, rather than always matched in reverb/frequency to blend with the instrumental — sometimes left sonically separate on purpose for a "hear the overdubness" aesthetic, citing later Lou Reed and Beatles recordings. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]]
- **When a dense vocal stack hits a wall, mute — don't add more.** When a 5-10 track harmony/double stack stops cutting through, muting roughly half the tracks (instead of EQ'ing or compressing harder) makes the remainder read as bigger, because each surviving part gets more space. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **On "happy accidents":** distrusts studio lore that credits every good idea to a single lucky accident, but agrees the real mechanism is deliberately putting yourself in a position — a live band, unfamiliar gear, imperfect takes — where accidents can actually happen, rather than treating it as pure luck.
- **On new gear vs. known gear:** constantly moves between modern and vintage rather than committing to either — has no interest in a "worse version of a classic synth" softsynth, but is drawn to blurring the line by running digital sources through analog gear like tape.
- **Frequency-history aside:** the TR-808 is cited as introducing genuinely new sub-bass content that didn't exist in recorded music before it (contrasted with pre-808 George Martin records having no true sub) — used to make the point that most "new" production ideas today are combinatorial rather than genuinely new sounds.

# Ranking Guitar Skills From Easiest To Hardest Tier List

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3s4iqLg9hLw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3s4iqLg9hLw)

## Synopsis

An S-to-F difficulty ranking of 30 guitar techniques, judged at their extreme (fast/accurate/musical) rather than a casual beginner attempt — the same tier-list format as [[Every Form of Ear Training Ranked]] from earlier in this batch, applied to physical technique instead of ear training. Useful as a technique-study roadmap.

## Techniques

- **F tier (trivial):** pick scrape (demoted to F− as the single easiest technique on the list — "give your mom a guitar and she'll get it instantly"); vibrato (fretting-hand only, no picking-hand involvement); open chords; palm muting; natural harmonics.
- **D tier:** bar chords (still beginner-level, but objectively harder than open chords); hammer-ons; pull-offs (common mistake: treating a pull-off as a passive "lift-off" instead of actively digging in and pulling the string slightly); sliding.
- **C tier:** tremolo picking (fast alternate picking restricted to one string — easier than full alternate picking specifically because there's no string-crossing); raking (mostly a muting-discipline challenge, paired conceptually with bending since a rake usually precedes one); trills (hammer-on/pull-off combined at speed — harder than either alone but still easy); tapping (most famous lines — Crazy Train, "One" — are easier than reputation suggests; odd-time-signature tapping, e.g. Plini's "Electric Sunrise," is the harder end of this tier).
- **B tier:** hybrid picking (pick + fingers combined; example: Eric Johnson's "Cliffs of Dover"); selective picking (Tosin Abasi's picking-plus-hammer-ons style — right hand stays relatively idle, left hand does most of the work); traditional string bending (common failure modes: bending under the string, dragging adjacent strings along, bending from the fingers instead of the wrist); unison bends (harder than a plain bend because an out-of-tune landing is more exposed, since it's compared directly against a sounding reference note); legato (nearly all left-hand, minimal picking); regular fingerpicking (acknowledged as capable of S-tier difficulty in its hardest repertoire — "Classical Gas" cited — but rated down to B since most fingerpicking material isn't that extreme); thumping (a Mr. T-associated technique using the thumb as a pick).
- **A tier:** downpicking (rated by its extreme, not casual use — Metallica's "Master of Puppets," mostly downpicked at 212 BPM, is the reference example); sweep picking (genuinely hard to acquire but "easy once you have it" — a deliberately lower placement than reputation would suggest); string skipping (fast execution requires picking-direction planning: start an ascending 2-notes-per-string line with an upstroke so the hand is already moving toward the next string; start a descending line with a downstroke for the same reason); tapped harmonics (touch the string exactly 12 frets above the fretted note, timed against the pick attack — harder than a pinch harmonic because the tap has to land accurately at speed); pre-bends (bending to pitch before picking, so you can only judge the target pitch by physical feel/tension, not by ear against a sounding reference).
- **S tier:** alternate picking (judged at its extreme — 16th notes at 180 BPM, not 8ths at 100 — despite being one of the very first techniques taught); economy picking (picking direction follows the string-crossing direction rather than strict alternation; the most common failure mode is uneven note spacing/timing); finger-style playing (fingerpicking plus a deliberate percussive element layered on top — rated harder than plain fingerpicking specifically because of that added layer); slapping on guitar (rated harder on guitar than on bass specifically because guitar's tighter string spacing leaves less room for the thumb).
- **S+ tier (promoted as the single hardest on the list):** alternate picking — chosen over economy picking specifically because fast, accurate alternate picking is both extremely difficult *and* far more broadly useful than economy picking, which tipped the final ranking.

# Something — The Beatles

> Harrison's most celebrated song. George Martin's lush string arrangement. McCartney plays one of his most melodic bass lines on the album. Frank Sinatra called it the greatest love song of the 20th century.

## Chords
**Key:** C major (middle 8 lifts to A major) · George Harrison

**Intro lick:** F – E♭ – G/D
→ **IV – ♭III – V** (the descending guitar figure)

**Verse ("Something in the way she moves"):** C – Cmaj7 – C7 – F – F/E – D9 – G
→ **I – Imaj7 – I7 – IV – IV/3 – II9 – V** (a **[[Chromatic Bass Lines|chromatic descending bass]]** C–B–B♭–A — the song's beating heart). The I → Imaj7 → I7 line-cliché ([[Seventh Chords]]) *is* that bass walk harmonized. The **D9 is a [[Secondary Dominants|secondary dominant]] (V9/V)** pushing to G.

**Middle 8 ("You're asking me will my love grow"):** A – C♯m/G♯ – F♯m7 – D – G – A
→ modulates to **A major** (I – iii – vi – … – V) before sliding home to C.

## Production & Arrangement
Abbey Road (1969) — George Martin's most restrained-but-lush production. The arrangement is a masterclass in **parts that sing independently**:

- **McCartney's bass is a second melody:** it never just follows roots — it counter-melodizes constantly (one of his most celebrated lines). It's the model for **melodic bass**, not a low-end anchor. **Why it reads as a conversation, not just a busy counter-line:** McCartney's most active bass moments land specifically when the vocal is resting — creating a genuine call-and-response with Harrison's voice rather than the bass running dense underneath it regardless of what the vocal is doing. → [[Slap Bass Dynamics — Contrast, Force, and Restraint]] tool #4 (the same ensemble-restraint mechanism, applied to voice instead of a horn/other instrument) — BassBuzz, [[10 Songs that Taught Me Bass (Easy to Effin’ Hard)]]
- **George Martin's strings** lift the choruses/solo without turning it saccharine — they enter late and swell, arrangement dynamics again.
- **Harrison's guitar solo is *composed* and singable** — doubled, warm (Leslie/compressed), it's a melodic statement you can hum, not a shred.
- **Ringo plays for the song:** restrained, tom-forward fills that answer the vocal phrases.
- Recorded warm and close; the space is intimate, the low end (that bass) is the star.

## Steal this
- **Bass as counter-melody:** write the bass as a second tune that moves against the vocal, not root-following → [[1 Electric Bass DI Track]] / [[1 Digital Bass Track]]. This is *the* Beatles bass lesson.
- **Chromatic descending bass under static harmony** (the C–B–B♭–A line cliché) for instant emotional depth → [[Chromatic Bass Lines]] · [[Seventh Chords]].
- **Compose the solo:** make the lead a hummable melody (double it, warm it) rather than improvised runs.
- **Arrange in dynamics:** strings/parts enter to lift, not to fill — silence and restraint first.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Something]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #178.*

**Form.** The song's emotional position is built into a **standing conflict between one rising hook and a constant falling undertow** — the three-bar hook that opens the track, closes every verse and closes the record is affirmative and ascending, while the bass and the tune spend the rest of their time descending. And the hook is **never given lyrics**, which he reads as the point rather than an oversight. The verse divides into **12 bars of verse proper plus a 6-bar refrain-like tail**, the back half of which is the intro hook again; the bridge is a four-square 8 in AA′. The structural signature is **elision** — the last bar of nearly every section lands on the downbeat of the next, so the hook phrase never gets its full two bars until the outro, the only place it stands alone.

**Harmony.** `Intro (3): IV – ♭III – V6/4 – I` · `Verse (18): I – Imaj7 – V/IV – IV · V/V – V · vi – [A♭] – G – V/V · IV – ♭III–V6/4 – I` · `Bridge (8, in A): [I – (G♯) – vi – (E) · IV – ♭VII – I] ×2`, the last unit re-read in C as V/V – V – I. The intro gets away with its cross relations because the **outer voices move stepwise in contrary motion**, which is also what forces V into second inversion. Inside the verse the V **resolves deceptively to vi** at the start of the tail ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]) before the phrase converges home. The bridge is a real **pivot modulation to A** ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]], [[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]); on paper the pivot is clean, and what makes it sound abrupt is the proximity of the E♭ and A chords, a tritone apart at the root.

**Notable.** The harmonic rhythm is punishingly slow, and the D→G root motion of bars 9–12 restates the C→F motion of bars 1–8 **in half the time**. The middle chords of the tail get **no Roman numerals on purpose** — they exist only as a consequence of harmonising the bass descent. The outro **feints at repeating the bridge modulation** and then cancels it; he hears two Beethoven coda gambits at once.

**Against this page.** Agrees on C major with the middle 8 in A.

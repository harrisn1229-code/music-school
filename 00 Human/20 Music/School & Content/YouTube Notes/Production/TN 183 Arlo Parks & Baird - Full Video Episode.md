## Synopsis

John sits with Arlo Parks (in a London studio) and her co-writer/producer Baird (Zooming in from his LA garden, then relocating indoors when the heat threatens his laptop) to break down three tracks from Arlo's album *Ambiguous Desire*: **Senses** (feat. Sampha), **Jetta**, and **Heaven**. The through-line across all three: near-zero processing on drums built almost entirely from chopped/layered found sounds and homemade samples, guitar and keyboard tones treated as one-off happy accidents rather than repeatable settings, and Arlo's vocal writing built by freestyling wordless melody first and excavating lyrics from lived nights out afterward. Baird repeatedly frames the philosophy as "limitations are a strength" and "you can only boil a song down to five or six essential characters" — restraint as the organizing production principle, not an afterthought. The episode closes on a Patreon-question segment (guitar-pedal specifics on *Devotion*/*Dog Rose*, hedged and half-remembered) and Tape Notes' standard two closing questions (must-have gear, advice).

## Techniques

### Senses (feat. Sampha)
- **Drum bed built entirely from chopped/layered samples, barely processed at all.** A named-layer stack — "functional snare," "new jack snare" (new jack swing character), a tom/kick hybrid that changes pitch across the song to track the chords, snare rolls, a chopped ride break, tambourine — combined with almost zero processing ("they don't really seem to be processed at all... I have a tuner on this one, which isn't doing anything"). Built in Ableton's Sampler from a large, disorganized personal sample-pack collection plus Baird's own recorded drum breaks. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Sample Browser and Clips Workflow]]
- **"Happy accident" Crumar keyboard texture: crank input gain with the chorus circuit on and no notes played, to generate a crackly, swirly noise texture, then run it through delay.** An old, cheap 1970s Italian Crumar keyboard whose chorus is binary (fully on or fully off, no in-between) — a tiny textural element that Baird says would visibly break the mix if removed, despite sounding "pretty thin" in isolation. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], **Crumar** (model unnamed — see [[Vintage Gear Worklist]])
- **A one-off, never-reproducible guitar tone: an Ibanez electro-acoustic nylon-string guitar played very loud through pedals, its own pickup effectively working as a microphone and picking up drum bleed from the room.** No dry DI was captured — "that's how it is." The melody was written by Arlo singing live against this exact take as it happened (a symbiotic, in-the-room writing loop, not sequential tracking). → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]]
- **[[MicroShift]] used as a mono→wide reveal, not constant widening: the guitar DI starts dead-center in the intro, then MicroShift widens it the instant the drums enter**, so the stereo image visibly "jumps out" as a structural cue rather than sitting wide throughout. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Arlo's vocal-doubling accuracy is a trained skill, not comping:** she sings full takes start-to-finish (never punching in), doubled/tripled/quadrupled, and the takes land in near-perfect alignment without VocAlign-style correction — a habit built as a teenager who didn't know comping was possible, so every take had to be perfect start to finish. → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **Vocal chain — see [[Arlo Parks Vocal Chain]] for the full breakdown** (a saved preset called "Really Really Basic Vocal Chain" plus a set of named parallel sends).
- Sampha's guest verse/piano was written in-person at his Hackney studio — his chord choices deliberately more harmonically "experimental" than the rest of the song, requiring Baird and Arlo to blend takes carefully to feel like one continuous world; his piano part was recorded separately and layered under a rough reference piano Baird had already cut, the two blended to register as a single instrument.

### Jetta
- **Opening hook is guitar-headstock string plucking** — an intentionally "absurd," non-guitar-sounding texture from Simon "Flanafi" Martinez, used as an ear-catching cold open. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **A homemade drum break sampled from one of Baird's own unreleased, unfinished songs**, layered with a short white-noise blast (an attention-grabbing transient) and a "skitter scatter" kick — the same sample hit repeatedly, deliberately NOT in time with the song, to inject human imperfection into an otherwise programmed groove. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **A homemade baritone guitar part, poorly intonated on the original take, salvaged by replaying it note-by-note** rather than tuning-correcting the original recording, with some notes deliberately allowed to swell and others cut short. Run through **Electro-Harmonix Pitch Fork** (a pitch pedal) plus "a free chorus plugin" (almost certainly [[TAL-Chorus-LX]], the vault's own free-chorus reference). → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Vintage Gear Worklist]]
- **Live drum overdubs layered on top of the programmed break later in the arrangement** (ride, claps) for added dynamics — a hybrid programmed-plus-live-kit approach, same instinct as Senses' "kit in the room" fills.
- **Vocal-as-instrument motif introduced here and continued into Heaven: four distinct chopped/repeated slices of Arlo's own voice used as a rhythmic, percussive element** rather than a melodic hook — the start of a deliberate shift toward treating vocals as sample material. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **A field-recorded voice note from a real night out with friends woven into the outro** as the song's literal ending — directly inspired by the documentary *Maestro* (Paradise Garage) and its use of real club-goer voice fragments. Arlo's own voice is the one heard. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]]
- **Songwriting method: journal the night, then build lyrics from documented sensory detail** (packing a bag, cigarettes, cash, phone) rather than abstraction — the same freestyle-melody-first, lyrics-second approach as Senses.

### Heaven
- **An out-of-tune studio piano's exact character was preserved by sampling it note-by-note into a custom virtual instrument**, because no plugin or replacement piano could reproduce that specific tuning quirk — "it's magic, forever." → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Piano Recording]]
- **"Springboarding": loop one exciting 4–8 bar section on repeat and sing freely over it before writing the rest of the song**, deliberately not building chronologically from the start — described as "throwing myself into the abyss of the next section." → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]]
- **Dual-chain bass: the identical live bass take (Squier Jazz Bass through an amp simulator) is duplicated and processed two different ways for two different sections** — verse: lows trimmed to leave room for the kick; drop/instrumental section: **Bassment (UnitedPlugins)** used specifically to add low end back in and make the same part read bigger and more present. → [[Bass Design]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **A reversed, chopped scratch vocal take buried in the mix purely as a buzzing "wasp"/insect-like ear-candy texture** — deliberately loud in isolation, inaudible as a discrete element in context; paired with an unexplained, randomly-labeled "Jingle Jangle" chopped/delayed texture track that both writers admit they can't fully account for the origin of. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Pitched kick drum on the drop** — the kick's pitch is moved down through the section for an increasingly "relentless," sub-heavy feel on a big system, described as a technique neither had tried before on this scale. → [[Kick Design]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **A vocal sample from Arlo's own side project with her brother (South Hill Experiment) used as a recurring textural anchor**, appearing at the start, middle, and end of the song. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **A deliberately out-of-time drum fill sourced from an old personal loop nicknamed "Perfect Jesus disco 120 bpm"** at the very end of the track, left loose/unquantized on purpose.
- Songwriting source: a specific real night out (a Kelly Lee Owens/Caribou secret set under LA's Sixth Street Viaduct) journaled in sensory detail the same night, brought into the session the next day — same method as Jetta.

### Q&A segment — Devotion / Dog Rose guitar chain (hedged, low confidence)
- **Patron question about the guitar pedal/distortion chain on *Devotion* and *Dog Rose* got an explicitly uncertain answer from both writers** — worth logging as a lead, not a confirmed fact. Baird: "I don't remember... I remember what distortion I would have used [for Devotion]... it was DI guitar, wasn't going through an amp... might have had a formant shifter on it... might have been just like the cheapest distortion pedal... it might have been a **DS1**, but I don't know. I don't have a great collection of that sort of stuff." For *Dog Rose* (originally demoed alone by Arlo before Baird joined): "it was all probably stock Logic stuff... whatever happens when you go Logic distortion and the first thing." → **Boss DS-1** (see [[Vintage Gear Worklist]], hedged sighting) and Logic's stock distortion plugin (Nate doesn't run Logic; no owned-equivalent mapping given the uncertainty).

### Closing questions (Tape Notes' standard two)
- **Arlo's must-have gear: the Roland Juno-106, used on "Two-Sided" — an entire song's synth part built on it purely because it was the only synth in the room that day.** Framed explicitly as evidence that constraint bred her favorite song on the record: "the limitations of only having this one synth and it feeling exciting in the moment is what made my favorite song on the record." → [[Roland Juno-106]]
- **Arlo's second must-have: the Shure SM7, used for the entire album** ("that's the microphone we used for the entire project") — described as a habit/comfort choice over "fancier" mics she's tried and abandoned. → [[Shure SM7]]
- **Baird's must-have: the Electro-Harmonix Pitch Fork (specifically the mini "Pitchfork Pico"), used across the record for subtle chorus-like thickening, octave doubling, and generating harmony/bass tones played directly on guitar** — Baird's stated exception to an otherwise self-professed dislike of guitar pedals. → **Electro-Harmonix Pitch Fork** (see [[Vintage Gear Worklist]])
- **Advice — Arlo: "limitations are a strength... you can do a lot with a little," and too many production elements usually means the wrong elements are present, not too many good ones.**
- **Advice — Baird: start a section messy, sloppy, and "too strange" rather than safe, then simplify afterward — "it's easier to make something simpler... than it is to make it saucy or cool after the fact."** Explicit rule of thumb: when unsure between too-conventional and too-weird, always choose too-weird, because only one of those directions is easy to walk back. → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]]

# You Like Me Too Much — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (**6 bars**, one drawn-out phrase that only *sounds* out of tempo because there is no backbeat under it) · Verse (16, four equal phrases) · Verse · Bridge (8) · Verse · Break (**a 12-bar instrumental blues frame with the sung title hook grafted onto the end**) · Bridge · Verse · Outro with a complete ending. Verse and bridge elide in both directions — the lyric runs straight through the seam and the harmony refuses to close there either.

The analytical payload is **root motion**. Where the standard pop/rock verse is driven by circle-of-fifths motion toward I or V, this one moves by step and by third, sets up its goals indirectly, and backs away from resolution every time it gets close ([[HAR-C01 — The three functions: T, SD, D]]). ii→IV and iii→V are the weak, indirect approaches that create the sense of avoidance, and the verse is **open at both ends** despite being full of tonic chords.

Opening on **♭III** in intro bar 3 is already unusual ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]), and its F natural sustained into the following major chord is the bluesiest instant in the song. The bridge piles applied dominants up to a **V-of-V-of-V** ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]) and ends as a wind-up on V whose resolution the next verse defers again by entering on ii.

The entire tune lives inside **six notes**; the bridge does not break that ceiling, taking its contrast from broadening out rhythmically instead.

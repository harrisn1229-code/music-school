## Synopsis

A lighter breakdown of an indie-rock track ("Eyeliner," with Tyler Aaron) built around a real-guitarist collaboration and a genuinely clever fake-guitar trick: Ableton's own physical-modeling **Tension** instrument standing in for a real "damp" guitar pluck. Covers a Simpler loop+fade technique for turning a one-shot bell/chime sample into an indefinitely-sustaining pad, the Tension-based fake guitar chain (amp preset → modulation → reverb → distortion, duplicated and left to diverge L/R over time), real double-tracked electric guitar glued with OTT/Glue Compressor, and a section-level instrumentation swap (synth bass → live electric bass) for the breakdown.

## Techniques

- **Tiny one-shot → indefinite pad, via Simpler Loop + Fade + slow Attack** — a vinyl bell/chime one-shot looped on just a small portion of itself, smoothed with the Fade parameter, with Attack raised so the note doesn't hit too hard — turns a short sample into a sustaining pad. → [[Simpler]]
- **A believable fake guitar from Ableton's own Tension instrument** — Tension (physical-modeling string synth) → a clean rhythm/funk guitar amp preset → a phasing modulation stage → light reverb → distortion/lo-fi coloring — a from-scratch "damp guitar" pluck that reads as a real recorded part. New plugin note created for Tension. → [[Tension]]
- **Duplicate a sound L/R, then let the two copies DIVERGE partway through the song** — rather than keeping two panned duplicates identical for the whole track, apply different automation/processing to each partway through so they read as two related-but-independent parts rather than one source mirrored — a variation on the "double-track a real performance" width principle for a source that started as a single duplicate. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **A synth doubling a lead line, faded in and out for movement** — rather than a static doubling layer, automate the doubling synth's presence to fade in/out under the lead so it contributes motion rather than just thickness.
- **Swap the bass instrumentation for a whole section** — replace the synth bass used throughout the track with a live electric bass specifically for the breakdown, changing the section's texture rather than relying on filtering/processing tricks alone. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

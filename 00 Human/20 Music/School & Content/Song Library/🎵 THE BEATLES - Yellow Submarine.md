# Yellow Submarine — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Verse (8, parsed 4+4, harmonically open) · Verse · Refrain (8, closed) · Verse · Refrain · Verse (instrumental) · Verse · Refrain. It uses a **refrain, not a bridge**; **three verses run back to back** in the middle, which is rare; and it starts *in medias res* on an unaccompanied vocal pickup.

The music is deliberately plain so the sound collage has something solid to stand on — five ordinary diatonic chords and a tune with a hole in it. What actually tells the sections apart is **harmonic rhythm** ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]): verse changes straddle the barline, landing on beat 4 and holding through the downbeat, while refrain changes sit square on the beats. Root motion falls by fifths almost throughout, which is what makes one chord in verse bar 3 a genuine puzzle — the pattern wants ii, but there is a C in the bass.

The tune is pentatonic in the Lennon manner ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]): **scale degree 7 never appears at all**, and degree 4 shows up only as a passing note. Narrow range, chant-shaped, pitched for a non-singer.

Variation is scheduled by section rather than written into the music, and the effects are counted as instruments in that scheme: guitar and maracas, then water, then party noise plus a sloppy brass band whose chords clash with the backing track, then submarine machinery, then Lennon echoing Ringo like a captain on a squawk box, then widened backing vocals.

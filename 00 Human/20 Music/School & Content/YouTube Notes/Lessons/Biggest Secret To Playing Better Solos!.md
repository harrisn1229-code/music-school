## Synopsis

A direct A/B: the same B minor backing progression (Bm–Gadd9–Dadd9–A–F♯7) soloed two ways — one scale position run straight through vs. deliberately accenting each chord change with its own chord tones/color notes. The core comparison restates this batch's chord-tone-targeting theme (see [[BEST Guitar Solo Exercise - How To STOP Playing Those Boring Licks!]], [[6 EASY METAL SOLOS That Sound Advanced!]]); two specific technique details are new: precise semitone-bend articulation, and substituting a diminished voicing for a dominant 7th chord.

## What's taught

- **Take 1 vs. Take 2 (restated core comparison).** Take 1: one B minor scale position, alternate-picked fast, technically fine but reads as "a mechanical exercise" — the diagnosis being that since all the backing chords ARE built from the B minor scale's own notes, running the scale straight through technically "works" but ignores the individual chord colors passing underneath. Take 2: same backing, but each chord gets its own targeted chord-tone/extension approach (root-5th-9th-♭3 arpeggiated dissonance on Bm; a sweep-picked/tapped lick outlining G major's chord tones plus 9th; a bend; a legato/hybrid-picked scalar phrase; a 3-notes-per-string A major arpeggio; a diminished-shape run resolving to B) — reads as musical AND still technically impressive, which is the video's stated goal (fast/technical playing and musicality aren't mutually exclusive).
- **New: precise semitone-bend articulation.** A specific technique warning: semitone (half-step) bends are commonly OVER-bent by players, landing closer to a whole-tone bend than the intended half-step — because the physical distance is small, it's easy to misjudge by feel. The fix is deliberate pitch-checking (ear or reference) to land exactly on the intended half-step, not "a little more" for extra expression, since overshooting changes the actual harmonic content of the bend, not just its intensity.
- **New: substitute a diminished voicing/shape for a dominant 7th chord as a soloing device.** Over the F♯7 (V7 of B minor, itself already a borrowed-major-V color — matches [[Harmonic Minor Modes]]'s existing borrowed-V entry), instead of outlining the dominant 7 arpeggio directly, run an ascending diminished shape that still resolves cleanly onto the B root — leverages the close harmonic relationship between a dominant 7 chord and a diminished chord built a half-step above its root (shared tritone/guide-tone content) as a soloing color substitution.
- **Chord voicings used in the backing (light restatement, see this batch's power-chord-extension entries):** Bm as a low-doubled power chord; G and D as power chords with an added 9th; A as a plain power chord; F♯ as a borrowed dominant 7 (raising the diatonic F♯m7's minor 3rd to major) for a stronger cadential pull into B minor.

# Rocky Raccoon — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Four bars of solo strummed acoustic on a tonally ambiguous Am7, then the same eight-bar loop seventeen times, then an implied four bars of held I where loop 18 would have started.

The single fact that matters: **the entire three-and-a-half-minute track is one eight-bar progression looped seventeen times.** Nothing in the harmony ever changes. Everything that makes it feel like a song with verses, refrains, a climax and an ending is done with vocal delivery, rhyme scheme and instrumentation — the same trick Chuck Berry and Little Richard run on the twelve-bar blues ([[MEL-C04 — Writing to a loop vs to changes]]). The loops are grouped in pairs, and one unpaired ninth pass is the whole design in miniature: after four paired statements, a single pass reads as an event.

The progression is `vi⁷ – V-of-V – V – I`, two bars each. Two internal lines do the work the chords do not: an **inner voice descends chromatically** G–F♯–F♮–E across the loop, audible once the backing vocals arrive, and in bar 8 the bass drops to B under the sustained C — a passing note, not a chord change ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]). The tune is almost entirely pentatonic ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]) with a chromatically filled descent running through it.

Strummed acoustic runs the whole way; everything else arrives and leaves on schedule, and the **subtractions are as structural as the additions** — dropping the tack piano is what marks loops 12–13 as a return rather than a continuation ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]).

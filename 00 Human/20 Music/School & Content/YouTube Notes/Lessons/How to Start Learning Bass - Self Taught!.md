# How to Start Learning Bass - Self Taught!

## Synopsis

A BassBuzz practice-philosophy video, built as a comedy sketch (a self-serious, faux-biblical "bass ghost" narrator handing down "six commandments of self-teaching") wrapped around a genuinely concrete framework for structuring self-taught bass practice with no teacher to guide it. It opens with three escalating cautionary examples — a player stuck after a year, one who's "barely progressed beyond 6 months" after *two years*, and one who still can't play a single full song after *14 years* — to establish the video's real thesis: the failure mode isn't lack of effort or lack of material, it's picking the *wrong* material with no way to check whether it's useful. The fix is a three-tier goal ladder (long-term destination, medium-term process commitment, short-term day-to-day material derived from the long-term goal) plus four coverage categories to check that derivation against — songs, technique, fretboard knowledge, and theory — closing with a quickfire list of chronic self-taught pitfalls and a final point that patience, not routine perfection, is the actual highest-leverage factor. It's a companion piece, in both content and structure, to BassBuzz's other beginner videos already in the vault ([[7 Days to Learning Bass Scales (Beginner Lesson)]], [[The PERFECT Bass Practice Morning Routine For Beginners]], [[Bass Exercises for Beginners (Strength, Stamina, Flexibility, Control)]]) — this one is the *organizing* layer that explains why/when to reach for those, rather than another drill set itself. One soft course plug (BassBuzz's "Beginner to Badass") appears mid-video but is explicitly framed as optional — the DIY path is treated as fully viable throughout, which is the video's entire premise.

## What's taught

- **[~0:00–1:15] Cold open — three escalating self-taught failure cases.** A player who still "sucks" after a year; one who's "barely progressed beyond what would take a normal person 6 months" after two years; one who, after **14 years**, still can't play a single song start to finish. Framed as the direct consequence of having no teacher to stop you from practicing the wrong things — the video's stated goal is preventing viewers from becoming any of these three. Intro bit: a faux-solemn "bass ghost" narrator delivers "the six commandments of self-teaching."
- **[~1:15–3:30] Commandment 1 — Set thy goals: the long/medium/short-term goal ladder.** Without a stated goal you can't judge whether any given exercise is *useful for you specifically* (a jazz walking-bass routine is great for one player, wasted time for a beginner who just wants a Chili Peppers bassline). Three linked tiers: **long-term goal** (the destination, months out — e.g. "play in a bar band," or "learn Hysteria by Muse" — motivating but feels like failure for the whole runway if it's the only goal you're tracking); **medium-term goal** (a *process* commitment, not an outcome — e.g. "practice 20 min/day, 4x/week for 3 months," or "1 hr/day, 5x/week for 6 months" for an ambitious target — the fix for demotivation because it's fully within your control, checked off session by session); **short-term goal** (the actual daily practice material, derived by breaking the long-term goal into its component skills — worked examples: bar-band goal → daily rhythm-with-a-drummer + simple chord-chart basslines; advanced-song goal → daily plucking-speed + fretting-coordination work). **Explicit catch named on-camera:** as a self-taught player, *you* have to do that long→short breakdown yourself with no teacher's expertise to lean on — which is exactly where self-taught players "flounder, practice the wrong stuff, and rage-post on Reddit years later." The next three commandments exist specifically to give the breakdown something to check against.
- **[~3:30–4:30] Commandment 2 — Learn thy songs (motivation as fuel, not a reward).** Cites Fender data: **90% of new guitarists quit within a year** (applied here to bass as the same cautionary stat). Claim: gating songs behind "earned" technique starves the motivation that keeps a self-taught player showing up, so songs belong in rotation early, not as a reward. Secondary benefit: songs naturally train rhythm, technique, and ear together instead of needing isolated drills for each. Alternative offered for players who aren't drawn to learning existing songs: freeform noodling/making up your own parts by ear, though the video notes this only works in combination with Commandment 3 (technique) since you need the coordination either way.
- **[~4:30–5:45] Commandment 3 — Suffer not poor technique: a 5-point exercise-quality checklist.** Technique = fretting/plucking/muting; usually the real answer to "why can't I play my goal song yet." Since there's effectively infinite possible exercises and no teacher to pick for you, the checklist that makes almost any exercise effective: (1) focus on *how* you play, not *what* you play; (2) practice with a metronome or drum track; (3) start slower than you think you should; (4) **don't speed up until you can play it decently at least 5 times in a row**; (5) track progress in a simple journal.
- **[~5:45–8:15] Commandment 4 — Make thy life easier: fretboard knowledge + theory, plus the assembled four-category checklist.** Fretboard knowledge (note names + fretboard shapes/patterns) turns a tab from "random meaningless numbers" into chunked, familiar information. Theory (not framed as mandatory for everyone) speeds up understanding what you're playing, memorizing songs, and writing your own parts — points to BassBuzz's separate dedicated theory video rather than teaching it here. **The four categories assembled as a coverage checklist:** songs + technique + fretboard + theory. **Worked example** applying it to the earlier "learn Hysteria" goal: songs — covered by definition; technique — the hard, practice-heavy part for that particular song; fretboard/theory — not strictly critical if you're willing to just memorize the tab number-by-number, but knowing note names/scales speeds memorization and keeps those categories from being neglected while all the time goes into technique. **Sample daily short-term routine given** *(general starting point, for an ambitious 1 hr/day medium goal)*: warm-up 5 min, scales 5 min, fretting exercises 10 min, plucking exercises 10 min, song practice in small chunks 15 min, free/fun playing 5 min.
- **[~8:15–9:45] Commandment 5 — Avoid common self-taught pitfalls (quickfire problem/solution list).** Buying the wrong bass → get a 4-string fretted bass you enjoy, professionally set up. Pressing way too hard with fretting fingers/thumb → minimum-viable-pressure + thumb-off exercises (pointed at BassBuzz's free PDF, not detailed on-camera). Putting stickers on the frets to mark notes → never do this, no nuance given. Learning only through Musician/Guitar Hero/Rocksmith-style games → treat as dessert, not dinner; supplement real lessons, don't substitute. Practicing unplugged → use an amp or headphones, both for fun and to hear the instrument as others will. Leaving the bass in its case/closet between sessions → keep it out on a stand (or the couch) so it's always ready to pick up. Learning entirely alone and feeling isolated → build community — jam with friends or use bass-focused forums.
- **[~9:45–11:10] Commandment 6 — Thou shalt be patient — named directly as the single biggest cause of self-taught failure.** Explicitly contrasted against "magic 30-day transformation" marketing. Self-teaching is a long game: not routine perfection or unbroken daily consistency, but showing up repeatedly past the point initial excitement fades, with gradual (not dramatic) progress. Closing pointer to a companion "what order to learn everything on bass" curriculum video, framed as a way to keep short-term goals fresh over a 6–12 month horizon — **that companion video is now in the vault: [[What to Learn on Bass (in Order)]]**, distilled into [[The BassBuzz 55-Step Bass Learning Roadmap]].

# Can You Take Me Back — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A 28-second fragment over one static chord, not even listed on the album sleeve. Two things make it worth studying: **prose-like phrasing that refuses to line up with the bar grid**, and the fact that the released version is an act of editing — it was excerpted from a longer take at the "I Will" session, and the haunting mid-verse fade is, in the source performance, a plain complete coda. The crop made the song.

**Form.** One verse plus a second fading out. The verse is **12 bars** counted internally as 3 × 4, but the three sung phrases straddle that division rather than sitting inside it. The first starts on a downbeat and ends on a hard accent just *before* the downbeat of bar 4, with the guitar vamp filling the rest. The second starts on a downbeat but plants its sustained high note in exactly the rhythmic slot where the first ended, tying across into the next four-bar group. The third is squeezed onto the tail of the second and melismas out to bar 12.

**Harmony.** One chord — a dominant seventh on the tonic, held throughout. The ♭3s in the tune grind against its major third, so a **cross-relation runs continuously** ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]).

**Melody.** Strictly **pentatonic** ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]), but spread across a tenth. Wide range on five notes is what gives a one-chord fragment any shape at all.

**Arrangement.** Paul on acoustic guitar, Ringo and John on light percussion. Nothing else. The lesson: over a static chord, phrasing is the only structure you have.

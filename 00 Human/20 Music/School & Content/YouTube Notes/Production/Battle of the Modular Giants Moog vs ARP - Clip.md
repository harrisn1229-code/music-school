# Battle of the Modular Giants Moog vs ARP - Clip

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MY-VAnYPxQc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MY-VAnYPxQc)

## Synopsis

A head-to-head bass-patch "shootout" at Rosen Sound: Rob Rosen on a 1973 Moog System 55, Anthony Marinelli on an ARP 2600, both building a bass patch under matched constraints (3 oscillators, 2 envelope generators — one filter, one VCA) and comparing results live. Genuinely useful because both patches are described in enough concrete detail to reconstruct the logic on any subtractive synth, and because the two builders independently make different, equally-valid choices at the same decision points (where to drive the signal, how much keyboard tracking to use) — a real A/B on synthesis choices that are usually presented as single "correct" answers elsewhere.

## Techniques

- **Rob's Moog System 55 bass patch:** 3 oscillators mixed via the CP3A module (praised for its own harmonic distortion character) into the 904 filter. Oscillator 1: pulse wave with PWM. Oscillator 2: also PWM'd, using a standalone 921 oscillator as the modulation source driving BOTH PWM'd oscillators' width together. A significant amount of extra sine wave blended into the pulse-wave oscillator specifically for low-end power (the same same/second-oscillator sine-blend logic as [[10 Tips For Synth Bass]]'s CS-80 trick). Oscillator 3: sawtooth, also used to modulate the filter (its triangle/rectangular output routed into the filter for extra movement/"life"). Signal path: filter's own 5-input mixer → preamp (driven hard, "frying" the already-filtered signal) → blended in parallel with the clean/undriven filter output → VCA.
- **Anthony's ARP 2600 bass patch:** Also 3 oscillators, 2 envelopes (filter, VCA), oscillators mixed via the ARP's own preamp before the filter (the ARP filter has only one input, unlike the Moog's 5-input mixer-filter). Driven hard through the preamp BEFORE the filter (the opposite placement from Rob's patch) — the filter then carves the already-distorted signal, rather than distorting the already-filtered one. Keyboard tracking added deliberately: the ARP 2600's keyboard-control-voltage output is inverted with no built-in attenuator, so tracking was added by flipping the voltage back the right way and manually halving it via patching — a moderate, not full, amount, added specifically because the patch read "a little dull" up top without any tracking at all.
- **Drive placement (before vs. after the filter) is a real creative choice, not a technical default — both are legitimate and sound different.** Driving after the filter (Rob's Moog patch) colors the already-shaped tone; driving before the filter (Anthony's ARP patch) hands the filter a more harmonically complex signal to carve. Both musicians praised the other's result on hearing it.
- **Both patches mix the driven/"fried" signal back with a clean, undriven parallel path rather than committing fully to distortion** — the synthesis-stage version of the wet/dry-mult philosophy that runs through [[Synth Bass on Steroids - The Power of EFX Pedals]].
- **Framing takeaway:** you don't need to exactly reproduce a specific vintage/iconic reference tone — "it depends what song they're in... you just want kick-butt sounds" — achievable on very different gear than whatever inspired the target sound.

# "Burn Rubber on Me" | Legendary Synth Bass REVEALED

## Synopsis

A full patch recreation of the iconic Minimoog bass from The Gap Band's "Burn Rubber on Me (Why You Wanna Hurt Me)" — oscillator layering, filter/envelope settings, and, just as important, the performance technique (behind-the-beat phrasing, pitch bend, vibrato) that makes a static patch sound alive.

## Techniques

- **Square-wave-plus-an-octave-up-Sawtooth is a go-to layering formula for synth bass, not just this patch.** Square wave carries the low fundamental (fewer harmonics, "basier," no competition in the low end); a Sawtooth one octave up adds "teeth"/harmonics without crowding the fundamental, because the octave gap keeps the saw's harmonic content out of the square's territory. Both oscillator levels cranked full in the mixer — deliberately, for extra saturation/edge at that stage, compounding with any DAW/preamp drive added after.
- **Filter cutoff fully closed (zero) to maximize envelope action**, so the filter's own attack/decay/sustain sweep does all the tonal shaping rather than sitting open with the envelope doing partial work.
- **Filter envelope (ADS) settings and what each one does audibly**: attack slowed slightly for a less clicky, more "talking" onset (too slow and it can't be played in time — there's a real playability ceiling); decay speed directly controls the character of the downward pitch/tone slope after the initial hit (faster = more percussive snap, slower = the sound never gets a chance to "talk"); sustain level must be raised for a held note to actually sustain — without it, only the attack/decay transient is audible and the note "dies."
- **VCA envelope as a simple gate, not a shaping tool**: full decay, full sustain, no attack — the VCA just passes through whatever the filter envelope has already sculpted, so all the expressive shaping happens at the filter stage, not the amp stage.
- **Performance technique is treated as inseparable from the patch itself**: playing slightly behind the beat, adding vibrato via the mod wheel then bringing pitch bend back down before releasing, and generally staying aware of wheel state (mod wheel left engaged unintentionally changes the next note's character) — the same static patch reads as flat/lifeless without this layer, and "talking"/expressive with it.
- **This layering approach transfers to any plugin or analog synth with an equivalent signal path** — it's the oscillator-layering + filter-envelope logic that matters, not the specific Minimoog hardware.

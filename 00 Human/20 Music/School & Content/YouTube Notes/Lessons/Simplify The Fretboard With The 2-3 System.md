# Simplify The Fretboard With The "2-3 System"

## Synopsis
Daniel Seriff's fullest, most systematic treatment of the diagonal-pentatonic idea this channel has produced — this video names it explicitly ("the 2-3 system," also called "the diagonal pentatonic") and works through the complete fretboard map rather than a single fragment. The pitch is direct: instead of memorizing five separate CAGE-style pentatonic boxes, learn one small repeating two-piece pattern — a "2-note set" (♭7 then root, a whole step apart) and a "3-note set" (♭3-4-5, each a whole step apart) — and chain it diagonally up or down the neck. Worked entirely in D minor, the lesson builds the starting shape on the D/G string pair, extends it upward through the B-string tuning-compensation rule onto the B/high-E pair (three full octaves, low E fret 8 to high E fret 17), then builds a second, independent diagonal region descending from the high E string down to fret 1, and closes with an "anchor on any D and improvise" challenge plus a garbled-but-plausible extension up a further octave to the 22nd fret. This is the clearest, most complete statement of a shape this vault had already partially reconstructed from two other Seriff videos ([[Dorian from the Minor Pentatonic Box]]'s Fingering 2 and [[Diagonal Pentatonic Blues Soloing]]'s diagonal major pentatonic) without ever pinning down the full fret map — this video is that missing base layer. A ~1-minute sponsor read for Seriff's paid "Diagonal Pentatonic Method" course sits at the midpoint; skippable, no additional technique content in it.

## What's taught
- **The core building blocks: a 2-note set (♭7 → root, whole step apart, root on the higher fret) and a 3-note set (♭3-4-5, each a whole step apart), placed on adjacent strings** — one of each covers all five pentatonic degrees across just two strings. (~1:00–2:00)
- **Starting shape, D string + G string:** D string frets 10 (C, ♭7) and 12 (D, root) = the 2-note set; G string frets 10, 12, 14 (F, G, A = ♭3, 4, 5) = the 3-note set. Full D minor pentatonic across two strings. (~1:00–2:00)
- **Practice instruction while under the fingers: use slides, hammer-ons, pull-offs, bends and vibrato right away** — "make more out of less" rather than hunting for more shapes before making music with the one you have. (~2:00–2:30)
- **The B-string tuning-compensation rule:** standard tuning is all perfect 4ths except G→B (a major 3rd, one semitone narrower); every time the pattern crosses onto/off the B string, shift **one fret toward the nut**. Demonstrated: B string frets 13 (C), 15 (D) = 2-note set; high E frets 13, 15, 17 (F, G, A) = 3-note set — one octave up from the starting shape. (~2:45–3:30)
- **"Copy and paste" claim, stated directly:** a lick played on the D-string/G-string pair transfers with identical fingering onto the B-string/high-E pair (modulo the B-string compensation) one octave up. (~3:30–3:50)
- **Full ascending diagonal, low E fret 8 to high E fret 17 (three octaves):** low E frets 8, 10 (C, D, 2-note set); A string frets 8, 10, 12 (F, G, A, 3-note set); D string frets 10, 12 (2-note set); G string frets 10, 12, 14 (3-note set); B string frets 13, 15 *(compensated)*; high E frets 13, 15, 17. One continuous climbing line across all six strings. (~3:50–4:40)
- **Second, mirrored diagonal region, built descending from high strings to low strings, rooted at high E string 10th fret (D):** high E frets 10, 8 (D, C, 2-note set played high-to-low); B string frets 10, 8, 6 (A, G, F, 3-note set); G string frets 7, 5 *(compensated -1 fret)*; D string frets 7, 5, 3; A string frets 5, 3; low E string frets 5, 3, 1. Covers fret 1 to fret 10. (~5:30–6:50)
- **Theory aside, stated as a direct correction of a common misconception:** the scale doesn't need to start or end on the root to sound like the key — this descending shape's lowest note is F (♭3), not D, and it still reads as D minor because the backing track's harmonic **context** establishes the key, not the note you start/end on. (~6:20–6:50)
- **Between the two diagonal regions (fret 1–10 and fret 8–17), nearly the entire usable fretboard is covered with just two shapes and one compensation rule.** (~8:00–8:20)
- **Octave-up extension attempt (low confidence — captions garble the fret sequence):** on a 22-fret guitar, the same 2-3 logic is claimed to work starting around the 22nd fret of the high E string; the stated fret list ("22 20 20 18 19 17 19 17 15 17 17 15 17 17 15 down to 13") doesn't cleanly resolve to a single fingering from the transcript alone — flagged as a directional claim ("the same logic keeps working an octave higher"), not a verified tab.
- **Technique tip for the 3-note set specifically:** "the middle note of the three note set is always just the perfect bend" — a standing target for adding expression rather than picking every note cleanly. (~8:20–8:30)
- **Movability, stated directly:** the whole system transfers to any minor key by finding the root anywhere on the neck, then building the 2-note set a whole step below it and the 3-note set on the neighboring string — no need to re-derive from D first. (~8:40–9:00)
- **Closing challenge, stated directly:** pick any D anywhere on the neck ("there's at least one on every string and two on some") and improvise from that root using only the 2-3 system over a D minor backing track, without pre-planning which diagonal region to land in. (~9:00–9:30)

# Nefertiti (Kurt Rosenwinkel solo, Mark Turner's *Ballad Session*)

> A Wayne Shorter composition — famous from the second Miles Davis Quintet — reharmonized/reinterpreted on Mark Turner's album *Ballad Session*, featuring Kurt Rosenwinkel on guitar. This page is not a chord chart or a chart to learn the tune from; it's Jens Larsen's style-trait analysis of four short excerpts from Rosenwinkel's solo, used to reverse-engineer *what makes his melodic language distinct* rather than to transcribe the tune itself. Full breakdown video: [[Kurt Rosenwinkel - This Is What Makes His Style Unique]]. Rosenwinkel's own stated practice method behind the "melody survives the chord change" idea below is documented in full on [[Never-Ending Scale Exercise]].

## Why Nefertiti is the right vehicle for this analysis
Shorter's tunes have "strong melodies and beautiful harmonies but the chords don't really move in the way that we're used to" if you're used to functional standards — the chords sit as distinct colors next to each other rather than resolving into one another. That non-functional harmony is exactly why the video can isolate *melody-first* playing: there's no strong functional pull to lean on, so whatever coherence the line has has to come from the melody's own logic (direction, range, motif), not from outlining a ii-V.

## Solo excerpts by section (Jens Larsen's analysis)

- **Opening phrase — A sus4 → D7alt(♭9)/B♭ → F♯sus4:** starts on a low D, climbs to a high F within one bar — a large fraction of guitar range covered fast, flagged as a Rosenwinkel signature. D major scale (no G) over the A-sus color → up to F, F♯, skip to B♭, play around a B♭ major triad shape for the altered sound → back to F → chromatic half-step descent down to D♯ to land the F♯sus4. The ascending scale run that opens the phrase **doesn't reset direction when the chord changes** — it keeps climbing straight through the barline into the D7alt, "free of the changes." → [[Solo Dynamics and Arc]], [[Never-Ending Scale Exercise]]
- **End-of-first-chorus phrase — E sus4 → E♭7♯11 → A7alt (2 bars) → A♭maj7:** a reharmonization Larsen notes differs from Nefertiti's usual changes — resolves more functionally here (real V–I motion back to the top of the form). Rosenwinkel is noted to **consistently emphasize the ♯11 on E♭7 every time it recurs across the whole solo**, not just here — a deliberate recurring color choice. Line: E7sus4 pickup → down to A → ♯11 on E♭7 → a long ascending run from A up to a high F built from a D♭maj7♯5 arpeggio, then a **leap down a fifth to B♭** instead of the "expected" scale-wise contrary-motion resolution — direction of the melody outranks counterpoint-style resolution rules here. Continuing into the two bars of A7alt: pickup scale run → up, C♯, down an augmented triad → G, skip to C for a descending C♯maj7 arpeggio → back to G → ascending B♭ major triad → descending diminished triad (source material read as B♭ melodic minor) → resolves down from C to B♭. Large range, non-sequential, "different arpeggios tied together" rather than one scale run — what keeps it vocal-like instead of mechanical. → [[Harmonized Scale Triads for Soloing]], [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]]
- **E major7♯11 excerpt:** E major triad, 2nd inversion, from a low B → up to a C♯ minor triad → up to the ♯11 (A♯) → a pentatonic-flavored fragment (A♯, C♯, D) landing back on the major 7 to close the line. Fast, wide-range motion from low B to D♯, then down to C♯ and up to F♯ to set up a chromatic half-step descent into the 5th of the next chord (A). → [[Upper Structure Triads from Seventh Chords]]
- **Motif excerpt — B♭m7 → Amaj7♯11 → E♭7 (Lydian dominant):** motif stated as an **F minor7 arpeggio, deliberately restricted to only those notes**, over B♭m7. Same shape then developed across the next two chords: a descending arpeggio → fifth interval into a descending Amaj7♭5 arpeggio on the Amaj7♯11 → further development on the E♭7 (Lydian dominant) re-emphasizing the ♯11/Lydian color → closes on a descending augmented triad back to A. Rosenwinkel's stated tendency: stick to a small pool of notes for a motif rather than immediately spreading across the whole scale — done here **without the motif reading as obvious or repetitive**, the harder half of the skill. → [[Motivic Development]]

## Steal this
- **Let an ascending (or descending) line keep going through a chord change instead of resetting direction at the barline** — treat the melodic idea, not the chord grid, as the thing generating the next few notes. → [[Never-Ending Scale Exercise]]
- **Cover more of the instrument's range inside a single short phrase than feels "safe"** — low-to-high-and-back within a bar or two, used as a deliberate identity trait, not an accident. → [[Solo Dynamics and Arc]]
- **Build a line from adjacent arpeggios/triads stitched together rather than one scale stretched over the whole passage** — non-sequential, large-interval movement that still reads as coherent because the arpeggios share close voice-leading, not because they're diatonic to one scale. → [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]], [[Harmonized Scale Triads for Soloing]]
- **Restrict a motif to a genuinely small note pool (e.g. one arpeggio) and develop it across two or three chord changes without telegraphing "this is the motif."** → [[Motivic Development]]
- **When a chord's color tone (e.g. a ♯11) is available, come back to it deliberately every time that chord recurs across a whole solo** — a repeated color choice, not incidental.

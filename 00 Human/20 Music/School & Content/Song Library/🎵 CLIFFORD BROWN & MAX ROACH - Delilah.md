# Delilah — Clifford Brown & Max Roach

> Old jazz standard covered by Clifford Brown & Max Roach — a bluesy AABA solo used by Jack Ruch as the worked example for [[Solo Analysis and Concept Extraction]]. Key noted in the source video as "F minor" but every chord in the analysis (i, iv, V) is consistent with **F♯ minor** as tonic — flagged as a likely captioning slip, not corrected in the quote below.

## Chords
**Key:** F♯ minor · **Form:** AABA, 8 bars per section.

**A section (i–iv–V):** **F♯m (i) → Bm7 (iv) → C♯7alt (V)** — a straightforward minor-key cadential shape, the altered dominant setting up the pull back to F♯m at the top of the next section. Not a ii–V (there's no ii chord present — iv stands in its place), so this doesn't map onto the vault's ii-V-I tag; it's closer to a plain minor i–iv–V turn.

**Bridge:** **Bm7 (iv) → C♯7 (V) → D7/D9 (♭VI7, a half-step-above approach chord) → C♯7 (V)** — the ♭VI7 works as a chromatic approach into the dominant rather than a real modulation, resolving back to the V before the final A section.

Full solo vocabulary mapped onto these changes (two full passes — blues-based, then melodic-minor-based, one different solo concept per A-section pass) is below. See [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]], [[Scale Modes Reference]].

## Solo concepts by chord (Jack Ruch's analysis)
Two full passes through the same AABA changes, each pass using a different vocabulary set — see [[Solo Analysis and Concept Extraction]] for the general method these came from.

**Pass 1 (bluesy):**
- Over F♯m (i): bluesy phrasing leaning hard on the ♭5 (blue note) — repetitive, signature minor-blues phrasing. → [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]
- Over Bm7 (iv): a **D major 7 arpeggio** (the relative major of Bm) — outlining the relative-major triad/7th over a minor chord gives a minor-9 color without naming it directly. → [[Chord Substitution]], [[Scale Modes Reference]]
- Over C♯7alt (V): an **upper-structure diminished-7 arpeggio built off the 3rd** of the altered dominant (3rd–5th–♭7–♭9 stack) — a classic bebop device; because a diminished 7th chord is symmetrical, the same shape slides up in minor thirds anywhere over the chord. → [[Altered Chords]], [[Diminished Chords]]

**Pass 2 (melodic-minor):**
- Over F♯m (i): **F♯ melodic minor** — the natural 7 (major 7th) over a minor tonic is the ear-catching move (vs. the expected ♭7 of a plain minor 7 chord). → [[Scale Modes Reference]]
- Over Bm7 (iv) and C♯7alt (V): **C♯ altered scale** (the 7th mode of D melodic minor / built as a mode of F♯ melodic minor's relative material) pushed hard for an "outside" sound. → [[Altered Chords]], [[Scale Modes Reference]]

**Bridge:**
- Over Bm7 (iv): same **D major 7 arpeggio** device as the A section.
- Over C♯7 (V): **F♯ harmonic minor** — reached for as an alternate way of conceiving the same territory as a C♯ Phrygian-dominant scale; the natural (major) 7th against the ♭6 gives a dramatic, "Spanish" color. → [[Scale Modes Reference]]
- Over D7/D9 (♭VI dominant, half-step above the eventual V): **Lydian dominant** (a mode of melodic minor: dominant scale with a ♯11/♯4 and ♭7) — Ruch's own favorite scale sound, used specifically as a half-step-above approach into the following V/target. → [[Scale Modes Reference]]

## Steal this
- **Two solos, one form:** run the exact same changes twice with two deliberately different concept sets (blues vocabulary, then melodic-minor vocabulary) — a fast way to double a practice session's output from one transcription.
- **Relative-major arpeggio over a minor chord** as a quick, ear-friendly minor-9 substitution — works on guitar or any comping instrument, not jazz-specific.
- **Half-step-above dominant as an approach chord** (D7/D9 → C♯7) — a reusable target/approach shape, see [[Secondary Dominants]].

# JAZ-C09 — Substituting a secondary dominant, and the minimum viable form

**Q.** The V is not the only dominant on a chart. Which tritone substitution is commonest in pop, and what is the smallest chord that will do the job?

> [!question]- Answer
> **♭II-of-ii** — the substitute for VI7 on the way to ii — is the commonest flat-five substitution in rock and pop, precisely *because* it is undramatic. It sits inside a progression as a chromatic passing step from iii down to ii, and most listeners never register it as a substitution at all.
>
> And the minimum viable form is **any major triad a semitone above the target**. It does not have to be altered, and it does not have to be a dominant 7th at all.

## Why it matters

Any dominant on a chart is a candidate, not just the V — the substitute is labelled relative to the chord it resolves to, as ♭II of that chord, which is why V/ii, V/V and V/vi can all be swapped the same way.

The minimum viable form is the part most likely to actually get used: a plain major triad a semitone above where you are going is a two-second edit to any progression, on guitar or in a piano roll, and it needs no altered-chord vocabulary to pull off.

This material comes from [[Ch12 — Tritone Substitution and Other Mystery Chords]], which extends the vault's own [[Tritone Substitution]] note rather than repeating it — the mechanism and the bossa-nova case studies were already covered there.

## Drill

Find a iii → ii move in one of your own progressions and slide a major triad a semitone above the ii into the gap. Then do the same in front of a V and a vi and compare how obvious each one sounds.

# making a smooth/sexy rnb beat in 6/8 time signature

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mMZaktspUAA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mMZaktspUAA)

## Synopsis

A from-scratch build whose entire premise is a single production lever: swap the project's time signature from 4/4 to 6/8, and watch it reshape every downstream decision — chord feel, guitar processing, bass width, and especially the drum programming. loner starts in Ableton's Options → Project → General Settings, sets **Signature** to 6/8 (naming 3/4 as the other option he reaches for), then builds a four-chord harmonic-minor guitar progression (chords 1–2 repeat, chords 3–4 switch up) through a genuinely deep 9-stage FX chain — amp sim → reverb → chorus → EQ → a second, specifically-named "Juno" chorus → compressor → tape ("Reels," hiss pushed up) → a pulled-back Kickstart pump → a final heavy reverb — chasing a "Kehlani/Ty Dolla $ign"-style sexy, slow R&B vibe. A simple root-following bass gets a single, very concrete widening move (Goodhertz Mid/Side, boost the side), a piano layer adds depth underneath, and Kontakt's Session Horns bring a brass hit with velocities deliberately pulled down to keep the texture soft rather than harsh. The most instructive section is the drums: loner states directly that this is "the most important part of this beat" for a 6/8 groove, and walks through how a snare that sits in the *same physical position* as a 4/4 beat reads completely differently once the surrounding counting flips from four to three, plus a spaced, mostly-silent kick that only becomes busy for a 3-hit flourish at the very end of the phrase. Spitfire Studio Strings (demo) close out the arrangement, reserved for the hook alongside the horns, while the verse/main groove stays deliberately sparse — guitar and bass only — explicitly to leave room for a vocalist to work. Short, dense, and unusually procedural for a 9-minute video: almost every sentence maps to a concrete signal-chain stage or arrangement decision.

## Techniques

**Time signature / groove foundation**
- **6/8 (or 3/4) as a genuinely different *bounce*, set at the project level before writing a single note.** Options → Project → General Settings → **Signature**, changed from the 4/4 default to **6/8** (3/4 named as the alternative he reaches for). Framed as not just a metronome-click difference but a decision that changes "the whole way you make the beat" — every downstream part (chords, drum placement, feel) gets built around counting in **threes** rather than fours. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Harmonic minor as the "sexy slow R&B" chord-color trigger.** A four-chord guitar progression (chords 1–2 identical, chords 3–4 a switch-up) voiced in harmonic minor, named directly as the source of a "Kehlani/Ty Dolla $ign"-style sexy, slow R&B vibe (captioned "kelani thai dollar sign"). No exact roman-numeral analysis given — a genre/vibe observation, not a reusable voicing recipe, so it stays here rather than on a theory note.

**Guitar — chord source + the 9-stage FX chain (the most detailed section of the video)**
- **Instrument:** [[Kontakt 7]], an unnamed guitar patch, playing the 4-chord harmonic-minor progression above. Played dry first — "very dry, no real depth to it" — before any processing, same before/after habit loner uses across other builds.
- **Full ordered FX chain** (the deepest single chain in this breakdown):
  1. **Bias FX (Positive Grid) guitar-amp sim** — the tone source, loaded first. Unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]].
  2. **Reverb** — an early-chain reverb stage, ahead of everything else that follows (distinct from the heavy reverb that CLOSES the chain — two separate reverb stages bookending the color/width devices in between). → [[Reverb]]
  3. **Chorus #1** — paired with an EQ move to "boost some of the frequencies," a width/texture pass rather than an obvious effect.
  4. **[[EQ Eight]]** — cut on the lows, a boost on some of the highs, and a cut lowering the mids — a scoop-and-lift shape rather than a single corrective move. *(general starting point — no exact Hz/dB given, qualitative only per the transcript)* → [[EQ]]
  5. **[[TAL-Chorus-LX]] — "the Juno chorus," called out by name as a repeat go-to ("I've used this in quite a lot of videos").** A SECOND, separate chorus stage stacked after the EQ — not the same instance as chorus #1 above — for extra width/vintage character on top of the first pass. → [[Modulation]]
  6. **[[Compressor]] — "rhythm guitar" preset, purely to tighten the part.** Dialed by ear via the **input knob** specifically to shave peaks off ("takes some of the peaks out") rather than a stated ratio/threshold. *(general starting point — no exact GR given)* → [[Compression]]
  7. **[[Kramer Tape]] — "Reels," run WITH another chorus pass, explicitly for a "wide, deep, vintage sound," with the Noise/Hiss knob turned UP (not down) for background texture.** Notable contrast against other loner builds already logged on this plugin's page, where "Reels" is more often pulled BACK for a subtler vintage color — here the hiss is a deliberate feature, not a side effect to minimize. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
  8. **[[Kickstart 2]], turned DOWN (pulled back), specifically to make the part "flow in and out"** — a subtle, mostly-inaudible pump rather than an obvious EDM-style duck; the mix knob is the whole technique here.
  9. **Reverb (final, heavy)** — a second, much larger reverb closing the chain, distinct from the smaller reverb stage near the top.
- Net result: a guitar that reads as processed/vintage/wide well beyond what a single "add reverb and call it done" pass would give — width comes from THREE separate devices (2 choruses + tape), not one.

**Bass ([[BASS Slot]])**
- **Deliberately the simplest part in the whole build: root notes only, copied straight from the guitar's chord roots, no extra notes, no independent bassline.** The complexity in this beat is all in the guitar's processing chain and the drums, not the bass part itself.
- **[[Ghz Midside 3]] — boost the Side channel to widen the bass and "really fill the frequency."** A single-move mid/side widening pass (no accompanying low-end mono-lock or filtering mentioned) aimed specifically at making a simple, otherwise-narrow bass patch feel bigger in the mix. → [[Width and Stereo]]

**Piano / depth layer**
- **A piano layered UNDERNEATH the guitar+bass foundation, purely "for more depth," with added top-line notes providing a small extra melodic hook audible in the background** — not a lead part, a bed-thickening layer. → [[BED Slot]]

**Brass ([[LEAD Slot]] / [[MIDS Slot]])**
- **Kontakt's Session Horns library — called out directly as "super fire, like realistic horns."** A short pattern laid over the progression; velocities deliberately **brought DOWN** because higher velocity read as "more harsh" and didn't complement the beat — a taste/texture call, not a mixing-level fix. → [[Kontakt 7]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]

**Drums — "the most important part of this beat" (loner's own framing)**
- **The snare sits in the SAME grid position it would occupy in a 4/4 beat — the reinterpretation comes entirely from counting differently around it, not from moving the hit.** Demonstrated with the metronome left audible: counted **"one-two-three, one-two-three"** instead of "one-two-three-four" — the same physical snare placement reads as a completely different, triplet-feel groove purely because of what surrounds it. A reverbed percussion layer sits underneath the snare for texture. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]]
- **Hi-hat 1: pitch brought DOWN, then shifted (nudged off-grid) more aggressively than a straight 4/4 pattern would tolerate — explicitly licensed by the slower 6/8 tempo.** Because the pattern isn't hitting on every subdivision, a bigger timing push doesn't read as "wrong," it reads as bounce — the same off-grid-shift logic already documented for UK garage on [[Pocket and Microtiming]], here applied because of tempo/density rather than genre. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **Hi-hat 2: an intermittent second hat that hits "every now and then," closing with a roll at the end of the phrase purely for variety** — not a constant pattern layer, a sparse accent.
- **Kick: deliberately minimal — "hardly even hitting" — landing on the downbeat ("the one") each pass, then breaking into a 3-hit flourish only at the very end of the phrase.** The restraint through most of the pattern is what makes the end-of-phrase 3-hit moment read as a fill rather than business-as-usual. → [[Kick Design]]

**Strings ([[AIR Slot]] / [[BED Slot]])**
- **Spitfire Studio Strings, DEMO version only (~15-minute session limit) — enough runway to lay one pattern and bounce it to audio before the demo cuts off.** Named specifically for its articulation variety; the violins were the chosen voice for this build, out of several available articulations. No owned equivalent tracked — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]

**Arrangement**
- **Hook vs. verse instrumentation is a deliberate arrangement split, not an accident: strings + horns are reserved for the hook only; the verse/main groove runs on guitar + bass alone, explicitly to leave the singer room to work.** Stated directly as the reasoning — a sparser verse "gives the singer plenty of room to do what they want." → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

## Synopsis

A beginner sweep-picking lesson framed around BERNTH's own honest early history — years of "faking" sweep arpeggios as dead-note rakes before deliberately fixing the technique — leading into two concrete, still-relevant beginner methods and a Paganini Caprice arrangement as motivating repertoire.

## Techniques

- **Anchor the first and last note of the arpeggio to the metronome.** Rather than trying to place every note correctly at once, lock in the lowest (first) note and the highest (last) note precisely on the click; the notes in between reliably fall into place rhythmically around those two anchors over time. Direct fix for the classic beginner "confusing mess of dead notes in the middle" sound.
- **Two-phase daily routine: slow-and-perfect, then deliberately too-fast-and-sloppy.** Most students over-index on perfection at a slow tempo and never leave it; BERNTH's own breakthrough came from also including a fast, sloppy, no-stakes pass at the end of each sweep session — not aiming for cleanliness at that point, just breaking the individual-string-attack habit at speed. Framed as more effective than a strict linear BPM climb (+2–4 BPM/day).
- **Motivation-driven repertoire choice.** Learning sweep picking purely because it's technically hard (a "flex") tends to stall; picking a specific piece/lick that's genuinely inspiring sustains practice better. Worked example: the opening section of Paganini's Caprice No. 5 (a well-known neoclassical shred arrangement), which is deliberately beginner-accessible because it sweeps in **one direction only** (upward) and connects just two common arpeggio shapes via slides — the intermediate continuation section adds a second direction and more shapes.
- **A named failure mode to recognize in your own or others' playing**: attacking every string individually (down/up strokes per string, like alternate picking) instead of one continuous sweeping motion — works at slow tempos but caps out hard as tempo rises, and is the single most common beginner sweep-picking problem BERNTH sees.

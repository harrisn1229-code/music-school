# 4 less obvious ways to make money as a musician

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdaVBIeOsW4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdaVBIeOsW4)

## Synopsis

David Bennett, on his own 7+ years as a working musician, walks through four income sources beyond the obvious live-gig/teaching pair — library/production music licensing, remote session work, care-home performances, and YouTube — framed around the idea that a sustainable music career is a diversified portfolio, not one big source.

## Techniques

- **Library/production music**: record and publish to a licensing database so TV/ad supervisors can license pre-cleared tracks on deadline, no commission owed upfront; play to your actual strengths rather than chasing every genre; royalties trickle in later via a PRO statement, usually with no way to see where the track actually landed; accept a low hit rate (roughly 1 in 20 tracks earns anything) as normal economics for the format.
- **Remote session work**: home recording + the internet turned session work fully asynchronous — passion-project singer-songwriters up to film scores, often for clients never met in person.
- **Care homes as a performance venue**: fills an underused daytime/midweek calendar slot, a genuinely engaged audience rather than background listeners, and a real documented connection effect — American songbook standards can reach otherwise non-responsive dementia patients.
- **YouTube**: ad revenue is the least interesting lever on its own; for a growing channel a viral video's real value is as a marketing engine driving inbound bookings/opportunities for the rest of a career; monetization stacks (ads, Patreon, sponsorships once an audience exists, merch); covers get copyright-flagged but can often still partially monetize.

# Everybody's Got Something To Hide Except For Me And My Monkey — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A John rave-up whose whole design is **extremely slow harmonic rhythm plus an invisible seam between verse and refrain**, so the track reads as an improvisation sliced arbitrarily out of a longer take rather than a composed song. Pollack hears it as an early sketch of the primal-scream manner.

**Form.** Three verse/refrain cycles into a fade. The verse is **12 bars sitting on I** and nothing else; the refrain is **24 bars** where the changes finally start moving. Because the verse has no cadence to end on, you can't hear where it stops. The **first refrain is eight bars short**, entering only at the point where the chords reach IV — without that cut the song would spend an unbearable stretch on one chord ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]). The refrain's poetic pattern thins as it runs: two phrases that sound like more verse, two sung only in their first halves, two entirely instrumental. **Words get sparser exactly as the chords speed up** ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). Two bars of 3/4 interrupt the last phrase.

**Harmony.** The core is I / IV / ♭VII, extended one further step around the flat side to **♭III** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). Two cross-relations fall out of that: V against ♭VII, and I against ♭III. The final two phrases run the same three chords **forwards then backwards**, a small palindrome mirroring the inside/outside gag in the lyric.

**Melody.** Mostly chanted on a repeated pitch, closer to rapping than to a tune; it becomes an actual melody only at the title phrase and the guitar lick that follows. The meter shift lands on top of that lick, so the best guitar line and the biggest rhythmic jolt arrive together.

**Arrangement.** Thick pulsing backing from stacked guitar overdubs. The shaken bell only *seems* continuous — it's dropped and reintroduced deliberately. The outro strips the instruments out for four bars, leaving the vocal noise exposed, then resumes the one-chord vamp.

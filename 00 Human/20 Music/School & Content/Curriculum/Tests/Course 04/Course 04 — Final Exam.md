> Committing to a position removes the decision that piano players never have to make.
>
> **Q12.** **Key and time signature · roadmap · range · rhythm hotspots · a tempo you can hold**
>
> **Q13.** **The roadmap and the hardest-bar tempo**
> The roadmap stops you playing into a repeat you didn't see; the tempo choice stops you outrunning yourself partway through.
>
> **Q14.** **Keep the pulse — thin it out and rejoin at the next downbeat. Never stop and never go back**
>
> **Q15.** **The position decides whether each note has one place or several — that's a reading load**
> *Rubric (5):* says an uncommitted position leaves the pitch-to-place decision open on every note (2) · says committing removes that decision so attention goes to reading (1) · connects the range step to picking a position that contains the whole phrase (2)
> Technique is whether your hand can execute a shape. The position choice is upstream of that: until you've made it, every written note carries an unresolved question about where to play it, and answering it in real time is what consumes the attention reading needs. Committing to a position converts guitar's one-to-many mapping into a one-to-one one, which is why it makes the page readable. The range step of the scan exists to serve this — checking the highest and lowest note first tells you which position contains the whole phrase, so you commit once rather than discovering mid-line that you have to shift.
>
> **Q16.** **The reference layer and the skill are different things, and only one is testable**
> Reps close the gap.
> *Rubric (5):* says the test measures knowledge about notation, which is genuinely necessary (2) · says reading itself is perceptual-motor and can't be assessed in text (2) · names daily cold-reading through the lesson plans as what builds it (1)
> The course teaches a reference layer — dots, navigation, why position matters — and that layer is real and load-bearing: without it, an unfamiliar sign stops the read cold. A test can measure exactly that, and should. What it can't touch is the real-time conversion of symbol to motion, because that's a motor skill and text can only ask about it. So the course does the part a course can do and hands the rest to the daily cold-reads in [[Sight Reading]] and to [[Course 25 — Reading I — First Position, Part One]], which is nothing but reps. The test is the floor rather than the goal, and that's a design decision rather than a shortfall.

# ValhallaVintageVerb

![[Images/ValhallaVintageVerb.png]]

**Category:** Reverb/Delay
**Vendor:** Valhalla DSP

## What it is

Valhalla's widely-used algorithmic reverb modeling the character of vintage 1970s-90s digital reverb units (plates, rooms, halls, and specialty modes).

## What it's used for

Vintage-flavored reverb character across vocals, drums, and instruments — one of the most commonly used third-party reverbs in modern production.

## FX chain notes

Used on a send/return as a primary or secondary reverb, often the main vocal reverb in a mix.

## Techniques

- **Reverb taste built around "dead on top" tails and a strong preference for a physical chamber over anything clinically accurate.** Greg Wells: "I like reverbs to be sort of dead on top, and my favorite reverb is the Capitol Chambers — the live chambers that they have, 30 feet under the floor down in the machine room at Capitol — that stuff just sounds amazing, sounds really old-fashioned, but it's just musical and incredible. I hate really clean and accurate reverbs, it sounds goofy to me." Owned-lane read on both halves of that quote: **Chamber mode** for the room type, **High Shelf/High Freq pulled well down** for the dead-top darkness — deliberately dialed AWAY from a bright/pristine setting even on a vocal, matching this device's own "darker tails sit behind bright sources" guidance below. Capitol Chambers itself is a physical room, not a purchasable unit — no [[Vintage Gear Worklist]] entry. *(qualitative — no decay/mix values given)* → [[Reverb]] (effect), [[Depth and Space]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]] — Greg Wells (Mix with the Masters), [[Bringing vocals out of the speakers with Greg Wells]]
- **Very heavy setting used specifically to darken/bury a free Pianobook violin bank, immediately after an EQ cut that already "drowned the sound out a bit."** On a chill/ambient Frank Ocean-style beat, a violin counter-melody gets pushed heavy into this plugin, then forced almost fully mono and hard-panned right for its own pocket in the mix ("I found when it was stereo it was filling too much space"). Also used as the "medium-sized vintage verb" stage of a separate Kontakt flute's 4-effect chain (reversed-rhythm reverb → **vintage verb** → half-time Cassette Crushed → EQ → Portal) and as the piano chain's reverb stage, right before a closing [[ValhallaDelay]]. → [[Kontakt 7]], [[EQ]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Frank Ocean Chill Ambient Chain]] — loner, [how to make chill ambient beats for frank ocean (sad/melodic chord technique)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=whI75NJcom8)
- **Use ROOM-style presets (not hall/plate) specifically for real-instrument samples, to sell "someone's actually playing it in a room."** General tip stated directly: "use room presets when you're picking a reverb, especially if you're using a sample with real instruments like a piano or Rhodes — you wanted to have that real feeling." Two applications in the same build: a small/reflective room preset on an electric-piano (Wurli) chord bed (small decay, high wet), and a separate ambient-style room preset on a doubled vintage-upright piano layer (kept short, high mix). → [[Reverb]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Future-Style Vintage Sample Chain]] — loner, [this is how frank dukes & cubeatz make vintage samples in 2022!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nak-TAIPQ-c)
- **Dark vocal room preset (size pulled well down, mix ~90) as the finishing move on a harmony vocal, applied only after that layer's AlterBoy chain runs at full 100% mix (vs. the main vocal's subtler, unreverbed ~50%-mix AlterBoy double).** Reconstructing a Post Malone-style pop beat: the main vocal gets a pitched-up/formant-down AlterBoy ghost double at ~50% mix and no reverb of its own, while the harmony vocal instead runs AlterBoy's "vocal chorus" preset → EQ → AlterBoy formant down (no pitch shift) at full mix/drive → this plugin's dark room preset, nearly full-wet — reverb is reserved for the more heavily-processed of the two layers. Also used as the "make it sound like it's in a room" reverb on a piano breakdown, standing in for an Abbey Road Chambers reverb named in the source (owned equivalent: 1970s Color, chamber-family mode). *(no exact size/decay figures given beyond "bring it down quite a bit")* → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Kramer Tape]], [[1 Harmony Vocal Track]], [[1 Acoustic Piano Track]] — loner, [making a billboard number 1 pop beat for post malone's next album from scratch!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WX_IB6K-xIo)
- **The third of three near-identically-processed vocal takes gets THIS added, and nothing else, as its whole distinguishing move.** In a three-layer vocal harmony stack (center / hard-right / hard-left, each a genuinely different melody/harmony, not a doubled take), all three share one base chain (Auto-Tune → compressor → [[Little AlterBoy]] → EQ); take 1 adds Little AlterBoy's "vocal dub" preset, take 2 swaps to its "vocal chorus" preset, and take 3 keeps the base chain but adds this plugin instead — "just to slightly switch it up, give it a different sound." Auto-captioned simply "vintage verb." *(general starting point — no decay/mix figures given)* → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Kendrick Lamar Soul Sample Chain]] — loner, [how to make the most realistic vintage soul samples for kendrick lamar!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oMbeATqXOww)
- **Chorus-modulated reverb, built as a 2-macro FL Patcher chain — the Ableton-native version is a rack: this plugin → a chorus set to a flanging preset, chained output-to-input.** Recreating one feature of the KMA Cirrus hardware pedal ("a chorus modulated reverb setting"): reverb Mix and Decay each mapped to their own macro, plus a separate Mix macro on the chorus stage — three knobs standing in for the whole chain. The stated reasoning for stacking a *separate* chorus after the reverb instead of using the reverb's own internal modulation: "you can modulate the reverb inside Valhalla but that's not giving you a unique sound, everyone can do that" — an arbitrary second device is what makes the combination personal. Closing EQ cut on the low end. → [[TAL-Chorus-LX]], [[EffectRack]], [[Reverb]], [[Modulation]], [[TEXTURE Slot]] — loner, [this secret plugin will change the way you process your melodies!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xwuodd40bug)
- **A short, contained room preset on a piano layer — mix and size both pulled well down for a small, close space rather than a wash.** A community-stem sample flip runs this plugin's room-reverb-family preset on a Postcard Piano progression, mix brought down to roughly 50% and size also reduced. *(exact size value not given — "bring that down")* → [[Reverb]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — loner, [turning your melodies into crazy full samples!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M8saMeKUdTE)
- **The Beatles' "Blue Jay Way" reverse-vocal-through-a-Leslie trick, recreated on a bounced/reversed vocal chop.** Beatles history note: they reportedly recorded the entire song backwards through a Leslie speaker, then chopped parts of that reversed pass to lead into sections of the forward song. Recreation on a period-accurate psych-rock sample build: bounce the vocal, reverse it, chop two short reversed sections and splice them back in as lead-ins, then finish with [[Phaser-Flanger]] + this plugin (as "a vintage verb"). Framed in the source as proof the era was genuinely experimental — "unheard of" when the Beatles did it. → [[Reverb]], [[Phaser-Flanger]], [[60s - Beatles]], [[Beatles Psych-Rock Sample Chain]] — loner, [these 1960's production secrets will change the way you make samples!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ngz_fZraTP4)
- **Very light send on a 3-take vocal stack, deliberately withheld from the driest of the three takes for contrast.** A dry center take + two hard-panned takes share a chain (Auto-Tune, a light formant effect, this plugin very lightly, [[Decapitator]], EQ) — but the left-panned take is called out as staying drier/without reverb specifically, so only the center and right takes carry the reverb, keeping one element of the stack anchored and unprocessed. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Auto-Tune Pro]] — loner, [instantly make your melodies sound like vintage samples with this secret trick!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=amvSl5ACFeo)
- **Notch out a "bad harmonic" resonance in the reverb return before it stacks in the mix** — when a reverb return reveals a specific ringing/resonant frequency the dry source didn't have (a "bad harmonic" introduced by the reverb algorithm itself, not the source), cut it with a narrow notch on the return rather than accepting it — caught while dialing in VintageVerb deep in a long sound-design chain. → [[EQ]] — Eli derp, [How To Make Insanely Phat Basses with @eliderp_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8a84OhMSfzM)
- **The 2022 long-vocal-reverb pick** — replaced Valhalla Room as Kenny's long/super-long vocal tier ("just like the way it's been sounding on vocals"); 100% wet on a send, band-limited on the return — Kenny Beats, [New Vocal Chain 2022](https://youtu.be/-SvBNOBWJ_8)
- **Concert Hall + 1980s Color as the default go-to** ("bright hall," a self-described "more 80s reverb guy than a 70s guy") over the 1970s Color's deader, darker top end — the 1980s setting is the default first reach for pads/synths, saving the darker 1970s Color for something meant to sit further back (e.g. behind a vocal); cites George Michael's "Jesus to a Child" (an extreme, artist-driven vocal reverb choice, likely a Lexicon-era digital hall) as the reference for how far reverb-on-vocal can go when the artist (not the engineer) pushes it — Disclosure, [First Twitch Stream Ever (Production Tutorial)](https://youtu.be/IObzp3OugtY)
- **Reverse-reverb "dream" texture** — full-wet verb + reversed MIDI, printed then reversed, for a swelling wash — Disclosure, [You've Got To Let Go](https://youtu.be/1iRPvK1gaOI)
- **High-pass the reverb send** so space doesn't muddy the low end (reverb send = Valhalla + EQ cutting lows + sidechain) — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8)

## Manual

- **No PDF manual** — Valhalla plugins are self-documenting (hover any control for the tooltip readout, bottom-left of the UI).

### Sources
- [ValhallaVintageVerb: The MODES](https://valhalladsp.com/2023/02/10/valhallavintageverb-the-modes/) — the official per-mode breakdown (the closest thing to a manual)
- [Valhalla DSP documentation archives](https://valhalladsp.com/category/learn/documentation-learn/) — Sean Costello's blog posts double as the deep docs
- YouTube: "Valhalla VintageVerb: A Complete Step-by-Step Guide" walkthroughs cover every control

**High-level points:**
- The plugin is a **Mode × Color grid**: ~20 algorithms (Mode) × three eras (Color: 1970s / 1980s / NOW). Mode picks the space, Color picks the technology — 1970s adds dark bandwidth limits and gritty pitchy modulation, 1980s is brighter with digital artifacts, NOW is clean.
- Its lineage is the classic **Lexicon 224/480-style** hall algorithms — lush, modulated, deliberately *not* natural.
- **Modulation (Rate/Depth) is the identity**: it detunes the tail slightly so long reverbs stay alive instead of ringing metallic.
- Designed for **send/return use** — Mix at 100% on a return, size the space with Decay + Predelay.
- Bass Mult × Bass Freq controls whether the low tail blooms (huge 80s toms) or tightens (busy mixes).

## Synopsis

Ten technique adjustments BERNTH says produced outsized results, opening with two string-noise fixes and a picking-consistency fix.

## Techniques

- **Index-finger pre-mute on the neighboring string during scale runs.** While fretting a note on one string (e.g. G), lightly rest the index finger against the adjacent string (e.g. D) even though it isn't being played — if an accidental pull-off motion or other noise happens on that neighbor, it's already muted. A preemptive, always-on muting habit rather than a reactive fix.
- **Don't let fingers/knuckles rub against the strings while playing.** Named as the fix that eliminated his need for a fretwrap/string dampener entirely — physical contact between the picking hand's fingers/knuckles and the strings (not the pick itself) was the actual noise source, distinct from palm-muting technique.
- **Eliminate individual thumb/index-finger joint movement within the pick grip for day-to-day consistency.** Distinct from wrist/arm motion size (covered elsewhere) — this is about the pick grip itself staying rigid: early on, the thumb and index finger moved independently while picking, which produced inconsistent results session to session (good technique one day, falling apart at the same BPM the next) with no repeatable cause. Switching to a stiff, closed-hand grip with no individual finger/joint movement (support the index finger with the other fingers, not full-fist tension) fixed the inconsistency directly.

# Harmonic Minor Improvisation Devices

Six concrete devices for improvising over harmonic-minor-flavored progressions (a minor i chord with a major/dominant V, the classic minor-key harmonic-minor trigger), taught by guest player Kyo inside a Brandon D'Eon lesson. Complements the scale-mechanics side of [[Harmonic Minor Modes]] and [[Harmonic Minor Scale Practice Ladder]] — this page is about *what to actually play*, not how to drill the scale shapes.

## 1. Emphasize the chord's characteristic/color tone, not just "the scale"

Over any chord, identify the one note that most defines its color and lean into it rather than treating the whole scale as equally available. Two worked examples over a Cm → Fm progression: avoid the 3rd of Fm (A♭) while sitting on the Cm chord (it clashes there), then deliberately emphasize that same A♭ once the chord actually becomes Fm. Over the V chord (G7, drawn from C harmonic minor), the target color tone is **B natural** — it's simultaneously the major 7th of C and the major 3rd of G7, and it's the note that most signals "harmonic minor" is actually happening rather than plain natural minor.

## 2. Chromatic approach / bebop-style passing tone into the dominant

Add one extra chromatic note to the scale to create a passing tone that lands arpeggio/chord tones on strong beats — mechanically the same device as the "blue note" in a minor pentatonic scale, and specifically named as **the bebop scale** concept (the extra note isn't something to memorize by name — the point is just deliberately placed chromaticism, not a new scale to learn). Over a G dominant chord, the added note is **F♯** — the chromatic note sitting a half step below the dominant's root, approached and left quickly (like a blue note, it's a passing color, not a note to rest on) — a small, reusable "charm" tone under any dominant chord.

## 3. Augmented triad symmetry over a minor-major7 chord

An augmented triad repeats itself (same notes, same shape) every **major 3rd** — so one movable shape covers three roots. Works especially well over a **minor-major7** chord (e.g. Cm(maj7)) for a modern-sounding color. Practical payoff: learn one augmented triad shape and you already know it transposed a major 3rd up or down, since the notes are identical.

## 4. Diminished arpeggio symmetry over the dominant (harmonic-minor V7)

For a dominant chord drawn from harmonic minor (the V7), build a **diminished 7 arpeggio starting from the dominant's 3rd** rather than its root. Worked over B7 (the V7 of E harmonic minor): the diminished arpeggio from B7's 3rd (D♯) yields D♯-F♯-A-C — every note except the root belongs to the B7 chord, and the "extra" note (C) is the ♭9, a genuinely good tension note for a harmonic-minor dominant. Like the augmented triad above, a diminished arpeggio repeats itself every **minor 3rd**, so the same movable shape covers four different diminished-chord contexts a minor 3rd apart.

## 5. The hidden major triad inside an augmented-major7 chord

An augmented chord with a major 7 (e.g. G augmented maj7) contains a **major triad built on its own 3rd** once the root is removed (G augmented maj7 minus its root G leaves a B major triad). Soloing with just that upper major triad — rather than the full augmented-maj7 arpeggio — reads as sophisticated/modern, because the ear hears a clean, familiar triad shape riding on top of a more complex harmony. General rule: over any augmented-major7 chord, target the major triad built on that chord's 3rd.

## 6. Borrowing a dominant sound briefly between two chords for tension

When a progression moves i → iv (e.g. Cm → Fm) with no dominant chord actually present, briefly *treat* the i chord as if it were a dominant (C7 instead of Cm) for a short passing moment mid-phrase, applying F harmonic minor over that brief dominant illusion, before landing on the real iv chord (Fm). This isn't sustainable for a whole phrase (it can read as a wrong note if held too long), but as a brief passing gesture it manufactures forward pull toward the iv chord that the actual chart doesn't harmonically provide. A deliberate, situational reharmonization move, not a rule to apply on every minor-to-minor chord change.

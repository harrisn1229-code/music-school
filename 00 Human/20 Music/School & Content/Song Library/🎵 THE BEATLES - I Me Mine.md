# I Me Mine — The Beatles

> Harrison's song about the ego. Alternates between a slow waltz and a faster hard-rock feel. Extended with Phil Spector overdubs for the Let It Be album — Lennon had already left the band by the time overdubs were recorded.

## Chords
**Key:** G minor verse (3/4 waltz) → G major chorus (4/4) · capo 2 on the recording

**Verse (waltz):** Gm – C – F – D – Gm … Cm – D – Gm – B♭ – E♭
→ **i – IV – ♭VII – V – i … iv – V – i – ♭III – ♭VI** (the major **C (IV)** gives a Dorian lift)

**Chorus ("I-me-me mine"):** G7 – C7 – G7 – D7
→ **I7 – IV7 – I7 – V7** (flips to a bluesy G **major**)

## Production & Arrangement
Harrison's **waltz-verse-into-rock-chorus** — notable as the **last thing the Beatles recorded together** (Jan 1970), later padded out with Spector strings. A 3/4 lament that snaps to 4/4 heaviness.

## Steal this
- **Meter flip for contrast:** a 3/4 verse crashing into a 4/4 chorus → [[Chord Progressions]].
- Minor-key lament + bluesy chorus.
- Strings to inflate a short song to length (Spector's move).

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — I Me Mine]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #170.*

**Form.** A folk/blues hybrid over a hard waltz, and the technically remarkable thing is the **metric modulation between the 3/4 verse and the 4/4 refrain, which is arithmetically exact rather than approximate**. In numbers: the verse runs one 3/4 bar per second (180 bpm); divide each bar into two dotted quarters and you get 120, which becomes the refrain's quarter note; coming back, split the 4/4 bar into two halves of two beats and you are back at the original bar rate. Against that precision everything else is left unresolved — both sections open out from i, there is no outro, and the track simply stops. Intro is the verse opening **foreshortened to 6 bars by eliding into verse 1**; verse an unusually long **32 bars, AA′BA″**; refrain **10 bars** — it wants to be a 12-bar blues frame and is cut short by the transition back ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]).

**Harmony.** `Verse (32): i – IV – ♭VII – V7 – i (×2, 8 bars each) · iv (4) – V9 (4) · i over a chromatic bass A–G♯–G♮–F♯ – F7 = VI (4+4)` · `Refrain (10): i (4) · iv (2) – i (2) · V (2)`. The verse plays the three minor scales against each other — **a major IV in a minor key**, and ♭VII moving to V, which puts the natural minor's G♮ against the harmonic minor's G♯ ([[SCL-C02 — Harmonic and melodic minor]], [[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). Steering the verse to **VI7** as its open target is genuinely unusual next to a refrain that ends conventionally on V.

**Notable.** The tune does little more than alternate scale degrees 5 and 6 at the structural level, which reinforces the unfinished quality. Paul's bass is the most composed element ([[POP-C04 — Four chords with a moving bass]]): on any chord lasting two or more bars he **jumps a third from the root to the chord's middle note**, and the final phrase carries a fully exposed chromatic descent. The piano is withheld until the refrain.

**Against this page.** The `key:` field here says A major. **Pollack reads the song in A minor** — the same sounding pitch as this page's "G minor verse, capo 2," but he treats the minor as the home key rather than the major refrain. Nothing above has been changed.

# Basic Bass Plucking Technique (Beginner Bass Basics)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CR8yQCZX2HQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CR8yQCZX2HQ)

## Synopsis

A short, focused BassBuzz beginner video that isolates the plucking-hand fundamentals down to their absolute floor — thumb anchoring, alternating index-middle plucking, and the pull-across-not-up tone rule — then immediately puts them to work in a two-string, open-string "disco" play-along groove (E and A strings only, no fretting). Every core mechanic here (thumb anchor, alternating plucking, pull-across) is already the vault's most thoroughly documented beginner topic — see [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]] and [[Bass Note Muting Technique]], both of which already carry multiple independent corroborations of these exact same rules from other BassBuzz videos, including [[Beginner Bass Lesson No. 1 (Your Very First Lesson)]]'s near-identical Step 5. The video's one genuinely fresh contribution is the specific two-open-string groove itself, built up across three tempos (slow/medium/full), plus a crisp, explicit statement of the left-hand mute move needed to keep two open strings from ringing together mid-groove.

## What's taught

- **Thumb anchor.** Rest the right thumb on the edge of a pickup (either one, if the bass has two — exact pickup doesn't matter for this drill) to anchor the hand so the fingers can consistently find the strings. *(Corroborates the existing thumb-anchor entries on [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]] and [[Bass Note Muting Technique]] fix 1 — no new mechanic.)*
- **Alternating index-middle plucking.** Pluck with the index and middle fingers, alternating back and forth (index-middle-index-middle…) — named "alternating plucking." Framed as what lets fast-sounding lines be played without the fingers themselves needing to move especially fast, since the workload alternates between two fingers instead of repeating on one. *(Corroborates the existing alternating-plucking-as-default entries on [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]].)*
- **The "big tip" — pull across, not up and away.** Demonstrated live, close-up: don't pull the plucking finger up and away from the bass after a pluck (the common beginner move) — pull it **across**, toward the thumb/the next string down. On the E string, the plucking fingers should end up hitting the thumb after every pluck; on the A, D, or G string, the plucking fingers should land on and touch the next-lowest string after every pluck. Pulling up and away instead **thins out the tone**; pulling across gives the real, fuller bass sound and reads as noticeably less like a beginner. Same plucking direction applies to both index and middle fingers. *(This is the vault's now-fourth-or-fifth independent corroboration of the pull-across/rest-stroke rule — see [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]]'s "third/fourth independent corroboration" entry and [[Bass Note Muting Technique]]'s "rest stroke" fix 2, both already sourced from other BassBuzz videos.)*
- **The disco groove — built up in stages, open strings only, no fretting:**
  1. **Isolate each string first.** Four free plucks (index-middle-index-middle, out of rhythm) on the open E string, then the same four plucks on the open A string, just to get the sound and feel of each string under the fingers before adding rhythm.
  2. **The whole groove, out of rhythm:** back and forth between E and A, four plucks each — called out as a genuinely common real-world bassline shape ("a lot of basslines really are this simple"), not just a warm-up.
  3. **Add rhythm — half notes, pluck on 1 and 3.** Count a 4-beat pulse; pluck on beat 1 and again on beat 3 (each note rings for two full counts, nothing played on 2 or 4). Full pattern: pluck 1 (index) → let ring through 2 → pluck 3 (middle) → let ring through 4, repeating on the E string for one 4-count, then the A string for one 4-count, alternating E-A-E-A across the phrase, finishing on the E string.
  4. **The muting problem this groove exposes, and the fix: touch the previous open string with the left (fretting) hand.** After finishing on the A string and moving back to pluck the E string, the A string is often still ringing underneath — audibly demonstrated. Fix: as you move from the A string to the E string, **touch the A string with the left hand** to stop it ringing, while being careful **not** to touch the E string itself (or you'll mute the note you actually want). Framed as an optional add-on for players who can handle the extra coordination — fine to skip and just enjoy the groove if it's too much at once. *(This is the vault's clean statement of fretting-hand open-string muting specifically inside a live two-string groove, rather than as an isolated concept — the general mechanic itself already exists as [[Bass Note Muting Technique]]'s "open-string mute: … the fretting hand reaching over to touch the open string.")*
  5. **Three-speed play-along.** The full E-A half-note groove is drilled at slow, then medium, then full speed (the "full speed… disco" feel referenced in the video's own teaser), each preceded by a 4-count. The channel offers extended versions of all three speeds, plus a no-bass-track version, on the BassBuzz site for further practice.

## Synopsis

Not a lesson in the usual sense from this channel — this is Jared (Sound Guitar Lessons, teaching professionally since ~19, ~6 years on YouTube posting weekly evergreen lessons) launching a new, deliberately looser weekly format he's calling a "practice log" (he wanted to call it a "blog," his wife vetoed it). The premise: his regular lesson videos are built to be timeless, prescriptive, one-stop tutorials; this new series is the opposite — recorded and posted same-day, no rehearsed teacherly framing, just "here's what I'm actually working on this week," admittedly risking that it clashes with his channel's usual algorithmic lane (hence the title). Most of the video (~20 of 25 min) is meta — why he's doing this, how it differs from his lesson content, his philosophy that he thinks of himself as a student sharing what works rather than a teacher instructing from above. Two genuinely useful, concrete threads run through the framing: (1) a real update on fixing chipped classical/fingerstyle nails without giving up his return to nails after years of playing without them, and (2) a walkthrough of the practice-system screen he uses to structure a 2:30 daily practice window, including a "finish designing it, then actually practice it" discipline he's imposed on himself. No guitar is played on camera in this video — he's explicit that's coming in future entries.

## What's taught

- **Nail repair for classical/fingerstyle guitar, glue-the-dead-part-only method.** Jared plays classical/nylon-string with nails for tone, has historically had weak/floppy nails, and is mid-recording-project so can't afford a 1-2 week regrowth wait when a nail chips. Method: superglue **only the white, already-dead free edge** of the nail tip — never let it touch the live nail bed/cuticle, which is the actual health risk with nail products. Only works on a **partial** crack/chip (a beginning slice, or a piece still overlapping/peelable) — not a nail that's fully snapped off. Used successfully on 3 separate chips during one intensive recording stretch. Full extraction and a reusable version of this now lives on **[[Guitar Nail Care]]**.
- **Post-glue polish: 500-grit classical guitar nail paper.** After the glue sets, buff the repaired tip with nail paper sold specifically for classical guitar players, 500 grit — smooths the glue bump and, per Jared, leaves the tip sounding *thicker* than the bare nail did, not just repaired.
- **"Healthy Hoof" cuticle oil, nightly, for future nail growth (not the current nail).** A viewer-recommended product, rubbed into the cuticle every night before bed. The logic (per Jared, presented as unverified — he calls it possibly "snake oil" but worth trying): it's the cuticle that feeds new nail growth, so benefits show up weeks later in nail that hasn't grown out yet, not the nail you have today.
- **Daily practice window: 3-hour block → 2:30 (150 min) of actual practice, 30 min of built-in breaks**, tracked to the minute with a phone app called **Multi Timer** — times both work blocks and break blocks. Describes this level of precision as sounding "OCD" but functionally calming/centering for him rather than stressful.
- **Practice-window category breakdown** (from a screen-share of his personal system, still being refined): **Best Warm-up** (~5 min, always done first, currently sung along with) → **Technique exercises** (drawn from his own "Top-Notch Techniques" course material — a running list of drills he's found effective over time) → **Vocabulary** (distinct from technique: scales through the circle of fourths, arpeggios/chord-tone work — tied to actual musical structure rather than pure mechanics) → **Ear training** → **Repertoire** (classical guitar rep + jazz standards) → **"Jared Blankets"** (his term for learning pop/rock covers outside jazz/classical — explicitly low priority, not yet recorded) → **Songwriting rehearsal** (playing through his own songs) → **"The Fee"** (recording a vertical/short-form clip of whatever he's practicing that session, purely for content — treated as a fun byproduct rather than a chore, and useful even unposted just to record-and-watch-back).
- **"Finish designing the system before practicing it" rule.** He's given himself explicit permission to spend practice time refining the system/checklist itself, but with a hard stop: once a category's system is "done," he has to actually practice it before moving on to design the next category. Framed as a guard against the trap of endlessly redesigning a practice routine as a way to avoid practicing it — a risk he names directly.
- **Deliberate exception slot.** He leaves room to go fully off-list on a given day if inspired (names drums, a newly-bought clarinet) as a pressure valve, as long as he returns to the core system afterward.
- **Linear execution for the "output" categories.** Rather than splitting attention across several repertoire pieces or songs at once, work one through to a recorded output before starting the next — named as his main current focus for the year.
- **Format rationale (general teaching-content lesson, not guitar-specific):** he separates "evergreen, prescriptive lesson content" from "real-time, current, less-polished process content" as two different value propositions for an audience — the former is a reference resource, the latter is relatability/behind-the-scenes. Framed explicitly as an experiment he'll drop if it underperforms or feels wrong.

# DRM-C07 — Velocity range, not velocity value

**Q.** What does dragging a velocity *range* do in Ableton, and when does it stop helping?

> [!question]- Answer
> Each hit plays back at a **random velocity within the band** on every loop pass, so no two passes are identical. Keep the band narrow — too wide reads as inconsistent playing rather than natural playing. It pays off far more on multi-sampled/round-robin kits, where a wider band also swaps articulation rather than just changing volume. Cheapest version with no randomisation at all: alternate hat velocity high/low/high/low.

## Why it matters

It's the one place randomisation genuinely models what a player does, which is why it works where random *timing* doesn't. The narrow-band constraint is the whole difference between the two.

## Drill

Set a narrow velocity range on a hi-hat line, loop it eight times, and listen for whether any pass sounds wrong. Widen until one does — that's your ceiling.

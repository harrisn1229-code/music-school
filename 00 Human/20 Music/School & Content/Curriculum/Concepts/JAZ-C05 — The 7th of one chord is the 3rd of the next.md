# JAZ-C05 — The 7th of one chord is the 3rd of the next

**Q.** Run the guide tones through Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7 and say what the two lines do.

> [!question]- Answer
> Dm7 is F and C · G7 is B and F · Cmaj7 is E and B. One line runs **C → B → B**, the other **F → F → E**.
>
> Nothing leaps. One voice holds while the other moves a half step, then they swap. **Each chord's 3rd is held over as the next chord's 7th, and each chord's 7th falls a half step onto the next chord's 3rd** — that is not a coincidence, it is what root motion by fifths does, and it is the mechanical reason the cell sounds inevitable rather than merely conventional.

## Why it matters

It turns voice leading into a procedure instead of a judgement call: solve the horizontal problem first, fix any leap bigger than a step by inverting the chord or choosing a different extension, and only then decide the full voicings.

It also gives the concrete test for a substitution. A substitute that keeps the guide-tone lines stepwise is a good one — which is exactly why the tritone sub works, since it *swaps* the guide tones rather than disturbing them.

## Drill

Write the 3rd and 7th of every chord in a tune as two lines. Circle every leap larger than a step and re-voice that chord until the leap is gone.

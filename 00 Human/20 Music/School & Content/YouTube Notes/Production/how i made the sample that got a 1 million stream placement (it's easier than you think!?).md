# how i made the sample that got a 1 million stream placement (it's easier than you think!?)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=atjGOpxBzag)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=atjGOpxBzag)

## Synopsis

loner reverse-engineers a three-element sample he sent out that ended up placed on a song with over a million streams, then explains — element by element — why it got picked and what he deliberately left OUT. The sample is built from just three sources: a simple four-chord AAS Lounge Lizard electric-piano progression (major-to-minor for an emotional arc), a second Lounge Lizard patch chopped and processed into a pulsing pad, and a counter-melody guitar pulled from a Kontakt "Evolution Strawberry" bank and run through a Bias FX amp chain. The most valuable material isn't the processing (which is fairly plain — EQ, reverb, chorus, delay, a Kickstart pulse) but the restraint argument that frames the whole video: loner also built a percussion loop and a horn layer for this same sample and cut BOTH before sending it, specifically because he was making an "afro sample" and didn't want to pre-commit the other producer's drum choices, and because he caught himself adding the horns "just to flex my muscles as a producer" rather than because the sample needed them. He states directly that if he'd kept those elements in, the song "wouldn't exist" — the other producer (credited only as "a producer tempo") needed the harmonic/melodic space to add their own transitions and melodic elements. Two closing rules of thumb: leave gaps between chord hits for "bounce," and the two things that actually make a sample work are good sound selection and a melody simple enough for an artist to sing over, that also suits the target genre's vibe.

## Techniques

**Sound 1 — chord bed (AAS Lounge Lizard, "blues comp" preset):**
- **A four-chord progression: F major → G major → C (passing note) → A minor → D (passing note) → E minor (pitched up an octave to match the chord before it).** Deliberately simple; the first two chords are major (melodic, upbeat feel), the last two minor (more emotional/sad) — a stated major→minor split used as an emotional-arc device rather than picking one mood and staying in it. → [[Melody Writing]], [[BED Slot]]
- **Leave audible gaps between chord hits, on purpose, BEFORE drums are added — the simplicity is what makes the gaps stand out and gives the part "bounce."** Also strummed the chords (rather than block-triggered) for a more natural, human feel. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Processing: EQ cutting low end and some mid-range frequencies, then a light spring reverb.** No specific frequencies given — a plain cleanup-and-space pass, nothing elaborate. *(general starting point)* → [[EQ]], [[Reverb]]

**Sound 2 — pad (AAS Lounge Lizard, "broken" preset, "I use this all the time"):**
- **Same chord progression as Sound 1, but placed slightly differently rhythmically, deliberately manipulated to read as a pad rather than a keys sound.** First-stage processing before any manipulation: a reverb whose preset is named "clean chorus" (mix set to ~50%), then an EQ cut on the low end, then a chorus for width, then [[ValhallaDelay]] for texture. → [[Chorus-Ensemble]], [[ValhallaDelay]], [[BED Slot]]
- **Bounce to audio, THEN chop it up and re-place the pieces slightly differently — a second, separate manipulation pass after the first processing chain, not part of it.** This chop-after-bounce step is what turns a straight chord layer into a pad with its own internal rhythm. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Add a Kickstart-style device to make the bounced/chopped layer pulse at half time.** Rhythmic amplitude pulsing laid on top of the chop, distinct from the chop's own rhythm. → [[Kickstart 2]]
- **A second, different-type reverb after the Kickstart pulse, then a final EQ cutting both low AND high end** (narrowing the layer into a supporting textural role rather than full-range). → [[Reverb]], [[EQ]]
- **Net effect: two different keys layers each carrying their own kind of "pocket"** — Sound 1's pocket comes from the gaps between chord hits, Sound 2's comes from how the chopped pieces land at different times, creating separate pockets of silence. Different mechanisms for the same "space to breathe" goal. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Depth and Space]]

**Sound 3 — guitar counter-melody (Kontakt "Evolution Strawberry" bank, "wire" preset):**
- **A simple counter-melody, sourced from a Kontakt one-shot/sample bank rather than performed live guitar.** Played dry first to establish the source tone before any processing. → [[Kontakt 7]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Guitar amp chain: Bias FX guitar-amp plugin ("between us" preset) → EQ cutting low end and mid-range frequencies → chorus (BOTH of the plugin's chorus presets stacked, "to make it super wide") → delay set to ping-pong (light amount, mainly for width, not an audible echo effect) → room reverb.** A five-stage chain where two separate stages (the stacked chorus AND the ping-pong delay) are both reached for specifically to widen the part, not to add obvious effect character. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[EQ]], [[Modulation]], [[Delay]], [[Reverb]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**What got cut — the headline lesson:**
- **Built and then removed a percussion loop, specifically because keeping it would pre-commit the OTHER producer's drum choices.** loner knew this was going out as "an afro sample" (going to be built on by someone else) and deliberately avoided baking in a perc loop that would push the eventual drum programming down a particular route — a sound-selection restraint move made for a collaborator's benefit, not a quality problem with the loop itself. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Built and then muted/cut a horn layer, catching himself adding it "just to flex my muscles as a producer" rather than because the sample needed it.** Named directly as a common producer trap: getting caught up in what other producers' samples sound like and layering in extra elements "for the sake of it." → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **States explicitly that keeping the perc loop and horns in would likely have cost the placement** — the song (credited only as "a producer tempo") needed room for the other producer to add their own melodic elements and transitions; a maximalist sample would have left no space for that contribution. Direct tie to this vault's existing "add the world, then delete it" arrangement principle, but applied to a SAMPLE meant for someone else to build on, not a finished track. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

**Finishing move:**
- **Pitch the entire finished sample DOWN by two semitones on the master, using a "sound shifter"-type pitch-shift plugin, as the very last step.** Owned-lane equivalent: Ableton [[Shifter]] in Pitch mode. → [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**Closing philosophy (stated directly on camera):**
- **"The two absolutely essential things for making a good sample are good sound selection and good melodies."** The chords in this sample are "so, so simple" but work because they have a real melody an artist can sing over, and — most important per loner — they suit the target vibe/genre perfectly (he knew he was making an afro sample and picked chords to fit that specifically, not a generic progression). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Melody Writing]]
- **When a sample "feels empty," the fix is usually NOT more layers — it's that the vocal (and drums) are the actual missing piece,** meant to be added by whoever the sample goes to. A direct argument against over-filling a sample that's intentionally left open for a collaborator. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

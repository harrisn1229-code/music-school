# Bryson Tiller Vocal Chain

**Reference/vibe:** present-but-filtered, moody, chopped-down R&B — the vocal stays up front in the mix (not buried the way a straight lo-fi vocal would be), but a lo-fi/filter pass at the end gives it a "muffled through something" character that's the genre's signature. Three lanes depending on era/song: a light-touch **present + filtered** lane (Exchange), a light-touch **present, layered** lane (Next to You), and a heavier **chopped and screwed** lane (Outside).
**Signal source:** built from scratch in this breakdown rather than pulled from a real session — the general shape (pitch tool → space → time-based FX → a character/lo-fi finishing stage) is the reusable part, not exact settings.

## Lane 1 — Present + filtered (Exchange, *Trap Soul*)

1. **[[Little AlterBoy]]** — pitch shifted up a touch; kept subtle, this is not a full character/robot effect, just enough lift to sit the vocal in its slot.
2. **Reverb → EQ → Delay** — standard placement/space stage (source didn't specify exact plugin per stage; on this vault, [[ValhallaRoom]] for the reverb and [[Pro-Q 4]] for the EQ are the natural first calls).
3. **[[kHs Bitcrush]] — Quantize (sample-rate) reduction as the finishing stage.** Bounce the whole processed vocal and drop the **Quantize** knob down until the top end reads as muffled/"filtered out" rather than clean-hi-fi, while staying present in the mix — this is the signature Exchange-era Tiller vocal texture. Source used Edison's sample-rate control; kHs Bitcrush's Quantize knob is the owned equivalent.

*Why:* the vocal is the main melodic element of the sample (per the source, "the most important part"), so it can't be washed out the way a background layer would be — the filtering has to land after everything else, as a final color pass, not as an upfront processing choice.

## Lane 2 — Present, layered (Next to You, *Anniversary*)

1. **[[Little AlterBoy]] → Reverb → EQ → Delay** — identical light-touch chain to Lane 1's steps 1–2, but **no** bitcrush/filter finishing stage this time — this song's vocal stays cleaner.
2. **A second vocal layer, chopped from a separate recording** (not a duplicate/double of the lead take) — layered under/around the lead for texture and thickness, rather than a straight ADT double.

## Lane 3 — Chopped and screwed (Outside)

1. **[[Little AlterBoy]] → [[Phaser-Flanger]] → Cableguys HalfTime** *(unowned — [[Plugin Wishlist]])* — gives a slowed, warped "chopped and screwed" feel.
2. **A shaper-box stage last** *(likely Cableguys ShaperBox — unowned, [[Plugin Wishlist]])* — described as "completely" changing the vocal's sound; treat this as the stage doing the real character transformation, with the pitch/flange/half-time stack before it only setting up the raw material.

## Vocal arrangement

| Section | Lead | Behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Verse | single, present, filtered | nothing, or one low double |
| Hook | doubled, filter opened | harmony thirds + one octave-down layer |
| Bridge / switch | the chopped-and-screwed lane | pitched-down copy of the same line |

**The move — the filter is the section marker.** The "muffled through something" pass is dialed *back* for the hook and pushed harder for the verse, so the same voice reads as arriving without a level change. Cheaper and more on-genre than adding parts.

**Stack count: 1 → 3–4.** Modest. He stays upfront rather than becoming a wall — that's what separates this from a straight lo-fi vocal.

## Notes / variations

- All three lanes share the same **skeleton** — pitch tool → space (reverb/EQ/delay) → a time-or-lo-fi finishing stage — with the finishing stage swapped per song: sample-rate reduction (Lane 1), nothing extra (Lane 2), or half-time + shaper (Lane 3). Pick the finishing stage by how "degraded" the target vocal should read.

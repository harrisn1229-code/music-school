# Natural Mystic Guitar Lesson - Chord Inversion Exercise

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_BrxUdo5XPA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_BrxUdo5XPA)

## Synopsis

Minor-triad inversion practice built entirely around one short, real song segment — Bob Marley's "Natural Mystic" (the Dm–Am reggae verse vamp) — rather than an abstract drill. Teaches the three inversion shapes of a minor triad on the top three strings, voice-leads between them, then layers a fingerstyle reggae bass line underneath to produce a solo-guitar arrangement playing chords and bass simultaneously. A worked example of Jared's stated general practice philosophy: extract a small, real musical segment, isolate the technical concept it demonstrates, and loop that segment as the actual exercise rather than drilling the concept in the abstract or performing the whole song.

## What's taught

- **The three inversion shapes of a minor triad, all confined to the top three strings (G-B-E), and explicitly reusable as a moveable shape family for any minor chord** — root position, 1st inversion (3rd in the bass), and 2nd inversion (5th in the bass). The same three physical shapes are shown first on **A minor**, then transposed up the neck to produce the identical three inversions of **D minor** — demonstrating the shapes are chord-agnostic, just relocated by root.
- **Voice-leading between the inversions, worked concretely on the Dm→Am move used in the song:** moving from a Dm shape to the *nearest* Am inversion (rather than jumping to a distant Am shape) keeps one voice held in common, moves one voice a half step, and one voice a whole step — named directly as voice leading, the same "closest available shape" principle documented generally on [[Chord Inversions]] and [[Chord Voicings]].
- **The song's actual harmonic content, isolated and looped as the practice unit:** the "Natural Mystic" verse segment used is just two chords, Dm and Am, articulated twice each in a repeating reggae strum pattern — deliberately NOT the whole song, extracted specifically because it's short enough to loop and internalize while still being real, recognizable music (rather than a generic Dm-Am vamp built for the lesson).
- **A reggae strum-and-mute technique for the chords:** three-finger (index/middle/ring) short, staccato articulation per chord hit, with the awkward open-low-E-string Am voicing deliberately muted immediately after striking to keep the hit short rather than avoided or re-voiced.
- **A fingerstyle bass line built from the chord's own root, syncopated to enter on the "and" of beat 2** (not on the downbeat) — Am's bass line targets the open 5th string (A), Dm's the open 4th string (D), each articulated with a light palm-muting technique (resting the picking hand lightly between the string and bridge) to simulate a bass instrument's tone rather than a ringing open string.
- **Combining the two layers into one solo-guitar part**, practiced in the stated build order: (1) chord shapes alone, up and down through the positions; (2) the reggae chord-strum pattern alone; (3) the bass line alone, isolated from the chords; (4) each chord's bass-line-plus-chord-hit combination looped individually before (5) stringing multiple inversion positions together into the full moving pattern, syncing to the tab's written repeat structure.

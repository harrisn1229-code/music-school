# Why Your Riffs Sound Boring & How To Change That! - Metal Music Theory No. 1

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t4qLIiw6dRQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t4qLIiw6dRQ)

## Synopsis

Episode 1 of a "Metal Music Theory" series: BERNTH argues that metal players often skip key-based composition in favor of throwing riffs together by feel, and shows the payoff of writing deliberately *in a key* — a worked E minor riff built from the diatonic chords, then spiced up with two harmonic tricks (a borrowed dominant V and open-voicing color tones on the bVI/bVII power chords).

## What's taught

- **Diatonic chord construction by stacking 3rds up the scale**, demonstrated in E minor: count up 3 and 5 scale degrees from each root to get that degree's triad, extend to the 7th by counting up 7. Full E natural minor chord set derived this way: Em - F♯° - G - Am - Bm - C - D. (Standard method — matches the vault's existing [[Chords by Key]] table for minor keys.)
- **Substitute the borrowed major/dominant V for the diatonic minor v, for a stronger resolution back to i.** In E minor, Bm(7) → i (Em) is described as pleasant but lacking real tension-and-release; changing the same chord to **B dominant (V7)** before resolving to Em gives a noticeably more satisfying cadence. Direct practical confirmation of [[Chords by Key]]'s own documented "borrowed major V (from harmonic minor)" rule — same mechanism, demonstrated live in a riff rather than stated as a table footnote.
- **A borrowed/non-diatonic bass note on the ii° voicing** — for the E minor riff's ii° (normally F♯ diminished), BERNTH instead voices a diminished-sounding chord with **D♯ as the lowest note**, a pitch outside the E natural minor scale — described as "borrowing that note" for a preferred diminished color rather than sticking to the strictly diatonic F♯° shape. (Stated at transcript-confidence only — the exact resulting chord/inversion isn't spelled out beyond "D♯ on the bottom, E minor diminished sound.")
- **Color-tone open-string voicings on plain power chords (bVI and bVII).** Rather than leaving the C and D power chords in the riff as bare root-5th shapes, add the open B and high E strings on top: over **C** (bVI), B and E add the **major 7th and major 9th** respectively, for a "floating," open-sounding Cmaj7(9)-type color; over **D** (bVII), the same open B and E strings become the **major 6th and major 9th** relative to D. Both extensions use notes already diatonic to the key (no further borrowing) and are chosen specifically because they're easy open-string grabs on top of a barred power-chord shape.

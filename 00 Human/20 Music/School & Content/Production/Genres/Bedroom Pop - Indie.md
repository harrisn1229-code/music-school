# Bedroom Pop / Indie

**What makes it the genre:** intimacy and *charm over polish* — one person's taste audible in every decision, imperfections kept as identity, small sounds presented close instead of big sounds presented far away. This is the home-studio-native genre: the "limitation aesthetic" (Steve Lacy cut Dark Red vocals through an SM58 into an iPhone) is a *choice* you now make deliberately with better gear. **This is your default lane — the other playbooks are trips; this is home.**

## The pillars

### 1. The sound is close
- Everything recorded near and dry-ish: vocal 3–6" off the mic, guitar amps small and roomy, no big-studio spaces. The listener is *in the room*, not in an audience.
- Your rig maps perfectly: [[Shure SM57]] for the gritty/dynamic vocal lane, TLM for the airy lane, [[Reverb Send Room]] small, [[Delay Send Slap]] doing most of the "space."

### 2. Charm beats correctness (the taste rules)
- Keep: slightly loose timing, breaths, amp hiss moments, the crack in take one. Fix: only what distracts on the third listen.
- Pitch: barely tuned (Humanize 100, retune 50+) or untuned. The Lacy/Clairo vocal reads *spoken-close*, not performed.
- One "wrong" sound per song on purpose — a detuned synth, a cheap drum machine, a clipped transition. It's the signature move of the genre.

### 3. Guitars — chorused, direct-ish, mid-forward
- The genre tone: clean-ish guitar + **chorus/vibrato** ([[Chorus-Ensemble]], [[kHs Chorus]]) — the DeMarco/Mk.gee shimmer-wobble. Try vibrato (pitch) over chorus (blend) for the seasick DeMarco lane.
- DI + tiny amp blends ([[1 Electric Guitar DI Track|Electric Guitar DI Track]] clean-funk variation) — Lacy's tone is basically direct with comp.
- Melody guitars answer the vocal (call-response), rarely play *with* it.

### 4. Drums — small, dusty, upfront
- Drum machine + pack sounds presented dry and close: little verb, tight decays, mono-ish ([[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]] with [[RC-20 Retro Color]] texture).
- Halftime pockets, simple patterns — the groove charm is in what's *left out*.
- Live-feel top loops (tambourine/shaker played into the mic, even sloppy) humanize programmed kits instantly.

### 5. Bass — melodic, round, DeMarco-forward
- The Hofner thumb tone ([[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Electric Bass DI Track]]) is *the* bedroom pop bass: dark, round, melodic fills between vocal lines. Often the second hook.

### 6. Synths — cheap-charming
- [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]]/[[Drift]] with wobble (detune, slow chorus) · [[Operator]] toy-FM tones · tape-warble everything lightly ([[RC-20 Retro Color]] wow/flutter).
- Pads stay behind the band ([[1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]]), leads are simple single-finger melodies.

### 7. Vocals — the diary voice
- Soft delivery, close mic, doubled loosely (drift kept — skip the alignment rules), harmonies quiet and woolly.
- Light chain: [[1 Main Vocal Track|Main Vocal Track]] minus soothe2/Fresh Air, Saturn light, [[LALA]] over Pro-C 2 · [[Dijon Vocal Chain]] for the raw end, [[Mac DeMarco Vocal Chain]] for the slap-wobble end.
- Talking/laughing/room-noise intros and outros — the genre's punctuation.

### 8. Mix presentation
- Mid-forward and a little dark (roll the extreme air off — sheen reads as "label money") · narrow-ish stereo with one wide element · master gentle: −10 to −12 LUFS, [[Kramer Tape]] doing the glue, skip the clipper.
- The mix should sound like the demo, matured — if it starts sounding "professional," pull something back.

## Arrangement

**Section map** — *"Dark Red"*, *"Pretty Girl"*, *"Chamber of Reflection"*

| Section | Bars | Slots filled | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 4–8 | MIDS (the loop) · TEXTURE — often room noise or a count-in | 2 |
| Verse | 16 | BASS · MIDS · LEAD · TRANSIENT · TEXTURE | 5 |
| Chorus | 8–16 | + BED · AIR | 7 |
| Bridge / break | 8 | one element removed, not all of them | 4 |
| Outro | short | stops abruptly, or the room noise outlasts the music | 1 |

**Entrances & exits**
- **The loop is in from bar 1 and plays under everything.** Sections are defined by what joins it, never by it stopping.
- **Nothing ever drops to zero.** There is no drop in this genre. A bedroom-pop breakdown removes *one* part.
- The song starts mid-idea — talking, a laugh, amp hum, a count-in — and often ends before you expect. 2:30 is a finished song.
- TEXTURE is on for the entire runtime. It's the floor, not an effect.

**The contrast move — add one thing.** A harmony vocal, a tambourine, a second guitar. Additive and monotonic: parts arrive and stay. Compare modern pop, which builds the chorus first and then *subtracts* to make a verse — bedroom pop runs the opposite direction, and that's why it feels like it's unfolding rather than being engineered.

**Density rule: 4–7 slots, all of them small.** The density is mid-range but every occupant is quiet and close. Loudness never carries a section; arrival does.

## Session recipe
Write and record simultaneously — commit sounds in the first hour ([[Psych-Pop (Tame Impala)]] shares this rule) · start with the chord loop on guitar or keys, mumble-track the melody immediately · finish songs fast and small (2:30 is a complete bedroom pop song) · your voice memos are demos: the [[Gorillaz Vocal Chain]]-style lo-fi treatments can make an actual phone take usable as a texture layer.

# 5 EASY METAL GUITAR SOLO TRICKS (that sound advanced!)

## Synopsis

Five phrasing/vocabulary tricks BERNTH says are on his own album ("Elevation"), all built from the plain A minor pentatonic box as the starting framework — the point being that advanced-sounding playing comes from what you do to a simple scale, not from learning a more complex one.

## What's taught

- **Chromatic ornamentation ("outside" playing) — visualize the half-step above/below every pentatonic note, and use them as passing tones that always resolve back into the scale.** Explicitly framed as playing "wrong" notes on purpose (they're outside A minor pentatonic) but getting away with it because the ear tracks the resolution back to the correct scale tone, not the passing note itself — the fast, easy route into a jazz-fusion-flavored sound without learning new scale shapes.
- **Slide-into-bend across three pentatonic positions, to fake one giant bend.** Slide from a low note up several frets/positions, then bend the arrived note up a further whole step — timed so the bend starts slightly *before* the slide physically ends, creating the illusion of one continuous pitch rise larger than any single bend could produce. Spans three pentatonic box positions in the worked example (sliding from the lowest note of position 1 up to bending into the highest note of position 3).
- **Pentatonic-Dorian hybrid — stay a 5-note scale, but swap the color.** Instead of choosing between A minor pentatonic (1 ♭3 4 5 ♭7) and full A Dorian, drop the 4th and ♭7th and add the major 2nd and major 6th instead: 1 2 ♭3 5 6. Still five notes, still fingerable like a pentatonic shape, but carries Dorian's characteristic major 6th color. Stated as portable to any scale/mode swap, not just Dorian (harmonic minor, Lydian, etc. named as other candidates) — Dorian recommended as the easiest starting point since it's only two added notes from minor pentatonic.
- **Arpeggio shapes hiding inside an extended (3-note-per-string) pentatonic scale.** Rather than running the extended pentatonic scale as a straight up/down line, pick out smaller shapes within it (partial-barre fragments) that outline arpeggio-like intervals — reframes "scale vs. arpeggio" as a false binary; arpeggios are already latent inside a scale shape once you look for non-linear paths through it. (Single-video tip, no dedicated home yet — kept here as the master record.)
- **Hybrid picking with deliberately large interval skips, to defeat the "boring pentatonic" complaint.** Add the picking-hand middle finger alongside the pick so a string can be skipped cleanly, then play the pentatonic scale with bigger interval jumps (skipping a string between notes) instead of the default stepwise up/down motion. Framed as the single biggest lever for making a "boring" 5-note scale sound advanced: listeners' ears predict and get bored by pure ascending/descending motion within the first few notes, regardless of which scale is being used — breaking the directional predictability (not switching scales) is what actually fixes it.

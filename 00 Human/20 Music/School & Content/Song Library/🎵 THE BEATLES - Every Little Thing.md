# Every Little Thing — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Generic early-Beatles pop on the surface, but Pollack's headline is an **irregular harmonic rhythm deliberately used to underscore syncopation in the tune** — a level of sophistication that separates this from "She Loves You".

**Form.** Neither the standard pop models nor strict folk alternation: three refrains alternating with double verses, with a single-verse guitar section breaking the pattern so it can't become mechanical. The 2-bar intro **starts on beat 2 with nothing to tell you where beat 1 was**, so the meter stays unmoored until the first verse lands. The verse is **6 bars** in three 2-bar phrases; the refrain is 8, one 4-bar phrase twice.

**Harmony.** A frugal budget — I, IV, V, iv — with the only real colour being heavy **♭VII** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). The syncopation is structural ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]): the verse's first two phrases both put their chord changes **off the phrase's own downbeats**, and the refrain does a slower version of the same thing by sustaining one chord across two bars. The verse's third phrase then drops into plain textbook motion, with the bass line rather than the harmony deciding an inversion — rare in the early catalogue, and it's what makes the phrase read as resolution.

**Melody.** Mixolydian rather than bluesy: ♭7 alongside the natural major third. The refrain runs **counterbalancing appoggiaturas** ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]) — one downbeat dissonance resolves down, the next resolves up, so the section stops feeling like one gesture repeated.

**Arrangement.** Noisily strummed acoustic in the middle, bass heavy and **doubled in the low octaves of a piano**, punctuated by **timpani played by Ringo** — an early instance of them bringing an exotic instrument in and playing it themselves. The guitar solo tracks the verse's shape exactly: close paraphrase of the tune for the first half, then an improvised extension of the vocal's single slow triplet.

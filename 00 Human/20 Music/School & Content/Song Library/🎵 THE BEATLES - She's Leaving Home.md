# She's Leaving Home — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** barred as a slow-spun **12/8**, where one long measure absorbs four bars of what would otherwise read as fast 3/4. Intro (a single measure of arpeggiation over I) · Verse (4 measures, phrased ABB) · Verse · Refrain (4 measures plus a **truncated quarter-measure**, so it ends short) · Verse · Verse · Refrain · Verse · Refrain · Outro, which restores the missing quarter measure.

Paul in **mimicry mode rather than blend mode** — a stylised send-up of turn-of-the-century drawing-room semi-classical, pointed at the parents. Pollack's defence is technical rather than sentimental: the modal ♭VII and the minor v are deliberate tells that mark it as parody ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]), and the harmony under the strings-and-harp gloss is stranger than the surface suggests.

Sevenths and ninths run nearly wall to wall, which is where the overlush quality comes from. **The harmonic rhythm decelerates across every verse** ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]), dense at the top and parked by the end — and the parked dominant is embellished with an appoggiatura and a ninth *simultaneously*, the most extreme single-chord elaboration Pollack can name in the catalogue to that point. The refrain's closing V-of-V resolves **straight into the next verse's I with no V intervening** ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]), and the outro's plagal IV–I ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]) has a churchy sentimentality that is plainly the joke.

Strings and harp were scored by Mike Leander, not George Martin, and are more restrained for it. The stereo master sits a half step lower and slower than the mono.

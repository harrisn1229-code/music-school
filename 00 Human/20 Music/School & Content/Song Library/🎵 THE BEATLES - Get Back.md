## Production & Arrangement
Let It Be / the rooftop concert — the Beatles going **back to a live-band R&B groove**. **Billy Preston's electric piano** is the secret weapon (bright Rhodes-y stabs), the rhythm section is tight and dry, and it was tracked essentially **live**. Loose, funky, un-produced by design.

## Steal this
- **Live-band tracking** for feel ([[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track]] etc.) — the groove is four people locking in.
- **Guest keys as the hook:** a bright electric-piano part ([[Keyscape]] Rhodes/Wurli) riffing over a simple progression.
- **Dry, un-produced mix** — commit to the room and the take.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Get Back]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #161.*

**Form.** Stylized blues built out of **one eight-bar phrase wearing four slightly different costumes**. Every section is eight bars except the four-bar intro, and they only sort into three classes when you look at harmonic shape and accent placement — the composition is in the differentiation, not the material. Note that this is **eight-bar blues, not twelve**.

**Harmony.** Three chords: I, IV and **♭VII standing in for V** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). `Intro (4): I (3) – ♭VII–IV` · `Verse (8): [I (2) – IV – I] ×2` — closed, and deliberately unaccented · `Refrain (8): [I (2) – IV – I–♭VII–IV] ×2` — open, cadencing so the next I lands · `Refrain′ (8): I (2) – IV – I–♭VII–IV · I (3) – IV` — the most open of the lot, three bars of I setting up a plagal cadence ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]) then halting on IV for effect · `Break (8): identical to Refrain`. The break and the first refrain variant are the same chords; **what separates them is where the accents fall**. The ♭VII–IV pair in bar 4 is almost always played **over an A pedal** ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]), the only exception being the first phrase of the first guitar break; and the sharp ensemble accents on those chords are withheld on the first pass of a repeated phrase and hammered on the second, consistently enough that he rules out sloppiness.

**Notable.** The tune outlines A7 with the flat seventh on top, its centre of gravity on **scale degree 5** — also its floor, a tenth below the ceiling — and **the third degree never appears**, which is why he calls the key Mixolydian rather than blues-inflected major. Played live with no overdubs; the refrain's syncopated arpeggio is led each time by **whichever of guitar or piano is about to take the next solo**. Both official releases are studio takes, not the rooftop.

**Against this page.** Same tonic (A). He specifies **A Mixolydian** where the page says A major, and reads the form as an eight-bar blues cycle rather than the I-V-C-V-C-S-V-C-O in `structure:`.

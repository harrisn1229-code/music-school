# Live Performance Setup

Structuring an Ableton Live Session for solo electronic performance — playing keys/pads live over a backing track rather than just pressing play on full songs. The core pattern is a "song group" of a few tracks per song, wired so switching songs and switching sounds within a song both happen automatically, leaving the performer free to actually play.

## From videos

- **Run your OWN back-catalog stems through a DJ controller's stem-separation faders instead of playing a static backing track — a genuine substitute for a full backing band.** Joy Anonymous use a Native Instruments Traktor Kontrol S8 (chosen specifically for its per-stem faders, normally meant for isolating stems in other DJs' tracks) to mix, loop, and re-key their own songs' stems live — described as "a real breakthrough" that let them improvise and communicate on stage the way a full band would, instead of singing over a fixed track. Paired live with a hardware drum machine (their TR-1000) and a bandmate live-sampling vinyl into an MPC to build ambient beds mid-set — the whole rig is deliberately the same gear they record with, blurring studio and stage. → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]] — Joy Anonymous (Tape Notes), [[TN 186 Joy Anonymous - Full Video Episode]]
- **A fixed 4-stage hands-on FX pedal chain for real-time, DJ-style drum-machine processing instead of post-hoc automation**: bit-crusher+filter → distortion/compression → tap-tempo delay → reverb, in that order, deliberately chosen to stay jam-able live rather than requiring a settings recall. Two performance details worth carrying to any similar rig: (1) blend dry signal back in under a heavily filtered/crushed effect to keep the attack transient audible while the tail is processed; (2) stopping the source and letting only the reverb tail ring out is used as an intentional ending/transition gesture, not an accident. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Delay]], [[Reverb]] — Anthony Marinelli Music, [[How I Process my TR-808 Drum Machine with OTO EFX Pedals]]
- **Treat a live improvised hardware performance as raw material for a later editing pass, not a finished take — two separate creative "modes" that shouldn't run at the same time.** Rodec/Sherman's Tom De Ridder's actual workflow: run instruments through a hybrid analog console/effects rig and just play for ~30 minutes, recording everything into a DAW as separate multitrack stems (including the wet/effected signal on its own track, separate from dry) — then, LATER, switch into an editing mindset and go find the best ~5 minutes, cutting/rearranging non-linearly, correcting errors, or reprocessing a stem with additional plugins, since all the separated tracks are still fully available. Framed explicitly like a writer's drafting-vs-editing distinction, or a composer's creative-vs-refining mode: disturbing the creative flow to self-edit in the moment is the thing to avoid — not every raw take needs to be a keeper, and the DAW pass afterward is where distillation happens, not during performance.
- **A DJ-style crossfader repurposed to crossfade between STEMS of one arrangement instead of two full tracks** — assign each channel to the X side, the Y side, or "always through" (plays regardless of fader position), so a physical crossfader becomes a compositional performance tool for one song's own elements (e.g. kick on X, snare on Y, hats always-through) rather than DJ-mixing between two separate songs.
- **Post-fader vs. pre-fader recording as a deliberate choice, specifically to capture human fader/crossfader FEEL as real audio rather than automation.** Post-fader recording bakes every live fader and crossfader move directly into the recorded audio — explicitly because manually drawing that many automation curves in a DAW to match "never gets the right timing and feel" a hands-on performance produces. Pre-fader recording is available as the alternative when the full unprocessed signal (without the performance baked in) is what's actually needed instead.
- **Standing up at physical hardware, rather than sitting at a screen with a mouse, is treated as a real contributor to performance feel, not just ergonomics** — likened directly to why an electric guitar is played standing rather than seated: body position measurably changes the "vibe" and immediacy available to a performer, independent of what the instrument itself is capable of. — Anthony Marinelli Music (feat. Tom De Ridder), [[Sherman Filter in a High-End Mixer Should Be ILLEGAL]]
- **A fully analog "preset" system on hardwired modular hardware — no digital memory, but instant, repeatable sound changes live — built for touring, not experimentation.** P-Thugg (Chromeo)'s touring modular rig (six matched analog voice units, each hardwired rather than manually patched) uses a 3-position switch per voice that literally rewires which envelope generators feed the filter vs. the amp, plus which octave/semitone transpose is live, at each switch position — the same principle as a 17th-century pipe-organ stop selector changing which pipes sound. Three presets × six modules = 18 sound changes available instantly mid-set with zero patching. **The design workflow runs backward from the finished record**: decide what sound is needed for which part of which song FIRST, then hardwire the preset positions to match that pre-planned sequence — you can't improvise the wiring live, so the sequencing has to be locked in before the rig is even built. **Manual override stays available via normalled jacks**: because the hardwired routing uses normalling (not permanent solder joints) at the switch points, inserting a patch cable at any of those points overrides the hardwired preset on the fly, same as normalling on any modular gear — so the fixed system doesn't fully sacrifice patchability, just makes it optional rather than mandatory. **Why hardwired instead of fully patchable, explicitly**: a rig this size, fully patch-cabled, would be too heavy to transport and would take far longer than the available 2-3-hour daily setup (sometimes only a 20-minute changeover between sets) to re-patch reliably every show — patchability was deliberately traded for touring reliability. → [[Vintage Gear Worklist]] (Synthesizers.com modules, chosen specifically for through-hole/header-based serviceability and touring reliability — a full spare of every module travels in a dedicated drawer) — Anthony Marinelli Music (feat. P-Thugg, Rob Rosen), [[MASSIVE Live Modular Synthesizer w P-Thugg from Chromeo]]
- **A "protected base + reserved experimentation" discipline for live improvisation on synths, to guarantee you can never accidentally go fully unlistenable mid-show.** Deliberately leave certain knobs/envelope settings on a live patch completely untouched (the "base" sound) while reserving ONE specific control (e.g. a single envelope generator or filter knob) as the only thing you allow yourself to twist for live improvisation — no matter how far that one reserved knob gets pushed, you can always return to a known-good sound just by backing that one control off, since everything else was never touched in the first place. Explicitly framed as a safety net against synthesis's own biggest live risk: "it can't be understated how easy it is to make something unlistenable" with one wrong knob move at the wrong moment. — Anthony Marinelli Music (feat. P-Thugg), [[MASSIVE Live Modular Synthesizer w P-Thugg from Chromeo]]
- **A DIY always-on visual tuner for live analog oscillators — reusable on any synth's output, not just modular.** Take a cheap (~$20) clip-on guitar tuner, remove its clip/mounting hardware, and wire/hot-glue a 1/4" jack onto its input so it can patch (or plug via a headphone/aux out) onto any oscillator's audio output — sine, triangle, saw, whichever — giving a constant visual tuning readout while playing, without interrupting the audio signal. Genuinely necessary on a multi-oscillator live rig, since analog oscillators drift with the room's temperature/humidity over a show and tracking can shift too far to catch by ear alone before it's audible. Demonstrated in two forms: a dedicated tuner per oscillator on a modular rig (so you always visually know which note you're sitting on before you even touch a key), and the same trick reduced to one unit patched into a synth's headphone output (an MS-20 in this case) when only one spare output is available live. → [[Voice Architecture]] — Anthony Marinelli Music (feat. P-Thugg), [[MASSIVE Live Modular Synthesizer w P-Thugg from Chromeo]]
- **Live-looping rig: five tracks (Drums, Rhodes, Bass, Guitar, Synth), picked for being versatile, warm, and none too overbearing** — the loop-building "sandwich" only works if every ingredient can be tasted; pick instruments that get reused across your own productions rather than one-off sounds, and keep each one from drowning the others out when they're all looping together. Usual build order is left to right: drums → Rhodes → bass → guitar → synth, arming each in turn via a controller button mapped to that track's Arm, recording a short phrase, and triggering playback before moving to the next. — You Suck at Producing, [Live Looping](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0f2KrrzsuUo)
- **Map performance faders to track Sends (not Volume), and set the tracks to Sends Only, to dodge a clipping ceiling** — a hardware fader's max position can correspond to well above unity (~+6 dB) on a track's actual Volume fader, clipping everything at full fader travel; mapping the faders to each track's **Send** level instead, with the track's own output set to **Sends Only**, keeps the same hands-on level control without the clip risk. Two extra faders cover a synth's low-pass filter cutoff (for live "wub" sweeps) and **global BPM**, so tempo can be ridden on the fly mid-set. — You Suck at Producing, [Live Looping](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0f2KrrzsuUo)
- **Click-track clips replace the built-in metronome for a live-looping session** — a handful of plain click-sound clips (three in 4/4, one in 6/4 for switching feel) sit on the drum track and get triggered like any other clip, with Live's built-in metronome switched off entirely — the click competes less with the sound of a loop actually starting than the default beep does. — You Suck at Producing, [Live Looping](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0f2KrrzsuUo)
- **Overdub a loop via a pad mapped to Live's Overdub button, instead of recording one full pass** — press play on the (click-track) backing clip, switch to a macro pad bank, and hit the pad mapped to **Overdub** to record new hits on top of what's already playing without stopping playback — builds a drum loop hit by hit rather than needing a clean single take. → [[Session View Workflow]] — You Suck at Producing, [Live Looping](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0f2KrrzsuUo)
- **Pad-bank navigation stands in for a full Launchpad/APC grid on a compact controller** — a highlighted "red box" over the first four clip slots mirrors what a dedicated grid controller would show; macro-mapped "pad bank" buttons move that box around the session grid, so a small 16-pad controller can still reach every track's clips by switching which 4×4 region is active, instead of needing hardware with a full-size grid. — You Suck at Producing, [Live Looping](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0f2KrrzsuUo)
- **Macro-mapped FX knobs ridden live during a loop's build/breakdown: reverb, delay, low-pass/high-pass filter, Beat Repeat Grid + Pitch Decay** — separate knobs bring in reverb and delay sends and sweep a low-pass then high-pass filter; a dedicated pad bank plus knob drives Beat Repeat's **Grid** (repeat rate) and **Pitch Decay** (falling-pitch stutter) for a build moment, finishing with a manual fade-out. → [[Beat Repeat]] — You Suck at Producing, [Live Looping](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0f2KrrzsuUo)
- **Arm Exclusive off, to play multiple instruments/tracks at once from one session** — by default, Ableton only lets one track be armed at a time; right-click a track's Arm button and deselect **Arm Exclusive** to arm several tracks simultaneously, so a single take can trigger multiple instruments together rather than one at a time. → [[Randomized Rack Design]] — You Suck at Producing, [Using Multiple MIDI Instruments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zcPkcRjPufI)
- **A proper keyboard split via Instrument Rack Key Zones — not two separate Pitch MIDI-effect range limiters** — a naive way to split two instruments across a keyboard is to drop the stock **Pitch** MIDI effect on each track and manually restrict each one's playable range; the catch is that unless the two ranges are set as perfect complements, this leaves a dead zone in the middle where BOTH devices block the same overlapping notes and neither instrument sounds. The clean fix: drag one instrument into the same Instrument Rack as the other (via the chain list) so both live on one track as two chains, then open the rack's **Key** zone editor (left-side tab) and set each chain's key range directly — one chain owns the lower keys, the other the upper, with no configuration mismatch possible since it's one editor for both ranges at once. → [[Randomized Rack Design]] (same chain-list mechanism used for chain-selector racks) — You Suck at Producing, [Using Multiple MIDI Instruments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zcPkcRjPufI)
- **Route separate physical MIDI controllers to separate tracks, when you have more than one controller** — with multiple hardware controllers connected, set each track's MIDI input to a specific controller (rather than "All Ins") so each physical keyboard automatically plays its own instrument without needing a key-split or arm-exclusive workaround at all. — You Suck at Producing, [Using Multiple MIDI Instruments](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zcPkcRjPufI)
- **Two-deck DJ layer for song-to-song transitions.** Two tracks ("Deck A"/"Deck B") each hold full-song clips, mimicking a two-turntable DJ setup — trigger clips from a hardware controller, ride the channel fader, and sweep a mapped filter (high-pass/low-pass, modeled on a DJ mixer's filter knob) between songs instead of a hard cut. — You Suck at Producing, [How To PERFORM with Ableton Live | Live Performance Setup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J-GeRU5SLNo)
- **"Song group" track stack: backing track + live instrument stack + sidechain-trigger track.** Each performed song gets three tracks: a **backing track** with everything not played live, an **instrument stack** driven from a USB MIDI keyboard for the live-played parts, and a **sidechain-trigger track** — a Simpler with just a click/impulse mimicking the backing track's kick pattern, so a ducking chain on the live instruments still tracks the (audio) backing track's kick even though the live instruments aren't directly reacting to it. → [[Sidechaining]] — You Suck at Producing, [How To PERFORM with Ableton Live | Live Performance Setup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J-GeRU5SLNo)
- **Keep MIDI always-listening, but the instrument rack itself off by default, via a dummy automation clip.** Set each song's instrument stack MIDI *From* to the USB keyboard with Monitor = **In** (always receiving MIDI, no arming needed), but leave the instrument rack device itself **off** by default so idle songs' synths aren't all running at once. A dummy clip on that track automates the instrument-rack device on at the top of the song and off at the end — switching songs is just triggering the right group's dummy clip. — You Suck at Producing, [How To PERFORM with Ableton Live | Live Performance Setup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J-GeRU5SLNo)
- **Automate the chain selector to swap instrument sounds mid-song, hands-free.** Inside a song's instrument rack, automate the **chain selector** across the timeline so it switches which chain (pluck → alternate → Reese, etc.) is active at specific bars without the performer touching anything — just play the keyboard and trust the automation to route to the right patch at the right moment. — You Suck at Producing, [How To PERFORM with Ableton Live | Live Performance Setup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J-GeRU5SLNo)
- **Choke groups on a performance Drum Rack.** A Drum Rack loaded across 16 pads for a live beat-pad routine, with choke groups set up so triggering one pad cuts off a previous still-ringing sample — standard hi-hat-style choking, applied to a performance routine rather than a drum kit. — You Suck at Producing, [How To PERFORM with Ableton Live | Live Performance Setup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J-GeRU5SLNo)
- **Real-time MIDI quantize via Max for Live, active only during performance — play slightly ahead of the beat.** A MIDI-quantize M4L device (active only while the project plays) shifts live-played notes onto the grid in real time. Because it can only shift a note *backward* onto the nearest grid point, the performer has to play slightly **ahead** of the beat so there's room to correct — playing late instead of early means the note misses the quantize window and the beat is simply missed, not fixed. → [[MIDI Generate and Transform Tools]] — You Suck at Producing, [How To PERFORM with Ableton Live | Live Performance Setup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J-GeRU5SLNo)
- **Separate cue/headphone output so the click and pre-listen never reach the crowd.** Route the metronome to a different audio output (not the main mix) feeding only the performer's headphones. Route a dedicated return track to that same headphone output, carrying a touch of every track's signal, so the performer hears a personal cue mix (a bit of everything plus the click) completely separate from what the audience hears. — You Suck at Producing, [How To PERFORM with Ableton Live | Live Performance Setup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J-GeRU5SLNo)
- **Register-split live keys as an alternative to quantize for a section.** Map the keyboard's low/mid/high registers to three different sounds simultaneously (e.g. a lead up top, a bass in the middle, a Reese below), changing together as the chain selector advances through the song — and for a section where quantize isn't used, trigger pre-arranged one-shot sound effects from pads instead, sidestepping the live-timing problem entirely for that passage. — You Suck at Producing, [How To PERFORM with Ableton Live | Live Performance Setup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J-GeRU5SLNo)

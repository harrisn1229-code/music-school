## Synopsis

Series closer: a structured beginner drill for the diatonic-chord-progression concept from Part 4. Write 5 separate four-chord progressions in C major, each chord filling exactly one bar, chords drawn only from the six diatonic triads (C Dm Em F G Am — no diminished), with doubling/repeats allowed. Walks through building one example progression (C–F–G–Em) in a piano roll from scratch, applying the Ma-mi-mi-Ma-Ma-mi pattern to know each chord's quality before building it. A DAW-agnostic drill format — useful as a five-minute warm-up exercise even though Nate is well past needing the underlying theory.

## What's taught

- **The drill**: 5 progressions, 4 chords each, 1 bar per chord, diatonic-only (C major's 6 usable triads), chords may repeat/double within a progression (e.g. C-C-F-F or C-F-G-C are both valid four-chord progressions).
- **Worked example progression**: C major – F major – G major – E minor, built one bar at a time by looking up each chord's quality via the Ma-mi-mi-Ma-Ma-mi pattern (C=I=major, F=IV=major, G=V=major, E=iii=minor) and constructing it with the half-step formula from Parts 2–3.
- **Workflow tip (DAW-agnostic)**: draw chords in the piano roll bar-by-bar, OR play them in on a MIDI keyboard and quantize + drag-to-length afterward — both land on the same one-chord-per-bar result.

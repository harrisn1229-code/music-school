# Disclosure: Make EVERY Sound Come Alive (18 Tricks) [Doja Cat Remix]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KS89XfqFE9A)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KS89XfqFE9A)

## Synopsis

A remix-stems breakdown built entirely around one theme: how to keep every element — a vocal stem you didn't record, a plain sine-wave pad, a hi-hat pattern — from sounding static and lifeless. Covers creative (mis)use of a transient designer and a wobble effect, granular warp abuse, oscillator-shape modulation, and an extreme, whole-song commitment to never repeating the same handclap sample twice.

## Techniques

- **Use a transient designer to duck an unwanted delay/reverb tail baked into a vocal stem, not for dynamics shaping.** When a received remix stem carries its own recorded delay/spill that can't be removed, pull the transient designer's sustain down specifically in the gaps between phrases to quiet that baked-in tail — an imperfect but usable fix distinct from the tool's normal punch/dynamics role. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]]
- **RC-20's Wobble effect at a LOW mix (~15-20%), not full wet, turns an obvious wobble into a subtle phaser-like effect instead.** "If you put wobble on RC-20 and back the mix down, you're basically making a phaser" — full wet reads as an obvious wobble modulation, a low mix percentage reads as phasing character instead — a deliberate repurposing of one effect into a different-sounding one via the mix knob alone. → [[RC-20 Retro Color]]
- **Abuse Ableton's Warp mode (Warp off, Complex/Texture mode, grain size pushed way up, playback slowed) for jumpy, granular artifacts on a vocal** — not for tempo-correction, but as a deliberate sound-design tool exploiting warp algorithm artifacts. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Manually pull down individual over-intense vocal-attack moments by ear (a few dB at a time) rather than reaching for a compressor.** A surgical, non-automatic fix for specific hits whose transient felt too aggressive — small moves (a handful of dB) that add up to a noticeably calmer vocal without touching overall dynamics processing.
- **Classic Disclosure synth patch: a sine-wave oscillator with a pitch bend and lower-octave layers, using the synth's own oscillator-SHAPE fade (sine ↔ sawtooth) as a movement source** — modulation coming from morphing the waveform itself, not just filter cutoff or amp envelope. → [[Serum 2]]
- **"How to make a synth sound alive": layer white noise processed through a bitcrusher and filter for grit and randomness, so the tone has "something a bit wrong with it" rather than sounding like a plain, static sine wave.** The same "controlled imperfection = life" philosophy documented elsewhere from Disclosure's other sources. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Add a rhythmic pulse (16th notes via an effects/gate device) onto an otherwise-sustained pad specifically to imply tempo, even though the underlying tone itself never changes.** A cheap way to give a static pad a sense of rhythm without actually re-triggering or reprogramming the note.
- **Trigger a synth rhythmically through a gated noise gate WHILE the underlying patch keeps running independent, ongoing random modulation (from the noise/filter layer above) — so every rhythmic trigger sounds slightly different rather than identical.** The combination (rhythmic gate + continuous randomization underneath) is what makes each hit distinct; the gate alone would just repeat the same static hit. → [[Modulation]]
- **Compression + saturation + limiting together lower peak level while making a mix sound louder and fuller.** With processing engaged the mix peaks lower (around -9 dB) than bypassed (around -6 dB) yet reads as noticeably louder and richer — the same theme already documented from [[Disclosure - Loudness the Smart Way (Don't Smash Your Drums)|Disclosure's dedicated loudness video]]. → [[Clipping and Loudness]]
- **Reversed, heavily-pitched-up rimshot for a subtle "growing" texture accent.** A rimshot with a whistle-like character gets reversed (turning its sharp attack-decay into a rising swell) and tuned up 25 semitones — used as a small, almost-subliminal "happy moment" inside an otherwise straightforward kit. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Use a genuinely different handclap sample for every single clap hit across the ENTIRE song — an explicit, whole-track commitment to zero repetition, credited to J Dilla's influence.** Not a once-or-twice variation trick, but a sustained discipline applied consistently from start to finish purely for human feel. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **Deliberately swung, "super off the grid" hi-hats chosen for rhythmic ambiguity** — swung far enough that the pattern could almost be misheard as a 6/8 feel layered against a straight beat, rather than a clean, easily-quantifiable groove.
- **Drum bus: light compression, saturation drive dialed to ~50% (with real headroom left before it gets obviously gritty at 100%), plus an Inflator specifically for boosting perceived stereo "edge" and fatness.** → [[Compression]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]

# Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)

## Synopsis
An 8-minute rapid-fire Q&A (Sol State channel, one of this channel's re-edits of Ian Kirkpatrick's own livestream material) that ranges across chord tools, his stance on mixing as a songs-producer, what makes a good artist or writer to work with, the business reality of spec work and constant rejection, a philosophy of originality built on an Instagram-flood analogy, manager selection advice, the Dua Lipa songwriting-camp origin of "New Rules," a producer's role when the artist is attached to an idea, and a look at his Cubase MediaBay sample-library workflow — a career/mindset grab-bag rather than a single technical breakdown. There's light overlap with existing Ian Kirkpatrick notes in this vault worth flagging: his line about not sending in demos built purely from stacked Splice loops touches the same "loops" territory as [[Ian Kirkpatrick - On Loops, Artist Ego and Originality]] but argues something different (that video is about not being embarrassed to reuse your own loop/effect; this one is about A&Rs instantly rejecting a demo with no original layer on top of stock loops); his "most songs get cut" comment echoes the "ratios are real" point already logged from the Bad Liar breakdown; and the Wyoming songwriting-camp mention is a different anecdote from the found-sound Wyoming story (Emily and Caroline's background talking gated into percussion) already routed under the New Rules note — same camp, different detail. Everything else here — Cthulhu for chords, the "anchors of familiarity" concept, manager advice, the MediaBay workflow, and what he looks for in a writer/artist — is new material not covered elsewhere in the vault.

## Techniques
- **Cthulhu for instant chord ideas** — as a keyboard player, he leans on Xfer Cthulhu's community chord presets, browsing live until something "leans on chords so nice" — a fast way into interesting harmony without deep theory knowledge. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **Don't chase final-mix skill as a songs-producer — chase a "decent demo"** — at his career stage he deliberately doesn't work toward a radio/Spotify-ready final mix; that's the mixing engineer's job downstream. His only bar for a demo is that it sounds decent enough to sell the song, because specializing beats spreading himself thin on a skill "someone else can just do better." → [[Mix Handoff]] — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **Obsession first, income later — get a normal job while you build** — you have to be "totally obsessed," constantly cooking up stuff and chasing why something doesn't sound right, but don't feel bad about needing a day job for years while it's hard to make money from production alone. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **What he wants from a writer: conversational lyrics, not a "series of lines"** — singles out Julia Michaels and Billie Eilish for writing lyrics that read as a story or conversation rather than a checklist ("you did this to me, I did this back") — the words don't sound like someone visibly trying to write a song. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **What he wants from an artist: a clear identity or direction** — direction "gives me something to work with"; an artist with a strong point of view is easier and more rewarding to produce for than one without one. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **"Anchors of familiarity" — plant a couple of recognizable reference points, then be original everywhere else** — a listener doesn't know if they like a brand-new song in the first few seconds, so he seeds small familiar elements to "lock people in" before the rest of the production goes original. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **Being a "better loser" is the actual skill** — even at his level, most songs he writes get passed on; he frames his edge as being good at losing (staying productive through constant rejection) rather than winning every time. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - On Loops, Artist Ego and Originality]] (companion "don't be precious" philosophy) — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **Everything is written on spec, always** — finish and send the song with no guarantee; only once it's chosen does his manager negotiate the actual deal. He frames every song as a competition to win, not a paid assignment. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **He doesn't trust his own "this is a hit" instinct** — too close to his own work to judge it objectively; relies on other people's reactions (or silence) to know whether a song actually landed. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **The Instagram-flood analogy for originality** — photographers feared Instagram would ruin photography by flooding it with amateur photos; instead it made the genuinely good work stand out more ("you're gonna see the diamonds"). He applies the same logic to an oversaturated demo pool — quality still surfaces. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **Don't build a demo purely out of stacked Splice loops — it's an instant no** — he (and every A&R) screens demos for "something unique"; a track that's just loops stacked on top of each other reads as generic and gets rejected fast. Distinct from [[Ian Kirkpatrick - On Loops, Artist Ego and Originality]], which is about not being embarrassed to reuse a single loop you've already used — this is about a demo with zero original layer on top of stock material. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **Manager advice: pick a hungry up-and-comer over a big, established company early on** — at a large management company you're a low priority on a crowded producer roster; a young manager who's also hustling to make a name will push harder for you because your win is also their win. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **"New Rules" started at a Dua Lipa songwriting camp in Wyoming** — spent a week at "Emily's place" in Wyoming with other writers, working with the specific aim of pitching Dua Lipa; wrote roughly four ideas that week and "New Rules" was the one that stuck. A different anecdote from the found-sound Wyoming story (Emily and Caroline's background talking gated into the percussion layer) already logged in → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - New Rules (25 Big Tips in 13 Min)]] — same camp, different detail. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **A producer's job when the artist loves something you're unsure about: defer to their passion** — if the artist is genuinely passionate about an idea, it isn't the producer's place to shoot it down; the job is to take the artist's vision and execute it well, not overrule their taste. — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)
- **Cubase MediaBay: index a 1.5 TB sample library and use star ratings for instant recall** — rather than manually digging through folders, he searches within selected folders (e.g. "percussion") across his whole indexed library, filters by his own star ratings (e.g. "show me all my rated kicks"), and sorts by duration to pull the right length fast — calls MediaBay the reason he loves Cubase. → [[Sample Browser and Clips Workflow]] — Ian Kirkpatrick (Sol State), [Music Production Q&A with Dua Lipa producer Ian Kirkpatrick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jELfbaiWEG4)

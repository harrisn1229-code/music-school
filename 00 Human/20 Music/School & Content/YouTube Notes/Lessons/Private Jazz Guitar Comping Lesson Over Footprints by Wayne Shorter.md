# Private Jazz Guitar Comping Lesson Over Footprints by Wayne Shorter

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tb7Vw2N3SzM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tb7Vw2N3SzM)

## Synopsis

A real 1-on-1 Zoom lesson (filmed as an experiment) with a returning student, Alex, who plays comping guitar behind a violinist in a working group and feels stuck repeating the same voicing/rhythm every chorus over "Footprints." Jared's core diagnosis: the student conflates two separate problems — "I only know one thing" (a voicing-quantity problem, solved cheaply) and "I want colorful substitutions" (a separate, harder skill to layer in later) — and the lesson is structured to fix the cheap, high-leverage problem first.

## Techniques

- **Voicing-multiplication by top-note: the single highest-leverage comping fix, demonstrated live.** For any static chord (worked on Cm7/Cm11), find 3–4 different inversions/voicings whose only meaningful difference is *which chord tone sits on top* — root, 5th, 3rd, ♭7 (the shell-voicing top note) — since the listener perceives a static chord largely through its top note. Moving from 1 voicing to 2 "doubles the value," to 3 "triples the value," with almost no new theory required — described as the single easiest, quickest win available to any comping player.
- **Modal comping over a static minor vamp — the harmonic reason "Footprints"' Cm11 supports so much freedom.** A chord written as "Cm11" on a lead sheet over a modal tune is really shorthand for **quartal/modal harmony rooted in C Dorian** (the ii of B♭ major) — not a fixed tertian chord to voice literally. Practical payoff: over that same vamp, ANY diatonic chord from the parent B♭ major scale can be substituted in and will still "work" because it shares the same modal color — worked live moving up a whole step to Dm-family voicings, and named (not worked) as also available: B♭maj7, E♭maj7, and their inversions, all diatonic to the same mode.
- **A quartal "hub" comping shape for modal minor vamps, moved diatonically.** Stacked-4ths voicing on strings 4–2, built off the 3rd fret (reads structurally like a "minor 11" shape without literally being one) — grooved as a repeating whole-step/half-step figure (3rd fret → 5th fret → 7th fret, back down) over the Cm vamp, then the identical shape relocated to F for the tune's F minor chord. Named a genuinely reactive, call-and-response-capable comping tool once internalized, not just a static pad.
- **Turnaround vocabulary chunking — a practical, gig-ready alternative to free-improvising every chord change.** Rather than trying to reharmonize a 4-chord turnaround (worked on the tune's own Fø7–F7♯9–E7♯9–A7♯5 turnaround) chord-by-chord in real time, memorize the whole 4-chord string as one linked unit, then build 2–4 total alternate "strings" (full alternate voicings for all four chords, changing mainly which chord tone sits on top) — practice and switch between complete strings, not individual chords in isolation. Stated as the realistic path to actually using new vocabulary on a gig, vs. getting lost trying to learn every possible chord-scale substitution for every single chord.
- **Half-step-away passing/approach chords as a "no theory required" comping color.** Insert a chord a half step above OR below any target chord, in any direction, as a quick passing/crunch sound before resolving — works purely because it resolves by half step, independent of its "correct" harmonic function/name; can be played briskly (a Blues-style passing-tone effect) rather than sustained. A simpler, function-agnostic sibling of [[Jazz Comping]]'s existing tritone-substitution-specific walking-dominant technique — use before formalizing it into a named substitution.
- **Right-hand articulation as a second, independent axis of variety, separate from left-hand voicing choice.** The same chord/voicing string can be played as a groove pattern, a straight strum, or arpeggiated — each drastically changes the perceived sound without changing a single fretted note. Combined with 3–4 voicing options, this multiplies into "10 different things" from a genuinely small practice investment.
- **Psychological framing, stated directly and worth preserving:** feeling like you're "faking it" by repeating one memorized, good-sounding option is not a problem to solve before gigging — "that's what's called practicing... go play the gig anyway." Add options one at a time (1 → 2 → 3), each fully internalized before the next, rather than trying to arrive with a complete system. A recurring feeling of staleness/dissatisfaction with one's own comping is reframed as the direct trigger for a breakthrough, not a sign of inadequacy.

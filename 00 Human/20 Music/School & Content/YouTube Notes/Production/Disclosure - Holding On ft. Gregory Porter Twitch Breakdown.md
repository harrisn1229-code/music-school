# Disclosure - Holding On ft. Gregory Porter Twitch Breakdown

Breakdown of "Holding On" (Caracal) — a garage banger that started life as a **piano ballad** written with jazz singer Gregory Porter, then got flipped into a house remix from the acapella. Guy walks the **2019 re-mix vs the 2015 original**, so it's rich in *what I'd fix now*: the killer **untamed vocal resonance** he only heard in a club, a stereo'd sub he calls "a crazy move," and a bare drum bus with "nothing interacting." Keepers: the strategy of **writing in an out-of-genre singer's comfort zone then remixing the acapella**, the **delayed drop (kick on beat 3) to make room for the vocal**, kick **boxiness EQ**, and the **Oxford Limiter as glue with character**. ## Techniques
- **Get an out-of-genre vocalist by writing in *their* comfort zone first, then remix the acapella.** Gregory Porter had never sung on a house/garage tune, so they wrote a great piano ballad with him (Howard + Jimmy Napes), then took the acapella and built the remix around it — re-chorded, restructured, sped up. → workflow / [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].
- **Delayed drop: bring the kick in on beat 3, not beat 1** — leave the kick out until the vocal hook lands ("shake it — *bang*") so the drop makes space for the singer and hits harder; it also syncs to the chord rhythm. A signature Disclosure move, great live ("the crowd counts 1-2-3-4… then it drops"). → [[House - EDM]] arrangement.
- **Resonance-tame a powerful vocal recorded through analog hardware.** A big sustained note through "nice uneven hardware" throws strong harmonics/resonances — you get the richness *and* a harsh peak you must notch out. The 2015 mix left it in and it was "killer" loud in a club; the 2019 fix is a single notch. Learn to hunt resonances, not just de-ess. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[De-essing]].
- **In a vocal stack, only the *loudest/most-forward* vocal needs the sibilant.** The upfront center vocal carries the T/S/P sounds; mute or tame the esses on the doubles rather than de-essing all of them. → [[De-essing]].
- **Kick EQ: cut the 500–700 Hz "knock-on-wood" boxiness**, boost the top-end click so it reads in a busy mix, add ~40 Hz for sub. (Self-flag: don't gut *all* of 100 Hz, and **never make the kick/sub stereo** — "a crazy move.") → [[Kick Design]], [[Width and Stereo]].
- **Oxford Limiter as glue *with character*** — on the kick+clap it adds a pleasing distortion/overdrive that crunches them together (limiters have a "feel"); without it the clap "sits above the mix." → [[Limiting]].
- **The bus processing is what makes a mix *interact*** — the bare 2015 drum bus had punch and width but "nothing gluing, nothing interacting"; the remix bus (comp → tape → [[Oxford Inflator]] → automated reverb → tube sat → EQ → Oxford Limiter) is what makes the parts squash each other into one. → [[Compression Staging]].
- **Volume-automate via the compressor's makeup gain** — one way to fade a part down slowly and unnoticed (while a reverb builds) so the next section's entry feels dramatic. → [[Automation]].
- **DIY risers on a Korg Monotribe** — hand-jammed LFO/air-raid-siren build-ups and a tremolo that starts center then widens + speeds up ("a crash made of frequency bouncing around"); the pre-Splice way to make your own transitions. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Depth and Space]].
- **Bass = one mono, glidey square-wave synth, no layering** — an old pre-[[Monark]] mono synth chosen purely for its perfect glide; overdrive → bit-crush buzz on top → delay (widen) → reverb (sub removed so it's not boomy). → [[1 Digital Bass Track|Digital Bass Track]].
- **Pads:** a recorded **Juno** bounced to audio ("can't get that resonance from any other synth" — though he ran it a bit hot/distorting, a self-flagged mistake) + a stock FM "digital poly" (DX-style twinkle up top) + a FabFilter saw on the drop + Logic strings doing single-note rises for tension. Chords: G minor 9 with a few 13ths, moved around. → [[Roland Juno-106]], [[BED Slot]].
- **Acapella sped up in Ableton** — warp + speed to 124 BPM in Ableton, then back to Logic (not Flex/pitch in Logic). The fast vibrato reads as classic garage. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]].

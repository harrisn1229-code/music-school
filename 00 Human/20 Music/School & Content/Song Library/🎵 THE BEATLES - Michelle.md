# Michelle — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4) · Verse (6) · Verse · Bridge (10) · Verse · Bridge · Verse (guitar solo) · Bridge · Verse · Outro. The four-bar intro doubles as the **back half of every bridge** and returns again in the outro — one idea covering three structural jobs.

Every verse opens on an F major chord, but the song's centre of gravity is F **minor**: the major triad is bracketed by minor material on both sides and lands as a small surprise each time. The intro is best heard as one sustained F minor chord with a chromatic inner line (F–E–E♭–D) moving against it — a prolongation, not a new chord per beat. Play the same scale four times faster and the effect vanishes; the slow rate is what makes it work.

The verse's I → iv7 turn inside two bars is the hook, parallel-minor borrowing where \"Yesterday\" used the relative ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The iv → ♭VII opening looks set to walk the minor circle of fifths and gets cut off by a diminished seventh, and that **vii°-of-V gets unusual airtime** ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]), dragging an augmented second into the tune with it.

The melody leaps far more than McCartney usually writes, and the leaps trace chord outlines so the tune keeps pointing at the harmony. The climax is bar 1 of the bridge, where the withheld high notes are finally spent over a 9–8 appoggiatura ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]). The solo sits in the baritone range of the electric guitar, deliberately not sounding like the Beatles.

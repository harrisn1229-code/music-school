# Master Chain

**Role:** The final 10%. If the buses did their jobs, every move here is ±1–2 dB. Two modes: **mix-into-it** (the tonal/glue stages live on the master from early in the mix, so you're always hearing "the record" — the Disclosure/SSL-Fusion habit) and **final loudness** (the last three stages, engaged at export time).

## Chain overview
Corrective EQ → adaptive clarity → glue → tape → imaging → density → clip → limit. Corrective before compression (the comp should react to a balanced signal); limiter dead last, always.

## Session Setup
- 🎬 *From video:* a strong stated preference on which sample rate to work/master in, corroborating and adding nuance to the §3 entry below — **avoid 48 kHz** ("the most digital sounding to me, I hear it every single time... a little strident, a little hard"); **44.1 kHz or 96 kHz preferred** ("so much more musical," "it loves 96"); **88.2 kHz the weak middle option** ("kind of bland, just kind of sits there"). One engineer's consistent ear over a decade of sessions, not a measured claim — full detail on [[Session Templates]]. → [[Session Templates]], [[Critical Listening for Mastering]] — Jack Joseph Puig (Mix with the Masters), [Listening to the details with Jack Joseph Puig](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=20CbTBdY8sk)
- 🎬 *From video:* a drums-first gain-structure checkpoint with a concrete number, from a working mix (not the mastering session itself, but the discipline that sets this chain's own input up correctly): route the drum bus through an analog-console-emulation stage into a bus compressor and set gain so it **hits the compressor at roughly −1 dB** — since drums are deliberately the loudest element, that reading confirms proper gain structure and guarantees headroom for everything layered in after. Check the 2-bus next: sweet spot **−5 to −6 dB**, leaving ~5 dB of headroom before 0. See [[Gain Staging]] for the full recipe. — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters), [Mixing Summits with Jaycen Joshua](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L6xx7DWKzng)
- 🎬 *From video:* near-master every section/stem individually BEFORE it ever reaches this chain — drums, hats, instrumental, and vocals each get processed (EQ'd, compressed, limited) close to a finished level on their own, so each one barely nudges the 2-bus when it lands, and this chain's own final limiting stage has almost nothing left to do. The limiter used per-stem for this is framed as **adaptive** rather than threshold-hunting (see [[Limiting]] for the full mechanism) — reacts to whatever energy it's given rather than chasing a fixed dB number. Corroborates this chain's own "a limiter should end up doing almost nothing" philosophy (§8 below) from the stem side rather than the final-bus side. → [[Limiting]] (effect) — Jaycen Joshua (Mix with the Masters), [Mixing Summits with Jaycen Joshua](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L6xx7DWKzng)
- 🎬 *From video:* import the stereo mix into a dedicated mastering session at a **minimum of 24-bit / 96 kHz** — even when the mix's own sample rate is lower, mastering at a higher rate reduces the aliasing your own processing (saturation, clipping, limiting) introduces; treat the mix's own settings as a floor, not a ceiling. Before touching any processor, pull in **~5 dB of headroom via clip gain** (not a fader) — a starting point, not a fixed rule — and roughly level-match tracks first if mastering several in one sitting. Signal routing, plainly stated: **file/waveform → inserts → any aux sends/buses → stereo output** — decide the order deliberately since each stage hits the signal in the order it's placed — Sage Audio, [How to Setup Your Mastering Chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y-5B4kOXUoM)

## Signal chain — mix-into-it stages

### 1. [[Pro-Q 4]] — Corrective
- Natural-phase while mixing (linear-phase only at final export — it costs latency and transient smear)
- HPF 20–25 Hz gentle · fix only *whole-mix* issues (a resonance every element shares); anything else belongs on a bus.
- 🎬 *From video:* the first insert in a mastering chain is consequential — it sets the direction of everything downstream — and there are two valid candidates for it, not just one: **subtractive EQ** (attenuate parts of the spectrum you don't want amplified later — the stated preference, since it "sets me up well for having a successful chain later on," matching this stage's own subtractive-first approach) or a **mastering preamp** (unowned as a hardware/plugin category — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]). Keep the move subtle either way — it's mastering, and it's the first insert. — Sage Audio, [How to Make a Mastering Chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WNkkwWh7DJg)
- 🎬 *From video:* a general ordering discipline for this stage's role — the first EQ insert in a mastering chain is typically **subtractive** (attenuate whatever shouldn't carry forward, stereo L/R or mid-side depending on where the offending content sits), saving **additive** boosts for a later EQ insert once the signal's already been cleaned up; some engineers combine both moves into one first insert instead, a valid variant on this stage's own approach — Sage Audio, [How to Setup Your Mastering Chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y-5B4kOXUoM)
- 🎬 *From video:* on a lo-fi master, keep this stage minimal on purpose — highpass everything at **20 Hz and below** ("just in case anything got caught," not expected to do much) plus a small dip around **200–250 Hz** to stop the low-mid range congesting once everything sums; explicitly framed as cleanup, not tone-shaping — the genre's whole appeal is not needing the "fancy mixing techniques" a cleaner style would call for here — Max Konyi, [Final Mix & Master | Studio One LoFi Tutorial | Part 9](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OKUCuyLBSg)
- 🎬 *From video:* treat any master EQ as a **bypassed guide** — do the tiny linear-phase move (mids up, −1 to −1.5 dB super-highs), then screenshot it for the mastering engineer instead of baking it in — Disclosure, [Looking For Love](https://youtu.be/NlJt0pCptGk).
- 🎬 *From video:* a general-purpose corrective-EQ recipe for this stage — linear phase at a **low-latency** setting (minimal pre-ringing, less transient smear than zero-latency), a **side-image-only HPF** (18–24 dB/oct) placed **between the source's fundamental and its 2nd-order harmonic** (makes everything below identical L/R without touching the fundamental), a small dip ~250 Hz (masking/mud), and a small dip in a resonant high-mid pocket (vocal nasal resonance, busy instruments). The source video runs a second EQ pass after dynamics (this chain doesn't have one — §5 Ozone 9 Imager plus this stage cover the same ground) that reapplies the same side-image HPF, since downstream dynamics stages reintroduce side-image lows — worth a quick recheck here if §2–§4 read wide in the lows. — Sage Audio, [The Only Music Mastering Video You'll Ever Need](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M88T8jFL2uU) → [[Width and Stereo]]
- 🎬 *From video:* this stage's own gain-staging ceiling and phase mechanism, stated as a general mastering-EQ rule rather than tied to any one move above — never move a band more than **±3 dB** here unless fixing a serious mix problem with no access to revise the mix; normal working range **0.25–2 dB**. Mechanically, this stage's **natural-phase** mode shifts the signal's phase on every boost/cut, and that shift **alters the shape of the curve itself**, not just tone — reach for linear phase (already this stage's own preference at final export, per the top of this section) when precision matters more than a natural feel. When genuinely unsure what a master needs beyond the moves already logged here: a generic starting point is a small boost **~80 Hz**, a small boost **~2 kHz**, a subtle shelf **~5 kHz and up** — louder kick, more prominent vocal, clearer master; not a fit for every mix. — Sage Audio, [How to EQ Your Master](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GF-XMVa3lW8) → [[EQ]]
- 🎬 *From video:* two SUBTRACTIVE dynamic-EQ moves that belong right here, at this stage, since dynamic-EQ subtraction is meant to run early in the chain. **Sibilance:** watch the analyzer for high-frequency spikes and match their timing against when an S is actually sung/spoken (confirms it's sibilance, not something else at the same frequency), then center a Dynamic band on the confirmed spot. **Muddiness:** a Dynamic band around **200 Hz**, using the plugin's Isolate/solo tool to pin the exact culprit frequency inside that bell's bandwidth by ear before attenuating — don't drag it too far down the spectrum chasing it, or it starts eating content unrelated to the mud. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[De-essing]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)
- 🎬 *From video:* a deliberately minimal two-move version of this stage, from a "keep peaks intact" personal chain — **side-image HPF at 12 dB/octave** (gentler slope than this stage's existing linear-phase side-HPF entries above, chosen specifically to keep phase rotation to a minimum while still focusing lows into the center) plus a **mid-image bell dip in the highs** for sibilance, center frequency mix-dependent. Discipline stated alongside it, worth carrying as a general rule for this whole stage: only change the spectrum where it's absolutely needed, since **all EQ types affect transients to some degree**, not just linear-phase — a reason to run this stage's cuts as sparingly as this source does. → [[EQ]] — Sage Audio, [My Favorite Mastering Chain - Why Simple is Better](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bLVMsAwjRPw)
- 🎬 *From video:* a worked EDM opening mid-side EQ recipe distinct from this stage's other mid/side entries — **side image:** cut lows, boost ~**200 Hz** (where the low-order harmonics that matter sit), high shelf for width; **mid image:** amplify the kick's fundamental, dip a small amount ~**250 Hz** (declutters so higher frequencies read louder), boost ~**3 kHz** for the same reason. Followed immediately in the source by a transient/tonal split-EQ pass (almost certainly Eventide/Soundtheory Split EQ, unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) with **per-band mid-side panning**: lows centered on transients, high-mids' tonal band subtly widened, highs' transients re-centered *and* tonal band widened a good amount — a genuinely new width move beyond this chain's existing mid/side EQ recipes. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — Sage Audio, [Creating an EDM Mastering Chain in 10 Steps](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yOJ-EtjFD34)

### 2. [[Soothe2]] — Dynamic unmasking
- **Soft mode** · low-ish depth (this is a master — 2–4 dB of reduction max) · sharpness/selectivity moderate · **fence the low end off** (set the low band edge ~120–150 Hz so it doesn't re-decide your kick/808 balance) · delta-listen to hear only what it's removing, then back off
- Pair with **[[Tonal Balance Control 2]]** open as a *meter* (not processing) to catch the broad tonal-balance errors Gullfoss used to auto-correct — nudge those on §1 [[Pro-Q 4]] instead.
- **Why:** Soothe2 dynamically suppresses resonances/masking as they occur (the same "unmask hidden detail, tame buildup only while the problem is present" idea), so the body returns the instant the resonance passes. *(Owned substitute for Gullfoss Master, which isn't licensed — Soothe2 covers the dynamic-unmasking half; Tonal Balance Control 2 + Pro-Q cover the tonal-balance half Gullfoss also did automatically. Note: standard [[Gullfoss]] IS owned — see the 🎬 note directly below for a way to use it here alongside/instead of Soothe2.)*
- 🎬 *From video:* run standard [[Gullfoss]] (owned, distinct from the unlicensed Gullfoss Master above) isolated to **mids and highs only** — pull the frequency-range limiters in to exclude the kick/low end, since Gullfoss's real-time analysis introduces latency that could smear a transient. Lean mainly on **Recover** to bring buried mix detail forward. (The source captions describe this as "reducing phase cancellation" — that doesn't match Gullfoss's documented mechanism; read it as the plugin's ordinary unmasking job.) — Sage Audio, [How to Master a Song Start to Finish](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aUygMNRx2yE) → [[Gullfoss]]
- 🎬 *From video:* resonance reduction reframed as multiband compression with a preset threshold, not a form of EQ — use the **"Bounce to the Grammy Awards"** preset here, which measures the mid and side images separately so suppression can differ between center and sides; adjust the pre-emphasis EQ bands from there to target whatever's too prevalent (commonly low-mids and the sibilance range, confirm by ear), higher-quality/oversampling settings to reduce phase cancellation, dial the final amount with the mix knob — Sage Audio, [Mastering Compression Settings](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YUn31aE9X_8)

### 3. [[Glue Compressor]] — The glue
- **Ratio 2:1** (②) · **Attack 30 ms** (③ — slow, transients pass) · **Release Auto** (④ — breathes with the tempo) · **Threshold for 1.5 dB GR on the beat** (①) · **Makeup** to exact unity (⑤ — level-matched A/B or it always sounds "better") · **Soft Clip OFF** (⑧ — let the dedicated limiter cap peaks) · **Dry/Wet 100%** (⑦)
- **Why:** The SSL-bus-comp move — 1.5 dB of slow stereo compression is what makes a collection of buses sound like *a record*. More than 2–3 dB here means a bus is too hot upstream — fix it there, not with the glue comp.
- 🎬 *From video:* BPM-time the release — the actual formula, not just "match the tempo": `60,000 ÷ BPM = one quarter note in ms`, ×4 for a whole note, minus the attack time = the release (140 BPM example: 429 ms → 1,716 ms whole note, minus attack). An out-of-time release makes the return-to-unity land like a rogue transient and can mess up perceived timing. Pairs with a vari-mu character compressor for tone (not owned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) — this stage's Soft Clip is the closest owned stand-in for the harmonic-coloration half — Sage Audio, [Mastering Compression Settings](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YUn31aE9X_8)
- 🎬 *From video:* when a kick dominates this stage's gain reduction on a kick-heavy mix, filter the compressor's own detector — a **self-sidechain highpass at ~70 Hz** — rather than reaching for an external sidechain track; the kick's dominant low end stops driving the compressor by itself while everything above 70 Hz (including the kick's own transient content) still triggers it normally. Worked settings on a gentle lo-fi glue pass: ratio 2:1, attack ~100 ms, release ~100 ms (slow enough that individual transients pass through), target only 2–3 dB GR max, landing ~2 dB with the self-sidechain filter engaged — Max Konyi, [Final Mix & Master | Studio One LoFi Tutorial | Part 9](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OKUCuyLBSg) → [[Sidechaining]], [[Compression]]
- 🎬 *From video:* a mastering-specific target for this stage — **1–2 dB total, Peak/RMS switch biased toward RMS with a little Peak blended in**, tuned so it's inaudible as compression, just quieter. The point: keeping this stage this light shrinks §8's job down to a few dB, which is what keeps the limiter itself from sounding noticeable. — Sage Audio, [The Only Music Mastering Video You'll Ever Need](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M88T8jFL2uU)
- 🎬 *From video:* whether to compress at this stage at all is a real question, not a given — mastering compression is often debated and should be used **only if the master actually needs it**. When it is warranted, compressor *type* sets the character: **optical** reads smooth/gentle (good for a subtle glue pass, matching this stage's own light-touch settings above), **digital** reads more aggressive (better suited to louder/harder genres) — pick the type for the genre before dialing in the settings above — Sage Audio, [How to Setup Your Mastering Chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y-5B4kOXUoM)
- 🎬 *From video:* an alternative to this stage's plain glue setting — **mid-side processing with de-linked triggering**, so the mid image compresses more than the side (mid usually drives the detector more), reading as stereo expansion without ever isolating processing to the mid alone. Paired feature on the source unit: **"dual phase rotation"** — mild phase cancellation introduced at higher frequencies, netting a fuller-sounding low end as a side effect; worth trying on any owned M/S-capable bus compressor even without a name match (source plugin unclear from captions — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]). — Sage Audio, [How to Master a Song Start to Finish](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aUygMNRx2yE) → [[Width and Stereo]]
- 🎬 *From video:* glue isn't only what THIS stage does — it's also a function of which buses/groups get routed together upstream. Rafa Sardina runs a hardware SSL/summing-box hybrid where "glue you also create with your choices of summing, your choices [of] share compression, [routing] certain elements in the mix" — routing drums and bass through the same bus for a blended feel, a separate bus for a more distinct part. Ableton-native equivalent: which tracks share a Group/return, not just what sits on this master stage, is itself a glue decision — made early, at song-evaluation time. His own master-stage glue chain (SSL-style comp + a subtractive GML-style EQ cutting ~300 Hz, a small additive Sontec-style "flavor" boost ~1 kHz) runs from EARLY in the mix, the same "mix-into-it" mode this chain documents at the top of this section — not just at export. → [[Session Templates]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[EQ]] — Mix with the Masters (Rafa Sardina), [[Rafa Sardina's mixing workflow]]
- 🎬 *From video:* the SSL-bus-comp move taken further — as ONE of several parallel stereo buses (a "multibus" setup, not this chain's single-glue-stage default) each doing a little, summed together. Checked at the loudest chorus, GR reads "barely three [dB]" at **30 ms / 4:1 ratio / 0.1 s release** — fast enough to never audibly hold the signal — paired with a second parallel-blend compressor run mostly dry and a passive tube EQ engaged for its circuit coloration alone, no EQ move dialed in. The driving constraint: an explicit brief to avoid sounding "overly compressed." Full structure (multiple simultaneous parallel buses, doubling via a second bus, the bypassed-vs-engaged A/B demo method) on [[Parallel Processing]]. → [[Solid Bus Comp]], [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[Compression]] — Michael Brauer (Mix with the Masters), [[The power of multi-bus processing with Michael Brauer]]

### 4. [[Kramer Tape]] — Tape finish
- **15 ips** (④) · **Flux very low** (① — barely driven) · **Record ~+1** (②) · **Playback to unity** (③) · **Wow & Flutter off** (⑥) · **Noise off** (⑦)
- **Why:** Final rounding — top-end sweetening + gentle transient softening that reads as "expensive."
- 🎬 *From video:* a hardware validation of this exact stage — bussing several tracks to a physical tape deck together and consolidating them to a stereo pass produces glue credited specifically to "tape's compression and saturation," the same principle this plugin runs here, just done earlier and on hardware. Also worth remembering: an A/B test found an all-digital mix of the same song read as *more* characterful than the tape-printed version — most of what reads as "warmth" gets decided upstream of this stage, not created by it. See [[Committing and Printing]] — Zach Scholz, [Using Analog Tape to Glue a Mix Together](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xk0LYE7HGAI) & [Why 70's Records Sound so Warm](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s6eGa5NOTYg)

### 5. [[Ozone 9 Imager]] — Imaging
- Band 1 (<120 Hz): width DOWN to 0 (mono low end — punch and vinyl/club safety) · top band: +5–10% width max if the mix needs it
- **Why:** Frequency-dependent width is the safe kind: mono where power lives, wide where sparkle lives.
- 🎬 *From video:* measured research puts a hard number on "safe" width — commercial masters keep L/R RMS within **0.8 dB** of each other, expansion above 200 Hz is fine but small at this stage, and any expansion here should be an M/S-balance/EQ move (this stage), never delay-based — a demonstrated A/B showed a delay-based expander losing focus on centered elements (lead vocal/bass/kick/snare) where an EQ-based side-image boost didn't. Amplify what's already there in the side image rather than manufacture new inter-channel differences. — Sage Audio, [Audio Mastering Science: Using Research to Make Informed Decisions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21dpmumO4So) → [[Width and Stereo]]
- 🎬 *From video:* a third additive mid-side EQ recipe for this stage, distinct from the two boost/attenuate recipes below — ear-sensitivity-driven rather than phase-driven. Amplify the **side image's highs and mid frequencies**, amplify **3–5 kHz on the mid** (the range ears are most sensitive to — reads as an overall louder mix without more gain), and optionally amplify **the kick's fundamental**. Placed after the corrective/de-essing/dynamics stages, same "additive EQ goes later" sequencing this chain already follows at §1. → [[EQ]] — Sage Audio, [How to Make a Mastering Chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WNkkwWh7DJg)
- 🎬 *From video:* a mid/side EQ width recipe pairing a matched boost/attenuate move rather than boosting the side alone — boost the **mid** image at **~80 Hz** (kick) and **~2 kHz** (vocal), then **attenuate the side image at those same two frequencies** to reduce phase cancellation between the mid and side content; separately, a **high shelf on the side image** widens perceived top end "more times than not." Distinct from this stage's existing mid/side EQ entries (which pair a side HPF + mid ~2.5 kHz boost, no matching side attenuation) — a genuine alternative recipe, worth A/B'ing rather than a straight replacement. → [[Width and Stereo]] — Sage Audio, [How to EQ Your Master](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GF-XMVa3lW8)
- 🎬 *From video:* four ADDITIVE dynamic-EQ moves that belong at this point in the chain — after §3/§4's compression and saturation, per this chain's own additive-dynamic-EQ sequencing rule (§1, and see [[EQ]]). **Kick:** a Dynamic bell centered on the kick's most prominent spectrum frequency, expanded **+3 dB** above the filter. **Vocal:** the same expansion mechanism, bell at **~2 kHz** on the mid/stereo channel, triggers whenever the vocal is sung. **Width:** a Dynamic shelf on the **side image ~7 kHz+**, range ~2 dB, expanding only when high-frequency content is present — dynamic width rather than this stage's own static Imager settings above. **Mid-range power:** a Dynamic band on the mid frequencies reads differently by placement — mid image makes drums/vocal more dynamic/impressive, side image makes guitar body and reverb tails more dynamic; worth balancing both. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Kick Design]], [[Masking and Carving]] — Sage Audio, [How to Use Dynamic Eq During Mastering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tvuBplNrYMs)

## Signal chain — final loudness stages (export time)

### 6. [[Oxford Inflator]] — Density
- Effect 15–30%, Curve 0 — perceived loudness before any GR is spent.
- 🎬 *From video:* a developer-recommended operating method for this stage, distinct from its existing Effect-15–30%/Curve-0 setting above — **Effect to 100, then use Input to dial how aggressive the processing reads**, rather than a low fixed Effect amount. Framed as prioritizing signal content with a positive value over content with a negative value, raising the ratio of signal-to-silence — the same upward-processing mechanism this stage already runs, just driven from the opposite end of the Effect knob. Worth A/B'ing against the existing setting rather than a straight swap. — Sage Audio, [How to Make a Mastering Chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WNkkwWh7DJg)
- 🎬 *From video:* Disclosure's current master is basically **just this + a clipper** — Inflator does the upward loudness, no EQ or comp on the bus — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8).
- 🎬 *From video (optional, rare):* **if — and only if — a mix reads disjointed rather than cohesive** (elements sound like they were recorded in different rooms), a subtle reverb send can go **right here** — after this stage's upward compression, before §7's clipper and §8's peak limiter — following a mastering-reverb tip: dial the send in with the limiter engaged (its makeup gain exposes tails that seemed subtle unlimited), check both loud and sparse sections and automate the send if no one level covers both, and use a realistic convolution reverb ([[Hybrid Reverb]]) or one with EQ + early/mid/late reflection control ([[ValhallaRoom]]) to boost cohesion only in the specific bands/reflection stages that sound disjointed. Never reach for this if the mix is already too blurred/masked — it will only make that worse. — Sage Audio, [Reverb on a Master: Everything You Need to Know](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vzJSKU7kGrU) → [[Reverb]], [[Depth and Space]]
- 🎬 *From video:* the mechanical reason this stage exists as a separate step from saturation and the §8 limiter — **upward processing reshapes the wave from its quietest points up**, preserving timbre/transient impact while adding loudness, vs. saturation (reshapes peaks) or limiting (retains peak shape, uses clean gain). Named alternatives for this exact slot, each with a different harmonic flavor: PSP Vintage Warmer 2's knee function, the Weiss MM-1 Maximizer (Softube) — neither owned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. — Sage Audio, [The Only Music Mastering Video You'll Ever Need](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M88T8jFL2uU) → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- 🎬 *From video:* mechanically, this stage doesn't work like a clipper — it pushes quieter signal upward, then gradually/gently rounds the amplitude down as it nears 0 dB rather than squaring the waveform; some call it a "soft clipper" but the wave-shaping is far more gradual than a clip's flat-topped squaring — Sage Audio, [Stop Limiting Your Masters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s_LDCzWx20g) → [[Clipping and Loudness]]
- 🎬 *From video:* whichever saturation source this stage (or a parallel saturator alongside it) uses — tape, tube, transistor, or otherwise, each with its own harmonic "clarity"/character flavor — keep it **subtle** at the mastering stage specifically (overdone saturation here reads as unpleasant distortion, not glue), and if the plugin offers it, **enable 2x-or-more oversampling** so the added harmonics don't alias — Sage Audio, [How to Setup Your Mastering Chain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y-5B4kOXUoM)
- 🎬 *From video:* pair this stage with a frequency-split parallel saturator for a two-tool density move — this stage does overall density (increase Effect, adjust Input to compensate), then a parallel valve/tube saturator (PSP per captions, exact model unclear, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) split so its low section targets the **kick's fundamental** specifically and its high section targets **vocal presence** specifically, not a generic wide low/high split. Result: the whole mix reads fuller from this stage while the kick and vocal specifically get more prominent from the saturator's targeted split. — Sage Audio, [How to Master a Song Start to Finish](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aUygMNRx2yE) → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- 🎬 *From video:* a direct mechanical comparison between this stage and the (unowned) Sonnox Oxford Limiter's Enhance function, answering a question the source says comes up often — **Enhance is the same processing as this stage with Curve set to 50**, but the two units land differently: this stage's harmonics run higher in amplitude in the lows and much subtler in the highs, while Enhance reads more balanced overall with extra emphasis on high-frequency harmonics that "tie the high range back in to the song's root note." Sharpens the Inflator-vs-Enhance comparison already logged on [[Plugin Wishlist]] with a concrete parameter match (Curve 50) rather than a general "similar processing" note. → [[Plugin Wishlist]] — Sage Audio, [My Favorite Mastering Chain - Why Simple is Better](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bLVMsAwjRPw)

### 7. [[kHs Clipper]] — Clip the peaks
- **Hard-clip mode** (② — Hardness max) — **Input to shave 1–2 dB** of transient peak (①). Hard clip = transparent (everything under the ceiling untouched, odd harmonics only on the shaved samples); soft clip = audible rounding/warmth — a tone choice, not a headroom tool.
- Aliasing paranoia is overblown here: clipping happens on transients, which mask it completely. But **beware oversampling** — enable kHs Clipper's oversampling and **verify with a true-/sample-peak meter** that the downsampling isn't recreating overs and silently eating the headroom you just made. *(Owned substitute for Gold Clip. If you need a guaranteed true-peak ceiling over a clipper's character, [[Pro-L 2]] in transparent mode is the owned fallback — but a limiter rounds transients where a clipper shaves them, so keep the clipper unless overs sneak through.)*
- **Why:** Clipping micro-transients (inaudible) hands the limiter a smaller job — the modern loudness recipe is clipper+limiter sharing the work, each doing what it's transparent at.
- 🎬 *From video:* lean on the clipper for loudness and let mix-wide saturation carry the rest — "enough saturation across the mix already, then clip; the clip *is* saturation." Keep tape on drums/bass, not here — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8).

### 8. [[Ozone 9 Maximizer]] — The limiter
- IRC IV · **ceiling −1.0 dBTP** (true peak — prevents streaming-codec clip) · push threshold for **2–3 dB GR max** on the loudest section
- 🎬 *From video:* this stage's role in a reactive, two-limiter stereo-bus pass — engage it only after listening confirms the bus needs it ("it's a reactive thing... I love to listen to music first and see if something needs something"), and when it's warranted, run [[Ozone 9 Equalizer]] with barely-there moves first (+0.3 dB ~80 Hz, +0.8 dB ~8 kHz) before this Maximizer module, for "a little more beef" rather than raw loudness. - 🎬 *From video:* the mechanical reason this stage's ~50 ms release floor (below) isn't arbitrary — a frequency's period is `1 ÷ frequency`, so 20 Hz (bottom of human hearing) works out to a 50 ms period; set the release *shorter* than that and it can alter a wave's amplitude mid-cycle (audible distortion), so 50 ms is a floor for full-range accuracy, not a target to undercut. Aliasing gets the same "why" here too: exceeding the frequency ceiling a low session sample rate (44.1 kHz) can represent reflects the excess back down the spectrum as harsh distortion — the mechanism behind this stage's own oversampling note above. → [[Limiting]], [[Pro-L 2]] — Sage Audio, [How to Master Loud and Clear Without Sounding Harsh](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cy-S6YmVe2U)

- 🎬 *From video:* a concrete release-time ladder for this stage, faster than this chain's own ">100 ms" target above — 50 ms is the sweet spot for a loud master without introducing distortion, faster mildly-to-moderately distorts, 250 ms+ for smoother, 5–10 ms for maximum loudness if some distortion is acceptable; and a mechanical note on the Attack control specifically — a limiter's true attack is always near-instant, what the Attack knob actually sets is the time before the release envelope begins (quicker = less distortion, longer = louder). *Tension flag: the 50 ms figure sits below this chain's own "release above 100 ms" target elsewhere on this stage — worth A/B'ing both, not a default swap.* → [[Limiting]], [[Pro-L 2]] — Sage Audio, [How to Limit Your Master](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SrFwQqDMJQM)

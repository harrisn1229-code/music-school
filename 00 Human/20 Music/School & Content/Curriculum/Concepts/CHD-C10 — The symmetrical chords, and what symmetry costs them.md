# CHD-C10 — The symmetrical chords, and what symmetry costs them

**Q.** What makes the diminished 7th and the augmented triad symmetrical, how many names does each have, and what does symmetry take away?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Diminished 7 is three stacked minor 3rds (3+3+3), four notes; augmented is two stacked major 3rds (4+4).** The pattern repeats, so any note can be read as the root — **four names for one diminished 7th** (and only three distinct dim7 chords exist), three for one augmented triad. What symmetry costs: no root the ear can pick out, no true inversion, and **no function of its own** — the chord's job has to be read off what follows it.

## Why it matters

That last cost is the useful half, and it reduces to one binary test: **is any note of the diminished chord the leading note of the chord that follows? No → a passing chord, colouring the line and driving nothing. Yes → a dominant substitute, and it will resolve.** The same ♯iv° is passing on the way to I (I's leading tone is not in it) and a functioning dominant on the way to V (♯4̂ *is* 5̂'s leading tone). One shape, two jobs, decided entirely by context — see [[Ch11 — Diminished: Songwriting Sleight-of-Hand]], since [[Diminished Chords]] teaches only the passing half.

## Drill

Slide one diminished 7th shape up three frets and confirm it is the same four pitches under a new name. Then find a diminished chord in a song you know, look at what follows it, and apply the leading-note test.

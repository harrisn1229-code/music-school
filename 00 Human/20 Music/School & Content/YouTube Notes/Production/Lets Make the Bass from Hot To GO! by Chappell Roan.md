# Lets Make the Bass from "Hot To GO!" by Chappell Roan

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mAqebsYA1ms)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mAqebsYA1ms)

## Synopsis

A multi-synth (Prophet-5, Jupiter-8, ARP 2600, then a deliberate Minimoog comparison) recreation of the "Hot To Go!" bass, built around one core recipe — octave-doubled oscillators with PWM-driven chorusing — plus a genuinely useful negative finding: why a synth WITHOUT pulse-width modulation can't fully substitute for one that has it, even via detuning.

## Techniques

- **The core recipe: sawtooth + square, one octave apart, with the square wave's pulse width modulated by a slow LFO for a "chorusing" richness — the low (square) oscillator's level backed off below the high (sawtooth) oscillator's so it thins rather than muddies.** PWM rate is a genuine sweet spot: too fast and "the synth sounds sick"; too slow and the part has "no attitude" — dial LFO speed and modulation-wheel depth to taste, not to a fixed number.
- **Filter resonance used deliberately as an informal high-pass, not for a resonant "peak."** On a bass patch, cranking resonance thins the low end out ("gets really thin and squirty"); backed off to near-zero it stays "bassy"/full. The move here is the reverse of the usual "add resonance for character" advice — use a *little* resonance specifically to shave excess low-frequency mud off a two-oscillator bass patch, not to create a resonant peak.
- **VCA envelope given a small nonzero attack (not zero) specifically for a "suction"/vacuum-like onset instead of a percussive "chop,"** paired with the filter envelope doing a slow attack/decay of its own for the tonal opening. Same "VCA envelope = mostly a gate, with a small deliberate deviation for feel" pattern seen elsewhere in this channel's patches.
- **Negative finding: a synth without pulse-width modulation cannot substitute for PWM chorusing via detuning alone, and the difference is audible as "sour" rather than "chorused."** On the Minimoog (no PWM), the only available substitute is detuning oscillator 2 — but PWM keeps the center frequency locked while its harmonics "wrap around" over time (reading as chorus without actual pitch drift), whereas plain detuning genuinely shifts pitch, which reads as slightly out-of-tune/sour rather than richer. Practical implication: when a target patch clearly uses PWM-based movement, check for PWM on the instrument/plugin you're using before assuming a detune workaround will pass for it.
- **The recurring meta-lesson, stated directly**: recreate the underlying *synthesis concept* (here: octave doubling + PWM chorusing + filter/envelope shaping), not the exact hardware — the same recipe is achievable "on a lot of different synths including some of the best plugins," and understanding *why* a given synth is or isn't a good candidate (has PWM or doesn't, has enough low-end headroom or doesn't) matters more than matching the original session's exact gear.

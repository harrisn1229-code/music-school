# Kick Design

Building and shaping a kick from a synth/kick-synth instead of relying on a sample — a recurring Disclosure habit (Latch's kick is a synth note; "No Cap" layers a tuned knock over a loop). The kick straddles two Palette slots: its **body** lives in [[SUB Slot]] (20–60 Hz) and its **click/beater** in [[TRANSIENT Slot]] / low [[BASS Slot]]. Companion to [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]] (the processing chain), [[Sidechaining]], and the [[Sound Palette]].

## Why synthesize a kick
- **Tune it to the song.** A synth kick's fundamental can be set to the track's root so it reinforces the key (Latch: the kick hits F every bar to establish an F-minor that otherwise avoids its root).
- **Shape any envelope.** You decide the pitch drop, the click, and the decay — impossible with a fixed sample. Guy learned kick-shaping on Logic's **Ultrabeat**; the modern tools are **Big Kick** and **Kick 2** (Nicky Romero).

## Anatomy of a synth kick *(general starting points — tune by ear)*
| Part | What it is | Starting point |
|---|---|---|
| Body / tone | The pitched sine/tri fundamental → [[SUB Slot]] | Tune to the song root (e.g. F1); amp decay 200–450 ms |
| Pitch envelope | The downward "pew" that reads as punch | +12 to +36 st → 0 over 15–40 ms |
| Click / beater | The attack transient → [[TRANSIENT Slot]] | Short noise/HF layer < 10 ms, or a click sample |
| Saturation | Harmonics so it reads on small speakers | [[Decapitator]] / Softube / tape — light |

## The Disclosure "knock" kick
All punch, no sub — ~100 Hz, "nothing at 50 Hz" (old UK-garage). Used when a **bendy sub loop or an [[SUB Slot]] bass already owns the low end**, so the kick only needs to knock through the [[BASS Slot]] without clashing (see "No Cap"). Pair with a real sub layer or an 808 for weight.

## Layering + placement
- **Two-kick layering:** a short punchy sub kick + a kick with a hat/click baked in; nudge the punchy one a hair ahead so its transient leads (see the fake-compression move in [[Compression]]).
- **Small-speaker check:** if the kick is sub-only it vanishes on phones — add an octave-up layer for 100–200 Hz warmth ([[Referencing and Translation]]).
- **vs bass:** kick and [[BASS Slot]] must not fight — sidechain or offset ([[Sidechaining]]).

## From books

- **Four-move kick EQ starting point, and where it disagrees with the vault's existing advice.** Low cut **40–60 Hz** (remove rumble — clears the [[SUB Slot]] of everything under the fundamental; note [[1 Drums Kick Track]] runs a lower 25–30 Hz HPF because that kick keeps its own sub weight, so 40–60 Hz is really the trap/808 setting where the [[Bass Design|808]] owns the sub) · low shelf **80–100 Hz** for thump (the low [[BASS Slot]] — what phones and small speakers actually reproduce as "kick") · boost **2–4 kHz** for snap (top of the [[MIDS Slot]] into the [[LEAD Slot]]; a [[TRANSIENT Slot]] job by role — matching the chain's existing "click +1–2 dB @ 3–5 kHz") · a band at **8–10 kHz** for click. ⚠️ That last one is printed as a **NOTCH** with "for click" as its reason — self-contradictory, since a notch there *removes* click; read it as a boost for click, or as a genuine narrow notch for a clicky resonance. Cross-check against Lophiile's opposing rule already on [[1 Drums Kick Track]]: don't low-pass a kick's top off ("a kick under a pillow"), shelf the lower-highs down and filter only the extreme highs, plus a ~1 kHz dip on boxy kicks. → [[EQ]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Masking and Carving]] — Whipped Cream Sounds, [[Drum Mixing EQ Guide - Enrichment|Drum Mixing EQ Guide]]

## From videos

- **The self-oscillating-filter kick trick (see the Habstrakt/Auto Filter entry below) applied on hardware with no dedicated pitch envelope, plus the full layer breakdown that completes it.** On a Minimoog (no direct oscillator-pitch envelope routing like an ARP 2600 has), push filter resonance to maximum so the filter itself becomes a sine-tone oscillator, then route the filter's own decay-time envelope to its cutoff — that decay IS the kick's pitch-drop speed, and contour amount is the pitch-drop DEPTH. Confirms the technique isn't ARP/Auto-Filter-specific — any synth whose filter can self-oscillate and be envelope-modulated can build a kick this way. **Completes the recipe with the other layers**: a separate tone oscillator (triangle for a softer kick, square for a bassier/harmonically richer one) mixed in underneath the pitch-drop; a little noise blended in for "air"/cushion; VCA decay time controls ring/sustain length (more = 808-like); VCA attack time controls roundness (a little softens the transient into "rounder," zero keeps it hard/percussive). → [[Envelopes and ADSR]], [[Auto Filter]] — Anthony Marinelli, [[How to Make Any Kick Drum on a Minimoog - 10cc I'm Not in Love]]

- **A single from-scratch synth-kick patch that covers the whole design space by varying only three controls**: oscillator (triangle/sine) → envelope generator modulates BOTH the oscillator's pitch (the downward "swoop") and a closed filter+VCA pair → mixer. Pitch-sweep depth is the main character control (none = tuned tonal thump; short/steep = traditional kick; longer = melodic "boom"/house kick); envelope decay/release shapes tail length; blending in pink (low-frequency) noise adds thickness without touching the pitched body; a sustain-capable envelope turns the same patch into a held, rhythmic tone. The same patch, softened (slower attack, duller filter, some noise, little pitch sweep), also produces a heartbeat sound. → [[Envelopes and ADSR]] — Anthony Marinelli Music, [[How To Make Any Imaginable Kick Drum Sound]]

- **Vary velocity and pan on individual kick hits, purely for bounce — no other processing mentioned.** On a Tory Lanez-style beat, extra "bounce" is added to the kick pattern simply by changing the velocity of individual hits and panning several of them, rather than adding new hits, swing, or a processing chain — a minimal-effort way to make an otherwise plain kick pattern feel more alive. Paired with a long, very subby 808 underneath. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Tory Lanez Underwater Synth Chain]] — loner, [how to make melodic r&b beats for tory lanez & chris brown (underwater synth/vocal sample)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JCup6FVW5o)
- **Borrow a kick from a specific companion track's outro for a sample-flip beat's drum character.** Programming drums for a *Used*/"Smoking on My Ex"-adjacent flip of Webster Lewis's "Open Up Your Eyes," loner reaches for the kick sound/character from "Forgiveness" — the outro track of SZA's *SOS* — pairing it with a heavy boom-bap-style snare, rather than sourcing a new kick from scratch. → [[Sound Selection Guide]] — loner, [the secrets behind sza's 'sos' production!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qbkFwpExtVQ)
- **Add deliberately hard, simple-spaced kicks under a softened R&B chop to restore low end an EQ pass removed.** Once a chord sample's low end has been EQ'd away, the beat "is crying out for" bass energy — answered with hard, punchy kicks on a simple, spaced-out pattern (not a busy one) rather than restoring low end on the sample itself. → [[EQ]], [[BASS Slot]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — loner, [these sampling secrets will instantly elevate your beats!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_97dAWKhLv0)
- **Arpeggiate a kick sample at Free-Rate, then copy the Rate curve onto a resonant HPF cutoff for a "clicky" character** — with [[Arpeggiator]] set to Rate Mode = Free on a kick-sample MIDI track, copy the drawn Rate automation curve and paste it onto a resonant high-pass filter's cutoff parameter (reshaping the pasted curve to taste), then pitch the sample up and tighten the amplitude envelope — together, a distinct "clicky" arpeggiated-kick texture built from correlated Rate/filter motion rather than a static tuned kick. *(general starting point — exact filter/pitch/envelope values not given in source)* → [[Arpeggiator]], [[Automation]], [[Rhythm and Groove]] — asd, [EXPONENTIAL RHYTHMS \[1/5\] Free Rate Arpeggiator](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WXzDesXWA3I)
- **Match a genre's drum-machine feel without pulling literal samples from it — thumpy kick + real acoustic hi-hats stand in for the [[Roland TR-909]]'s own kick+hat combination.** A Drake *Honestly, Nevermind*-style house remake: rather than pulling literal TR-909 samples (the machine that defined house's kick/hat sound in the 80s), reach for a thumpy kick + real acoustic hi-hat samples to hit the same feeling, then layer extra low-velocity notes onto a straight four-on-the-floor kick pattern for swing. → [[Roland TR-909]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[House - EDM|House / EDM]] — loner, [how to make authentic house beats for drake's 'honestly, nevermind'!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E63Q8-gXoMU)
- **A two-layer trap kick: a hard, aggressive trap kick underneath a lighter, softer top layer** — the kick half of *Rodeo*'s signature "modern trap meets old-school boom-bap" drum hybrid; the hard layer carries the low-end punch/aggression, the light top layer adds the click/definition on top rather than a single kick doing both jobs. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Layering and Stacking]] — loner, [the secrets behind mike dean's 'rodeo' production!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V9FhndW8t74)
- **A short kick on headphones can still ring far longer than intended on a real club system — factor in room reverberation of low frequencies, not just the sample's own length.** Origin story behind deadmau5's short-kick philosophy: a track's kick sounded fine at home but became an undifferentiated wall of ~30 Hz hum in a real club, because low-frequency waves propagate and reflect off room surfaces and physically exist far longer than the audio file's own decay — an 8th-note kick can effectively ring almost a full quarter note once room reverberation is factored in. Practical implication: err shorter than feels necessary on headphones, since the room adds length back. **Genre-level rule: a big kick OR a big bass sound, not both simultaneously** — house favors a prominent kick with modest bass; dubstep inverts it (overwhelming bass, kick doesn't need to carry much low end). **Psychoacoustic size trick:** drench a kick in a big hall reverb BEFORE the real bass enters (works best as the first sound in an intro) — the reverb-heavy kick primes the listener's sense of scale with no real sub-bass present yet, so when the actual bass lands later it reads as enormous by contrast even if the patch itself isn't extreme in isolation. → [[Bass Design]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Reverb]], [[Referencing and Translation]] — Steve Duda & deadmau5 (Sol State), [How Duda Changed Deadmau5 Kicks FOREVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z3rDduXys5I)
- **Decompose every kick into transient (click) / body (punch) / tail (sub decay) and diagnose which section actually needs fixing**, rather than judging "the kick" as one whole. Analytical findings from 9-10 Disclosure records: transients run dark/less clicky than typical EDM kicks; tails are extremely short (mostly an 8th note, some down to a 16th), leaving room for a busy bassline underneath; the envelope typically starts higher (~73 Hz) and pitch-falls to a lower sub note. **A concrete 3-layer recipe, each layer with one job:** a body layer for punch (sub content rolled off so it doesn't fight the sub layer), a dedicated sub layer for felt low-end weight, and a click/transient layer for top-end definition. **A downward pitch envelope on an 808 (e.g. in Battery) is a direct punch dial** — deeper envelope = more "pop," shallower = smoother. Starting point for from-scratch synthesis: a kick plugin (Big Kick), tuned to the song's key, with Attack and mostly Decay/length as the main per-song adjustments — shorter/punchier specifically when the bassline is busy. **Layering caution:** more layers isn't automatically better — careless layering causes phase cancellation; before adding a layer, check whether the kick needs a new layer or just a different base sound. → [[Bass Design]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Referencing and Translation]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — Disclosure (analyzed by Sol State), [Disclosure's Signature Kick](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N60Jcn9zfBo)
- **On a two-layer kick, separate the layers by frequency instead of actively phase-aligning them.** Rather than nudging/phase-checking a synth "low kick" against an acoustic "top kick," roll the lows OFF the acoustic top layer entirely and keep lows ONLY on the low layer — the two barely occupy the same frequency space to begin with, which minimizes how much any residual phase issue could matter even if one exists; then just listen and trust it's usually close enough to in-phase. → [[Phase Alignment]], [[Layering and Stacking]] — Disclosure, [Loudness The SMART Way (Don't Smash Your Drums)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xfqCnOYjRNY)
- **Two kicks per track, each doing a different job — punch and top end split across two separate layers**, with the punchy layer's length automated shorter/longer through the arrangement rather than staying fixed. Genre rule for which kick to reach for: a near-constant, unbroken bassline needs a very short, clicky, punchy kick (the sustained bass already owns the low end, a long kick just fights it); a straight four-on-the-floor house track without that competing sustain can support a longer kick. In UK garage specifically, the kick can sit almost entirely below 100 Hz and barely register as "hit" on its own — the bassline underneath is what delivers the actual chest-thump feeling, a genre-specific inversion of "kick owns the low end." → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Bass Design]] — Disclosure, [11 Best Music Tips from "In My Arms"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7pBkjzNT9eI)
- **Phase-align a kick and 808 instead of sidechaining or EQ-carving them apart** — nudge/crossfade the 808 sample against the kick until their waveforms line up and stop cancelling; technically the cleanest of six compared methods, but only realistic when the 808 sits on the same note/timing every time it hits (a normal arrangement with pitch changes rules it out). Rolling off the 808's low end where the kick lives, by contrast, sounded worse than every other method tested — it strips the kick's own fundamental. → [[Sidechaining]], [[Bass Design]] — Oliver (Sol State), [Oliver ENDS The "Sidechain Your 808" Debate](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ORENdwJV15Y)
- **Compress the kick's BODY while leaving the attack untouched, to widen the attack/body contrast for extra punch** — rather than compressing the whole kick evenly, target the sustain/body portion specifically so the transient reads comparatively louder and sharper against it; confirmed by ear progressively bringing the attack level up while the body stays squeezed. → [[Compression]] — Max Konyi, [House Music Tutorial in Studio One in 15 Minutes | Weekly Challenge #04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CIO3Z_a0gaA)
- **Add a Hold stage to a drum's amp envelope (~30–50 ms) before Decay begins** — so the decay curve only ever shapes the tail, never eats into the initial body/punch of the hit. A small, general drum-envelope-design habit worth applying to any synthesized kick. → [[Snare Design]] — Max Konyi, [Synthwave in Studio One with Stock Instruments | Weekly Challenge #9](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IA61_2JAd84)
- **Stop stacking processing chains on a kick that doesn't hit — swap the sample instead** — the habit to unlearn: piling on compressor after compressor, EQ after EQ, chasing a kick that "doesn't hit right"; the actual fix is almost always upstream — pull the sample out, audition a different one, and drop the processing back down to near-nothing. Reinforces [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]]'s "selection IS the recording step" — a chain fixes a close-but-not-quite sample, it doesn't rescue a wrong one. — You Suck at Producing, [5 Habits I Had To UNLEARN To Become A Better Music Producer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wTugkLmMSCM)
- **Build a kick from scratch using LFOs mapped as the envelopes, not the synth's normal ADSR** — starting from a blank [[Serum 2]] patch (Basic Shapes sine wavetable), map **LFO1 → Level** and hand-shape its curve for the amp envelope, and **LFO2 → Coarse Pitch**, forced **unidirectional** (Option+Shift+click) and curved downward, for the pitch-drop — LFOs give hand-drawable curve shapes the stock envelope stages don't. Push it further with hand-drawn wavetable harmonics + phase editing (phase = a per-harmonic delay, changes the tone independent of harmonic amplitude), a sample-sourced noise layer, and a full FX chain (Distortion → widener → second Distortion → filter Pre + high-pass → EQ8 → OTT → Multiband → a heavy distortion rack), finished by **resampling and chopping the result** for glitch variation. Full patch recipe on → [[Serum 2]] — You Suck at Producing, [5 Ways To Make Any Genre Sound 'Hyper'](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MFCiyKwWjeQ)
- **Trigger a maxed-resonance self-oscillating filter with a short impulse, then use an envelope on its Frequency to generate the whole kick body — no dedicated oscillator needed.** A self-resonating filter model starts ringing its own pure sine tone once Resonance is pushed to maximum and any brief signal passes through it — after that initial trigger the filter sustains the tone on its own, so the original input source becomes disposable. Route an envelope to the filter's **Frequency** knob and its decay becomes the kick's pitch-drop and body decay, functionally replacing a synth oscillator + pitch envelope with just a filter. → [[Auto Filter]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **To push a saturated kick louder without blowing up the master, scoop the ONE frequency causing the clip instead of just pulling the fader down.** Drive the kick's saturation/level as far as it'll go until it audibly clips, back off just to the edge, then resample/freeze that result and listen for the specific frequency doing the clipping — a narrow EQ cut right at that frequency (not a broad level pull-back) is what actually buys back headroom, because it removes the one offending band rather than taming the whole hit. → [[Saturator]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [How Habstrakt makes his SIGNATURE KICK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mlNUCjTmfTA)
- **Layer a real/acoustic kick sample under a synth kick purely for roundness, then reverb JUST that layer at a level where the reverb itself is inaudible.** Starting from a mid-scoop-plus-click-control synth kick, blend in a natural-sounding acoustic kick underneath specifically for roundness the synth layer lacks, then send an ambiance reverb onto that acoustic layer alone — the reverb isn't consciously audible in the mix, but it's what lets the kick pop through rather than sitting flat. Distinct from reverbing a kick for perceived scale (see the deadmau5 psychoacoustic-priming entry above) — here the reverb's job is presence/cut-through on a background blend layer, kept under the threshold of being heard as reverb. → [[Hybrid Reverb]] — Jauz (Sol State), [Tiny Details Most Producers Miss](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hv9r2rU-2qk)
- **Resolve a kick/bass frequency clash by deciding who owns the sub, not by EQing both** — pull up an analyzer-view EQ on each (Pro-Q 4's frequency-spectrum overlay) and play them together to find where each one's fundamental actually peaks (a real example: kick peaking ~55 Hz with a second bump ~120 Hz, bass also peaking ~55 Hz — direct collision); pick ONE to hold the sub (the source with more low-end content, if the kick is otherwise interchangeable) and high-pass the OTHER's low end up until the clash stops. If high-passing the kick guts its punch and leaves it a "one-trick pony," don't keep EQing it thinner — swap in a different kick sample with more inherent high-end/character *first*, then apply the same low-cut to that new sample. → [[Bass Design]], [[Masking and Carving]] — You Suck at Producing, [Mixing Kicks and Bass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MSyMma9M6AA)
- **Find boxy "cardboard" resonance by ear with a narrow-Q sweep, not by guessing a frequency** — put a high-Q EQ point on the kick, play it in the mix, and slowly sweep the frequency while listening for the boxy/cardboard-sounding resonance; once found, cut it (subtractive EQ) rather than boosting around it — the frequencies on either side of the cut read as *bigger* once the boxy peak is gone, which is the whole logic of subtractive EQ. A second narrow steep cut just below that same area can clean out leftover "gunk" the same way. → [[EQ]] — You Suck at Producing, [Making Kicks Hit Hard + Getting Paid with DistroKid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BKyhiAniCxs)
- **Sampler's ADSR as a transient shaper on a sampled kick** — set Loop to Trigger mode so a hit plays the full sample regardless of note length; instead of using Attack to fade in, turn up **Initial** for a plateau at the top of the attack, let **Sustain** act as transient emphasis, and enable **Decay** to shape the tail into a more percussive form. Limit mapped ranges (Initial ~0–200 ms, Decay ~50 ms–2 s) so macro sweeps stay musical. → [[Sampler]] — dnksaus, [the last drum kit you'll build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfOPVl0tzCE)
- **Retune and resample the same kick sample per track instead of sourcing new ones.** Keep one old kick drum, tune it differently each time, drop it into Sampler and resample the result; if it reads too low or high, drag in another pass and reshape the amplitude/pitch envelope rather than auditioning new samples. → [[Sampler]] — Habstrakt (Sol State), [Habstrakt Track Breakdown: "Infinite"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WonRpr4fCbo)
- **Pitch-envelope punch on kicks/808s, kept moderate** — a pitch envelope (Amount + Peak mapped to a macro, plus Decay) adds extra transient "knock" on top of a sample's own transient — Amount ~48 with Peak limited to a narrow range (~0–33 rather than the full 0–100, which read as too extreme) gives subtle additional punch without turning the kick into an obvious pitch-sweep effect. → [[Sampler]] — dnksaus, [the last drum kit you'll build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfOPVl0tzCE)
- **Transpose range for kick variation: roughly -5 to +7** — mapping a sampled kick's Transpose to a macro refreshes it into many different-feeling hits, but kicks tolerate a much narrower range than other drums (snares can go -12 to +24) before the transposed result stops reading as a kick. → [[Sampler]] — dnksaus, [the last drum kit you'll build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfOPVl0tzCE)
- **Drum Bus Transient/Damp as a quick post-reverb snap control** — pushed up, Drum Bus's Transient knob emphasizes the initial hit (harsh fast); pulled down, it shortens the decay — useful specifically *after* a reverb send since it transient-shapes the reverb tail too, not just the dry hit; Damp then rolls off any extra brightness a Transpose move introduced. → [[Drum Bus]] — dnksaus, [the last drum kit you'll build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfOPVl0tzCE)
- **Split the kick into body + transient layers, then raise *only* the transient late in the song** — with the transient on its own track you keep the kick poking through a dense outro without adding low-end energy. Ian's core kick habit (body + sub + a high-passed room "roomie" = one kick). → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Make Your Kick Stand Out](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GqBnRpNiHrI) & [New Rules (25 Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iHSmH57_wY8)
- **High-pass the *room* kick layer since the tight kick already has the sub** — "one kick, but roomier" without doubling the lows. → [[SUB Slot]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Pretty Please (24 Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j5aA59B_T58)
- **Brick-wall EQ (~41 Hz) on a duplicate kick adds a harmonic** — a split-kick trick for extra low-end character. → [[EQ]], [[Pro-Q 4]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Bad Liar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WrJWYpehZLY)
- **A muffled four-on-the-floor kick that sits in the gaps** — Tristan Salvati's move on "Fever": a muffled kick on the four gives four-on-the-floor "without paying full price frequency-wise." → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Fever Stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mc3foPZpyaw)
- **Torque for kick thump/harmonics** — tracks a frequency and adds a harmonic while keeping the original, "adds a lot of thump." → [[Saturation and Distortion]] — Ian Kirkpatrick, [Livestream 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DQ-ZeEyLKXY)
- **The "car EQ" curve** — shelf the lower-highs down + filter only the extreme top (a full low-pass = "kick under a pillow," attack gone); ~1 kHz dip for boxy distorted kicks; narrow/mono a too-stereo kick sample; trim the low-frequency tail short enough to punch but not so short you lose the lows — Lophiile, [All About Basslines stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tnruvmw_OWc) & [Make Your Drums Slap](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v4ZhwlhF5yE)
- **Tune 808s up high, then drop them back** — pitch the 808 up an octave-plus to actually hear the note center, tune it, return it to the sub register; never tune a sub by guessing at 40 Hz. Slides: pitch the audio 808 to the note with warp OFF first, then warp mode Tones + clip transposition envelope for drawn slides — Lophiile, [All About Basslines stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tnruvmw_OWc)
- **Kick EQ starting points: cut the 500–700 Hz "knock-on-wood" boxiness, boost the top-end click, add ~40 Hz sub.** The 500–700 Hz box usually needs to come down; lift the click so the kick reads in a busy mix; a little 40 Hz for weight. Don't gut *all* of 100 Hz (it's a good part of the kick), and **never make the kick/sub stereo** ("a crazy move"). → [[Width and Stereo]] — Disclosure, [Holding On](https://youtu.be/8H8U-WjvdVc)
- **808 kick + a *pitch envelope* (in Battery) to add the punch** — put a pitch envelope on an 808 to bring out (or cut) the ~100 Hz "pop," turning a static 808 into a tunable synth kick. Layer as click + crackle + big boom, each EQ'd to its role. → [[Roland TR-808]] — Disclosure, [Ultimatum](https://youtu.be/uWe3UvI4KlU)
- **In a very low key, tune the kick to a *fifth above* the root and bend it** — a C-minor song's C sub-kick is too low (speakers flap, inaudible outside a club), so pitch the kick to G (or lower) and make it *bend* (boom-boom). Always tune the kick to the key — a kick on the wrong note fights the song. → [[SUB Slot]] — Disclosure, [Birthday](https://youtu.be/b-6x_4XRwOQ)
- **Automate kick *length* by section** — long kick in the sub-less intro/verse, short kick in the chorus where the sub enters; an easy kick-vs-sub anti-clash move. — Disclosure, [Birthday](https://youtu.be/b-6x_4XRwOQ)
- **Short-and-punchy so the sub owns the low end** — the "Big Kick" is only ~120 ms with release automation (~40 ms then it opens) — a *pop*, not a subby EDM kick — precisely because "there's so much sub in this tune." Leave the room for the bass. → [[SUB Slot]] — Disclosure, [Never Enough](https://youtu.be/lGopQwQo9Ag)
- **Two-kick stack: thump + click** — the big kick provides the thump; a second acoustic/clicky kick on top (copy-pasted, matched MIDI) provides the attack detail — Disclosure, [Never Enough](https://youtu.be/lGopQwQo9Ag)
- **Synth-note kick tuned to the key** — the whole low end is a synthesized kick; tune the fundamental to the root so the kick establishes an ambiguous key — Disclosure, [How We Made Latch](https://youtu.be/u-0Q41XB7U4)
- **All-knock, no-sub kick** — ~100 Hz classic Disclosure kick over a bendy sub loop so the kick + loop are "full" with no separate bassline — Disclosure, [NO CAP](https://youtu.be/kz5dmb3QdI8)
- **Learned kick-shaping on Ultrabeat** — envelope-shape a stock kick synth; Big Kick / Kick 2 do the same job now — Disclosure, [You & Me](https://youtu.be/9zsU5Y8r_Ow)
- **Push kick saturation right to the edge of clipping, then back off one notch (Abstract)** — dial saturation on the kick up toward audible breakup to add air/harmonics to the transient, then pull back the smallest amount from the point it clearly becomes too saturated — keeps the added harmonic content without losing the transient. → [[Saturation and Distortion]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Kicks make kicks, bass makes bass — don't tune the kick to the bassline.** deadmau5 pushes back on the "tune your kick to the bass" EDM pro-tip: a kick is fundamentally a transient/punchy element, not a bass instrument — reach for a sub oscillator or a dedicated bass patch for low-end duty instead of trying to make the kick carry it. A kick CAN function as the track's fundamental low frequency that the arrangement "spins around," but that "very very seldomly works" because it forces writing the whole track's melody/harmony around that one fixed root note — a constraint most tracks can't accept. Practical fix for a kick swallowing a mix (a reviewed track read as pure 30 Hz mud with nothing else audible on a real club system): shorten the decay to roughly an eighth note and optionally carve some low end down, then handle the actual bass separately with a low synth plus a small EQ lift around 30-80 Hz. Sits in direct tension with Disclosure's "always tune the kick to the key" habit elsewhere in this note — a real philosophical split between artists on how much low-end/pitch duty a kick should carry. → [[Bass Design]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]] — deadmau5 (Sol State), [Deadmau5 RANTS about Kicks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5Bzu8xNp5X4)

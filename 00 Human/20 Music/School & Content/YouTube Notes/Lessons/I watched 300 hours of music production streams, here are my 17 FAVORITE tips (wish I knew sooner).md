## Synopsis

A rapid-fire compilation of 17 production tips pulled from streams and interviews with Skrillex, Ian Kirkpatrick, Lophiile, Lax City, Oliver, Cashmere, Morgan Page, Jauz, Zedd, and Abstract (captions render several of these names phonetically — corrected against known producers where the match is high-confidence). The tips span melody writing, layering, bass lines, mixing philosophy, workflow/discipline, EQ, drum design, and the business of finishing tracks. Several segments overlap tips already documented in full elsewhere in this vault from the same creators (Ian Kirkpatrick's "singable phrases" and "violate space," Ian's "zone on demand," and Lophiile's octaves-plus-passing-fifth bass line) — those are cross-referenced rather than duplicated below. This page is the master record for the tips in this compilation that aren't already logged in depth.

## Techniques

- **Sing the melody to test if it's memorable (Skrillex)** — if you can't sing your own melody, don't expect listeners to remember it either; singing it out loud also forces you to feel the music more deeply while writing, versus just programming notes. → [[Melody Writing]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Make the whole musical phrase singable, not just the melody (Ian Kirkpatrick)** — a listener never perceives layer count, only the resulting outline, so a huge stack of layers can still read as one simple, memorable "phrase" if it resolves down to something hummable hours later. Full technique + Ian's restraint/Bad Liar framing already logged → [[This ONE Question Will Improve Every Song You Make]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Reach for a counter-melody before rewriting a melody that isn't working (Skrillex)** — when a lead melody won't come together, the fix often isn't changing that melody but adding a second, different melodic line underneath it; sometimes a counter-melody is what makes an otherwise-nonsensical opening idea click into "a unit." → [[Melody Writing]], [[Layering and Stacking]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Give each layer its own MIDI part — "accent layers" for subtle life instead of a new melody** — don't stack every layer on the same MIDI pattern; different layers playing different things (a chord layer, a wobble arp, etc.) build richer texture than doubled unison parts. When a melody already works and you don't want a full counter-melody, add a smaller "accent layer" — something between a counter-melody and a new melody — to subtly bring life to what's already there. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Build bass lines from octaves plus an occasional passing fifth (Lophiile)** — jump the octave up and back down, and land a passing note (often a fifth) on top of the chord in between; a whole usable bassline can be "mainly octaves with the occasional tiny fill." Already logged in full from Lophiile's own stream → [[Lophiile - All About Basslines (Twitch Stream)]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **"Violate space" — establish a reverb tail then abruptly cut it off (Ian Kirkpatrick)** — set up a room/reverb, then slam it off so the song "feels like it stops for a second"; described as one of his favorite production moves (his joke: only in productions, not real life). Full technique + Pretty Please chain example already logged → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - Pretty Please (24 Big Tips)]], [[Depth and Space]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Deliberately break your DAW or a plugin to escape overthinking (Lax City)** — when a hook or melody isn't happening, tweak a parameter that isn't "supposed to be touched" (his example: pushing a master pitch control outside its intended range); everything in the DAW can be recorded, so a broken or misused plugin setting can produce sounds you'd never reach by working normally. Forces creating in the moment instead of overthinking. — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Judge a mix move by the feeling it produces, not the technical parameter itself (Oliver)** — ask "is this EQ move / this reverb giving me the feeling I want," not whether a meter or setting looks correct; tips and tools are just a means to a feeling, and the real stop condition is "does it feel right, am I bobbing my head" — not a technical checklist. — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Get into "the zone" on demand as a trainable, disciplined skill, not luck (Ian Kirkpatrick)** — pros show up and put in the hours whether or not inspiration is there; getting in the zone still requires finding a vision/theme for the song first (his example: spending most of a full session day just hunting for the theme of a duet before writing a note), but showing up on schedule regardless is what separates pros from amateurs — "introducing discipline into my creativity" without taming the creativity itself. Fuller version of this tip (vision-beats-habit framing) already logged → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - Ear Candy Adventures]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Say no to writer's block by working through it — most of your output goes unheard (Cashmere)** — even top artists generate far more material than what ships (his example: Daft Punk released only a few hours of music across a 30-year career); the fix for writer's block isn't waiting for inspiration, it's writing something good — crossing that finish line once puts you "back in the mode" to do it again. — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Watch for "becoming blind" — overworking a session can actively undo progress (Morgan Page)** — long sessions (12-hour days) don't produce better work than shorter ones; most people only get 3-4 genuinely productive musical hours in a day, and pushing past that risks "working backwards" in a mix without noticing. Concrete fix: take short 10-20 minute breaks to reset your ears, and stop once you're forcing progress rather than making it. → [[Referencing and Translation]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **The "7k trick" — cut synths/other elements around 7-7.5kHz to give drum transients room (Jauz)** — instead of only carving low end between kick and bass, high-pass or notch synths around 7-7.5kHz so hats and drum high-end have uncontested space up top; the synths lose a bit of crispness but stop fighting the drums for the same band, and the whole mix reads dramatically cleaner and louder without touching the drums at all. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[EQ]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Ask "does this snare slap?" as a blunt gut-check, played at full volume (Zedd)** — for genres that want a big snare, play the snare alone at full volume and force a direct yes/no answer instead of second-guessing it through EQ/compression tweaks. → [[Snare Design]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Close your eyes to judge a vocal's proximity and placement, not just its processing (Zedd)** — closing your eyes removes the visual bias of plugin GUIs and DAW track colors so you focus purely on where the vocal actually sits in the mix; the goal is a vocal that feels like it's right in front of you, not distant — a useful check whenever a vocal "sounds far away" even though nothing obvious is wrong on paper. → [[Depth and Space]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Push kick saturation right to the edge of clipping, then back off one notch (Abstract)** — dial saturation on the kick up toward audible breakup to add air/harmonics to the transient, then pull back the smallest amount from the point where it clearly becomes too saturated — described as "pushing it to the edge of clipping" and backing off just enough to keep the added harmonic content without losing the transient. → [[Kick Design]], [[Saturation and Distortion]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Produce for one dedicated vocalist as a fast-track learning exercise (Cashmere)** — working repeatedly with the same singer (his example: co-writing/producing several songs together with vocalist Dev) forces you out of working alone and teaches you to build songs around someone else's voice instead of your own instincts; recommends producing 5-6 songs for one vocalist specifically as a way to quickly learn to simplify and serve a vocal. — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)
- **Decide in advance that you will outsource mastering, so you actually finish mixes (Abstract)** — pre-commit to handing mastering to someone else, which removes the temptation to endlessly reopen a "finished" mix (switching headphones, tweaking forever, never moving on); do the mixdown yourself as well as you can, apply a limiter to bring reference-level loudness, then hand off and don't touch the project again. → [[Mix Handoff]] — Sol State, [I watched 300 hours of music production streams, here are my 17 FAVORITE tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQHvtinwQQ8)

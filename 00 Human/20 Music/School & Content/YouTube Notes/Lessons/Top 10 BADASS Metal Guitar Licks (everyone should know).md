## Synopsis

Ten metal/shred licks, each doubling as a named technique exercise — Phrygian dominant string transitions, a tapping harmonic, the "Dopamine lick" (finger-rolling + subdivision switching), the augmented scale with a downstroke-escape motion, playing-in-3rds, extended stretch pentatonic with irregular rhythmic grouping, deliberate semitone-clash dissonance, "modular picking," a 2-string economy trick, major-arpeggio sweeping extended by tapping both ends, and multi-finger (middle+ring) tapping.

## Techniques

1. **Phrygian dominant string-transition workout.** Down/up picking pattern that alternates a downstroke on the high string with an upstroke on the adjacent lower string, then reverses (upstroke-to-downstroke) when the pattern repeats — a genuine string-transition drill disguised as a lick.
2. **Tapping harmonic.** At the end of a legato run switching 16th-note triplets into 32nd notes, the tapping finger *lightly touches* the string exactly one octave above the fretted note instead of actually tapping down — produces a harmonic rather than a fretted pitch. Distinct technique from a normal tapped note.
3. **Chord-outlining arpeggio phrasing over a changing progression** — a third confirming worked example of choosing arpeggio notes per chord rather than running one scale through the whole progression, so the chord changes are audible in the solo alone.
4. **The "Dopamine lick"** (a frequently-requested, difficult passage from the artist's own song). Requires **rolling the ring finger to bar the D and G strings** for an F minor arpeggio, specifically to keep the two barred notes cleanly separated rather than blurring together. Also switches between 32nd notes and 16th-note triplets with only two 16th notes of lead-in before the triplet tapping burst starts — a real rushing/dragging risk at the switch point. Ends in a stretch-pentatonic tapped run (see [[Stretch Pentatonic Shapes (3-Notes-Per-String Extension)]]).
5. **The augmented scale as an underused dissonant alternative to diminished.** Named directly: rock/metal players default to the diminished scale/arpeggio for dissonance and rarely reach for the augmented scale, despite it being an equally strong, distinct-sounding option. Paired with the **downstroke-escape picking angle** — one consistent angle across the whole lick, chosen so the pick escapes the strings after a downstroke (as opposed to escaping after an upstroke) — described as more useful here than the alternative escape angle for this specific lick's shape.
6. **B harmonic minor "playing in 3rds"** — a classic metal sequencing cliché (skip one scale note, i.e. play scale degrees a 3rd apart in sequence) moved through several fretboard positions, closed out with a 16th-note-triplet run using the same downstroke-escape motion as trick 5.
7. **Extended stretch pentatonic (3-notes-per-string) with a deliberate irregular rhythmic grouping** (repeating pattern of groups like 3-2-3-2-1-2-3...) rather than a straight run — confirms the existing vault system for stretch pentatonic shapes and adds a rhythmic-interest layer on top of it. Followed by a sweep-picked arpeggio tail adding the major 9th (F♯) as a color tone outside the plain pentatonic, requiring an index-finger bar plus picking-hand palm-muting to control unwanted harmonics.
8. **Deliberate semitone-clash dissonance via letting two adjacent notes overlap and ring together** (worked example: E and F, a minor 2nd apart) rather than picking them cleanly separated — the overlap itself, not just the interval choice, is what produces the effect.
9. **"Modular picking"** — a named hybrid technique combining sweep picking, economy picking, and chord-strumming motion: a fixed rhythmic picking pattern (three downstrokes, then three upstrokes, repeating) executed while the fretting hand *deliberately* lets notes overlap rather than damping them, producing a dense, complex texture. Cited as used in the artist's own recordings ("Dopamine"'s chord riff, a section of "The Kindling").
10. **2-string economy-picking trick for neoclassical arpeggio licks** — replace alternate picking's back-and-forth with a smooth, connected downward (sweeping/economy) motion plus fretting-hand legato for most notes; confirms the same 2-string economy device already documented from a separate video (see [[Sweep Picking Arpeggio Shapes]]).
11. **Major-arpeggio sweep picking, flagged as commonly neglected in favor of minor arpeggios** despite being needed constantly in practice (even minor keys contain major chords). Bar three strings with the middle finger, keep notes cleanly separated (picking-hand palm-muting is fine to help control unwanted harmonics), and — the genuinely fresh idea — **extend the arpeggio with tapped notes on *both* the highest and the lowest string**, not just the more common top-extension-only approach.
12. **Multi-finger tapping using the middle *and* ring finger** (not just the middle finger alone) while the pick stays anchored between thumb and index finger. Worked example: middle finger taps E (12th fret), ring finger taps F (13th fret), then pulls off back to E — enabling up to 5 notes on one string, extending arpeggio range beyond the fretting hand's own stretch limits. Closes with a simpler two-finger tapped interval (G and B on adjacent strings), again requiring careful separation to avoid unwanted overlap.

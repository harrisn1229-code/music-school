# 2 String Shredding Trick - Economy Picking VS Alternate Picking

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LXNR7FyfnzE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LXNR7FyfnzE)

## Synopsis

A direct alternate-picking-vs-economy-picking comparison on the same 2-string lick over a D minor cadence with a harmonic-minor-flavored V chord, showing how reducing to two downstrokes plus legato removes the hardest part of a 2-string phrase (the string transition) without changing a single note.

## Techniques

- **The 2-string economy trick: reduce to one downstroke per string, replace every other picked note with a hammer-on or pull-off.** Same exact notes and phrasing as the alternate-picked version, but the picking hand only executes two downstrokes total (one per string) instead of alternating through the whole phrase — eliminates the challenging string-transition moment entirely rather than trying to execute it faster. Start the pattern with an upstroke specifically to land in position for the following downstrokes.
- **Diagnosing the alternate-picking version first, then engineering a workaround rather than "just practicing it more."** The straight alternate-picked version (described as gallop-rhythm-like, extended across 2 strings) has good downstroke placement for timing control, but the constant string-crossing gets messy at high tempo — explicitly framed as "work smart, not hard": look for a picking-pattern change that removes the difficulty structurally before assuming more repetition is the answer.
- **Cadence: D minor → C major → Bb (power chord, perfect 5th on bottom) → A major**, both D minor and C major voiced with an open, non-standard color (major 2nd swapped in for the usual minor 3rd). **The A major (not A minor) ending is the harmonic-minor move:** the natural fifth-scale-degree chord in D minor is A minor, but raising the 7th scale degree (C→C♯) turns it into A major, producing a more resolved/dramatic, slightly exotic cadence than the plain A minor would — a direct instance of harmonic minor's classic V-chord major substitution, demonstrated A/B against the plain-A-minor version for comparison. Doesn't imply the whole piece is in harmonic minor — just this one cadence point borrows the color, which is described as the more common real-world usage than committing a full passage to the mode.
- **Chord-tone soloing reinforcement:** the lead line plays root/3rd/5th of each chord plus other scale tones, specifically so the guitar solo alone implies the chord changes — demonstrated by contrasting it against a version that doesn't touch the chord tones and audibly loses that implied harmony.

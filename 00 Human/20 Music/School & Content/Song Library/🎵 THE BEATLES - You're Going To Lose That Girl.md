# You're Going To Lose That Girl — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4 bars — call it phrase **A**; it is also the verse's third phrase and the title hook, so it works as intro and mini-refrain at once) · Verse (12, three even phrases as **BBA**) · Verse′ (**14**, extended by a two-bar half phrase that pivots into the bridge) · Bridge (**7 bars**, two parallel but unequal phrases) · Verse′ (guitar solo) · Bridge · Verse′ · Outro. Run the song from the top through verse 2 and the phrase order is a palindrome.

The series' showcase for **pivot modulation into a key that shares no chords with home**. E major goes to G major for the bridge — a genuine interlude, not a flirtation — and both the trip out and the trip back are engineered on borrowed chords ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]). G is reachable only as the relative major of the parallel minor ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]).

Going out, the ♭VII is re-heard as the dominant of ♭III ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]) — grammatically clean, still a surprise. Coming back, G's own ♭VII is re-read as **♭II of E**, the Neapolitan, dropping straight onto I in root position with no dominant in between. The outro then closes a song built almost entirely on I→V motion with a **double plagal cadence** ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]).

Shortening the bridge by one bar exactly where the harmonic trick happens is what throws weight onto it; squaring it up would make it tidier and duller.

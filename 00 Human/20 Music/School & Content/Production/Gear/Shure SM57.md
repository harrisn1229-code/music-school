# Shure SM57

**Category:** Microphone (dynamic, cardioid)
**Vendor:** Shure
**Ballpark value (used, 2026):** $70–90

## What it is

The most-used instrument microphone ever made — cardioid dynamic with a midrange presence peak, effectively indestructible, and the reference sound for snare and guitar cab.

## What it's used for

Guitar amps and snare first; also horns, percussion, gritty character vocals, and anything loud.

## Signal chain notes

On the [[Fender Blues Junior]] grille; needs real preamp gain (dynamic, no phantom required).

## Manual

- 📄 [[Shure SM57.pdf|Local PDF]] · [Official user guide](https://pubs.shure.com/view/guide/SM57/en-US)

**High-level points:**
- The sound **is** the frequency response: presence bump ~5–6 kHz, rolled-off lows and top — sources come back "pre-mixed" toward the midrange.
- **Placement is the EQ.** On a cab, an inch of movement across the speaker cone changes tone more than any plugin.
- Handles absurd SPL — there is no amp too loud for it.
- Strong proximity effect: right on the grille = thick lows; a hand-width back = leaner and roomier.
- No pad, no switches, no phantom needed; it works forever.

## Key Parameters

*(No onboard controls — placement variables are the parameters.)*

| # | Parameter | What it is / does | Technique | Artist / record example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ① | Cone position (cap vs edge) | Dust cap = bright/aggressive; cone edge = darker/warmer | Start at the dust-cap edge, angle 20–45°; slide toward the edge to darken | The SM57-on-cab sound is on essentially every classic rock/indie guitar record |
| ② | Distance from grille | Proximity bass + room ratio | 0–1" tight and dry (standard); 4–6" opens the amp's "size" | — |
| ③ | Snare placement | Over the rim, aimed at center | 1–2" above the head, watch the hi-hat bleed angle | The standard snare mic on decades of records |
| ④ | Angle (on/off axis) | Softens the presence peak | Angle off-axis to shave harshness on bright amps before reaching for EQ | — |
| ⑤ | Pairing with a condenser | Blend dark dynamic + airy condenser | 57 + [[Neumann TLM 102]] on the [[Fender Blues Junior]], phase-check, blend to taste | Classic two-mic cab technique (watch polarity) |
| ⑤b | Pairing with a ribbon (not owned) | Same blend logic with a ribbon standing in for the condenser's air role | 57 (mid-rangey, cuts through) + [[Royer 121]] (warmer, fuller), positioned by ear — an inch or two off the grille, slightly off-center of the cone | Jack Ruch's home rig — [My Home Recording Setup for Guitar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KYTGFneEJ2A) |
| ⑥ | Preamp gain | Dynamics need ~50–60 dB for quiet sources | On quiet fingerpicking prefer the TLM 102; the 57 shines loud | — |

# I Need You — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

George's second writing credit, read here as a folk-modal tune wearing pop-rock clothes. The structural hook is a negative one: **the V chord is withheld from the entire verse** and doesn't appear until the bridge — and the melody's missing fourth degree arrives at exactly the same moment ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]).

**Form.** Two-bridge model with a single verse between the bridges. The verse is an odd **14 bars** in four phrases, the fourth running half as long as the first three; the asymmetry reads as free verse rather than as a mistake. The bridge is **9 bars** of two unequal phrases and stretches exactly where the verse compressed. The 8-bar coda grows out of the final verse, starting where that truncated fourth phrase would otherwise sit.

**Harmony.** Seven chords, nothing more exotic than V-of-V. The verse establishes home by indirect routes — IV→I, and the even weaker stepwise ii→I — rather than by dominant motion. The bridge is the only place the key is pinned down, and it does it with V and V-of-V back to back: the reverse of the usual layout, where the verses hammer the tonic and the bridge wanders off. The coda is the I–vi–IV cliché **with the V deleted**, consistent with a song that treats the dominant as a rationed resource.

**Melody.** The verse tune is pentatonic ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]) with one exception, a ♭7 at the end of the second phrase — and that single out-of-scale note carries the whole modal impression. Appoggiaturas are dense, and several are left dangling at phrase ends, hanging unresolved against the tonic chord ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]).

**Arrangement.** Acoustic rhythm guitar against an electric pedal-tone guitar that reads almost as a keyboard. A turn figure introduced in the two-bar intro returns at the end of three of the four verse phrases, overlapping the vocal's last two notes — and its second half is what resolves those hanging vocal appoggiaturas. Real tension, no loose end.

# VOI-C09 — Comping is rhythm before it is harmony

**Q.** What is a comping part judged on first, what practice order follows from that, and what is the commonest failure?

> [!question]- Answer
> **The rhythm, not the notes.** So drill the rhythm **alone on one static shell voicing**, metronome on 2 and 4, until it is automatic — then move it through real changes with real voicings. The commonest failure is **playing too much**: chords on 2 and 4, or one per bar, nearly always sit better than constant strumming.

## Why it matters

Five rhythms cover most situations — **Charleston** (beat 1 and the "and" of 2), **push** (a sustained hit on the "and" of 3 that pulls into the next bar), **downbeat-anchored** (unobtrusive, safe behind a soloist), **off-the-one** (the "and" of 1 and beat 3), and **response** (a reply to a phrase the soloist just finished). Separating rhythm from harmony is also what makes new voicings usable: changing only the top note of a shape you own doubles what you can do with it.

## Drill

15 min: one shell voicing, metronome on 2 and 4, one minute per rhythm — Charleston, push, downbeats, off-the-one, response. Then run the best one through a ii-V-I.

# Finger Exercises You Should Do Every Day! - Beginner & Advanced Workout

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k3kZ4TpupQI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k3kZ4TpupQI)

## Synopsis

Two fretting-hand exercises spanning a full difficulty range: a basic "semi-chromatic scale" no-lift finger-angle drill the presenter says he under-practiced as a beginner because nobody explained *why* it mattered, and an advanced two-shape sweep-picked position-shifting exercise that further refines the same fretting angle at speed.

## Techniques

- **Basic exercise — semi-chromatic scale, frets 5-8 on every string, alternate picked, 4 notes per string, with a no-early-lift rule.** Fingers stay pressed down on the fretboard until they're physically needed again on the next string — don't lift preemptively. This forces a genuinely good perpendicular fretting angle, because a poor angle causes the resting fingers to accidentally block/mute the next string's note.
- **Built-in audio self-check.** With the correct angle, adjacent notes can briefly overlap/ring together without dissonant blocking; the wrong angle instead produces an audible clash or a muted note — practice specifically listening for that tension as the mistake signal, not just watching the hand.
- **Recommended dose: 5–15 minutes daily** — framed as one of the single most important fretting-hand exercises precisely because good left-hand angle underlies every other fretting-hand technique (clean hammer-ons/pull-offs, stretch, speed).
- **Advanced exercise — two repeating one-note-per-string shapes, moved across neck positions, played with sweep picking (some pull-offs).** Deliberately not meant to sound musical — pure finger-and-brain workout. Reinforces the same fretting-angle discipline as the basic exercise but under speed, since the fast tricky inversions make it nearly impossible to succeed with a flying pinky or a bad angle; also sharpens pull-off snap at high tempo specifically. Could alternately be played with straight alternate picking instead of sweep picking (removing the pull-offs) — noted as a variation the presenter wants to explore further, not yet demonstrated.

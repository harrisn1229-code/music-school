# Because — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Three-part vocal harmony overdubbed three times over arpeggiated electric harpsichord, and a home key that never quite settles. Pollack's structural point is the two-bar **mini bridge**: it keeps steering toward D major — a ♭II Neapolitan behaving like a new tonic — and then a diminished seventh melts it back to i. The song establishes C♯ minor by ordinary i–ii–V–i means over and over precisely because that interlude keeps undermining it ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]).

**Form.** Underneath, an ordinary verse–verse–bridge–verse, blurred by the recurring mini bridge. The full bridge is built as an extension of the mini bridge. The outro looks on paper like a verse plus a mini bridge, but the vocals go wordless throughout, which binds the *preceding* mini bridge to the outro into a symmetrical ABA — inverting the separation the song has drilled every previous time.

**Harmony.** Stock is i, half-diminished ii, V and VI, plus **IV in major form borrowed from melodic minor** ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]], [[SCL-C02 — Harmonic and melodic minor]]). The demonstration piece is the pair of diminished sevenths: enharmonically the same four pitches, sounding identical, but the voice leading roots them differently so one melts back to i and the other functions as vii-of-IV. Same chord, two functions, one song.

**Arrangement.** A layered intro — harpsichord alone, then guitar doubling it plucked near the bridge so the attacks match, then voices and bass, then a brass-voiced Moog. The arpeggio figuration groups **3 + 3 + 2**. The track ends on the diminished seventh, unresolved: since that chord has resolved to i every previous time, verse-plus-mini-bridge is a loop with no exit and breaking it is the only available ending.

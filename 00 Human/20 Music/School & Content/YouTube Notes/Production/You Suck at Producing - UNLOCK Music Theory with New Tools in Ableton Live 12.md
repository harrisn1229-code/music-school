# UNLOCK Music Theory with New Tools in Ableton Live 12

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Br2a6XxMZ5M)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Br2a6XxMZ5M)

## Synopsis
A "Prance Connors" character-lecture walkthrough of the music-theory-adjacent tools new in Ableton Live 12: the top-of-screen Scale selector, the MIDI Transform menu (specifically Arpeggiate → Connect), the Chord device's scale-aware shift mode, and the piano roll's MIDI Generate tab (Seed/Notes, Stacks, Shape). The through-line is that Live 12 now lets someone with minimal theory knowledge build a melody, turn it into a chord progression, derive a bassline from the roots, and generate a second contrasting section — all without hand-calculating scale degrees, because the tools stay locked to whatever scale is set globally. Most valuable for anyone who wants to lean on Ableton's scale-awareness instead of mental chord math.

## Techniques
- **Global Scale selector (top of Live) drives every downstream tool** — turn it on/off, set the root (e.g. F) and scale (e.g. major/minor), and the change mirrors into every MIDI clip's piano roll automatically; right-click it to choose whether accidentals display as sharps, flats, or both. → [[Scale]]
- **Turn a melody into a chord progression with Add Interval** — select all notes in a melody clip and hit **Add Interval** to duplicate every note and shift the copy up/down; traditional chord tones are spaced two scale degrees apart, so hitting Add Interval twice (spaced 2 scale-degrees each time) stacks a full triad on top of the original melody line. → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Scale]]
- **Derive a bassline from a chord clip** — copy the chord clip onto a bass track, then delete every note except the root of each chord, giving an instant root-motion bassline that's guaranteed to match the harmony. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **MIDI Transform → Arpeggiate → Connect fills gaps between existing notes** — Spread controls how far the generated notes can sit from the originals, Density controls how many get generated, Tie glues some of them into longer notes, and Rate sets their speed; used here to turn a plain block-chord bassline into a funkier moving line. → [[MIDI Generate and Transform Tools]]
- **Changing the Scale doesn't auto-move already-written notes — Fit to Scale does** — after switching the global scale (e.g. F major → F minor), each clip still needs its own **Fit to Scale** hit to push its notes into the new scale; every clip (chords, bass, melody) has to be fit individually, they don't cascade. → [[Scale]]
- **MIDI Generate → Seed (Live 12.0-era name for what later became the Notes generator) — pitch-range-constrained melody generation** — set Voices to 1 for a monophonic line, dial down Density, set a pitch range (e.g. F2–F3) to keep it inside a useful register, and adjust note Duration; a fast way to sketch a new melodic idea inside the current key without playing anything in. → [[MIDI Generate and Transform Tools]]
- **The Chord device's scale-aware toggle turns semitone shifts into scale-degree shifts** — with scale-awareness off, the device's chord-tone shift knobs move in plain semitones (so a "minor 3rd" shift stays a minor 3rd even if the underlying scale changes to major, which can produce out-of-key chords); switching scale-awareness on re-maps those same shift controls to scale degrees instead, so a "+2" now always lands on the correct diatonic chord tone (major or minor 3rd, whichever the current scale calls for) as the scale changes underneath it. → [[Chord]]
- **Chord device Learn mode captures a whole voicing, then that voicing is transposable everywhere** — play a custom chord in and hit Learn; the device stores both the notes and the shift controls needed to reproduce it, so the exact voicing can then be moved up/down across the whole keyboard. → [[Chord]]
- **MIDI Generate → Stacks builds a full chord progression from scratch inside the piano roll** — pick a generator length (e.g. 4 bars), hit the **+** button repeatedly to add multiple chord instances, and for each one browse alternate voicings with the up-arrow; Ableton labels each generated chord with its name, notes, and scale degrees underneath, so there's no guessing what got generated. Chords can be individually resized/offset (overlapping slightly) for a more legato, less blocky feel. → [[MIDI Generate and Transform Tools]], [[Chord Progressions]]
- **MIDI Transform → Shape draws a melody by hand-drawing its contour** — switch the transform to Shape and literally draw the up/down contour of the desired melodic line directly in the piano roll, then dial Density down, tie notes together, and adjust the Range (highest/lowest note) and Rate to taste. → [[MIDI Generate and Transform Tools]]

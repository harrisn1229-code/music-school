# How To Make Any Imaginable Kick Drum Sound

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u7-40sDPxQI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u7-40sDPxQI)

## Synopsis

A short, focused synth-kick tutorial on the ARP 2600 (chosen for visual patch clarity, but applicable on any synth capable of modulating oscillator pitch with an envelope): a single core patch — oscillator → filter (closed, modulated by envelope) → VCA (same envelope) → mixer — that covers the entire range from a tight, dull "house" kick to a boomy car-bass kick to a heartbeat, purely by varying pitch-sweep depth, envelope decay/release, and how much low-frequency noise is blended in for thickness.

## Techniques

- **Core patch**: triangle or sine oscillator → envelope generator modulates BOTH the oscillator's pitch (for the downward "swoop") and a closed filter/VCA pair (fast attack, variable release) → mixer. This one patch, with only pitch-sweep depth and envelope decay/release/sustain changed, covers the entire kick-drum design space.
- **Waveform choice**: triangle over sine when a touch more harmonic content/brightness is wanted in the body.
- **Pitch sweep depth is the main character control**: none = a tuned tonal thump (playable to the song's key); short/steep = a traditional kick; longer = a more melodic pitch-swept "boom" or house-kick character.
- **Adding low-frequency (pink) noise blended under the tone** gives the kick more perceived "thickness" without changing the pitched body.
- **Sustain-capable envelope**: setting the envelope to hold at a sustain level (rather than a pure decay-to-zero) turns the same patch into a held/rhythmic tone that still leads with the same pitch-swept kick attack — a "two-section" hit (quick transient into a sustained tail).
- **Heartbeat variant**: slower attack, duller filter setting, some pink noise, little to no pitch sweep — same core patch repurposed for a completely different sound by softening the transient instead of sharpening it.

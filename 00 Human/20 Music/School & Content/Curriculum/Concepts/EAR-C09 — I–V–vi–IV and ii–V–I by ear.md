# EAR-C09 — I–V–vi–IV and ii–V–I by ear

**Q.** You spelled both of these correctly on paper and answered "idk" when they played. What's the training move that closes that specific gap?

> [!question]- Answer
> Drill them as **single fused gestures**, not as chains of chords to identify one at a time. A ii–V is eventually heard as one chunk. Run the progression drill sequence: play the bass notes alone and sing the degree numbers → add the chords back and name each quality out loud → **quality-swap** (change one chord's quality, hold everything else) → **position-swap** (hold the quality, move the degree) → transpose the whole thing and repeat.

## Why it matters

Written, heard and named are three separate skills, and right now only the first is connected. These two progressions are the highest-value pair to connect first because you already own the written half of both.

## Drill

Loop I–V–vi–IV in C. Sing the bass degrees. Then swap the vi for VI and hear what changed. Then move it to E and do it again.

# The power of multi-bus processing with Michael Brauer

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7qfyx7sGEi4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7qfyx7sGEi4)

## Synopsis

A short, console-footage Mix with the Masters clip where Michael Brauer (Coldplay, John Mayer, Adele — see also [[Michael Brauer's mixing inspiration]]) walks through his signature "multibus" stereo-bus technique on a real record, live in the session: instead of one serial 2-bus chain, he sends the mix in parallel to several stereo buses (he names A/B/C/D), each carrying its own light processing, then sums them back together. On this particular track the demoed bus runs an SSL-style bus compressor, a pair of units he calls "the Germaniums" (a Wet/Dry-blend parallel compressor stage), and a Curve Bender (a Pultec-circuit tube EQ) engaged mostly for its passive-circuit coloration rather than for any EQ move. The through-line of the whole clip: every individual stage is doing *almost nothing* by the numbers — barely 3 dB of gain reduction on the SSL even at the loudest chorus, a compressor blend leaning heavily dry, an EQ with no bands actually pulled — but stacking many small, nearly-inaudible moves across multiple parallel buses adds up to more perceived level and "punch" than any single heavier move would, while staying explicitly un-compressed-sounding (a specific complaint from the artist/label he was mixing for and deliberately avoided). He closes with the A/B demo he runs at his own mixing seminars: play a section with the multibus processing fully bypassed (straight to the stereo bus), then play the identical section again with A/B/C engaged — the difference he's pointing at is "openness and excitement," not level (he flags that the bypass pass is gain-mismatched, since fewer active buses means less summed gain, so the balance shift on that pass isn't itself the point). Auto-captioned, spoken narration throughout with high confidence; the two back-to-back playback demos near the end are unspoken audio and aren't transcribed. No hard numbers are given for the Curve Bender or the Germaniums' actual settings beyond the blend-knob position — this is a philosophy/workflow clip more than a settings sheet, but the SSL numbers and the blend-position number are both stated on camera.

## Techniques

- **"Multibus" processing as a distinct structural pattern from a single parallel-comp send: route the SAME stereo signal to MULTIPLE (Brauer names four: A/B/C/D) parallel buses simultaneously, each carrying different processing, then sum them all back together — rather than one send/return pair or one serial 2-bus chain.** This is the mechanism behind the whole video, demonstrated live by toggling entire buses on/off ("I'll take B off, take C off, take D off"). Doubling is also folded into the same structure — a source that needs extra weight (his example: the guitar solo) gets sent to *two* of the parallel buses at once rather than gain-boosted on one. → [[Parallel Processing]], [[Stereo Field]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Deliberately keep every individual stage's gain reduction almost invisible, even at the song's loudest point, and let the sum of many small stages do the work instead of one stage doing an obvious amount.** On the SSL-style bus compressor, checked specifically at the chorus (the loudest section, where you'd expect the most GR): "it's barely three [dB]" — with settings stated on camera as **30 ms / 4:1 ratio / 0.1 s release** (fast release, so the compressor recovers almost instantly and never visibly holds the signal down). The stated reason: the mix had a specific note to avoid — "the last thing I want to do on this track... is to get it to sound overly compressed. That was a complaint that they'd had" — so the ceiling on every stage is set by that constraint, not by a target GR number. *(general starting point for a similarly "not overly compressed" brief — 1–3 dB max on the mix-bus compressor even at the loudest section, fast release so it's never audibly holding)* → [[Solid Bus Comp]], [[Compression]], [[Compression Staging]], [[Master Chain]]
- **A Wet/Dry blend knob on a parallel-style bus compressor, deliberately parked mostly DRY (his description: "leaning way more towards dry like at about 2:00" on the knob), so what's audible isn't compression at all — it's just the character of the input stage.** His own framing: "what you're seeing there is really not compression but just how the input is going in, and again it's slight." A useful reframe for any parallel-blend compressor (owned equivalent: [[Solid Bus Comp]]'s Dry knob, or a New York-style send per [[Parallel Processing]]) — when the blend is mostly dry, judge the unit by its input-stage coloration, not by how much GR the wet path shows. → [[Solid Bus Comp]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[Compression]]
- **Insert a passive tube EQ purely for the coloration of its circuit, with no EQ move dialed in at all.** The Curve Bender (a Pultec-circuit tube program EQ) is in the chain "really just to bring the level down," but Brauer calls out that simply activating it — signal passing through the tube stage and passive network — "tends to open up a bit in sound... and it just feels really good, so even if there's no EQ, it opens up the sound a little bit." Confirms the same passive-EQ mental model already on the owned equivalent's note: "there is no 'flat' bypass tone — the output tube stage colors the signal even with every control at zero." Owned equivalent for the named unit (Curve Bender = ADL/Anamod's officially-licensed reissue of the Pultec EQP-1A/MEQ-5 circuit; unowned hardware, no vendor plugin tracked): [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]]. → [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]], [[EQ]]
- **The cumulative philosophy stated outright: "just doing a little bit on a lot of places, and it adds up and it feels natural" — perceived loudness/punch built from many small, individually-inaudible moves stacked across parallel paths, instead of one heavier move on one path.** "You add all this up, add all the little things I'm doing and then you still got a lot of level, it's still punchy, the perceived level is louder than it seems to be just because that's what multibus compression can do." The explicit trade this avoids: a single hard-compressed bus reads as "compressed" the way this mix was told not to; several barely-there stages in parallel read as louder/punchier without that tell. → [[Parallel Processing]], [[Compression]], [[Compression Staging]]
- **A live A/B demonstration method for teaching/auditioning a multibus setup: play a section fully bypassed (everything sent straight to the stereo bus, no parallel buses engaged) immediately followed by the identical section with the buses re-engaged, on the exact same passage (his worked example: the B section of the verse into the chorus).** Framed as what he runs at his own mixing seminars. The named difference he's listening for on the "on" pass is **openness and excitement** — not simply "louder." He flags the gotcha in this kind of test: bypassing buses changes the summed gain (fewer active buses = less total gain arriving at the stereo bus), so the bypassed pass reads with its own balance off ("if I'm going to buses A and D, it's obviously more gain, so the balances are a bit off on the vocals") — a reminder that a raw bypass-vs-engaged A/B on a multibus setup isn't level-matched by default, and the balance shift on the "off" pass is a side effect of the test, not itself the finding. → [[Referencing and Translation]], [[Master Chain]]

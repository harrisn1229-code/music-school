# Course 09 — Ear Training Method

Recognising what you're hearing by its **feeling against a tonic**, rather than by measuring intervals as abstract distances. Scale degrees first, then chord qualities, then progressions, then transcribing a song without a chart.

**What it assumes**

- Nothing. Lesson 1 starts at singing back a note you just heard.

**What this unlocks**

- [[Song Practice]] by ear — [[Transcribing and Playing by Ear]] is the method for learning a queued song with no chart in front of you.
- [[Course 02 — Chord Function]] stops being paper. Hearing function is what makes "what does this chord do" a real question rather than a naming exercise.
- [[Course 11 — Bass Writing & Design]] — "roots first, *by ear*" is step 2 of writing a bassline, and it's the one place a production course names an ear skill as the blocker.

> [!info] Standing, not scheduled
> **This runs every day, alongside whatever else is active**, and it doesn't take a scheduled slot the way a reading course does. Ear training is a daily drip rather than a course: three minutes a day beats an hour a month.
>
> Each lesson installs a **daily routine**, not a body of reading — which is why the reading here is short on purpose and the homework block is called *The routine*.

## The lessons

> [!tip] There is a listening lane in this course, not just a singing one
> Three hour-long **listen-along** drills from Max Konyi — whose method this whole course is built on — sit alongside the daily routines: [[Immersive Ear Training - Major Scale (1-Hour)|Major]], [[Immersive Ear Training - Minor Scale (1-Hour)|Minor]] and [[Immersive Ear Training - Chromatic Scale (1-Hour)|Chromatic]]. Each plays a tone against a drone, speaks the correct degree, then replays the tone so the feeling and the number bind. They are designed for a walk or a commute and ask for no concentration at all.
>
> **They do not replace the routines.** The daily blocks make you *produce* the feeling by singing it, which is what locks it in; these give you volume of exposure, which is what makes recognition automatic. Do both — the listening is free time you were not otherwise using.

### Lesson 1 — The Tonic, and Being Able to Sing It

**Read**
- [ ] [[Scale Degree Ear Training]] → *Foundation: pitch matching and tonic recognition*

**The material**
- Two prerequisites, worth being honest about before anything else.
- **[[EAR-C02 — Pitch matching is the fallback the whole method rests on|Pitch matching]]** — can you sing back a heard note, find a heard pitch on the instrument, and sing a random pitch then locate it? Every later skill falls back on "sing it or find it" the moment recognition fails, so a shaky fallback means getting **stuck** rather than getting slower.
- **[[EAR-C01 — Tonic recognition, and the three levels of practising it|Tonic recognition]]** is the other, and the ear already does it automatically — that's what makes music sound structured rather than like noise. The training is becoming consciously aware of something already happening.
- Three escalating levels: stop a scale one note short and *sing* the resolution; stop a progression on a non-tonic chord and sing the resolution; then do it with a song you've never heard, stopped at random.
- **Singing the resolution trains it harder than listening for it.** Producing the feeling is what locks it in.

**Concepts**
- [[EAR-C01 — Tonic recognition, and the three levels of practising it]]
- [[EAR-C02 — Pitch matching is the fallback the whole method rests on]]

**The routine** — this is the homework. ~10 minutes, daily, not a reading block.
- [ ] **Review** — stop a C major scale on B and sing the resolution before playing it. Then A minor. Then a key you don't play.
- [ ] **Daily routine** (~10 min) — one song you know: flag the tonic chord every time it recurs, including mid-phrase. Then one song you don't, paused at random, singing the resolution before you check.
- [ ] **Technique** (~5–10 min) — [[Improvising Over a Drone Track]] · bass or guitar. Hold a drone on the tonic and improvise against it, so the resolution you have been *singing* gets found under the fingers. Not the reading's vocal drone method — it trains [[EAR-C01 — Tonic recognition, and the three levels of practising it|the same recognition]] generatively.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Three-Stage Warm-Up (Physical, Mental, Resonance)]] · guitar, Stage 3 (resonance) — sing every note while playing the day's warm-up pattern. The note-with-your-voice check is [[EAR-C02 — Pitch matching is the fallback the whole method rests on|pitch matching]] itself, folded into a physical routine instead of run as a standalone drill.
- [ ] **Listen** (background, ~1 hr) — [[Immersive Ear Training - Major Scale (1-Hour)]] · put it on during a walk or a commute. Passive exposure to the whole degree set against a drone, with the answer spoken after every tone. It asks nothing of you — that is the point, and it is the cheapest hour in this course.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 09 — Lesson 1 Test]]

### Lesson 2 — The Twelve Feeling States, Against a Drone

**Read**
- [ ] [[Scale Degree Ear Training]] → *The 12 scale-degree feeling states* — the table, and the four practical notes under it

**The material**
- The central claim of the method: **every scale degree has its own recognisable feeling state relative to the tonic**, and it's the same *type* of experience whether it arrives as a melody note, a chord, or a bass note.
- The table's labels are one person's words, offered as a starting vocabulary. Find your own, then discard the words and keep the recognition.
- **[[EAR-C03 — Major 3rd vs minor 3rd — the first drill|Start with 3 versus ♭3.]]** Not arbitrary: hearing *a 3rd* without hearing *which* 3rd is the most common starting gap, and quality is the half carrying the emotion.
- Two structural facts make the other twelve tractable. **[[EAR-C04 — The two worlds — the 5-axis and the 3-axis|Degrees group into two worlds]]** — 1-4-5-2 clean and neutral, 3-6-7 warm and glowing — and almost every confusion happens *within* a world rather than across them.
- And **[[EAR-C05 — Close in pitch is not close in feeling|close in pitch is not close in feeling]]**: 5 and ♯4 sit one fret apart and are about as opposite as the system has.
- Always practise against a **sustained tonic drone** while learning. In real listening you won't need one — the mind holds a virtual tonic once it knows the key.

**Concepts**
- [[EAR-C03 — Major 3rd vs minor 3rd — the first drill]]
- [[EAR-C04 — The two worlds — the 5-axis and the 3-axis]]
- [[EAR-C05 — Close in pitch is not close in feeling]]

**The routine** — this is the homework. ~10 minutes, daily, not a reading block.
- [ ] **Listen** (2 min) — [[Visual Ear Training - The Perfect Melody]] · the gentlest thing in this lane: a familiar melody with every degree labelled on screen as it sounds. Nothing to answer, nothing to identify. Run it a few times before the hour-long drills if twelve feeling states feels like a lot.
- [ ] **Review** — against a C drone, alternate E and E♭, saying the feeling word before the degree name. Twenty reps, then move the drone to A.
- [ ] **Daily routine** (~10 min) — drone practice with sustained tones on each degree. Familiarity over speed.
- [ ] **Listen** (background, ~1 hr) — [[Immersive Ear Training - Chromatic Scale (1-Hour)]] · all twelve feeling states against a drone, which is this lesson's title as an hour of audio. [[Immersive Ear Training - Minor Scale (1-Hour)]] is the gentler way in if twelve at once is too much. Key changes every few minutes, so a degree that only feels right in one key gets exposed.
- [ ] **Listen** (2 min) — [[Visual Ear Training - The Perfect Melody]] · the gentlest thing in this lane: a familiar melody with every degree labelled on screen as it sounds. Nothing to answer, nothing to identify. Run it a few times before the hour-long drills if twelve feeling states feels like a lot.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 09 — Lesson 2 Test]]

### Lesson 3 — Chords as Meta Feeling States

**Read**
- [ ] [[Scale Degree Ear Training]] → *Chords, chord qualities and progressions as "meta feeling states"*
- [ ] [[Seventh Chords]] and [[Chord Inversions]]

**The material**
- The counter-intuitive part, and the one that unlocks chord recognition: **[[EAR-C07 — A chord is a gestalt, not a stack of degrees|a chord is not the sum of its component degree feelings.]]** Combining them produces a genuinely new, higher-level experience — the way syllables fuse into a word you hear whole.
- So don't decompose chords into degrees in real time. Train **each quality × position combination as its own flavour.**
- That's the direct fix for confusing the sevenths. **[[EAR-C06 — Which seventh is it — dominant, major, or half-diminished|Cmaj7, C7 and Cm7♭5 are three memorised flavours]]**, not one triad with three different arithmetic operations applied to it.
- **[[EAR-C08 — Inversions are heard from the bass note's own degree feeling|Inversions are their own feeling states too]]**, driven by the bass note's degree feeling. A 3rd in the bass doesn't feel like 1, so an inverted chord never feels landed — which is how you can call *inverted* before you can call *which chord*.
- One honest caveat from the source: hearing **inner voices** works against the ear's natural fusing instinct and stays effortful even when trained. The fallback for a genuinely hard voicing is brute force — outer notes first, then inward.

**Concepts**
- [[EAR-C06 — Which seventh is it — dominant, major, or half-diminished]]
- [[EAR-C07 — A chord is a gestalt, not a stack of degrees]]
- [[EAR-C08 — Inversions are heard from the bass note's own degree feeling]]

**The routine** — this is the homework. ~10 minutes, daily, not a reading block.
- [ ] **Review** — Cmaj7, C7 and Cm7♭5 back to back, naming the feeling of the top note in each. Then blind and shuffled, ten reps.
- [ ] **Daily routine** (~10 min) — the seven diatonic triads of C in random order against a drone, named by position-and-quality as one label ("minor-on-6"), never by spelling.
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 09 — Lesson 3 Test]]

### Lesson 4 — Progressions, Bass Motion, Cadences

**Read**
- [ ] [[Transcribing and Playing by Ear]] → *Working out chords by ear*, and the bass-motion bullets under *Real-time transcription workflow*
- [ ] [[Cadences]]

**The material**
- Progressions are where ear training usually stalls. **[[EAR-C09 — I–V–vi–IV and ii–V–I by ear|Start with the two most worth owning: I–V–vi–IV and ii–V–I]]**, drilled as single fused gestures — a ii–V is eventually heard as one chunk, not two lookups.
- The five-step progression drill is the engine: bass notes alone with the degree numbers sung → chords back in, naming each quality out loud → **quality swap** (change one chord's quality, hold everything else) → **position swap** (hold the quality, move the degree) → transpose and repeat. The swaps are what isolate the variable.
- Then the landmarks that make real songs tractable. **[[EAR-C10 — Half-step bass motion is a diatonic landmark|Half-step bass motion]]** in a major key is almost always IV→iii or vii°→I — and a major chord dropping to a minor chord a half step below is IV→iii, the only diatonic place that happens.
- **♯4 in the bass** means a V/V with its 3rd in the bass (D7/F♯ in C) or a **♯iv half-diminished** (♯ivø7 — F♯ A C E in C), before you test anything. **[[EAR-C11 — Cadences by ear|Cadences by ear]]** are the same trick at phrase scale: the ending tells you where the harmony thought it was going.
- Three modes of working out a chord, escalating as familiarity runs out: **immediate recognition** → **deduction** (narrow with clues, then test candidates) → **brute force** (outer notes first). Label whatever you work out and drill it, so it moves permanently into the first category.

**Concepts**
- [[EAR-C09 — I–V–vi–IV and ii–V–I by ear]]
- [[EAR-C10 — Half-step bass motion is a diatonic landmark]]
- [[EAR-C11 — Cadences by ear]]

**The routine** — this is the homework. ~10 minutes, daily, not a reading block.
- [ ] **Review** — loop I–V–vi–IV in C and sing the bass degrees. Swap vi for VI and name what changed. Then move it to E.
- [ ] **Daily routine** (~10 min) — the full five-step progression drill on one progression, borrowed from a song you're learning in [[Song Practice]].
- [ ] **Test** — [[Course 09 — Lesson 4 Test]]

### Lesson 5 — Transcription, and the Routine That Keeps It

**Read**
- [ ] [[Transcribing and Playing by Ear]] in full — *Finding the key*, *Playing a melody by ear*, *The daily practice routine*
- [ ] [[From Ear Training Apps to Real Music]] — why app skill does not transfer, and the five exercises that bridge it. **The one to take away:** melody wants the arrangement pushed to the background, chords want it left to fuse into a single quality. Opposite instructions, and mixing them up is why chord transcription stalls.

**The material**
- The move that makes all of this work without perfect pitch: **[[EAR-C12 — Finding the key with no perfect pitch|sing the note that feels like the tonic, play any random note, judge what degree *that* is against what you sang, and back-calculate.]]**
- "I played a G and it feels like 4, so the key is D." It recurs in every transcription example in the source, and it's the reason relative hearing is enough.
- **[[EAR-C13 — The five-step melody-by-ear process|The five-step melody process]]** — memorise, map the contour, sing slowly and pencil in the degrees you feel, deduce the rest, verify on the instrument.
- Step two is the one people skip: **contour before degrees.** Getting the shape first constrains the degrees you can't hear by the ones you can. And when you're wrong, the learning is in *which degree got confused for which*, not in the fact of being wrong.
- Finally the routine itself. **[[EAR-C14 — The four skills a daily routine has to touch|Four skills any complete routine touches]]: melodic scale-degree, bass-note, chord-quality, and chord-number/position.** Keep the whole thing under five minutes. This is the piece that outlives the course.

**Concepts**
- [[EAR-C12 — Finding the key with no perfect pitch]]
- [[EAR-C13 — The five-step melody-by-ear process]]
- [[EAR-C14 — The four skills a daily routine has to touch]]

**The routine** — this is the homework. ~10 minutes, daily, not a reading block.
- [ ] **Review** — unknown song: sing the tonic, play a random note, back-calculate the key, check against a tuner. Five songs.
- [ ] **Daily routine** (~10 min) — one melody transcribed end to end by the five steps, logged with the degree you got wrong.
- [ ] **Technique** (~10 min) — [[Steal Like a Bassist — Ear-Training from Known Melodies]] · bass or guitar. [[EAR-C13 — The five-step melody-by-ear process|The five steps]] run on a melody you can already sing cold, where the ear cannot be wrong — sing the root, sing the melody, then find it in the shape. Its stumbling block is the useful part: not every melody starts on degree 1.

# Mix with the Masters 2011 - Michael Brauer & David Kahne

## Synopsis

An early (2011) Mix with the Masters seminar session — mostly mentoring philosophy from Michael Brauer and David Kahne to workshop participants rather than a specific song breakdown, with a couple of concrete arrangement principles worth keeping.

## What's taught

- **"The trick of it all is to have it be smaller but sound like it's bigger" — achieved mostly through writing/arranging, not engineering.** Stated directly: engineering is where a mix "gets small" (i.e., disciplined, restrained) — the illusion of bigness is built upstream, in how a song is written and arranged, not compensated for later at the mix stage.
- **Emphasis comes from making something shorter/rhythmically tighter, not louder.** "If you change the rhythm of something you're trying to get emphasis... if you make it shorter, it's enough — it's the shorter that makes it work, not the louder." A concrete alternative to reaching for volume/level automation when a moment needs to land harder.
- **Deliberately map a song's peak before arranging toward it**: identify where the highlight/peak of a song is, then decide explicitly whether to sustain that level, pull back after it, or build even higher past it — treated as a planning decision made ahead of arrangement, not something discovered by accident while mixing.
- **Tools are tools — technique inspires, it doesn't substitute for the writer/engineer's own creativity.** Framed explicitly to workshop participants: "these are toys... I give you everything I know, but they're just tools, and if you don't have the creativity, that's what you can do with them" — a stated caution against treating any specific technique as sufficient on its own.

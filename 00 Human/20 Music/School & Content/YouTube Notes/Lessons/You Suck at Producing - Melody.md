# You Suck at Producing: Melody

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ojyW99sr2_E)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ojyW99sr2_E)

## Synopsis

A genuinely strong single-melody-writing lesson, more foundational-theory-grounded than most videos in this channel's catalog. Demonstrates the difference between improvising with no constraints (meandering, directionless) and writing with explicit rules: a bounded pitch range, balanced contour (what goes up comes back down), balanced motion (stepwise vs. leaps), and — the standout technique — antecedent/consequent phrase structure (repeat a phrase's first half exactly, vary the second half) as a formula for cohesive, memorable melodies. Substantial enough to expand the vault's [[Melody Writing]] technique note with a new foundational section, since the existing note covered only multi-melody arranging, not single-melody construction.

## Techniques

- **Constrain the range with an explicit top and bottom note before writing anything** — an unbounded improvisation tends to wander in both pitch and phrase length; picking a top and bottom note (roughly an octave-plus-a-third apart) and staying inside that band does most of the work of preventing an aimless melody.
- **Balance melodic contour (what goes up comes back down) and motion (stepwise vs. leaps)** — a melody that climbs for a while should eventually descend, and vice versa; a melody moving stepwise for a stretch should eventually leap (often back the other direction) rather than staying uniformly stepwise or uniformly leap-based. Combined with the range constraint, this naturally produces an arc shape with one clear peak note.
- **Antecedent/consequent phrasing: same first half, different second half** — write a phrase, then write a second phrase whose first half exactly repeats the first phrase's first half (the "question") while its second half differs (the "answer") — this call-and-response repetition-with-variation is what makes a melody read as cohesive and memorable rather than an unstructured stream of notes.

# Ian Kirkpatrick — Vocal Production for Dua Lipa ("Fever")

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0cG4_vxe1tY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0cG4_vxe1tY)

## Synopsis

A full teardown of Dua Lipa & Angèle's "Fever" lead + background vocals — mostly **her demo vocal on an SM7 in the exact chain**, kept because Corona stopped a re-track. The centerpiece is his **reverb-width automation** (30% wide → 100% to take the song "small to finale"), reverb-cutting for drama, resonance-specific de-essing at the ear's harshest frequency, breath-separation, the send-only transient sidechain trigger, and half-speed/double-time vocal tricks. A masterclass in treating the vocal as the whole arrangement.

## Techniques

- **The kept demo take** — Dua did 1–2 takes ("we'll redo later"), then Corona hit; the record is her **demo on the SM7 in this exact vocal chain**. Honest first takes win. → [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]], [[Shure SM57]], [[Ian Kirkpatrick - Using AutoTune to Craft Hit Songs]]
- **Reverb-WIDTH automation: 30% → 100% across the song** — the vocal reverb's *image/width* opens from 30% wide (intro) to full 100% left-right by the finale. "The listener defines the room they're in; then you blow their mind — that was 30, now it's 100." Imaging as an arrangement tool, small→big. → [[Reverb]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Stereo Field]], [[Depth and Space]]
- **Cut the reverb with fades for drama** — he *cuts* reverbs (easier + more control than tails) with little fades, so "the whole song feels like it stops for a second" on a lyric ("suddenly…"). → [[Reverb]], [[Automation]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **De-ess the specific problem resonance, not "sibilance" generally** — knocked out the "**e" of "fever**" ring at ~**2.1–2.6 kHz** (near the ear's harshest resonant band) with a dynamic EQ (C3/C2) rather than a broad de-ess. → [[De-essing]], [[EQ]], [[Soothe2]]
- **De-ess backgrounds *individually and harshly* for center definition** — heavy per-track de-essing on BGs removes their sibilance so the **stereo image stays wide but the definition lives in the mono center** (the lead's "s" defines the middle). → [[De-essing]], [[2 Backup Vocals Group]], [[Stereo Field]]
- **Backgrounds chain: H-Delay (vintage/blue) → OTT → sidechain → heavy de-ess, panned ~70%** — the BG group's actual inserts. → [[OTT]], [[Delay]], [[Sidechaining]], [[2 Backup Vocals Group]]
- **Send-only transient track as the sidechain trigger** — instead of keying the duck off the kick sample (wrong shape), he made a **dedicated send-only transient trigger** (a "fever" transient) so he controls the sidechain attack/release. → [[Sidechaining]], [[Kick Design]]
- **Automate the vocal *level going into* the compressor** — pulling the input down ~4 dB at a spot to shape how the comp responds (an intended "moment"; he notes it didn't fully land). → [[Automation]], [[Compression]]
- **Print the reverb, mono it, compress it a ton** — the vocal verb is printed to audio, summed to mono, then heavily compressed — a dense, controllable ambience rather than a live wide tail. → [[Reverb]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[Compression]]
- **Half-speed tracking (again) + double-time vocal** — tracked at slower tempo and sped up (the "weird" New Rules vocal sound); a **double-time part** where Caroline sang the rhythm at *half* speed and Cubase sped it to double time. → [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Dua Lipa Vocal Chain]]
- **Separate the breaths to their own track** — breaths pulled to a track *below* the lead bus, straight to stereo out, **high-passed, unprocessed** — so the OTT/C2 smashing the lead doesn't inflate the breaths; "preserves the realness." → [[De-essing]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]], [[Compression]]
- **A vocal chop running the whole song** — chops of the original writing-session vocal woven throughout, always changing. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Leave a beautiful part *quiet* if it doesn't fit** — a melody he loved didn't fit the finished arrangement, so he automated it down to barely-there rather than force it — "prioritizing the song, not my production." → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Ian Kirkpatrick - New Rules (25 Big Tips in 13 Min)|serve the song]]
- **Cubase real-time transpose (mouse-wheel) for placing samples** — the clip transpose lane lets him audition/place pitched samples fast by ear. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Everything is printed** — recurring theme: commit every effect/synth to audio.

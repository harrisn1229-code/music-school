# EAR-C08 — Inversions are heard from the bass note's own degree feeling

**Q.** How do you tell root position from an inversion by ear, without identifying the full chord first?

> [!question]- Answer
> By the **bass note's own scale-degree feeling**. A root-position bass note carries the "1" feeling — solid, landed, *this is it*. An inverted bass note carries whatever that degree feels like: a 3rd in the bass feels like 3, a 5th feels like 5. So you can know a chord is inverted **before** you know which chord it is, purely from the bass not feeling like home.

## Why it matters

Inversions are their own feeling states, not the root chord plus a formula applied afterward. This is also the doorway into bass-motion tracking, which is the main landmark in real transcription.

## Drill

Play C/E, C/G and C in random order. Call inverted-or-not from the bass alone before naming the inversion. Ten reps.

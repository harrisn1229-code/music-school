# Helter Skelter — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A blues with **no 12-bar frame and no V chord anywhere** — the blues content lives entirely in the vocal's blue third and a nearly pure pentatonic tune, not in the changes. Pollack's point is that nothing in the form or harmony explains why the track is frightening; the effect comes from the lyric's compulsive circling, the vocal, and a mix printed deliberately into the red.

**Form.** Verse/refrain three times, with the **intro returning midstream** and a **double fade-out**. Verse 1 is a four-square 16 bars; verses 2 and 3 run **14**, reaching ♭III two bars early, which tightens the pace but leaves the section lopsided. The same trimming is applied to the refrain's vamp after the instrumental pass. The outro runs six distinct stages across roughly **40% of the track's running time**.

**Harmony.** Total vocabulary is **I, ♭III and IV** plus one ♭VI in the intro, and nothing else — no dominant at any point ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). **I against ♭III is a permanent cross-relation**, the same collision the vocal makes melodically. The chords change slowly enough that harmony reads as texture rather than motion, and a chromatic line falls through the intro, marking the descent before a word is sung.

**Melody.** Essentially **100% pentatonic** ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]) with a single appoggiatura exception. The blues feel comes almost entirely from the **blue third sung as a quarter-tone smear** ([[BLU-C06 — Blue notes and the blues scale]], [[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]) — get the blues from the melody, not the changes.

**Arrangement.** The vocal is loud throughout but varied inside that: yelling, screaming, sputtering, laughing. The backing track is metallic and mechanical, tracked hot and mastered close for impact; the quieter early take keeps the lyric and vocal and has none of the brutality. The outro halts the rhythm entirely, restarts it, **fades to silence and then fades back in** — a structural event, not a mistake, and it buys a second ending for free.

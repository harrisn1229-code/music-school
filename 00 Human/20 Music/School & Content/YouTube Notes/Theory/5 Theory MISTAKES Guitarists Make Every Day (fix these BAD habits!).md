# 5 Theory MISTAKES Guitarists Make Every Day (fix these BAD habits!)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dpg_L1o5_O0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dpg_L1o5_O0)

## Synopsis

Five recurring theory mistakes BERNTH names from his own playing and from teaching students, each paired with a worked audio example showing exactly why the mistake sounds wrong and how the fix sounds right.

## What's taught

- **Mistake 1 — swapping in a "cooler" scale without checking its characteristic note against the actual chords.** Worked example: a simple Am → G cadence (i → ♭VII in A natural minor). Soloing with **A harmonic minor** instead of natural minor clashes badly, because harmonic minor's raised 7th (G→G♯) fights directly against the G major chord's own root — the exact note that makes harmonic minor "sound cool" is also the note that makes it wrong here. Fix/exercise: compare natural minor and harmonic minor scale-degree by scale-degree AND chord-by-chord (not just note-by-note) — only one note differs, but that single note changes multiple chords built on it, which is what actually determines whether a scale swap will clash.
- **Mistake 2 — building intervals/thirds from outside the key instead of the actual scale.** Worked over E minor: stacking thirds correctly gives G (minor 3rd on E), then E on C's own third (major 3rd, not D♯) — sounds melodic; using "generic" major/minor thirds without checking scale membership sounds wrong. Exercise: play a scale up in thirds, naming each interval quality out loud as you play it (E-G minor 3rd, F♯-A minor 3rd, G-B major 3rd...).
- **Mistake 3 — always starting scale/arpeggio phrases from the root.** Named as BERNTH's own biggest beginner misconception — that a scale/arpeggio "belongs" to its root and must start there. Fix: a dedicated improvisation practice block where starting on the root is explicitly disallowed — target the 3rd, 7th, or an extension like the 9th instead over a backing track.
- **Mistake 4 — writing riff ideas down with no rhythmic information (tab numbers only).** A tab with fret numbers but no time signature/rhythm/tempo becomes unreadable weeks later, and is useless for collaborating with other musicians. Fix: learn basic rhythmic notation (even just within tab) and use notation software (BERNTH demos Guitar Pro) that flags rhythm errors — too many/few beats in a measure — as you write, giving real-time feedback while capturing daily riff ideas.
- **Mistake 5 — deferring fretboard note-name visualization.** Named as BERNTH's own biggest overall regret — learning this "too late." Suggested entry point: learn the natural notes (piano's "white keys") across the neck first, then fill in the sharps/flats afterward, rather than trying to learn all 12 notes' positions at once.

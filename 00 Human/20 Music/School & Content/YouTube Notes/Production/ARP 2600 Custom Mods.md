# ARP 2600 Custom Mods

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KoiG6ToV5Gs)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KoiG6ToV5Gs)

## Synopsis

Anthony Marinelli walks through custom hardware modifications installed on his own ARP 2600 around 1979, driven entirely by the constraints of pre-memory live session/film-scoring work: needing fast octave/interval changes, longer envelope times, and independent envelope triggering, without patch recall to fall back on.

## Techniques

- **Per-oscillator octave switches (Minimoog-style rotary-octave equivalent)**: a 3-position switch per oscillator — center = actual pitch, down = an octave lower, up = an octave higher — each paired with a preset-voltage fine-tune knob (black knob for the down position, white for up), so the octave jump can be calibrated precisely in tune rather than a fixed hard octave.
- **Effectively 5 selectable octaves per oscillator**, combining the switch position with the tunable preset voltage on each side.
- **The same preset-voltage knobs double as an instant interval/unison control**: dial in a specific voltage on a second oscillator's preset knob to get a fixed interval against oscillator 1 at the flip of a switch, rather than manually detuning by ear each time.
- **A switch extending ADSR/AR envelope times to roughly 10× the stock maximum** — the stock 2600's longest decay/release is "functional but longer than necessary"; the modded switch gives genuinely long sustain/release, long enough to hit a bass note and walk away to play something else. Tradeoff: some added noise in that mode, mitigated with contact-cleaner spray on the switches.
- **A separate gate input for the AR envelope generator, independent of the keyboard gate** — normally both envelope generators fire together off the keyboard gate or the manual trigger; this mod lets the AR envelope free-run off an external clock or another module (demoed with a noise source clocked independently) while the other envelope generator stays keyboard-triggered.
- **Clean on/off switches on filter/VCA inputs** — kills hum/bleed and noisy-pot artifacts from an unused input by fully gating the signal off rather than leaving a pot at zero (which can still leak noise).
- **Extra mult (passive multiple/splitter) jacks patched in** beyond the synth's stock mult — lets one control signal (e.g. a sine LFO) feed multiple destinations (an oscillator plus the filter) simultaneously without running out of routing points; also usable informally as mini-to-1/4" cable extenders/adapters when connecting to another synth (demoed connecting to a Korg MS-20).

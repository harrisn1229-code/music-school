## Synopsis

A short but genuinely clever technique: chaining TWO vocoders in series to turn spoken vocal syllables into a playable, in-tune bass instrument. The first vocoder (standard internal carrier) is used purely for texture/menace; the second, with its Carrier switched to External and fed by an actual pitched bass part, imposes real musical notes onto what was otherwise just unpitched speech — solving the problem that a warped/distorted vocal alone doesn't actually hit any notes.

## Techniques

- **Double-vocoder chain: one for texture, a second (External carrier) to impose real pitch** — warp a vocal's timing aggressively and run it through OTT + a first Vocoder for menacing texture/tone (still just distorted speech at this point, not musical notes); add a SECOND Vocoder after it with Carrier set to External, fed from a separately-played pitched bass instrument — the second vocoder maps that instrument's actual note-to-note pitch onto the already-textured vocal, producing a genuinely playable "vocal bass" that follows real chord changes. → [[Vocoder]], [[Bass Design]]

# I'm Down — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A deliberately regressive fifties R&B rave-up on a B-side, where the tone of the shouting matters more than the words and sounding improvised on the spot is part of the aesthetic. What makes it worth studying anyway: underneath the thrown-together surface the blues frames are **carefully varied** — the verse runs a distorted 14 bars while the breaks and refrains run genuine twelves.

**Form.** Verse, verse, guitar-solo break, verse, organ-solo break, then three refrains fading out. No bridge and no relief section — the entire song is blues frames in alternation.

**Harmony.** Strictly I, IV and V. The **14-bar verse** is a standard twelve-bar frame with its middle phrase stretched by one extra statement of the title phrase; cut two bars out of the middle and the textbook form is sitting right there ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]). Extending one phrase and leaving everything else alone is what makes the lopsidedness read as energy rather than error. The first break holds V through bar 10 where the later ones move to IV.

**Melody.** Saturated with ♭3s and ♭7s throughout. Verse 1 opens with Paul **completely unaccompanied** — no bass note, no drum, nothing to locate either key or downbeat — and it retains the power to startle no matter how familiar the record is.

**Arrangement.** The first and last phrases of every verse carry sudden syncopated accents followed by an abrupt rest in the backbeat ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]). That gives the verse bookend symmetry, sets it against the bouncy texture used everywhere else, and imposes a slow periodic rhythm across the whole composition. The two breaks are staged to escalate over identical harmony by varying only the stops: the first **keeps** the verse's dramatic pauses, the second **drops** them, which loosens the section and sets up the jam-session feel for the rest of the record without anyone actually playing looser.

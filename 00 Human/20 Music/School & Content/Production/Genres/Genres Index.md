# Genres Index

Genre playbooks — what makes each genre *sound like the genre*: the identity ingredients, per-element techniques mapped to the plugin collection, reference tracks, and a session recipe. Companion to the [[FX Chain System]] chains (which are genre-neutral defaults with genre variations).

- [[Psych-Pop (Tame Impala)]] — pumping crushed drums, lead-instrument bass, effects-in-the-wrong-order guitars, buried layered vocals, hard panning
- [[Hip Hop - Trap]] — 808+kick as one instrument, hats as melody, hard-tune as timbre, negative space, dry loud presentation
- [[Modern Pop]] — the vocal hierarchy, verse/chorus contrast engineering, disciplined masking, ear-candy budget
- [[60s - Beatles]] — McCartney bass lanes, Ringo drum physics, ADT doubles, block harmonies, hard-pan courage, tape glue
- [[Jazz Fusion]] — contrast as the composition principle, parallel sus chords, chromatic bass into Lydian dominant, physically-modeled instruments, the three-tier reverb system
- [[House - EDM]] — the sidechain pump as heartbeat, surgical low end, texture drums, arrangement as energy management
- [[Neo-Soul - Lo-fi]] — Dilla time, Rhodes-centered harmony, RC-20 texture, loose stacks, warm dark mixes; includes a Chill-Hop / Lo-fi-Hop instrumental-beat variant section
- [[Electro Funk-Hop]] — harmonized monophony over block chords, Lydian↔relative-Dorian writing, chiptune sources against real instruments, bass as rhythm instrument, minimal-processing mixing
- [[Bedroom Pop - Indie]] — **the home lane**: charm over polish, close and dry, chorused guitars, diary vocals
- [[Dance Pop (Dua Lipa, Charli XCX)]] — nu-disco polish ↔ brat-club abrasion; the vocal as rhythm instrument
- [[Bossa Nova (João Gilberto)]] — samba compressed onto one nylon guitar: thumb surdo + syncopated stabs, the 2-bar anticipation sway, m6/maj7#11 harmony, close-dry-quiet mixes (includes learning videos + progressions to steal)
- [[Synthwave]] — Juno chorus on everything, gated LinnDrum snares, 16th-note bass engine, neon brightness as depth; the bright-forward polysynth bed
- [[EDM Subgenres]] — quick-identity cards: deep/tech house, future bass, melodic dubstep, heavy/wobble dubstep, synthwave (full playbook: [[Synthwave]]), techno, DnB, UK garage
- [[Rap Subgenres]] — quick-identity cards with examples: boom bap, trap, drill, rage, plugg/pluggnb, cloud, phonk, G-funk, jazz rap, melodic/emo, club, alt/abstract

## Arrangement at a glance

Every playbook now carries an `## Arrangement` block — section map, entrances and exits, the contrast move, and a density rule (how many [[Sound Palette]] slots are filled at once). Side by side:

| Genre | Section grid | The contrast move | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Psych-Pop (Tame Impala)]] | 16, loose | a **global effect pass** — filter or phaser over the whole mix | 3 → 8 |
| [[Hip Hop - Trap]] | 8/16, static loop | **subtract, then reintroduce** | 1 → 5 |
| [[Modern Pop]] | 8/16, strict | **density swing** — build the chorus, delete to make the verse | 4 → 8 |
| [[60s - Beatles]] | 8, growing | **a new colour per section** — an instrument nobody's heard | 1 → 6 |
| [[Jazz Fusion]] | open | **change the feel** — straight↔swung, half↔double time | 3 → 6 |
| [[House - EDM]] | **16, near-universal** | **silence, then everything** | 1 → 7 |
| [[Neo-Soul - Lo-fi]] | 8-bar blocks | **mute one lane for eight bars** | 2 → 5 |
| [[Electro Funk-Hop]] | 8/16 | **modulate by re-anchoring the bass** a third below | 2 → 6 |
| [[Bedroom Pop - Indie]] | loose | **add one thing** — additive, never subtractive | 1 → 7 |
| [[Dance Pop (Dua Lipa, Charli XCX)]] | 16, club grid | the **filter** (Dua) or the **silence** (Charli) | 2 → 7 / 1 → 4 |
| [[Bossa Nova (João Gilberto)]] | AABA | **who has the melody** — voice, then sax, then voice | 1 → 5 |
| [[Synthwave]] | 16, verse/chorus | **the arp arriving** | 5 → 7 |
| [[Rap Subgenres]] | per lane | drill: rewrite the 808 slides, hold everything else | 3 → 6 |
| [[EDM Subgenres]] | per lane | techno: no sections at all ↔ future bass: everything, every 16 | 1 → 7 |

**What the table shows.** Genres split into two families by *mechanism*, and it cuts across the usual style boundaries:

- **Density genres** — pop, house, trap, dance pop. Sections differ by *how many slots are filled*. Wide swings, engineered payoffs.
- **Timbre genres** — psych-pop, synthwave, drill, techno. Density stays roughly constant and sections differ by *processing and mute state*. Emptying out reads as broken, not dynamic.

Two outliers prove it's a real axis: **bossa nova** changes neither, and moves the melody between players instead; **jazz fusion** changes the rhythmic feel and lets density follow.

Practical use: pick the mechanism before you pick the parts. Arranging a psych-pop track with modern-pop density swings gets you a pop record with psych sounds — which is a different song than the one you meant to make.

## How new genres actually diverge

New genres form when an established one gets ONE of its defining pillars changed, not several at once — change tempo/drum-groove/instrumentation/harmony all together and the result reads as unrelated noise, not a new genre listeners can place ("break all the rules and you're not making house music anymore — you're not making anything people can latch onto"). Example: Led Zeppelin's hard rock → folk-rock songs swap only the guitar (electric → acoustic); tempo, song structure, vocal delivery, and everything else stays put, which is exactly why the folk-rock songs still read as recognizably Led Zeppelin rather than a different band. Practical takeaway when building a genre-fusion idea: identify which single pillar (tempo, drum groove, harmony vocabulary, primary timbre/instrumentation) is changing, and hold the others to genre-standard so the listener has a bridge in. — David Bennett, [[David Bennett on Music Theory, Composition & the YouTube Life Sonic Sorcery Podcast No. 04]]

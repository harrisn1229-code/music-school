# Here There and Everywhere — The Beatles

> McCartney's ballad partly inspired by Pet Sounds. All four Beatles snapped fingers on a separate track. Lush three-part vocal harmonies. One of McCartney's personal favorites.

## Chords
**Key:** G major

**Intro:** G – Bm – B♭ – Am7 – D7
→ **I – iii – ♭iii – ii7 – V7** (the B♭ is a chromatic passing chord sliding iii→ii)

**Verse:** G – Am – Bm – C … F♯m7 – B7 – Em – Am – Am7 – D7
→ **I – ii – iii – IV** (a stepwise climb) **… V7/vi → vi → ii7 – V7**

**Middle 8 ("I want her everywhere"):** F – B♭ – Gm – Cm – D7 – Gm
→ modulates to **G minor** (the **[[Parallel Minor|parallel minor]]** of G major) before sliding back home — the darkening that makes the return to major feel like sunlight. The verse also uses **V7/vi (B7 → Em)**, a [[Secondary Dominants|secondary dominant]].

## Production & Arrangement
Revolver (1966) — McCartney wrote it right after hearing **Pet Sounds**, and it shows in the vocal craft:

- **The varispeed vocal trick:** the lead was recorded with the **tape sped up**, so on normal-speed playback McCartney's voice sits slightly slower/softer/more tender than he actually sang — the secret behind its floating, fragile quality.
- **Lush close three-part harmony** — stacked, blended, arranged as a single instrument (Pet Sounds / Beach Boys lineage).
- **Finger snaps** (all four Beatles, on a separate track) as the *only* percussion for much of it — texture instead of a kit.
- **Gentle, clean guitars**, restrained bass, no drama in the mix — the arrangement stays soft so the harmony and vocal carry everything.

## Steal this
- **Varispeed the vocal:** track (or pitch) the lead a touch sped-up so playback is softer/more intimate — [[Little AlterBoy]] / [[kHs Pitch Shifter]] for the modern version. The Beatles' softness trick.
- **Parallel-minor bridge:** drop to the parallel minor for the middle 8 so the return to major feels like relief → [[Parallel Minor]] · [[Borrowed Chords]].
- **Percussion as texture, not kit:** finger snaps/claps ([[Hand Clap Studio]]) instead of drums on a gentle song.
- **Harmony as one instrument:** arrange close 3-part stacks and blend them tight → [[1 Harmony Vocal Track]] / [[2 Ensemble Stack Group]].

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Here, There, And Everywhere]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #96.*

**Form.** A classic two-bridge model with one verse between the bridges, no instrumental break, opened by one of their rare ad-lib intros. Intro 4 bars; verse 8 as **2 + 2 + 4 (AAB)**, where the B is itself a miniature AAB at half the durations (1 + 1 + 2); bridge strictly 6 bars, but the melody and lyric **elide into the next verse** by repeating the back half of its first phrase, so the section boundary is hard to locate; outro is the first half of the verse with a new melody set to the title words.

**Harmony.** `Intro: I – ♭III – ii – V` · `Verse: I – IV · I – IV(=VI of e) · ii°ø – V-of-e · ii°ø – V-of-e · vi – ii · IV – V` · `Bridge (B♭): I – vi – ii · (g minor) iv – V – i – iv – V` · `Outro: … I – IV – I`. Home is G, but the song also visits **its relative minor, its parallel minor, and the relative major of that parallel minor** — none of them full modulations, all furtive sidesteps, and the restlessness under a placid surface is the whole design. The **second chord of the song gives the game away**: B♭ against the preceding G, a chromatic-mediant cross-relation that pre-announces the bridge ([[POP-C11 — Chromatic mediants and pop modulation]]). Two deceptive cadences do the emotional work ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]], [[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]) — into B♭ to open the bridge, the slip to G minor, then back into the parallel major at the top of the next verse. The ending is a **plagal I–IV–I** ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]); substitute V and the difference is immediate.

**Notable.** By strict reckoning G major isn't formally established until the verse's final bar, and the chord on F♯ in bars 5–6 is a **half-diminished seventh** he could not find anywhere else in the catalogue. Paul's lead was recorded low and slow and sped up on playback; the lead vocal and the bridge guitar licks are **selectively** double-tracked rather than shadowed throughout.

**Against this page.** Agrees on G major.

# Chord Progressions - Beginner's Guide - Part 2 (Major Chord Formula)

## Synopsis

Part 2 teaches the major-triad build formula two ways: as a half-step recipe (root, +4 half steps, +3 half steps) and as a scale-degree shortcut (the 1st, 3rd, and 5th notes of the root's major scale). Defines half step / whole step first, then walks the +4+3 formula on C, G, B♭, and A to show it's root-invariant. Entirely foundational — matches what's already documented with full tables in [[Chords]].

## What's taught

- **Half step** = the very next possible note (C→C♯, E→F with no black key between); **whole step** = two half steps (C→D, F→F♯).
- **Major chord formula: root, +4 half steps, +3 half steps** — universal on any root (worked on C, G, B♭, A). Matches [[Chords]]'s triad table (major = 4+3 half-steps).
- **Scale-degree equivalent: a major chord is the 1st, 3rd, and 5th notes of its root's major scale** — same chord, described two ways (interval-counting vs. scale-degree), with the 1-3-5 framing flagged as more important once bigger/altered chords come into play later in the series.

## Routed to

None — the +4+3 major formula and its 1-3-5 scale-degree equivalent are already documented with a full table across all four triad qualities on [[Chords]] ("Major | 1 3 5 | 4 + 3 | C E G"). No new formula, setting, or technique beyond what's already there.

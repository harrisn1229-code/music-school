# Ob-La-Di, Ob-La-Da — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (an unusual **5 bars** — one bar of piano fanfare, then a 4-bar vamp, with the real downbeat at bar 2) · Verse (8, ABA′B′) · Refrain (8) · Verse · Refrain · Bridge (8) · Verse · Refrain · Bridge · Verse · Refrain · Outro. Take verse and refrain as one unit and the whole song reduces to the short two-bridge model.

Four diatonic chords — I, IV, V, vi — even phrase lengths, a deliberately childlike harmonic plan. The only relief is the refrain's **deceptive cadence to vi** ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]), and it is not decoration: repeating that same deception is what launches the outro and buys the extra bars needed to land the title phrase. The bridge is the one section that opens up, starting on IV and building to a big V.

The craft is in the verse tune, whose four phrases have plain surface contours but whose **higher-level motion walks up the scale** ([[MEL-C01 — Melodic contour]]): odd phrases nudge the ceiling up (3̂→2̂, then 5̂→6̂), even phrases fall through the whole range back to the bottom.

Two things separate the released take from the *Anthology 3* version, and both come down to how much is left to chance ([[RHY-C08 — Quantize, humanize, groove templates]]). The released **bass limps** — arpeggios phrased "one, two-and, three, four-and" — where the earlier take runs even eighths; that limp is the actual hook. And the overdubs are placed almost perversely at random, where the earlier take applied the same details in neat, consistent patterns.

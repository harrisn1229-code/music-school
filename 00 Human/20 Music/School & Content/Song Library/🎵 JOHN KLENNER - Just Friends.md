# Just Friends — John Klenner

> A 32-bar standard that **starts on the IV chord**, not the I — and whose two halves are close enough to identical that the whole tune reduces to a small handful of repeating harmonic units. Well suited as a comping study tune because the ♯IV° diminished, a chromatic-2-5 substitute reading of it, and a tritone-substitution option on the return to IV all show up inside one repeating shape.

## Chords

- **A (bars 1-8):** `IV(B♭maj7) | IV | iv-minor(B♭m7-E♭7) | iv-minor | I(F, often 3rd in bass) | ♯IV°(B°/A♭, or read as A♭m7-D♭7, a chromatic 2-5) | ii-V(Gm7-C7) | I(F, or read as iii/Am)`. Larsen collapses bars 3-4 into one mental unit — "just B♭ minor to F," not two separate chords — and treats the ♯IV° bar the same way, as one category rather than a fresh analytical problem each time through.
- **Bridge-ish 8 (bars 9-16):** dominant-of-the-dominant (**D7**, 2 bars — a common Lydian-dominant spot) resolving through **ii-V(Gm7-C7)** back toward IV, reached either by a **straight cadence** or the **tritone substitution Fm-B7 → B♭**.
- **Second half (bars 17-24):** repeats bars 1-8 exactly (same IV / iv-minor / I / ♯IV° / ii-V shape).
- **Turnaround/tag (bars 25-32):** ends with **Cm7-F7** resolving home; because the tune itself starts on IV rather than I, the classic "I-VI" turnaround becomes a **ii-V-to-IV** shape instead: `F(I) → Dm7(vi) → [D♭m, chromatic passing minor] → Cm7(ii/V-of-IV) → F7(V7/IV)`. The D♭m bar is a comper's-liberty embellishment — it can clash a beat against the melody and resolve immediately, something you can add as an accompanist that would be much harder to justify soloing over yourself.

## ♯IV° diminished — two usable chord-scales

The diminished chord in bars 6 and 22 (spelled B° with A♭ in the bass, an inversion) is the harmonic focal point of the tune for comping purposes. Rather than the symmetric whole-half diminished scale (Larsen's stated default *avoidance* on diminished chords generally — it's a theoretical/mathematical construct, not what's naturally happening in most standards' written harmony), derive a usable scale by changing exactly one note of the parent F major scale:

| Derivation | Change from F major | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Raise the 4th (for the ♯4), then also raise the 5th to cover A♭ | B♭→B, G→G♯ | **F harmonic minor** |
| Raise the 4th, then lower the 6th instead (Larsen's actual working choice) | B♭→B, A→A♭ | **C harmonic major** |

Practical rule: check the diminished chord against the tune's actual melody to see which of these (or neither) fits — don't default to the diminished scale by habit. Full derivation and general theory → [[Diminished Chords]].

## Comping approaches (all three demonstrated on this tune)

1. **Drop-2, chord by chord** — superimposed-shape thinking throughout (Dm7 grip = B♭maj9, E♭7 thought of as E♭m6, F major voiced as Am). Full worked voicing choices → [[Chord Voicings]], [[Jazz Comping - Jazz Chords and Approaches - Just Friends - Jazz Guitar Lesson]].
2. **Bare triads** (same chord, bass note dropped) — B♭maj7 → D minor triad; B♭m7 → D♭ major triad; discipline rule: keep *thinking* the real chord's name even while playing only 3 notes, so the harmonic reasoning doesn't drift into a progression that isn't actually there. → [[Chord Voicings]]
3. **Open comping** — arpeggiation, sustain-a-note-plus-moving-inner-melody, and **riff comping** (stick with one idea for ~8 bars, tracking the tune's own AABA-shaped repetition) as the practice on-ramp to genuinely free playing. → [[Melodic-Harmonic Blending (Comping While Soloing)]], [[Jazz Comping — Rhythmic Motif and Walking Bassline]]

## Steal this

- **Collapse a chord run into "one word."** Any time a stretch of the progression reduces to a single recognizable sound (iv-minor for 2 bars, ♯IV° treated as one category regardless of how it's spelled), learn it as that one thing rather than as separately-memorized individual chords — directly reduces how much there is to actively think about while comping.
- **The D♭m chromatic-passing-minor turnaround trick** generalizes beyond this tune: any I-VI-ii-V-shaped turnaround can take a chromatic passing minor chord between the vi and the ii, added specifically as a comper's embellishment (not something a soloist is expected to navigate). → [[Turnarounds]]
- **Riff comping as a deliberate practice stage**, not just a performance choice — locking to one repeating idea for a chunk of the form before opening up to free playing. → [[Jazz Comping — Rhythmic Motif and Walking Bassline]]

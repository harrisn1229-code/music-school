## Techniques

- **De-essing first, with a mid-side de-esser (shelf + Bell filters), subtle attenuation only (no more than a few dB).** Because it's a mid-side processor, the mid image gets attenuated more often than the side (the center usually carries more sibilance), which nets both more accurate de-essing on the vocal/lead content *and* an incidental, subtle expansion of the side image's highs as a side effect — not the point of the move, but a real one. Exact plugin unclear in captions (unnamed "mid-side de-esser") — owned equivalent: [[Pro-DS]]. → [[De-essing]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Master Chain]]
- **A 4-band dynamic EQ pass, where one band's level triggers a *different* band's gain reduction — a masking-driven duck, not per-band self-triggering.** Band 1: a gradual low-shelf-style slope attenuating unneeded lows. Band 2 (~250 Hz): a follower linked to Band 4's gain, so it attenuates whenever Band 4 crosses its own threshold. Band 4 (~2.5 kHz): configured purely as the *trigger* for Band 2 rather than processing anything itself. The reasoning stated directly: 250 Hz often masks 2.5 kHz, so ducking 250 Hz specifically whenever 2.5 kHz gets loud lets that higher content read through instead of getting buried underneath it. Band 3: a second de-essing bell over the residual sibilant range, mid image only, on top of the dedicated de-esser from the prior stage. Plugin name garbled in captions ("Shady EQ") — closest owned equivalent for the band-to-band triggering mechanic is [[Pro-Q 4]]'s per-band Dynamic EQ with external sidechain (Pro-Q 4 doesn't natively let one internal band drive another band directly the way this plugin does — routing one band's output as another band's sidechain input would need to be approximated manually). → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Upward-EQ as an expander: a mid-image bell over the kick's fundamental and a side-image shelf over the highs, both set to EXPAND (not attenuate) when triggered.** Same dynamic-EQ mechanism as the masking-duck bullet above, run in the opposite direction. The kick-fundamental bell (mid image only) subtly expands whenever the kick is present, adding dynamic punch specifically on the beat without touching the sides; the high-frequency shelf (side image only) subtly expands the highs, reading as extra width up top without touching the kick's low-end anchor. Stated result: more dynamic in the lows, wider in the highs. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[EQ]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Pair an upward processor with a frequency-split saturator: Oxford Inflator for overall density, then a parallel valve/tube saturator centered on the kick's fundamental (low section) and the vocal's presence range (high section).** Inflator: increase Effect, adjust Input to compensate for the added gain (standard Inflator gain-staging, per its own note). Second stage — a "PSP" saturator per captions, exact model unclear — run for parallel valve/tube character, split so the low section targets the kick's fundamental specifically and the high section targets vocal presence specifically, not a generic wide low/high split. Stated result: the overall mix reads fuller from the Inflator while the kick and vocal specifically get more prominent from the saturator's targeted split. → [[Oxford Inflator]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Master Chain]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **An optional, very subtle "collective" reverb on the whole master, run at 100% wet on its own aux and blended in with the aux's channel fader — not the reverb's own dry/wet knob.** Create an aux track, place a good-sounding studio-emulation reverb on it with wet/dry left at 100%, then dial the amount coming through via the aux track's own fader. Explicitly framed as mix-dependent, not a default: works well for cohesion on some masters, but a room reverb applied heavily across everything can ruin a mix — the discipline is keeping it blended in *very* subtly. → [[Reverb]], [[Master Chain]]
- **An optional mid-frequency aux for upward processing on an isolated band, whose output is ALSO routed into the reverb aux above — not a standalone parallel chain.** A second send/aux track: a linear-phase EQ isolates just the mids, then some form of upward processing on that isolated band brings quieter mid-range detail forward, blended back against the original signal via the aux's channel fader. The extra step: that mid-focused aux is *also* sent into the collective-reverb aux from the prior bullet, so the recovered mid detail shares the same reverb space as the rest of the mix instead of sitting apart from it, keeping everything cohesive. Framed around a translation point — mids matter disproportionately for a master because nearly every playback system reproduces mids even when it can't reproduce the true highs or lows. → [[Oxford Inflator]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Mid-side master-bus compression with de-linked triggering for stereo expansion, plus a "dual phase rotation" feature for a fuller low end.** Bus compressor (a vari-mu-style ["daily"/vari-mu, per captions] unit is named as one option; use whichever compressor's sound and mid-side functionality you prefer). First enable mid-side processing, then de-link the triggering so the mid image compresses more than the side (the mid is what mostly triggers the detector in most mixes) — the same underlying mechanism as this vault's existing mid-image-only compression width trick, but reached via de-linked triggering rather than isolating processing to the mid exclusively. Second named feature: "dual phase rotation," which causes mild phase cancellation at higher frequencies, netting a fuller-sounding low-frequency range as a side effect. Exact plugin unclear in captions ("press work") — likely a vari-mu-character bus compressor; see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Master Chain]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Gullfoss isolated to mids and highs only (excluding the kick/low end) to protect transients from its latency, leaning mainly on Recover.** Because the plugin's real-time analysis introduces latency that could smear a transient, the frequency-range limiters are pulled in to keep it working only on mids and highs, away from the kick — the same "exclude a range it shouldn't touch" mechanism already documented on [[Gullfoss]]'s own note. Recover is the main control reached for here, described in the video as dynamically making buried mix detail easier to hear — read this as the plugin's standard unmasking job (pulling masked content back into audibility) rather than a literal phase-cancellation fix; the captions' "reduce phase cancellation" framing doesn't match Gullfoss's documented mechanism, which is unmasking, not phase correction. Owned: standard [[Gullfoss]] (not [[Gullfoss Master]], which isn't licensed) covers this directly. → [[Gullfoss]], [[EQ]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Pultec-style tone shaping placed near the end of the chain, once the corrective/clarity stages are already done — small, deliberate moves only.** With the earlier stages (dynamic EQ, Gullfoss) already carrying most of the clarity work, a Pultec-style EQ handles final tone: boost the lows a touch, boost the air band around **16 kHz**, dip roughly **700 Hz**, boost roughly **3 kHz** — every move gradual and subtle, explicitly framed as a late-chain tone pass, not a corrective one. → [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]], [[EQ]], [[Master Chain]]
- **Two limiters in series for a specific combined character — the Oxford Limiter feeding a Pro-L 2 — plus a stance against true-peak limiting mode in favor of oversampling with very low lookahead.** Named pairing: the Oxford Limiter first (credited with a "thick and full" sound, mainly from its Enhance function), then FabFilter Pro-L 2 (reads "modern," its dynamic algorithm helps retain impact) — combined, described as full, aggressive, and upfront, a different result than either alone. Separately: avoid true-peak limiting specifically, instead running oversampling with very low levels of lookahead — a contrarian stance that echoes (from a different Sage Audio video, already on this vault's [[Pro-L 2]] and [[Limiting]] notes) the argument that true-peak mode itself can read as quieter/less impactful the same way heavy lookahead does. → [[Pro-L 2]], [[Limiting]], [[Master Chain]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Loudness metering: measure integrated LUFS and target roughly −10 LUFS for streaming, accepting some platform turn-down.** On this example master the measured loudness landed close to **−10 LUFS integrated**, described as working well for streaming distribution even if the platform normalizes it down somewhat afterward — a more moderate target than some of this vault's other Sage Audio sources (which push closer to −9 to −7 LUFS for pop/trap), consistent with this video presenting a general, moderate example rather than a genre-maximal loudness push. → [[Clipping and Loudness]], [[Master Chain]]

# I Can Finally Tell You What I've Been Working On

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z_Jy4kdx5pc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z_Jy4kdx5pc)

## Synopsis

An announcement for a new course, "Bass DNA," that turns out to include a full preview lesson with genuinely structured content: a 5-category framework for what oscillator CONFIGURATION to reach for when designing a synth bass, organized around what the bass actually needs to DO in a track (clarity, room-shaking weight, character, scale, harmonic complexity) rather than by synth model or preset name.

## Techniques

- **Five bass oscillator configurations, as a starting-point framework** (all other parameter choices — filter, envelope, etc. — are then dialed to taste from whichever configuration fits the track):
  1. **Single oscillator** — one audio oscillator, one waveform, straightforward tuning — the clarity baseline.
  2. **Sub oscillator** — add a dedicated sub oscillator for room-shaking low-end weight, at the cost of a less focused pitch center.
  3. **Overtone oscillator** — layer additional oscillators tuned ABOVE the fundamental at overtone-series intervals (an octave-and-a-fifth, and two-octaves-and-a-third are the specific intervals demonstrated) for "character" — balance the blend between the fundamental and the added overtone layers to taste.
  4. **Unison oscillator** — a polyphonic synth's unison mode, stacking many voices on one note for a massive, "coursing," wide bass that "slams like a ton of bricks" — trades pitch clarity/focus for sheer scale.
  5. **Audio-rate modulation** — modulate an oscillator, filter, amplifier, or ring modulator with ANOTHER OSCILLATOR running in the audio-frequency range (not a slow LFO) for genuinely complex harmonic content — described as making an analog patch "start to sound like digital." Can be layered with a plain single-oscillator base underneath for a fuller combined result.
- **Match the configuration (or combination of configurations) to what the track actually needs from the bass** — clarity, room-shake, character/distinctiveness, sheer size, or harmonic complexity are treated as separable qualities a track may need one, several, or all of at once; mixing more than one configuration together is explicitly normal when a single quality isn't enough.

# Diatonic Modes Practice Ladder

Brandon D'Eon's leveled drill progression for internalizing the major scale's seven diatonic modes on guitar — the major-scale counterpart to [[Harmonic Minor Scale Practice Ladder]], sharing most of its drill shapes. See [[Diatonic Modes]] for the underlying theory (mode construction, character, chord-per-degree table) this ladder drills.

## The ladder

1. **One major scale shape, memorized and transposable to any root.**
2. **Per-mode drilling across all 12 keys.** Random-key generator + stopwatch + metronome: generate a random key, play the one mode currently being drilled in that key, repeat across all 12 keys before moving to the next mode. Trains the mode's shape, interval knowledge (relating notes back to the key), and fretboard knowledge simultaneously. Same method as Routine 1 in [[The Five-Category Practice Method]].
3. **All seven modes of one key, played as one continuous octave-spanning pass.** Random key generated, then walk through all seven modes back to the starting mode an octave up, starting as low on the neck as possible without open strings — timed with a stopwatch as the progress metric.
4. **Musical application: improvise over backing tracks** using everything drilled in levels 1–3. The explicit checkpoint that isolated scale-running isn't the end goal.
5. **Fixed position, cycling key — the inverse of a position-per-key drill.** Pick one fixed fretboard position and, across all 12 randomly-generated keys, find and play whichever mode's shape sits in that position for each key. **Contrast with [[Positional CAGED Soloing with Matched Modes and Arpeggios]] and [[Harmonic Minor Scale Practice Ladder]]'s Level 7, which fix the key and walk multiple positions** — this drill fixes the position and walks all 12 keys instead.
6. **Three-notes-per-string, all 12 keys, ~130 BPM.** Same three-notes-per-string mechanic as the harmonic minor ladder's Levels 5–6.
7. **The chain exercise (capstone).** Pick a random key, improvise continuously across the whole fretboard without stopping, in a subdivision matched to actual skill level. Rated the single hardest and most effective scale-internalization method — a real test of key knowledge rather than a fun improvising exercise (that's Level 4's role).

# Behind the Track｜'greedy' by Tate McRae

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9-vwH1taig)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9-vwH1taig)

## Synopsis

Ryan Tedder (with co-writers/producers Jasper, Amy Allen, and Grant Boutin) walks through writing and producing Tate McRae's "greedy" — a deliberately early-2000s-Timbaland-referencing dance-pop record, built fast from a solo drum-programming sprint, anchored by one unchanging hand-drum motif, and written from a conversation-first songwriting method.

## What's taught

- **"Every four bars, introduce something new or change something slightly" — a stated arrangement ethos for maintaining dynamic movement.** Applied as a running principle throughout the record, not just this song — a concrete cadence for pacing new elements/variations into an arrangement rather than looping a section unchanged.
- **Fast beat-building sprint: build the core beat alone, in ~20 minutes, before the room writes to it.** With the team assembled, the producer isolates with headphones for a self-imposed 20-minute window to get drums + a core riff down — starting with the **drum loop first**, before any synth or riff — then brings the group back in to write over the finished groundwork rather than building collaboratively from zero.
- **Deliberate genre-reference beat-making**: explicitly built to emulate early-2000s Timbaland-style production (referencing "Cry Me a River," "Get Ur Freak On," the *Justified* album, Missy Elliott), drawing on direct studio experience from having been in the room during those original sessions — genre pastiche grounded in first-hand technique knowledge, not just ear-reference.
- **One continuous, unchanging motif as a hypnotic anchor.** A hand-drum sample plays a repeating melodic/rhythmic motif that runs through the ENTIRE song without ever stopping or varying — deliberately used raw and unmanipulated because it "feels like a human played it" and its ambiguous origin (can't tell what instrument it is) adds to the hypnotic effect. Flagged as a major, mostly-unnoticed contributor to why the track feels cohesive.
- **Conversation-first songwriting: talk about real life before writing anything, and write about whatever the artist most wants to vent about.** Spend real time early in a session just talking with the artist about what's going on in their life — the topic that surfaces as what they most want to talk/vent about is treated as the correct subject for the song, rather than picking a theme abstractly first.
- **"Start mid-conversation" as a lyric-opening device.** The opening line is written to sound like the listener has walked in partway through an existing conversation ("Are you serious? I can't figure you out.") rather than a scene-setting intro — deliberately not "song-writerly," used specifically because it pulls the listener in immediately.
- **The "t-shirt line" as a songwriting north star.** Before diving into full lyric-writing, settle on one core sentence (or even one word) that captures what the song is fundamentally about — described literally as "the line you'd put on the t-shirt" — and use it as the throughline check for the rest of the write.
- **A chopped, repeated vocal ad-lib as texture, dialed back from its original loudness.** A rhythmic "so-so-so-so" vocal chop, originated as an intuitive ad-lib on the original demo vocal, was kept running throughout the track as a textural layer — originally much louder in an early mix, pulled down in level (but not removed) once its job as background texture rather than a lead element became clear.
- **A drum drop-out ("catch your breath") right before a big beat re-entry**, credited to a specific collaborator's call — a deliberate full or near-full stop just before the drop, creating a held-breath tension moment distinct from a filter sweep or riser.
- **Deliberate emotional juxtaposition again**: swaggy, dance-focused Timbaland-style production layered under lyrically "desperate"/emotionally vulnerable content — explicitly targeting a track that registers as danceable on first listen but carries a heavier emotional undertone underneath.
- **Live keys over a programmed 808 foundation, tracked in 1-2 takes.** Core chord structure recorded live by a keyboardist directly on top of an already-programmed 808 bass part, rather than programmed separately — captured quickly (1-2 takes) once the foundation groove was already locked.
- **A "descending melody" post-chorus vocal chant as a distinct hook-writing device**, credited as a specific collaborator's (Amy Allen's) recognizable signature move — a short, catchy descending vocal phrase placed specifically in the post-chorus slot as its own hook layer separate from the main chorus melody.

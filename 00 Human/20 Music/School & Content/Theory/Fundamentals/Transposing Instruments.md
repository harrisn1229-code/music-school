# Transposing Instruments

Part of [[Music Theory]] → [[Notation]]. Some instruments **sound** a different pitch than what's written on their part — a **transposing instrument**. Relevant if arranging or writing parts for horns/woodwinds beyond Ableton MIDI (session players, notated charts).

## Why they exist
- **Woodwinds** come in different sizes to cover a wider combined range without forcing players to learn new fingerings per size — one fingering system works across the whole family (soprano/alto/tenor/baritone sax, etc. all finger the same way).
- **Brass** instruments were historically built in many different keys, especially before valves (when they could only play the harmonic overtones of a single fundamental note) — so music was written so fingerings/slide positions stayed consistent across instruments in different keys. Certain keys became preferred for timbre/range and stuck around even after valves made the original constraint obsolete.

## Common transpositions (written → sounds)
| Instrument | Sounds relative to written |
|---|---|
| Piccolo | one octave higher |
| Flute, oboe, bassoon | as written (non-transposing) |
| B♭ clarinet, B♭ trumpet, soprano sax | major 2nd lower |
| Bass clarinet | major 2nd + 1 octave lower |
| Alto sax | major 6th lower |
| Tenor sax | major 2nd + 1 octave lower |
| Baritone sax | major 6th + 1 octave lower |
| F horn (French horn) | perfect 5th lower |
| English horn | perfect 5th lower |
| Contrabassoon | one octave lower |

*(Table condensed from the source's full chart, which also covers cello/viola/trombone/violin/glockenspiel as non-transposing or specific-interval cases — see the enrichment page for the complete visual reference.)*

## How to use it
To write for a transposing instrument, figure out the **sounding** pitch you want first, then transpose it by the **opposite** direction/amount before writing it into that instrument's part (e.g. want a written-C-actual-pitch on B♭ clarinet → write a D, since the clarinet sounds a major 2nd lower than written). Most modern notation software automates this once the instrument is set correctly.

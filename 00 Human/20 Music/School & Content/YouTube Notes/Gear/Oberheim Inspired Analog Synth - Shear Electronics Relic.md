## Synopsis

A NAMM-booth premiere of the Relic, a mother-son-built boutique synth (Shear Electronics) recreating the original 1970s Oberheim signal path with discrete, surface-mount analog voice cores. First public demo, one prototype, not yet shipping. Mostly a gear reveal, but two design choices are genuinely transferable technique.

## Techniques

- **The filter's TYPE/slope, not just its cutoff, is a continuous CV-controllable parameter** — one knob blends continuously from a 4-pole bandpass down through 2-pole lowpass to 4-pole lowpass, and because it's voltage-controlled, an envelope can be patched to sweep the filter's *shape* over time (demoed on an electric-piano patch: 4-pole bandpass at the attack, settling to 4-pole lowpass by the decay). A different instrument example of the same idea already documented from the Sherman Filterbank. → [[Filters and Resonance]]
- **Deliberately pushing per-voice VCA output gain past 0 dB (up to +24 dB, "to 11") as a controlled distortion source**, rather than treating gain staging as strictly clean-signal-only — demonstrated audibly adding grit to a brass patch at a gain setting well past unity.
- **High-resolution touch encoders that reveal a hidden fine-adjustment digit on touch** (e.g. an attack knob that jumps in visible 10ms steps at a glance, but shows and lets you dial an exact value like 62ms while your finger is on it) — a UI approach worth knowing exists when evaluating hardware for how finely a parameter can actually be dialed in, versus how coarse its visible steps look.

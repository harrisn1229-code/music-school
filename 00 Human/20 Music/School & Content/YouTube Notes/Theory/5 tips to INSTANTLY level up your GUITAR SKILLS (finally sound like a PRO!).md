# 5 tips to INSTANTLY level up your GUITAR SKILLS (finally sound like a PRO!)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xe3lY_Egqrg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xe3lY_Egqrg)

## Synopsis

Five escalating levels taking a plain power-chord riff (in G minor) to a genuinely musical progression, each level a direct A/B improvement over the last: pick a key first, color the power chord's octave with a 9th, add diatonic 3rds while dropping the root, weave a melody into the voicings, then use triads with minimal hand movement for speed. Levels 1–2 restate ground already logged from earlier videos in this batch; levels 3–5 are new and routed.

## What's taught

- **Level 1 — decide the key before writing chords (restated, see prior videos).** Derive the diatonic chords of a chosen key (worked: G minor → Gm, A°, B♭maj, Cm, Dm, E♭maj, Fmaj) and mix/match only from that set, rather than hunting by ear for a chord you can hear in your head but can't locate on the fretboard.
- **Level 2 — replace the power chord's octave with the 9th (restated, matches the "add color tones" theme already logged from this batch).** A plain power chord (root + 5th) reads as emotionally neutral; playing the 9th on top instead of the octave adds a whole-tone dissonance against the root for a much more colorful, suspenseful sound with one note changed.
- **Level 3 — add diatonic 3rds (major or minor, per each chord's actual quality in the key) AND drop the root from the voicing.** Instead of full "bonfire" open chords, voice each chord as just 3rd-5th-9th (no root) — legitimate because a bass part is already covering the root, so the guitar voicing doesn't need to restate it. Framed as one of the biggest unlock moments in the video for writing genuinely interesting-sounding progressions from otherwise simple diatonic material.
- **Level 4 — weave a melody directly into the chord voicings, instead of adding a separate lead line.** His stated personal favorite trick: rather than layering a whole additional melodic guitar track over a chord progression, introduce small internal movement within the chord voicings themselves (a note moving inside/on top of the chord shape from one chord to the next) so a melody is audible without ever leaving the harmony part — described as the single biggest before/after jump in the whole video.
- **Level 5 — use triads (root-3rd-5th, minimal shapes) with deliberate minimal-motion voice leading for fast chord changes.** The original power-chord riff required large position jumps between chords, which becomes a real speed bottleneck; the fix is choosing each new chord's triad voicing so its root sits as physically close as possible to the previous chord's voicing (minimal hand movement, eyes tracking "nearest root" rather than a fixed shape), which also generalizes directly to soloing — the same nearest-chord-tone mindset keeps a fast-moving solo melodically accenting each chord.

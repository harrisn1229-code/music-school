## Synopsis

Series opener for Max Konyi's step-by-step lo-fi build-a-track tutorial (Studio One, but explicitly DAW-agnostic in technique). Max frames the whole series as an answer to a gap in music-production education: instrumentalists learn by copying cover songs note-for-note hundreds of times before writing originals, but producers rarely get an equivalent "play along and copy the whole production" exercise — hence a full from-scratch beat, decision by decision, across the series (this queue covers parts 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9 — see [[Piano Chord Progression Studio One LoFi Tutorial Part 2|Part 2]] · [[Drums Studio One LoFi Tutorial Part 3|Part 3]] · [[Bass Synth and Bassline Studio One LoFi Tutorial Part 5|Part 5]] · [[Retro Lead Synth Studio One LoFi Tutorial Part 7|Part 7]] · [[Final Mix & Master Studio One LoFi Tutorial Part 9|Part 9]] — parts 4, 6, 8 weren't queued for this pass). This part itself is short: it sets the two foundational decisions — tempo and key — before any sound is made.

## Techniques

- **Tempo choice for lo-fi: 75 BPM, a deliberate middle ground.** Max notes lo-fi generally runs roughly 60–90 BPM and picks 75 as the center of that range rather than committing to either extreme. Reinforces [[Neo-Soul - Lo-fi]]'s documented 70–95 BPM pocket — this video's number sits at the low end of that window.
- **Key choice driven by the target bass note's physical character, not by melodic convenience.** Max picks the key of E major specifically because a low E bass note has "good thick cozy resonance" — he wants that note as the track's root/tonic so every return to the I chord lands on a physically satisfying low E. The reasoning: choose the key by asking what the root note of the bass will *feel* like at the bottom of the mix, not just what's easy to write in. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Skill-level-adaptive way to use a build-along tutorial** — beginners should copy every note/decision exactly (hands off creative control, to learn how a track is actually assembled); intermediate producers should follow the same overall process but vary the melody/chords/key/drum pattern; advanced producers can just watch for isolated tricks. A reusable framing for how Nate might use any of this series or similar sequential build-alongs.

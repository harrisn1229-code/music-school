# Synthet - Harmony Tricks for ALL Producers

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8o7G1YkM3Ng)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8o7G1YkM3Ng)

## Synopsis

A companion piece to [[Synthet - Harmonies, Easy!]] pitched at producers who don't necessarily sing: it nails down major-3rd vs. minor-3rd distances in semitones (with the "Happy Birthday" interval as an ear-reference), explains that mixing major and minor thirds against the underlying key is what keeps a harmony from sounding "awful" (a run of only major 3rds does), notes the 5th as a more "neutral" alternative interval, and — the most re-usable production move — shows using Auto-Tune to reharmonize a duplicated vocal take onto chord tones (the root, the 5th) rather than singing the harmony live.

## What's taught

- **Major 3rd = 4 semitones, minor 3rd = 3 semitones up from the melody note** — the "Happy Birthday" opening interval is the easy ear-reference for a major 3rd. Whether the harmony interval should be major or minor is usually audible by ear once you know what to listen for.
- **Stay diatonic — don't harmonize with only major 3rds** — duplicating a melody a major 3rd up start-to-finish, ignoring the key, "sounds awful"; mixing major and minor 3rds to match the underlying scale/chord ("some minor, some major") is what makes it work. Reinforces [[Suspended Chords|the general rule]] of picking harmony notes that are in the key.
- **The 5th is a more "neutral" interval than the 3rd** — a 3rd carries more emotional color (major = brighter, minor = sadder); a 5th sits more neutrally, useful when you want harmonic support without pushing the emotional read one way or the other.
- **Chord-tone harmonies as a starting point** — for a first attempt, harmonize using the exact notes of the underlying chord (root, 3rd, 5th) rather than a fixed melodic interval; build up from single added notes to full chord-tone stacks.
- **Reharmonize a duplicated vocal in Auto-Tune instead of singing the harmony** — duplicate the lead vocal take, then use Auto-Tune's manual/graphical retune to snap the *duplicate* onto a different scale degree (demoed: the 5th) — a way to generate a harmony line without a second real performance. Optionally convert the duplicate to a different-register/gender voice for separation. → [[Pitch and Formant]]

# EAR-C10 — Half-step bass motion is a diatonic landmark

**Q.** You hear the bass move by a half step inside a major key. What are the only two likely places, and what narrows it further?

> [!question]- Answer
> **IV→iii (4→3)** or **vii°→I (7→1)**. If you also hear a major chord moving to a *minor* chord a half step below, there's only one diatonic spot that happens: **IV→iii** in major (**♭VI→v** in minor). Related landmark: a **♯4 in the bass** almost always signals either a secondary dominant of V (V/V with its 3rd in the bass — in C, D7/F♯) or a **♯iv half-diminished** (♯ivø7 — in C, F♯ A C E).

## Why it matters

Bass motion is the primary chord-to-chord landmark in real transcription — you read it first and confirm the quality on top second. One recognised half step can collapse a whole progression's candidate set.

## Drill

Play IV→iii and vii°→I in C. Then play both in A♭ without looking. Name which one you're hearing from the bass move alone.

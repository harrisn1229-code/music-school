# Roland R-8

## What it is
The "Human Rhythm Composer" — Roland's post-TR flagship, built to answer the criticism that drum machines sound like machines. 16-bit samples, velocity pads, and per-hit "feel" parameters made it the pro's programmable session drummer at the turn of the 90s.

## What makes the sound
- **Clean 16-bit PCM** of studio-recorded kits — hi-fi where the TR line was character; the R-8's identity is *polish*.
- **Humanize engine**: per-note pitch/decay/nuance offsets and "feel patches" that randomize velocity/timing within ranges — programmed grooves that breathe, years before DAW humanization.
- **Sound cards** (electronic, contemporary, jazz, the famous *Dance* card with 808/909 reprints) made it a chameleon — plenty of early-90s "909" is actually an R-8 card.
- Velocity- and position-sensitive pads made finger-drumming expressive; individual outs for console processing.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] with R-8/R-8 card samples; Ableton velocity/timing humanization + [[Group Humanizer]] replicate the feel engine natively.
- **Paid:** Roland Cloud (R-8 kits appear in their libraries), UVI Beat Box Anthology 2.
- **Free:** R-8 and Dance-card samples circulate in vintage archives.

## Used on
- Genre/era: new jack swing and 90s R&B programming, early-90s house/techno (often via the Dance card), pop sessions that wanted "drum machine tight, human loose" — ubiquity without marquee-credit documentation, so genre-level per the rule.

## Synopsis

An 8-step "bare minimum" theory roadmap aimed specifically at self-taught rock/metal players: fretboard visualization, intervals for riff-writing, diatonic chords in a key, cadences, odd-meter rhythm, scale-first songwriting, arpeggios/triads for melodic accenting, and cross-genre practice. Most steps restate ground the vault already covers in depth (fretboard visualization, intervals, diatonic chords, cadences, arpeggio shapes — see [[The 5 MOST Important Music Theory TRICKS! (Finally Sound Like A PRO)]] for the same author's closely overlapping beginner survey). Two points are specific and reusable enough to route: the odd-meter grouping trick, and the "scale is the foundation, chords are derived from it — not the other way around" reframe for exotic-scale songwriting.

## What's taught

- **Odd-meter rhythm — break big/scary time signatures into small counted groups instead of counting straight through.** For 7/8 (or 5/4, or a 17/8 example cited from a Grammy-winning Dream Theater track), don't count "one-two-three-four-five-six-seven" — identify smaller sub-groups within the riff/rhythm itself (e.g. 2+2+3, or the video's spoken example "one-two, one-two, one-two-three-four, one-two-three, one-two") and count those. States a real-world payoff: after practicing counting in odd meters this way, ideas in odd time signatures start coming out naturally while writing, without deliberately reaching for them — cites his own riff that shifts 5/4 → 4/4 → 6/4 as something he wrote without consciously tracking the meter change, as a result of this practice.
- **Scale-first songwriting — the fix for "I learned an exotic scale and it didn't work over my riffs."** Diagnosis: picking up an exotic scale (harmonic minor, melodic minor, Phrygian dominant, super Locrian) and trying to bolt it onto existing riffs/chords fails because the causality runs backward — the scale should be the compositional foundation a riff/progression is built FROM (chords are derived from the scale's notes, per [[Chords by Key]]), not something layered on after the fact. Exception noted: a riff confined to the lowest string/root note only has enough harmonic freedom that scale choice barely matters.
- **Arpeggio/triad bare-minimum set for escaping "stuck in one scale" soloing:** one shape each for major, minor, augmented, and diminished — enough to start melodically accenting chord changes (the neoclassical/symphonic-metal sound cited as the payoff). Beyond the four basic shapes, recommends studying 7th-arpeggio interval structure specifically so shapes can be built on the fly rather than relying on memorized diagrams — matches [[The BEST Arpeggio Trick! (ONE easy pattern for ALL shapes)]]'s one-shape-many-qualities system logged separately.
- **Cross-genre practice as a growth strategy, stated as personal experience rather than a drill.** Playing/improvising in non-metal styles (jazz, blues, Latin, pop) — even without wanting to master them — is credited with directly improving his metal playing and songwriting diversity; framed as a "don't limit yourself to only practicing your home genre" recommendation.

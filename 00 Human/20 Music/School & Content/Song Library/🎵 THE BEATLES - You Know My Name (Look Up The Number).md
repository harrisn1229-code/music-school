# You Know My Name (Look Up The Number) — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** four takes of the same fragment, end to end — hard rock, lounge/samba, Goon Show, jazz club — each Intro · Verse · Refrain with local variations. Better described as a **fragmentary song delivered as a medley of four alternate takes of itself** than as character variations on a theme. The verse is an odd **7 bars**, the asymmetry manufactured by the harmonic rhythm briefly doubling in bar 5 ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]).

Each take alters the actual material, not just the mood: take 2 replaces the pedal-point bars with percussion, take 3 drops the two-bar opening entirely and swaps the iii chords for a first-inversion tonic, take 4 fills the old pedal bars with a jazz piano figure.

The verse shape is peculiar — it **starts on IV and ends on V**, and the tonic makes one furtive appearance in bar 5 as the only chord in the section not on a downbeat. The V-of-V is made to **wait through an intervening chord change before resolving**, a trademark going back to "Eight Days A Week" ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]); strip the camouflage and the verse is a circle of fifths. The first take's tune is **three notes**.

The backbeat is the primary device separating the four settings ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]), and the joins are engineered as carefully as the takes — take 2 has **no final chord at all**, only the tail of a spoken line running past where the music stopped. Pollack's lesson: a joke track still needs a properly built chart under it.

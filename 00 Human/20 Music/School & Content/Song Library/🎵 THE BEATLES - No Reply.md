# No Reply — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Verse (16, shaped AA′BA″) · Verse · Bridge (a full 16, the same length as a verse) · Verse · Outro. Only one bridge, and only one verse after it; the verse lyrics run **ABB** rather than ABA, because the title phrase already returns inside the bridge.

Everything in the song points at **the third phrase of the verse**. It is the one place the harmonic rhythm doubles — every other phrase changes chord on each of its first three bars and holds through the fourth, while the B phrase changes **every bar, on the eighth before each downbeat** ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). It is also where the syncopation turns violent and where the arrangement opens up.

The harmony keeps backing down while the lyric confronts, and that mismatch is the song. The verse opens on a ii⁶₅ that sounds like ii and IV superimposed; the B phrase runs vi → iii, then the IV7 arrives with the melody's E sustained over it and the expected forward move never comes — it **slides back to iii**. The bridge's E major into A major briefly suggests a real key change before resolving into V-of-ii and dropping straight home ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]).

Loud-versus-quiet is scheduled, not incidental ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]): acoustic guitar, bass and **drums with no cymbals** for verse phrases 1, 2 and 4; cymbals, piano and electric guitar added only for phrase 3 and the bridge. The peak phrase is then recycled as the coda rather than replaced.

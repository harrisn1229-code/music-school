# Randomized Rack Design

Building Ableton racks that **generate their own variation** instead of sitting static — mapping [[LFO]]/[[Expression Control]]/[[Envelope Follower]]/[[Shaper]] onto rack macros (and chain-select bars) so a single rack keeps producing fresh combinations, textures, and happy accidents on its own. The throughline of dnksaus's channel: constant, hands-off variation as a sound-design tool, not a performance gimmick. Companions: [[Sound Palette]], [[Automation]] (deliberate, authored movement — this note is the *generative*, self-randomizing counterpart), [[Drum Rack]], [[Looper]].

## From videos

- **A one-click "regenerate everything" randomizer, built and shipped specifically to fight creative block, not just to explore variations.** A purpose-built plugin's team called its infinite-randomized-presets button "the final piece of the puzzle" for solving beatblock — a single click re-rolls a full new combination across the whole instrument's parameter set at once (texture layer, variance, filters), rather than randomizing one macro or one device at a time. The design lesson generalizes to any owned rack: a rack-wide Randomize wired to hit *every* generative macro at once (texture/pan/ADSR variance, filter type, noise-layer blend) gives a faster "unstuck" button than randomizing devices individually. → [[Voice Architecture]] (the per-note variance this pairs with), [[Sound Palette]] — loner, [I Created The 'Perfect' Plugin For Producers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kI2f9JNImGE)
- **An earlier (2017) worked example of the chain-volume-fade dry/wet macro, plus the same trick nested one level deeper for a mono/stereo split.** Building the "Glitch Delay" rack: rather than mapping a delay's own Dry/Wet knob, a dry passthrough chain and a wet delay chain are grouped, their Chain Select zones stretched full-width and faded past each other, and the chain selector itself mapped to a macro — the identical mechanism documented elsewhere on this page as "the chain-volume-fade dry/wet macro" (from a separate, later dnksaus video). Reason given for choosing the harder two-chain route here specifically: it leaves room to nest a *second* device into the wet path later. That nesting happens one step further in: the delay is regrouped into its **own** inner rack with a mono chain and a stereo (Ping Pong Delay) chain built the same way, both chains' parameters mapped to the *same* macros already driving the outer rack — a reusable "nested rack, shared macros" pattern for adding a parallel variant of an effect without duplicating its controls. Full build: [[Glitch Delay Rack]]. → [[LFO]], [[Delay]], [[Glitch Delay Rack]] — asd, [Ableton Sound Design | Glitch Delay Audio Effect Rack](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=95e5bfB8th8)
- **Turn any drum loop into a random variation engine with "Group Notes → Play One"** — slice the loop to a new MIDI track by **transient** (each hit becomes its own pad, and even a single repeated hat sample yields per-hit micro-variations), pull the kick and snare out as a stable backbone, then stack every remaining slice at the same time position and right-click → **Group Notes → Play One**. A stacked group fires exactly *one* of its notes at random per pass. Set the group to 32nd notes, duplicate across the bar, then randomise velocity and **Chance**, using deviation markers to bias the spread (mid velocity with deviation pulled down = mostly quiet with occasional jumps). Glitch it further with **Beat Repeat → Auto Filter** (resampling filter type for the 8-bit/sample-rate-reduction character), with an **LFO modulator set to random shape and sync'd to 32nd notes** mapped to filter frequency — match the LFO rate to the note subdivision, and limit its range so it never filters out entirely. Group the chain to keep the dry signal and mix the filtered version in as colour. → [[MIDI Generate and Transform Tools]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]] — Tom Casey Music, [[Tom Casey Music - This One Ableton Hack Makes Drums Sound Insane]]

- **The two-chain crossfade case: LFO → Chain Selector for an automatic morph between exactly two sounds** — group two instruments into a rack (Command/Control+G), drag each chain's zone bars so they overlap across the selector's range (rather than distributing them into separate non-overlapping zones), then map an **LFO** to the Chain Selector for a continuous, automatic crossfade back and forth between the two — slow the LFO **Rate** (~2 or slower) to hear the morph clearly, and try non-triangle LFO shapes (including a random/noise shape) for a jumpier, less predictable morph. The "morph between two specific sounds" application of the same selector mechanism used elsewhere on this page for randomization. → [[Morph]] — You Suck at Producing, [How To Morph One Sound Into Another (4 techniques)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jdXhjfQhk3Y)
- **Scale-locked randomize-and-cherry-pick as an unstuck workflow** — when stuck for a part (bassline, in the source example), run a randomizer with the scale locked to the song's key over a MIDI fragment and scan the results for the one workable moment rather than expecting a full finished idea — most passes are unusable, but it's a faster route to *some* idea than staring at a blank page. → [[Bassline Writing]] — Synthet, [how to randomize a song..](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jn-7yqOFQS8)
- **The Chain Selector, laid bare: a plain 0–127 macro-mappable value picking which rack chain plays** — the mechanism underneath nearly every randomized-rack trick on this page: group anything into a rack (Command/Control+G), open its Chain window, and the blue selector bar spanning 0–127 is itself a mappable macro destination — drag it, or map a macro/Random Generator/LFO to it, and whichever chain's zone currently contains that value plays. — dnksaus, [Chain Selector should be illegal holy sh*t](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U-XT1kPp-Yg)
- **127-chain hard ceiling — narrow an oversized sample/preset dump before distributing** — the selector's range tops out at 127/128 values (MIDI-range-sized); dragging in more chains than that (470 snare samples, in the demo) needs trimming to the first 127/128 before **Distribute Ranges Equally** (Command/Control+A, right-click) spreads them across the full range — extras beyond the ceiling just get deleted from the chain list. Know this before building a huge randomizer rack from an oversized folder dump. → [[Sample Browser and Clips Workflow]] — dnksaus, [Chain Selector should be illegal holy sh*t](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U-XT1kPp-Yg)
- **Chain-selector racks dropped one-per-pad into a Drum Rack for an instantly-randomizable kit** — save a finished sample-selector rack and drag it into a Drum Rack pad; repeat for every pad, and the rack's top-level Random button re-rolls the *entire* kit at once, one new sample per pad, in a single click. Demoed with stock factory samples as a baseline, with the explicit recommendation to swap in your own favorites per pad group — a lighter, sample-only sibling of the fuller macro-reshaping [[Drum Rack|"Sketch Kit"]] documented elsewhere on this page. → [[Drum Rack]] — dnksaus, [Chain Selector should be illegal holy sh*t](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U-XT1kPp-Yg)
- **The same selector trick generalizes to audio-effect presets, not just samples** — build a rack per stock effect (e.g. Chorus) where every one of that effect's factory presets becomes its own chain, so mapping the selector to Random picks a genuinely different *preset*, not just a re-randomized parameter value; built in parallel Standard and Suite editions, plus a "3-in-one" version stacking three preset-randomizer racks in series for compound combinations. → [[Plugin Wishlist]] — dnksaus, [Chain Selector should be illegal holy sh*t](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U-XT1kPp-Yg)
- **A live multi-preset randomizer doesn't remember the winning preset's name, and costs real CPU while every preset sits loaded** — the Chain window only shows numeric position, so landing on a preset you like via Random gives no readable label to recall later; drag that one winning chain/device out of the rack to keep it — which also cuts CPU, since running every preset in the rack live simultaneously (rather than just the currently-selected one) is genuinely CPU-intensive. → [[Gain Staging]] — dnksaus, [Chain Selector should be illegal holy sh*t](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U-XT1kPp-Yg)
- **Shrink a chain's selector range to zero, then redistribute, to temporarily exclude one option without deleting it** — inside an existing multi-chain randomizer rack, to pull one chain out of the random pool without removing it: select all chains, drag that chain's bottom-range marker from right to left and its top marker from left to right (collapsing its zone to nothing), then re-run **Distribute Ranges Equally** so the rest reclaim the freed range — more reversible than deleting the chain outright while auditioning. — dnksaus, [Chain Selector should be illegal holy sh*t](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U-XT1kPp-Yg)
- **A packaged "starter kit" turns the selector trick into a build-your-own randomized synth** — a rack scaffold of multiple randomized effect-preset chains stacked on an instrument: audition live with the random generators on, exclude unwanted chains with the shrink-and-redistribute move above, then once a combination lands, turn off/delete the random generators, drag the winning chain/preset out, and save the result as its own standalone instrument. → [[Plugin Wishlist]] — dnksaus, [Chain Selector should be illegal holy sh*t](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U-XT1kPp-Yg)
- **Random LFOs: map several destinations to independently-randomized LFOs at once, then resample the best pass** — a utility rack that patches multiple parameters (e.g. several controls on a growl/distortion device) each to their own independently-random LFO in a single step, rather than wiring one Random-source device per destination by hand; treat the result as a resample-and-pick-the-best-take tool since it can take a few attempts to land somewhere good. → [[LFO]], [[Plugin Wishlist]] — dnksaus, [Dank Sauces Rack Pack - Demoing Every Rack!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xuKc_a6xxjk)
- **A lead rack built specifically to lean on Live 11's per-rack randomize buttons** — praised because Live 11 added a native randomize control to every rack; a preset built to make fast use of that built-in variation-hunting rather than adding its own custom randomization layer on top. → [[Plugin Wishlist]] — dnksaus, [Dank Sauces Rack Pack - Demoing Every Rack!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xuKc_a6xxjk)
- **Every rack in a starter-pack collection built fully randomizable from the ground up** — across a full instrument/rack pack (kicks, bass, keys, snares, texture layers), every single preset carries a randomize button by design, framed explicitly as a starting-point philosophy: load the rack, hit randomize repeatedly to explore variations, then layer effects on top rather than treating any preset as a finished sound. Reinforces the channel's throughline that generative variation belongs at the rack-design stage, not bolted on afterward. → [[Sound Palette]] — dnksaus, [Instrument Racks + Dank Sauces Updates](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kd16qh8_h4g)
- **Turn noise into tonal chords via root/quality/inversion selection** — a rack ("Color Washer") applied to noisy, non-tonal sources selects a root note and a chord quality (major/minor), with further control over triad-vs-seventh voicing, octave doubling, and which chord tones play (e.g. just root + fifth) — Timbre controlled via a Decay-style knob — turning an unpitched noise source into a played, key-aware chordal texture. A rack-based cousin of the Vinyl-Distortion-into-Auto-Shift generative-tone trick documented elsewhere in this note's source videos. → [[Plugin Wishlist]] — dnksaus, [Instrument Racks + Dank Sauces Updates](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kd16qh8_h4g)
- **Preset-space morph + mutate, without hand-building a two-preset chain-morph rack** — [[Gradient]] (Patches, paid Max for Live) stores two or more full device presets into "corners," then an XY pad morphs continuously between them (with specific parameters excludable from the morph, and any in-between blend saveable as a new corner); a **Mutate** button randomizes to an entirely new preset within that space in one click. A lighter-weight, single-device alternative to building the chain-volume-fade or chain-selector rack patterns documented below when the goal is *preset* variation on one device rather than *which device/chain* fires. Pairs naturally with the hotkey-resample workflow on [[BIP for L9]] — mutate, then resample the keeper instantly. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[BIP for L9]] — dnksaus, [10 Devices You Should Know About (Max For Live)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B0i_S4jbhso)
- **Freeze a randomized velocity pattern into a fixed, repeatable one via Live's own Randomize-velocities command** — rather than a live Random-source device that re-randomizes every playback, draw MIDI notes by hand, select them all, and run Live's built-in **Randomize velocities** command: every note gets a different, but now *fixed*, velocity that repeats identically on replay (re-randomize a single note in isolation if just one lands wrong) — a simpler, device-free route to the same "freeze a generative pass" goal as the Slope-control technique below. → [[Sampler]] — dnksaus, [Sampler is more OP than you might think](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rW6pjAyYHcY)
- **Whole break-beat loops distributed across an Instrument Rack's chains** — the Sampler/Simpler Zone-distribution idea, one level up: group full drum-break loops as separate chains in an Instrument Rack, select them in the Chain List, and **Distribute Ranges Equally** so each key plays back a different whole break rather than a single hit. Each chain still needs its own manual beat-slicing to be sub-loop-playable (no batch "save as default" for that); a workaround for a simple few-notes-per-loop feel is duplicating that note range across an octave (roughly C1–D♯1), or reaching for the [[Expression Control]] Random-to-chain-selector pattern above instead. → [[Sampler]], [[Expression Control]] — dnksaus, [Sampler is more OP than you might think](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rW6pjAyYHcY)
- **Multi-Shaper: randomize eight [[Shaper]] curves at once instead of hand-stacking them** — dnksaus's own Max for Live fix for a genuinely slow stock workflow (mapping several Manual-mode Shapers by hand for one expressive automation patch): one Randomize reshapes all eight curves together, a single control flips all eight between Mod/Remote or bipolar/unipolar, Rotate shifts each shaper's mapping onto the next shaper for instant variation, and Mono forces all eight to share one shape; Manual-mode scrubbing across all eight doubles as a preset browser. **Multi-Shaper MIDI** triggers the same per incoming note instead of per macro turn. Full detail on [[Shaper]] and [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Shaper]] — dnksaus, [I fixed ableton's Shaper](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=45l00IhqVd4)
- **The full generative song-writing rack: Scale → Chord (rack of presets) → Random Generator → instrument** — the channel's fullest expression of this note's throughline. Start with a [[Scale]] device (saved as default so it always loads in the song's key) feeding a [[Chord]] rack (several presets, chain-selector distributed equally and macro-mapped, Strum/Tension/Crescendo mapped to all siblings — see [[Chord]]); drive that chain-selector (plus Strum/Tension/Crescendo) with the **"Random Generator" utility rack** (nine Quick-Mapped [[Expression Control]] instances on Random, some in Mod mode and some in Remote — documented earlier in this note) instead of a single Expression Control, so a different chord preset/spacing/strum fires on every incoming MIDI note. Layer a melody line the same way: [[Arpeggiator]] (Style/Steps/Gate each mapped to their own Random Generator, Style's range restricted so it can't wander into chord-stack mode, Rate restricted to a window like 16th–8th and driven by a **Binary**-mode Random Generator for a two-speed coin flip) into a [[Velocity]] device (Fixed mode, random range ~20–30) for humanized dynamics. A "Bass Lock"-style sub layer (his own device, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) underneath tracks the rack's root/chord automatically. → [[Chord]], [[Scale]], [[Arpeggiator]], [[Velocity]], [[Expression Control]] — dnksaus, [generating music using our BRAINS (+ controlled randomness)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FykE1BOrcOg)
- **Velocity-as-instrument-selector instead of a chain-selector macro** — rather than routing a multi-instrument rack's chain-selector bar to a macro or Random Generator, distribute the rack's chains by **Velocity range** instead and place a fully-randomized [[Velocity]] device right before it — every note's random velocity picks which of 6–8 layered instruments fires, achieving the same "different sound per note" result as the sound-blender chain-selector pattern (see below) through an entirely different signal path. → [[Velocity]] — dnksaus, [generating music using our BRAINS (+ controlled randomness)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FykE1BOrcOg)
- **Comping a generative take from stacked take lanes** — loop a section (Command+L) and record from a generative rack for a while; because it's a loop, each pass stacks as its own take lane (Live 12.2+ shows them via a dedicated button; older versions: right-click → Show All Take Lanes, or the key command). Use the pencil tool (B) to paint in whichever take's notes sound best across the loop, section by section — the standard way to turn a long random-generation session into one usable, curated take. — dnksaus, [generating music using our BRAINS (+ controlled randomness)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FykE1BOrcOg)
- **Scale-quantized generative noise-to-melody rack** — a noise burst ([[Vinyl Distortion]]'s Crackle) into a short, self-oscillating [[Echo]] feedback loop (Feedback past 100%, Modulation LFO on Random) generates a wandering semi-melodic tone hands-off; [[Auto Shift]] (scale-aware, High mode) after it snaps that wandering pitch to the song's actual scale, turning free-running noise into an in-key generative texture rather than random dissonance. Inspired by analyzing Live 12's default-project rain-generator rack (credit: Chuck Sutton) and re-purposed as a tonal version. → [[Echo]], [[Auto Shift]], [[Vinyl Distortion]] — dnksaus, [Scale-Aware Abuse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NZyKN5Wn7A0)
- **Scale-degree chain duplication as a chord-stack generator** — instead of a dedicated chord device, group a pitch/scale-aware device (e.g. [[Auto Shift]]) into a rack, duplicate the chain several times, and set each duplicate's shift in **scale degrees** rather than semitones (e.g. +2, +4, +7/octave, −1) — an always-in-key chord stack built from chain duplication. Distribute the chain-select ranges equally and drag them all to one end of the bar, then drag each duplicate's volume-ramp (white bar) so a single macro gradually brings in more harmony layers as it's turned up — a deliberate, authored version of the chain-volume-fade trick documented elsewhere in this note. → [[Auto Shift]] — dnksaus, [Scale-Aware Abuse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NZyKN5Wn7A0)
- **Follow Action as a hands-off pattern shuffler** — drag a batch of clips (drum-kit pattern previews from the [[Sample Browser and Clips Workflow|Clips tab]], or a curated pile of melodic loops) into Session View, select them all, open the Launch section (Shift+Tab), turn on **Follow Action**, set it to **Unlink** with a short per-clip time (a quarter note for fast switching), set the next-clip behavior to **Any** (random pick from the group) and turn on **Legato** so each switch keeps the current playback position instead of restarting from the top — the result randomly shuffles between the loaded variations hands-off, generating combinations you wouldn't have arranged by hand. → [[Granulator 3]] — dnksaus, [granulate EVERYTHING](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZVLM1XMT6Z0)
- **Live I/O resampling as a generative feedback source** — turning on a device's **I/O** section (e.g. [[Granulator 3]]) lets it resample live input straight from another currently-playing track instead of a static file; pair with an [[LFO]] in **Square** mode set to trigger the resample (capture) automatically on a fixed interval (e.g. every 2 bars, shape skewed) so the source material keeps refreshing itself hands-off — a live-generative feedback loop rather than a one-time freeze. Credit: Nicholas Théo's channel. — dnksaus, [granulate EVERYTHING](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZVLM1XMT6Z0)
- **Keymapped "insert random stock device" + the 3-random-devices creative-block exercise** — a free Max for Live device (built on Live 12.3's newly-exposed ability to insert stock devices into a chain via the M4L API) spawns a random *stock* Ableton device on a keymapped shortcut, with a configurable exclude-list, insertion position (left/right of the selected device, or always last), no-repeat-until-every-device-has-spawned behavior, and support for custom default presets so a randomly-spawned device (e.g. Vocoder) opens on *your* preferred starting patch rather than its factory default. The creative case: Ableton-community figure Chuck Sutton's exercise — spawn three random devices and sculpt something out of them across five notes — as a deliberate constraint against reaching for the same habitual effects, and a way to rediscover under-used stock devices (Cabinet, specifically). The capstone expression of this whole channel's randomization throughline: where earlier tricks randomize *parameters* on a chosen device, this randomizes *which device* is even in the chain. — dnksaus, [randomly insert plugins (live 12.3)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9qzAiUUHveQ)
- **The "Sketch Kit" — a self-randomizing drum kit for fast idea capture** — build a [[Drum Rack]] up front from your own proven, favorite samples, with every pad wired so a single Randomize press both picks a new sample per pad (via each pad's own [[Sampler]] chain-selector macro) *and* reshapes each chosen sample (via macro-mapped ADSR/transient/pitch/start-point controls) simultaneously — a handful of go-to samples becomes a huge combinatorial space, loadable at the start of any project so you're never blocked curating sounds before an idea gets down. Push-compatible via its randomize wheel. → [[Drum Rack]], [[Sampler]] — dnksaus, [the last drum kit you'll build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfOPVl0tzCE)
- **Exclude specific macros from rack-wide randomization** — a Randomize button/wheel can be configured to skip individual macros — reserve that exclusion for broad, deliberate creative choices (e.g. a synth's Global Time, or overall kit tuning) that you'd rather set by hand, while leaving per-sample micro-variation macros (start point, transpose, decay) in the randomizable pool. — dnksaus, [the last drum kit you'll build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfOPVl0tzCE)
- **Inverted-range macro as a rack "kill switch"** — set a macro's minimum to 1 and maximum to 0 (inverted) so the linked rack/chain is fully off at the macro's zero/default position, and moving the macro gradually brings it back in — a one-knob way to A/B or performance-fade a whole processing stage. — dnksaus, [the last drum kit you'll build](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfOPVl0tzCE)
- **The "sound blender" — randomized multi-instrument rack** — group ~8 deliberately unrelated sounds (piano, organs, Juno emulations, strings, vocals — "think of it as a sound smoothie: pick ingredients that taste good together") into one Instrument Rack, select all the chain zones in the Chain Editor, right-click **Distribute Ranges Equally**, then map that chain-select bar to a macro. Add an [[Expression Control]] (Source = Random, maxed, Remote mode) mapped to the same bar so a *different instrument fires on every MIDI note* — the whole rack becomes a randomized instrument blender. Watch CPU: 8 simultaneous instruments cost far more than cycling presets within one synth (Serum/Diva/Omnisphere). → [[Expression Control]] — dnksaus, [looper is actually a SOUND BLENDER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fDTkW7H4FaE)
- **The "Random Generator" utility rack** — instead of wiring up a Random-source Expression Control by hand on every parameter you want randomized, build one rack with **nine Expression Control instances** (Source = Random) on a single page, Quick-Map each in one pass, and set some to **Mod** mode (blends with the existing value/automation) vs. **Remote** mode (fully overrides) depending on whether the target should stay manually tweakable elsewhere. → [[Expression Control]] — dnksaus, [looper is actually a SOUND BLENDER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fDTkW7H4FaE)
- **Random Generator's Slope control smooths the jump to the next random value** — rather than snapping instantly to a new random value on every trigger, a Slope control glides from the current value toward the next one, softening the Random Generator rack's per-note jumps into something more like a portamento than a hard switch. Once a generative pass produces a keeper, route the rack's MIDI output to a new track and record it — freezing that one random take into fixed, repeatable MIDI (see [[Automation]]). → [[Expression Control]], [[Automation]] — dnksaus, [Expressive Chords Trick - Live 12.2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lEUkcbhdDqo)
- **Bias a coin-flip with a Shaper instead of a flat 50/50** — route a [[Shaper]] in Manual mode (a simple hand-drawn shape) into the chain, then map a Random-mode LFO to Shaper's manual-value knob; skewing the LFO's max value past 50% biases the odds of a triggered event (e.g. whether a resurfaced loop plays) rather than leaving it a hard coin flip. → [[Shaper]] — dnksaus, [looper is actually a SOUND BLENDER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fDTkW7H4FaE)
- **Chase Bliss Habit-style self-mutating resurfacing rack** — a long-buffer (16-bar) [[Looper]] at the start of a chain, grouped into a rack with the dry signal muted by default and a manual dry chain restoring pass-through, with a Binary LFO through a **Volume Macro** (Utility → Gain mapped to a macro, capped at 0 dB) driving how often ~30-second-old material resurfaces faintly over the current signal — a generative "tape memory" effect built entirely from stock devices. → [[Looper]] — dnksaus, [looper is actually a SOUND BLENDER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fDTkW7H4FaE)
- **Binary LFO as a macro "coin flip"** — an [[LFO]] set to **Binary** mode outputs only the mapped parameter's minimum or maximum value each cycle (a hard toggle, not a sweep); map it in Remote mode to a rack macro (e.g. an octave-range Speed knob, -12/+12) for constant, hands-off A/B switching. Raise the LFO's Smooth so flips aren't a hard click. Stack a second Binary LFO mapped to the first LFO's own **Depth** to randomize how far it swings, and a third (lower Smooth = more click-like) mapped to a toggle parameter (e.g. [[Looper]]'s Reverse) for random direction flips — note that toggling mid-phrase can generate audible clicks, which is sometimes the point and sometimes not. → [[LFO]] — dnksaus, [delay just became an AUTO SLICER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zAjmcohzIwc) & [looper is actually a SOUND BLENDER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fDTkW7H4FaE)
- **The chain-volume-fade dry/wet macro** — inside a Rack's Chain window, each chain's volume automation shows as a white bar on top of its blue chain-select zone; drag two chains' white bars to opposite ends of the selector range and shift them past each other by one so each chain fully mutes outside its own end. That range, mapped to a macro, becomes a genuine dry/wet crossfade knob between two whole chains (e.g. a dry passthrough chain vs. a fully-wet FX chain) with no separate Utility/gain-blend device needed. → [[Randomized Rack Design]] — dnksaus, [delay just became an AUTO SLICER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zAjmcohzIwc)
- **Recreate a device's built-in Ducking on any custom wet chain** — put a Compressor at the end of the wet chain, turn on external Sidechain sourced from the track's **"rack pre-effects"** signal (the dry input before the rack), dial Threshold (Peak mode) + Release — the wet chain ducks under the dry source and blooms back in the gaps, the general-purpose version of what [[Echo]]'s native Ducking control does. → [[Sidechaining]] — dnksaus, [delay just became an AUTO SLICER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zAjmcohzIwc)

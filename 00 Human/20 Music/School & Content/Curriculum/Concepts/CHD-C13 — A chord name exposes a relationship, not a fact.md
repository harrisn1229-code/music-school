# CHD-C13 — A chord name exposes a relationship, not a fact

**Q.** When one sonority admits several valid names, what decides which name is correct?

> [!question]- Answer
> **The name is chosen to expose a relationship, not to state a fact.** The right name is the one that reflects the sound you actually hear and the voice leading as it resolves — not the one with the fewest accidentals, and not the one a chord chart would print. Where a shape has several valid names, the choice is an analytical convenience, so pick the one that predicts where you are going.

## Why it matters

The same four notes are Cmaj7/E in the key of C and Em(add♭6) with E as the tonal centre. C-E-G-A is C6 or Am7. Em6 is a half-diminished a third away or a rootless dominant 9th a fourth up — and **each name implies a different resolution**, which is the whole payload. A diminished 7th has four names, an augmented triad three, and neither collection changes. Chord quality comes from the actual intervals, not from the case of a Roman numeral: an uppercase or lowercase symbol signals position and function, and the stacked intervals decide major, minor or dominant.

## Drill

Take a chord of your own that needed a slash to write down. Name it two different ways, and for each name say what it predicts about the chord that follows.

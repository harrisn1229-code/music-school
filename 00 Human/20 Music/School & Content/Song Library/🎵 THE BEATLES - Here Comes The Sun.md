# Here Comes The Sun — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

George's pastoral, and the one Beatles track where metrical sleight-of-hand is the main event: a folk-shaped run of verse/refrain pairs wrapped around a bridge built from a three-bar phrase in **11/8 + 4/4 + 7/8**, repeated six times. Pollack's angle is that the odd meters aren't decoration but a tempo illusion — eighth-note groupings that make the beat appear to stretch and snap back while the underlying pulse never moves.

**Form.** The intro is the four-bar verse phrase played twice, instrumental, so the tune isn't given away early. The refrain is an odd **9 bars parsed 5 + 4**, closed in shape but ending on V, with its final bar in 7/8. The bridge repeats its three-bar phrase six times, then drops the 7/8 on the last pass and holds V for four bars of 4/4 to load the final verse.

**The meter trick.** In the refrain, even eighths are accented in groups of three against the 4/4 backbeat and the count balances exactly — twelve plus four is sixteen eighths, two bars — so the disorientation resolves on schedule ([[RHY-C04 — Polyrhythm and hemiola]]). The bridge runs the same idea and **refuses to let it balance**: 11/8 heard as a wobbly four-beat bar where three beats run half again as long and the fourth snaps back, the 4/4 re-establishing the original tempo, the 7/8 stretching again. Metric modulation, not just an odd bar count.

**Harmony.** Plain material deployed so cadences pile up. The bridge is the "Hey Jude" double plagal pushed one more fourth around the circle into a **triple plagal** approach ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]), open at both ends — which is why six repeats never sound finished.

**Melody.** Verse and refrain sit almost entirely inside one pentatonic set ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]). Monotony is dodged by **reharmonising repeated fragments rather than varying them** — the title phrase carries different chords across its first two appearances.

**Arrangement.** Guitar, Moog, bass, drums in order of entry, with entrances staggered on purpose. Vocal density is the second dial: single-tracked, then overdubbed, then harmonised across successive verses.

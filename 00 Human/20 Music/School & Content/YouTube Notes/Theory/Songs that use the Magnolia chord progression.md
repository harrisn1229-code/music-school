# Songs that use the Magnolia chord progression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UMiRm8NS1ok)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UMiRm8NS1ok)

## Synopsis

A progression named (by jazz-circle convention, not Bennett) after a 1928 tune — I–I7–IV–iv — surveyed across Beatles tracks, older standards, and a few reharmonized variants (an augmented substitution, a pedal point, and a half-diminished swap), each keeping the same essential top-line motion.

## Techniques

- **The progression: I–I7–IV–iv** (C–C7–F–Fm).
- **I7 is a secondary dominant** (V of IV), pulling into F; **iv (minor four)** then guides back home via a minor plagal cadence — same closing device as the Creep progression's IV→iv→I.
- **A genuine top-voice line runs through the whole loop**: C→B♭→A→A♭(→G), independent of which register it's actually voiced in — this stepwise motion is a big part of why the progression feels connected rather than four separate chords.
- **Reharm 1 — augmented substitution**: swap the plain iv for a ♭VI augmented triad built on the same notes minus the root (same essential sound, different bass note) — heard in "Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds" (The Beatles).
- **Reharm 2 — pedal point**: hold one bass note under all four chords (every chord in the progression shares that scale degree as a chord tone) for a grounded-yet-tense quality — heard in "That's the Way of the World" (Earth, Wind & Fire, D♭ pedal).
- **Reharm 3 — half-diminished swap**: replace iv with a ii7♭5 built a third above it (the same notes as a first-inversion vi6 chord, just reordered) — heard in "White Christmas."
- **Songs**: "Dear Prudence," "Magical Mystery Tour," "Hold Me Tight" (The Beatles); "What Goes Around" (Ringo Starr); "When the Saints Go Marching In"; "Santa Claus Is Comin' to Town"; "When the Swallows Come Back to Capistrano"; "The Fear" and other tracks (Lily Allen).

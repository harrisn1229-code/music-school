# Back In The USSR — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

An album opener built as a palate cleanser — straight hard rock after two years of studio experiments — stacked with deliberate references (the Beach Boys everywhere, Chuck Berry underneath the conceit). The analytically useful claim is that it is **neither a twelve-bar blues nor four-square**: it takes the blues chord budget and none of the blues form.

**Form.** The intro pounds four bars on V with mounting pressure to resolve, and pointedly declines to repeat its own syncopated whack in the last two bars. Verses are four-square 8s; the refrain is an unusual **6 bars** whose last phrase exists in two versions depending on what follows; the bridge is the loosest section, always carrying a two-bar extension. The 6-bar refrain and the elastic bridge are what keep a three-chord rocker from sounding like a grid ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]).

**Harmony.** I, IV and V, with **♭III carrying more weight than any of them**. The harmonic rhythm is slow enough that you can hear ♭III play two different roles ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]], [[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]): parked between two statements of IV in the verse, so it reads as hesitation; and in the refrain a sub-subdominant creeping up on IV from below. The recurring chromatic bass fills work as a motif in their own right and the bridge backing vocals double them.

**Melody.** Very bluesy, leaning hard on the flattened third against A major harmony so a cross-relation sounds most of the time ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]). The melodic seventh is avoided completely in the vocal and handed to the sawtooth guitar riff instead — the note is in the song without ever being in the tune.

**Arrangement.** A thick overdubbed patchwork choreographed section by section, one element added at a time. Handclaps exist from the first refrain but are only pushed forward at the end, which is cheaper than adding a new part.

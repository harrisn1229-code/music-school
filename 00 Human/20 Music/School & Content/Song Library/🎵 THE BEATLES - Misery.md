# Misery — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4) · Verse (8) · Verse · Bridge (8, slower harmonic rhythm) · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Outro (the verse's last two bars looped to a fade). Standard two-bridge model with **no instrumental verse** — the verses are so closed and chord-poor that a solo chorus would have felt airless.

A four-chord song (IV, V, I, vi) that gets its variety from how **one** chord is used: the vi works as subdominant, as dominant substitute, and as plain relative minor at different points ([[HAR-C01 — The three functions: T, SD, D]]). The intro starts away from the tonic and converges fast, pre-stating almost everything — unison singing opening into harmony, decorative piano, the I–vi–V cell.

The verse's best move is rhythmic: the IV is held across the bar 4–5 divide so the chord change **straddles the phrase boundary instead of marking it**, which is harmonic-rhythm syncopation wrung out of a progression with nothing else going on ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). One formula worth noting — the verse's last bar goes to vi when another verse follows, and stays on I when a bridge follows.

The verse tune is short, jumpy and pentatonic in its first half, with the remaining scale degrees held back for the second; the bridge inverts that with a stepwise descent through a full octave ([[MEL-C01 — Melodic contour]]). The piano edit pieces are the first outside performance dropped onto a Beatles track to supply something the band could not play. John's unaccompanied outro ad-libs land because they are the first solo voice in the song.

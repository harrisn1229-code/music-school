# What is the most overused song title?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vmQ1Qoy0OgY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vmQ1Qoy0OgY)

## Synopsis

A countdown of the most-reused song titles (via a Wikipedia/Spotify cross-check), used to make one songwriting point: titles are uncopyrightable, and the most-reused ones are the most generic/universal sentiments ("Hold On," "I Want You," "Breathe," "Home," "Without You" — the top five, all over 100 uses each, "Hold On" over 300) versus a distinctive title like "Bohemian Rhapsody" or "Strawberry Fields Forever," which never gets reused because it's too specific to the one song it names.

## Techniques

- **A song title is the listener's first impression, and in the streaming era that impression often lands before the song is ever heard (playlists, search, recommendation feeds) — a strong argument for a distinctive title over a generic, easily-forgotten one**, even though a generic title costs nothing legally (song titles aren't copyrightable) and plenty of successful songs use one anyway.
- **A title doesn't need to appear anywhere in the lyric to be effective** — "Bohemian Rhapsody" is never sung in the song itself, yet is one of the most recognizable, specific titles that exists; distinctiveness comes from the phrase's own character, not from lyrical repetition.

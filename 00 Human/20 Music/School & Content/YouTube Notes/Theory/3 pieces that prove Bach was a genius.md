# 3 pieces that prove Bach was a genius

## Synopsis

Three worked examples of Bach's contrapuntal technique — a retrograde canon, a "riddle canon" that has to be decoded before it can be performed, and a fugue exposition — used to make concrete what "Bach was a genius" actually means in craft terms: everything heard is derived from one small kernel of material through strict, learnable procedures.

## Techniques

- **Retrograde canon ("crab canon" / *Canon Cancrizans*): the second voice plays the exact same melody as the first, but backwards, starting from the opposite end and meeting in the middle** — not a *new* countermelody, the literal same notes read right-to-left. Bach didn't notate it as two staves; the original manuscript is a single line of music with a reversed clef and reversed key signature at the end as the only instruction that it must also be read backwards simultaneously. Built on a royal theme given to Bach by the King of Prussia as a compositional challenge, adding the constraint that the retrograde-canon trick had to work around a pre-existing melody rather than one Bach invented himself.
- **Puzzle/riddle canon (*Canon a 2*): the score withholds performance information and the player has to solve it before playing.** Marked *Quaerendo invenietis* ("seek and you will find"). The clues here: the title states it's for two players; the two clefs used differ, and one bass clef is printed upside-down, meaning the second player reads the same notes from the opposite direction (the first voice reads C–D–E♭ on the alto clef; the second reads the identical notes as D–C–B♭ off the inverted bass clef). The score doesn't state WHEN the second voice enters — that has to be worked out by ear/harmony: testing entry points, the only bar where the two voices don't clash is bar 4, which is the "solution."
- **Fugue exposition, mechanically named parts**: **subject** (the short opening melody the whole piece derives from) → **answer** (the subject restated in the second voice, transposed up a fifth to the dominant — subject in C minor, answer in G minor) → **countersubject** (new material the first voice plays *underneath* the answer, which itself recurs later in the piece, including inverted/contrary motion) → subsequent entries build via **sequence** (a fragment — here, the subject's first four notes — repeated a step higher each time). Worked example: the C minor fugue, Well-Tempered Clavier Book 1.
- **The underlying principle stated directly, worth carrying as a general definition of strong counterpoint**: independent melodic lines (the horizontal axis) that also combine into coherent harmony when stacked (the vertical axis) — "logical and beautiful, complex and emotive" at the same time, not one traded off against the other.

# Zach Scholz - I Tried Recording a Song Like It's 1984

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iQ1QHsdhsiA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iQ1QHsdhsiA)

## Synopsis
Zach Scholz builds a track entirely in "period-authentic" 1984 layers — drum machine first, then synth bass, synthetic keys, a brass layer, and a synth lead — then prints the finished digital mix through a real early-80s reel-to-reel (a newly acquired TASCAM 34, run alongside his existing TASCAM 30) and compares the tape-processed version against the pure digital mix for viewers to weigh in on. The video doubles as a demo of the TASCAM 34's headline features — 15 in/s high-fidelity playback and a pitch-control knob for varispeed tempo/pitch tricks — and sits inside an ongoing era-recreation series on his channel (he cross-references an earlier "recording a song like it's 1966" video and a companion video on how the 80s sound was built). The through-line is that the 80s sound comes as much from workflow constraints — drum-machine-led arrangement, all-synthetic harmonic layers, tape as the final glue step — as from any single piece of gear.

## Techniques
- Frames the whole video as an A/B experiment: builds the song entirely ITB in "period-authentic" layers, then at the end prints the finished mix out to a real early-80s reel-to-reel and compares that tape pass against the pure digital mix — the hardware version of the same mix-bus "tape finish" move documented on [[Kramer Tape]], done for real instead of with a plugin.
- New-to-him deck for this video is a TASCAM 34 (run right next to his existing TASCAM 30) — the standout spec is that it runs at 15 in/s, giving noticeably higher fidelity than his other reel-to-reels; lines up with the [[Kramer Tape]] Tape Speed rule (faster ips = tighter/cleaner low end + extended top vs. the warmer, more head-bump character of a slower speed).
- Also calls out the TASCAM 34's pitch-control knob as a feature he's planning to lean on going forward: slowing or speeding the tape shifts tempo and pitch together (true analog varispeed) — the same speed-changes-the-sound principle behind [[Sony TC-630]]'s slower-speed setting sounding warmer/less hi-fi, except used here as a deliberate creative tool rather than a side effect.
- Finishing-pass signal path: final mix out of the DAW into the TASCAM 34's input, printed at the deck's fastest speed (15 in/s), then tape playback captured back into the computer for the tape-vs-digital A/B — print-and-reprint, not real-time tape monitoring inside the mix.
- Drum foundation is a [[Linn LM-1]] drum machine, picked as the period-correct choice — he frames drum machines like it as historically pivotal because they let songwriters build a whole track by looping a programmed pattern instead of needing a live drummer; the beat gets programmed first and every other layer is built on top of it.
- Bass layer is a Minimoog patch: main oscillator set to square, the other oscillators switched to saw and blended in underneath, cutoff dialed in and resonance pushed up (which also thins out some low end) — a classic layered-oscillator analog synth-bass build, occupying the [[BASS Slot]].
- Keys layer is deliberately "synthetic" sounding rather than acoustic — period-correct go-tos he names: Jupiter-8, Juno, Yamaha DX7, and Prophet, plus Arturia's VST recreation of the Casio CZ-101 (a digital synth he calls "corny" in isolation but says still sounds good sitting inside a full mix) — the [[BED Slot]] harmony layer under the track.
- Adds a brass-synth layer on a Prophet 5 to complement the keys: chorus added, reverb added, and the amp envelope's attack slowed down — sits in the [[MIDS Slot]] alongside the keys bed.
- Lead part goes back to the Minimoog: three sawtooth oscillators running together with one of them pitched up and mapped to the mod wheel, so pushing the wheel works like a vibrato/character control while he plays the part into the open space in the arrangement — the [[LEAD Slot]] occupant for this mix.

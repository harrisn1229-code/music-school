# Ask Me Why — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

The most over-engineered form in the early catalogue. It's a mutated two-bridge model in which **the verse exists in three versions, each cut for a different structural job**, and Pollack's argument is that the corny Latin backbeat makes the craft easy to miss.

**Form.** The initial verse runs 13 bars and ends **open on V**, so it can only lead to another verse. A first variant is identical until its last three bars, then rewritten to close and hand off to the bridge — it appears exactly once. A much shorter second variant alludes to the verse rather than restating it, carries the title phrase and functions as a refrain, appearing three times and altered each time. Everything enters on a pickup from the middle of the previous bar.

**Harmony.** Solidly E major, leaning toward the relative minor mid-verse. The engine is **stepwise streams of parallel sevenths** rather than plain triads. The build works by the harmony changing character: streams give way to root motion, the harmonic rhythm broadens, and a V⁹ lands just before the melodic peak ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). Secondary dominants do the lifting — V-of-vi, V-of-V and an augmented tonic acting as V♯5-of-IV ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). The first variant swaps a borrowed minor iv in where the initial verse had major IV ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The refrain variant is deliberately weak — streams plus a plagal IV–I ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]) — so it powers the song down instead of climbing, and on its second pass its final bar is swapped for the augmented tonic, buying a clean run into the second bridge without replaying the whole first variant.

**Melody.** Almost everything sits inside one octave; only the initial verse's last phrase breaks out to a falsetto peak. First pass John descends from high G♯; on the repeat he goes up to high B — a small masterclass in holding something back.

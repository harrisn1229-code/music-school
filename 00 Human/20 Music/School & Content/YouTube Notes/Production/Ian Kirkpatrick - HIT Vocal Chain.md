# Ian Kirkpatrick — HIT Vocal Chain

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LMjkfMIhXdA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LMjkfMIhXdA)

## Synopsis

Ian walks the lead-vocal chain on "Don't Stop Caring About Me," where the vocal's personality comes from **chopping** it and **smashing it with compression**. He shows the chopped "don't come out" hook (a chop that stops *before* the snare), the LFO Tool duck that automates on at the drop, and the full insert order: clean-sweep EQ → UAD 1176-style smash → responsible OTT → multiband → Pultec highs boost → de-ess. Ends on the demo-vocal lesson and why Pultec is his transparent EQ of choice.

## Techniques

- **Personality comes from chopping + heavy compression** — the vocal "gets its personality from the chopping and the fact that it's getting smashed to bits by the compressor." Chop aggressively, then compress hard. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Compression]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]]
- **Chop that stops *before* the snare** — he cut the "don't" so it ends before the downbeat rather than clipping on the snare — you hear it stop ("a little *oh what the—*"). → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Automate the LFO Tool duck ON at the drop** — the ducking switches on exactly when the chorus hits, so the chopped vocal pumps only in the hook. → [[LFO Tool]], [[Sidechaining]], [[Automation]]
- **Clean-sweep EQ first** — an EQ to remove sub-audible low end (visible 22–25 Hz energy) so the compressor reacts to what you can hear, "not some invisible bass." → [[EQ]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[SUB Slot]]
- **Slight high boost** — a small top-end lift "because it felt like it should have one." → [[EQ]], [[AIR Slot]]
- **UAD 1176-style "smash" — hold shift and smash the buttons (all-buttons-in)** — the British-mode all-buttons distortion for aggressive, colored reduction; reduces the vocal "as much as it can" and distorts a little. → [[UAD UA 1176LN]], [[Compression]], [[Parallel Processing]]
- **"Responsible" OTT** — a light OTT (multiband up/down) after the smash for glue, kept modest. → [[OTT]], [[Multiband Compression]]
- **Pultec near the end — boost highs at 10k, then de-ess** — "this plugin has a lot to do with what comes after it": boost 10 kHz air with the Pultec, then de-ess to tame the added sibilance. → [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]], [[De-essing]], [[AIR Slot]]
- **Printed doubles with MicroShift, from the demo** — the doubles are the *demo* vocals (done in the room, less "spin"/effort) with SoundToys MicroShift; he kept them because the relaxed demo delivery sounded better. → [[Little MicroShift]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[2 Backup Vocals Group]]
- **Pultec to soften drum highs too** — same Pultec attenuates ~10k on the drums and boosts ~8k, purely to soften the high end — his go-to transparent EQ. → [[UAD Pultec EQP-1A]], [[EQ]], [[Kick Design]]

## Philosophy

- **"When Dua puts her voice on a record, it's a different record."** The demo delivery — natural, un-forced — often beats the "trying" take. Keep honest performances. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - Using AutoTune to Craft Hit Songs]]
- **Chain order matters as a system** — each plugin sets up the next (Pultec boost → de-ess), not a bag of independent moves. → [[Compression Staging]]

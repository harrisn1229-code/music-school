## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — For You Blue]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #175.*

**Form.** The plainest song on the album, and one of very few Beatles originals where **every single section is a strict twelve-bar blues frame** ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]). Six frames, a six-bar intro with a three-beat pickup, a tacked-on ending, and nothing else. George plays the intro alone; the band enters for the first verse; the two instrumental breaks sit back to back, slide first, piano second.

**Harmony.** I, IV and V plus one cameo of V-of-V in the intro. `Intro (6): I – IV6/3 – V/V – V (2 bars)` · `Verse (12): I – IV – I – I · IV – IV – I – I · V – IV – I – V`. George uses the **quick-change variant**, putting IV in bar 2 so the usually static opening phrase has somewhere to go ([[BLU-C03 — Quick change, 8-bar and minor blues]]), and ends the frame on **V rather than I** so each section pushes into the next instead of closing. Bar 11 carries the standard chromatically rising bass D – F♯ – G – G♯ – A. Running IV straight into V-of-V in the intro exposes a cross relation the band usually spelled in the opposite order.

**Notable.** The tune is wide and jumpy — a tenth, F natural up to A — and saturated with **blue thirds and sevenths** ([[BLU-C06 — Blue notes and the blues scale]]). Its signature move is a **rising leap of a sixth** in the first phrase, then followed through as triadic leaps that also span a sixth. The slide solo picks up the tune's jumpiness without quoting it; the piano solo opens with chopped chords descending the blues scale. Two things on the release aren't from the take: John's spoken introduction is flown in, and George's lead vocal was overdubbed a full year later, in January 1970.

**Against this page.** Pollack reads it in **D major** — which matches this page's own Chords section (A shapes, capo 5) but not the `key:` field, which says G major. Nothing above has been changed.

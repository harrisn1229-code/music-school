# 3 Minor key chord progressions you should know

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XCzZHPD1qKk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XCzZHPD1qKk)

## Synopsis

Three minor-key progressions, organized by which minor-scale "flavor" (or blend of flavors) each one draws from — Aeolian/Dorian, Aeolian/harmonic minor, and pure Aeolian — building a small palette of minor-key moods.

## Techniques

- **"Minor climb" / "House of the Rising Sun" progression — i–III–IV–V** (Am–C–D–[E], the opening of "House of the Rising Sun") — a walking-up-the-scale feel, mixing natural minor (i, III) with a Dorian-borrowed IV chord (D major requires F♯, the raised 6th degree Dorian has but natural minor doesn't) for a moment of extra brightness inside an otherwise minor passage.
- **Aeolian–harmonic-minor blend — i–iv–III–V** (Am–Dm–C–E) — the first three chords stay in plain natural minor; the closing V chord borrows harmonic minor's raised 7th (G♯) to become a genuine dominant, adding drama and pulling hard back to the top of the loop.
- **"Aeolian closed loop" — i–iv–i** (Am–Dm–Am) — pure natural minor, and a CLOSED loop: it begins and ends on the tonic, so — unlike most short loops that need to repeat before the ear feels resolved — this one already resolves within a single pass.

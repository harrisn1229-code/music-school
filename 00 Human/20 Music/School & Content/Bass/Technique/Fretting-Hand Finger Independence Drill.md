# Fretting-Hand Finger Independence Drill

BassBuzz's "Finger Fixer" exercise — a two-bar fretting-hand pattern built on the **B minor scale** shape, aimed squarely at beginner fretting-hand weak points: mixed-up notes, a weak ring finger, an even weaker pinky, and a fretting hand that "looks like a lost and undecided spider" from uncoordinated finger choices. Framed as the jump-start exercise of a three-exercise beginner routine (see [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]] for exercise two, [[Bass Note Muting Technique]] for exercise three) — this one builds finger strength/coordination to a level solid enough to tackle common beginner songs, and doubles as a plucking-hand wake-up since every fretted note still has to be plucked.

## The pattern

Assumes the **B minor scale** shape is already known (taught in BassBuzz's own beginner course) — most of the fingerings below reuse fretting patterns from that shape rather than introducing new ones, so the exercise is really about **coordination and strength**, not new note-finding. See [[Bass Scale Shapes — Major, Minor, Pentatonic & Blues]] for the general movable minor-scale shape and fingering this exercise draws on.

- **Bar 1:** ring–pinky–index–ring–pinky–ring–middle–pinky.
- **Bar 2:** ring–pinky–index–ring–pinky–index–ring (the "weird one" — an intentionally less-natural finger order than bar 1, there specifically to break rote-pattern autopilot).
- **Keep fingers low to the frets** throughout — the fix that actually delivers the strength/accuracy payoff; fingers hovering high above the fretboard is the beginner default this exercise is designed to correct.
- **Bar 2's ring-finger curl-back** is flagged as the hard part: the ring finger has to **curl back** rather than reach straight down, a motion most beginners haven't built yet. Isolate and slow down specifically at that finger if the rest of the bar is fine.

## How to practice it

- **Slow the tempo to 50–75% of target speed**, or **isolate one bar at a time**, before attempting the pattern at full speed and as a whole — don't force full-speed, both-bars playthroughs before the ring-finger curl and the finger order are separately solid.
- Count-in 1-2-3-4 and play through; the exercise is short enough (two bars) to loop repeatedly inside a 10-minute session.

## Where it sits in the routine

Positioned as the **first** of a three-exercise, 10-minute daily bass routine (morning is the video's suggested slot, purely for consistency — before "kids and dogs and chores interfere" — not because the exercises are time-of-day-dependent). Early sessions might spend the whole 10 minutes on this one exercise alone; as it solidifies, fold in exercise two ([[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]]) and exercise three ([[Bass Note Muting Technique]]) for a more rounded session. Claimed 30-day outcomes from BassBuzz's own student testers: stronger fingers (pinky included), more accurate note-hitting, less "flailing and flying fingers," fewer wrong-string hits, and better muting (the last of those is exercise three's territory, not this one's).

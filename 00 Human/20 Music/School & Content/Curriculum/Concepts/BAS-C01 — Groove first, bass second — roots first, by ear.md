# BAS-C01 — Groove first, bass second — roots first, by ear

**Q.** What are the first two steps of writing a bassline, and what's the trap in each?

> [!question]- Answer
> **1. Finish the drum groove, then write the bass around it.** The trap: bass notes do **not** have to start on the kicks — lock to the pocket, not the kick map. Rhythmic choices made with no drums playing are provisional and a real loop will overrule them. **2. Land the root notes under the chords before any decoration** — by ear. The trap: if you can't hear the roots, that's an ear-training gap, not a writing gap. The beginner-tier version is genuinely one root per bar on each chord change; quality doesn't matter yet because only the root is sounding.

## Why it matters

Almost every bad bassline is one of these two steps skipped. Register is the third trap in the same breath: the identical root line an octave too low goes from fine to undefined mud — move it up rather than processing it.

## Drill

Write a bassline over C–Am–F–G as one root per bar. Then add subdivision. Then add the octave. Stop there.

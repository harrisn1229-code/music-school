> [!danger]- 🔒 Answer key — do not open until you have answered every question
> **Q1.** **Key and time signature · roadmap · range · rhythm hotspots · a tempo you can hold**
> In that order, and the order is part of the answer.
>
> **Q2.** **Key and time signature — it's the half that doesn't prevent the derailment**
> Knowing the key stops wrong accidentals. It does nothing about repeats you didn't see or a tempo you can't sustain.
>
> **Q3.** **Repeats, 1st and 2nd endings, D.S., Coda**
> Anything that makes the music play in an order other than left to right.
>
> **Q4.** **The highest and lowest note — it decides which position holds the whole phrase**
> Choose the position before you start and you avoid a shift mid-phrase, which is where reads collapse.
>
> **Q5.** **Tuplets, syncopation and dotted figures — count them silently during the scan**
> Finding them in the scan means the first time you meet them isn't at tempo.
>
> **Q6.** **The hardest bar**
> *Also accepted:* not the easiest · the most difficult passage
>
> **Q7.** **Keep the pulse — thin it out and rejoin at the next downbeat. Never stop, never go back**
> Drop notes, thin the chord, or play only the downbeats until you catch up. Continuity is the thing being trained.
>
> **Q8.** **Because it converts the exercise into a different one, and trains hesitation**
> *Rubric (5):* says sight reading trains continuity under pressure, not accuracy (2) · says slowing down removes the pressure that the exercise exists to create (2) · says the habit it builds — stopping to fix — is the one that ruins a live read (1)
> Practice and sight reading are two different exercises that look similar. Practice is for accuracy, and slowing down is exactly right there. A sight-reading rep is training the ability to keep going through material you can't fully play, because that's the situation the skill exists for. Dropping the tempo until everything is playable removes the very condition being trained, and worse, it rehearses stopping and fixing — which is the habit that ends a performance when there's a band who won't wait.
>
> **Q9.** **The test reaches only the reference layer; the skill is perceptual-motor and needs reps**
> *Rubric (5):* says reading is a perceptual-motor skill (2) · says a text test can only measure the facts around it, not the doing (2) · names the daily lesson plans or the practice lane as where the skill actually comes from (1)
> Everything a written test can reach — dots, navigation signs, the steps of the scan — is knowledge *about* reading. The skill itself is the real-time conversion of symbol into motion at tempo, which no amount of correct answers demonstrates and no note can install. It's built by cold-reading new material daily through the lesson plans in [[Sight Reading]], which run in the `theory` practice lane. Passing this test means the reference layer is in place so the reps aren't blocked; it doesn't mean you can read.

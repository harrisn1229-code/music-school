# Roland JD-800

## What it is
Roland's apology for the menu-diving 80s — a huge digital synth covered in **59 sliders**, one per parameter, marrying D-50-style sample-plus-synthesis voices to a panel you could play like an instrument. It became the sound of 90s rave pianos and trance leads.

## What makes the sound
- **Four-tone patches**: up to four sample-based partials per voice, each with its own resonant digital filter and envelopes — dense, glassy stacks with real motion, brighter and bigger than the [[Roland D-50]].
- **The slider panel invited performance**: filter sweeps and envelope grabs recorded straight into tracks — rare for 1991 digital synths and part of why JD sounds feel alive.
- **The preset canon**: the **"Ac.Piano" bright digital piano** became *the* rave/trance piano — hard, bell-like, cuts through any mix; plus huge hoover-adjacent stacks and crystal pads.
- Onboard multi-effects glaze everything in early-90s gloss.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Nexus]] (the trance-ROMpler descendant — rave piano/supersaw territory lives there), [[Omnisphere]], [[Serum 2]]/[[DUNE 3]] for the layered digital stacks; [[Wavetable]] four-layer instrument racks approximate the partial structure.
- **Paid:** Roland Cloud JD-800 (exact recreation).
- **Free:** rave-piano sample packs cover the famous preset.

## Used on
- Robert Miles — "Children" (1995): the lead piano is the JD-800's Ac.Piano preset (widely documented in gear lore) — dream-trance's founding sound.
- Genre/era: 90s rave/hardcore piano stabs, early trance leads and pads ([[EDM Subgenres]]), J-pop/city-pop late period.

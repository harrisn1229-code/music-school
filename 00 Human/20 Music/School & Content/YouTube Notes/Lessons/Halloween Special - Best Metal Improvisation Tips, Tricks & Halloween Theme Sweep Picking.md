# Halloween Special - Best Metal Improvisation Tips, Tricks & Halloween Theme Sweep Picking

## Synopsis

Two topics: a sweep-picked arrangement of John Carpenter's *Halloween* theme (written in 5/4) as a picking-trick showcase, and a separate segment on improvising over a dark/sinister metal chord cadence.

## Techniques

- **The arrangement is in 5/4** (5 quarter notes per measure vs. the usual 4/4) — cited as a genuinely useful rhythmic workout on its own, independent of the sweep-picking content, since maintaining an odd meter under fast picking is a real skill.
- **Hammer-on-as-pick-repositioning trick:** instead of an upstroke on one string followed immediately by a downstroke on the next (a direction change that can throw off timing at speed), **replace that upstroke with a hammer-on** — this frees the pick to already be correctly positioned for the following downstroke without needing to physically reverse direction. Feels awkward played slowly but pays off directly at speed; described as a trick still used regularly.
- **Second trick — staying in "sweep mindset" through a 2-string section instead of switching to alternate picking:** an upstroke followed by two downstrokes locked in one sweeping motion, filled out with hammer-ons/pull-offs rather than picking every note — keeps the picking hand relaxed while the fretting hand does the harder work. (Alternate picking can technically play the same notes, but breaks the continuous sweep feel.)
- **Practical note:** if the fretting stretch in the arrangement's first section causes strain, move the whole shape to a higher position on the neck rather than pushing through pain.
- **Musical color note:** the "scary/haunting" character of the arrangement comes from its interval choices (tritones/half-step tension), not from the picking technique itself.

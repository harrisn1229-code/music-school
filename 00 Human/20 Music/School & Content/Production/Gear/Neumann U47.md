# Neumann U47

**Category:** Microphone (large-diaphragm tube condenser)
**Vendor:** Georg Neumann GmbH

## What it is

The vintage large-diaphragm tube condenser mic that became the default vocal mic of the golden studio era — switchable cardioid/omnidirectional via a VF14 tube-driven capsule circuit, prized for a warm, rich low-mid body without harshness on top. Not currently owned — referenced as a comparison point to the owned [[Neumann TLM 102]] (both large-diaphragm Neumann condensers; the TLM 102 is the modern, solid-state, non-switchable-pattern descendant of the same warm-detail family).

## What it's used for

First-call lead-vocal mic for a huge range of voices — historically Frank Sinatra's, Nat King Cole's, and Dean Martin's go-to mic, still reached for as a default today precisely because it "works for everybody," with fallback mics kept on hand for the voices it doesn't suit.

## Signal chain notes

🎬 *From video:* "My go-to microphone is a U-47. If that doesn't work with the artist, I'll try a Brauner microphone, or I have so many options, so I'll go get one that finally works." Used with a Neve preamp (the console's 88R module) into a compressor barely engaged at all, because the singer's own mic technique (leaning in and backing off) was already doing most of the leveling work live — see [[Vocal Mic Technique]]. Distance kept to roughly six to seven inches, adjusted by ear per singer. — Al Schmitt, [Recording techniques with Al Schmitt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_9d6jopvF6Q)

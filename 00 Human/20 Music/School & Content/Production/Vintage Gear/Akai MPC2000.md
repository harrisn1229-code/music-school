# Akai MPC2000

## What it is
The MPC democratized — Akai's post-Linn, mass-market MPC (especially the 2000XL revision) that put the pads-and-swing workflow in every bedroom and project studio of the late 90s and 2000s. If the [[Akai MPC60]] built hip hop's grammar and the [[Akai MPC3000]] perfected it, the 2000/2000XL is the machine a whole generation actually *learned* on.

## What makes the sound
- **16-bit / 44.1 kHz sampling** with Akai's house converter character — slightly rounded, punchy, forgiving; cleaner than the 12-bit ancestors but still recognizably "MPC" rather than DAW-flat.
- **The same swing/quantize/note-repeat vocabulary** inherited from the Linn-designed machines — the pocket is the product → [[Group Humanizer]].
- XL added the chop-shop workflow conveniences (auto-chop, time stretch with its famously crunchy artifacts) — **the 2000XL time-stretch grain** is its one genuinely unique sonic fingerprint, still sought after for lo-fi textures.
- Zone/slice sampling + effects board options made it a self-contained beat studio — the "one machine, one producer" archetype of 2000s beat culture.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] + [[Simpler]]/[[Sampler]] (slice-to-drum-rack = the XL chop workflow); MPC groove files for the swing; [[RC-20 Retro Color]]/[[kHs Pitch Shifter]] abuse approximates the time-stretch crunch.
- **Paid:** Akai MPC Software / MPC Live-era hardware (lineage), XLN XO for a modern chop workflow.
- **Free:** Akai MPC Beats (the free modern descendant).

## Used on
- Genre/era: the backbone of late-90s/2000s underground and mainstream hip hop production and the 2000s beat-scene culture that followed — ubiquity again beats clean documentation, so credits stay genre-level; the marquee-name MPC credits in this library attach to the [[Akai MPC60|60]] and [[Akai MPC3000|3000]].

## Production & Arrangement
Part of the **Abbey Road medley** — McCartney's piano ballad that swells, via George Martin's orchestra, straight into **Carry That Weight**. A lullaby verse detonating into a belted, orchestral chorus: maximum dynamic contrast in 90 seconds.

## Steal this
- **Whisper-to-belt dynamics:** a tender piano/vocal verse into a full orchestral, gang-vocal chorus — contrast is the payoff.
- **Segue by swell:** let orchestration ramp one section into the next ([[Reverb Send Long]], string pads).
- **Reuse a lullaby melody** as an emotional anchor across a medley.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Golden Slumbers]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #189.*

**Form.** Paul's anthem-meets-show-tune mode, and his hook is that the two most obvious things about the song are both misleading. Intro is two bars of piano vamping the a7 figure. Verse **21 bars, phrased 8 + 4 + 5 + 4**; refrain **19 bars, phrased 6 + 4 + 5 + 4** — it sets up as an AA couplet of 6 + 6, but the second line elides into what is really a recap of the way the verses end. It earns the label refrain rather than bridge because the title phrase lives there, and both sections share the same 5 + 4 tail, which is what makes the recap land. Both counts are audible distortions of an ordinary four-square sixteen.

**Harmony.** Almost entirely diatonic, six chords, moving mostly around a short cycle of fifths. `Verse: vi7 (4) – ii (4) | V (4) | I – iii – vi – ii9 (held) | V – I` · `Refrain: I – IV7 – I (6) | I – IV7 (4) | I – V-of-vi – vi – ii9 (held) | V – I`. Harmonic rhythm is the real variable inside the section — leisurely across the first two phrases, radically faster in the third, a middle setting for the last ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). The tart ii9 is set up by holding **E natural through the entire phrase** ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]): it belongs to every other chord there and only becomes a dissonance when the D chord arrives.

**Notable.** The opening a7 **omits the E entirely** — a bare C–G fifth trading off with a lone A underneath — which makes the chord hard to place; superimposing the tonic triad on its relative minor this way is a long-running Beatles device ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]). The tune is fully diatonic with the **leap of a sixth recurring as a motif** ([[MEL-C01 — Melodic contour]]), one of them disguised across a phrase boundary. Paul sings one unaccompanied track, no doubling, and shifts into a menacing third voice for the refrain, cast against the lullaby setting.

**Against this page.** Agrees the song lives in C. His heading is "a minor → C Major": the verse *begins* away from home and converges fast, and A minor is never actually made a tonal centre.

# Deacon Blues — Steely Dan

> Becker and Fagen's meditation on romantic losers and alternate lives. Pete Christlieb's saxophone solo is widely considered one of the greatest in rock. Extended multi-section structure over jazz harmony. From Aja (1977).

## Production & Arrangement
The peak of Steely Dan's obsessive studio perfectionism (*Aja*, 1977) — Fagen & Becker famously auditioned dozens of the best LA/NY session players and comped from countless takes to get a "perfect," effortless-sounding record. Pristine, high-fidelity jazz-rock.

- **Jazz harmony in a pop song:** lush **maj7 / min9 / dominant-with-extensions** voicings ([[Extended Chords]] / [[Seventh Chords]]) — sophisticated, "wrong-but-right" changes.
- The famous **tenor sax solo (Pete Christlieb)** was cut in a couple of takes late at night — a hired virtuoso as the hook.
- Immaculate, wide, clean mix — every element in its own space, tons of headroom.

## Steal this
- **Comp for "effortless":** the relaxed feel comes from meticulous take selection — comp hard, hide the effort.
- **Jazz voicings in pop:** replace plain triads with maj7/9/13 extensions for that Steely Dan sophistication → [[Extended Chords]] · [[Seventh Chords]].
- **Hire/feature one virtuoso solo** as the centerpiece (sax here) — record it fresh, keep it short.
- **Pristine hi-fi mix:** everything clean, wide, and separated; lean on [[Pro-Q 4]] carving + generous headroom.

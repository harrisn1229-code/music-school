# Top 3 Shred Tricks For INSANE Neoclassical Guitar Licks!

## Synopsis

Three named devices for a neoclassical (Yngwie Malmsteen-adjacent) sound: visualizing a full scale along a single string rather than a boxed position, chromatic ornamentation notes added below scale tones, and fast 3-notes-per-string runs confined to the top two strings for a dramatic closing climax.

## What's taught

- **Trick 1 — one-string scale visualization, played as a position-hopping pattern rather than straight up/down.** The full A minor scale is visualized along one string (high E, root to octave, 5th fret to 19th fret) instead of the usual boxed multi-string shape. Rather than simply running the one-string scale straight up or down (which already sounds somewhat neoclassical on its own), the actual lick plays each position's lowest note, jumps to that position's highest note, then moves to the next position — a deliberately non-linear contour. Framed as a hand-synchronization exercise as much as a melodic device, since a different left-hand note is required for every single picking stroke with no slurs to hide timing looseness. Practice method: isolate and metronome just the first note of each position-jump first, since accurate landing on those anchor notes is what makes the rest of the notes fall into place.
- **Trick 2 — chromatic ornamentation notes, distinct from a chromatic passing run.** Not the same device as stringing several chromatic notes together (already covered elsewhere) — this is adding a single chromatic neighbor tone directly below a scale tone as a grace-note-style ornament (worked example: approaching the root A on the 7th fret/D string from G♯, one fret below). Described as the single fastest, easiest-to-apply device of the three for immediately adding a neoclassical/classical flavor to an otherwise plain scale phrase.
- **Trick 3 — fast 3-notes-per-string shapes confined to the top two strings (B and high E) for a climactic ending.** Used specifically as a closing/climax device rather than throughout a phrase. Standard picking-angle discipline applies (escape-direction choice to avoid getting stuck between the B and E strings) — same angle logic as this batch's other alternate-picking videos, not a new mechanical point on its own.

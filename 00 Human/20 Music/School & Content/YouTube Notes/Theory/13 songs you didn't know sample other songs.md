# 13 songs you didn't know sample other songs

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=951suvmMDs4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=951suvmMDs4)

## Synopsis

A catalog of hidden samples and interpolations across pop/dance/hip-hop, with a clear working definition drawn between the two (a sample reuses the actual original recording; an interpolation re-performs the melody/riff without using the original audio). Includes a real production technique — Daft Punk's tempo-shift-as-pitch-shift on "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger" — plus a reversed-sample case (Moby) and the Mellotron's role as an early proto-sampler on two different Beatles tracks.

## Techniques

- **Sample vs. interpolation, defined by example**: a sample reuses the actual original recording (Gotye's "Somebody That I Used to Know" loops the first two chords of the actual guitar recording from Luiz Bonfá's "Seville"); an interpolation re-performs the melody without using the original audio (the "Timber" harmonica riff replays — doesn't sample — the riff from Lolo's original recording; Coldplay's "Talk" interpolates Kraftwerk's "Computer Love" synth melody; Childish Gambino's "Redbone" is closer to a heavily-altered cover of Bootsy Collins' "I'd Rather Be With You," with Collins credited as a songwriter rather than paid a sample-clearance fee).
- **Tempo change as a free pitch-shift, deliberately exploited**: Daft Punk's "Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger" samples Edwin Birdsong's "Cola Bottle Baby" and subtly increases its tempo — which, because tempo and pitch are coupled in a simple speed change, also lifts the sample's key up by one semitone as a side effect. Same tape-speed physics as [[Varispeed 3.0]]'s Speed knob, used here specifically to both tighten the groove and land the sample in a more usable key in one move.
- **A riff built from a chopped-up different-song excerpt, not a straight loop**: Daft Punk's "One More Time" lifts and rearranges segments from Eddie Johns' "More Spell on You" to construct a new riff, rather than looping one continuous section — cited via a Tracklib breakdown analysis of the source.
- **Reversing a sampled string swell for a different emotional read**: Moby's "Porcelain" lifts a swelling string sound from Ernest Gold's soundtrack for the 1960 film *Exodus*, then reverses it — the backwards playback is what gives the swell its distinctive, unplaceable character. Owned-lane equivalent: [[kHs Reverser]].
- **The Mellotron as an early proto-sampler, used two different ways on two different Beatles tracks**: each key triggers a pre-recorded tape loop; on "Strawberry Fields Forever" it plays back individual flute notes (a real instrument's notes made keyboard-playable one at a time); on "The Continuing Story of Bungalow Bill" it instead plays back one long pre-recorded tape loop of a Spanish guitar intro as a single sustained "sample" rather than note-by-note — the Beatles simply triggered the whole pre-recorded phrase. → [[Vintage Gear Worklist]] (added, unchecked — not yet documented in the reference library).
- **A hidden 2000s dance-hit sample worth chasing down**: Stardust (revealed as a Daft Punk side project) sampled Chaka Khan for a 2000s dance hit — cited but not fully named in the video's transcript; worth a manual follow-up listen if useful as a Chaka Khan sample reference.
- **Framing on sampling's reputation**: sampling isn't "cheating" when royalties/credit are properly cleared — it's presented as a legitimate way to introduce an older, possibly under-heard original to a new audience and extend its legacy, echoing this vault's existing [[Sample Clearance]] guidance on clearing before release.

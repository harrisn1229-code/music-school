# CHD-C06 — Upper structures: a chord over a foreign bass

**Q.** What is an upper structure, and what decides whether it produces a genuinely new chord or just an inversion?

> [!question]- Answer
> A chord — usually a triad — played over a bass note that is not its own root. **If the bass note is already a tone of the upper chord you have an inversion and nothing new. If it isn't, you get a new composite chord** with its own name, function and resolution. The reliable home position is the upper structure's root on the **♭6 of a major key** (the natural 7 of a minor key).

## Why it matters

One memorised triad becomes six different chords depending on which bass note goes under it — a ♭6 major (maj7♯11), a ♭7 dominant 13, a iv minor 6/9, an altered dominant, and two rarer suspended-dominant readings. That is the fastest route from "I know three shapes" to a large chord vocabulary, and it is the same mechanism as a slash chord pushed further, because here the bass note need not be a chord tone at all. The real skill is the bass motion that connects two readings, not the names.

## Drill

Pick one minor triad, place it on the ♭6 of a major key, and write a progression that uses at least two of the six bass-note options, connected by smooth bass motion.

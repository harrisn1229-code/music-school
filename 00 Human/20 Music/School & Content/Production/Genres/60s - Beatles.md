# 60s / Beatles

**What makes it the genre:** performance-forward recording with committed sounds — few mics, tape compression doing the glue, hard-panned mixes, harmony as arrangement, and every "effect" printed at the source. The limitations *were* the aesthetic; recreate the limitations, not just the EQ curve.

## The pillars

### 1. Bass — the McCartney lanes
- **'64 Hofner lane:** neck-pickup, tone down, **palm-muted with a pick or thumb** — the "Hofner sound" is the muting, not just the instrument ([[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Electric Bass DI Track]] playing-technique section). Short thumpy notes, melodic but supportive.
- **'67 Sgt Pepper lane** (actually the Rickenbacker): DI + amp **blend**, amp mic pulled 5–8 ft back so the room is in the tone, the blend compressed together (LA-2A standing in for the RS124), notes ringing out ("A Day in the Life"). Melodic counterpoint bass — the second lead vocal.
- Bass mixed louder and rounder than modern pop; sub extension is limited (HPF ~40) but 80–250 Hz is *rich*.

### 2. Drums — Ringo physics
- Few mics: kick + overhead mentality. In the box: mono-ize the kit or heavy overhead-style bus, [[Kramer Tape]] pushed (7.5 ips for the darker lane), tea-towel-damped sounds (tight, thuddy, no ring — sustain DOWN on [[Transient Master]]).
- Compress the *kit*, not the pieces ([[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]] Beatles variation: slow release, let it pump musically).
- Toms are melodic and huge in fills; hats/cymbals dark (LPF — 60s cymbals never sizzle like samples do).

### 3. Guitars — jangle and grind
- Clean-edge-of-breakup through small combos: compression *forward* (squashed strumming = the AM-radio chug), [[Kramer Tape]] over modern drive, tremolo/Leslie warble ([[PanMan]], [[ValhallaUberMod]]).
- Hard-pan rhythm guitar fully one side, its answer the other. No stereo wideners — width came from *placement*.

### 4. Vocals — the harmony IS the arrangement
- Block harmonies in fours, minimal tuning (Humanize 100 or none), blend from the *takes* not the compressor ([[1 Harmony Vocal Track|Harmony Vocal Track]] Beatles variation).
- **ADT/slap:** the double is a tape-delayed copy (~120–140 ms wobbly slap — [[Delay Send Slap]] with EchoBoy wobble up) — that's the "Beatles double," not a re-sing.
- Plate and chamber verbs only ([[ValhallaPlate]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] 70s/chamber), mono-ish returns.

### 5. Mix presentation
- Hard-pan courage: drums one side, bass center-ish, guitars split, vocal center — lopsided is correct.
- Everything through one tape stage ([[Kramer Tape]] on the master, real drive) · limited top end globally (the "warmth" is just less 10 kHz+) · mono-check constantly; these mixes were *made* in mono.

## Arrangement

**Section map** — *"Something"*, *"Paperback Writer"*, *"A Day in the Life"*

| Section | Bars | Slots filled | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 2–8 | one instrument stating the hook — often the guitar figure | 1–2 |
| Verse | 8–16 | BASS · MIDS · LEAD · TRANSIENT | 4 |
| Chorus | 8 | + **block harmony vocals** · BED | 6 |
| Middle eight | 8 | new key or new instrument colour — the section that changes something structural | 4–5 |
| Outro | short | ends cold, or one figure repeats out | — |

**Entrances & exits**
- **Arrange in overdub passes, not in a timeline.** Four tracks means each section is literally a layer added on the next pass — that's why the arrangements grow monotonically and rarely subtract.
- **The harmony vocal entering IS the chorus.** Not a level change, not more drums — the block harmony arrives and the section has lifted. This is the single most transferable move on the page.
- Bass is melodic counterpoint throughout and gets *busier* in the chorus, not simpler ("Something").
- **The middle eight has a job**: change key, change instrumentation, or change register. A middle eight that's just a quieter verse is a wasted section.

**The contrast move — a new colour per section.** Not density, not filtering: an instrument nobody has heard yet. Piccolo trumpet, a Mellotron, an orchestra, backwards tape. One new sound per section is the entire arrangement strategy, and it's why 2:30 records hold up.

**Density rule: 4–6 slots, growing.** Scarcity is the mechanism — four tracks forces a decision about what deserves a pass. Recreate the limitation, not just the EQ curve.

## Session recipe
Track to a click loosely or not at all · commit sounds at the source (print the compression/echo) · arrange in layers-per-section like overdub passes on 4-track — scarcity forces the good decisions.

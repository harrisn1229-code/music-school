# Michael Jackson's engineer tells real story of "Smooth Criminal"

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4n1JBCGB8y8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4n1JBCGB8y8)

## Synopsis

Matt Forger explains the origin of "Annie, are you OK?" from "Smooth Criminal" — Michael Jackson picked up the phrase from CPR training with his brothers ("Annie" is the name of the CPR-practice mannequin, and "Annie, are you OK?" is the exact taught opening line), and the catchy phrase later resurfaced as a song hook that he then built a full narrative around. Pure songwriting-origin history, but the framing around it is a real, reusable songwriting principle.

## What's taught

- **Don't discard "gibberish" placeholder lyrics sung during early melody-writing — they often contain a buried suggestion of what the song is actually about.** Forger's generalized point (echoed by a lyricist he'd separately spoken with): when a writer starts singing nonsense syllables/words to find a melody, specific words that surface aren't always random — treat them as worth investigating before rewriting the lyric from scratch, since they can point toward the song's real subject the way "Annie, are you OK?" did once Jackson recognized the hook and built "Smooth Criminal"'s narrative around it.
- Jackson's described songwriting order: start from the hook (the chorus), then flesh out a verse narrative that leads into it — hook first, story built around it, not the reverse.

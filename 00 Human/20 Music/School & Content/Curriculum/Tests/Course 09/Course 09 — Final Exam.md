> **Q19.** **Sing the tonic, play any note, judge its degree, back-calculate — an E that feels like 5 makes the key A**
> *Rubric (5):* gives the method in the right order (2) · says no absolute pitch is needed because only the relationship is judged (1) · works the example to A correctly (2)
> Sing the note that feels like home, then play any note at all and ask what degree *that* feels like against what you sang. The instrument supplies the one pitch whose name is certain, and the felt relationship converts it into a key. Here: if E is degree 5, the tonic sits a perfect 5th below E, which is A. The key is A.
>
> **Q20.** **Memorise · contour · pencil in felt degrees · deduce · verify. Contour is skipped**
> *Rubric (5):* gives the five steps in order (2) · names contour-before-degrees as the skipped one and says why it matters (1) · names all four routine skills — melodic scale-degree, bass-note, chord-quality, chord-number/position (2)
> The five steps run memorise, map the contour, sing slowly and pencil in the degrees you can genuinely feel, deduce the rest, then verify on the instrument. Step 2 is the one people skip, and skipping it means guessing each note independently with nothing to constrain the options — the shape is what narrows the degrees you can't hear. The four skills a complete routine touches are melodic scale-degree, bass-note, chord-quality and chord-number/position.

# Condensing 18 pages of 'All of Me' into a Lead Sheet

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TeYjsRCCskA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TeYjsRCCskA)

## Synopsis

A worked demo of turning an over-transcribed full piano/vocal arrangement into a 1-2 page lead sheet (melody + chords only, the format used throughout jazz Real Books). Uses a MuseScore transcription of John Legend's "All of Me" — 18 pages down to 1 — as the running example, then confirms the same reduction exists as an official purchasable option (10-page full arrangement vs. 5-page lead sheet, also cheaper). Framed for self-taught musicians intimidated by dense sheet music.

## Techniques

- **The reduction procedure, in order**: (1) drop every stave except the melody line — keep the vocal melody on treble clef rather than the right-hand piano part, since they're usually near-identical and the vocal line is the actual "lead"; (2) drop the left-hand piano stave entirely once its content is just outlining the chords already written above the staff — the chord symbols alone carry that information; (3) delete duplicate sections (repeated verse/chorus) and replace with a repeat mark instead of writing them out twice; (4) strip lyrics if not needed, since they eat page space without adding playable information. Applied in sequence: 18 pages → 5 pages (staves cut) → 1 page (duplicates + lyrics cut).
- **A structural instrument-only exception worth keeping**: the intro is kept even though it isn't the vocal melody, specifically because it's recognizable — the reduction rule is "cut redundant/non-essential," not "cut everything except the vocal line unconditionally."
- **The explicit trade-off, stated plainly rather than left implicit**: a lead sheet loses the specific voicings/arrangement details of the original recording. Right tool when the goal is *reinterpreting* or improvising over the tune (a lead sheet leaves the arrangement decisions to the player, same principle as a jazz Real Book chart); wrong tool when the goal is reproducing the original arrangement note-for-note, where the full transcription is still needed.
- **A sheet-music-anxiety framing worth keeping for practice-material design**: the presenter states he actively avoided reading standard notation for years (used made-up letter systems instead) until forced to in a formal setting — offered as the reason a lead sheet is a good *entry point* into notation-reading, since it strips away the visual density (multiple staves, full voicing, dynamics/articulation markings) that makes a full score intimidating, while still requiring real staff-reading of the melody line.

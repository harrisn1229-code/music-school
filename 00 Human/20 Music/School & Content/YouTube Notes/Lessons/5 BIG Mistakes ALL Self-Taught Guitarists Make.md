# 5 BIG Mistakes ALL Self-Taught Guitarists Make! (comedic horse-drawing framing, real technique content)

## Synopsis

Five self-taught-player blind spots, from picking mechanics to practice sound choice to live-performance posture. The video is framed comedically (BERNTH draws a horse from memory throughout), but every segment carries a concrete technique or practice-routine fix.

## What's taught

- **One-sided pick-escape bias.** Self-taught players tend to develop a comfortable pick-escape direction (up or down out of the strings) and default to it, which limits lick vocabulary and causes real problems on material that specifically requires the other direction. Fix: deliberately include exercises requiring escape in **both** directions, not just your natural one.
- **A combined alternate-picking + sweep-picking + fretboard-visualization workout, built from the modes of C major.** Sweep an arpeggio on every scale degree starting from B diminished (the 7th degree) and working backward to C major (the root); simultaneously alternate-pick through all seven modes, always starting the mode's root on the high E string and ending its root on the A string. Deliberately combines three skills (sweep arpeggios, alternate-picked scale runs, fixed start/end fretboard targets) in one exercise rather than three separate drills — genuinely a **routine** (implements mode/arpeggio knowledge, needs active thought), not a mindless drill.
- **"Single-note domination" — self-taught rock/metal players routinely neglect chords/strumming.** Fix given: work funk-style rhythmic dead-note patterns across multiple strings into the practice routine — doubles as timing practice and as a wrist-unlocking exercise (can't play tight funk dead-note patterns with a locked/full-arm picking motion).
- **Practice with a transparent, uncolored ("dry") tone, not a distorted or fully clean tone.** Distortion masks fretting-hand noise and sloppy technique that a clean signal would expose; a transparent, dry practice tone (no reverb/delay/compression) reveals inconsistencies a clean tone alone can hide, while still sitting closer to real playing conditions than plain clean.
- **Practice posture should match live posture (guitar on a strap, standing or seated the way you'll actually perform), and body tension should be actively released before/during a live performance** — stage-fright-driven over-gripping of the neck is named directly as a common live-only problem that doesn't show up in relaxed home practice, with the fix being deliberate physical relaxation and slightly laid-back timing rather than rushing.

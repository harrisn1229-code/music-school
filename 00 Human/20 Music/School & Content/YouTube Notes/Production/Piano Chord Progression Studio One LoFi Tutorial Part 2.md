# Piano Chord Progression | Studio One LoFi Tutorial | Part 2

## Synopsis

Part 2 of the [[Make a Chill LoFi Beat in Studio One Part 1|lo-fi build-along series]] — lays the harmonic bed with piano, chosen first because "lo-fi is so heavily based on the harmonic feeling of the track." Covers patch prep (release time, gain), a send reverb setup, an aggressive lo-fi high-cut EQ, sus2 chord voicings on the I and IV, and Max's editing philosophy for keeping a performance human (move outliers by hand, don't quantize, leave the rest raw). A compact but idea-dense video — almost every move here is a reusable lo-fi/keys technique rather than a Studio-One-specific step.

## Techniques

- **Piano patch prep before playing a note:** release stretched to ~254 ms so note-ends don't cut off abruptly ("check that for yourself" — not a universal number, just his ear), all internal reverb/delay/modulation/routing disabled on the instrument (space comes from a send, not the patch), and gain boosted +6 dB because the stock patch was too quiet. General lesson: shape the *instrument's own* envelope/gain before reaching for outboard processing.
- **Send reverb, not insert, for the piano's space** — a dedicated reverb bus ("verb one") so the piano can blend dry-vs-wet to taste rather than committing a fixed amount on the track. Settings used: room/hall type left mostly at default, switched from "eco" to **high-quality mode** for a better-sounding tail. → [[Reverb]], [[1 Acoustic Piano Track|Acoustic Piano Track]]
- **The defining lo-fi EQ move: aggressive high-cut on the keys.** After a token low roll-off at 20 Hz, the real move is a high-cut pulled down to **~1.5 kHz** — "a lot of the low-fi sound is high frequencies being gone." Result: the piano sounds "distant, underwater almost." Framed as a per-element decision, not a blanket rule — some elements in a lo-fi mix keep highs (framed as something to experiment with track by track). → [[EQ]], [[Neo-Soul - Lo-fi]]
- **Sus2 voicings on the I and IV for an open, "cozy" harmonic color** — in the key of E major, picks two major triads (E and A, the I and IV) and voices both as sus2 (third dropped to the second) instead of plain triads, specifically for the open/unresolved sound sus chords have. The line arpeggiates the E sus2 from the bottom up to the third, holds, then descends into an A-chord answering phrase.
- **Answering-phrase melodic structure for a two-chord progression** — the I-chord arpeggio poses a musical "question," the IV-chord voicing that follows functions as the "answer." A simple, reusable way to make a two-chord loop feel like a phrase instead of two isolated chords.
- **Humanizing philosophy for recorded MIDI: correct only what's glaring, leave the rest raw.** Record the part live (even imperfectly) rather than programming it, then edit by hand: nudge only notes that are *particularly* early/late closer to the beat (no quantize), spot-check velocities for anything way too heavy/soft and adjust by feel, but otherwise keep the "washy and wavy" imperfection — it's part of the lo-fi vibe. Only fix what would distract the listener. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]]
- **Hold the last chord note of a phrase over, including across the loop boundary** — each chord's final note is sustained into the next chord rather than cut cleanly; when the pattern loops, that held note gets cut short by the loop point, so it should be manually extended/held over on the looped copy too for consistency.

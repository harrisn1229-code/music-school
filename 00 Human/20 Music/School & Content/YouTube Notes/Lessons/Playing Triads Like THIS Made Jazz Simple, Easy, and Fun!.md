## Techniques

- **Device 1 — triad + fixed enclosure on every note (the Charlie Parker device).** Take a diatonic triad (worked first on the tonic, C major triad = C-E-G) and wrap **every note** of it in the same enclosure shape: **one diatonic scale step above** the target note, then **one chromatic half step below** it, landing on the target. This is literally Charlie Parker's own vocabulary, played by Parker over a Cmaj7. Matches the vault's "diatonic above, chromatic below" enclosure row exactly (see [[Bebop Vocabulary]] §2) — the innovation here isn't a new enclosure shape, it's applying that one fixed shape systematically to **every note of a triad** rather than a single target.
- **Device 1 generalizes to any diatonic triad, not just the tonic.** The same enclosure-wrapped-triad shape was demonstrated off the **G triad** (V, the 5th chord in C) and the **A minor triad** (vi, the 6th chord in C) — both stated to "sound great" — establishing that the device is triad-agnostic: pick any diatonic triad in the key, wrap every note in the same above/below enclosure, and it reads as idiomatic jazz vocabulary regardless of which scale degree the triad sits on.
- **Avoid-note-driven triad selection over the tonic.** When combining Device 1 with a full scalar run over Cmaj7, the video explicitly restricts the triad choice to the diatonic triads that **don't contain the note F** — C major (C-E-G), E minor (E-G-B), A minor (A-C-E), G major (G-B-D) — leaving out D minor and B diminished, both of which contain F. F is Cmaj7's classic **avoid note** (the 4th sitting a half step above the major 3rd), so this is a triad-selection filter built directly on avoid-tone theory: pick your diatonic triad palette by first excluding any triad carrying the chord's avoid tone, then apply Device 1 freely inside what's left. *(general starting point: this same "which triads contain the avoid tone" check generalizes to any diatonic major-7 tonic — work out the parallel four-of-six triad shortlist before soloing over it, not on the fly.)*
- **Device 2 — harmonized passing-tone triads (targeting between two chord tones).** Pick two notes of the underlying chord's arpeggio to connect — worked example: **C (root) to E (3rd)** over Cmaj7. Walk the major scale stepwise between them (C-D-E). Harmonize **each scale tone passed through** with its own diatonic triad in the key, treating that scale tone as **the 5th of the harmonizing triad** (so the triad built under D as a 5th would be a G triad, for instance — the video doesn't spell out every intermediate triad by name, but states the top-note-as-5th rule directly). Apply the **same enclosure pattern as Device 1** (diatonic step above, chromatic half step below) to target each harmonized triad's top note, then **arpeggiate** the triad once you land on it, moving to the next passing tone and repeating. Closes the phrase with an **E minor triad tacked on the end**, since E minor also works as a diatonic option over the same tonic. Also demonstrated moving between other chord-tone pairs of the same arpeggio: **3rd (E) to 5th (G)**, and **5th (G) to 7th (B)** — the device generalizes to any two-note stretch of the tonic arpeggio, not just root-to-3rd.
- **Everything demonstrated over C major works over any diatonic chord in the key, by function.** Stated twice as a general rule (not just for the tonic-based lines but for the harmonized-passing-tone lines too): because the triads and enclosures are drawn from the parent scale rather than tied to one specific chord, the same lines/devices transfer to whichever diatonic chord happens to be sounding underneath, without rework.
- **Device 1/2 transplanted onto the ii chord, then generalized via ii/V interchangeability.** Take the same triad-plus-enclosure patterns already drilled over the tonic and apply them to **D minor** (ii). The stated payoff: "you can play D Minor ideas over G7 and vice versa" — ii-chord vocabulary and its paired V chord are treated as functionally interchangeable targets for the same lines, so a D-minor-based line built this way also works straight over G7 without modification. Chained into a full **251 in C (Dm7 → G7 → Cmaj7)**: play the D-minor-derived lines over the ii-V, then switch to the tonic-based lines (Device 1/2 material already built over C major) once the progression resolves to Cmaj7. → [[The ii-V-I]] for the underlying cadence and function relationships this move relies on.
- **Add rhythmic variation once the note-choice devices are solid.** Explicit next step named directly: stop playing straight eighth notes and vary the rhythm of the same triad/enclosure lines — the devices above are taught rhythm-neutral (as straight eighths) specifically so rhythm can be layered on afterward as a separate variable.
- **Combine with general bebop/jazz language, not just triads in isolation.** Closing framing: triads moving up/down the C major scale, decorated with enclosures, can be freely mixed with other jazz vocabulary rather than treated as a closed self-sufficient system — the triad devices are one ingredient, not the whole line-building toolkit. → [[Bebop Vocabulary]].

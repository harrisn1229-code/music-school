# Amazing Guitar Ear Training Exercises (hearing & singing modes of the major scale)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mioBDHfAA_A)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mioBDHfAA_A)

## Synopsis

Jared's deep-dive on the single ear-training exercise he says "changed the game" for him personally: **singing through all seven modes of the major scale, one at a time, over a sustained single-note drone on the root.** This is a distinct, more advanced technique than [[Guitar Ear Training Essentials - The Missing Link for Guitarists]] (lesson 2.9, his singing-first foundation — scale-degree numbers/solfège on the plain major scale plus real melodies) — that video builds the raw "can I sing a scale degree back accurately" skill; this one applies that skill across **every mode of the major scale**, so the singer ends up producing and identifying every chromatic degree that can appear above a root (♭2, 2, ♭3, 3, 4, ♯4, 5, ♭6, 6, ♭7, 7) in the actual modal context each one belongs to, not as an isolated interval. The mechanism is the same "singing is the diagnostic test" philosophy as the Missing Link video and as Max Konyi's drone method on [[Scale Degree Ear Training]], but the drilling structure is new and guitar-modes-specific: start on Lydian (the brightest mode) and walk down to Locrian (the darkest) by lowering exactly one scale degree a half-step at a time — the same bright-to-dark "color tones" ladder documented on [[Diatonic Modes]], just sung instead of played. Jared is candid throughout that he's not a comfortable or trained singer and still misses intonation sometimes, which is part of the video's point — the bar is "am I singing the right note and can I hear it," not vocal polish. For a non-singer or someone who doesn't know the mode shapes yet, he gives an explicit guitar-first on-ramp: map the modes structurally on one string before trying to sing them.

## What's taught

- **The core exercise: sing through the modes of the major scale over a root drone.** Take a string you can comfortably sing both the open note and its octave on, and hold a drone on that root (any drone method works — the guitar string itself, a held note, a drone app/track). Sing each mode of the major scale, up and down, over that sustained root. **Why it works better than isolated interval drills, in Jared's own words**: you have to *sing* each note above the root and confirm by ear that it's right, which forces production and identification simultaneously — and because you're doing it across every mode, you end up hearing/producing every chromatic scale degree in multiple different modal contexts rather than just once in the abstract.
- **The mode-to-mode drilling order — walk the [[Diatonic Modes|brightness ladder]] one note at a time, starting at the brightest end:**
  1. **Lydian** — `1 2 3 ♯4 5 6 7` (the starting point; brightest/most "floating" mode).
  2. **Ionian / major** — lower the ♯4 to natural 4: `1 2 3 4 5 6 7`.
  3. **Mixolydian** — lower the 7 to ♭7: `1 2 3 4 5 6 ♭7`.
  4. **Dorian** — lower the 3 to ♭3: `1 2 ♭3 4 5 6 ♭7`.
  5. **Aeolian / natural minor** — lower the 6 to ♭6: `1 2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7`.
  6. **Phrygian** — lower the 2 to ♭2: `1 ♭2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7`.
  7. **Locrian** — lower the 5 to ♭5 (last and hardest): `1 ♭2 ♭3 4 ♭5 ♭6 ♭7`.
  Each step changes exactly one degree, so the ear only has to relocate one note at a time rather than relearning a whole new scale — matches [[Diatonic Modes]]'s "each mode differs from its baseline by exactly one distinguishing degree" color-tones framing, just walked in a specific brightest→darkest sequence rather than treated as seven independent facts.
- **Locrian flagged as the genuinely hardest mode to sing, and why.** The 5th degree is normally the most stable, reliable "anchor" pitch to sing against a drone (after the root itself) — Locrian removes it (♭5), so there's no safe scale-degree to fall back on mid-phrase; Jared's own workaround is to lean on the 4th as the anchor instead. General lesson: **when a mode/scale removes your usual anchor tone, consciously pick a substitute anchor before attempting it**, rather than trying to wing it.
- **Singing method — three interchangeable ways to vocalize, pick whichever helps**: scale-degree numbers, solfège syllables, or a light "buzzing"/lip-trill-adjacent tone. **The buzzing tone is offered as a specific tool for a shaky/uncomfortable singer**: it regulates breath more evenly and keeps the throat relaxed, and Jared notes numbers are *harder* to sing accurately than the buzzing tone — so if pure sung numbers feel out of reach, warm up on the buzz first, then translate to numbers once the pitch is secure.
- **Intonation self-check habit**: check the intonation of *just the new/changed note* each time you move to the next mode, rather than re-verifying the whole scale — since only one degree changed, that's the only note actually at risk of being wrong.
- **"Be messy" framing / realistic progress arc**: Jared states he "literally couldn't do it at all" when he first tried this — it seemed impossible — and improved through a slow, deliberate self-check loop ("is that the three? Am I singing the three? Am I singing the two?"), repeated over time, not a fast unlock. Consistent with [[The Practice Priority Pyramid]]'s general "start small, be kind to yourself" framing carried over from the Missing Link video.
- **Guitar-first on-ramp for someone who doesn't know the mode shapes yet or isn't ready to sing cold**: before attempting to sing anything, **play through the modes on one string**, playing the root note in between each scale tone (e.g., root, 2, root, 3, root, ♯4, root...) — explicitly *not* for ear training yet at this stage, just to structurally map the mode's shape and internalize where the root sits relative to each degree. Work through the modes in the same Lydian→Locrian bright-to-dark order on the guitar first, *then* start the singing version once the shapes are solid — checking intonation between notes, holding the drone, singing slowly, and building up to fluently singing each full mode up and down before moving to the next.
- **"Parent scales" framing**: the major scale is one "parent scale" that these 7 modes derive from; other parent scales (melodic minor, harmonic minor, etc.) generate their own mode families the same way — see [[Scale Modes Reference]] for the melodic/harmonic-minor mode sets this video gestures at but doesn't teach directly. Jared references a free downloadable **"Printable Parent Scales PDF"** (his own resource, at soundguitarlessons.com/scales) covering what he considers the 7 most important parent scales, each laid out in 5 fretboard positions — a mapping/reference resource, not something to extract line-by-line here, flagged for awareness only.
- **Two other Jared videos referenced in this lesson**: a dedicated **"modes explained"** video covering the mode structures/formulas in full (referenced twice as the prerequisite for viewers who don't know the mode shapes — not yet captured in this vault, link when found) and **[[Modes of Melodic Minor Scale on Guitar & What Chords to Play Them Over]]**, his video on the modes of the melodic minor scale and what chords they're typically played over (this vault's page for it, if present, has its own extraction).

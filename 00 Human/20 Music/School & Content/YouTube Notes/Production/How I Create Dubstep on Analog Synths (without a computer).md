# How I Create Dubstep on Analog Synths (without a computer)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zovxgM-MU-s)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zovxgM-MU-s)

## Synopsis

Anthony recreates a dubstep-style rhythmic wobble entirely with vintage analog gear instead of DAW automation/LFO drawing: a Korg MS-20 (chosen for its two-filter "shredder" character) has both its high-pass and low-pass filters modulated by envelope generator voltage, and that envelope is fired not by a keyboard but by gate pulses from a vintage Dr. Click clock/sync unit — synced to the DAW's tempo and switchable between quarter note, eighth note, eighth-note triplet, and 16th-note subdivisions.

## Techniques

- **Two-filter "shredding" for dubstep-style aggression**: the MS-20's high-pass and low-pass filters run in series with heavy resonance and light distortion for extra growl, giving a more aggressive, textured character than a single filter alone.
- **Clock-synced gate-fired filter envelope, as a hardware alternative to DAW LFO automation**: a Dr. Click sync unit generates a DAW-tempo-locked pulse, subdivided to quarter/eighth/eighth-triplet/16th notes; each pulse fires an envelope generator that modulates both filters simultaneously, producing the rhythmic "wobble" pattern that dubstep synth lines are known for — entirely without drawing automation or an LFO synced by plugin.
- **Live rhythm switching**: because the subdivision is a physical selector on the clock unit, the wobble pattern can be changed live/on the fly between quarter, eighth, triplet, and 16th feels while playing, rather than being locked to a pre-drawn automation lane.
- **Two-oscillator chorusing**: a light detune between the MS-20's two oscillators adds subtle chorus-like thickness to the base tone before the filter modulation is applied.

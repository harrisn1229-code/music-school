# how southside makes dark synth trap beats for future from scratch (dark melody music theory)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TVjyuF5-xUo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TVjyuF5-xUo)

## Synopsis

A from-scratch dark-synth-trap breakdown by loner, reverse-engineering Southside's beats for Future/Roddy Ricch. Two synth pads are layered to build the harmonic bed (a textural Analog Lab preset run through reverb/delay/EQ/a three-stage ShaperBox-style pulse, plus a second "Lost" pad underneath to cover the low end), then a genuinely concrete music-theory segment walks through *why* the chords sound dark: two- and three-note voicings built around a semitone-apart internal clash, ending on a borrowed iv chord re-voiced to stay in range. A Kontakt counter-melody and a copied-chord "hook stab" round out the melodic content, both processed with tape/reverb/delay for vintage character. Drums come from loner's own unreleased kit (room + bounce snare + simple hats/rolls/percs + hard 808/kick) so nothing there is a sourceable pack. The most valuable material is the chord-voicing theory (a clean, repeatable worked example of the "semitone-clash for tension" trick this vault already tracks elsewhere) and the arrangement logic (no kick for the first 8 bars, half-time toggling for energy contrast, and pitching a bounced pad down an octave to signify the verse) — both fully genre-transferable outside dark trap. loner explicitly closes on a restraint principle: keep melodic elements minimal and leave room for the artist. Note: this video's chord shape (D♯m walking to a semitone-clash voicing, resolving on G♯m/iv) is nearly identical to the one already logged on [[Future-Style Vintage Sample Chain]] from a different loner video — likely his recurring "Southside dark trap" teaching example, not a coincidence; see the cross-reference there.

## Techniques

**Main pad — sound + FX chain (0:00–1:40)**
- **Instrument: Arturia Analog Lab, "Providence" bank, "Selena" preset** — picked for its unusual dark texture; sounds "a bit mad" completely dry, but the raw texture is what makes it flip well. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Reverb: heavy but short** — a big-sounding verb with a short decay, so it thickens the hit without smearing into a wash. → [[Reverb]]
- **Delay: half-time, set to 1/8** — deliberately chosen over a straight subdivision so more of the notes' onsets get doubled/reinforced by the repeats. → [[Delay]] (effect)
- **EQ: heavy low-end cut** — the raw preset's low end was "just a textural element" and didn't sound good, so it's cut rather than kept. → [[EQ]]
- **Three-stage pulse (ShaperBox-style — owned lane: [[Shaper]]):** (1) a **volume shaper** for the base rhythmic pulse; (2) a **filter shaper** that brings the high end in gradually on each pulse; (3) a **width shaper** that moves the sound from stereo → mono → stereo on the same cycle. Three simultaneous shaper roles stacked on one sound, not just one. → [[Shaper]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**Music theory — dark chord voicing (1:40–3:10)**
- **Chord 1 (bar 1):** a D♯ minor triad, but the top note is pulled down from F♯ to F — putting two chord tones (D♯ and F) a semitone apart, which is what reads as "dark" immediately, before any processing. → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Melody Writing]]
- **Chord 2:** the same shape but with only two notes — F and F♯ (F♯ replacing F) — so the internal clash is now the ENTIRE chord: just two notes, one semitone apart. Called out explicitly as where the real tension comes from.
- **Variation notes:** an added F later, then a G♯, purely for melodic variety within the same dark palette — "you could really choose any notes as long as it has that dark sound."
- **Repeat structure:** the two-chord shape (with variation notes) repeats for the first 6 bars.
- **Chord 3 (bars 7–8):** a G♯ minor chord — the **iv** chord of the scale — voiced with its top note (D♯) dropped an octave specifically to keep the chord in the same register as everything before it, rather than letting the progression climb.
- **The whole progression is 2–3 note voicings only** — never a full triad-plus-extensions; sparse voicing is part of why it reads as dark/minimal rather than lush.

**Second pad — filling the low end (3:10–3:40)**
- **Instrument: "Lost" preset, from the "Loophole" bank (Analog Lab)** — added specifically because the first pad's texture "doesn't fill much of the low end." → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Settings tweaked from the preset default:** release brought up (was too short), filter cutoff brought down slightly.
- **FX:** a simple band-pass EQ, cutting some high end and some low end so the two pads sit in their own bands rather than fighting. → [[EQ]]

**Counter melody — Kontakt "Tape Synths" (3:40–4:50)**
- **Instrument: "Tape Synths," a Kontakt library from Fono Loop** — chosen specifically for unique/awkward-sounding textures loner says aren't usable in every beat but land well in the right spot; layerable sounds with its own filters/envelopes/modulation. → [[Kontakt 7]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Delay: Valhalla Delay, heavy feedback, time set in milliseconds (not a synced subdivision) and kept short** — deliberately not a rhythmic delay; the short ms setting reads "almost like a chorus" rather than a distinct echo. → [[ValhallaDelay]], [[Delay]] (effect)
- **Reverb: a vintage-style reverb, pushed heavy on the mix.**
- **Detune/vintage color: "Reels" tape plugin, "Excited Tape" preset** — harshness pushed up and wow/flutter pushed up, specifically to make the layer sound old/detuned. → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Half-time: automated in at certain points only, not left on constantly** — so the effect reads as a deliberate arrangement move rather than a fixed setting. → [[Automation]]

**Melodic hook stab (4:50–5:30)**
- **Copy the first chord** (same D♯m voicing from the theory section above) and place it on bar 1 and bar 5 only — a single repeated chord hit, not a new melodic idea.
- **FX:** reverb → very heavy delay → EQ cutting the lows.
- **Purpose:** used as the hook-arrival signal in place of a riser or crash cymbal — "you know when this part comes in, that's the hook section." → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Restraint principle stated directly:** don't add more melodic elements beyond this — leave room for the artist to do their own thing; these beats are "often super simple and don't require a lot."

**Drums — loner's own unreleased kit (5:30–6:20)**
- Source: an unreleased "2022 essentials drum kit" (loner's own product) — not a sourceable pack, so nothing here routes to the wishlist.
- **Build order:** room sound → bounce snare → hi-hat (simple two-step base pattern) → hi-hat rolls → a second hi-hat layered in purely for extra bounce → percs (one plain perc, one "ambient" perc, one perc used as a drum fill — velocities pulled down and panned for that fill specifically) → a hard, distorted-character 808 → a hard kick.
- → [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Layering and Stacking]]

**Arrangement (6:20–end)**
- **Intro:** the two main pad sounds only — nothing else.
- **Hook:** kick deliberately left OUT for the first 8 bars, then brought in. The half-time counter-melody effect is engaged for the first half of the hook, then switched OFF for the second half — adding the kick and removing the half-time at the same moment makes the second half read as noticeably more energetic and higher-pitched, purely from those two toggles. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Automation]]
- **Verse:** take the FIRST pad sound, bounce it to audio, and pitch the bounced audio down an octave — used specifically as an arrangement signal to the listener (and the artist) that this is the lower-energy verse section, not a tone choice. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Extending the beat:** the same section structure is copied again to lengthen the arrangement, rather than writing new material.

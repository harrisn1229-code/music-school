# You Suck at Producing: How to Use EQ

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gBE9pVGVPgk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gBE9pVGVPgk)

## Synopsis
An early (2016), beginner-level introduction to subtractive EQ using Ableton's stock EQ Three on a single sample — the oldest and most basic video in this batch. Walks through killing a problem low-end band, discovering the problem isn't fully gone, widening the band's crossover to capture the rest of it, then partially reintroducing what was cut for a musical balance. The underlying habit (widen the crossover to fully capture a problem zone before deciding how much to restore) is the one thing worth carrying forward from an otherwise very basic tutorial.

## Techniques
- **Kill a band completely first, then decide how much to bring back** — with EQ Three's 3-band split (low/mid/high), start by pulling a problem band's volume all the way down rather than nudging it — a full subtractive pass first makes it obvious how much of the problem that band was actually responsible for.
- **If a band-kill leaves a residual problem, widen the crossover into the next band instead of leaving it** — cutting the Low band on EQ Three can still leave a chunk of the same problem content audible in the Mid band. Rather than accepting that, raise the Low/Mid crossover frequency so more of that range falls under the band already being cut, then re-listen. → [[EQ Three]]
- **Reintroduce part of what you cut for balance, rather than leaving a band fully dead** — once a problem frequency zone is fully isolated and cut, bring some of it back in (rather than leaving that band at zero) to land on a musically balanced result instead of an over-corrected one.

# How To MASTER Stella By Starlight... A Chord By Chord Guide!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J2Evyk539A)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2J2Evyk539A)

## Synopsis

Nathan Borton walks "Stella By Starlight" (key of B♭ major) progression by progression, naming each chord move and giving a concrete scale/target-tone answer for soloing over it. This is the tune-specific companion to Borton's own [[The 7 Must Know Licks To Solo Over Any Jazz Standard]], which used Stella as its closing etude precisely because "this standard almost has every type of progression in it" — that earlier page flagged that no standing Stella note existed yet "in case a future lesson teaches the tune itself"; this is that lesson. Rather than teaching licks in the abstract, Borton takes each of Stella's harmonically tricky spots in order and answers one question per spot: *what scale or target tones make this move make sense?* The recurring toolkit across the whole tune is small and reusable: a diminished-chord substitute for a non-functional dominant-to-minor move, "interlocking ii-V" spots where a dominant resolves into a minor or half-diminished chord built on its own root rather than the expected I, and — the single most repeated device in the video — soloing over a **backdoor dominant** (♭VII7-type chord resolving down a step, not always to the tonic) with the **melodic minor scale built off the backdoor chord's own fifth**. The video closes with a full worked chorus combining all of it, then hands off to "categorize the sound, then go transcribe and steal it" as the actual practice method. No production/mixing content — pure instrument/theory pedagogy, same lane as the vault's other Nathan Borton jazz-guitar breakdowns.

## What's taught

- **A7 → Cm7 (non-functional dominant-to-minor move) — substitute a B♭ diminished chord/arpeggio over the A7, second inversion (D♭ in the bass) preferred.** Stated reasoning: A7 literally moving to Cm7 has "pretty terrible" voice leading, but A7♭9 and a diminished chord built a half step above the dominant's root (B♭° over A7) share the ♭9-3-5 territory, and the B♭° chord's D♭ (2nd-inversion bass note) resolves down a half step into C, matching Cm7's root. Soloing: run the (whole-half) diminished scale or the B♭ diminished arpeggio straight over the A7. → [[Diminished Chords]], [[Chord Substitution]] § 2 (a sibling "diminished chord = rootless dominant" device), [[🎵 VICTOR YOUNG - Stella By Starlight]]
- **F7 → Fm7 ("interlocking ii-V," major flavor) — a dominant resolving to a minor chord on its own root, generalized as the key's ii7 → iim7 → V → I pattern; here landing on the borrowed iv (Fm7) of B♭.** Soloing: target the **3rd of the dominant** (A, the 3rd of F7) and let it fall a half step to **A♭**, the ♭3 of the following Fm7 — a two-note guide-tone move that carries the whole chord change. → [[The ii-V-I]], [[Guide Tones]], [[Borrowed Chords]]
- **A♭7♯11 → I (backdoor dominant #1) — solo with the melodic minor scale built on the backdoor chord's own 5th, here E♭ melodic minor over A♭7.** Method stated directly: count up a 5th from the backdoor chord's root (A♭-B♭-C-D♭-E♭) and use that note as the melodic-minor tonic. Also notes the chord right before A♭7 is E♭maj7, so the whole two-chord stretch can be thought of simply as "E♭ major going to E♭ minor" rather than as two unrelated harmonic events. → [[Chord Substitution]] § 3 (Backdoor substitution), [[Scale Modes Reference]] § Melodic Minor (functionally the IV-degree Lydian-dominant relationship — melodic minor a 4th below the dominant root gives that dominant its Lydian-♭7 color; here framed as "off the 5th" from the chord's own root instead), [[Turnarounds]]
- **Em7♭5 → A7 → Dm7 (minor ii-V landing on the iii chord) — two interchangeable readings, both landing on the same melodic-minor target.** Reading 1: play D harmonic minor straight across the whole progression. Reading 2 (stated as simpler): ignore the Em7♭5 entirely, treat A7 as an **altered dominant** resolving to its "one" (Dm), and solo the **melodic minor scale built off A7's own ♭9 (B♭)** — i.e. B♭ melodic minor, the standard altered-scale-as-melodic-minor-a-half-step-above device. → [[The ii-V-I]], [[Scale Modes Reference]] § Melodic Minor (VII-degree/altered-scale relationship), [[Altered Chords]], [[Diminished Chords]]
- **E♭7 → F (backdoor dominant #2, resolving to "the five chord of the key, kind of" rather than straight to I) — same melodic-minor-off-the-5th device as backdoor #1, applied to a different target.** 5th of E♭ = B♭, so solo **B♭ melodic minor** over the E♭7. Confirms the device isn't tied to resolving specifically to the tonic — it's a general answer for any backdoor-shaped (♭VII7-type) dominant regardless of where it resolves. → [[Chord Substitution]] § 3 (Backdoor substitution), [[Scale Modes Reference]] § Melodic Minor
- **A7 → A half-diminished ("interlocking ii-V," minor flavor) — the minor-key twin of the F7→Fm7 device above, this time resolving to a half-diminished chord on the dominant's own root.** Target tones: the **3rd of A7 (C♯) falling to the ♭3 of Aø7 (C)**, and separately the **5th of A7 (E) falling to the ♭5 of Aø7 (E♭)** — two independent half-step guide-tone drops available over the same change, either one usable on its own. → [[The ii-V-I]], [[Guide Tones]], [[Diminished Chords]] (the half-diminished-family chord)
- **G7 → minor I (B section, plain 5-to-minor-1) — solo with the melodic minor a half step above the dominant's root, here A♭ melodic minor over G7.** Same altered-scale mechanism as the Em7♭5-A7-Dm7 entry above (melodic minor rooted a half step above the dominant), restated on a bare 5-to-minor-1 rather than a full minor ii-V. → [[The ii-V-I]] § *The minor version*, [[Scale Modes Reference]] § Melodic Minor
- **Backdoor dominant #3 (B section closer, "go into one like it did previously in the song") — same melodic-minor-off-the-5th device recurring a third time in the same tune**, confirming it as Stella's dominant recurring color rather than a one-off. → [[Chord Substitution]] § 3 (Backdoor substitution)
- **Closing minor ii-V walkdown — a chain of minor ii-Vs that has to be transposed down a whole step at each repetition rather than treated as one static shape.** Stated practice instruction: "make sure to move these through each one of these two-fives... be able to move them down a whole step each time" — i.e. drill the same minor-ii-V vocabulary (Em7♭5/A7-family language, per the entries above) as a movable shape rather than memorizing it fixed to one key center. → [[The ii-V-I]] § *Chains*, [[Turnarounds]]
- **Practice method for the whole tune, stated as the actual point of the lesson: categorize sounds by type (not by tune), then go transcribe other players' solos specifically listening for where they use that same categorized sound, and steal it.** Framed as the reason knowing "technically what's going on" matters at all — the harmonic labels aren't the goal, they're a filing system for what to listen for when transcribing. Demonstrated by Borton playing one full worked chorus combining every device above. → [[Solo Analysis and Concept Extraction]]

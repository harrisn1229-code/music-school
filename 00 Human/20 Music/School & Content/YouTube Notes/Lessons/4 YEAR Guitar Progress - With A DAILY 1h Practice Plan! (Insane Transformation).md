## Synopsis

A self-critique progress retrospective (2017-2020 footage compared to 2021), narrated year by year, naming specific technique failures at each stage and what fixed them — most notably an open-fingered picking-hand grip that caused audible string-scraping noise, corrected to a curled, tighter grip. Closes with the concrete daily practice structure credited for the improvement.

## What's taught

- **2017 → 2018:** open-hand picking grip (fingers not curled) caused constant unwanted string noise from fingers touching strings during fast passages; full-arm picking motion for speed rather than an isolated wrist motion, described in retrospect as physically exhausting (soreness in back/shoulders/arm after sessions) and a sign of poor technique, not just a stamina issue. Left-hand: flat finger angle (not curled), inconsistent thumb position, pronounced "flying pinky."
- **2019:** picking-hand noise improved noticeably; a Kemper amp's more transparent tone (vs. heavy distortion) is credited with *exposing* technique flaws that a muddier, more distorted tone had been hiding — reframed as a diagnostic tool, not just a tone upgrade. Left-hand pinky-flying persisted as the main remaining problem at this stage.
- **2020-2021 (the actual fix, stated as the video's most useful concrete tip): switched pick grip from an open-fingers grip to a grip where the picking-hand fingers are curled/closed around the thumb, pick exposed at the tip only.** Directly eliminated the picking-hand-fingers-scraping-the-strings noise problem named as a running issue since 2017 — described as feeling unnatural at first (a placement/hold test without a pick even recommended before adding the pick back in) but quickly becoming the new natural default. Also allowed lifting the picking fingers cleanly off the strings during fast passages, rather than dragging across them.
- **The practice structure credited for the whole arc:** waking at 4:30 AM six days a week (looser sessions on the seventh), working 4-5 rotating topics for 15-20 minutes each (named: alternate picking, sweep picking, legato, chord theory, scale theory, improvisation), varying which blocks get worked each session to keep coverage broad and avoid staleness.

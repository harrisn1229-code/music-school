# how to make authentic house beats for drake's 'honestly, nevermind'!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E63Q8-gXoMU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E63Q8-gXoMU)

## Synopsis

loner reverse-engineers the production language of Drake's *Honestly, Nevermind* (2022) — a house-influenced album — and remakes a beat in its style, breaking down drums, melodic percussion, vocal chops, chords, ambient pads, bass variety, and arrangement along the way. The throughline is genuine house-music lineage rather than a surface Drake impression: the video repeatedly traces the album's moves back to 80s Chicago house DJ culture, specifically the 1980 Loleatta Holloway acapella "Love Sensation" that Chicago DJs sampled and remixed 300+ times (heard, per this video, on Black Box's "Ride on Time," one of the songs that flipped that same acapella) — the same tradition Drake's team is knowingly continuing with vocal chops and bouncy house piano chords. Most valuable here: the specific TR-909-inspired-but-not-909-sampled drum approach (thumpy kick + real acoustic hi-hat samples + a delay on the main hat, learned from listening to "Texts Go Green"), the melodic-percussion one-shot idea (a repeating soft synth bell, inspired by "Massive"), the vocal-chop recipe (reverb + a delay set to a DIFFERENT time than the hi-hat delay — the stated key to swing — + a Pan effect), the house piano chain (cheap/non-vintage piano sound → reverb → EQ filtering → delay → heavy compression), the varied-bass-source lesson (a guitar bass on one track vs. an 80s-style DX7 synth-bass preset with micro pitch shift on another), and a concrete generalizable EQ-automation trick (map a knob via "last tweaked," then automate a filter band's frequency to slowly build in high end across a bridge section). The video closes with three explicit takeaways for producers outside house: melodic percussion works in any genre's samples (R&B, trap), bounce should be designed into every element (not just one-chord-per-bar chord writing), and arrangement structure (drums-first intros, pulling drums out for a melody-only section, bringing them back) is worth stealing regardless of genre.

## Techniques

**Drums**
- **TR-909-inspired feel, not TR-909 samples** — house's drum-machine DNA (a "hard-thumping" kick mixed with real acoustic hi-hat samples) traces back to the Roland TR-909; rather than pulling literal 909 samples, the remake uses a **thumpy kick + real acoustic hi-hat samples** to match Drake's album feel specifically. → [[Roland TR-909]], [[Kick Design]]
- **Basic four-on-the-floor kick pattern + extra low-velocity notes for swing** — the core kick pattern is straight four-on-the-floor with additional ghost notes programmed at low velocity layered in specifically to add swing/bounce, rather than relying on the DAW's swing/groove setting alone. → [[SUB Slot]], [[BASS Slot]], [[Kick Design]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **Delay on the main hi-hat, sourced from listening to "Texts Go Green"** — on close listen, the main hi-hat in "Texts Go Green" carries a delay; recreated here with **Fruity Delay** (owned equivalent: [[Delay]] — Ableton's stock delay). → [[Delay]], [[1 Drums Hat Track]]
- **Three-hi-hat-part house pattern** — a simple four-step hat following the kick, a separate open-hat part, and a third hat used purely as a simple fill at the very end of the loop — three distinct roles rather than one hat pattern doing everything.

**Melodic percussion**
- **A repeating soft synth-bell one-shot as melodic percussion, inspired by "Massive"** — one of the album's standout tracks has a soft synth bell that repeats the same single note through the whole song; recreated with a bell one-shot from the creator's own upcoming "Leo's Volume 2" kit, laid down as a very simple repeating pattern. Flagged in the video's own takeaways as portable outside house — a filtered tonal perc works in an R&B sample just as well as a percussive vocal chop works in a hard/dark trap sample.

**Vocal chops**
- **Genre-lineage vocal sampling, traced explicitly to house's origin** — in 1980, Loleatta Holloway's *Love Sensation* acapella was sampled/remixed 300+ times by Chicago DJs (heard on Black Box's "Ride on Time," which sampled that same acapella), making it one of the most iconic vocals in house history; Drake's album continues that specific tradition of sampling vocals in house music, and the video does too. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Chop one small part from a short vocal loop (sourced from Splice)** rather than chopping a whole phrase — a single small fragment does the melodic/percussive job.
- **Vocal chop chain: reverb → delay (set to a DIFFERENT time than the hi-hat delay) → Pan effect** — the delay-time *mismatch* between the hi-hat delay and the vocal-chop delay is called out explicitly as "a massive key to creating swing" — two elements each delayed but on different clocks reads as loose/alive rather than mechanical. → [[Delay]], [[Reverb]]

**Chords / house piano**
- **A "cheap," non-vintage piano sound as the house-chord source, deliberately avoiding a realistic/vintage patch** — bouncy melodic pianos are called out as essential to the house sound (again traced to Black Box's "Ride on Time," another Loleatta Holloway "Love Sensation" flip); the choice to use a cheaper/less-realistic piano patch rather than a vintage-modeled one is deliberate, not a budget compromise.
- **House piano chain: reverb (for size/bigness) → EQ (heavily filtering out a lot of frequencies, setting up the later automation trick below) → Fruity Delay → heavy compression.** → [[Delay]], [[Compressor]], [[Reverb]]
- **Bounce as a genre-agnostic lesson** — explicitly called out as the single most portable idea for producers who don't make house: rather than a simple one-chord-per-bar approach, use bouncy house chord-writing as inspiration toward more creative, groove-driven progressions in any genre.

**Ambient pads**
- **Two layered one-shots from an unreleased sample pack ("Layers Volume 2") build the pad, each on its own simple chain** — layer 1: reverb + Fruity Delay + EQ. Layer 2: a second one-shot from the same pack layered on top (chain not separately detailed). "40 and Drake" (a reference to Noah "40" Shebib, Drake's longtime producer) triggers an ambient-pad expectation the album delivers on. → [[1 Synth Bed Track]]

**Bass — varied sources, not one formula**
- **"Currents"-style track uses a guitar bass**; **"Falling Back"-style track uses a typical 80s synth bass, the kind you'd hear from a Yamaha DX7** — the album doesn't commit to one bass source, it varies the bass character track to track. → [[Bass Design]], [[Bassline Writing]]
- **DX7 VST from Arturia, "Rebound Bass" preset, with a little micro pitch-shift added on top** — the specific patch used to recreate the 80s synth-bass character (unowned — see wishlist). → [[Plugin Wishlist]]

**Arrangement**
- **Drums-first intro, 4 bars** — many songs on the album start with just the drums, no melody yet; the remake does the same for its first 4-bar loop.
- **16 bars of melody-only with a sweep-down transition** — drums drop out entirely for the next 16 bars, leaving only the melodic elements, with a sweep-down effect signaling the section change.
- **16 bars of drums back in** to progress the beat after the melody-only section.
- **Bridge/pre-hook: automate an EQ band's frequency to slowly introduce high end, building into the hook.** Concrete, DAW-agnostic automation workflow demonstrated (FL Studio specifics, portable idea): click the target knob, then use the host's "last tweaked"/"last touched" control-surface shortcut to grab that exact parameter for automation, and draw a slow rise so the filtered-out highs from the earlier EQ move gradually return across the bridge — a build technique, not just a mix move. → [[Automation]], [[EQ]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Drums re-enter for the bridge's final 4 bars, alongside a riser sound** (from the same upcoming "Leo's Volume 2" kit) to punch into the hook.

**Stated takeaways (video's own closing list)**
1. Melodic percussion travels across genres — a filtered tonal perc in an R&B sample, or a percussive vocal chop in a hard/dark trap sample.
2. Design bounce into every element rather than defaulting to one-chord-per-bar writing — let house chords inspire more creative progressions generally.
3. Structure more adventurously — drums-first starts, filtering elements out, bringing them back in — regardless of genre. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

## Synopsis

A catalog of 17 rhythmic/melodic figurations for realizing a chord progression as an actual piano accompaniment part, each with a real song example — full extraction moved to its own reusable page since this had no prior home in the vault.

## What's taught

See [[Piano Chord Accompaniment Patterns]] for the full 17-pattern catalog (held chords, quarter/eighth-note pulses, top-bottom pattern, arpeggio variants, Alberti bass, tresillo/dembow, 3:2 and 4:3 polyrhythms, waltz and "Turkish march" oom-pah patterns, staccato comping) and the closing arrangement principle (choosing a register that doesn't collide with the rest of the band). That page is the master record, since this technique catalog is reusable well beyond this one video.

One framing point worth calling out here specifically: the video explicitly notes that *Don't Look Back in Anger* (Oasis) reuses the exact top-bottom accompaniment pattern **and chord progression** of *Imagine* (John Lennon) — patterns (and even progressions) aren't owned, borrowing one for a new song is standard songwriting practice, not derivative in a bad sense.

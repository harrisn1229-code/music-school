# Has Tom Jones ripped off Radiohead?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E6u3NHQcL8U)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E6u3NHQcL8U)

## Synopsis

A same-format entry in the channel's "rip off" mini-series (see [[18 Songs That 'Rip Off' Other Hits]], [[7 Songs That 'Rip Off' Other Tunes]]): compares Tom Jones's "Talking Reality Television Blues" to Radiohead's "I Might Be Wrong," finding strong instrumental similarity despite no credited connection.

## Techniques

- **A concrete similarity-analysis checklist for comparing two tracks' A-sections, useful as a general method beyond this specific pair**: same key, similar tempo (differing by only ~7 BPM), similar electric guitar tone, near-identical guitar riff built over a similar bass line climbing D→F→G, and a shared device — both pedal a high D note on the offbeat (Tom Jones's version occasionally substitutes A for D). B-sections diverge more but share one specific device: both end their phrase on a deliberately incomplete/short bar — Radiohead skips 2 quarter notes fairly subtly; the Tom Jones track skips 5 eighth notes far more overtly, producing an audible "chopped up" feel rather than a subtle omission.
- **The legal distinction that determines whether instrumental similarity alone is enough for a copyright claim, stated as a general principle**: melody is normally the legally decisive element (it's what most reliably "distinguishes" one song from another) — contrasted against Lana Del Rey's "Get Free" vs. Radiohead's "Creep" (which DID lead to a dispute, because that case turned on matching vocal MELODY). This Tom Jones track has no vocal melody at all, so any claim would have to rest purely on shared chords/rhythm/bassline/tone/"vibe" — normally a much weaker basis, EXCEPT that Marvin Gaye's estate won exactly that kind of "vibe"-only case against "Blurred Lines" (Robin Thicke/Pharrell), establishing that groove/instrumentation-level similarity CAN be legally actionable even without melodic overlap. Useful as a general two-tier framework: melody-match claims are the strong/traditional case, vibe/groove-only claims are a weaker but now-precedented alternative route.

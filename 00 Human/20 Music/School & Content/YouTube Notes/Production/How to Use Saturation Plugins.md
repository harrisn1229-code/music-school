# How to Use Saturation Plugins

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0XtjaRN3j4E)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0XtjaRN3j4E)

## Synopsis

A practical walkthrough of three saturation plugins at increasing complexity (Softube Saturation Knob, Softube Harmonix, and the owned Saturn 2), organized around a mixing-vs-mastering usage split and a concrete THD ceiling for mastering-stage saturation.

## Techniques

- **A concrete THD ceiling for mastering-stage saturation: keep it under ~1% total harmonic distortion.** On an individual instrument/track, saturation can be pushed to intense, obvious distortion — but on a master bus, keep it subtle, targeting under roughly 1% THD, since the master is the whole mix and errs toward "enhance," not "color." → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **There is no universally "best" saturation type — different types genuinely sound and behave differently, so A/B multiple types on the actual source material rather than defaulting to one.** Different components (tube, tape, transistor, transformer) produce different harmonic formations, different onset points for saturation, and different compression knee character — which aspects of an instrument or mix get emphasized varies by type, so the "right" choice is source-dependent, not fixed. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Saturn 2's modulation section can make almost every parameter (distortion type, saturation amount, tone controls) program-dependent — start from a preset and inspect its filters to learn the technique rather than building from scratch.** This lets a signal's own dynamics/timbre drive the saturator's behavior in real time (comparable to, but broader than, the transient-triggered Drive-modulation technique already logged on this plugin) — powerful but complex enough that the video's own recommendation is to load a preset and reverse-engineer its modulation routing before attempting to build one from an empty state. Saturn 2's per-band (multiband) and mid-side saturation, plus per-band frequency emphasis before distortion (targeting more distortion at specific frequencies), make it the most flexible of the three plugins covered. → [[Saturn 2]]
- **A structural note on a mid-tier saturator worth knowing even unowned: a "Master Desk"-style saturation mode specifically designed to leave transients untouched — useful vocabulary for describing what a bus/master saturator should NOT do to a signal's attack.** Softube's Harmonix (unowned) offers this as one of several character options alongside tube and "modern"/triode-tube modes, plus separate low-cut/high-cut filters to keep saturation from touching specific frequency ranges, and a Character dial to bias distortion toward lows or highs before it's applied. Not tracked on the wishlist — Saturn 2 already covers the vault's practical saturation needs — but the "transient-transparent saturation mode" concept is worth remembering when evaluating any saturator. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]

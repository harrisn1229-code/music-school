# Simplify Music Theory By Learning These 4 Simple Rules

## Synopsis
An earlier (2019), more basic Daniel Seriff lesson that teaches the exact same "four rules of the major scale" framework he later expanded on in [[BIGGEST Music Theory MYTH That Will RUIN Your Understanding]] (2024) — the two videos are a matched pair, same instructor, same four rules, different worked examples and different pitch of difficulty (this one aimed squarely at an intermediate guitarist just learning to spell scales; the 2024 video leans into the "weird" enharmonics like E♯/C♭ as its hook). Before the rules, he sets up three prerequisites: know the notes on the guitar neck, understand half-steps (one fret) vs. whole-steps (two frets), and know the major scale's step formula (W-W-H-W-W-W-H) — plus the framing point that **"keys and scales are the same thing"** (playing in the key of C major = using the notes of the C major scale). He then walks all four rules through four different worked examples on guitar (E major, A major, F major, D♭ major) — a useful complementary set to the 2024 video's F♯/G♭/C♯/C♭ examples, since together the two videos now demonstrate the rules across sharp keys, flat keys, a single-accidental key, and a "complicated" multi-flat key. Closes with the same lead-magnet PDF plug (four rules + "15 logical major scales" worksheet) that the 2024 video also ends on, suggesting it's a recurring evergreen offer for this channel rather than video-specific.

## What's taught
- **Keys and scales are the same thing** — "playing in the key of C major" means "using the notes of the C major scale"; a point he flags as often left implicit in other teaching. (~2:15) → [[Keys]]
- **Three prerequisites before spelling a scale:** know the notes on the guitar neck, know half-step (1 fret, including open strings) vs. whole-step (2 frets), know the major scale formula is **whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half** (W-W-H-W-W-W-H). (~1:00–2:00) → [[Scales]] (major scale step pattern already documented)
- **Rule 1 — no mixing sharps and flats in the same key.** Worked live on the high E string starting on E: E→F♯(W)→G♯(W)→A(H)→B(W)→C♯(W)→ then the 7th degree is spelled D♯, explicitly rejected calling it "D♭" because the key had already committed to sharps. Names the **sharp keys**: G, D, A, E, B, F♯, C♯; the **flat keys**: F, B♭, E♭, A♭, D♭, G♭, C♭. Resulting E major scale: E F♯ G♯ A B C♯ D♯ E. (~2:40–4:10) → [[Scales]] (Spelling rules §1, already documented from the sibling video — same rule, corroborating source)
- **Rule 2 — all seven letters of the alphabet must be present, none skipped.** Worked on A major starting 5th fret high E string: A→B(W)→C♯(W)→D(H)→E(W)→ then catches himself about to say "G♭" for the 6th degree, flags that this would violate *both* rule 1 (mixing flat into a sharp key) and rule 2 (skipping the letter F entirely) — names the collision explicitly as a teaching moment. Resolves to F♯, then G♯, back to A. Resulting A major scale: A B C♯ D E F♯ G♯ A. (~4:20–5:40) → [[Scales]] (Spelling rules §2, corroborating source)
- **Rule 3 — never use two different accidental versions of the same letter in one key.** Worked on F major starting 1st fret thinnest (high E) string: F→G(W)→A(W)→ then catches himself about to call the next note "A♯," rejects it because A is already used, resolves to **B♭** instead. Resulting F major scale: F G A B♭ C D E F — the "one-accidental" key, called out as a key that trips people up because it's easy to reach for A♯ instead of B♭ without the letter-reuse check. (~6:00–7:20) → [[Scales]] (Spelling rules §3, corroborating source)
- **Rule 4 — the next scale degree is always the next letter of the musical alphabet**, wrapping G back to A, regardless of how "complicated" the key looks. Worked on D♭ major starting 2nd fret B string: D♭→E♭(W)→F(W, natural — not E♯, because rule 4 demands the letter after E♭ is F)→G♭(H)→A♭(W)→B♭(W)→C(W, natural)→D♭. Resulting D♭ major scale: D♭ E♭ F G♭ A♭ B♭ C D♭ — picked deliberately as the "complicated-looking" example to show the rule holds even when several degrees land on natural notes inside a flat key. (~8:00–9:40) → [[Scales]] (Spelling rules §4, corroborating source)
- **Explicit framing that rules 3 and 4 are "somewhat redundant" with rules 1/2 but worth teaching separately** — approaching the same constraint from multiple angles (mixing check, letter-skip check, letter-reuse check, next-letter check) is presented as what actually makes the rule set click for students who've struggled with scale spelling before. (~5:45–6:00, ~7:25–7:40) — a teaching-method note, not new theory content.
- **Free downloadable PDF**: "four rules of the major scale" one-pager + a "15 logical major scales" worksheet, offered via the instructor's site (danielseraf.com) — not itself instructional content, noted for completeness. (~10:00–10:40)

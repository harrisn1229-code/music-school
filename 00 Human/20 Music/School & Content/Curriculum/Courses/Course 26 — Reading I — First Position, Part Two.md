# Course 26 — Reading I — First Position, Part Two

The second half of Section One of Leavitt's *A Modern Method for Guitar, Volume 1* — pages 30 to 59, which finishes everything the book does without leaving the open first position: six more major keys, three minors in all three of their forms, sixteenths and triplets and dotted figures, three right-hand techniques, and the eleven movable chord forms the section ends on.

**What it assumes**

- [[Course 25 — Reading I — First Position, Part One]] — pages 3 to 29 read at tempo: the first position in C, accidentals, eighth notes, rests, ties and dots. None of it is re-taught here.
- A guitar, [[A Modern Method for Guitar - Volume 1.pdf]] open on a stand, and a metronome.
- No theory. This course teaches no ideas and tests none — it is the place you play the page.

| Lesson | Title | Pages | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sixteenths, Triplets and the Relative Minor | 30–44 | performance check |
| 2 | Movable Forms and the Remaining Open Keys | 45–59 | performance check |

**What this unlocks**

- [[Course 27 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part One]] — Section Two, which drops open strings entirely and moves the hand up the neck. It assumes the first position is fluent, because everything after page 60 is the same reading skill relocated.
- First-position reading in C, G, F, B♭, D, A and E♭, plus A minor, E minor and D minor in all three minor forms.
- The eleven movable chord forms and the Chord Simplification and Substitution Chart (p. 58) — enough comping vocabulary to play the accompaniment to a song in any key.
- Alternate picking, reverse alternate picking and the older down-stroke-per-string technique, each taught as its own etude rather than as a note in the margin.

## How to run it

- **It teaches reading through repertoire, not drills.** Concept pages are sparse — across pages 30 to 59 it is pp. 31, 37 and 42, plus the scale page that opens each new key. The rest is melodies, duets and etudes you sight-read, and progress comes from playing them.
- **The duets need a second part.** Leavitt is explicit that there is no student/teacher division — both guitar parts are written to be studied, and almost all of them stand alone musically. A recording, a teacher, or DAW playback of the other line all work.
- **Review is not optional.** *"REVIEW ALL MATERIAL"* is printed at pp. 37 and 52, after the milder *"Regular review of all material is a must!"* at p. 9 and *"Review of all material is a must"* at p. 18. Finished pages stay in rotation, and that includes everything from Part One.
- **From the preface:** *"Do not skip or 'slight' anything, and also do not attempt to 'completely perfect' any one lesson before going on. Playing technique is an accumulative process."*
- **Page numbers below are the book's own printed numbers.** One of them is absent from the vault's scan — p. 34 (Another Duet in G). It is named in the homework anyway, because it is part of the lesson; source it elsewhere or skip it knowingly.
- **The keys arrive one page at a time.** Each new key opens with its scale in the first position and its rhythm-accompaniment chord diagrams on the same page, then the duet or solo that reads it. Play the scale before the piece, every time.

## The lessons

### Lesson 1 — Sixteenths, Triplets and the Relative Minor

**The material**

- **Pages 30–44.** Two new major keys, three new rhythmic figures, the relative minor in two keys, and four duets to read them in.
- **Concept pages** — p. 31 sixteenth notes; p. 37 the triplet, with both picking types to be practised separately; p. 42 the dotted eighth and sixteenth. The grand barre arrives with the key of F on p. 35.
- **The relative-minor rule**, stated in one paragraph on p. 39: *"The sixth degree of any major scale is the tonic of its relative minor key. The major and relative minor key signatures are the same. There are 3 different scales in each minor key."*
- **Page 34 is missing from the vault's scan.** *Another Duet in G* is listed in the book's own contents and named below; source it elsewhere or skip it knowingly.

### Lesson 2 — Movable Forms and the Remaining Open Keys

**The material**

- **Pages 45–59.** The remaining open-position keys, the chromatic scale, three right-hand techniques taught as etudes, and the chord chapter the whole rhythm track has been building toward.
- **The principle of movable chord forms, p. 45** — a form with no open strings can be slid up the neck, which is what makes eleven shapes enough for any key. It is also the idea Section Two applies to scales from p. 60 on.
- **Page 58 collects all eleven movable forms** and adds the Chord Simplification and Substitution Chart. Leavitt's own caveat: *"having only eleven chord forms at your command will cause you to move up and down the fingerboard much more than is desirable… The more forms you know, the less distance you have to travel."*
- **Page 59 teaches a second picking technique** — attack each new string with a down stroke — and is explicit that it is an addition to alternate picking, not a replacement: *"THE MOST CONCENTRATED EFFORT MUST STILL BE PLACED ON ALTERNATE PICKING."*

**Homework**

- [ ] **Play** — *Rhythm Accompaniment: The Principle of Movable Chord Forms*, p. 45, and the exercises under it.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Chromatic Scale*, 1st position, p. 46, and *Speed Studies* patterns 1–3 on the same page, in both octaves.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Key of D Minor* scales, 1st position, p. 47 — all three forms — and the *Rhythm Accompaniment* exercises below them.
- [ ] **Play** — *Endurance Etude* (*Picking Etude No. 3*), pp. 48–49. Observe the tempo changes and vary the dynamics.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Key of B♭* scales, 1st position, p. 50, and the *Rhythm Accompaniment* exercises below them.
- [ ] **Play** — *Duet in B♭*, p. 51. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — *Reverse Alternate Picking Study*, p. 52. Strict attention to the picking as marked.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Key of D Major* scale, 1st position, p. 53, and *Duet in D* below it. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — *Dynamic Etude* (*Etude No. 4*, duet), pp. 54–55. Both parts, and play the dynamics rather than reading past them.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Key of A* scale, 1st position, p. 56, *Duet in A* below it, and the *Rhythm Accompaniment* exercises on the same page.

- [ ] **Play** — the *Movable Chord Forms* compilation, p. 58 — all eleven, chromatically up and down the neck — and read the Chord Simplification and Substitution Chart under it.

## Course final

A **performance check**, not a test page. There are no `type: school-test` pages in this course and nothing here enters the spaced-repetition drip — sight reading is a motor skill, and the drip is for facts.

The check is a rep. Open the book at an unseen page at or below p. 59, play it **once, at tempo, with no practice run**, and keep an even tempo through the wrong notes rather than stopping to fix them. Pass is your own honest read of whether it held together. Nothing in the system can hear the guitar, so nothing here pretends to grade it.

Reading decays the way ear training does. The course closing is not a reason to stop reading — it is the point at which [[Course 27 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part One]] takes over and Section One becomes review material.

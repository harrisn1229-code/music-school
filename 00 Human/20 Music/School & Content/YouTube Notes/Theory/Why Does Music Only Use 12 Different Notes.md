# Why Does Music Only Use 12 Different Notes?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lvmzgVtZtUQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lvmzgVtZtUQ)

## Synopsis

A foundations-level explainer on why Western music settled on 12 notes per octave and why the actual tuning of those 12 notes (12-tone equal temperament) is a deliberate historical compromise rather than a physical inevitability. No new home existed for this material in the vault, so it seeded a new reference note.

## Techniques

- **Intervals, not frequencies, are what matter** — a melody is a sequence of intervals; transposing preserves the melody because the relationships, not the absolute pitches, are the content.
- **The octave (2:1 ratio)** is the near-universal anchor for tuning systems worldwide, because notes an octave apart are perceived as the same note in a different register (octave equivalence).
- **Consonance ranking tracks ratio simplicity**: octave (2:1) > perfect 5th (3:2) > perfect 4th (4:3) > major 3rd > minor 3rd > major 6th > minor 6th, then the traditionally dissonant intervals (major 2nd, minor 7th, tritone, major 7th, minor 2nd) — still musically useful for tension, just less consonant.
- **12 notes is the practical sweet spot**: enough to access all the useful intervals while keeping notes roughly evenly spaced (needed for playability across keys). Adding a couple of extra microtonal notes unevenly destroys that spacing.
- **Alternatives exist but cost playability**: 19-note and 24-note (quarter-tone) octave divisions both preserve the standard intervals while adding microtonal access, at the cost of a much harder-to-play instrument (Ivan Wyschnegradsky's 24-note quarter-tone piano cited as a real example).
- **Just intonation vs. 12-tone equal temperament**: just intonation tunes every interval to a pure ratio relative to the root, sounding gorgeous in one key but progressively more out-of-tune in others (the pure ratios don't transfer when the root changes). 12-TET instead tunes only the octave purely and divides it into 12 EQUAL steps, so every key is equally (im)pure — the standard tradeoff that makes an instrument playable in any key without retuning.
- **The audible cost of equal temperament**: 4ths/5ths are only ~2 cents off pure (inaudible); the major 3rd is over 13 cents sharp of pure, audible as a "beating"/wah-wah effect in a major triad — which disappears if the chord is retuned to just intonation.

## Production & Arrangement
One of McCartney's earliest songs (written as a teenager, cut for Beatles for Sale, 1964) — a gentle, bright acoustic ballad. Notably Ringo played light percussion by **slapping his knees / a packing case** rather than a full kit, keeping it soft and intimate. Simple major-key warmth with a sweet descending melodic turn.

## Steal this
- **Soft-percussion substitute:** knee-slaps / boxes / brushes ([[Hand Clap Studio]], light perc) instead of a kit on a gentle song.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — I'll Follow The Sun]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #60.*

**Form.** The acoustic surface says folk song; **the chords and the tune are not remotely folk**. He reads it as the same blending trick they had been running with blues, with folk elements substituted in, and hears the verse harmony as carrying a deliberate or at least unfixed awkwardness — a 1960 Quarrymen tape has the same form in G with the melody sitting badly on the chords, and the released version reharmonises heavily without fully smoothing it out. Textbook two-bridge model. The intro is **2 bars, and turns out to be nothing but the last two bars of the verse pre-announced** — the same two bars serve as the outro. Verse 8 bars, scanning as two 4-bar phrases metrically but melodically a single long phrase; bridge 8, two genuinely parallel phrases.

**Harmony.** `Intro / verse tail: I – V – IV – I` · `Verse: V – IV7 – I – V-of-V · I – iii(6/4) – V-of-V – V – I – V – IV – I` · `Bridge: ii – iv – I – V-of-IV ‖ ii – iv – I – ii`. Three curveballs in the verse: an **F7 that never resolves to B♭** (a dominant seventh used for colour, not function), a **V-of-V whose resolution is deferred** ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]), and iii turning up in 6/4. Both verse and bridge start away from the tonic and converge on it; the bridge then closes on **ii**, exactly what the following verse needs since the verse opens on V.

**Notable.** What holds the verse together is a **concealed chromatic line cliché in an inner voice** — D – E♭ – E – F♯ — implied purely by voice leading rather than played out loud, plus a speed-up in harmonic rhythm across the second half. The bridge answers with the same device made explicit, a descending A – A♭ – G carried by John's vocal counterpoint, which is what produces the **minor iv** ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]), rare in a Paul song of this period. The verse tune is a series of **upward leaps of a fourth** across an unusually wide arch ([[MEL-C01 — Melodic contour]]), and there is **no percussion part at all**.

**Against this page.** Agrees on C major.

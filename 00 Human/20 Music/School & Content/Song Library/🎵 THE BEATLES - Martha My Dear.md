## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Martha My Dear]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #137.*

**Form.** The polite parlour-song surface hides a **14½-bar verse**, a chain of secondary dominants, and a bridge that lurches into a key a whole tone up without preparation — and none of it announces itself. An unusually large share of the running time is instrumental, and that drives the whole layout: **the intro is a complete verse section played without a vocal**, which means only one sung verse can precede the first bridge, and the mid-song instrumental verse leaves the closing verse as the only other sung one. **Two sung verses in the entire song.** There are two bridges, but the extension appears after the first only. The verse's 14½ bars run in four phrases with the first cut short by six beats ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]), and the cause is melodic — the opening fragment repeats itself in a Charleston-style syncopation that pushes two extra beats into the first line. The bridge is a square 16, paying the verse's metrical debt; the extension goes back to irregularity with **three-bar phrases**.

**Harmony.** `Verse (E♭): I – I – I / V-of-iii · iii – V-of-V-of-V – V-of-V – V-of-V · V – IV9 – V7 – IV7 · V7 – IV7 – V7`. That second phrase is a **descending chain of secondary dominants** ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]) aimed so hard at B♭ that the section's centre of gravity sits there rather than on E♭ — and B♭ is only retroactively confirmed as **V, not I**. `Bridge (F): vi7 – ii7 · I(add6) held 4 · ii – V – ii – V / V-of-vi [over a C pedal] · vi – ii7` — a real key change made to feel more remote than a whole step by being entered and left with no transition at all ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]), with a **bass pedal on C** through the third phrase ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]). The way home is a **root move down a major third**, G minor to E♭, functioning as iii→I.

**Notable.** A jazzy, blues-tinged E♭ where the A♭ chords all arrive as 9ths and 7ths that never resolve as suspensions would. The arrangement is strictly additive over a **dry, close-miked, un-pedalled piano**, and the one structural surprise is that **drums enter at the bridge extension** — a passage that occurs exactly once.

**Against this page.** Agrees on E♭ major (this page's capo-3 C shapes).

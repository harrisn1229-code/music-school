# How David Bowie uses Modes

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c-bLHPh5SNc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c-bLHPh5SNc)

## Synopsis

Four David Bowie songs analyzed as a compact tour through different flavors of modal writing: a fully committed Mixolydian vamp ("Golden Years"), modal interchange where the harmony borrows but the melody doesn't follow ("Heroes"), a Mixolydian vamp combined with Phrygian false-relation dissonance in the same song ("Aladdin Sane"), and a genuine mode-to-mode pivot within one parent scale, deliberately smoothed over by a pentatonic melody ("Let's Dance").

## Techniques

- **A fully committed Mixolydian vamp, not just a borrowed chord**: "Golden Years" is built entirely on an F♯ Mixolydian I–♭VII vamp (F♯ to E major); the vocal melody itself lands on the ♭7 scale degree, confirming the mode is structural rather than a passing color chord. Contrasted directly against "Heroes" below as the "real mode vs. borrowed chord" test case.
- **Modal interchange test: does the melody follow the borrowed chord's altered note, or stay diatonic?** "Heroes" verse is plain D major; the chorus lands on a C major chord and an A minor chord, both borrowed from D Mixolydian (C is the ♭VII). But the vocal melody never touches the borrowed C♮ — it stays inside D major throughout — which is the evidence used to call this modal interchange (a borrow) rather than a genuine key-wide Mixolydian passage like "Golden Years." → [[Modal Interchange]] (a clean second example of the melody-stays-diatonic-while-harmony-borrows mechanism the page's opener already describes).
- **Shifting an entire modal vamp up a step mid-song, then dropping a solo over the shifted version**: "Aladdin Sane" reuses "Golden Years"'s I–♭VII Mixolydian vamp (G to F), then for one and a half bars shifts the whole vamp up a whole step (A to G) before returning — timed so Mike Garson's atonal piano solo sits over the shifted vamp. The vamp keeps running underneath the chromatic solo the whole time, anchoring an otherwise free passage back to the song's harmonic identity.
- **Phrygian ♭2 verse, plus a false relation from a riff that doesn't transpose with the chord change**: the "Aladdin Sane" verse starts on B minor (Phrygian, flattened 2nd degree), and its third chord is a C major triad where a C♯ would be diatonically expected — that C♮ is the Phrygian ♭2. A repeating piano riff built on a C♯ octave (consonant over the B minor tonic) gets reused unchanged over the C major chord, creating simultaneous C♯ and C♮ — a **false relation** (the same pitch class appearing as two different chromatic spellings at once) that produces a deliberate, controlled dissonance rather than a clash to avoid.
- **Modal pivot between two modes of the SAME parent scale (not two parallel modes sharing a tonic) — a different device from "Heroes"'s modal interchange**: "Let's Dance" verse mixes B♭ Dorian (natural 6th) and B♭ Aeolian (flat 6th) chord-to-chord, but the vocal melody is built from the minor pentatonic scale, which omits the 6th degree entirely — so the melody fits cleanly over either chord color without ever having to commit to Dorian or Aeolian. The chorus then pivots the same seven notes to a new tonic: A♭ Ionian (verse's parent major scale) becomes B♭ Dorian as the *actual tonal center*, by repeatedly resolving the E♭7 buildup chord down to B♭ minor instead of the "expected" A♭. Distinguished explicitly from "Heroes": that song stays rooted on one tonic (D) and borrows a chord from a parallel mode; this song shifts which scale degree functions as tonic within one fixed set of seven notes.

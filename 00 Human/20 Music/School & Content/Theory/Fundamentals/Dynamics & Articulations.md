# Dynamics & Articulations

Part of [[Music Theory]] → [[Notation]]. Reference glossary for the symbols that shape *how* a note is played, beyond just pitch and rhythm.

## Dynamics (how loud)
Full ladder, loudest to softest: **fff** (very very loud) → **ff** (very loud) → **f** (loud) → **mf** (medium loud) → **mp** (medium soft) → **p** (soft) → **pp** (very soft) → **ppp** (very very soft) → **n** (niente, inaudible).
- **Hairpins** (< / >) or the terms **crescendo**/**diminuendo** show gradual volume change.
- Placement: **below** the staff for instrumental parts, **above** the staff for vocal parts (stays clear of the lyrics — see [[Writing for Voice]]).
- Specific interpretation (exactly how loud is "f"?) is left to the performer — notation only shows *relative* volume.

## Articulations (how a note is treated)
| Symbol | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `>` | Accent | with additional emphasis |
| `.` | Staccato | short and detached — originally meant "half the written value" (a literal durational instruction), drifted over time to mean "detached" generally rather than strictly short |
| `–` (line) | Tenuto | emphasized, held for full value (properly "legato" in older usage, implying connection into the next note) |
| `^` | Marcato | ("marked") short and accented |
| Marcato + extra stress | Marcatissimo | heavily/more emphatically accented than plain marcato |
| (bow-heavy accent) | Martelé | bowed-strings-specific: a very heavy accent from heavy bow pressure on a down-bow |
| `▼` | Staccatissimo | very short and forceful |
| `sfz` | Sforzando | suddenly loud and accented |
| fermata (bird's-eye) | Fermata | hold longer than indicated |
| `≠` (slashes) | Tremolo | rapidly alternate between two notes |
| `V` | Up bow | (bowed strings) start at tip of bow |
| `⊓` | Down bow | (bowed strings) start at frog of bow |
| `tr` | Trill | rapidly alternate two adjacent notes |
| arpeggio wavy line | Arpeggio | "roll" the chord, notes added separately |

## Symbols affecting groups of notes
- **8va (ottava)** — play an octave higher (or lower, depending on placement); **15ma** = two octaves; **22ma** = three octaves.
- **Slur** — most commonly groups notes to be played smoothly/connected; in vocal parts, a slur over multiple notes on one syllable is a **melisma**; can also serve as a phrase marking over a larger group, showing the music's overall shape.
- **Pedaling marks** — show when the piano's damper pedal should be held down/released; older scores use different symbols for down vs. up than modern notation.

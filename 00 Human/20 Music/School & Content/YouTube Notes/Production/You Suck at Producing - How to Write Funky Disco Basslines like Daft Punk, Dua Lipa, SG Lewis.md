## Synopsis
Underbelly frames bass's job as sitting at the intersection of rhythm (locking with the kick), harmony (playing chord roots), and melody (licks between chords) — "the glue that holds the band together" — then builds four distinct, disco-influenced basslines against four increasingly energetic drum sections of the same track, all centered on the same tonal target (A). The core funk vocabulary demonstrated: syncopating off the kick, jumping the same note up/down an octave ("uppers and downers"), and chromatic stepwise walks connecting one phrase to the next, all while deliberately writing a fresh rhythmic idea for each new section rather than reusing the same pattern.

## Techniques
- **Bass has three simultaneous jobs: rhythm, harmony, melody** — rhythmic because it locks with the kick to form the groove/low end; harmonic because it plays the root note of whatever chord is currently sounding; melodic because it can carry the main hook or fill the gaps between chords. Bass sits at the intersection of all three, which is what makes it "the glue" holding a band/arrangement together. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Start a bassline by literally copying the kick pattern onto the bass track, then edit down** — paste the kick's rhythm onto the bass, crop it to a 1-bar loop, and trim away the extra hits before shaping it into an actual bassline; gives a rhythmically-locked starting point instead of writing from a blank clip. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Syncopate deliberately: move notes off the kick's strong beats onto weak/off-grid spots** — switch the grid to 16th notes and nudge some bass hits away from where the kick lands (1, 2, 3, 4) onto the weaker in-between positions; this single move is described as "the key to making a bassline funky fresh." → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Octave jumps ("uppers and downers") are the core funk-bass motion device** — take some of the syncopated notes and shift them up (or down) an octave; described as mimicking how a real funk bassist physically moves around the neck, and it's the main way these lines get their bounce. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Set the song's scale/root via Ableton's Scale feature before writing melodic motion** — pick a root (here, A) and a scale (natural minor, "90% of all music") so subsequent note choices stay in key. → [[Scale]]
- **Chromatic walk-up/walk-down connects one phrase to the next, regardless of scale** — instead of jumping straight to the next section's starting note, step chromatically (including out-of-scale passing tones) up or down to arrive at it; called out as working "regardless of what scale you're in," since it's a connective device rather than a harmonic one. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Write a genuinely new rhythmic idea for each new arrangement section, anchored to the same tonal center** — across four different drum sections (rising energy, peak energy, a new B-section idea, and a wind-down), each bassline keeps returning to the same root (A) as "home" but gets a distinct rhythm, register, and contour rather than reusing the same pattern at different intensities. → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Lower velocity on notes leading into a big arrival** — softening the notes that "walk up" toward a held target note emphasizes the arrival itself. → [[Bassline Writing]]

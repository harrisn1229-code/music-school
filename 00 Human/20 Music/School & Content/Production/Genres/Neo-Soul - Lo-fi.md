# Neo-Soul / Lo-fi

**What makes it the genre:** human time-feel (behind-the-beat everything), warm degraded texture, extended harmony (7ths/9ths/11ths as the default), and the Rhodes as the harmonic center. Perfection is the enemy — the drag and the dirt ARE the style.

## The pillars

### 1. The pocket (Dilla time)
- Drums drag: snare *late*, hats loose (negative swing quantize or nudge by hand), kick roughly on — the "drunk" feel is the tension between late backbeat and steady pulse.
- Play parts in loose, quantize barely or not at all; velocity variation everywhere.

### 2. Rhodes / keys — the center
- [[1 Electric Piano Track|Electric Piano Track]] fully: Keyscape Rhodes, tube bark, chorus, tremolo. Voicings: rootless extensions (let the bass own the root), lots of 9ths.
- Alt centers: Wurli for grit, [[Keyscape]] Pianet for the dreamier lane. [[Keyscape]]'s **"Creative" preset category** (beyond the standard piano/EP patches) is a deep source worth auditioning for jazzy chord textures. → Max Konyi, [[Jazzy LoFi Hip-Hop - Beat Breakdown]]
- **Writing the actual chords: melody-first harmonization** — write drums, then a simple diatonic top-line melody in the upper-mid register (leaves room to stack dense voicings underneath), THEN build the chords under it note by note (chord-tone or extension choice per melody note, inversions for smooth bass motion, mostly diatonic with one non-diatonic "special" chord like a tritone-sub cadence into the tonic). Full method + worked A minor example: [[Chord Progressions]] → [[How to Write Neo-Soul and R&B Chords - A Simple Method|Max Konyi]].
- **A half-diminished-centered jazzy vamp is a strong, easy default loop**: F♯ø7 → Fm6 → C9/E (over E), ambiguous between major/minor — the iiø7 half-diminished pre-dominant color (see [[Borrowed Chords|Borrowed Chords]]'s workhorse table) doing the genre's harmonic heavy lifting on its own, looped with a secondary low-octave arpeggio layer under the main voicing for movement without changing the harmony. → Max Konyi, [[Jazzy LoFi Hip-Hop - Beat Breakdown]]

### 3. Bass — round, felt, behind
- Hofner thumb-style ([[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Electric Bass DI Track]] technique section — this is exactly the modern hip hop Hofner use) or muted P-bass samples: dark (LPF ~3–5 kHz), compressed smooth ([[LALA]]), *slightly behind* the kick.

### 4. Texture — the lo-fi layer
- [[RC-20 Retro Color]] is the genre plugin: wobble + noise + crush on keys, loops, even the whole [[2 Drums Group|Drums Group]]. [[Kramer Tape]] 7.5 ips for the rounder version.
- **A concrete from-scratch RC-20 lofi preset, module by module**: **Noise** — pink over white (skip vinyl this time), quiet; **Wobble/Flux** — full flux to audition, then pulled back to a subtler amount, following the notes slightly, switched to MONO (not RC-20's stereo default); **Distort** — subtle tube for grit, skip Digital/bitcrush this pass; **Space** — big; **Magnetic** — a volume-fluctuation + flutter/tremolo layer, dialed to taste. Sidechain everything against the kick as a baseline habit — "especially in lofi" — for the genre's spacious, meaningful-kick-hit feel. → [[RC-20 Retro Color]], [[Sidechaining]] — Max Konyi, [[Jazzy LoFi Hip-Hop - Beat Breakdown]]
- Vinyl/tape noise beds *under* the mix (quiet, constant) glue sparse arrangements.
- Dark mixes on purpose: gentle global LPF tilt ([[Ghz Tiltshift 3]] darker), nothing sizzles.

### 5. Vocals — close, soft, stacked loose
- Intimate and dry-ish: light comp, no hard tune (Humanize 100 or none), doubles loose on purpose (skip the tight-alignment rules — drift is warmth here).
- Harmonies as pads: oohs/aahs LPF'd and buried ([[1 Harmony Vocal Track|Harmony Vocal Track]] pad variation), more chorus/width than a pop stack.

### 6. Mix presentation
- Small and warm beats big and bright: mono-ish placement, room verb only, master gentle (−11 to −12 LUFS, [[Master Chain]] ballad variation, skip the clipper).

## Arrangement

**Section map** — *"Didn't Cha Know"*, *"It Runs Through Me"*, PARTYNEXTDOOR *"Joy"*

| Section | Bars | Slots filled | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 4–8 | BED (Rhodes alone) · TEXTURE | 2 |
| A | 8 | BASS · BED · LEAD · TRANSIENT · TEXTURE | 5 |
| B | 8 | drums out, or keys out — **one thing leaves** | 4 |
| C | 8 | the removed thing returns, something else goes | 4 |
| D | 8 | fullest pass, then it stops | 5 |

**Entrances & exits**
- **Arrangement by subtraction across 8-bar blocks** — the *"Joy"* breakdown in the videos below builds four sections without writing a single new part. Everything you need is in the loop; the sections are which parts you mute.
- The Rhodes is in from bar 1 and defines the harmony for the whole song. It's the one thing that rarely leaves.
- **TEXTURE runs the entire song** — the vinyl/tape noise bed is constant, and it's what makes a sparse section still feel like a record.
- Bass enters last when tracking and it's the last thing you'd remove.

**The contrast move — mute one lane for eight bars.** No builds, no drops, no filter sweeps. The genre's dynamic range is tiny by design, so removing the drums for eight bars is an enormous event inside it.

**Density rule: 4–5 slots, flat.** The narrowest density swing on this shelf. The interest lives in the *pocket* and the harmony, not the structure — which is exactly why the drag and the extensions have to carry so much weight.

## Session recipe
70–95 BPM · start with the Rhodes progression (extensions first — if it sounds "jazzy" solo, it's right) · drums played loose over it · bass last, reacting to both · resist cleaning up the timing.

## From videos

- **A genuinely jazzier, 6/8 sibling lane — recognized by counting the drums in THREE, not the usual four.** PARTYNEXTDOOR's "Joy" (*P3*) sits in 6/8; the tell is the drums grouping one-two-three, one-two-three instead of one-two-three-four. Built on a Bm9–C♯m7–Amaj9 vamp (minor 9ths/7ths resolving on a major 9th with an inverted color tone — see [[Chord Inversions]]), a rolled bass-note-first chord voicing for natural bounce, and swing pushed to ~50% specifically because it's "unique" 6/8 material — the same swing-for-odd-meters habit this channel also applies to afrobeats. Arrangement built entirely by subtraction across four 8-bar sections rather than new material per section. Full recipe: [[PARTYNEXTDOOR Jazz Sample Chain]]. → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — loner, [how to make moody vintage r&b samples for partynextdoor (sounds like a real jazz sample)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wD4RzEKo7vU)

## Chill-Hop / Lo-fi-Hop (instrumental beat variant)

A closely related, instrumental-first lane — the "lo-fi beats to study/chill to" beat-tape sound — sharing this genre's jazzy extended harmony and warm, imperfect texture but built more like a drum-led beat than a vocal-centric neo-soul arrangement. Slightly brighter/tighter than the Dilla-time pillar above; less about the drag, more about a tight, high-pitched kick and a jazzy chord bed under a simple lead.

- **Beatbox the groove before programming drums** — vocalize the target beat into the track first (voice/body find a groove faster and more expressively than fingers on a MIDI grid), then program the actual drum hits to match what got beatboxed — reveals swing/feel before committing to a grid. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **86 BPM chill-hop kit: tight high-pitched kick, a second kick-sample pitched way up and repurposed as the snare, 3 panned clap layers at low volume, jazz hats, filtered "weird sample" toms, shaker + cymbals** — a full worked drum-kit build for the genre, several elements repurposed/found rather than purpose-sourced. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Jazzy extended-chord progression example:** Dm9 → Am7(11) → F♯m7♭5 → Fmaj7 → E♭9(♯11) → Dm → Am, repeat with F♯m7♭5 → Am9 → Am6 → an altered chord — a dense ii-based extended/altered vocabulary that still sits comfortably under a laid-back beat.
- **Lock harmony with a rough real-guitar take before finalizing the arrangement** — a quick, deliberately unpolished guitar pass just to confirm the chord voicings read well over the groove; refine or re-record after.
- **Muted saw lead with a mod-wheel-gated LFO pitch wobble** — LFO1 → both oscillators' pitch, but only audible once the mod wheel is raised — an expressive, performable wobble rather than a static modulation. → [[Modulation]], [[Wavetable]]
- **Distorted/filtered piano as a low-key background texture layer**, not a lead part. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Stock "chill wave" pad** reached for directly as the fast-path harmonic bed once the sketch is locked.

*Source: Max Konyi, [[How I Make Chill-Hop in Studio One]] — captions, low-medium confidence.*

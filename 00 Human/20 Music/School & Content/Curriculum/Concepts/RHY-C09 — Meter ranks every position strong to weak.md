# RHY-C09 — Meter ranks every position strong to weak

**Q.** Give the strong/weak hierarchy in a 4/4 bar, strongest first — and say what it's responsible for.

> [!question]- Answer
> **Beat 1 strongest, then 3, then the backbeats 2 and 4, then the offbeats between them** — and the ranking continues inside the beat: on the beat, then the "and", then the "e" and "a" weakest. Meter is what *creates* that ranking; an undifferentiated stream of identical beats has no structure until it's grouped, and quadruple grouping is what makes 1 and 3 strong in the first place. The hierarchy is then the thing that makes any placement sound "on" or "off" at all.

## Why it matters

It's the reference everything rhythmic is measured against — syncopation, half-time and pocket are all defined as departures from it, so none of them mean anything without it. The standard backbeat falls straight out of it too: kick on the strong 1 and 3, snare on the weak 2 and 4. Matching velocity to the ranking is also most of what makes a programmed part read as played rather than gridded.

## Drill

Say a 4/4 bar's eight eighth-note positions out loud, ranking each one strong or weak as you go.

# Bossa Nova (João Gilberto)

**What makes it the genre:** samba's percussion section compressed onto **one nylon-string guitar**, under cool-jazz harmony, played *quietly*. Born late-1950s Rio — João Gilberto invented the guitar style (classical fingerstyle technique + tamborim rhythm), Jobim supplied the songbook, Getz/Gilberto (1964) took it worldwide. The identity is intimacy: soft dynamics, close mics, straight 8ths, and a two-bar sway built on **anticipation** — nothing is loud, nothing is swung, everything breathes.

## The pillars

### 1. Guitar — the whole engine (rhythm + cadence)
- **Two jobs, one hand:** thumb = bass root/5th as ringing half notes on beats **1 and 3** (the surdo drum); index/middle/ring = short syncopated chord stabs on the top 3–4 strings (the tamborim).
- **Level 1, one-bar starter** (count straight 8ths, "1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &"): bass on 1 and 3 · chords on **2** and the **& of 3** — "boom **cha**, boom-boom **cha**."
- **Level 2, the real two-bar cadence:** bar 1 chords on 2 and & of 3; bar 2's chord arrives an **8th note early** (& of 4, tied over the barline), then again on the & of 3. That anticipation is *the* bossa sway — if a take feels close-but-stiff, this push is what's missing.
- Feel rules: **even 8ths, never swung** · bass legato while chords stay short/damped (slight fret-hand lift for the muted "chick") · ~110–140 BPM · João never looped one pattern rigidly — small bar-to-bar variations are the style, not errors.
- Chain: [[1 Acoustic Guitar Track|Acoustic Guitar Track]] (nylon ideally) — keep it dry and up front; [[Reverb Send Room]] only. Steel + fingers instead of nylon = the "indie bossa" hybrid, a legit lane variation.
- **Full standalone reference for this exact pattern** (hand setup, two-bar hit list beat-by-beat, incremental practice method, root/5th upgrade, live-comping adaptation) → [[Bossa Nova Comping Pattern]], extracted from [[How to Play a Bossa Nova Guitar Rhythm]].

### 2. Percussion — soft samba kit (felt, not heard)
- **Kick (= surdo):** beats 1 and 3 with pickup 8ths on the **& of 2** and **& of 4** ("boom…ba-boom ba-boom"), locked to the guitar thumb → [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]], soft sample, no click.
- **Rim click / cross-stick (= bossa clave):** the signature "tick" — two bars: **1, & of 2, 4 | 2, & of 3** (also played flipped, 2-3) → [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]] with a side-stick sample.
- **Hat or ride (= ganzá/chocalho):** steady straight 8ths, low velocity, brushes/blasticks flavor → [[1 Drums Hat Track|Drums Hat Track]].
- **Shaker/cabasa carpet:** continuous 8ths, light offbeat accent. Pandeiro/tamborim 16th figures as optional color — one quiet loop is plenty.
- Bus treatment: gentle — this genre inverts the usual [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]] aggression; no slam, no pump.

### 3. Bass — root–fifth, invisible
- Half notes mirroring the kick (1 and 3, occasional pickup 8th). Never busy — in João-style solo arrangements the guitar thumb *is* the bass. → [[1 Electric Bass DI Track|Electric Bass DI Track]], clean, dark top.
- **Two distinct root-fifth treatments depending which bossa lineage the track is aiming at.** A **traditional Brazilian** feel (Getz/Gilberto's "The Girl from Ipanema") matches this section's half-note description almost exactly — mostly root-fifth per chord, but with the option to **double the root** instead of playing the fifth on some repeats, varying octave placement bar to bar rather than looping one shape. A **jazz-bossa** feel (Horace Silver's "Song for My Father") instead uses a syncopated **dotted-quarter, eighth, dotted-quarter, eighth** rhythm with a **root, fifth-fifth, root-root, fifth-fifth** note pattern — busier and more syncopated than the traditional half-note version, worth reaching for when the arrangement leans more jazz-combo than Brazilian-guitar. Full root-fifth theory and a 3-step formula for adapting the same two notes to other styles → [[Root-Fifth Bass Lines and the 3-Step Style Rhythm Formula]]. — BassBuzz, [[Bass Lines *Any Style* with Just 2 Notes (for Beginners)]]

### 4. Keys — Jobim's answers
- Sparse piano comping in the same syncopation as the guitar stabs, **answering** the guitar rather than doubling it; small two/three-note voicings high on the keys → [[1 Acoustic Piano Track|Acoustic Piano Track]].

### 5. Vocal — breathy, conversational
- Barely above the guitar, almost spoken (Astrud Gilberto is the reference). Close-mic'd, minimal reverb, no tuning artifacts → [[1 Main Vocal Track|Main Vocal Track]] with the gentlest settings; intimacy is the instrument.

### 6. Harmony — the chord vocabulary
- No plain triads — everything wears extensions: **maj7/9, 6, m6, m7/9, 7(#11), 7b9, 13, m7b5**. The **m6** and **maj7(#11)** colors are especially bossa. Bass lines love chromatic descent.
- Guitar voicings: root under the thumb on string 6/5, chord compressed onto strings 4–2 / 3–1 (small 4-note shapes that slide chromatically): **Am6 = x04550 · Dm9 = x5355x · G13 = 3x345x · Cmaj9 = x3243x**.
- **Progressions to steal:**
  1. Minor ii–V–i (the workhorse): `Dm7b5 → G7b9 → Cm6/9` (Blue Bossa, Black Orpheus)
  2. Blue Bossa 8-bar loop: `Cm9 | Cm9 | Fm9 | Fm9 | Dm7b5 | G7b9 | Cm9 | Cm9` (+ `Ebm7 | Ab7 | Dbmaj7`)
  3. Ipanema A-section (tritone-sub shimmer): `Fmaj7 | G7(#11) | Gm7 | Gb7(b5) | Fmaj7`
  4. One Note Samba chromatic descent: `Dm7 | Db7(#11) | Cm7 | B7(#11)` (iii → bIII7 → ii → bII7 in Bb)
  5. Black Orpheus verse (minor ↔ relative major): `Am7 | Bm7b5 → E7b9 | Am7 … Dm7 → G7 | Cmaj7`
  6. Corcovado opener (diminished-as-dominant): `Am6 | Ab°7 | Gm7 → C7 | Fmaj7`
  7. Original-friendly loop: `Imaj9 → ii9 → bII7(#11) → Imaj9` (e.g. `Cmaj9 | Dm9 | Db7#11`)

### ✅ Enriched — "Girl from Ipanema" in seven shapes

[[Rick Beato - How to Play Bossa Nova Guitar - Jobim Style]] *(enriched 2026-08-09)* — the full page has every fingering fret-by-fret. The headline: **the entire tune is seven chord shapes plus one right-hand pattern**, taken unsimplified off the Getz/Gilberto recording.

- **A section shapes:** A♭6/9 · B♭m6 · E♭m9/B♭ — and the third is the second with the index finger moved down one fret. Two of the three A-section chords are one finger apart.
- **B section adds:** A6 · a movable dominant-7th shape (three occurrences) · a 5th-string-root m7 · a 7♭5.
- **Right hand:** pluck only fretted notes, never strum. Thumb = one unit, three fingers = another. Pattern is `chord · chord · thumb · chord · chord · chord · thumb · chord`, opening with thumb-and-chord together.
- **The actual skill is pattern variation**, not the chords — the record gets progressively more complex and the goal is improvising across two or three patterns. Matches the *anticipation sway* pillar above.
- **Bridge as motion, not a chord list:** A6 → down a step to G7 → up a fifth to Dm7 → down to B♭7 → E♭m7 → B7 → F♯m7 → down a fifth to B♭7♭5 → E♭m7 → A♭7♭5. Mostly fifths and steps — the same machinery as [[The ii-V-I]].

Worth noting that shapes 2→3 being one finger apart is the same derivative-fingering pedagogy [[A Modern Method for Guitar - Enrichment|Leavitt]] uses from p. 67, arrived at independently.

### ✅ Enriched — the right-hand comping pattern itself

[[How to Play a Bossa Nova Guitar Rhythm]] *(enriched 2026-08-11)* — where the Beato video above teaches Ipanema's *chords*, this one teaches **the pattern itself as its own subject**, built from zero with an incremental practice method. Full standalone reference → [[Bossa Nova Comping Pattern]].

- **Bass-controlled voicing:** fret the bass note (e.g. D major 7 rooted at the 5th fret/A string) instead of using an open string, so the thumb has full independent control over the bass line — the same "bass player vs. chordal player, two separate voices" idea as Pillar 1 above.
- **The two-bar hit list:** bass = half notes on 1 & 3 throughout; chord attacks land *with* the bass only once (bar 1 beat 1), then on beat 2, and everywhere else on offbeats — only 2 of ~6 chord attacks in the whole cycle land on the beat, with a final on-beat hit closing bar 2 on beat 4.
- **Incremental build method:** add one chord attack at a time over a rock-steady bass rather than reading the finished pattern off a chart; isolate offbeats (skip every downbeat, compare to a ska/reggae feel) if the syncopation is the sticking point.
- **Next level:** alternate root/5th in the bass instead of a static root, mainly worth it when sustaining one chord for a longer stretch.
- Applied directly to **"Wave"** — see below, this closes out the "Wanted" item.

## Arrangement

**Section map** — the *Getz/Gilberto* template, and most of the songbook: **AABA**.

| Section | Bars | Slots filled | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro | 4–8 | guitar alone | 1 |
| A | 8 | guitar · voice | 2 |
| A | 8 | + bass · soft samba kit | 4 |
| B (the bridge) | 8 | + keys answering the vocal; harmony moves furthest from home | 4–5 |
| A | 8 | as before, or an instrumental solo takes the melody | 4 |
| Out | 4–8 | back to guitar and voice, or a fade | 2 |

**Entrances & exits**
- **The guitar is the arrangement.** It's the whole percussion section and the harmony at once — thumb plays the surdo, fingers play the tamborim. Everything else is optional decoration.
- Percussion enters on the *second* A, never the first. That single entrance is the record's biggest dynamic event.
- The *Getz/Gilberto* move: Portuguese vocal → instrumental solo → English vocal. Different voices on the same melody instead of a new section.
- Keys answer the vocal in the gaps and never play with it (pillar 4).

**The contrast move — who has the melody.** Not density, not dynamics. Voice, then sax, then voice again. This is the only genre here where the arrangement barely changes and the *soloist* carries all the sectional information.

**Density rule: 2–5, and quiet throughout.** The dynamic range across an entire bossa record is smaller than a single pop chorus. Adding energy the way another genre would — louder, denser, brighter — breaks it. Contrast is horizontal, not vertical.

## Session recipe (Ableton, stock)
1. ~120–130 BPM, **groove/swing OFF** — straight 8ths everywhere.
2. Guitar first: record the two-bar pattern on [[1 Acoustic Guitar Track|Acoustic Guitar Track]]; it carries bass + rhythm alone. Everything else decorates.
3. Drum Rack: soft kick (surdo pattern), side-stick (clave), closed hat + shaker (8th carpet) — program the grids exactly as in Pillar 2, velocities humanized low (30–70).
4. Bass and piano only if the arrangement needs them; keep both sparse and behind the guitar.
5. Mix philosophy: **close, dry, quiet** — guitar and vocal up front, percussion mono-ish and tucked, [[Reverb Send Room]] over [[Reverb Send Long]], nothing wide, nothing loud. Inverts the modern-pop loudness instinct.

## Reality check
- There is no "one true pattern" — João varied constantly. Learn 1-bar → 2-bar anticipation, then treat variations as vocabulary.
- Swinging the 8ths turns bossa into lounge pastiche; keeping them straight *and* soft is the actual skill.
- Teachers place the stabs slightly differently — that's style, not contradiction; the anticipation and the thumb are the invariants.

# Junk — Paul Mccartney

> Tender fingerpicked ballad about abandoned objects finding new owners. Written during the Rishikesh retreat with the Maharishi. An instrumental version, 'Singalong Junk,' also appeared on McCartney (1970).

## Chords
**Key:** A major (leans on its relative F♯ minor) · gentle 4/4 ballad

**Verse:** F♯m – C♯7 – D – A
→ **vi – V7/vi – IV – I** (the C♯7 is a secondary dominant pointing at F♯m before the line falls to IV–I)

**"Buy, buy…" chorus:** D7sus4 – Dm – A – E
→ **IV(sus) – iv – I – V** (the **Dm** is the borrowed minor-iv — the bittersweet McCartney signature)

> Harder than it sounds — lots of extended/suspended voicings. Verified via Ultimate Guitar / Hooktheory.


## Production & Arrangement
From **McCartney (1970)** — his first solo album, recorded mostly **at home on a Studer 4-track** (plus some Abbey Road time) with **McCartney playing every instrument himself**. A deliberate reaction to Beatles polish and the breakup: loose, intimate, lo-fi, unfinished-on-purpose.

- Sparse and wistful — a home-demo feel with lightly-strummed guitar, bass, and a fragile lead vocal; even has an instrumental reprise ('Singalong Junk').
- Nostalgic waltz-y lilt; the emptiness is the emotion.

## Steal this
- **Home-demo aesthetic:** leave a song sparse and unfinished-sounding when the fragility *is* the point.
- **Instrumental reprise:** re-use the same changes as a wordless version for a second emotional pass.
- **Waltz lilt** + wistful [[Seventh Chords|7ths]] for nostalgia.

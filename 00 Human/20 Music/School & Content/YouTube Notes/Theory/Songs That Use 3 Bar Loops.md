# Songs That Use 3 Bar Loops

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJI0j_KVQdI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJI0j_KVQdI)

## Synopsis

Songs built from 3-bar (rather than the default 4-bar) phrase loops — the phrase-length equivalent of an odd time signature, using the same "our ears are primed for the default grouping" mechanism to create forward momentum, jolt, or hypnotic effect.

## Techniques

- **The core mechanism**: just as 4/4 is the default time signature, the 4-bar phrase is the default loop length — ears are primed to hear music in groups of four bars, so a genuine 3-bar loop catches timing expectation off guard, reading as if the music is "skipping a bar," producing forward momentum/energy. Examples: "Children of the Revolution" (T. Rex) and "Bullet with Butterfly Wings" (Smashing Pumpkins) choruses; "Hey" (Pixies); Tame Impala's "Let It Happen" (the 3-bar loop credited as central to the track's hypnotic, trance-inducing feel).
- **The effect is most audible when a song SWITCHES from 4-bar to 3-bar mid-song**: Slipknot's "Duality" (4-bar intro → 3-bar main riff), Green Day's "Boulevard of Broken Dreams" (4-bar body → 3-bar climactic outro), Childish Gambino's "Redbone" (4-bar body → an outro loop where a chord alternates between D minor and D major every other rotation).
- **The "is it really 3, or is it 6?" ambiguity — same logic as odd-time-signature phrase counting.** A loop that alternates content on every SECOND repetition (a chord swap, a melodic variation) is more accurately described as a 6-bar loop with 3 bars as its smallest repeating unit, not a true 3-bar loop: Redbone's outro (D minor/D major alternation), Radiohead's "Videotape" (the piano riff is slightly altered every other pass), Ronnie Foster's "Mystic Brew" (and its famous sample, A Tribe Called Quest's "Electric Relaxation") — 3 bars is the technical minimum unit, but most of the actual melodic phrasing fits better across 6.
- **Tempo perception can reclassify a loop between "3-bar" and "6-bar" depending on how you count the beat**: LCD Soundsystem's "Never as Tired as When I'm Waking Up" reads as a 3-bar loop at 63 BPM or equivalently a 6-bar loop at 126 BPM — two descriptions of the identical music, not a real ambiguity in the material itself.

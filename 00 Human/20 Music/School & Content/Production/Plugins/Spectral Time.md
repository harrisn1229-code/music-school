# Spectral Time

**Category:** Reverb/Delay
**Vendor:** Ableton

## What it is

Ableton's stock spectral delay (Live 11 Suite) — splits the incoming signal into frequency bins and delays them independently rather than repeating the whole signal at once, with a built-in harmonic freezer alongside the delay section.

## What it's used for

Turning a delay into a frequency-aware texture/transition tool — smeared "laser," watery, or metallic repeats distinct from [[Echo]]/[[EchoBoy]]'s whole-signal echo, plus instant frozen-harmonic pads via its Freeze section.

## FX chain notes

Inserted on a lead, pad, or vocal layer for a texture/transition effect rather than a rhythmic echo; often driven hard (Time + Dry/Wet maxed) so the spectral smear is the whole point rather than a subtle blend.

## Techniques

- **Automate Tilt (and Time) live for a "space laser" sweep; Max softens a positive-Tilt transient; Freeze in ms mode for a refreshable melodic capture** — automating **Tilt** (optionally alongside Time) in real time is "my favorite way to abuse" Spectral Time, giving a rising/falling space-laser sweep rather than a static smear. **Max** pulls a positive-Tilt sweep back while keeping some transient character, or use it to blur further. Runs best in **Ultra** mode when the full spectral detail matters; push **Feedback** for genuine delayed trails instead of a single smear. The **Freeze** section reads best on melodic material when triggered in **milliseconds mode**, since that lets you keep refreshing exactly where the freeze lands rather than committing to one static capture. → [[Delay]] — dnksaus, [Ableton Delay Sound Design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DCIEayyv1bk)
- **Tilt orders which frequencies delay first — a linear frequency-bin sweep, not a flat echo** — with Time and Dry/Wet both up, turning up **Tilt** (e.g. +340 ms) delays the lowest frequency first and the highest frequency last, up to the Tilt time set; a **negative Tilt** (e.g. -240 ms) reverses the order, delaying the highest frequencies first. Used in his own track "Vitamin D" with Tilt pushed forward on an octave-up switch for a wet, watery transition effect, and in "Bread" as a section-to-section transition device. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — dnksaus, [The Underrated Spectral Stock Plugins in Ableton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AoYHYsrnjYo)
- **Freeze section — instant or time-synced harmonic capture** — Freeze locks whatever harmonic content is currently sounding; instead of manually re-triggering it, **Re-trigger** can fire automatically on new incoming notes, or **sync by time** with a **Rate** knob that refreshes the freeze on a set interval — a hands-off, continuously-refreshing frozen texture rather than a one-shot capture. — dnksaus, [The Underrated Spectral Stock Plugins in Ableton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AoYHYsrnjYo)
- **Spray scrambles delay timing per bin for an airy "laser" texture** — with Time brought down and Dry/Wet up ("lasering" the sound), turning up **Spray** randomly scrambles each frequency bin's delay timing for a more scattered, airy result — a spectral cousin of [[Grain Delay]]'s Spray control. → [[Grain Delay]] — dnksaus, [The Underrated Spectral Stock Plugins in Ableton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AoYHYsrnjYo)
- **Mask splits transient-like vs. laser-feedback character by frequency range** — shifting **Mask** left limits the delay to lower frequencies only, reading as more of a transient-focused adjustment; shifting it right (positive) limits the delay to higher frequencies, reading as more of a laser-feedback texture. — dnksaus, [The Underrated Spectral Stock Plugins in Ableton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AoYHYsrnjYo)
- **Built-in Shifter section as an automatable "switch-up"** — a pitch/frequency-shift section lives inside the device itself; automating it live while a section plays reads as a switch-up effect distinct from the Tilt/Mask moves above. → [[Automation]] — dnksaus, [The Underrated Spectral Stock Plugins in Ableton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AoYHYsrnjYo)
- **Resolution/quality setting trades fidelity for character** — a selectable resolution mode (Ultra = high-def, can read as "too high-def"; a lower "Mid"-type setting reads as a more digital, "laser" character) — pick by ear rather than defaulting to the highest setting. *(described qualitatively — the transcript's exact mode names were unclear)* — dnksaus, [The Underrated Spectral Stock Plugins in Ableton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AoYHYsrnjYo)

# Don't Bother Me — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

George's first solo composition on a Beatles record, and Pollack's point is that it is **not an imitation of his bandmates** — it already contains what George would spend a decade on: minor key, sensual modal melody, sad-angry inward lyric. The essay's centrepiece is a comparison with "All I've Got To Do": the **same five pentatonic notes rearranged around a different tonic** produce a major-feeling song there and a deeply minor one here ([[SCL-C04 — Parallel vs derivative mode thinking]]).

**Form.** Two bridges with the usual verse arrangement plus a half-solo verse. The 4-bar intro is built from material that pre-states the bridge. The verse is **12 bars in three even phrases** — blues-like in shape if not in style. The solo section is built like "From Me To You": the instrumental paraphrases the vocal melody closely, then the voice returns for the last phrase so it reads as a refrain.

**Harmony.** The v chord is **minor**, so it can't function as a dominant, and defining the key falls instead to VII and IV. The important twist is the **major IV inside a minor mode — Dorian, not Aeolian** ([[POP-C10 — Dorian IV vs Aeolian iv]]): George uses the minor iv inside the verse's stream-like first two phrases where it passes smoothly, and **saves the Dorian major IV for the third phrase** as the surprise. The bridge slows the harmonic rhythm to one change every two bars, which makes the verse's per-bar rate sound like an acceleration when it returns, with no tempo change at all.

**Melody.** Pentatonic throughout, missing the 2nd and 6th ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]), and how the missing degrees are spent is the lesson ([[SCL-C05 — Characteristic notes]]). The **2nd is withheld until the climax of the bridge**, where it's also the highest note in the song; the **6th is absent from the tune entirely**, which is exactly what lets the harmony flip IV between minor and major without the melody objecting. Pollack's caveat: none of them read music, and doing this intuitively is more impressive, not less.

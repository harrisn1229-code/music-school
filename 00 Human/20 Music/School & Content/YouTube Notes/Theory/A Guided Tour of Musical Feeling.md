# A Guided Tour of Musical Feeling

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AARDtj6wL3U)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AARDtj6wL3U)

## Synopsis
Max Konyi's foundational ear-training video — a 50-minute walkthrough of all 12 scale degrees of the chromatic scale, each played against a sustained tonic drone and described purely in terms of its subjective "feeling state" rather than its pitch or interval name. The premise: tonal music organizes every note as a relationship to a home note (the tonic), and that relationship produces a distinct, learnable, non-verbal quality — a "vibe" — independent of octave, instrument, or absolute pitch. Konyi walks degree by degree (1, 5, 4, 3, 2, 6, 7, ♯4, ♭7, ♭3, ♭6, ♭2), for each one: describing its feeling in his own words, contrasting it against its most commonly confused neighbors on a "feeling-circle" (not the circle of fifths — a circle arranged by which degrees people actually mistake for each other), and playing short melodic examples that isolate it. This is the master reference for the whole feeling-state ear-training method that the three "Answering Your Ear Training Questions" videos build on — the most valuable video in this batch for anyone starting the method from zero, since it's the only one that actually walks through and demonstrates all 12 degrees rather than just discussing the training process abstractly.

## Techniques
- **The core method: scale degrees are feelings, not pitches** — the same scale degree produces the same subjective quality regardless of octave, key, or instrument, because the quality comes from the *relationship* to the tonic, not the absolute frequency. Explicitly NOT a physical sensation ("look for it in your body") — a subtle emotional/qualitative tone, closer to a mood than a sound.
- **Train against a sustained drone** — the tonic held underneath is the explicit reference point while learning; in real listening this collapses into an internal "virtual drone" the mind holds automatically once it knows a piece's key, so the drone is a training scaffold, not a permanent requirement.
- **Degree 1 (tonic)** — perfect, complete, whole, resolved, stable; the minimum-energy state. Easiest to bootstrap: play a descending major scale and focus purely on the felt sense of landing on the last note.
- **Degree 5** — pure, stable, consonant, smooth, but *active* (not minimum-energy like 1); "open," "expansive," "outward." Defines its own axis/family of feeling shared with 1, 4, 2.
- **Degree 4** — same clean/neutral family as 5, but *contractive* / inward-curling rather than outward; described as "square" or "flat" in shape, not in pitch. Commonly confused with 1 and with ♭7.
- **Degree 3** — a totally different family from 5/1/4/2: sweetness, warmth, glow, vitality — "a whole new world," "always feels good." Most commonly confused with 6 and 7 (both share some of 3's warmth mixed with something else).
- **Degree 2** — clean/crystalline/placid like 5, but sharper, more "razor-like," described as "5 multiplied" or "5 dialed up." Confused with 5 and 6.
- **Degree 6** — 3's sweetness diluted with 4's neutrality; softer, lighter, more "pastel" and muted than 3 itself. Confused with 2 and 3 — the distinguishing test: does it have the glowing/warm "3 quality" (then it's 6 or 3) or the clean/neutral quality with no warmth (then it's 2)?
- **Degree 7** — melancholy, a "tinge of sad, tinge of strain" — combines the 5-feeling and the 3-feeling, sharpened into dissonance by sitting a half-step below the tonic. Confused with 3 (which is purely warm, no melancholy) and ♯4 (which has more "alien" intensity, less "mild sadness").
- **Degree ♯4** — one of the strongest, most energized feeling states in the whole set; bright, strange, "alien," "spiky." Sits diametrically opposite 1 on the feeling-circle (maximally different), despite being only a half-step from 5 melodically — the clearest demonstration in the whole video that melodic closeness ≠ feeling closeness. Confused with 7 and with ♭2 (context-dependent whether a pitch reads as ♯4 or ♭5).
- **Degree ♭7** — lightest of the "flat"/minor-leaning degrees; powerful, almost fun, "the lightest minory feeling there is" — NOT dark despite technically being a "flat" degree. Confused with 4 (no minorness at all) and ♭3 (properly dark).
- **Degree ♭3** — defines "minorness" itself; serious, dark, gloomy, a bit of sorrow, "not fun." The reference point for what people mean when they say a chord/passage "sounds minor." Confused with ♭7 (lighter) and ♭6 (more intense).
- **Degree ♭6** — more intense than ♭3, less than ♭2; desperate, hopeless, "no joy, no hope" — a genuinely negative emotional valence, distinct from ♭3's merely somber quality.
- **Degree ♭2** — the most extreme/dissonant degree in the set; "in your face," bitter, spiky, "the world's going to end" — directly above the tonic by a half-step, so melodically the closest possible neighbor to 1 but feeling-wise about as far from it as anything gets. Confused with ♭6 and ♯4 depending on context.
- **Circle-of-feeling adjacency predicts confusion pairs** — arranging the 12 degrees by feeling-similarity (not the circle of 5ths) puts commonly-confused degrees next to each other on the circle; degrees that are melodically a half-step apart (5/♯4, 1/♭2) tend to sit at maximum distance on this feeling-circle instead, which is the video's central point: pitch-distance and feeling-distance are two different metrics.
- **Words are training wheels, not the destination** — Konyi is explicit that his verbal descriptions ("glowing," "spiky," "square") are his own subjective approximations, offered because people ask, not because they're objectively correct; the actual goal is to discard the words entirely once the felt recognition is instant, the same way a native speaker doesn't consciously translate a word's syllables.
- **The whole method requires repetition, not insight** — Konyi repeatedly frames a failure to distinguish two degrees as simply "you haven't heard enough of X while being conscious it was X," not as a conceptual gap — the fix is always more consciously-labeled listening reps, never a better explanation.

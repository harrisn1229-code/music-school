# How to Process Vocals Like a Pro

📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I06KKVnCAVU)

## Synopsis

A third full lead-vocal chain from Sage Audio (companion to [[Clean Vocal Chain]] and [[Vocal Masterclass]]), this one starting further upstream — at recording level and clip gain — before the familiar EQ/compression/saturation/reverb/delay/automation stages. The most novel content is the recording-level range, clip-gain-before-compression sequencing, and a specific set of additive-EQ starting points.

## Techniques

- **Stage 0 — Record within a −18 dB to −3 dB range, chosen by INTENT, not just "hot enough."** Recording nearer −18 dB gives a cleaner, more transparent signal; recording nearer −3 dB (via higher preamp gain) captures more preamp coloration/character. Recording below this range means whatever digital gain is added later also amplifies the noise floor proportionally — the range exists to balance clean signal-to-noise against how much preamp character is wanted. → [[Gain Staging]], [[Vocal Mic Technique]]
- **Stage 1 — Clip gain BEFORE compression, not compression alone, to balance excessive performance dynamics.** A compressor reacting to an unevened performance gets hit harder by the loud moments, making its compression more audible/noticeable than necessary. Manually riding clip gain on specific loud/quiet sections FIRST (using the waveform display to visually judge balance) evens out the performance before compression ever sees it, letting the compressor do less work and stay more transparent. → [[Compression]], [[Gain Staging]]
- **Stage 2 — Subtractive EQ starting points that apply in nearly every case, regardless of the specific vocalist/key.** High-pass up to ~80 Hz at 18 dB/octave (plosives, rumble, electrical hum); attenuate sibilance in the 4-10 kHz range BEFORE compression specifically to reduce how much a later de-esser needs to work. → [[EQ]], [[De-essing]]
- **Stage 3 — A concrete attack/release recipe pair for two distinct vocal characters.** For punchy, upfront, highly intelligible (pop/rap): **longer attack + shorter release**. For smoother, more relaxed/blended-into-the-mix: **shorter attack + longer release** (opto-style compressors lean this way naturally too). Target **3-5 dB of gain reduction**, then use makeup gain to bring quiet detail forward. → [[Compression]], [[Compression Staging]]
- **Stage 4 — Saturation as upward compression + harmonic generation combined, scaled to mix density.** Mixing multiple saturation/harmonic-generation types builds a fuller, more complex vocal than one type alone. A busy, dense mix (typical pop) needs MORE saturation to separate the vocal from the crowd; a sparse mix (singer-songwriter) needs less. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Stage 5 — Four additive-EQ starting points, each targeting a distinct character goal.** For **air/cut-through**: high shelf at ~15 kHz. For **fullness/warmth**: bell at ~2 kHz, medium Q. For **body/clarity**: bell at ~150-200 Hz. For **presence/crispness**: bell at ~4-6 kHz, medium Q. Explicitly starting points to critically listen and adjust from, not fixed settings. → [[EQ]]
- **Stage 6 — Two SEPARATE reverb sends with different, BPM-derived timing, plus post-reverb EQ on the return.** Send #1 (thickening/doubling): a very short reverb time, **~1/16 note** at the song's tempo. Send #2 (stylized/character): a longer time, **~1/2 note**. Standard BPM-to-ms formula: `60,000 / BPM` = one quarter note in ms, scale up/down for other note values. EQ can be added AFTER the reverb (on the return) to shape just the tail's character independent of the dry vocal. → [[Reverb]]
- **Stage 7 — A dedicated delay send, deliberately separate from the reverb sends, with its own top-end rolled off.** Keeping delay on its own send (not combined with reverb) means the reverb tail isn't also delayed — an important separation for independent control. Attenuating the delay send's high frequencies keeps sibilance/harsh content from being amplified by the repeats. Delay time (long stylized vs. short thickening) is a taste/mix-density call. → [[Delay]]
- **Stage 8 — Automate volume, sends, and plugin parameters as the final "separates a pro vocal from an amateur one" step.** Concrete examples: bump saturation amount + ~0.5 dB volume specifically during a chorus for it to "pop"; increase reverb send right before a section transition to signal the change; briefly push a delay send to an extreme amount for 2-3 seconds as a deliberate, aggressive creative effect. Framed as the highest-leverage remaining step once the static chain is dialed in. → [[Automation]]
- **Bonus/situational: gate early in the chain and a de-esser later are useful but NOT universal additions — reach for them only when the specific performance/recording environment calls for it**, unlike the stages above which the video treats as close to default.

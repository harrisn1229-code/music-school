> **Q4.** **Because the ear judges groove speed almost entirely from snare placement**
> Backbeat spacing is the cue, so doubling the gap halves the perceived tempo.
>
> **Q5.** **~85–95 BPM · backbeat on 2 and 4 · straight 8th hats · swung and laid back · sampled**
> The open hat sits on the offbeat.
> *Also accepted:* around 90 BPM
>
> **Q6.** **Written ~130–145 but felt half-time · snare on 3 · 16th hats with rolls · tight kit**
> The rolls are 32nds and triplets over a 16th bed, the hats are loose, and the 808 doubles as kick and bass.
>
> **Q7.** **The bed is 16ths — the 32nds and triplets are rolls layered on top**
> Calling the whole hat part triplets misses the structure and gets the default subdivision wrong.
>
> **Q8.** **The hats run at the written tempo while the backbeat runs at half of it**
> *Rubric (5):* says the hi-hat subdivision follows the fast written tempo (2) · says the backbeat on 3 makes the groove feel half as fast (2) · says the rapper phrases against the slow backbeat while the track keeps detail and energy (1)
> Two clocks run at once. The hi-hats subdivide the actual 140, so the track stays busy and detailed at the surface. The snare on beat 3 sets the groove's felt pulse at around 70, and that's the layer a vocalist phrases against — long enough between backbeats to fit words without rushing. A rapper gets a comfortable slow frame to sit in, and the beat still has enough activity to hold attention. Neither tempo alone would give you both.
>
> **Q9.** **It's the kick and the bass at once — so it has to be tuned to the key**
> *Rubric (5):* names both roles, kick and bass (2) · says it therefore carries pitch as well as impact (1) · says it must be tuned to the key, and that its note choices become part of the harmony (2)
> A separate kick and bass split the work: the kick supplies impact and the bass supplies pitch, and the kick can be tuned loosely or not at all because nothing depends on its note. An 808 collapses both into one element, so every drum hit is also a bass note. That forces tuning to the key, because an out-of-key 808 clashes with the harmony every time the pattern plays, and it means the drum programming and the bassline are the same decision — you can't change the rhythm without changing what the low end is playing harmonically.

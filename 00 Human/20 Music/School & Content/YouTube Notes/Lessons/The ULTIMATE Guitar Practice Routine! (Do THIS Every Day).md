## Synopsis

BERNTH's 5-step personal practice-routine system, built from 15 years of playing. Step 2 (pick 4 topics based on your actual goals) restates the topic-block system already logged from [[The 6 BEST Guitar Solo EXERCISES (finally learn to SHRED!)]] — not re-routed. The other four steps are distinct: fixed daily practice timing for consistency, a three-tempo-zone technique system (with mid-tempo flagged as the most neglected), adding a "theory twist" to pure technique drills, and session tracking/logging as a habit-formation tool.

## What's taught

- **Step 1 — fix a consistent daily practice time, chosen for personal energy/focus, not willpower.** Framed as one of the most important factors for long-term consistency: forcing a morning session on a non-morning person (or an evening session when already exhausted) burns out within a few weeks. His own choice: ~4:30 AM, specifically because nobody else is awake/interrupting. The actual time matters less than picking one that's sustainable and sticking to it.
- **Step 2 — pick 4 topics matched to your actual goals (restated, see [[The Five-Category Practice Method]]).** 15–20 min per topic daily; topics should follow from what you actually want to play (thrash metal → alternate picking/downstrokes; jazz/fusion → theory-heavy topics), not a generic list; don't over-deliberate the "perfect" four — starting matters more, and topics can be swapped later.
- **Step 3 — three tempo zones for technique practice, mid-tempo flagged as the most neglected.** (1) **Very slow** — close attention to technique itself: hand synchronization, fretting-hand finger curvature (not flying away from the fretboard), actually being in time even at a slow tempo (harder than it sounds). (2) **Mid-tempo (his own range: ~100–130 BPM)** — playing tight and locked-in at a realistic performance tempo; explicitly named as the zone he personally neglected for years by jumping straight from slow-learning tempo to max-speed pushing, and the zone most relevant to actual live/studio work, more than pushing 16th notes past 200 BPM. (3) **High-speed** — pushing tempo specifically to develop raw technique ceiling, done only after the first two zones are solid.
- **Step 4 — add a "theory twist" to pure technique drills.** When running scale-sequence or picking-technique exercises, deliberately vary WHICH scale/chord type is used instead of always the same major/minor in the same fretboard area — e.g. run picking drills through harmonic minor or the modes (Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian for rock/metal) instead of always natural major/minor; for sweep picking, include diminished and augmented shapes alongside major/minor. Same physical practice time, but every session also reinforces fretboard/theory knowledge as a side effect.
- **Step 5 — track sessions in a written log to build the habit, especially early on.** A plain notebook: date, the day's 4 topics, specific exercises worked, and tempos achieved. Stated payoffs: visible BPM progress within days is motivating, and the act of logging itself reinforces consistency until the routine becomes automatic (his own comparison: "like brushing my teeth"). Framed as most valuable in the first few weeks of adopting a routine — not necessarily a forever habit.

# How to Create Depth and Width with Reverb and Delay

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XGSytDiGJl0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XGSytDiGJl0)

## Synopsis

A precise breakdown from Sage Audio of exactly which parameters create perceived depth vs. width, and why: level/fader for depth, reverb (time/reflections/pre-delay/wet-dry ratio) for depth, delay primarily for width but also usable for depth via slapback, and panning for basic width. Several concrete numeric thresholds make this unusually actionable.

## Techniques

- **Level/fader position IS depth, mechanically — louder reads as closer, quieter reads as farther, because that's how real distance affects perceived loudness.** The most basic and reliable depth tool in a mix; every other technique below is refining/adding to this baseline. → [[Depth and Space]]
- **Reverb depth is controlled by four specific, separable levers: RT60 (decay time), wet/dry ratio, reflection density/pattern, and pre-delay — each pushes depth independently.** **RT60 greater than the source's natural decay = farther away.** **Wet/dry ratio** is a direct depth dial: more wet = farther, more dry = closer — blend to taste rather than treating wet% as a "how much reverb" knob only. **Reflection density/pattern** (plate = dense reflections front-loaded at the start of the tail; hall = roughly evenly-spaced reflections) shapes realism/character more than depth directly, but character still colors how believable the depth reads. **Pre-delay** (time between the dry signal and the reverb's onset — e.g. 50 ms means the dry hits first, reverb 50 ms later) is often reached for to keep a vocal clear, but is ALSO a depth lever in its own right: hearing the direct signal first before the reverberant tail affects perceived distance. **Reverb times under ~1 second add amplitude/character/realism WITHOUT significantly contributing to depth** — useful when you want a room-ish complexity or thickness boost without pushing something back in the mix. → [[Depth and Space]], [[Reverb]]
- **Delay's default job is WIDTH via phase cancellation between near-identical signals — several concrete methods, each producing a different flavor of wide.** Core mechanism: a delayed copy of a signal, summed against the original, phase-cancels partially and pushes the combined image toward the full 180° stereo field (the same physical effect that happens naturally when the same source is dual-mic'd during tracking). Methods: (1) **sample delay** on just the L or R channel of an instrument/vocal — alters L/R phase relationship directly, widening the image. (2) **Mid/side EQ boosting the side channel** — a similar widening effect via a different mechanism. (3) **Full hard-panned duplicate-and-invert**: duplicate a mono signal to a second track, phase-invert one copy (summing them produces silence, confirming true inversion), pan one hard left and the other hard right — takes a mono signal from dead-center (0°) all the way to the full 180° field. (4) **Directional placement via short delay (up to ~35 ms)** on just one channel: delaying the RIGHT channel makes the LEFT ear hear the sound first, so the brain perceives the source as coming from the left (and vice versa) — a Haas-effect-style directional trick distinct from the symmetric-widening methods above. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Phase Alignment]]
- **Delay can ALSO create depth, via slapback, but with an exact minimum time threshold and a caveat that reverb-based depth reads more believable.** A slapback delay must be **greater than ~130 ms** to read as depth — below that threshold, the ear fuses the delay with the original into one perceptually-louder (not "farther") signal, since the two repeats aren't separately resolvable. Above ~130 ms, the brain hears two distinct events (original + delayed "reflection"), reading as a small room. Explicitly noted: **reverb-based depth is more complex/nuanced and generally more believable than delay-based depth** — delay-for-depth is a usable tool, not a full substitute for reverb when believability matters. → [[Delay]], [[Depth and Space]]

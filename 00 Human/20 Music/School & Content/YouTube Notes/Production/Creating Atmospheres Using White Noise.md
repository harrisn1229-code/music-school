# Creating Atmospheres Using White Noise | You Suck at Producing #56

## Synopsis

A short, focused recipe for building an atmospheric texture bed from nothing but white noise, a Vocoder, and a touch of Vinyl Distortion — no vocal or musical source required. Walks through each Vocoder parameter's contribution to the atmosphere's character (Attack for smoothness, Release for responsiveness, band count for tone, Formant for pitch center, width for spread) and finishes with an LFO on Attack so the atmosphere evolves over time instead of sitting static.

## Techniques

- **Vocoder on pure white noise as a from-scratch atmosphere generator, finished with Vinyl Distortion crackle and an LFO for evolution** — feed white noise into a Vocoder with no vocal/musical input at all; Attack pushed near max gives the smoothest, most "sweet" result (lower it to bring grit back), Release lowered smooths the response further, band count (e.g. 8) trades grit for smoothness, Formant shifts the perceived pitch center, and the width controls narrow or widen the spread. Layer [[Vinyl Distortion]]'s Crackle on top for texture, then map an LFO to Attack so the atmosphere's character continuously shifts over time. → [[Vocoder]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]
- **Vinyl Distortion's Crackle density as a simple "how dirty/aged" dial on any source that already has signal** — not just the generative silence-to-noise trick documented elsewhere; raising Crackle on a live source layers vinyl-style aging proportionally on top of it. → [[Vinyl Distortion]]

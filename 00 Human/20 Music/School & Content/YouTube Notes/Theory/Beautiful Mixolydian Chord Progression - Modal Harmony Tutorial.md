# Beautiful Mixolydian Chord Progression | Modal Harmony Tutorial

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N2fxXcpVU8M)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N2fxXcpVU8M)

## Synopsis
A worked, in-depth tutorial building a purely-Mixolydian I–v–ii–IV progression in D Mixolydian, then dressing it up through four voicing/extension variations, then adding optional non-diatonic spice chords on top. This is the deepest practical companion to [[Modal Pop]]'s Mixolydian section in the vault — it shows, step by step, exactly *how* to make a mode audible rather than just naming its chords, and directly demonstrates two of [[Modal Pop]]'s "making a mode actually sound modal" rules (emphasize the characteristic note; use the chord that note produces). Explicitly references Max's companion video on flipping chord polarity — see [[Flip Your Chords for Better Progressions!]].

## What's taught
- **Building a purely-diatonic modal progression**: D Mixolydian = D major scale with a ♭7 (C natural instead of C♯) — 1 2 3 4 5 6 ♭7. Chosen progression is **I – v – ii – IV** (D major – A minor – E minor – G major): same as a major key's I–?–ii–IV except the **v is minor instead of major**, because Mixolydian's ♭7 (relative to D) makes the chord built on the 5th degree minor rather than the parallel-major-key's V. This single altered chord is what makes the loop read as modal rather than a plain major-key progression. → [[Diatonic Modes]], [[Modal Pop]], [[Chords by Key]]
- **Voicing variation 1 — open triads, close voice-leading**: invert each chord (2nd inversion I: 5–root–3rd) and voice each successive chord as close as possible to the last (minimum motion between chords) rather than all in root position; produces a "delicate," airy sound suited to an intro, high strummed guitar/keys part, bells, or voices, as opposed to a denser low-register voicing.
- **Voicing variation 2 — root-position with extensions**: Dmaj add9 → Am7 (voiced 5–♭3–♭7) → Em(add9, no 7th) → a "G6sus2" chord (G triad with the 3rd dropped to the 2nd, i.e. sus2 over a 6th). Emphasizes tracking the **top note of each chord as an implied melody line** — the note you'd sing back if humming the progression — as a compositional check while voicing. → [[Chord Inversions]], [[Extended Chords]]
- **Voicing variation 3 — mixed inversions + bigger extensions**: I (root) → Am7/C (1st inversion, root note held while the chord moves under it) → Em7add11 (a stretch voicing, pinky on the 11th) → Gmaj7sus2 (also nameable as "Dmajadd11/G"). Two names for the same chord = a reminder that naming depends on which note you treat as functional root/bass ([[Chord Naming by Context]]).
- **Voicing variation 4 — max density**: Dmajadd9/F♯ (1st inversion) → Am7add11 → Em7 (plain) → Gmajadd11, voiced **1-11-5-3** (11th below the 3rd). Flags the **3-against-11 voicing rule**: when the 3rd and the 11th (=4th) sit a half-step apart and clash, voice the 3rd ABOVE the 11th (major 7th interval between them) rather than below it (which produces an ugly flat-9-like interval, "octave plus a half step") — a concrete extended-chord voicing rule not yet in [[Extended Chords]]. → [[Extended Chords]]
- **Spicing a modal progression with the mode's "operative note" (the ♭7 here) inserted into MORE chords than the ones that naturally contain it** — directly demonstrates [[Modal Pop]]'s "emphasize the characteristic note" rule in practice: put a C (D Mixolydian's ♭7) into the bass under every chord in the loop (D/C, Am/C or "Em2," Gmaj7sus2/C), which recolors the whole progression around a C pedal and makes the mode's signature note audible even under chords that don't naturally contain it. → [[Modal Pop]], [[Loop-Based Harmony]] (pedal point)
- **Polarity-flipping a diatonic chord for extra color, in first inversion for a smoother bassline** — flip the normally-minor v (E minor) to E major, voiced in first inversion (G♯ in the bass) so the bass moves stepwise D→A→G♯→G; alternatively flip the IV (G major) to G minor(add6/9) instead. This is the exact technique Max has a dedicated video on — see [[Flip Your Chords for Better Progressions!]] — applied here inside a modal (not just major-key) context, confirming the polarity-flip trick generalizes past plain major/minor keys. → [[Borrowed Chords]]
- **A half-step-approach bass trick for recoloring an upper structure**: keep the ii chord's upper notes (A minor 7) but slide only the BASS note up a half-step toward the next chord's root (A → F, approaching E minor), which recolors the same upper structure as an F major9 chord for that beat before landing on E minor — works best when the moving bass note itself leads smoothly into the next chord's actual bass note. → [[Chord Inversions]], [[Chord Naming by Context]]

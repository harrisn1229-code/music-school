## Production & Arrangement
Revolver — famous for its **dual harmonized lead-guitar line** (Harrison + McCartney playing interlocking parts in harmony), bright and jangly, driving the whole song.

## Steal this
- **Harmonized twin-guitar lead:** write a melodic line and double it a 3rd/6th above for a bright, interlocking hook ([[Morgan Amps Suite]], double-track it).
- **Guitar riff as the song's identity**, not just the vocal.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — And Your Bird Can Sing]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #98.*

**Form.** A two-bridge model with a single verse between the bridges, plus a full repeat of the guitar-solo verse just before the outro. Intro 4 bars (one chord, and deliberately only the *first half* of what later becomes the complete solo); verse 8 as 4+4 AB, the two phrases separated partly by harmonic rhythm — the first four bars sit on one chord, the second four move; bridge 8 over a chromatic bass descent; outro the obbligato again, with a complete ending rather than a fade.

**Harmony.** `Verse: I (4 bars) | ii | IV | I (2)` in E — closed, and **the key is established plagally**, with the dominant withheld from the entire song until the bridge ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]). In the solo verses bar 6's IV is swapped for **V**, partly because IV clashes with the solo line but mainly because V makes the climax land. `Bridge: iii (4) | I | ii (2) | V` over a bass walking G♯ – G♮ – F♯ – F♮ – E; it sets up as though modulating to G♯ minor and the leisurely chromatic descent quietly deposits it back home instead. The song ends on **IV in second inversion** — rarer than an unresolved ending on V, and unprepared by anything else in the arrangement.

**Notable.** He reads the guitar obbligato as Baroque-shaped — perpetual even eighths, embellished stepwise content, ritornello-style cyclic return — dropped into a deliberately dirty mix, with the pentatonic touches typical of John over it ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]). John's vocal is *artificially* double-tracked and split hard left/right; the rhythm section is clunky on purpose, so nearly all the syncopation comes from the guitar lick and the voice.

**Against this page.** Same key (E major). He hears no chorus at all — verse/bridge only — where this page's `structure:` reads I-V-C-V-C-B-C.

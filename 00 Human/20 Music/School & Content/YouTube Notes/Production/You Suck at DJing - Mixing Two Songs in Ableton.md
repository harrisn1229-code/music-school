# You Suck at DJing: Mixing Two Songs in Ableton

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F18M_QjfUuw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F18M_QjfUuw)

## Synopsis

The foundational DJ-mixing-in-Ableton setup video — the series' actual "how it's actually done" lesson that the bass-swap and tempo-ride videos build on top of: BPM detection via Warp, matching the master tempo, and routing both decks through sends (not direct-to-master) so blending them doesn't clip.

## Techniques

- **"Warp from here (straight)" on the first downbeat is the BPM-detection method for any dragged-in song.** Move the play marker to the exact first downbeat, right-click it, select Warp from here (straight) — Live analyzes and reports its best-guess BPM from that point; round to the nearest whole number and use that as the song's working tempo.
- **Set the project's master BPM to match the song's detected tempo before doing anything else** — a mismatched master BPM (e.g. a default 120 project with a 107 BPM song) makes playback sound sped up/slowed down relative to the actual track; matching it first is a prerequisite for every other DJ-mixing step.
- **Route each deck to Sends Only (not directly to Master) so a blended mix doesn't clip.** In each track's In/Out section, set Audio To → Sends Only — sends cap out at 0 dB, so there's a hard ceiling that prevents the fader-riding done during a blend (see [[DJ Mixing]]'s EQ/BPM techniques) from pushing the signal into clipping the way a direct Volume fader could.
- **Add a Utility device on each return track as a clipping safety net for when both decks play together.** Two songs playing simultaneously during a blend can sum to a hotter combined signal than either alone; a Utility on the return gives headroom to pull back before the combined signal pushes Live's master bus into the red.

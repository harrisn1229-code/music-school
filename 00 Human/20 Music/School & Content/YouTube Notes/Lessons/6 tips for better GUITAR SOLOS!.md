# 6 tips for better GUITAR SOLOS!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MT7l5K0rt6A)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MT7l5K0rt6A)

## Synopsis

BERNTH breaks down his own intro guitar solo from his debut album ("The Kindling," *Elevation*) into six techniques: dynamics via placed breaks, overlapping-interval phrasing, creative scale-run variation, melody harmonization (including a free diminished-arpeggio harmonization trick), scale-native dissonance as color, and an improvise-then-curate composition workflow.

## What's taught

- **Dynamics via placed breaks:** two short silences inserted after the opening melody and after the first scale run, giving the solo room to breathe and building anticipation before the more technical material rather than opening with a long uninterrupted run.
- **Overlapping notes/intervals instead of single-note-only lines:** deliberately include 2nds, 3rds, 4ths, unisons and 7ths as intervals within a lead line, not just scale/arpeggio single notes — flagged as underused in rock/metal soloing. Practice suggestion: improvise over a familiar scale shape using only intervals, no single notes.
- **Creative scale-run variation:** instead of a plain ascending/descending run, use an alternate-picking pattern that breaks the straight-line shape, and for a descending phrase, play a short fragment and repeat it rather than running straight down.
- **Harmonizing melodies, including a free trick for diminished material:** a diminished sweep-arpeggio lick harmonizes for free by replaying the identical shape a minor 3rd higher (exploiting the shape's own minor-3rd symmetry). General rule for any melody: harmonize by adding a consistent interval drawn from the underlying scale — 3rds/5ths for a straightforward sound, 4ths/6ths for a more fusion-leaning color.
- **Scale-native dissonance as a deliberate color choice:** reach for dissonant intervals (his preference: the minor 2nd) specifically when both notes already belong to the harmonic context/scale being played over — the difference between dissonance that reads as intentional color versus a wrong note. The diminished-arpeggio material is cited as the same principle at work.
- **Improvise-then-curate composition workflow:** the solo wasn't written note-by-note on paper — it was improvised for many hours over a looped riff in Cubase, recording and saving favorite takes/ideas, then building the final solo part-by-part by revisiting saved takes away from the instrument. Combines spontaneous improvisation with a deliberately planned tension curve.

# 'Life On Mars?' is revenge on Frank Sinatra

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dd-b8GbOPKg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dd-b8GbOPKg)

## Synopsis

The origin story (sourced from a 2002 Bowie interview) behind "Life on Mars?": Bowie wrote an English lyric for a French song in 1968, it was rejected in favor of a rival lyricist's version, which became Frank Sinatra's "My Way." Bowie, reportedly furious, deliberately wrote "Life on Mars?" as a harmonic answer to "My Way" — the video proves it bar-by-bar rather than just repeating the anecdote.

## Techniques

- **Take an existing song's harmonic opening device and invert its direction to generate a genuinely different piece, rather than varying it superficially.** "My Way" and "Life on Mars?" (transposed to the same key, F) are harmonically identical for the first seven bars: both open on the exact same descending line-cliché progression (F, descending by semitone) — the same device documented generally in [[Line Cliché]]. "My Way" continues that line DOWNWARD into a conventional jazz ballad. "Life on Mars?" instead takes the identical starting chords and reverses the direction — ascends by semitone each bar (top note of each chord rising instead of the bottom note falling) — and keeps climbing until it resolves into an entirely new key (B♭ major) for the chorus. The shared opening makes the divergence audible as a deliberate reference rather than coincidence; flipping ascending/descending on a borrowed line cliché is a concrete, reusable way to build a new song that both echoes and answers a specific existing one.
- **A songwriting-motivation note worth keeping as color, not technique**: Bowie wrote this as a commercially unproven artist (one flop album, still writing lyrics for other people's songs) only months before "Space Oddity" — the song that launched his career — making the "answer song" itself a pivot-point work, not something written from an established position.

# these sampling secrets will instantly elevate your beats!?

## Synopsis

A short, dense loner tutorial (7:18) built as three worked examples rather than one long breakdown: flipping a random sample into a Metro Boomin-style dark trap beat, flipping a different sample into a smooth R&B beat, and a dedicated "beat block" exercise for starting from nothing. loner sources the raw material live on **sample.io**, deliberately dialing the site's view-count filter down to surface *underused* material rather than whatever's already been flipped by everyone else — a sourcing habit that's more concrete and repeatable than most "go find a good sample" advice. The dark-trap flip demonstrates a full loop-to-beat pipeline (chop → consolidate → 3-slice audition → EQ → simple drum bounce → reverse experiment → clone for a second section with a different pattern → repurpose a bad transition as a layer instead of discarding it). The R&B flip pairs a reverse+time-stretch+halftime treatment on the chords with a hard-kick/ambient-perc drum pattern and automates both the halftime and an EQ high-cut to separate the intro from the drop. The beat-block segment is the most broadly reusable idea on the page: find a sample with exactly one instrument in it (no vocals, no other instrumentation), chop it, and build a chord progression underneath in the correct key — using the sample as a creative anchor rather than staring at a blank session. Light on exact plugin names and numeric settings (loner refers to "the free sampler" without confirming which one, and doesn't show on-screen values for EQ/reverse/stretch amounts), so most bullets below are captured qualitatively per the depth standard rather than with invented numbers.

## Techniques

- **Source samples on sample.io filtered by genre AND capped view count (<21,000 views) to avoid already-flipped material.** Set the genre filter (used Fun Console / Brass and Military / Latin here), cap the view-count filter low so only lower-visibility uploads surface, then Shuffle and audition results by ear until one has usable chords (specifically listened for "nice minor chords"). The view-count cap is the transferable habit — it's a proxy for "nobody's flipped this to death yet," the same underused-source logic as trading in Discord communities rather than grabbing the top Splice result. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Rough-chop first, consolidate, then re-slice into 3 for audition — separate the "get it into pieces" pass from the "pick the good pieces" pass.** Chop the sample roughly on its transients without worrying about precision, consolidate the chopped track into one clip, then split THAT into three broad slices and play through them specifically to find a usable chop — a two-stage workflow (rough-chop → audition-chop) rather than trying to find the keeper chop on the first pass. → [[Sample Browser and Clips Workflow]]
- **Dark-trap chop treatment: EQ out the low end of the chop, keep the drum bounce deliberately simple ("like Metro"), and try reversing part of the chop as a variation to audition.** For a verse-section chop with no vocals/extra instruments (valued specifically for "setting a vibe" while staying empty), EQ the bass out of the sample itself (clears room for the beat's own [[SUB Slot]]/[[BASS Slot]] element) and keep the drum programming sparse rather than busy — cited as Metro Boomin's drum-bounce restraint. Reversing a portion of the chop is offered as a quick variation to try, not committed to unconditionally. → [[SUB Slot]], [[EQ]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **Clone the chop for a second section and build it as a deliberately DIFFERENT part: cut more highs, write a whole different stop-start drum pattern.** Rather than reusing the first section's chop/pattern for a second part of the beat, clone the chop, EQ out more of the high end on the clone, and write an entirely new "stop start" drum pattern for it — the structural contrast between sections comes from re-treating one source differently, not from a second sample. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **When a chopped transition sounds bad, layer it under/over an existing element instead of discarding it.** A second chop's transition wasn't landing on its own, so rather than re-chopping or cutting it, it was layered on top of a bell sound already present in the beat — turns an unusable transition into a textural layer instead of wasted material. → [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **R&B/soul flip: reverse the chord chop, time-stretch it to the target tempo (125 BPM here), then apply halftime feel and EQ off the top end.** A two-chord chop is reversed and time-stretched to the song's BPM, then given a halftime treatment and an EQ pass removing high end — the reverse+stretch+halftime+EQ sequence (same family of moves as the darker trap flip elsewhere in this note-set) is what turns a bright soul chord into a smoothed-out R&B pad. *(general starting point — no reverse/stretch ratio or EQ frequency given on screen)* → [[Sampling and Sound Selection|Reverse Sampling]], [[EQ]]
- **Add deliberately hard, simple-spaced kicks under a softened R&B chop to restore low end the EQ pass removed.** Once the chord sample's low end has been EQ'd away, the beat "is crying out for" bass energy — answered with hard, punchy kicks on a simple, spaced-out pattern (not a busy one) rather than restoring low end on the sample itself. → [[Kick Design]], [[BASS Slot]]
- **Replace every other snare hit with an ambient percussion sound for a less mechanical R&B backbeat.** Instead of a straight repeated snare pattern, alternate: snare, ambient-perc-hit-in-place-of-snare, snare, ambient-perc-hit, etc. — breaks up the predictability of a repeated snare sample cheaply, without needing a second full snare sample. → [[Snare Design]]
- **Automate halftime engagement so it switches on for the drop and off for the intro, instead of committing the whole beat to one feel.** The halftime treatment applied to the R&B chop is automated on/off rather than baked in for the whole track — off (or different) in the intro, engaged specifically "when it drops," so the same chop reads as two different rhythmic feels across the arrangement. → [[Automation]]
- **Automate an EQ high-cut to darken/thin the intro, then clear it when the drums enter, as a cheap intro/drop separation move.** A second automation clip drives the chord chop's EQ so the intro plays filtered/darker, then the EQ opens back up when the drums come in — stated purpose is purely to create separation between the intro and the drop section, not a tonal correction. → [[Automation]], [[EQ]]
- **Beat-block exercise: find a sample with exactly ONE instrument and no vocals, chop it, and write chords underneath it in the correct key.** The stated fix for staring at a blank session: source a single-instrument sample (a Japanese flute recording here, with no other instrumentation layered in), chop it up, then write/drag in a chord progression pitched to the correct key underneath the chopped flute — the existing sample supplies the melodic anchor and mood, so the rest of the beat gets built reactively around it instead of from a blank page. Explicitly framed as a repeatable creativity exercise, not a one-off trick. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[TEXTURE Slot]]
- **A sampler's built-in one-shot delay + pitch-shift, used to add movement/character to a plain one-shot.** loner runs the chopped flute one-shots through "the free sampler['s]" built-in delay (not identified further on screen — see report) and pitch-shifts the delayed repeats up, so the echo reads at a different pitch than the dry hit — described as "amazing" for making a static one-shot feel alive. *(plugin identity unconfirmed from the transcript — general technique, not linked to a specific owned or unowned tool)* → [[Delay]]
- **Round the beat out with a counter-melody plus a light delay on an underlying piano part.** After the flute + chords foundation is in place, a counter-melody is figured out by ear and a light delay is added to a piano part sitting underneath — filling in low end and adding "bouncy drums" is mentioned as the final finishing step, without further detail. → [[Melody Writing]]

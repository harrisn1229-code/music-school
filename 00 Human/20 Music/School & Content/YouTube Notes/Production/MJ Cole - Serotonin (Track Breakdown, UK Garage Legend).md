# MJ Cole "Serotonin" (Track Breakdown) [UK Garage Legend]

📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GO8WNJkWhQ0)

## Synopsis

A relaxed, unpolished session walkthrough from a UK garage pioneer — notable for how loose and non-dogmatic the workflow is: chords auditioned across several synths, no rigid grouping discipline, and a lyric session that started from a word list rather than a finished song structure. The most reusable production ideas are the reversed-vocal-ramp sampling technique, the SSL-style hat-bus compression settings, and a full, named master-bus chain.

## Techniques

- **Two-kick layering (one with more attack blended into a fuller body layer) with velocity variation applied throughout the whole drum pattern for feel** — not a single kick sample, and deliberately not uniform velocity; "I'm all over the velocities the whole time" to keep the drums from feeling mechanical. Snare built the same layered way. → [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **16th-note-triplet swing as a simple, unremarkable default** — his own framing: "nothing more special than that." Noted in passing: an imperfectly-trimmed sample with a slight gap at its start has the same practical effect as intentionally delaying that sample — an accidental source of swing-like feel, not deliberately engineered but worth recognizing when it happens.
- **Hat/top drum bus: SSL-style bus compressor with a long attack and a short release, specifically to let the initial transient "pop" through before the compressor grabs it and then recovers quickly** — the attack is slow enough to pass the drum's pop, the release fast enough that the gain reduction doesn't linger into the next hit. → [[Compression]]
- **Audition the same chord idea across multiple different synths before committing, sometimes layering two that both worked.** A habit of trying a chord progression in more than one patch/synth rather than building it once and moving on — not every experiment gets used, but the process of comparison is routine.
- **Reversed vocal "ramp" sampling technique: take a vocal, add reverb, space small fragments out, bounce to audio, then reverse that bounced file to get rising pitch/texture ramps, then sample/chop those ramps as new source material.** A distinct sound-design source built entirely from processed-then-reversed vocal material rather than a synth or sample pack. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]
- **A loose, word-list-first vocal session instead of a fully written song** — didn't treat the session "like a song," worked from a list of words on paper and had the vocalist sing from that directly, letting the melody/lyric take shape in the room rather than arriving pre-written.
- **Processed delay-return chain: vocal → delay (quarter-note timed) → that delay's own return bus gets a dedicated compressor (sidechained to the kick) → THEN reverb after the compressor.** Distinct from a plain delay send: the delay repeats themselves get compressed and ducked to the kick before the reverb smears them further, rather than sending a dry delay straight into reverb. → [[Delay]]
- **808 processed for small-speaker translation: two 808 samples layered an octave apart (low + high), with the pair driven/distorted specifically to generate harmonics up top** so the low end still registers audibly on a phone speaker or anything that can't reproduce true sub frequencies — a deliberate translation move, not just a tone choice. → [[Bass Design]]
- **Tambourine built from two separate tambourine layers (higher + lower), bounced to one audio file, with a stereo spreader plugin on top for width.**
- **Don't group/bus elements out of habit — only group when there's an actual reason to.** Explicit stance: "if something sounds good I don't feel like I need to group it, then I just won't" — organizational tidiness isn't pursued for its own sake.
- **A half-time section built by bouncing the main drums to audio and running that bounce through a filter** (Logic's Auto Filter) rather than programming a separate half-time pattern from scratch — a fast way to get an arrangement variant from existing material.
- **Master bus chain: Supercharger (used on literally every track as a signature compression/character move — "it just canes it") → Pultec-style EQ with a small ~12 kHz top lift.** Named go-to tools beyond this chain: RC-48 (character/compression unit), "soft tube"-style compressors, and a FabFilter limiter as his trusted final stage. → [[Master Chain]]

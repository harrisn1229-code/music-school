# loner - do you need pedals to make industry level melodies!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H-3SmPmGJOg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H-3SmPmGJOg)

## Synopsis

A head-to-head format video: loner and a collaborator, Kingfisher, are each handed the same sample "starter" and race to build a melody around it — loner staying entirely inside VSTs, Kingfisher routing everything through a rack of guitar pedals — to test whether pedals are actually worth it for melody-making versus staying in-the-box. loner keeps his half deliberately simple (a dark, synthy one-shot preset, reversed and chopped) and hands off. Kingfisher's half is the meat of the video: he loops and detunes the starter in a looper pedal, runs a stretched Mellotron bell layer through a pitch-shifting delay pedal, adds two more Mellotron phrases but pulls them back to single one-shot notes once a reverb+delay combination made things "too cluttered," layers a pad through the same reverb pedal for texture, and finishes with a supporting key layer, an LFO-shaped rising/falling filter, an arpeggio, and a synth bass. His stated takeaway is the video's actual thesis: pedals produce trial-and-error, sometimes-unique textures that pure in-the-box work wouldn't stumble into, but the workflow is genuinely less efficient — worth it only if that fits how you like to work. loner then takes the composite back, adds a second pass of plugin-based pedal-emulation texture (Output Portal, recorded live into an audio editor to catch happy accidents), builds a counter-melody from a reversed/stretched string patch, and finishes the beat with drums (a Wheezy-style off-grid clap, hats driving the bounce, kick and percs last). Short and light on hard numbers (auto-captioned spoken narration over a fast edit), but dense with concrete workflow moves for melody-layering, restraint, and "record the happy accident" sound design.

## Techniques

**loner's VST pass (opening)**
- **"Cry About It" preset** — a dark, synthy one-shot patch used to start the beat; VST name not stated in captions. *(general character: dark/synthy)*
- **Reverse + chop the starter sample** as the very first move to finish off the sample before handing it to Kingfisher — a fast way to make a stock starter feel personal before any melodic work begins. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

**Kingfisher's guitar-pedal melody build**
- **Loop + detune in a looper pedal (Chase Bliss Blooper)** — looped the starter's pattern, then stretched it out and added detune via the pedal's **stability** knob, producing pitch drift/wow character on the loop rather than a locked, static repeat. → [[Modulation]], `Chase Bliss Blooper` (wishlist)
- **Pitch-shifting delay on a Mellotron bell layer (Chase Bliss Thermae)** — ran stretched-out Mellotron bells through the pedal to get a **pitch-shifting delay** effect (each repeat lands at a different pitch rather than a flat echo). → [[Delay]], `Chase Bliss Thermae` (wishlist), `Mellotron` (on [[Vintage Gear Worklist]])
- **Restraint after over-layering: two Mellotron phrases got cut back to one-shot notes.** Two additional Mellotron key-sound phrases were added, but once run through the reverb pedal (Mood) and the granular texture pedal (Microcosm) together, the combination "got way too cluttered with the reverbs and delays" — the fix wasn't better settings, it was using only the **first note of each phrase** as a one-shot melody (paired with a harp sound) instead of the full phrase. **Lesson: stacking reverb + granular texture on a melodic phrase can bury the phrase itself — when that happens, strip the phrase down to a single hit rather than fighting the wash with EQ/automation.** → [[Reverb]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], `Chase Bliss Mood` (wishlist), `Hologram Microcosm` (wishlist)
- **"You never know what's going to sound good until you experiment"** — the stated core of the pedal workflow: combinations of effects are auditioned by trial and error rather than dialed in from a plan, deliberately embracing over-layering early ("I knew I was kind of going overboard... but I wanted to let all my ideas flow out") so there's plenty of raw material once everything runs through the pedals.
- **Pad texture: Mood with a fast delay time + Microcosm layered on top** — the final melodic layer added was a pad run through the reverb pedal set to a **fast delay**, then further textured with the granular pedal, for interesting evolving texture rather than a static pad wash. → [[Reverb]], [[Delay]], [[TEXTURE Slot]]
- **Supporting key layer + LFO tool for a rising/falling filter effect** — a supporting key part was added to fill out low-mid frequencies, then shaped with an **LFO tool** to give it a rising-and-falling movement (read as an LFO-modulated filter cutoff). → [[LFO Tool]], [[BASS Slot]] / [[BED Slot]]
- **ARP to fill the high end and lift energy** — an arpeggiator was added specifically to occupy the top end of the arrangement and increase perceived energy once the lower/mid layers were locked. → [[Arpeggiator]], [[AIR Slot]]
- **Synth bass reused from the intro** — rather than writing a new bass part, the synth bass used earlier in the intro was carried through as the final low-end layer.
- **Stated trade-off (the video's thesis): pedals = more trial and error and sometimes-unique textures, at the cost of efficiency** — "there can be a lot of trial and error on the way to the final product, but through experimentation you can come across some pretty unique textures that never would have come about if you had just stayed inside the DAW... it's not the most efficient way to process your sounds."

**loner's second pass (finishing the beat)**
- **Portal (Output) run and recorded live into an audio editor to capture happy accidents** — loaded [[Portal]] on the starter for its textural "pedal lock"-style sounds, hit record into an audio recorder (Edison, FL Studio's built-in editor — no owned equivalent needed, the technique is DAW-agnostic: record the plugin's live output continuously while auditioning presets/knob moves) and let it run while browsing presets, listening for a moment worth keeping rather than programming a result upfront. Landed on a preset with an almost-percussive character. → [[Portal]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]
- **Repurpose a "happy accident" texture as a drum fill** — the percussive Portal capture above was used like a drum fill at the end of the loop rather than as a melodic/textural layer, because its transient character suited that role better. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Reverse + chop the end of a recorded texture to land the transition** — reversed the captured Portal take, then chopped the tail off until the hit landed right — trial-and-error editing by ear ("we could try and reverse this one... maybe... whoa yep that's it").
- **EQ cut (~2–3 dB) to make a layer "less present"** — a straightforward attenuating EQ move (stated as "bring this down by one, two... bring it down by three," i.e. incremental dB cuts) used specifically to push a layer back in the mix rather than to correct tone. *(general starting point: a broad cut in the 2–4 kHz presence range reads as "less present"; exact band not specified in the transcript)* → [[EQ]]
- **Counter-melody from a reversed, stretched string patch** — built a counter-melody in an augmented-strings instrument, bounced it to audio, reversed it, then time-stretched the reversed audio for the final melodic texture — the same reverse-then-stretch move used on the Portal texture above, applied to a melodic (not just textural) part. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Melody Writing]]
- **Revisit and change an earlier melodic choice once the arrangement fills in** — after the counter-melody landed, the Bell melody from earlier in the beat was changed to fit better against the new layer — a reminder that melody choices made early in a layered build are provisional until the fuller arrangement is audible.
- **Drums added last, Wheezy-style clap placement** — a "classic Wheezy clap" was used with a deliberately different bounce, placing the clap **off the normal backbeat position** rather than in its usual spot. Hats supply the main bounce/groove; the kick and additional percussion are added after the hats, with percs going in just before the final kick. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Rhythm and Groove]]

# 6 Songs with Confusing Intros

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ltvAG9GZWrQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ltvAG9GZWrQ)

## Synopsis

A follow-up "confusing intros" survey (companion to an earlier video of the same theme) — six songs whose intros lack the wider musical context needed to place the downbeat correctly, so the ear latches onto the wrong pulse until later instrumentation corrects it.

## Techniques

- **Early-start illusion ("and-of-4" downbeat)**: "Take It Easy" (Eagles), "Misty Mountain Hop" (Led Zeppelin), and "Drive My Car" (Beatles) all effectively begin on the last eighth note before the true downbeat — the ear perceives an extra 8th note that isn't there, as if one bar briefly became 9/8, until the verse arrives and reveals the true 1.
- **Late-start illusion (beat-2 downbeat)**: a different Beatles song and Hall & Oates' "You Make My Dreams Come True" both start on beat 2 of the bar rather than beat 1 — the opposite direction of confusion from the early-start group, so the ear counts one full beat too late until corrected.
- **Meter misdirection (compound vs. simple)**: "Hold the Line" (Toto) sounds like straight 4/4 from the piano chord placement, but the song is actually in 12/8 — what's heard as four quarter notes is really four dotted-quarter groupings of triplet 8ths, producing a genuine polymetric-feeling shift once the correct subdivision clicks in.
- **General mechanism, restated across all six**: with no surrounding musical context, the ear naturally "entrains" (locks in) to whatever pulse it hears first as if it were the downbeat — a completely involuntary process, not a deliberate listening choice.

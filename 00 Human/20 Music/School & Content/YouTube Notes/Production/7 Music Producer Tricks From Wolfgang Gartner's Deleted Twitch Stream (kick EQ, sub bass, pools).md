# 7 Music Producer Tricks From Wolfgang Gartner's Deleted Twitch Stream (kick EQ, sub bass, pools)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)

## Synopsis
Sol State pulls a run of concrete production habits out of a since-deleted Wolfgang Gartner Twitch stream, moving fast across arrangement, sound design, vocal doubling, songwriting ritual, and mastering. The throughline is specificity — square-wave subs get a per-note harmonic notch rather than a generic EQ pass, kick and bass get named problem frequencies instead of "cut the mud," and the master chain gets both a volume-automation move and a two-limiter split rather than one blanket limiter. It closes with an unusually candid look at his own songwriting ritual: visualizing a finished song by the pool before ever touching an instrument.

## Techniques
- **Build the pre-drop fill as its own dedicated section, not an afterthought** — the moment where the track's effects/elements sequentially cut off and fall away, opening space right before the drop, is treated as its own composition problem worth "days" of isolated work; the strength of that suspense directly sets how big the drop reads when it lands. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Wolfgang Gartner (Sol State), [7 Music Producer Tricks From Wolfgang Gartner's Deleted Twitch Stream (kick EQ, sub bass, pools)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)
- **Use a filtered square wave for sub bass instead of a sine or triangle** — a square wave run through a low-pass filter (to strip it back to just the sub) hits harder than a sine or triangle-wave sub; his default "plain sub" patch is a square wave more often than not. → [[Bass Design]] — Wolfgang Gartner (Sol State), [7 Music Producer Tricks From Wolfgang Gartner's Deleted Twitch Stream (kick EQ, sub bass, pools)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)
- **Notch out the major harmonic of a square-wave bass, per note** — square waves throw off a prominent "major" harmonic that can pop out and clash with a minor-key song; find that harmonic's frequency (different for every note in the bassline) and cut it with a very narrow EQ, then repeat the same narrow notch for every distinct note/harmonic in the part. → [[Bass Design]], [[EQ]] — Wolfgang Gartner (Sol State), [7 Music Producer Tricks From Wolfgang Gartner's Deleted Twitch Stream (kick EQ, sub bass, pools)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)
- **Double the lead vocal an octave up on almost every track** — record (or pitch-shift) a higher-octave layer of the vocal and layer it back with the main take; used on "almost all" of his vocals to add energy and accentuate the emotional peak of a line, a trick he traces back to Earth, Wind & Fire records. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]] — Wolfgang Gartner (Sol State), [7 Music Producer Tricks From Wolfgang Gartner's Deleted Twitch Stream (kick EQ, sub bass, pools)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)
- **Visualize the finished song by the pool before writing a note** — a shift away from starting a song by banging on drums/keys until something sounds cool: sit outside by the pool, close his eyes, and imagine the actual finished song/vision first, then come inside and write toward that vision — never starts writing without a vision anymore. — Wolfgang Gartner (Sol State), [7 Music Producer Tricks From Wolfgang Gartner's Deleted Twitch Stream (kick EQ, sub bass, pools)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)
- **Roll off the extreme highs and lows on every master, then bump presence with a small treble shelf/bell** — the high-pass/low-pass rolloff isn't fixed (cutoff and steepness vary song to song, sometimes barely doing anything, sometimes much more aggressive), but some rolloff on both ends happens on every master; separately, bump the treble by 1–2 dB somewhere in the ~11–16k range (a ~15k bump on the example song shown). → [[Master Chain]], [[EQ]] — Wolfgang Gartner (Sol State), [7 Music Producer Tricks From Wolfgang Gartner's Deleted Twitch Stream (kick EQ, sub bass, pools)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)
- **Three specific problem frequencies to notch on kicks and bass: ~110–125 Hz, ~250 Hz, and ~500–600 Hz** — these three ranges are the recurring trouble spots he EQs out of kick drums and bass to get a clean low end, on top of generally cutting the excess ultra-low content that's easy to miss in kick/bass samples. → [[Kick Design]], [[Bass Design]] — Wolfgang Gartner (Sol State), [7 Music Producer Tricks From Wolfgang Gartner's Deleted Twitch Stream (kick EQ, sub bass, pools)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)
- **Automate the master volume to get subliminally louder across the song, and ride the breakdowns down** — the master fader is automated so overall level creeps up through the song (e.g. +0.21 dB by the final drop, a further +0.3–0.5 dB bump into the last drop) so the track feels like it's quietly building in loudness as it plays; breakdowns get pulled down (roughly −2 dB, e.g. −1.74 dB on the example) and then ramped back up in a short automation move right before the drop hits. → [[Master Chain]], [[Automation]] — Wolfgang Gartner (Sol State), [7 Music Producer Tricks From Wolfgang Gartner's Deleted Twitch Stream (kick EQ, sub bass, pools)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)
- **Split limiting duty across two limiters on the master** — a Waves L2 doing light gain reduction (roughly 1–2 dB) feeding a FabFilter Pro-L2 doing more (roughly another 1–2 dB), rather than asking one limiter to absorb the whole loudness push. → [[Limiting]], [[Pro-L 2]] — Wolfgang Gartner (Sol State), [7 Music Producer Tricks From Wolfgang Gartner's Deleted Twitch Stream (kick EQ, sub bass, pools)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P-0vQIKHu4k)

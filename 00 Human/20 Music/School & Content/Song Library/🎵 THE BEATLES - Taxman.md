# Taxman — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (a fake spliced count-off, then 2 bars vamping the bass ostinato) · Verse/Refrain (**13 bars** — an 8-bar couplet balanced against a lopsided 5-bar phrase, [[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]) · Bridge (**9 bars**, the second phrase stretched by one) · the pair twice more, with a guitar solo occupying the verse portion of an otherwise ordinary pass · Outro (fade).

**There is no V chord in this song.** The harmony is I, ♭VII and IV ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]) with one late ♭III, and the mode is deliberately undecidable: **the third degree is simply absent from the tune** ([[SCL-C05 — Characteristic notes]]), so Mixolydian and Dorian are both live readings while the guitars keep supplying a major/minor I. The tune itself is pentatonic and mantra-like, circling a few motifs in a narrow range ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]).

The ♭III arrives once, late, purely to signal the end is coming, its two sustained bars filled by a descending bass counterpoint. Despite the asymmetry the verse reads as blues-adjacent — AAB rhetoric, obbligato-filled gaps, and a ♭VII→IV that paraphrases the blues V→IV.

The whole track is **one long crescendo of density**, staged verse by verse in those gaps: guitar chops and cymbal slashes, then tambourine, then cowbell and falsetto rejoinders, then a three-part shout. Paul's bass ostinato is **one bar long with a saw-tooth contour** — tense and tightly wound where "Day Tripper" and "Paperback Writer" use two-bar arches — and after taking the solo he stays in, doubling that ostinato an octave up.

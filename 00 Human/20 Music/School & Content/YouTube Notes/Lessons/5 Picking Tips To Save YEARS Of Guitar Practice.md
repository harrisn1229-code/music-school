# 5 Picking Tips To Save YEARS Of Guitar Practice!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WmQWTOimz4k)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WmQWTOimz4k)

## Synopsis

A deep picking-technique episode: pick size/grip choices, a knuckle-scratching fix, a 5-step progressive wrist-unlocking exercise ladder, and — stated as the video's real payoff — training ONE relaxed wrist motion that transfers across every picking technique (alternate, economy, legato, hybrid, sweep) instead of developing a separate motion per technique.

## What's taught

- **Gear/grip note:** switched to small Jazz III-size picks over larger picks specifically to reduce getting stuck between strings; settled on a loose, natural "let the thumb fall" grip after experimenting with a tighter O-shape grip that gave inconsistent results session to session.
- **Knuckle-scratching fix.** A real physical problem when switching to smaller picks: knuckles rubbing against upper strings during fast picking caused string noise and literal skin abrasion. Diagnosed as a **picking-hand** technique issue, not a fretting-hand one — fix is keeping the picking-hand knuckles/fingers clear of the higher strings, which also eliminated his reliance on a fretwrap for muting.
- **5-step wrist-unlocking exercise progression, explicitly sequenced:**
  1. Dead-note pattern that's physically impossible to play with full-arm/elbow motion — forces wrist isolation by design.
  2. Same dead-note approach with a harder rhythmic grouping (1-2-3 repeating) layered on top.
  3. Two-note-per-string patterns built from chord shapes/arpeggios/cadences — trains string-transitioning specifically (constant string changes after only 2 picks).
  4. One-note-per-string patterns using the same chord/arpeggio shapes — a harder string-transition drill than step 3.
  5. A minor scale ascending/descending with a deliberate subdivision switch mid-exercise (16th notes → 16th-note triplets) — trains keeping the same relaxed wrist motion across different rhythmic densities rather than tensing up at the faster subdivision.
- **The stated big-picture lesson:** most players (himself included, historically) develop a *different* physical motion/angle per picking technique — one feel for 2-note-per-string, another for 3-note-per-string, another for sweep or hybrid — and everything falls apart switching between them mid-song. The fix is training one relaxed, unlocked wrist motion and applying it to all techniques, rather than five separate skills.
- **Concrete daily workout named:** a short (~90-second) combined drill touching alternate picking, economy picking, legato (hammer-on/pull-off), hybrid picking (for chord voicings), and sweep picking in one pass, specifically to keep the same wrist motion consistent across all five.

# Delay

General best practice for delay — throws, movement, and using echoes as arrangement. Specific tools: [[EchoBoy]], [[Echo]] (Ableton), the delay sends. See also [[Reverb]], [[Depth and Space]].

## What it is
Repeats — for depth, rhythmic interest, width, and dramatic tape/feedback throws.

## Owned (first-call)
[[EchoBoy]] · [[Echo]] (Ableton) · [[Delay]] (Ableton, basic stock delay) · [[Grain Delay]] (Ableton, granular) · [[Spectral Time]] (Ableton, frequency-bin spectral delay) · [[Note Echo]] · [[ValhallaDelay]]

## From videos

- **A very tight (short-time, low-feedback) analog delay used purely to THICKEN a repeated bass note, not for audible echo/rhythm.** Tuned so tight the individual repeats aren't perceived as discrete echoes — the delay reads as added density/weight on the note itself rather than a rhythmic effect; too much added past a point starts to thin the sound out instead of thickening it, so there's a ceiling to find by ear. Layered with the phaser entry on [[Modulation]] (delay for thickness, phaser for sweep) and, like every effect in this source, always mult'd against the dry signal — see [[Parallel Processing]]. → [[Bass Design]], [[Modulation]], [[Parallel Processing]] — Anthony Marinelli, [[Synth Bass on Steroids - The Power of EFX Pedals]]
- **The BPM-to-milliseconds formula for timing any delay that doesn't offer native note-value sync: `60,000 / BPM` = one quarter note in ms.** At 120 BPM: 500 ms quarter note; halve repeatedly for eighth (250 ms)/sixteenth (125 ms); double for half (1000 ms)/whole (2000 ms). Dotted note = ×1.5 (dotted quarter at 120 BPM = 750 ms); triplet = ÷1.5 (quarter-note triplet at 120 BPM = 333 ms). Delay is the one processor where this formula is directly sufficient — no by-ear correction needed, unlike reverb decay or compression timing (see [[Reverb]], [[Compression]]). 🎬 *From video:* set the ms field directly from this table when a delay lacks tempo sync. → [[Reverb]] — Sage Audio, [How to Time Every Audio Effect to the BPM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MXz2hPL36PQ)
- **A "throw" delay kept permanently set up as a quick reach-for-anything effect: a single delay tap, often with little or no regeneration at all.** "One of my PCM 42 things — one of the things I'll usually have set up as just a throw that I can quickly put a little bit of on anything — is just a one-time delay, and a lot of times it doesn't even have any regeneration." The move itself needs no specialized hardware — a stock delay with Feedback pulled to (or near) 0, always patched and ready, reproduces the behavior; the named unit (Lexicon PCM 42) is a personal-favorite tool for it. → [[Echo]], [[TEXTURE Slot]] — Mix with the Masters (Andy Wallace), [[Mix with the Masters 2011 - Andy Wallace]]
- **Cross-wire a delay unit's own internal stereo FEEDBACK path — left channel's repeats feed back into the right side and vice versa — for a genuinely bouncing delay, distinct from the cross-feed-a-SEND trick logged below.** "In the Echo Boy, you can cross-balance the feedback mix so that I can have feedback from the right feeding back into the left and vice versa." Different mechanism from Bob Clearmountain's send-routing trick just below this entry (which cross-feeds a *source* into the opposite side of a stereo effect from the outside) — this one rewires the repeats *inside* the delay itself. Direct owned match: [[EchoBoy]]'s Ping-Pong/multi-tap mode. → [[EchoBoy]], [[Stereo Field]] — Mix with the Masters (Andy Wallace), [[Mixing Avenged Sevenfold - Andy Wallace]]

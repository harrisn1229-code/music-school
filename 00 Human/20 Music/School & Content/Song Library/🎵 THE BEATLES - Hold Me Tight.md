# Hold Me Tight — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A standard two-bridge, no-solo layout in an odd key choice for them. Pollack's angle is that the song's mixed reputation has nothing to do with its construction: **chromatic inner-voice line clichés run through most of the chord changes as a structural device rather than decoration**, and an arpeggiated ostinato figure, transposed to fit each chord, holds the rhythm track together in bass and guitar.

**Form.** A 2-bar intro that establishes the key, exposes the ostinato and plants a tag that becomes a hook. The verse is 16 bars phrased **4 + 4 + 8**, the long third phrase balancing the opening couplet and carrying an arch to a clear climax on the downbeat of bar 13. The bridge is an unusual **7 bars** whose sub-phrases don't parse neatly — though a metric elision at the end of verse 2 lets you hear it as 8 if you count the pivot bar in. The outro is a fake third bridge that grinds to a halt, with the ritardando starting three bars early.

**Harmony.** Seven chords, three of them borrowed: V-of-V, minor iv and **♭III** ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]], [[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). ♭III is the rare one — the relative major of the parallel minor, which stops looking remote once you imagine the song in minor, and against I it produces an audible wavering between major and minor third. The line clichés are directional and sequenced for effect: the verse's first phrase **rises**, its third phrase **falls**, so the coaxing opening turns grim before the climax; the bridge's line snakes, which is where its suspense comes from. Harmonic rhythm is load-bearing — the root changes slow from every bar to every other bar on approach to the climax ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]) — and the **minor iv arrives immediately after that climax**, denying the release the major IV would have given.

**Melody.** Narrow and repetitive by design. The first four bars of the long phrase hammer the same two notes, which is what makes the long-delayed high note land; the syncopation agitating the previous bars cuts out at the same moment.

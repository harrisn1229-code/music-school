# Synthet - Make the Smoothest Song Jumps

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EBjBO2F4X2Q)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EBjBO2F4X2Q)

## Synopsis

A transitions survey pitched against the "just throw in a white-noise riser" default: mine elements already in the track for transition material instead of importing new samples, treat breaks/silence as an equally valid transition device, and — when a riser genuinely is the right call — dress it up with a big reverb, a low-cut filter automated upward, and a slow fade-in rather than reaching for a raw stock riser sample.

## Techniques

- **Mine the track's own elements before importing a transition sample** — a stock white-noise riser "works" but is dull and doesn't engage the listener; first check whether the existing drums, bassline, or another element can be repurposed into a transition (a fill, a pitch/filter sweep on something already playing) before reaching for outside material. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Breaks are a transition too, not just a build** — removing elements (not necessarily everything) gives the next section "well-needed space" and can be just as effective as an added riser; the brain can't process too many simultaneous elements, so subtracting makes what remains read as more. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] (subtraction scheduling, already documented)
- **Bar-repeat as a cheap, effective build** — go to the fourth bar of a section, cut it, and repeat it (looped) for a build — "such an easy one" but reliably effective.
- **Dress up a riser rather than dropping in a raw sample** — when a white-noise/riser transition is the right call, a big reverb + a low-cut filter automated upward + a slow fade creates a more considered version of the same idea, described as an "old ADM tracks" style move.
- **Framing:** transitions don't just connect parts of a song, they connect the listener to the story being told — worth genuine attention rather than a default reach for a stock riser.

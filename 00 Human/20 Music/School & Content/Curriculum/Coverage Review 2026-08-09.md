## 3. Bowman's sequence vs. the 8-class map — the headline

Bowman is an independent, published ordering of the same subject, so where he disagrees with our map it's worth examining. **Five divergences.** None is an automatic correction; they're for you to rule on.

### D1 — He teaches all 15 major keys before anything else. We teach one concept across all keys.

Bowman: 30 numbered lessons, **one key each**, all 15 majors then all 15 minors, before a single chord is built. *"It is suggested that you have a complete understanding of each key before you proceed to the next."*

Us: Class 1 teaches derivation rules → chord function → scale construction, each as a *concept* applied to whatever key illustrates it.

**Assessment: keep ours.** His is a memorisation curriculum for a working reader who must sight-transpose. Yours is a writing-and-producing curriculum. But his ordering exposes something real — **the school never asks you to know all 15 keys cold**, and that's a genuine hole for someone who wants to write in any key without a chart. Candidate for a standing drill, not a class.

### D2 — He teaches every chord type twice, from two directions.

Part Two §A builds every chord by **scale degree**; §B rebuilds every one of the same chords by **stacked interval**. Only then, §C, do chords go into keys.

Us: Course 02 (Chord Function) teaches each chord type once.

**Assessment: this one is worth stealing.** The two derivations catch different errors — degree-thinking breaks on altered chords, interval-thinking breaks on enharmonic spelling. It's cheap to add as a second angle inside existing lessons rather than new lessons. **Recommend adopting in Phase 3.**

### D3 — He puts intervals *after* chords. We put them first.

Bowman reaches "Basic Rules of Intervals" at p. 73, well after triads and sevenths. FND-C05–C08 (interval inversion, quality names, letter distance, compound intervals) sit in Class 1, near the start.

**Assessment: keep ours.** His order works because his §A route to chords doesn't need intervals. Ours does. No change.

### D4 — Modes get five pages at the very end. We give them a course.

Bowman: modes are one line in §E, after pentatonics, before whole tone.

Us: SCL has 8 concepts, Course 03 is Scale Construction, Class 3 is built on modal colour.

**Assessment: keep ours, emphatically.** He's writing for a general musician; modal colour is central to how you actually work. This divergence tells you his book is a floor, not a ceiling.

### D5 — Three of his ten teaching steps have no equivalent in our Homework block.

His method is **Formula → Write → Apply key signature → Play → Listen → Analyze → Verbalize → Memorize → Quiz → Review.**

Our Homework has `**Drill**` (recall) + `**Applied**` (do it) + `**Test**` (quiz). That covers Formula, Play, Analyze, Quiz, Review.

**Missing: Write it, Listen to it, Verbalize it.**

- **Write it** — by hand, in pencil, several times. His single most-repeated instruction.
- **Listen to it** — repeatedly, which is not the same as EAR-strand ear training; it's hearing the thing you just spelled.
- **Verbalize it** — say it out loud. Cheap, and it's the step that catches "I recognise this" masquerading as "I know this."

**Assessment: the strongest finding in this review.** Adding a fourth Homework line is a one-word change to the template *now* and a 53-lesson rewrite after Phase 3. See §5, Decision 1.

### 5b — RHY, sourced

[[Groove and Microtiming]], built on four papers, with two findings that contradict producer folklore:

- **Syncopation is an inverted U** — medium syncopation maximises both desire-to-move and pleasure; more is worse (Witek et al. 2014, *PLOS ONE*).
- **Hip hop swing is subtle** — median ratio **1.2:1** (≈55%) across 30 canonical breaks, and **uncorrelated with tempo** (Frane 2017, *Music Perception*).
- **"Humanize" is not reliably good** — quantized versions rated *higher* for swing than originals; expanded microtiming rated lower (Datseris et al. 2019, *Scientific Reports*). Expert listeners are more sensitive to bad microtiming than non-experts, and *fixed* offsets test worst of all (Kilchenmann & Senn 2015).

The note is honest that finding three is contested, and gives the practical rule: **the burden of proof is on the microtiming, not on the grid.**

# Why Stranger Things & The X-Files sound similar

## Synopsis

Both title themes arpeggiate the same enharmonically-ambiguous chord — spelled Cmaj7 in one and Am♭6 in the other, but built from identical notes — and the video explains why THIS specific chord (rather than a plain minor or major 7) reads as spooky/suspenseful: the ♭6 sits only a semitone from the 5th, creating a rubbing dissonance that's softened into unease rather than harshness by being arpeggiated instead of struck as a block chord. Confirms via a direct interview quote that the Stranger Things composers deliberately chose a Prophet synth to chase an X-Files-like mysterious sound.

## Techniques

- **The shared chord, two spellings of the same notes**: Stranger Things arpeggiates what reads as a Cmaj7 (C-E-G-B) — but a strong bass note (E) underneath recontextualizes the identical notes as **Em♭6** instead, since Cmaj7 and Em♭6 share every pitch. The X-Files theme arpeggiates the exact same voicing directly as an **Am♭6**-type chord. Neither composer necessarily thought of it the "other" way — the perception is entirely a function of which note dominates the bass. → [[Chord Naming by Context]], [[Slash Chords]].
- **Why THIS chord reads as spooky**: the ♭6 sits only a semitone away from the 5th, so the two notes rub against each other — genuinely dissonant if played as a block chord, but tolerable and atmospheric when **arpeggiated** (staggered one note at a time) rather than struck together. Harnessing a semitone-interval dissonance this way is named directly as a classic horror/suspense scoring technique.
- **Confirmed direct influence, not speculation**: Kyle Dixon (Survive/Stranger Things composer) is quoted directly (from an NPR interview) saying they chose a Prophet-style synth specifically "to get a very mysterious, X-Files-like sound" — an explicit, on-record acknowledgment of the connection, not just Bennett's own ear.
- **The requested brief**: Michael Stein (the other half of the Stranger Things composing duo) said the show's producers specifically asked for a "Jaws-like suspense" quality — ties the chord choice back to a director-given brief, not just a stylistic default.
- **Wider influence context**: the Stranger Things soundtrack draws heavily on late-70s/80s synth scoring — Tangerine Dream and John Carpenter are named directly as reference points — while still reading as fresh/original rather than pastiche.

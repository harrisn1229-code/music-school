# Ian Kirkpatrick — The Production Secret Behind Huge Pop Hits

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNBfmebDjqI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNBfmebDjqI)

## Synopsis

The clearest statement of Ian's production philosophy: **the complexity of great pop is not the track count — it's knowing when to perfect something and when to leave it alone.** The hardest, most "complex" decisions are the ones where you *don't* fix something. Illustrated with three hits he admires for a single committed imperfect choice.

## What's taught (philosophy)

- **Don't interfere with the songwriting / don't distract from the story.** The biggest advice he gives: production should never pull the ear off the vocal and the story. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **"Perfection" can mean leaving something alone.** The most complex decision is often "you know what, I'm gonna leave the kick like that" — committing instead of fixing. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Sia — "Chandelier":** the last line of the verse ("throw them back till I lose count") is suddenly **hard-tuned a gajillion percent** — probably a demo vocal someone chose to keep. Ian's instinct would've been "re-record that," but someone decided *no, leave it.* → [[Auto-Tune Pro]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Adele — "Hello":** all the drums are **muffled / low-pass filtered** and kept that way, which helps the vocal stand out. A committed tone choice, not a fix. → [[EQ]], [[Kick Design]]
- **Kanye — "All of the Lights":** the snare is "so like a *tone*" — a sound Ian says he'd have thrown out if he'd started with it, but Kanye committed and it's iconic. Confidence over preciousness. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **The takeaway:** pop's complexity = *when* to perfect and *when* not to. Be confident, commit, don't be precious. → [[Ian Kirkpatrick - New Rules (25 Big Tips in 13 Min)]] (the "rate of information" companion idea)

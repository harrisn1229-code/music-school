# You Suck at Producing: Stereo Width

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zjRTE41iu7s)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zjRTE41iu7s)

## Synopsis
A first-principles explainer of the Haas effect and why the "duplicate it and hard-pan both copies" instinct doesn't actually widen anything on its own — then three concrete ways to get real width from one mono source: offsetting one panned copy in *time* (via Ableton's per-track delay preference, not a delay device), detuning one copy slightly in *pitch*, and modulating a delay device's time with an LFO for an evolving, phasey width. Ties all three back to the shared underlying mechanism — a slightly-altered copy of the same sound — that also powers reverb, chorus, phaser, and flanger.

## Techniques
- **Duplicate + hard-pan-both-sides alone does NOT widen a mono sound** — grouping two identical copies and panning one hard left, one hard right sounds virtually identical to the mono original, because both copies still reach both ears at exactly the same time; there's no actual difference for the ear to perceive. Width requires the two copies to differ — in time or in pitch — not just in pan position. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Track Delay (the "T" preference) offsets one panned copy in time for real Haas width** — with two hard-panned copies of the same sound, open the track delay preferences (the small "T" icon, bottom-right of the arrangement/session view) and shift *one side's* track delay by a small amount; that tiny time offset between the two panned copies is what actually produces audible width, distinct from routing a delay device onto one side. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Stereo Field]]
- **Detune one side ~15 cents instead of time-offsetting it** — the pitch-based version of the same trick: instead of a track-delay offset, detune one of the two panned copies by roughly 15 cents; it reads as a similar width effect and starts to sound like a built-in chorus or phaser, because chorus/phaser are built on exactly this same "copy of the signal, altered slightly" principle (pitch-shift instead of time-shift). → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Modulation]]
- **LFO-modulated delay time on one side for an evolving, "phasey" width** — put a simple Delay on one side of the panned pair, unsync it and set it to a time value, then map an LFO device (Max for Live) to the delay's time knob; the continuously-shifting delay time keeps re-randomizing the Haas offset for a wide, unstable, evolving texture rather than a static width. → [[Delay]] (Ableton), [[Modulation]]
- **Reverb, chorus, phaser, and flanger are all the same trick at different settings** — every one of them is fundamentally a copy of the source, altered slightly in time and/or pitch, recombined with the original; the video frames this as the throughline connecting the whole family of space/width effects.

# Disclosure: How To Get MORE From Your Vocals (12 Tips)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZCpgHFNlXxg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZCpgHFNlXxg)

## Synopsis

Disclosure walk through a vocal-heavy garage session, covering the mix chain on a lead vocal (compression, dynamic EQ, de-essing, the "gem Dopamine trick" for brightness without harshness), a UK garage-specific high-frequency carving decision (vocals have to cede space to trebly hi-hats, on purpose, for genre authenticity), an 80s slap-back-delay vocal effect, and — the most reusable lesson — a whole philosophy of *not* over-tuning group/harmony vocals: real, untuned pitch variation between takes is what makes a stack sound human and rich rather than phasey and robotic.

## Techniques

- **The "gem Dopamine trick": boost highs while de-essing** — after a standard de-esser tames sibilance, [[Gem Dopamine]] adds a bit more de-essing *and* brightens the top end at the same time in one move, rather than de-essing and EQ-boosting as two separate steps. → [[Gem Dopamine]], [[De-essing]]
- **Vocal chain order on a lead take:** compression (pulling ~4 dB) → dynamic EQ (pulling out low-mid resonances, "a couple of resonances here") → de-esser → gem Dopamine (brightness + extra de-ess). Genre-specific finishing move: dial the vocal "a little breathier and less mid," add a bit more Dopamine, and back off compression slightly for an airier top-line vocal. → [[De-essing]], [[Compression]], [[Gem Dopamine]]
- **In UK garage specifically, carve highs OUT of the vocal on purpose to make room for trebly hi-hats** — garage's characteristic sound leans hard on bright, present hi-hats; rather than dulling the drums to avoid clashing with the vocal, cut some vocal high end instead so the hats keep their genre-appropriate treble. A tasteful amount of top-end "distortion"/grit on the vocal is treated as an intentional genre callback (old vinyl garage records naturally distort at the top when played back), not a flaw to clean up. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[EQ]]
- **80s slap-back delay instead of a short reverb for a chorus vocal that needs to feel "there" without sounding dry** — a single, clearly-audible echo repeat (the Phil Collins move) on the main vocal, plus a second, quieter slap-back delay layered underneath it. A reverb is still present but set extremely low (~1%), with its **pre-delay matched to the slap-back delay's timing** so it reinforces the same rhythmic pocket instead of smearing against it — subtle enough that "you're not really hearing it, your brain just sort of knows it's there." → [[Delay]], [[Depth and Space]]
- **Don't tune a vocal stack — deliberately keep every take's natural pitch variation.** Recording "a truckload of takes until we had perfect takes" (not one take auto-tuned/comped into many) means every layer bends slightly differently on the way into a note; stacking those un-tuned variations is what creates the richness — if every layer were pitch-perfect or auto-tuned individually, the stack would phase and sound artificial instead of human. Same philosophy applied to harmonies: harmonies are rarely tuned either. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Vocal Tuning]]
- **A harmony voicing trick: let one harmony NOT follow the melody, so a passing note lands a 7th in the chord** — rather than every harmony line tracking the lead melody in parallel, one harmony deliberately diverges from the expected note; the resulting interval creates a 7th in the implied chord under that moment, which is what makes that specific harmony "tasty" — losing the 7th on the next chord change is what makes the following chord read as more major by contrast. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **Change microphone or recording room between song sections to sell a change in space** — a clear, close verse vocal versus a big group-vocal pre-chorus can be reinforced by literally recording them differently (different mic, or a different room), not just through mix processing after the fact — used deliberately here to help the pre-chorus feel like it opens up into a bigger space. Vocal captured on an SM7 with the singer seated on the floor. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Melody-writing as a numbers game: generate many hook ideas fast, then pick favorites for chorus/pre-chorus roles** — described as "like putting money in a machine" — a jam-based group session where everyone hums/sings melody ideas until one clearly stands out and the others stop, then that idea gets assigned a section role (chorus vs. pre-chorus) after the fact rather than being written for a predetermined slot. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **A jumping 4th interval in a melody line reads as more R&B** — called out explicitly as a favorite melodic move in the verse, an ascending or descending perfect-4th leap contributing to the song's R&B feel. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **Auto-Tune used for its phasey/flangey artifact character, not for pitch correction** — a light touch of Auto-Tune on some (not all) vocal tracks, explicitly not because the pitch needed fixing (take is fine without it) but because the plugin's own processing artifacts add an interesting textural flange/phase quality when used this way.

# Tell Me What You See — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (**2 bars** vamping on I) · Verse (**16 bars** — an 8-bar verse-like couplet, then an 8-bar refrain-like couplet with slower harmonic rhythm) · Verse · Bridge (8, of which the second phrase is **entirely instrumental**) · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Outro (a compressed variant of the bridge's first phrase, hummed wordlessly, to a full ending).

A modest song with one instructive lesson: the harmony is **only I, IV and V**, and every formal boundary is articulated by changes in **harmonic rhythm** rather than by chord choice ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). Two chords per bar in the verse's first couplet, one per bar in the second, one every two bars in the bridge — the deceleration alone tells you where you are in the form. The chord opening the bridge functions as V-of-IV because of the F natural in the tune above it, but it is still the same triad either way ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]).

The tune opens with a dramatic **upward octave leap** and is loaded with appoggiaturas ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]); contour-wise it gets stuck circling the fifth degree, and — a genuine oddity — the seventh degree never appears anywhere in it.

It is also the most Latin-flavoured original the band had written to that point: assorted hand percussion stands in for the kit through most of the song, and **the complete kit does not appear until the bridge** ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]). Two voices alternate between harmony and unison from phrase to phrase, which given the lyric reads as almost programmatic.

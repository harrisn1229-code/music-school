# Synthet - One Click Creative Automations!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kkGGj98D5Pc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kkGGj98D5Pc)

## Synopsis

A workflow tip built around FL's "Sequence" articulator tool: instead of hand-drawing simple fade/pitch automation shapes, create a volume automation clip on a sample, then run it through the Sequence tool to generate varied, non-obvious automation-curve placements automatically — usable on any automatable parameter (volume, filter cutoff, EQ bands), not just the sample it started on. Closes with the standard "cut the bass before the chorus, then bring it back" master-channel automation move as one concrete application.

## Techniques

- **Use the Sequence articulator to generate non-obvious automation placements** — create a quick volume automation clip on a sample (the automation button on the sample), then open its Articulator tools and run Sequence — a fast way to try out a range of creative automation-curve placements rather than manually drawing each variant by hand. Works with swing settings on the sequenced automation too. → [[Automation]]
- **Not limited to volume** — the same Sequence-generated automation approach applies to filters and EQ bands, not just volume fades; described specifically as "really cool on synths."
- **Standard structural move: cut the bass pre-chorus, then restore it** — automate the master channel (or the bass bus) to remove low end just before the chorus hits, then bring it back at the drop for a harder-hitting impact — cited as the standard automation move for this, worth knowing even outside the Sequence-tool trick.
- **Automate effect on/off states, not just knob values** — toggling an entire effect (or effector-plugin slot) on and off via automation was called out alongside volume/reverb/filter automation as a legitimate target.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — It Won't Be Long]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #10.*

**Form.** An album opener whose real interest is that **the form refuses to parse**. Three two-line couplets recur as `X Y X Z Y X Z Y X`, and depending on how you cluster them you get a refrain plus a bridge, a compound verse with a bridge, or a compound verse with a refrain — he lays out all three and does not fully commit. It reduces to the two-bridge model with one verse between, but only if you parse the second half of the verse as a **mini-refrain that also serves as the intro**. Two oddities survive: the verse is not doubled first time round, and its non-refrain half is **seven bars, one short of balanced**, because the last bar of the first phrase elides into the first bar of its repeat ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]). Intro/mini-refrain 8 bars as 2 + 2 + 4, starting on vi and converging on I; verse 15; bridge a square 8, harmonically open.

**Harmony.** `Refrain: vi (2) – I (2) – vi (2) – IV·♯ii°7 – I (2)` · `Verse first half: I – ♭VI – I ‖ I – ♭VI – I – I` · `Bridge: I – B+ – Bm – C♯7 (V/II) – IV – V – V/V – V`. **vi acts as a pseudo-dominant** to open the refrain, and **♭VI as a dominant substitute** in the verse, borrowed from the parallel minor ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]) — his nickname for it is the Buddy Holly chord. The bridge is genuinely open at the end because it exits through **V-of-V voiced as a ninth** ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]).

**Notable.** The rest is a study in **chords that are byproducts of voice leading rather than choices**. In refrain bar 6 the outer voices hold while the inner two move chromatically, producing a diminished seventh on ♯ii over a static bass — and it lands where the harmonic rhythm quickens, which is why it bites. In the bridge the bass descends chromatically under a held B and the augmented and minor chords falling out of that motion get **no Roman numerals** at all. The outro cadence uses a barbershop stream of chromatically descending dominant sevenths. The antiphonal "yeah"s are the trick worth studying: John is mixed forward and sings his on the offbeat against the others.

**Against this page.** Agrees on E major.

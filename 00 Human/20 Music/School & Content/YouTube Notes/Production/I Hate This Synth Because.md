One genuinely new, specific patching trick and one panel-reading gotcha are captured below.

## Techniques

- **ARP 2600 (3620 keyboard) split-voice trick with no patch cords**: take the cable from the keyboard's "upper voice" output jack and plug it directly into an oscillator's input — that oscillator becomes the upper-voice half of a split keyboard, freeing the other oscillator(s) to be the lower voice, entirely via a normal keyboard-routing jack rather than a dedicated split/dual mode.
- **A real panel-reading gotcha, confirming this note's "read signal flow, don't assume" rule**: on at least one synth in the collection, the filter TYPE selector (band-pass/high-pass/low-pass) is physically located in the VCA section of the panel, not the filter section — tracing backward from the output is what actually reveals which filter mode is active, since looking in the filter section itself won't show it. The same synth labels its three envelope generators A, C and E (skipping B and D), a numbering scheme tied to a shared modulation matrix rather than a literal "three envelopes in a row" — worth checking a panel's own labeling logic before assuming missing letters mean missing modules.

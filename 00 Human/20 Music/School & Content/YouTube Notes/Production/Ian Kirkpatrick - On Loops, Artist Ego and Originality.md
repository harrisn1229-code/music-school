# Ian Kirkpatrick — On Loops, Artist Ego and Originality

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AEmk1bKq21k)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AEmk1bKq21k)

## Synopsis

A short, honest monologue on **letting go of the pride/ego that blocks creativity** — using a Splice loop as-is, or reusing a favorite effect/loop/vocal-process you've "already done," or an artist dismissing their own signature move. His conclusion: the only person who knows you've repeated yourself is *you*, and that private pride can cost you a great idea.

## What's taught (philosophy)

- **Drop the "using a loop as-is is cheating" pride** — he used to feel that; realized it's "ridiculous" and that Splice loops are literally *for* that. Ask: "am I letting ego block a potential creative moment?" → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Ian Kirkpatrick - Using AutoTune to Craft Hit Songs|give me a reason to care]]
- **"I already did this" is a fake constraint** — refusing to reuse an effect/guitar loop/vocal process because you used it before, even though **no one else has heard those other songs** — "the only person who knows is me." Don't kill a great idea to protect your own sense of novelty. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **The real originality line** — there's a difference between not wanting to make *another Don't Start Now* (a known, public signature everyone would clock) and just being privately bored of your own habit. The first is worth avoiding; the second doesn't matter to anyone but you. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Jon Bellion example — artists dismiss their own gold** — Bellion said "I always do the same melodies," and Ian's reaction was "I love that thing you do all the time." Your signature move is what people love; don't apologize it out of the song. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

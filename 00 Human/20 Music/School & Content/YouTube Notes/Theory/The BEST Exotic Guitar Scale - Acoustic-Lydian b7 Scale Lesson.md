# The BEST Exotic Guitar Scale? - Acoustic/Lydian b7 Scale Lesson

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4UcHICzOiQU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4UcHICzOiQU)

## Synopsis

A practical, fretboard-first introduction to the acoustic scale (Lydian dominant / Lydian ♭7 / overtone scale — the 4th mode of melodic minor, matching [[Scale Modes Reference]]'s own Melodic Minor table row IV). Builds it as "major scale with two altered notes" rather than deriving it from melodic minor, and includes a concrete avoid-note warning for improvising over a dominant tonic.

## What's taught

- **Build order: start from the major scale, raise the 4th (Lydian), then also lower the 7th (Mixolydian's defining note).** Both changes stated individually first (C major → C Lydian via ♯4; C major → C Mixolydian via ♭7) before combining them — same "learn the one changed note" pedagogy used across BERNTH's whole modes series.
- **Result: a scale that's simultaneously Lydian (raised 4th) and Mixolydian (lowered 7th) — hence "Lydian dominant."** Two names given for context: acoustic scale, overtone scale (in addition to Lydian ♭7/Lydian dominant).
- **Tetrachord analysis as an alternate way to see the scale's identity:** first tetrachord (C-D-E-F♯) is Lydian's whole-whole-whole shape; second tetrachord (G-A-B♭-C) is a minor-scale-shaped whole-half-whole — the scale is literally two different tetrachord qualities stacked.
- **Concrete avoid-note warning for dominant-tonic improvising:** playing the natural major 7th (from habitually defaulting to Ionian/major) over a chord that's actually a dominant 7th (minor 7th in the harmony) creates an audible clash — explicitly named as a common source of "sometimes it sounds right, sometimes it sounds terrible" confusion when a player doesn't know which scale degree is the culprit.
- **Practical takeaway stated directly:** knowing exactly which two notes differ from the major scale (and why they clash or resolve against a dominant chord) prevents random, unexplainable-sounding mistakes when improvising over dominant-tonic harmony.

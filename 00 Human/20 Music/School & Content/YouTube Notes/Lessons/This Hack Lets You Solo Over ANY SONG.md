# This Hack Lets You Solo Over ANY SONG

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Iie0h9bilXA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Iie0h9bilXA)

## Synopsis

A leveled, one-chord-at-a-time introduction to chord-tone soloing, framed as a universal "solo over any song" method rather than a theory lesson. The overall shape (isolate one chord, add chords one at a time, mix chord tones with pentatonic scale, repeat in a new position) closely tracks material already routed from this batch; the one genuinely new addition is a passing-tone layer introduced at the final level.

## Techniques

- **The leveled sequence, worked over Cm–B–A–E then a second progression (Cm–E♭–B♭–A♭):** Level 1 — isolate just the first chord and play only its chord tones as it sounds, ignoring the rest of the progression (doubles as ear training, since you have to actively wait for and recognize the target chord). Level 2 — add the next chord's tones. Level 3 — play chord tones across the full progression, alternating choruses between pure chord-tone playing and the matching minor pentatonic scale. Level 4 — repeat the entire process in a different fretboard position, picking a pentatonic shape that's already "in the neighborhood" of the new position rather than an arbitrary one. Level 5 (second, faster progression) — repeat the chord-tone/pentatonic alternation, now adding **passing tones** between chord tones.
- **Passing tones don't have to be in key.** Because they're only held briefly in transit between two chord tones, passing tones can step outside the current key/scale without clashing — the brevity of the note is what makes an out-of-key passing tone read as connective motion rather than a wrong note.
- **Explicit patience framing**: don't expect this process to click in a single session — spend real weeks on it if needed, since guitar is a lifelong pursuit and there's no real cost to taking longer on foundational material.

# 5 INSANE SOLOS OVER CHORDS MY INSTAGRAM FOLLOWERS SENT ME

## Synopsis

Five follower-submitted chord progressions, ranked easiest-to-hardest and soloed over, with a harmonic-analysis commentary track running under the comedy. Two genuinely reusable ideas beyond specific progressions: a "should-be" analysis technique for spotting deliberately altered chord qualities (major swapped in for an expected minor/dominant degree), and a slow-tempo practice tip for subdivision counting.

## Techniques

- **"Should-be" analysis: compare a chord progression's written qualities against what strict diatonic function predicts, and treat every mismatch as a deliberate color choice, not an error.** Worked on the hardest submitted progression (Emaj9–Bmaj7–Amaj9–C♯maj9, mostly diatonic to E major): B should function as a dominant 7th (V), not a major 7 — voicing it major instead removes the dominant pull and reads as a **borrowed/parallel-major substitution**. C♯maj9 should be minor (vi of E), so voicing it major turns it into **the parallel major of the vi chord** — described as sounding deliberately "wrong" against what the ear expects from that scale degree, which is the point. Reusable checklist: for any chord in a progression, ask what quality the parent key's diatonic function predicts, then treat any mismatch as an intentional color/borrowing move worth naming rather than a mistake to "fix."
- **Root motion in fourths as a way to build harmonic momentum without functional (tonic/subdominant/dominant) pull, when voice-led carefully.** The hardest progression's root movement runs in fourths throughout — strong root motion by interval, but with chords that don't relate to each other diatonically, producing a modal (no strong pull toward a tonic) feel rather than a functional cadence. The progression was explicitly voice-led (not just root-planned) to keep it musical despite the lack of functional glue; also set in 5/4 for a "funky fusion" groove.
- **Practicing at slow tempos: count the underlying subdivision (8ths/16ths), not just the beat, or slow tempos become harder to lock in than fast ones.** Stated directly against the common assumption that faster = harder — a 50 BPM tempo is flagged as genuinely difficult to sit in the pocket with unless the subdivision is being actively counted underneath the beat.
- **1–4 and 1–4–5 vamps as intentional "easy" scaffolding**, confirming the practice value of minimal chord counts (2–3 chords) for isolating a scale/harmony tool without other complexity competing — same principle used to build each stage of [[Improvisation Difficulty Ladder — Pentatonic to Coltrane Changes]].

> **Q1.** **Cmaj7 – Am7 – Dm7 – G7 — to end a section by pointing back at the top**
> *Also accepted:* I – vi – ii – V
>
> **Q2.** **| Dm7 G7 | — just the ii–V, which is enough to point back at the I**
> The four-chord version compresses to two bars, one bar, or V alone on beats 3–4.
>
> **Q3.** **Compressing a good turnaround gives a good short one; expanding a bare ii–V gives you nothing**
> *Rubric (5):* gives the compress-not-expand reasoning (2) · gives two endings from V7, ♭II7, ending on the ii, or changing only the last bar between repeats (2) · says a loop ending on I feels closed and gets boring on repeat (1)
> The four-chord form carries decisions — which vi, whether the dominants are substituted, where the chromatic motion sits — and compressing keeps whichever of those survive. Starting from `Dm7 G7` there is nothing to keep. For the loop: ending on V7 or on ♭II7 gives the classic pull; ending on the ii is softer and very common in neo-soul, leaving the V implied so the loop never fully closes; and changing only the last bar between repeats buys two loops of material for one bar of writing.
>
> **Q4.** **I–vi–ii–V twice — a turnaround, looped**
> *Also accepted:* a looped turnaround · I vi ii V, twice
>
> **Q5.** **They move to the subdominant and back — the borrowed minor iv is the moment**
> The shape is I → I7 → IV → iv → I → V/ii → ii → V.
>
> **Q6.** **Because four bars of real harmonic movement answer them**
> Question and answer inside one eight-bar unit, which transfers straight to a verse.
>
> **Q7.** **III7 – VI7 – II7 – V7 — all dominants, each falling a fifth, and only the V7 diatonic**
> In B♭ that is D7 – G7 – C7 – F7, two bars each. D7, G7 and C7 each bring a note from outside B♭ major (F♯, B♮, E♮); F7 is the key's own V7.
>
> **Q8.** **Harmonic rhythm drops from two chords per bar to one per two bars — a 4× slowdown**
> Not a key change, not a tempo change, not new instrumentation.
>
> **Q9.** **The Montgomery-Ward bridge — I7 – IV7 – II7 – V7**
> Three of its four root moves are fifths; IV → II is the seam, and only the V7 is diatonic — I7, IV7 and II7 each bring one note from outside the key (in C: B♭, E♭ and F♯).
>
> **Q10.** **AABA and 16+16**
> *Also accepted:* AABA and two 16-bar halves
>
> **Q11.** **It must not resolve to I — it has to reach V so the second half has somewhere to launch from**
> The second half's ending is the tune's real cadence.
>
> **Q12.** **Form first, then named functional chunks rather than individual chords**
> *Rubric (5):* establishes the form and section boundaries before reading any chords (2) · names functional chunks — a 2-5 to six, IV→iv→I, V7/V, a turnaround — as the unit of memory (2) · says repeated sections only have to be learned once (1)
> Count the bars and find the section boundaries before looking at a single chord symbol, because the shape tells you how much of the chart is genuinely new. Then read the chords in groups: "a 2-5 to six" rather than "half-diminished, then dominant, then minor". Twenty-plus symbols reduce to six or seven things to hold. The payoff compounds — the same chunk turns up in the same structural slot across unrelated standards, so a chunk learned once is recognised everywhere, and a repeated A section costs nothing the second and third time.

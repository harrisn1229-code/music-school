# 28 Songs That Are Based On Classical Music

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qA-tkCRwNVM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qA-tkCRwNVM)

## Synopsis

A large catalog of pop/rock songs directly built on classical pieces, framed around the public-domain angle: most classical music is old enough to be copyright-free, which is explicitly why so much pop borrowing from it is legally uncomplicated (contrasted at the end against one case where the source material ISN'T public domain). Companion/prequel to [[14 Songs That 'Rip Off' Classical Music]].

## Techniques

- **A recurring, reusable transformation toolkit for turning a classical melody into a new pop song, extracted across many of the video's examples rather than any one case**: change the TEMPO (Billy Joel's "This Night" speeds up and swings Beethoven's Pathétique 2nd movement melody); change the RHYTHM while keeping the pitch content (same song); alter the NOTE COUNT of a figure (Lukas Graham's "Better Than You" turns Beethoven's 3-note Moonlight Sonata arpeggio into 4 notes); radically change TEMPO in the opposite direction, slow enough to obscure the source (Phil Collins' "A Groovy Kind of Love" slows Clementi's Sonatina No. 5 so much that speeding the pop version back up is what reveals the likeness); use the source as a stylistic/textural STARTING POINT rather than quoting its melody (Zeppelin's "Stairway to Heaven" intro imitates Bach's Bourrée's two-voice texture — melody-over-steady-bass — without using its actual melody, contrasted directly against Jethro Tull's "Bourée," which IS a fairly direct adaptation); transpose the SAME chord with one bass-note change (John Williams' Star Wars score reuses Holst's "Mars" chord, altering only the bass note from C to G).
- **A concrete example of instrumentation borrowed from a specific classical recording, not just melody/harmony**: "Penny Lane" (Beatles) — McCartney heard a piccolo trumpet played in a TV broadcast of Bach's Brandenburg Concerto No. 2 and asked George Martin to source the same instrument for the song's solo, an instrumentation choice traced to one specific viewing rather than a general classical influence.
- **The public-domain/copyright distinction that frames the whole video, stated as an explicit legal principle**: most classical music referenced here is old enough to be copyright-free, making direct melodic/harmonic borrowing legally uncomplicated — EXCEPT the video's closing example, Sia's "Breathe Me" vs. Philip Glass's "Metamorphosis One" (written ~30 years before this video, still under copyright), where the same kind of borrowing WOULD carry real legal exposure if proven, unlike every other example in the catalog. Practical takeaway: "based on a classical piece" only sidesteps copyright risk when the classical piece itself has actually entered the public domain — genuinely recent contemporary-classical work has no such exemption.

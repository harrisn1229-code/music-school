# Sequential Prophet VS

## What it is
Sequential's last synth before the company folded — and its most original: **vector synthesis**, four digital wavetable oscillators per voice mixed live with a joystick, through real Curtis analog filters. A commercial failure with an outsized afterlife: its DNA became the Korg Wavestation and Yamaha SY22 after Sequential's team dispersed.

## What makes the sound
- **Vector mixing**: four oscillators (A/B/C/D) sit at joystick corners; the patch *is* the movement between them, recordable as an envelope — evolving, morphing pads that breathe over 10+ seconds, unheard of in 1986.
- **Digital waveforms into analog Curtis filters/VCAs** — glassy, gritty wavetable edges rounded by real analog warmth; the hybrid recipe every "digital-analog" synth since has chased.
- 127 single-cycle waves (bells, formants, metallic partials) — inharmonic colors far outside the VCO vocabulary, with the filter keeping them musical.
- The classic register: huge shifting sci-fi/ambient pads and hollow bell-textures → [[BED Slot]], [[TEXTURE Slot]].

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Wavetable]] (Ableton — wavetable oscillators + filter; automate the wavetable position for the vector motion), [[Serum 2]] (morphing wavetables are its native language), [[Omnisphere]] (sampled VS waves are literally in its core library — the documented lineage).
- **Paid:** Arturia Prophet VS V, Groove Synthesis 3rd Wave (hardware spiritual successor).
- **Free:** Surge XT wavetable scenes with macro-morph assignments.

## Used on
- Genre/era: late-80s/90s film-score and ambient texture work, new-age and art-pop pads — a sound-designer's synth whose credits hide in cue sheets; genre-level per the rule. Its documented legacy is architectural: the Wavestation lineage and Omnisphere's wave library carry it forward.

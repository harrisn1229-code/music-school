> [!danger]- 🔒 Answer key — do not open until you have answered every question
> **Q1.** **I–V–vi–IV and ii–V–I**
>
> **Q2.** **Drill them as single fused gestures rather than as chords to identify one at a time**
> A ii–V is eventually heard as one chunk. Identifying chord by chord is the thing that doesn't survive real time.
>
> **Q3.** **Bass alone → chords back in → quality swap → position swap → transpose.**
> In full: play the bass notes alone and sing the degree numbers; add the chords back and name each quality out loud; swap one chord's quality; swap a chord's position; then transpose the whole thing and repeat.
> *Also accepted:* the five in order · any reasonable wording
>
> **Q4.** **The quality swap isolates quality; the position swap isolates scale degree**
> Change one chord's quality and hold everything else, or hold the quality and move the degree. Each swap changes exactly one variable so you can hear what that variable does on its own.
>
> **Q5.** **IV→iii (4→3) or vii°→I (7→1)**
> Those are the only two places a diatonic major key puts adjacent bass notes a semitone apart.
>
> **Q6.** **IV→iii — it's the only diatonic spot where a major chord drops to a minor chord a half step below**
> *Also accepted:* ♭VI→v is the minor-key equivalent
> The quality pair does the narrowing: vii°→I is diminished to major, which is a different sound entirely.
>
> **Q7.** **A secondary dominant of V (V/V, with its 3rd in the bass), or a ♯iv half-diminished (♯ivø7)**
> In C that is D7/F♯ or F♯ A C E — ♯4 is V/V's 3rd and ♯ivø7's root. A iiø7 (D F A♭ C in C) contains no ♯4 at all. Either way it's a signal to stop hunting and test two candidates.
>
> **Q8.** **Authentic: full arrival · plagal: no pull · half: suspended · deceptive: surprise**
> Authentic is the door closing. Plagal is already-home rather than coming-home. Half is a question left open, the sentence stopping mid-breath. Deceptive is surprise plus incompleteness.
>
> **Q9.** **The half cadence never promised resolution; the deceptive one promised it and went elsewhere**
> A half cadence stops on the dominant, so the ear is left waiting. A deceptive cadence sets up the pull of an authentic cadence and then lands somewhere that isn't home — which is why the feeling includes surprise, not just incompleteness.
>
> **Q10.** **Immediate recognition → deduction → brute force. Then label it and drill it.**
> Drilling is what moves it permanently into the first category.
> *Rubric (5):* gives all three modes in the right order (2) · describes what deduction and brute force each involve (1) · says you label and drill whatever you worked out (1) · says the point is to move it permanently into immediate recognition (1)
> Immediate recognition is the goal state: you know it on hearing it. Deduction narrows with clues — bass motion, key, what came before — then tests candidates. Brute force takes the outer notes first and works inward, and it's the fallback for a voicing you've never met. The step people skip is the one after: whatever you worked out the slow way gets named and added to the drill, so the next encounter is recognition instead of deduction. Without that, the same chord costs full price every time.

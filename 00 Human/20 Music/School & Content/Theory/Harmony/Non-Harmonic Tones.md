# Non-Harmonic Tones

Part of [[Music Theory]]. A **non-harmonic tone (NHT)** is a note that doesn't belong to the chord sounding underneath it — the source of melodic "spice" and forward motion inside otherwise-static harmony. Classified by how it's **approached** and **resolved** (step, leap, or common tone).

## The 9 types

| Name | Abbr. | Approach | Resolution | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Passing tone | PT | step | step (same direction) | fills the gap between two chord tones a 3rd apart |
| Neighboring tone | NT | step | step (returns to origin) | steps away and immediately back |
| Appoggiatura | APP | leap | step (opposite direction) | leap-then-step-back — the classic "leaning" ornament |
| Escape tone | ET | step | leap (opposite direction) | step-then-leap-away |
| Changing tones | CT | any | step | a *pair* of NHTs flanking the resolution note on either side |
| Anticipation | ANT | any | common tone | a chord tone from the *next* chord, sounded early |
| Suspension | SUS | common tone | step (down) | held over from the previous chord, resolves **down** |
| Retardation | RET | common tone | step (up) | held over from the previous chord, resolves **up** |
| Pedal tone | PED | common tone | common tone | a chord tone that becomes non-harmonic under moving harmony above it, then becomes a chord tone again |

## Suspension labeling
Suspensions are identified by two stacked intervals-above-the-bass: **the note of suspension, then the note of resolution**. The standard above-the-bass suspensions are **7-6**, **4-3**, and **9-8** (which can also appear as 2-3 an octave lower, sometimes called 9-10). The exception is the **2-3 (bass) suspension**, where the suspended note IS the bass — measured instead against whichever upper voice sits a 2nd above it.

**Planning a suspension**: decide which chord tone the suspension needs to be *present* in the approach chord before writing it:
- **9-8** — resolves to an octave above the bass, so any chord works.
- **7-6** — resolves to a 6th above the bass, so the approach chord needs a 1st- or 2nd-inversion triad (root position lacks the 6th).
- **4-3 / 2-3** — resolve to a 3rd above the bass, so the approach chord needs anything *except* 2nd inversion.

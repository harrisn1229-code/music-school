# how jetsonmade makes bouncy/melodic beats for playboi carti & lil keed (from scratch)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OQZbvASWIQE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OQZbvASWIQE)

## Synopsis

loner reverse-engineers jetsonmade's approach to a bouncy, melodic "Uzi-style" trap beat built for Playboi Carti/Lil Keed territory — a sample-flip build (not an actual jetsonmade session) that walks through constructing a multi-layer melodic sample from scratch, then finishing it with a deliberately sparse drum pass in a new project. The sample itself stacks five distinct elements — a Kontakt mallet, a reverbed drum-kit percussion hit, an Analog Lab shaker/bowl texture, an inaudible-but-felt sub-fill layer, and a harsh high-attack bell — around a self-recorded, heavily processed melodic vocal that gets bounced, double-timed, chopped, and partially reversed for variation. The most valuable ideas aren't the individual sounds but the workflow moves: building a verse version of the sample by reversing and pitching the main mallet part down an octave (with vocals pitched down to match), structuring the whole flip like an actual song (hook + verse) so a producer buying or using it already knows how to build on it, and a genuinely counterintuitive drum decision — skip the kick entirely because the embedded 808's own attack already does that job, then literally pull the bass line back OUT of the finished sample bounce rather than programming a new one. The restraint philosophy ("the sample is doing most of the work... you don't want to go too crazy on the drums") is stated explicitly and ties directly into the genre observation that Uzi/Carti-style beats stay deliberately open rather than percussion-dense.

## Techniques

### Building the sample (5 melodic/textural layers)

- **Mallet lead — Kontakt instrument, EQ'd to cut the lows, then bounced to audio.** The foundational melodic element; already bounced by the time of recording, so its live chain isn't shown, but the low-cut EQ move is stated directly — clearing low end out of a mallet/pluck-type lead so it doesn't fight the [[BASS Slot]]/[[SUB Slot]] material underneath. → [[Kontakt 7]], [[EQ]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Percussion layer 1 — a "perk" (percussion one-shot) from a drum-kit pack, high AND low cut with EQ, then a lot of reverb added, purely for texture/character rather than rhythmic weight.** → [[EQ]], [[Reverb]] (effect), [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Percussion layer 2 — Analog Lab, a combined shaker + bowl sound, found by clicking through presets until one had "a really cool cool sound, a cool texture."** A preset-audition-by-ear workflow rather than a targeted search. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **A felt-not-heard sub-fill layer added underneath the mallet purely to fill the low and low-mid frequencies — "you can hardly even hear it but it just fills up the lower frequencies and the mid frequencies."** A textbook [[SUB Slot]]/[[BASS Slot]] weight layer: present in the mix for physical fullness, not for conscious audibility. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[SUB Slot]], [[BASS Slot]]
- **Bell accent — a second, higher-frequency element added on top, deliberately harsher and with more attack than the mallet, purely for extra bite/attack character.** → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Stab-bass layer — a specific, frequently-reused preset (auto-captioned "knits her base"/"knitter bass," exact plugin/vendor not identifiable from the caption), release brought down for a short, stabby (not sustained) character.** *(low confidence on the preset/plugin name — auto-caption garble; the release-shortening move itself is clear)* → [[Bass Design]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]

### The vocal chop (recorded, processed, bounced, double-timed, chopped)

- **Two separate self-recorded melodic vocal takes, each run through its own chain but sharing a base structure: [[Little AlterBoy]] → heavy EQ → heavy reverb.** Stated reason for the heavy EQ specifically: "obviously I can't sing, so it's drowning out the bad frequencies and the stuff you don't want" — EQ used defensively, to mask pitch/tone imperfections in an untrained vocal performance rather than for tonal shaping. → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[EQ]], [[Reverb]] (effect), [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Take 2 adds a tremolo control panning the vocal side-to-side, plus a bit of delay, as its one distinguishing move against take 1.** Consistent with the vault's recurring "give each vocal layer its own distinguishing chain" pattern (see [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]) — the base AlterBoy/EQ/reverb chain is shared, the tremolo+delay is what makes this take different. → [[Ghz Trem Control 3]], [[Modulation]], [[Delay]] (effect)
- **Bounce both vocal takes to a single audio stem, then double-time it (stretch to twice the speed) and chop it to fit the other elements — a fast way to generate a warped, faster-feeling vocal texture from a slow original performance.** → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Reverse just the last two small chopped parts of the vocal stem, purely for a variation/different feel at the very end of the phrase.** A cheap, localized variation move rather than reversing the whole chop. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

### Verse version (reverse + pitch-down)

- **Reverse and chop the main mallet part to match the original rhythmic pattern, then pitch the whole thing down one octave — used specifically to signal the verse section (lower energy/different feel vs. the hook), not as a tone choice.** Vocals are pitched down to match for the same section, so the hook and verse share the same sonic identity but read as clearly different energy levels. → [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Little AlterBoy]]
- **A declick/degrade pass specifically to remove pops introduced by heavy audio chopping** ("I always go to this generic [tool] to stop the pops... when you've chopped it a lot, for some reason the audio adds clicks"). *(FL Studio-specific device name not reliably identifiable from the caption — likely garbled; general lesson kept, no owned-tool guess made)* → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

### Structuring the flip for handoff

- **Structure a sample intended for flipping/selling like an actual song — a hook section and a verse section — rather than a flat loop.** "It's always best to structure it how you would with a beat... it gives the producer who's flipping it, they know how to structure it, it's already done for them, so it just saves them a job." A pure workflow/etiquette tip for anyone building or selling sample flips. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

### Drums (new project, sample dragged in)

- **Deliberately minimal drum pass: clap + a basic hi-hat with a few rolls, velocity varied on the hits, plus some low notes following the chords' root notes and "bounce."** Stated philosophy: "the sample's doing most of the work in terms of the bounce, so you don't want to go too crazy on the drums... there's no point trying to do some extra stuff you don't need to do." → [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Genre-convention restraint: no extra percussion layered in, explicitly because "Uzi's beats are normally very open."** A direct style-target observation (Playboi Carti/Lil Uzi Vert/Lil Keed plugg-adjacent production) driving the arrangement decision, not a general rule. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **No separate kick added at all — the embedded 808/bass already has a heavy attack, and "finding a kick that matches over the top of that is quite difficult," so the decision is to just leave it; the 808 alone "already hits."** → [[Bass Design]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]], [[Kick Design]]
- **Extract the bass/808 that's already baked into the finished, bounced sample instead of programming a new one: right-click the sample audio, use a "make unique"-then-extract move to isolate the 808 element, then drag that isolated bass across the arrangement as the beat's bassline.** Reuses the sample's own low end rather than building a fresh 808 pattern from scratch. *(FL Studio's audio-isolation feature specifically — owned-lane equivalent for pulling a bass line out of a finished bounce would be Melodyne-based note extraction, see [[Plugin Wishlist]], or manual resampling/filtering)* → [[Bass Design]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

# 4 Songs with Confusing Intros

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oIHeDUiQW-g)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oIHeDUiQW-g)

## Synopsis

A second follow-up "confusing intros" video (companion to the earlier one and to "6 Songs with Confusing Intros") — four more songs whose intros mislead the ear about where the downbeat falls, including one case the host explicitly flags as subjective rather than universally heard as confusing.

## Techniques

- **Early-start illusion, same mechanism as the earlier videos**: "Fashion" (David Bowie) and "Veridis Quo" (Daft Punk) both start on the "and of 4" — Veridis Quo stretches the ambiguity unusually long, since the kick drum doesn't fade in to reveal the true pulse until roughly 45 seconds in.
- **Subjective backbeat placement — a genuinely divisive case, not a universal illusion.** "Car Wash" (Rose Royce): some listeners hear the opening handclaps as landing on beats 1 and 3 (the "wrong" backbeat placement — a common musicians'-joke mistake), others correctly hear them on 2 and 4. The host is explicit that this one isn't purely objective: with only a clap for a long stretch before full instrumentation enters, there's real, extended ambiguity about which beat is which, so different listeners can land on different correct-sounding interpretations.
- **Confusion sustained across an entire verse before resolving.** "Beetlebum" (Blur) has an unusually long confusing intro — the muted guitar chugs invite counting the first chug as the downbeat, but it's actually the slide note that's the true downbeat; the mismatch isn't cleared up until the drums finally enter at the end of verse one, so the "wrong" pulse persists through the whole intro AND first verse, not just a few bars.

# Fretting-Hand Coordination 5-Level Daily Drill

BassBuzz's third five-level daily drill in the vault (see [[Slap Bass 5-Level Daily Drill]] for the first, a right-hand/slap specialty, and [[One-Finger-Per-Fret 5-Level Daily Drill]] for the second, a fretting-hand warm-up built on a five-fret blues riff). This one is the channel's own foundational One Finger Per Fret fretting-hand exercise — one riff, five progressive levels, each adding exactly one new goal on top of the same core pattern, with the riff itself changing shape only twice (once at level 4, once at level 5). Where the second drill's goal stack ends in root-fifth-octave theory and moving roots, this one stays purely mechanical throughout: fret placement → progressive add/subtract fingering → flying-fingers range reduction → directional string-crossing lifts → minimum fretting pressure. Tempo target throughout is a flat **half notes at 120 BPM (beginner) / quarter notes at 120 BPM (advanced)** — unlike the other two drills, there's no BPM ladder to climb; the goal is holding that one tempo cleanly while stacking on more simultaneous technique goals level by level.

## Level 1 — Basic pattern and fret placement

**The riff (G string, frets 9-12):** index-middle-ring-pinky ascending (9-10-11-12), then pinky-ring-middle-index descending, same position. Plucking hand: alternate index-middle throughout — this drill is fretting-hand-only.

**The technique point:** aim finger placement at the **end of the fret**, right behind the fret wire, not mid-fret-space — that's what produces a clean note with the minimum pressure required. A rattling/buzzing note usually means fretting too far back in the fret space, not too little pressure; correct placement first, and only add a bit more pressure if the rattle persists after that.

**Start absurdly slow** — no tempo at all — before adding any rhythm. Once comfortable, bring in a drum-loop app (BassBuzz uses Drum Beats Plus/iPhone or Loops/Android) and find a tempo that's your genuine edge: mostly clean, with occasional slips, not sloppy the whole way through.

**The core method — bite-sizing:** the single biggest named mistake is trying to perform the whole exercise start-to-finish before any individual piece is solid. Fix: isolate the exact failing transition (e.g. just ring-pinky, ring-pinky on a loop) until it's consistently clean, *then* reintegrate it into the full pass. This is the same **additive/chunking** method documented in [[Interleaved Practice, Chunking, and Deliberate-Practice Drills#The additive approach (chunking)|The additive approach (chunking)]] and used identically in [[One-Finger-Per-Fret 5-Level Daily Drill]]'s level 2 — build the exercise from solid sub-pieces, don't drill the whole thing hoping the weak spot fixes itself.

*Cross-link:* this level's fret-end-placement/rattle fix is the same mechanism as [[Fretting-Hand Strength, Stretch, Stamina, and Finger Control#Exercise 1 — Strength builder (rattle elimination)|Exercise 1 — Strength builder]] on a different note, from a different BassBuzz video — two independent sources agreeing on the same fix.

## Level 2 — Add fingers ascending, subtract descending

Same riff. New goal: **ascending**, add each finger without lifting the ones already down — index stays as middle lands, index+middle stay as ring lands, index+middle+ring stay as pinky lands, so the hand progressively *closes* onto the position. **Descending**, the reverse — release fingers one at a time (pinky, then ring, then middle) instead of lifting-and-pressing simultaneously.

**Why it helps:** each transition becomes a single motion (add one finger, or subtract one finger) instead of a simultaneous lift-one/press-one, which halves the coordination demand once it's automatic.

**Tempo gate to move to level 3:** half notes at 120 BPM (beginner, one note every two beats) or quarter notes at 120 BPM (advanced). Explicitly normal to spend a month or two stuck here before passing the gate — the video is direct that "a lot of gigging bass players can't actually do level two all that well."

*Cross-link:* this is the identical ascending-add/descending-subtract mechanic documented (from a different specific riff) in [[One-Finger-Per-Fret 5-Level Daily Drill#Level 2 — Fix flying fingers|One-Finger-Per-Fret 5-Level Daily Drill's own level 2]] — two BassBuzz drills teaching the exact same fix on two different patterns.

## Level 3 — Reduce flying-fingers range of motion

Same riff, same fingerings. New goal: keep released fingers **close to the neck** instead of springing away from it ("flying fingers"). Named payoff: less wasted motion means both energy savings and a shorter trip back to the next note — a direct speed lever, not just a tidiness cue.

**Isolation ladder if the pinky won't cooperate (the most common sticking point):** isolate down to just the pinky first — hold the other three fingers down, press/lift only the pinky, working the range of motion down as small as it'll go. Once that's under control, the *ring* finger's release will often start dragging the pinky along with it — isolate that specific pair next (ring-pinky, ring-pinky, or ring-middle, ring-middle) before reintegrating into the full riff.

**Progress-tracking tip:** once tempo starts moving, log it on paper — date, exercise name, BPM reached, and a one-line felt-sense note. Visible proof of improvement for the days practice feels stuck.

Tempo gate: same half-notes/quarter-notes-at-120 target as level 2, now with flying fingers under control too.

*Cross-link:* [[Fretting-Hand Strength, Stretch, Stamina, and Finger Control#Exercise 4 — Finger controller (flying-fingers fix)|Exercise 4 — Finger controller]] is a third, differently-shaped fix for the same failure mode (an isolated three-bar transition drill rather than this level's whole-riff self-check).

## Level 4 — String-crossing with directional lift

**The riff changes shape.** D string fret 9, index-middle-ring-pinky, then shift to the G string, index-middle-ring-pinky; reverse coming back — G string pinky-ring-middle-index, then D string pinky-ring-middle-index.

**The level-4 goal:** lift each finger *in the direction of the string it's next needed on* — not just off the fretboard. **Ascending:** the instant the pinky's D-string note plays, the index finger should already be moving toward the G string so the rest of the hand can follow it into position. **Descending (the trickier direction):** after the pinky's D-string note, the pinky's release should aim back *toward* the D string — where it's needed two notes later — rather than lifting straight up; the same logic applies finger-by-finger through ring and middle. Once the index lands the 9th-fret D-string note, the pinky should already be arriving over the 12th fret of the G string, set behind the index and ready.

Named explicitly as a level many gigging professional bassists never fully nail — worth training regardless, both for reduced physical effort over a full set and as groundwork for speed later. Tempo gate: same 120 BPM target, now judged against all four accumulated level goals simultaneously (fret placement, add/subtract fingering, minimized flying fingers, *and* directional lifts).

*Cross-link:* this is a more specific, string-crossing-aware version of [[Playing Fast on Bass — Efficient Technique, Setup, and Bridge Plucking#Lever 1 — relaxed, efficient fretting-hand technique|Lever 1 — relaxed, efficient fretting-hand technique]]'s "minimize fretting-hand travel" principle — that note names the general goal, this level names the specific *direction* to lift in.

## Level 5 — Full chromatic scale + minimum-pressure "system reset"

**The riff:** a full 12-note, one-octave chromatic scale, G to G, across three strings. Ascending: A string fret 10 (index) through fret 12 (pinky, index-middle-ring-pinky), shift to D string fret 9 through fret 12 (index-middle-ring-pinky), shift to G string fret 8 through fret 12 (index-middle-ring-pinky). Descending: pinky-ring-middle-index back down through the same three strings in reverse (G string 12→9, D string 13 down through the fingering, A string 14 down to the starting fret) — more string-crossing and more vertical shifts than level 4.

**The level-5 goal — minimum pressure via a "system reset":** deliberately relax the fretting hand to **zero pressure** first (fingers touch the string but produce no sound), then add pressure back in very small increments until the note just goes clean. That minimum-clean-pressure point is usually noticeably lighter than the pressure a player habitually uses without checking. Hold the new lighter pressure for the whole exercise, and periodically re-check that grip hasn't crept back up under fatigue or focus elsewhere ("gripping the neck like you're trying to murder it"). **The reset is demonstrated on all three riff versions** — levels 1-3's G-string pattern, level 4's two-string crossing, and level 5's full chromatic run — it's a technique to reapply at any level, not a level-5-only trick.

Tempo gate: same half-notes/quarter-notes-at-120 target, now held across all five accumulated goals at once — fret placement, add/subtract fingering, minimized flying fingers, directional lifts, *and* minimum pressure.

*Cross-link:* this is a second, independent BassBuzz demonstration of the **minimum-viable-pressure (MVP)** technique documented on [[Goal-Tiered Practice and the Self-Taught Bass Commandments#Commandment 5 — Avoid common self-taught pitfalls (quickfire list)|Commandment 5's pitfall table]] (sourced there from a different video) — same zero-then-gradual-add mechanism, same "no harder than needed" endpoint.

## Beyond level 5

Once level 5 is clean at target tempo, go back to level 3 (the last version before string-crossing was introduced) and push the BPM higher — quarter notes at 120, or sixteenth notes, given as concrete advanced targets. All five level *goals* generalize to any other bass finger exercise: the specific riff is disposable, the transferable skill is the goal stack itself (end-of-fret placement → progressive add/subtract fingering → minimized flying-fingers range → directional string-crossing lifts → minimum pressure) plus the bite-sizing method for isolating whatever transition is failing at any given level.

## How to run it

- **Don't skip levels** — each one adds exactly one new layer onto the same underlying riff (mechanically, not thematically, unlike the theory-layered [[One-Finger-Per-Fret 5-Level Daily Drill]]), same non-skippable-levels logic as both sibling drills in this folder.
- The tempo target doesn't climb level to level here (unlike the other two bass drills) — what climbs is the number of simultaneous goals you're expected to hold at that one tempo.
- Bite-size relentlessly: the video's own framing is that the exercise's value comes from finding weaknesses precisely enough to isolate and fix them, not from grinding full reps.

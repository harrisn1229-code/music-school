# CHD-C08 — Alteration proper: the dominant's movable tones

**Q.** What does "altered" actually mean, which tones can be altered, and what is the one rule about the melody?

> [!question]- Answer
> An alteration is a chord tone **shifted a half step**, not a note added or removed. It is a dominant-family device, and the movable tones are the **5th and the 9th** (sometimes the 11th): **♭5, ♯5, ♭9, ♯9, ♯11**. The rule: **do not alter a note the melody is sitting on.**

## Why it matters

This is the third of the three colour operations and the one the other two get confused with — sus removes, add adds, altered *moves*. Alterations are free substitutions for any dominant 7th, especially the V7 going to I: more tension in, stronger resolution out. Worth knowing that the symbol names a scale as much as a note stack, which is why **G7♭9 and G7♭13 are interchangeable as written symbols**, and why 7♯11 and 7♭5 share one physical voicing — the spelling only changes which note is implied absent.

## Drill

Take a V7 in a loop and add one alteration at a time — ♭9, then ♯9, then ♭5, then ♯5 — and hear how each changes the resolution. Then play the melody over it and drop whichever alteration collides.

# You've Got To Hide Your Love Away — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** a cross between the two-bridge pop model and the folk verse/refrain ballad — a central unit of two verses plus a refrain, twice, with a scanty intro and, unusually, a closing **instrumental verse that ends the song** rather than a solo placed mid-song. Almost every section length is irregular ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]): the primary verse is **18 bars** as 4+4+4+6, the verse′ is **20** as 4+4+4+8, and the refrain is **12 bars, AA as 6 + 6**. All four sung verses have different lyrics, which pushes it toward ballad rather than pop.

Four chords — no modulation anywhere. Pollack uses it as his set piece on **♭VII** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]), a Beatles habit that suddenly explodes on this album, and calls the style **"almost modal"** rather than modal because the V chord stays major where a pure modal reading would want a minor v. The spicy by-product is a repeated **cross relation between the major third inside V and the flat seventh inside ♭VII** — substitute IV for ♭VII wherever V precedes it and the clash disappears, along with everything interesting about the song. The instrumental verse closes with a **double plagal cadence** ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]).

This is the first ternary meter in the series, and every section starts squarely on the downbeat, which suits the flattened mood. The contrast between sections is nearly all in **range** ([[MEL-C05 — Register and intensity]]), and the percussion accumulates section by section and never resets.

# Roland CR-78

## What it is
The CompuRhythm CR-78 — the first microprocessor-controlled programmable rhythm box, wrapped in wood like living-room furniture because that's what rhythm boxes were: home-organ accompaniment. Musicians hijacked it, and its soft analog ticks and bumps became one of the most atmospheric drum sounds on record.

## What makes the sound
- **All-analog voices from the preset-rhythm-box lineage**: bridged-T resonant circuits for kick/bongos/toms, filtered noise bursts for snare/hats/cymbal, plus signature **metallic beat/tambourine ticks** — everything is small, round, and polite. It *whispers* where an 808 booms.
- **Preset latin/ballroom rhythms** (rhumba, bossa, waltz…) that could be combined and, crucially, **user-programmed** (4 memories via the WS-1 programmer) — the patterns themselves are part of the sound; "In the Air Tonight" is essentially a CR-78 preset feel.
- Mute/fade and accent controls let it breathe dynamically; the lack of low-end weight makes it sit *behind* a mix like a texture → almost a [[TEXTURE Slot]] instrument rather than a [[TRANSIENT Slot]] one.
- Warm noise floor and gentle envelopes — it reads "intimate, nocturnal" in almost any arrangement.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Impulse]] with CR-78 samples; [[Cassette]] or [[RC-20 Retro Color]] to deepen the vintage haze; [[Echo]] dotted-eighth for the classic drift.
- **Paid:** Roland Cloud CR-78 (official), UVI Beat Box Anthology 2, Samples From Mars.
- **Free:** CR-78 sample sets are in every free vintage-machine archive.

## Used on
- Phil Collins — "In the Air Tonight" (1981): the CR-78 carries the entire first three minutes before the drum fill.
- Blondie — "Heart of Glass" (1978): the CR-78 loop threaded under the disco kit.
- Ultravox — "Vienna" (1980): the sparse, reverbed CR-78 heartbeat.
- Genre/era: late-70s/early-80s art pop, new wave, ballad atmospheres; modern indie/bedroom tracks reach for it as a "quiet drama" kit ([[Bedroom Pop - Indie]]).

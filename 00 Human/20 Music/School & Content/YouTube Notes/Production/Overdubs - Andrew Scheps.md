# Overdubs - Andrew Scheps

## Synopsis

A short Mix with the Masters session walkthrough with Andrew Scheps (Red Hot Chili Peppers, Metallica, Adele) capturing two overdubs before handing a song off to another engineer to mix. The first is a quick, almost throwaway experiment: reamping a bass DI through a guitar fuzz/synth pedal just to see if it works. The second and more developed piece is a live drum overdub built specifically to capture room/character rather than clean hits — Scheps busses the kit's existing close mics into a keyboard amp (a flat-response driver, not a guitar amp meant to color the sound) and then re-mics that amp's room with a single distant ribbon mic, so every mic that's "live" on the session is only feeding the amp, and the only signal that actually gets recorded is the one room capture. He directs the drummer through a build (simple, slow hits at the start of the take, progressively busier toward the end) rather than a fixed part, then cleans the resulting crushed, ambient track up with light EQ and a gate before sending it off — explicit that the gate's job here is shaping the release/decay of an already-distorted room signal, not just floor cleanup.

## Techniques

- **Bass DI reamped through a fuzz/synth pedal (Electro-Harmonix Micro Synth) purely as an experiment — "could be a terrible idea but it might be a really good idea."** No settings given (by-ear pedal knobs, not automated), but the workflow is explicit: DI signal out, into the pedal's input, printed as its own overdub layer rather than replacing the clean bass. → [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[Saturation and Distortion]] (effect), [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **A full-kit "lively amp" room overdub: bus the kit's existing close mics (kick, snare, both toms, overhead, and a second front-of-kit mic) together, feed that bus into a flat-response keyboard amp to physically "live" the room, then capture only that amp's room sound with one distant mic (an RCA KU3 ribbon, positioned high).** None of the close mics are actually recorded in this pass — they exist only to drive the amp. This is a deliberate reamp-the-whole-kit-through-a-speaker move for a distressed/ambient drum texture, distinct from close-mic room-mic technique (which mics the room directly rather than reamping through a speaker first). → [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Drum Mic Technique]]
- **Direct the overdub as a dynamic build, not a fixed part: big, simple hits or slow fills (quarter-note triplets) at the start, progressively busier toward the end of the take.** Explicit performance instruction to the drummer for an outro overdub — the arrangement arc is played live rather than programmed/edited in afterward. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **A gate used specifically to shape the RELEASE/decay of an already-crushed, distorted room signal — not floor cleanup.** "I'm going to use a gate to shape how the release is because I think it's pretty important to make sure that that's right, because it could go a lot of different ways." Light EQ was already applied on the way in; the gate is the deliberate tone-shaping tool on top of that, controlling how long the distorted room tail is allowed to ring before the next hit. → [[Gate]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]

# If I Fell — The Beatles

> Lennon's complex intro modulates from a different key into the main verse. Close two-part harmonies between Lennon and McCartney throughout. One of the most harmonically sophisticated early Beatles songs.

## Chords
**Key:** D major (the intro floats in from E♭ minor before settling)

**Intro:** E♭m – D – D♭ – B♭m – E♭m – D – Em – A
→ a chromatic descent that resolves onto **ii – V (Em – A)** of D.

**Verse:** D – Em – F♯m – Fdim7 – Em – A
→ **I – ii – iii – ♭iii° – ii – V** (the **Fdim7** is a chromatic passing chord between F♯m and Em)

**Bridge ("…cause I couldn't stand the pain"):** D9 – G – Gm – D – A7
→ **I – IV – iv – I – V7** (the **Gm** borrowed minor-iv is the heartbreak chord)

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — If I Fell]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #50.*

**Form.** **The intro is in a different key from the song** — eight bars in D♭ major, during which a D major chord passes by looking incidental, and on the repeat that same D chord is reinterpreted as I of the actual home key. He rates the pivot among their cleverest and most daring. The other oddity is that there is **no discrete bridge or refrain at all**: contrast comes from an extension that grows out of the middle two verses. Intro 8 bars, two parallel phrases, fully developed as its own section with material used nowhere else and its own backing texture. Verse (original) 10 bars — two parallel 4-bar phrases plus a 2-bar connector into the next verse; verse + extension keeps the first 8 and substitutes an asymmetric **7-bar** extension for the connector.

**Harmony.** `Intro: [D♭] ii – ♭II – I – vi · ii – ♭II – [D] I – ii – V` · `Verse: I – ii – iii – ♭iii° – ii7 – V (×2) · I – iv – V` · `Extension: I → V/IV (2 bars) – IV – iv (2) – I – V7`. You assume the opening E♭ minor is i of the home key; the free-fall through D and down to D♭ leaves you genuinely lost for a couple of bars, and only the return to E♭ minor in bar 5 resets it as ii of D♭ ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]). After an opening that disorienting, the rest of the song never modulates at all. Stepwise motion is the unifying idea and runs in the **harmony** as much as the tune; the bittersweetness comes from the **minor iv**, which enters quietly in the verse connector and then recurs everywhere ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The ♭iii° is not functional — it is the incidental product of three lines moving between the chords on either side ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]).

**Notable.** Both sung verse phrases, the connector *and* the extension all end on V, which is how a ballad with no bridge keeps circulating. John harmonises **below** Paul in genuine two-part counterpoint — no George vocal at all — and the tell is the **open fourths and fifths** dropped in where you would expect thirds.

**Against this page.** Agrees on D major. A refinement: this page says the intro floats in from E♭ minor; **Pollack reads the intro as being in D♭ major, with that E♭ minor as its ii**.

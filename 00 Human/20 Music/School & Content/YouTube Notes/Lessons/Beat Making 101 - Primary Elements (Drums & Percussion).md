## Techniques

- **Standard kit vocabulary:** kick (foot, low thud, anchors the beat), snare (usually left hand, lands on a consistent beat), hi-hat (metallic, keeps the rhythm going, typically constant), toms (pitched, higher up the kit, used mainly in fills), cymbals — crash (splashy accent, typically at phrase starts/big impacts) vs. ride (a softer cymbal that can substitute for the hi-hat's timekeeping role).
- **Minimum viable beat = kick + snare + a timekeeping element** (hi-hat or a substitute like a ride) — toms and other cymbals are secondary, mainly for fills/accents.
- **Percussion is a distinct layer from the kit** — non-kit rhythmic textures (tambourine-type sounds in the worked example) that fill gaps and add subdivision texture the core kit isn't covering, rather than replacing any kit element.
- **Ear-training exercise: isolate and identify kick/snare/hi-hat/percussion by listening critically to real songs**, not just for enjoyment — the same "train your ear" advice already given at more depth (with a concrete practice method) in [[Rhythm and Groove]]'s "pretend to be a beginner drummer" bullet (from [[Pattern Creation - Realistic Drum Programming - The Ultimate Guide (Part 2)|Pattern Creation Part 2]]).

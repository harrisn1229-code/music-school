# Think Different with Chord Substitutions

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8TMOAiaFJp0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8TMOAiaFJp0)

## Synopsis

Short, dense guitar lesson from Jack Ruch on soloing/comping over a minor i–iv vamp (D minor to G minor — "the beginning of a minor blues") using two stackable chord-substitution devices instead of just running the minor scale. First: sub each minor chord for its **relative major**, played as a major 7 arpeggio (F major 7 over D minor, B♭ major 7 over G minor) — same notes, brighter overlay sound. Second: insert the **secondary dominant** of the chord you're heading to as a transition chord (D7 → G minor, A7 → D minor) — the dominant-five of the target, subbed in right before it lands. The lesson's real payoff is combining the two: play the relative-major arpeggio over the minor chord, then walk through the secondary dominant, then resolve onto a chord tone of the next chord — a full arpeggio-based line-connecting system rather than two isolated tricks. A third layer — coloring the secondary dominant with the **harmonic minor scale of the chord it resolves to** (G harmonic minor over the D7 approaching G minor; D harmonic minor over the A7 approaching D minor) — adds tension before the resolution. ## What's taught

- **Relative-major substitution over a minor chord, played as arpeggios** — every minor chord has a relative major built a minor 3rd above its root (same key signature, same notes): D minor's relative major is F major, G minor's is B♭ major. Play major-7 chord voicings/arpeggios (F maj7, B♭ maj7) *over* the minor chord instead of running the minor scale — same harmonic content, brighter/more colorful overlay sound. → [[Chord Substitution]], [[Keys]] (relative major/minor already documented there as a key relationship; this is the applied-improvisation use of it)
- **Secondary-dominant approach chords to transition between the two vamp chords** — going D minor → G minor, insert a D7 (the V7 of G minor) as a passing/transition chord right before landing on G minor; coming back G minor → D minor, insert an A7 (the V7 of D minor) the same way. Framed simply as "the dominant five chord of wherever you're heading" — i.e. a secondary dominant used as a melodic/arpeggio target, not just a harmonized chord change. → [[Secondary Dominants]]
- **Connecting the two devices into one line-building system** — over D minor: play the F major 7 arpeggio, find a way to transition into D7 (the secondary dominant), then resolve onto a chord tone of the upcoming G minor. Over G minor: B♭ major 7 arpeggio → A7 → resolve onto a D minor chord tone. This sequencing (relative-major arpeggio → secondary dominant → resolve on a chord tone of the next chord) is the actual technique — the two substitutions aren't meant to be used in isolation. → [[Chord Substitution]], [[Secondary Dominants]]
- **Layering the harmonic minor scale onto the secondary dominant for tension** — once the arpeggio shapes are comfortable, add color tones from the harmonic minor scale *of the chord being approached*: use G harmonic minor over the D7 (which is heading to G minor) to build tension before resolving; use D harmonic minor over the A7 (heading to D minor) the same way. The harmonic minor's raised 7th over the dominant chord gives the extra "pull" toward the target. *(General principle, stated qualitatively — no exact scale degrees or fingerings called out in the transcript beyond naming the two scales.)* → [[Scale Modes Reference]] § Harmonic Minor
- **Applied over a minor-blues-style i–iv vamp** — the whole lesson frames these substitutions as a way to "break out of tendencies/habits" when soloing over a simple, repetitive minor progression (D minor / G minor, i.e. i–iv in D minor) rather than defaulting to running the D natural minor (Aeolian) scale start to finish. → [[Blues Form and Harmony]] § Minor blues
- **Three combined lick examples** are demonstrated over a backing track (tabs/backing track gated on the creator's Patreon, not available in the transcript) — no notated pitches to extract beyond what's described above.

# Ian Kirkpatrick — New Rules Plucks & Synths Layers

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UR4hLfw2FsE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UR4hLfw2FsE)

## Synopsis

Ian opens the New Rules project to show that the huge chorus synth is actually **many separate synth layers printed and combined into one stem**, and that his workflow is to **print each synth and move on** (so there's no live instance to recall). Names the sources (a "massive preset" that's just Brass, Sylenth, Kontakt-labelled prints) and re-confirms the "each layer has a job" principle — the bottom layer adds sustain/presence, and losing the attack layer bums him out.

## Techniques

- **Print every synth, then reach for another** — "I'd do a synth of something and then print it — I don't want to have an instance of that synth." Commit to audio, keep moving; this is why a final-project stem hides what was really done. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **The chorus synth = many layers combined to one stem** — several synths (one sounds like **Sylenth**, one a "massive preset" that's just **Brass**, others he mislabeled "Kontakt") printed and merged. The listener hears one synth. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[BED Slot]], [[LEAD Slot]]
- **Each layer's job:** a **bottom layer adds sustain / makes the notes more present**; removing it loses the percussive attack and presence ("I'm bummed we lost that attack"). One layer's volume is automated. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Automation]], [[SUB Slot]]
- **Sylenth is still a go-to** — "almost vintage software," still awesome; a recommended synth source. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **The stem's processing:** OTT (zero upward — "there was some purpose"), an image/width plugin (maybe from automating width once), **LFO Tool at 65%**, a brickwall limiter he doesn't remember adding. Audit-your-chain lesson again. → [[OTT]], [[LFO Tool]], [[Width and Stereo]]
- **The New Rules vocal chop is Emily & Caroline** — the chopped vocal texture is his collaborators' voices (the same background-talkers gated into the percussion). → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]

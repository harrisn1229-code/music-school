> [!danger]- 🔒 Answer key — do not open until you have answered every question
> **Q1.** **Find the shared note · hold it where it is · move the rest to the nearest note of the next chord**
> Then repeat chord to chord. The shared tone is the pivot the voicing rotates around, and everything else moves by the smallest available interval, usually a step.
>
> **Q2.** **The lowest voice — it stays on each chord's actual root**
> The ear judges a chord's identity from the bass, so voice-leading the bass too would smooth the motion and cost the harmonic clarity the root motion supplies.
>
> **Q3.** **No — the method is about minimising motion, not about naming the result**
> You can voice-lead an entire progression correctly without ever working out which inversion you are in.
>
> **Q4.** **The character — the individual voices start leaping and stop sounding like lines**
> Root position to root position can be a large jump even between closely related chords, so every voice sounds like it is jumping around the scale rather than singing.
>
> **Q5.** **A close triad with the middle note moved up an octave — the stack becomes 1-5-3**
> Same three notes, same chord, wider spacing with a string skip in the middle, and an open, harp-like sound.
>
> **Q6.** **Chain the triads by ascending thirds, moving exactly one voice a half or whole step per change**
> In C: C, Em, G, B°, Dm, F, Am, back to C. Always to the closest spread voicing of the next chord, so the whole thing descends the neck.
>
> **Q7.** **The third-chain touches every triad, and the closest-voicing rule forces a new inversion each recurrence**
> Coverage is a side effect of the voice-leading rule, not a separate exercise bolted on.
>
> **Q8.** **G7 to C — a real cadence, to land the study rather than fade out on another triad**
> It is the only point where an actual dominant 7th appears instead of a bare triad.
>
> **Q9.** **Spread voicing in theory jargon · playing near the nut on open strings in guitar jargon**
> Same term, two unrelated meanings, and conflating them is the standard trap in this material.
>
> **Q10.** **Hold each shared tone, move everything else by step, and leave the bass on the plain roots**
> *Rubric (5):* applies the hold-the-common-tone rule correctly across the changes (2) · describes at least one change concretely, naming the held note and where the others went (2) · states that the bass stays on the roots and is not voice-led (1)
> A correct answer walks the loop change by change: name the note the two chords share, say it does not move, and account for where each remaining note went and by how far. The chords should end up in mixed inversions rather than all root position, and most voices should be holding or moving a step. Underneath, the left hand or the bass plays each chord's plain root throughout, which is what keeps the progression legible while the upper voices are inverted — the arrangement is still heard as root position because of the bass, however the top is voiced.

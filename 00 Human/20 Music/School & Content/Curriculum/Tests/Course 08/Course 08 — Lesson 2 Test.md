> [!danger]- 🔒 Answer key — do not open until you have answered every question
> **Q1.** **The feeling changes and the harmony doesn't — the 3rd colours the chord and is less conclusive**
> Brighter or sadder depending on the chord's quality, and more melodic than the root, which is where a phrase goes when it wants to be finished.
>
> **Q2.** **Root settled · 5th stable but neutral · 7th leaning · 9th open and floating**
> The root is final and safe. The 5th is strong without being conclusive. The 7th is unresolved and wants to move. The 9th adds colour without changing the chord's function, which is why it's the pop landing note.
>
> **Q3.** **Landing on the root constantly**
> It agrees with the harmony on every downbeat, so nothing is ever held in tension long enough to want resolving. Agreement everywhere means no line at all — just the chord restated in a higher register.
>
> **Q4.** **3rds and 7ths — they stop the line agreeing with the chord on every strong beat**
> *Also accepted:* 9ths as well · anything other than the root and 5th on downbeats
>
> **Q5.** **The relationship to the chord changed, not the notes — tension arrived where there was none**
> *Rubric (5):* says the pitches and the harmony are unchanged, so the difference is purely in which notes land on strong beats (2) · says landing on chord tones that agree with the harmony reads as an arpeggio (1) · says targeting 3rds, 7ths and 9ths creates tension that has to resolve, which is what makes it read as a line (2)
> An arpeggio and a melody can be made of the same pitches. What separates them is whether the strong beats confirm the chord or argue with it. If every downbeat lands on a note the chord already contains and already emphasises, the ear hears the harmony being spelled out and nothing more — there is no question anywhere in it. Move those same landings onto the 3rd, the 7th, the 9th and each strong beat now carries something unresolved, so the notes between them stop being decoration and start being the answer. Nothing about the harmony moved; the line simply stopped agreeing with it on schedule.
>
> **Q6.** **The harmony supplies it — hold one note and let the chords recontextualize it underneath**
> The same pitch becomes the 5th, then the 3rd, then the 9th, so it moves without moving. Long notes work, and the melody can afford to be simple because something else is changing.
>
> **Q7.** **Rhythm, contour and phrasing — the melody has to generate its own movement**
> A held note over one chord for eight bars is just a held note. Nothing underneath is going to recontextualize it.
>
> **Q8.** **The static loop — the listener is already in a looped frame**
> A repeating melodic figure over a repeating chord reads as a hook rather than as laziness, where the same repetition over moving changes sounds like the topline gave up.
>
> **Q9.** **Nothing was wrong with the line — the thing that was moving it went away**
> The melody was leaning on the chords for its motion, and over one chord there are no chords to lean on. It needs rhythmic and contour interest written into it that the moving version never had to carry.
>
> **Q10.** **Over a static loop, rhythm is the only source of motion left that you fully control**
> *Rubric (5):* says a static loop supplies no harmonic motion, so the topline has to generate its own (2) · says rhythm is the highest-leverage of the three sources available (1) · says writing the groove on one note first keeps the rhythm from being muddled by pitch decisions, and pitch is layered on afterward (2)
> The loop-versus-changes distinction is what makes rhythm-first a structural answer rather than a preference. Over changes, the harmony is already moving, so a simple sustained line is doing real work and rhythm is one option among several. Over a static loop, that source is gone: contour and phrasing remain, but rhythm is the one the producer has complete control over bar to bar and the one the listener tracks most closely in a loop-based genre. Writing the groove first — one note, no pitch decisions, until the rhythm alone is satisfying — makes sure the part that has to carry the section is finished before anything else complicates it. Pitch is then a variation applied to something already working, rather than the thing you hope will rescue a rhythm you never really wrote.

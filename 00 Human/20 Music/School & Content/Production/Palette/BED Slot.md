# BED Slot — 250–800 Hz

Warm sustained harmony; the emotional floor of the song. The biggest swap list in the palette — and the #1 source of mud when double-booked.

## Swap list (interchangeable occupants)

- **Synth bed/pad** — [[Omnisphere#Patch Recipes|Omnisphere bed recipe]] · [[Serum 2#Patch Recipes|Serum 2 bed recipe]] · [[1 Synth Bed Track|Synth Bed Track]] chain
- **E-piano / Rhodes** — [[Keyscape]] Rhodes LA Custom E / Classic Mark I (neo-soul, hip hop)
- **Wurlitzer** — [[Keyscape]] 140B/200A (warmer/barkier flavor of the same object)
- **Juno pad** — [[Model 84 Polyphonic Synthesizer]] (saw+sub → Chorus II; bedroom-pop/psych lane)
- **Organ** — sustained harmony with more grit
- **Rhythm guitar (open/chorused)** — the [[Bedroom Pop - Indie]] bed
- **Acoustic piano (sustained)** — [[1 Acoustic Piano Track|Acoustic Piano Track]] · [[Keyscape]] LA Custom C7
- **Drift/Wavetable stock pads** — [[Drift]] `Pad` folder, [[Wavetable]] `Pads`

## Mix rules

- Sits **behind** the vocal: wide sides, soft center (M/S — high-shelf the sides, dip the mid; [[Pro-Q 4]] per-band M/S).
- HPF 120–150 Hz always — the warmth illusion lives at 250–500 Hz, not below.
- One bed at a time; verse Rhodes → chorus pad is a **swap**, not a stack.
- Movement beats level: slow filter/WT motion keeps a quiet bed interesting.

## When it's the hook
Intros, breakdowns, and post-chorus moments where the vocal exits: open the bed's high end (+shelf 8 kHz), widen, let internal movement carry it. The Tame Impala pulse lane ([[Psych-Pop (Tame Impala)]]) is a bed-as-hook genre.

## When it competes
- **vs Vocal** (the classic 200–500 Hz mud): dynamic dip in the bed keyed by the vocal — [[Soothe2]] external sidechain or [[Pro-Q 4]] dynamic bands = automatic vocal pocket.
- **vs BASS** ([[BASS Slot]]): HPF harder; if it still fights, the bed loses low-mids, not the bass.
- **vs MIDS/COMP** ([[MIDS Slot]]): thin the bed's top (LP ~2 kHz) so comping/riffs own the articulation range.

## Drum lanes in this slot (from books)

- **This is the drum kit's boxiness slot.** Toms: cut **300–800 Hz** to remove boxiness (sweep a narrow boost through the range to find the honk, then flip to a 3–5 dB cut — *general starting point, the source gives ranges only*). Snare body/"umph" shelf **200–300 Hz** lands on this slot's bottom edge, and the drum-bus mud cut on [[2 Drums Group]] (250–400 Hz) sits right here too — so the tom boxiness cut and the kit mud cut are the same move at two scales. → [[EQ]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[Snare Design]] — Whipped Cream Sounds, [[Drum Mixing EQ Guide - Enrichment|Drum Mixing EQ Guide]]

## Bass tone lanes in this slot (from videos)

- **"Honky the dwarf" — ~500 Hz nasal quality; "Muddy the dwarf" — ~250 Hz mush sits on this slot's bottom edge.** Cut Honky (a 500 Hz slider, or a bass's own contour switch as a workaround when there's no dedicated 500 Hz control) to fix a persistent nasal tone; boost it deliberately (Honky's alter-ego is Growly) plus switch to the neck pickup to chase Steve Harris's snarl on Iron Maiden's "Power Slave." Muddy is a genuine two-way control here, not just a cut — thin tones can take more mud added back in for fullness; John McVie's "Rhiannon" tone lives on the balance point between too much (muddy) and too little (hollow). Full breakdown: [[Bass EQ Frequency Zones — The Five Dwarves]]. → [[EQ]], [[BASS Slot]] — BassBuzz, [[How to Get the PERFECT Bass Tone]]

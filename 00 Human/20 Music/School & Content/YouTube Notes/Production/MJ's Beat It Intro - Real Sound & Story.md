# MJ's "Beat It" Intro - Real Sound & Story

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l34u9-ZtcV4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l34u9-ZtcV4)

## Synopsis

Mostly provenance/history rather than technique: a conversation with a Synclavier collector (Kevin) about the origin of "Beat It"'s famous synth intro. The seven-note phrase came from a promotional demonstration record for the Synclavier II ("The Incredible Sounds of the Synclavier"), a preset patch programmed and performed by Danny Yeager (uncredited) — Michael Jackson heard the record, liked the sound, and the team recreated the exact preset and replayed the same seven notes for the actual recording rather than sampling the demo record directly.

## What's taught

- **Provenance**: "Beat It"'s intro is a recreated Synclavier factory preset (an uncredited Danny Yeager composition/performance from a mail-order demo record), not an original patch built for the song.
- **One transferable production nuance**: on this preset, using reverb to let the tone "ring" fully (rather than cutting it short) reads as noticeably different/better than the same patch with a short, un-reverbed attack — a reminder that a preset's reverb tail can be load-bearing to its identity, not just decoration.

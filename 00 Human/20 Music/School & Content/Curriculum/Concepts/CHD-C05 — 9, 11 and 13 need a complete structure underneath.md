# CHD-C05 — 9, 11 and 13 need a complete structure underneath

**Q.** What makes an added 2, 4 or 6 count as a 9th, 11th or 13th — and what is the test *not* about?

> [!question]- Answer
> It counts as an extension only when a **complete lower structure** is present: a full triad (1-3-5) for the 9 and 11, a full seventh chord (1-3-5-7) for the 13. Pull the 3rd and the added 2 stays a **2**; pull the 7th and the added 6 stays a **6**. It is **not** about octave placement — an extension voiced right next to the root is still called a 9.

## Why it matters

The common shorthand "9 = 2, 11 = 4, 13 = 6, an octave up" is usually true and is not the rule. The real test settles the cases that actually come up: Cadd9 versus Csus2 is one fact — is the 3rd there — and Cmaj7♯11 is never called ♯4 because it sits on a complete seventh chord. The standard omissions fall out of the same logic: drop the 5th on a 9th chord, drop the **3rd** on an 11th because it clashes with the 11, and drop the 5th, 9th and especially the 11th on a 13th.

## Drill

Voice C9, Cadd9 and Csus2 back to back and say which note is missing in each. Then play a full seven-note C13 and remove notes until it stops sounding muddy — check what you kept against the omission list.

# TEXTURE Slot — full-band overlay, low level

Ambience, era, "air in the room." Glue, not content — the listener should feel it leave, not hear it play.

## Swap list (interchangeable occupants)

- **Vinyl/tape noise** — [[RC-20 Retro Color]] noise module (ducked = reads as real media)
- **Real tape/cassette hiss** — print through [[Sony TC-630]] (hardware) or [[Cassette]] (Wavesfactory, its Static/Dynamic noise controls) for a genuinely captured noise floor rather than a synthesized one
- **Granular atmosphere** — [[Omnisphere#Patch Recipes|Omnisphere texture recipe]]
- **Room tone / field recordings** — rain, street, crowd, amp hum
- **Reverb bed** — a 100%-wet long tail ([[ValhallaSupermassive]]) fed quietly by the bed
- **Foley loops** — vinyl run-out, tape start/stop, breath

## Mix rules

- Level: **–18 to –25 dB under the mix** — the mute test is the level test (mute it: the mix should feel *smaller*, not quieter).
- One texture per song usually; per section at most.
- Full-band but not full-time: drop it in choruses so they open, return it in verses (texture = intimacy).
- Mono is fine — often better (a wide texture eats the bed's width contrast).

## When it's the hook
Intros/outros/transitions: the texture *exposed* (vinyl crackle alone before the beat drops) is a classic hook moment — the one time it comes up in level. Also the [[Bedroom Pop - Indie]] "one wrong sound per song" candidate.

## When it competes
- It shouldn't be loud enough to compete. If it masks anything, it's 6+ dB too hot.
- **vs AIR** ([[AIR Slot]]): crackle brightness vs hats — LP the texture ~8 kHz.
- Cumulative mud: HPF textures ~100 Hz like everything else ([[SUB Slot]] stays clean).

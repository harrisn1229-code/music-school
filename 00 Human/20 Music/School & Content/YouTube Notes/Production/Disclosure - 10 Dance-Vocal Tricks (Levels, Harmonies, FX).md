# Disclosure: 10 Dance-Vocal Tricks (Perfect Levels, Powerful Harmonies, Crazy FX)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CWmIqmSzhE8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CWmIqmSzhE8)

## Synopsis

A deep session walkthrough of a Howard-featuring vocal (built on a "robot singing in a toilet" pitch-correction concept, Todd Edwards-style choppy sampling, layered tremolo, and deliberately violent effects use) followed by a genuinely reusable back half on getting vocal level right against the music — the most practical, broadly-applicable content in the video. Core message on levels: your ears lie at working volume; check on multiple real-world speakers (laptop, iPhone, headphones, car) at LOW volume, because if you have to lean in to hear the vocal, it's already too quiet for a hook-driven track.

## Techniques

- **Recording a vocal through an effect on the performer's end, not added afterward** — Howard sent his vocal take already run through tremolo on his own master output, not as a separate effect added in the mix; described as a surprising, "how did you do that" move that changes the character of the raw material itself. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]]
- **Layer two tremolo instances at different, non-matching rates for a "battle of the tremolos" chop effect** — one tremolo running straight eighth notes, a second running swung sixteenths, both active on the same vocal simultaneously; reached for Soundtoys' tremolo specifically because it supports swing, which a straight-time tremolo can't do. → [[Modulation]]
- **Automate compressor Attack time as a performance/expression tool, not just a static setting** — riding the compressor's attack up and down through a phrase (shown as a dense automation lane) lets specific words/accents punch through with more transient than the surrounding vocal, functioning like a live-performed dynamics move rather than a fixed setting. → [[Automation]], [[Compression]]
- **Deliberately extreme, "wrong-sounding" pitch correction is a legitimate creative choice distinct from natural tuning** — going for an obviously robotic, hard-quantized vocal effect ("sound like a robot singing in a toilet") is a different tool than Melodyne-style natural-sounding tuning, not a worse version of it; the choice is about what the part needs, not about which tool is "better." → [[Vocal Tuning]]
- **A "bass note of the vocal" layer — literally the lowest note the vocalist can sing, tracked and added as its own layer** purely for weight when the full vocal stack lands; distinct from the main melody/harmony layers, its only job is low-end reinforcement of the vocal's own pitch content.
- **Keep a gospel/choir backing layer un-tuned on purpose, for natural brightness contrast against a heavily processed lead** — while the lead vocal goes through extreme robotic pitch correction, a backing choir layer stays untouched, so its natural, slightly-imperfect brightness sits underneath and contrasts with the processed lead rather than matching its artificial character.
- **Pick a "flavorful" compressor (1176-style, saturating) specifically to compensate for a vocal that was recorded too clean** — chose the CLA-76 (1176 emulation) over a cleaner CL 1B specifically because the vocal was known to have been recorded direct into an interface with no outboard color (no Neve, no hardware compressor) on the way in; the saturating compressor's harmonic distortion adds character that a genuinely clean signal chain never had a chance to pick up. → [[Compression]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Briefly flange/ring-shift an entire vocal group (or the whole mix) for a few seconds as a deliberate, violent effect move** — inspired explicitly by Kevin Parker/Tame Impala's habit of pushing the whole mix through a flanger for a moment and pulling it back out; framed as permission to use effects more aggressively on buses or the master than usual mixing caution suggests, including keeping a couple of "mess with it" effects on the master bus and jamming/automating them live like a DJ set to capture happy accidents. → [[Modulation]], [[Master Chain|8 Master]]
- **Route every vocal element through one shared vocal bus specifically so the entire stack can be pushed/pulled with a single fader** — the practical reason for a dedicated vocal bus beyond glue processing: one fader move rebalances the whole vocal group against the music at once, which matters a lot for the level-checking workflow below.
- **Vocal bus processing: EQ first (bringing up presence/"airs"), then Soothe 2** — Soothe here is framed less as "resonance suppression" and more as a net gain in perceived loudness plus width plus brightness, with its frequency-focus control biased toward the bright side. → [[EQ]], [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Automate vocal volume inversely against a filter cutoff automation — turn the vocal UP (~3 dB) as the cutoff closes down, and back off slightly as it opens** — compensates for the perceived loudness/energy loss that happens when a filter closes, keeping the vocal's presence roughly constant through a filter sweep rather than letting it seem to recede. → [[Automation]]
- **Check vocal-to-music balance on multiple real speaker types, at LOW volume, not your main studio monitors turned up.** Explicit rotation: laptop speakers, iPhone speakers, headphones, car, all at a quiet listening level — if you find yourself leaning in or turning the whole mix up to hear the vocal clearly, the vocal itself is too low relative to the music. Turning speakers DOWN while mixing specifically helps get vocal balance right (loud monitoring flatters a buried vocal). → [[Referencing and Translation]]
- **A quiet vocal can be a deliberate hook — but know which genre convention you're using.** Some classic hip-hop deliberately mixed the rapper quiet, either from mixing too loud or intentionally forcing the listener to turn up — a legitimate, specific choice. For hook-driven house/pop where the goal is a recognizable, singable line, the opposite applies: the vocal needs to be clearly up front so the hook lands even in bad listening conditions (a restaurant, a car) — reference points cited: "Lola's Theme," "Gypsy Woman."

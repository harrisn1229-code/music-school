## Techniques

- **The five critical-listening categories for mastering, each with a too-far-either-way failure mode:** Dynamics (ratio of quiet to loud passages — too dynamic and quiet details vanish on consumer systems, too compressed and the listen gets fatiguing; compressors/maximizers/limiters/clippers act here), Loudness (overall level, inherently tied to Dynamics — two identical-peak mixes with different dynamic range need different loudness to read the same), Timbre (overall frequency response plus the ADSR — mainly attack/decay of transients — plus harmonic/disharmonic distortion; compressors, EQs, and exciters do the most work here), Width (ratio of centered to distinctly-L/R signal, shaped with mid-side EQ/compression/saturation and stereo imagers; too wide loses mono compatibility and reads unfocused, **too centered reads closed-in and lacking a natural sense of space**), Depth (ratio of perceived background to foreground/dry to wet, the most complex category since its perception ties into all the others at once). → [[Critical Listening for Mastering]], [[Width and Stereo]], [[Depth and Space]], [[Clipping and Loudness]], [[EQ]]
- **The quickest way to increase perceived depth: a very subtle maximizer.** Raising quieter ambient details just enough to clear the masking threshold gives the listener a reference point for background vs. foreground — once those details are audible at all, the depth relationship reads. → [[Depth and Space]], [[Oxford Inflator]]
- **A second depth lever: subtle amplification of the side image in the mid-frequency range.** A width-adjacent move (boosting the sides) that reads back as *depth*, not just width — worth trying alongside the maximizer move above rather than instead of it. → [[Depth and Space]], [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Genre-aware saturation/clipper choice, worked EDM-vs-jazz example.** EDM with an intentionally aggressive kick/snare/pluck → skip tape emulation (its subtle wave-shaping + high-frequency attenuation would fight the intended aggression), reach for a **Clipper** instead (imparts distortion to each transient, keeps the aggressive character). Jazz mastering → tape emulation is right here (peaks attenuate gradually, shaped in a way indicative of the genre's era — can even tune the emulation toward the specific machine that era of jazz would have run through), and works as a genuine alternative to limiting, not just a color pass; a Clipper would be the wrong call (no reason to impart aggressive transient distortion, no reason to push toward a loudness that needs clipping). → [[Critical Listening for Mastering]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Clipping and Loudness]], [[Kramer Tape]], [[kHs Clipper]]
- **Genre + playback-context checks on a target you're imagining, before committing to it.** Wanting a wide stereo image on an EDM track headed for clubs (often multispeaker mono playback) → reconsider how wide to actually go, since the venue caps how much translates. Wanting a pop song bright and aggressive but knowing it's headed mainly to earbuds → be careful not to overshoot brightness, or it reads harsh on that chain. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Referencing and Translation]]
- **A named general mastering chain order:** corrective EQ → saturation → stereo imaging → maximization → clipping → limiting — matches this vault's own [[Master Chain]] stage order (corrective EQ → glue → tape → imaging → density → clip → limit) closely enough to read as corroboration rather than a new sequence. → [[Master Chain]]
- **The three skills that separate a mastering engineer from someone who can just operate the tools:** critical listening specific to mastering (the five categories above), deep historical/genre knowledge of tonal character across at least a few genres (why records in a genre sound the way they do, over the decades), and the ability to imagine a sound before it exists (know the target before you start shaping toward it — explicitly named as the hardest of the three). → [[Critical Listening for Mastering]]

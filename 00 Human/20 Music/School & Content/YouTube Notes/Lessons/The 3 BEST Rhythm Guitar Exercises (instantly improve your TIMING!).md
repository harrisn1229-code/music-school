# The 3 BEST Rhythm Guitar Exercises (instantly improve your TIMING!)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hj7O0FeZmyo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hj7O0FeZmyo)

## Synopsis

Three timing/rhythm exercises building from picking-technique switching, through odd note grouping in plain 4/4 (no odd meters required), to a hard combined exercise mixing both — plus three closing practice tips for timing discipline generally.

## Techniques

- **Exercise 1 — switch cleanly between eighth notes (downstrokes, palm-muting/open-note mix, picking hand resting on the bridge) and eighth-note triplets (alternate picking, hand lifted for strumming-style dead notes) without rushing into the triplets or dragging back out of them.** Combines a rhythmic-subdivision switch with a picking-hand *position* switch (bridge-anchored vs. lifted) in the same exercise — echoes the anchored-vs-floating wrist distinction elsewhere on this channel, here applied as a deliberate mid-exercise switch rather than a fixed per-style choice.
- **Exercise 2 — rhythmic displacement and odd note grouping entirely inside plain 4/4** (no odd meters like 7/8 or 5/4 needed). Core device: play a straight eighth-note stream but group/accent it in 5s instead of 2s or 4s — **explicitly not quintuplets**, the underlying eighth-note pulse stays constant, only the accent grouping changes, so the accented note drifts steadily away from the beat until the full pattern resolves after 5 measures. Advanced variant: drop the unaccented in-between notes entirely, playing only the group's first and third note on the accented strings — sparser and harder to place accurately.
  - **Counting method: Konnakol-style vocal syllables** (e.g. "Ta Ka Di Mi Ti" for a group of 5) instead of counting numbers — drawn from the Indian percussion vocal tradition, and specifically recommended once tempo is too fast to count numbers reliably.
  - **Simpler on-ramps before tackling groups of 5:** within one 4/4 measure (8 eighth notes total), combine groups of 2s and 3s — the only three possible combinations are 2-3-3, 3-2-3, and 3-3-2; or use a single repeating group of 3 eighth notes that spans measure boundaries (rather than resetting every bar) until the pattern lands back on beat 1 after 3 measures.
- **Exercise 3 (hardest) — combine fast downstrokes and alternate picking across mixed note values including 16th notes and quarter-note triplets, focused on switching between groups without rushing or dragging.** Recommended only after exercises 1–2 are comfortable; suggested approach: isolate and master each note-value group alone before combining them.
- **Three closing practice tips:** (1) start the metronome click on beat 2 or 4 instead of always beat 1, for an extra timing challenge (a technique borrowed from how many drummers practice); (2) deliberately practice playing consistently laid-back or consistently slightly rushed as an intentional stylistic choice — only works as a real effect if it's *consistent* ahead-of/behind-the-beat, not random; (3) record practice sessions in a DAW and review objectively for unintentional rushing/dragging patterns (personal finding: a tendency to start tricky/fast sections slightly early).

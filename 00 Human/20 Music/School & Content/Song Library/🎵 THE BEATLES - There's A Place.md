# There's A Place — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** the small single-bridge model. Intro (**5 bars**) · Verse (**15**) · Verse variant (rewritten to hand off to the bridge) · Bridge (**10**) · Verse · Outro (the final verse, one bar shorter, into an antiphonal fade). Every irregular section length comes from **the same cause: a stretched vocal pickup into the next section** ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]).

The music contradicts the lyric — a confident claim over harmony that never finds its footing. The signature sonority is the **major seventh landing on the very first downbeat**, and the key is established by I–IV oscillation rather than by any cadence. Bars 7–8 sit on two full bars of V and leave it unresolved, veering toward the relative minor in what sounds like a modulation the next verse simply ignores ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]); the variant verse re-opens on V and hands it to a [[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]] into vi at the bridge.

The bridge is the clearest case. Its first three chords set up a textbook pivot modulation to B major — and **B never comes; a G♯ major chord aborts it** and yanks the music back to C♯ minor. Pollack half-blames inexperience and half-argues the groping quality is exactly right.

Two competing hooks fight all song: the wailing harmonica motif on the anxious side and the rising figure carrying the title phrase on the reassuring side. The harmonica hook is then **folded into the backing vocal** in bars 9–12 ([[MEL-C03 — Motifs and how to develop them]]), and the outro simply alternates the two.

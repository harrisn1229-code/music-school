# Digital Audio Manipulation with Shawn Everett

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dDeF1qs3g4I)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dDeF1qs3g4I)

## Synopsis

A short, screen-recorded Mix with the Masters segment of Shawn Everett live-editing a session, building out a metallic texture layer ("metal two") from scratch. It's dense with concrete moves but thin on narration — Everett talks through what he's clicking almost in shorthand ("let's go like that," "that one could be inverted, who knows"), so most of the value here is in the *process*, not quotable numbers. Two distinct techniques get demonstrated back to back: (1) a duplicate-and-mangle sound-design recipe — take one source, make five-plus copies, apply a different manipulation to each (reverse, phase invert, splice in a pasted section, pitch-shift by a different rough amount), crossfade the edit points, then mute/arrange the results across the timeline to follow the track's dynamic build; and (2) a Melodyne pitch-editing pass — switching off polyphonic detection to make manual note-dragging easier, then moving a whole detected note by hand and deliberately accepting some loss of the original note's character as part of the creative result rather than something to fix. A small workflow habit bookends the clip: color-coding edits red (old) vs. green (new) to track edit generations at a glance.

## Techniques

- **Color-code edits by generation: old edits red, new edits green.** A lightweight session-organization habit for keeping track of which regions/clips are prior takes vs. the current pass while iterating live — "let's make all the old ones red, all the new ones are going to be green." DAW-agnostic visual bookkeeping, not tied to a specific tool. *(0:00)*
- **Duplicate a source into 5+ copies to build one texture layer ("metal two"), then apply a DIFFERENT manipulation to each copy so no two read as identical.** The starting move for a new texture: pick a source, make five or more duplicates, and treat each one as a separate mini-edit rather than a uniform stack. *(0:10)*
- **Reverse each duplicate differently — different reverse points/lengths per copy ("different reverse puppies") — rather than reversing every copy the same way.** Explicitly framed as intentionally messing each one up differently: "let's just do different reverse puppies, like that — just mess them up." *(0:20)*
- **Phase-invert one of the duplicated layers** as one of the per-copy variations ("that one could be inverted, who knows") — a low-effort way to add a layer that's harmonically related but timbrally distinct from its neighbors. *(0:30)*
- **Splice a pasted-in section from elsewhere onto one of the duplicates** — "let's paste a little section on that, for some reason, like that" — adding source material into one copy rather than only reshaping what's already there. *(0:35)*
- **Pitch-shift each of the 5+ duplicated copies by a DIFFERENT rough amount (spread roughly ±2 to +6 semitones), not uniformly, then crossfade the edit points.** Talked through copy by copy: one nudged up slightly, one flagged to maybe go down and lengthened, one set to −4, one to −2, one left alone, one to +2 (lengthened further), one to +4, one to +6 — then the copies are cross-faded together and re-ordered ("we'll go with seven... six is gonna go up here, let's put five here, four — just like a Bob Ross," i.e. arranging the pitched layers by ear/feel rather than a strict ascending/descending order). *(0:45–1:40)* *(qualitative — exact semitone values are best-effort reads off vague captioned dialogue, not confirmed settings; treat as a rough spread, not a recipe to copy number-for-number)*
- **Mute and arrange the mangled variants to follow the track's dynamic rather than playing them all at once.** After the manipulation pass, most of the duplicated/pitched copies get muted; only the ones that match a given moment's energy are brought in — "we have like a bit of a blast-off going on over here, there's nothing over here, I'll just use the end of that" — auditioning a tightened edit against the dynamic before committing. *(1:50)*
- **Switch Melodyne off Polyphonic detection specifically to make manual note-dragging easier.** Before pitch-editing a note, the algorithm is switched away from polyphonic ("it's trying to go polyphonic, but it's much easier to manipulate this stage in") — a workflow choice made for editing ergonomics, not accuracy. *(2:20)*
- **Identify the note's starting pitch before moving it, then drag the whole detected note down by hand to a target pitch.** Reads the detected note as around F3, with the target start point at A2, then drags the "big old hunk of note" down to land there — treated as a manual, by-ear pitch move rather than an automated shift. *(2:30)*
- **Deliberately accept losing some of the original note's detail/character as part of the creative result, rather than fixing it.** While dragging the note, some of its original character gets lost in the edit — flagged on camera and accepted rather than undone: "I might sneak in a little of that one, because I start losing some of the note on this one, right here... at this point it's just kind of, you're just kind of being creative and designing however you want it." The stated philosophy: an imperfect/damaged edit is a valid creative outcome, not always a mistake to correct. *(2:50)*

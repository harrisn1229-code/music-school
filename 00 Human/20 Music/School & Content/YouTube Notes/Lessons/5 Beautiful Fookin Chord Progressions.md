# 5 BEAUTIFUL FOOKIN CHORD PROGRESSIONS

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IInm-zxd3VE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IInm-zxd3VE)

## Synopsis

Five originally-written chord progressions performed back-to-back for viewers to rank; only one ("For the Odds") has its key and full chord spelling revealed on camera, and it's a genuinely useful worked example — a IV-chord opening voiced as a rootless Lydian-flavored extension, followed by a ii–V–I cadence to the true tonic and a descent through the relative-minor-side diatonic chords.

## Techniques

- **"For the Odds" progression, fully spelled — key of A major: Dmaj7♯11 (rootless IV) → Bm7–E7 (ii–V) → Amaj7 (I) → F♯m9 (vi) → C♯m11 (iii).** Opens on the IV chord voiced as a *rootless* maj7♯11 (the ♯11 is the Lydian-characteristic tone of the IV chord in a major key — playing IV with its own ♯11 leans into that Lydian color rather than plain major 7), delays the tonic by a full ii–V–I cadence before landing on Amaj7, then descends through the two other diatonic minor-family chords (vi, iii) extended to 9 and 11 respectively. A reusable shape: **open on a rootless, extended IV chord (leaning Lydian) → resolve the "real" arrival with a full ii–V–I → close by stepping down through the relative-minor-side diatonic chords, extended.**
- **Deliberately withholding the key from listeners as a rank-blind exercise** — the other four progressions are performed with chord identities intentionally unrevealed until a follow-up video, so a listener ranks purely by ear rather than by chord-spelling bias. (Confirms the general "rank by ear first" instinct already used elsewhere in this batch's harmonic-analysis videos, just inverted — analysis withheld instead of given.)

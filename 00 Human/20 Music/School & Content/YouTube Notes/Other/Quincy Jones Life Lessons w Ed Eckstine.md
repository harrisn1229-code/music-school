## Synopsis

A long, personal tribute conversation with Ed Eckstine (record executive, longtime Quincy Jones collaborator, son of Billy Eckstine) reflecting on Quincy Jones' life and working method following his passing. Almost entirely biography and personal reminiscence, honestly — but contains one genuinely reusable creative-direction technique worth capturing.

## What's taught

- **Direct musicians with evocative/synesthetic language (color, taste) instead of literal technical instructions, then refine from their interpretation.** Quincy Jones would describe a desired change as needing to be "more yellow," or ask Bruce Swedien for "a little more garlic salt" / "a little more red pepper" on a mix — deliberately non-technical language that lets the player or engineer supply their own interpretation (a bassist reading "more yellow" as "mellower," for example), which Jones would then nuance further rather than dictating the exact technical move up front. Framed as giving real creative room while still steering toward an outcome.
- **A "write everything down, connect it visually" planning habit**: score paper, legal pads, note cards — capturing musical ideas the moment they're heard (even lying in a hammock), with circles and arrows connecting related thoughts, mind-map style, used for planning at the scale of an entire album's sequencing (documented specifically being used this way while working on *Thriller*'s track order).
- The rest of the video is biography — Quincy Jones' range of roles (composer, arranger, producer, executive, film/TV composer), personal stories from decades of collaboration, and tribute material with no further production technique.

# Best Daily 10 MIN Picking WORKOUT! (Maximum Speed, Control, Endurance)

## Synopsis

Deliberately alternate-picks arpeggio shapes (minor, major, diminished, descending chromatically) that would normally be swept or economy-picked — a self-described "torture session" designed specifically to break the habit of leaning on one fixed picking angle, since one-note-per-string arpeggio shapes force the picking pattern to flip direction on almost every string.

## What's taught

- **Why arpeggio shapes, alternate-picked, is uniquely hard:** most fast fretboard-spanning material (3-notes-per-string scale runs) lets you settle into one favorable picking angle and hold it, because the same stroke tends to open each new string. One-note-per-string arpeggio shapes remove that shortcut entirely — the pattern is constantly flipping which stroke opens the next string, so no single fixed angle serves the whole shape.
- **The actual training target is stated explicitly: NOT "learn to alternate-pick arpeggios instead of sweeping them."** At real performance tempo, sweep/economy picking remains the faster, easier choice for these shapes — physically superior for raw speed. The point of drilling them with strict alternate picking instead is to build a more adaptable picking-angle vocabulary that transfers to *other* odd alternate-picking patterns encountered elsewhere, not to replace sweep picking as the go-to technique for arpeggios specifically.
- **Slow-motion angle analysis:** unlike a fixed 3-notes-per-string angle, this material requires the wrist to subtly rotate through two distinct angles within the same phrase depending on transition direction — shown via side-by-side slow-motion footage of both angle types back to back.
- **Progression:** starts with two arpeggio shapes only (minor, major) to isolate the picking-hand problem from left-hand complexity, adds a third (diminished) shape at the end of the line for extra harmonic color, then pushes tempo — reporting the exercise starting to break down around 120 BPM 16th notes as an honest personal benchmark, not a "mastered" claim.

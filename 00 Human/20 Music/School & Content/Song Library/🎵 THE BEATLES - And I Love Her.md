# And I Love Her — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Paul's first released straight love song, and the essay Pollack uses to define pivot chords and relative-key modulation properly. The bittersweetness is a tonal ambiguity that never resolves — the song flip-flops continually between C♯ minor and E major — and the second event is a **modulation up a half step at the guitar solo**, entered on the new key's IV.

**Form.** One bridge only, with two verses before it and three after, the middle of those three given to the solo. A 4-bar intro, an odd **10-bar verse** phrased across five short units, a four-square 8-bar bridge, then a complete ending.

**Harmony.** The vocabulary is just the six chords shared by E major and C♯ minor, which is what makes the pivoting frictionless — a chord is heard first in the old key and re-heard a bar or two later in the new one ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]], [[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]). The intro's ii → I is a weak cadence toward the major that establishes nothing; the verse then repeats a **IV → I plagal cadence in the relative minor** three times to ground you in C♯ minor ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]) before walking IV–V–I back to E. The bridge converges on the major's V, and that V is then denied its resolution — the next verse simply restarts with its minor plagal cadence. The final chord is a Picardy third.

The half-step upshift is a fifties staple made strange by entering on **IV of the new key** — a G minor triad with no notes in common with the four-sharp world it interrupts. All the shock is in that first chord; a few bars later the new plane is unremarkable.

**Arrangement.** Sparse by design: acoustic lead and rhythm guitars, bass, claves. **Resting on beat one is a motif** — the opening hook and every vocal phrase begin with a rest.

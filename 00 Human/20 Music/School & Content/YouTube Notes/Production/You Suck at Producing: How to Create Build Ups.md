# You Suck at Producing: How to Create Build Ups

📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVNVxrgd8cw)

## Synopsis
A hands-on walkthrough of building an EDM-style buildup from two ingredients, built live in Ableton's Arrangement view. The first ingredient is the "double up" — a chunky drum-rack hit repeated on the beat, then progressively split and doubled in speed (splitting the clip and halving the gap between hits, working the fixed grid down to 1/32) until the pattern accelerates into a rapid-fire pattern right before the drop. The second is a rising pitch riser: a single-voice synth (a Mini Moog-style patch, one triangle-wave oscillator) playing a very short low note followed immediately by a long high note, with Glide set to its slowest/most gradual setting so the pitch climbs continuously from near-bass range up past the edge of human hearing over the whole length of the buildup. Both elements are timed to resolve together right at the last hit before the drop. The video's comedic thesis ("the drop doesn't matter, it's all buildup") is a joke, but the underlying lesson — that a buildup is really just rhythmic density increasing over time plus a continuous pitch rise — is the real, reusable takeaway.

## Techniques
- **The "double up": build rhythmic density by repeatedly splitting and halving a clip** — start with one chunky drum-rack hit landing on every beat with space between hits, then in Arrangement view split the clip (Ctrl/Cmd-click → Split) at the point where the doubling should begin, and keep splitting/duplicating to fill in the gaps at double, then quadruple speed, working the fixed grid down toward 1/32 as the hits get denser — the "double up" name comes from the fact that each pass literally doubles the previous hit rate. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Pick a "sufficiently chunky" sound as the buildup's rhythmic anchor** — the doubling technique needs a hit with enough weight/attack to read as escalating tension when it's repeated faster and faster; a thin or weak sample won't carry the effect. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **A slow-Glide, single-voice synth riser climbing from low to near-inaudible-high** — on a mono, one-oscillator (triangle wave) synth patch, place a very short low note immediately followed by a long high note stretched across the whole buildup's duration, and set the synth's Glide (portamento) to its maximum/slowest setting so the pitch continuously slides from the low note up toward the top note instead of jumping — the low note should sit fairly low (not the lowest possible) and the top note should sit above the normal range of human hearing so the rise never resolves to an audible pitch, just keeps climbing. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Time the riser and the doubled-drum pattern to both resolve on the last hit before the drop** — both buildup elements are lengthened/trimmed so they run out exactly together, landing on the final hit right before the drop lands. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

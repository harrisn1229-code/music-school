# Feel Good Inc. — Gorillaz

> *Demon Days* (2005), lead single. This note is a bass-focused breakdown of the main riff, its slide, and the short bass solo, as taught by [[How to Play Feel Good Inc. on Bass|BassBuzz's Josh]] and cross-checked against Gorillaz's real touring/session bassist **Shay Adelican**, who contributed a voice note on feel and tuning. Named by the video as "the riff that sold a million bass guitars" — Murdoc's own DVD commentary on the music video credits the riff's syncopation for why it hits so hard.

## Chords
Key: E♭ minor (natural/Aeolian) — the same handful of chords do double duty across the main riff, the "Windmill" hook, and the De La Soul rap verse, just in slightly different orders.

**Main riff:** E♭m – B♭m7 – A♭m7 – E♭m → **i – v7 – iv7 – i**

**"Windmill, windmill for the land" hook:** E♭m – B♭m → **i – v**, resolving down into Dm – Am under the De La Soul verse entry (the same i–v shape, a whole step lower)

**De La Soul rap verse:** Dm – Am – Gm – Am → **i – v – iv – v** (concert pitch: E♭m – B♭m – A♭m – B♭m)

→ Everything here is **minor i–v–iv** in one order or another — a natural-minor loop with no raised leading tone anywhere, so that "v" never actually functions as a real dominant (it's a plain minor v, not a V7). Worth checking directly against the brief's hunch: this is **not** the axis progression or the I–vi–IV–V doo-wop loop — both are major-key devices built on a leading-tone V7, and this track never leaves E♭ natural minor. It reads more like a repeating modal loop, though at three chords deep it's a shade busier than the "static one/two-chord vamp" the vault's `modal-vamp` tag is defined around, so it's left untagged here rather than stretched to fit.

## The riff: fretting and plucking as two independent systems

The riff mixes open strings (0) with fretted notes (2nd and 3rd fret) — the beginner trap is **"finger syncing"**: matching plucking-hand finger choice to fretting-hand finger choice (fret-with-index → pluck-with-index, fret-with-middle → pluck-with-middle). Never do this — it's inflexible and collapses the moment a line needs to pluck the same note twice or fret with the ring/pinky, which happens constantly in real basslines. Treat the two hands as unrelated systems:

- **Fretting:** index on every 2nd-fret note, middle on every 3rd-fret note — `open, open, index, middle, middle, index, open, open, middle, index, middle, open`.
- **Plucking:** straight alternating regardless of what the fretting hand is doing — `index, middle, index, middle, index, middle` for the full 12-note riff, twice through.

## Rhythm: syncopated, felt via the drum part

The riff's notes mix on-beat and off-beat hits — genuine syncopation, not a straight pulse. *(The exact beat-by-beat count is only approximately captured from the source transcript — treat the specific subdivision below as a general sketch, not a verified chart.)* Rough shape: on, off, on, off, off, off, continuing through the bar. The riff has a rest on beat 2 and beat 4 before landing on the next beat 1.

**Internalizing it — hum the drum part instead of counting numbers:** the drum track has a snare on beat 2, snare on beat 4, kick on beat 1 — hum **"bum-snare, bum-bum-bum-snare, bum-kick, bum"** in place of the bassline's own silences, locking your feel to what the drums are actually doing rather than trying to count blank space. See [[Syncopation and Metric Displacement]] for the general beat-ruler method this is a worked example of.

**Shay Adelican on the feel:** "it can be quite wobbly cuz it's a funky one — so have some fun" — the wobble is expected, not a sign you're playing it wrong.

## Note length / muting

Right notes + right rhythm still sounds wrong if the notes don't *end* on time — a held note has to be actively stopped (touch the string with the fretting hand or the plucking hand), it won't stop on its own. For this riff's rest on beat 4: release the fretting pressure on the last note before the rest, adding a safety mute if needed so the silence is actually silent. Full technique → [[Bass Note Muting Technique]].

## The slide

Written with a scoop mark and no note leading into it — it's about the **destination**, not the starting fret. Slide up to the **7th fret**, starting a few frets below (a middle distance — one fret away reads as anticlimactic, the max distance is excessive). Use the **pinky** (least shifting required afterward). Press down for the full slide but **don't over-press** or the hand sticks partway through; keep your eyes on where you're going, not where you started. Full method → [[Bass Slide Technique]].

## The bass solo

Short, and explicitly a synthesis of everything above rather than new material:
- **Fretting:** pinky, index, pinky, shift, index, open.
- **Plucking:** alternating index-middle-index-middle-index, then free choice for the last two notes after the phrase break.
- **Rhythm (beat-ruler shape):** on, off, off, off, off, on, on.

## Tuning

The riff is in **E♭**. Retune only the **E string** down a half step to E♭ — this riff stays entirely on the E and A strings, so the D and G strings can be left standard. Confirmed by Gorillaz's own bassist Shay Adelican: *"the riff is E flat, so I detune my E string to E flat... yeah, the song is in E flat."*

**Advanced variant (Shay's actual live approach):** rather than crossing from the low string to the A string the way BassBuzz teaches it to beginners, Shay plays the **entire** main riff on the single detuned low string — notes read fatter on the thicker string, and it means less retuning to manage on stage. BassBuzz teaches the cross-string version first because it's easier to learn, but flags the single-string version as the "cooler" thing to graduate to once the basic riff is solid.

## Steal this

- **Never sync a fretting-hand fingering choice to a plucking-hand fingering choice** — work out each hand's pattern independently; a plucking pattern that mirrors the fretting pattern will force inefficient same-finger repeats the moment the two hands' shapes diverge (they almost always do). → [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]]
- **Borrow the actual drum hits as your rhythm-counting syllables** when a line is syncopated enough that blank counting breaks down — hum the kick/snare pattern that's really driving the groove instead of numbers. → [[Syncopation and Metric Displacement]]
- **A slide with no note leading into it is about the landing fret, not the starting fret** — pick a starting distance that reads as a real gesture (not one fret, not the whole neck) and let the ear register the destination.
- **When a real player's live version differs from the record you're emulating, default to matching the record** unless you're deliberately reinterpreting the part — that's the version a listener will actually recognize.

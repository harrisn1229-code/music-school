# Synth Bass on Steroids: The Power of EFX Pedals

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j90QAnWZklA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j90QAnWZklA)

## Synopsis

A companion piece to [[10 Tips For Synth Bass]] (same channel, same wet/dry mult philosophy from that video's tip 10) — Anthony Marinelli runs a straight synth bass sound through five classic guitar effects pedals (Electro-Harmonix Big Muff fuzz, MXR stereo chorus, Mu-Tron Phasor II, MXR analog delay, A/DA flanger), always blending the effected ("wet") path back with the untouched ("dry") path via a hardware mult rather than committing fully wet. The throughline across all five: the dry path supplies fundamental/definition, the wet path supplies "spice"/personality, and the mix ratio between them is the actual creative decision — described as "adding spice to your food," each pedal a different spice.

## Techniques

1. **Fuzz (Big Muff, silicon) — crunch/color, always blended with dry to keep the fundamental.** Full-wet fuzz on a bass loses low-end weight; blending in the dry signal restores the fundamental underneath the crunch.
2. **Chorus (MXR stereo) — subtle warble that rounds out an otherwise flat straight bass tone.** Easy to under-notice in isolation; the effect is really heard once mixed back with dry — a flat/plain bass becomes rounder and more three-dimensional. Speeding the LFO rate up makes the movement more overtly audible, useful for a more obvious chorus character.
3. **Phaser (Mu-Tron Phasor II) — best on repeated bass notes, adding a sweep over a rhythm the dry signal already carries.** Dry supplies the rhythm/fundamental; the phaser layer adds motion on top. Needs only a small amount in the blend — brighter patches take phaser more audibly than dark/muted ones.
4. **Analog delay (MXR) — tuned very tight (short time, low feedback) purely to thicken a repeated note, not for audible echo.** The individual repeats blur together into added density rather than being heard as discrete echoes. Past a certain amount the effect starts thinning the sound instead of thickening it — there's a ceiling, found by ear, not a fixed setting.
5. **Flanger (A/DA) — makes a static/repeated bass part read as "alive"/moving rather than dead.** Demonstrated on an 80s-style repeated bass line; without the flanger the same part reads comparatively static.
6. **The general mixing move across all five: a hardware mult splits the dry signal to two paths — one straight to the mixer, one through the pedal — recombined at whatever wet/dry ratio suits the moment.** Ratio is a spectrum, not a fixed target: mostly-dry-with-a-touch-of-effect for subtle color, or mostly-effected-with-a-touch-of-dry-for-definition when more character is wanted. Multiple pedals can be layered on the same wet path (e.g. chorus + fuzz together, or delay + phaser + chorus stacked) for combined character.

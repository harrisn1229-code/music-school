# loner - how to add your own vocals to your r&b samples (in depth tutorial)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NP27-3YFlDk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NP27-3YFlDk)

## Synopsis

A dedicated, start-to-finish walkthrough of loner's own self-recorded-vocal process — distinct from his usual sample-flip breakdowns (which mostly source vocals from Splice or community stems) in that this one is entirely about singing your OWN vocal takes and building them into a sample from scratch, requested "a lot" by his audience. Structure: (1) write the chord progression WITH the vocal melody already in mind, choosing a key that suits his own voice; (2) record 3 layers — a main melody plus two harmonies, each sung with a deliberately different vocal tone/character, using wordless "ooh" vocalizing rather than lyrics (he states plainly he isn't a strong singer and this is his workaround); (3) chop/comp/tune the takes; (4) run each of the three layers through its OWN distinct processing chain (EQ → formant shifter → reverb, plus delay on the third layer) so they read as three different voices, not one voice tripled; (5) consolidate the finished stack to one track and pitch it around to explore; (6) a battery of vocal-flip texture tricks — reverse+pitch, time-stretch to double-speed, octave-up+half-time, and a compound reverse+heavy-delay+master-pitch-down finishing move. **Note on DAW:** loner works in FL Studio throughout (Playlist, Fruity Slicer/Stretch, "sound shifter" pitch tool) — this note keeps his UI/menu steps out and extracts only the transferable technique, per house convention (Nate runs Ableton). Settings are given fairly precisely for the processing chain (frequencies, formant amounts, delay feedback %) — one of the more numbers-heavy loner breakdowns on this topic. Low-to-moderate confidence on a couple of exact numbers where the auto-captions likely garble a plugin name (**"fruity picture"** → almost certainly FL Studio's built-in **Pitcher**/basic auto-tune-style plugin; **"little ultraboy"**/"ultraviolet" → the same recurring **[[Little AlterBoy]]** homophone documented across many other loner videos on this channel).

## Techniques

### Writing for the voice, before recording
- **Write the chord progression with the vocal melody already in mind, not as a separate later step.** While building the chords, sing rough melodic ideas in his head the whole time and check whether each chord voicing would work under a vocal — songwriting and topline are developed together, not sequentially.
- **Pick the key by vocal fit, not by the chord progression's own logic.** Tries the progression in different keys specifically to find one that suits his own vocal tone and range. Landed on **A minor** for this build — deliberately chosen because it uses only the white keys, reads as very melodic, and is easy to fit vocal runs into. General lesson: audition a progression in a few keys with your own voice before committing, the same way you'd audition a sample in different keys. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Chord Progressions]]

### Recording your own vocal (workaround for a non-singer)
- **Sing wordless "ooh" phrases instead of lyrics, specifically because it's easier to actually hit the notes.** States directly he's "not a singer" and can't execute "crazy melody runs" on lyrics, but singing on an open "ooh" vowel lets him land notes he otherwise couldn't — his default approach on every sample vocal, not just this one. A concrete self-recording complement to the vault's existing "ask a singer for oohs/ahs" sourcing tip. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Vocal Mic Technique]]
- **Track with the auto-tune/pitch plugin live in the signal path from the start**, on a simple single mic-input channel — not comped-then-tuned afterward, tuned WHILE singing.
- **Take one headphone ear off while tracking**, specifically so the natural (untuned) voice stays audible under the auto-tune in the cans — auto-tune in both ears can mask whether the actual pitch/delivery is landing. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]]
- **Keep only the strongest short phrase from a take and copy/paste it to rebuild the rest of the run**, rather than accepting or re-tracking the whole phrase. Here: liked only the first three notes of a take, kept that fragment, copy-pasted it into a second slot, and re-sang just the second half separately rather than a full retake. → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]]
- **Record 2–3 additional layers on top of the main melody, and change your actual vocal TONE/character while singing each one** — not just apply different processing afterward. Each harmony pass is sung with a deliberately different vocal color/approach so the layers already sound like different voices before any plugin touches them; the resulting texture contrast is explicitly called out as sounding better reversed/chopped later because of that baked-in variation. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Edit pass before any effects:** chop every layer so it's perfectly in time, and trim out all excess silence between phrases — done across all layers before any processing starts, and before layers are auditioned together (solo/mute the others while working on one).

### Three-layer processing chain — each layer gets a genuinely different treatment
Reinforces the channel's established rule (see [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]) that each stack layer needs its own distinguishing chain, with concrete numbers this time:

- **Main (lead) vocal:** EQ — cut around **200 Hz** and below (low end was carrying unwanted rumble/frequencies) → boost the high end a touch for presence/mix-standout ("never do too much, keep it simple") → **[[Little AlterBoy]]**-style formant shifter, formant **down ~5** (auto-captioned "minus five") — pitch stays the same (still A minor), only the formant/timbre drops, lowering the perceived pitch without touching the actual key; visually the vocal's main energy sits ~335 Hz before the shift, ~340 Hz after (a very small, subtle move, not a big character change) → a short, present reverb — the **"vocal distance sci-fi"** preset (already logged from a different loner video's 2000s R&B chain — third confirmed sighting of this preset name), pulled back to **~50% mix**, size nudged up slightly for depth without burying the vocal — "you still want it to be present." → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Reverb]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Second layer (first harmony):** same EQ + formant-shifter treatment, but formant only **down ~2.5** — noticeably less shift than the main vocal, so it reads as a distinct, less-processed character rather than a copy at a different level → a **vintage-style reverb** (not the same preset as the main), pushed to sit further back/"drown out a little more" than the main vocal's short reverb. **The differentiator here is depth of formant shift AND reverb type/wetness together, not one or the other.** → [[Little AlterBoy]], [[ValhallaVintageVerb]], [[Reverb]]
- **Third layer (second harmony):** EQ (low cut, same starting move as the other two) → a **spring reverb**, deliberately set big/heavy (contrast against the main vocal's restrained reverb) → a delay, kept **single** (not ping-pong or dual — a deliberate choice against his usual default) on **8th notes**, with the delay's own **built-in modulation/EQ nudged up slightly** and **drive pushed up a touch** for a bit of grit on the repeats. → [[Reverb]], [[Delay]]

### Consolidate and explore
- **Bounce all three finished layers down to one track before any further creative manipulation** ("consolidate them"), then freely pitch the whole stack up/down to audition new characters before deciding on further processing. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

### Vocal-flip texture tricks (post-consolidation)
- **Chop out a liked section, make it a unique sample, reverse it, and pitch the reversed copy down a whole semitone specifically to avoid it clashing in frequency with the (non-reversed) main vocal still playing.** The pitch-down isn't decorative — it's there so the reversed texture layer doesn't fight the main vocal's fundamental when both play together. Immediately reads as a new texture/sound from one simple move. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Pitch and Formant]]
- **Time-stretch the vocal to double-time (double playback speed)** for a distinct texture; optionally reverse the sped-up result afterward to hear a second variation.
- **Fix an over-fast reversed/double-timed chop with pitch-up-an-octave + half-time, rather than backing off the stretch amount.** When a double-timed-then-reversed section played "a little bit off," rather than undoing the double-time, pitch the chop **up an octave** (sound-shifter-style pitch tool) then apply **half-time** on top — the half-time slows and stretches the already-pitched material back into a usable groove instead of sounding rushed. A specific troubleshooting combo, not just "half-time everything." → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Compound finishing move: combine the reverse-chop technique with a super-heavy ping-pong delay, then pitch the WHOLE consolidated vocal down on the master as the final step.** Take the same first reversed section used above, add a delay set to **ping-pong**, **16th notes**, **mix pushed really high**, **feedback pushed to ~93%**, **drive up**, the delay's own **modulation brought DOWN** (opposite direction from the third-layer chain above), a light EQ pass, and let it repeat across the whole loop section. Finish with a pitch-shift plugin (**"sound shifter"**) inserted on the **master**, pitching the entire finished vocal stack down — stated as a standing preference: "I always find things sound better pitched down, especially [vocals]." → [[Delay]], [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]

# Jazz Harmony

Part of [[Music Theory]]. The hub for the **JAZ** strand.

Jazz was deliberately shallow in this vault until 2026-08-09 — *"dip a toe."* That was reversed: the strand is now built out properly. The reason is practical rather than stylistic. Almost everything that makes modern hip hop, neo-soul, R&B and jazzy pop sound the way it does is jazz harmony arriving second-hand, and the fastest way to write those chords deliberately instead of by accident is to learn where they came from.

You do not need to want to play jazz to need this.

---

## The four ideas the whole subject runs on

Jazz harmony looks complicated and is mostly four ideas applied repeatedly.

**1. Sevenths are the baseline, not an extension.** In common-practice harmony a triad is the default and a seventh is a colour. In jazz the seventh is the default — a "C chord" means Cmaj7 or C6 unless something says otherwise. Every rule below assumes four-note chords. → [[Seventh Chords]]

**2. The ii–V–I is the sentence.** Nearly all standard-repertoire harmony is ii–V–I units, chained, transposed and disguised. Learn to see them and most lead sheets stop looking arbitrary. → [[The ii-V-I]]

**3. Any chord can be replaced by another sharing its function.** Substitution is the engine of variety, and it follows rules rather than taste. → [[Chord Substitution]]

**4. The 3rd and the 7th carry the harmony; everything else is colour.** Two notes are enough to state a chord's identity and to voice-lead a whole progression. → [[Guide Tones]]

Get those four and the rest — reharmonization, bebop line construction, modal playing — are applications.

## Numerals in jazz vs. the classical style used elsewhere here

Worth flagging because the vault mixes them and it causes real confusion.

- **Classical style (used in most of this vault):** case shows quality. `ii` is minor, `V` is major, `vii°` is diminished. See [[Chord Naming by Context]].
- **Jazz/Berklee style:** always uppercase, quality shown by suffix. `IIm7`, `V7`, `Imaj7`, `♭VII7`.

Both name the same chords. Jazz style wins on lead sheets because chord qualities get altered constantly and case stops being able to carry the information — a `V7♯9♭13` has no lowercase equivalent.

**This vault uses classical style in theory notes and jazz style when quoting a jazz source.** Intervals decide quality, not numeral case.

## What "comprehensive" means here, and what it doesn't

Now covered properly: functional harmony, substitution, voice leading, reharmonization, the two standard forms, modal playing, and the melodic vocabulary.

Deliberately **not** covered, because they're performance disciplines rather than writing tools and nothing in the school needs them:

- Solo transcription practice as a discipline (the *technique* is in [[Transcribing and Playing by Ear]])
- Chord-tone-targeting practice drills over changes as a performance skill (the *technique* is in [[Chord-Tone Soloing]])
- Big-band arranging and section writing
- Walking bass line construction as a bass-player skill — [[Bassline Writing]] covers what's needed for writing
- Free jazz and post-1960 avant-garde — [[20th-Century Harmony]] covers the adjacent material

If any of those become relevant, they're additive and nothing here blocks them.

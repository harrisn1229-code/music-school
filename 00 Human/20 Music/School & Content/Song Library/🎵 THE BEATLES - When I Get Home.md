# When I Get Home — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** refrain-first, and it also has a bridge — compare "Tell Me Why". Refrain 8 bars (two parallel 2-bar phrases balanced by one 4-bar phrase) · Verse 8 · Refrain · Verse · Refrain · Bridge (**10 bars**, the only non-square section, five 2-bar phrases grouping 2 + 3) · Verse · Refrain · Outro.

The key scheme is the event. *A Hard Day's Night* is full of songs using either a relative or a parallel major/minor shift; **this is the only one that uses both**. The refrain starts in A major and ends in A minor, and that A minor is then the relative minor of the C major that owns the verses and bridge ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]], [[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The hinge is a chord that puns: G is **♭VII at home and V of C** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]), so the pivot is inaudible as machinery. Harmonic rhythm is deliberately uneventful for the first half of the refrain so those moves land as events.

All of it runs on barely six chords, and the sense of where home is never settles — enough that Pollack thinks the hard ending on C sounds forced and a fade would have served better. The verse's second chord is written IV but is more properly a **ii⁶₅**, a first-inversion D7 with the melody holding D♮ against a double-stopped bass fifth.

The refrain's rhythmic hook is a hard syncopation on the last eighth of the bar **with no demarcation of the next downbeat at all**, which is what makes it wrench ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]).

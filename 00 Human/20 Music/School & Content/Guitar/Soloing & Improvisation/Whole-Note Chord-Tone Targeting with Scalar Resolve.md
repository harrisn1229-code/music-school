# Whole-Note Chord-Tone Targeting with Scalar Resolve

Brandon D'Eon's two-stage drill for building confident chord-tone targeting, deliberately unglamorous (sustained whole notes) so the ear and hand fully register each chord's sound before any scalar material is layered on top. Compare [[Chord-Tone Soloing]] (Jared's jazz-vocabulary practice ladder, built around a fast turnaround and chromatic approach tones) — this drill is a simpler, slower on-ramp aimed at basic chord-tone confidence rather than bebop decoration, and works well as a prerequisite before that page's ladder.

## Stage 1 — Whole-note thirds

Over a chosen chord progression (worked example: Am–C–Em–D), play only the **3rd of each chord** as a sustained whole note through that chord's full bar. **Thirds specifically, not roots** — most beginner improvisers default to roots by habit, so targeting thirds forces genuinely new territory and builds a fuller picture of each chord's color. This stage is a hard gate: don't move to Stage 2 until it's reliable without fail.

## Stage 2 — Chord-tone-then-scalar, resolving back

Once Stage 1 is solid, halve the chord-tone duration: hold the chord tone for 2 beats, then fill the remaining 2 beats of the bar with scalar movement (whichever scale/mode fits the chord — e.g. Dorian or the matching minor pentatonic). **The scalar material must resolve back to a chord tone at the end of its 2 beats** — it isn't free noodling, it's controlled motion away from and back to a known-good landing point.

## Notes on scope

The full version of this drill repeats both stages across every root (not just thirds — roots and fifths too) and in every fretboard position, not the single worked example above. Treated properly, it's a real time investment (the source estimates roughly a month per drill done thoroughly) rather than a one-session exercise.

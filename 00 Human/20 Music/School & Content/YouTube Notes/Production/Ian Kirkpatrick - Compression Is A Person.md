# Ian Kirkpatrick — Compression Is A Person

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oPSBLf-HpTI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oPSBLf-HpTI)

## Synopsis

Ian's beginner-friendly framing for understanding a compressor: **personify it.** The compressor is a person you're giving instructions to — each control answers a question about *when* and *how* it acts. Then he shows a **manual/fake compressor** done with volume automation.

## What's taught

- **Personify the compressor** — every knob is an instruction to a person:
  - **Threshold** = "at what point do you want me to do something?" (what level triggers me — "when it's *this* loud").
  - **Attack** = "how fast do you want me to do it once I'm triggered — right away, or get up slowly and walk over?"
  - **Ratio** = "how *much* of that thing do you want me to do — a little or a lot?" (2:1 = half).
  - **Release** = "how fast do you want me to relax after I've done it — chill right away or back off slowly?"
  - **Output/makeup** = "how loud you want me to be after." → [[Compression]], [[Compression Staging]]
- **Manual/fake compression with volume automation** — he printed a snap from **Battery**, took the latter half, and **dropped the volume by 8 dB** so the tail disappears — "I'm doing a manual compressor." The volume drop = the ratio/amount; if the volume *returned* to original, that ramp = the release time. → [[Automation]], [[Sidechaining]], [[Compression]]
- **Automating peak reduction** — sometimes he automates the compressor's peak reduction across a part instead of a static setting. → [[Automation]]

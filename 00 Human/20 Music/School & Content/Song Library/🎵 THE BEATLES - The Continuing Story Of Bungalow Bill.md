# The Continuing Story Of Bungalow Bill — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Refrain (**11 bars**, ABA′B′, the odd length coming from a truncated second phrase) · Verse (**12 bars**, slower tempo, AAB, blues-shaped without being a blues) · Refrain · Refrain · Outro (roughly 4.8 refrain cycles, cut off by the lead-in to the next track). Refrain-first and refrain-heavy, which is what makes it read as a room full of people rather than a studio take.

A folk-ballad singalong whose surface is childishly simple and whose key scheme is anything but: **three keys — C major, A major and A minor — locked together because A minor is the relative minor of one and the parallel minor of the other** ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]). The refrain alone contains two of them; the G ending its second phrase is the hinge, V at home and **♭VII in A**, which makes the move up readable as a pivot rather than a bare jump ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]).

**Minor iv in a major key** is the recurring device, a fifties rock cliché Lennon kept using: sour on first appearance, and in the third phrase the thing that opens the door to the minor mode ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The verse's last two bars re-read as vi – iv in C, which is how it hands back to the refrain, and its E-to-G move produces the standing Beatles G♯/G♮ cross-relation.

Best arrangement lesson: the **bassoon that carries the song out is already inside the ensemble from the second outro repeat**, well before it is exposed.

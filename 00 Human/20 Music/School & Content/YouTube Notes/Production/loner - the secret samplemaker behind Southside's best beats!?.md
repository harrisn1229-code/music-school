# the secret samplemaker behind Southside's best beats!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WLOHe9CJChY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WLOHe9CJChY)

## Synopsis

loner reverse-engineers the sample-based side of Southside's beats — specifically the work of **Jake One**, credited as a co-producer/samplemaker behind several key Future/Travis Scott records Southside is known for. Three songs anchor the breakdown: *Percocet and Stripper Joint* (Future) for the core vintage-sample-flip formula (a soft, dark minor-chord Rhodes progression pushed through a vintage-coloring chain, layered with dry guitar accents, synth accents, and a synth-brass B section over Southside's drums), *A Man* (Travis Scott) for Jake One's signature bright, unorthodox Oberheim SEM-style synth sound (attack-automated chord stabs processed through a wah, layered with thicker pulsating pads and a soft sine background melody), and *Looking Exotic* (Future) for a stabby bass + catchy lead + reversed Splice vocal combination under Southside's drum programming (hi-hat rolls placed to complement other hits, a Spinz 808 with distortion). The throughline across all three: Jake One's samples read as "vintage" and "unique" mostly because of *processing* choices (an unintentional-lowpass-via-denoiser trick, single-reverb vintage character, attack automation for chord variety, reversed samples for texture) layered on top of fairly simple source material (a few Rhodes chords, a couple of synth chords) rather than exotic instruments alone. Most valuable here: the exact 4-stage vintage-sample processing chain (valve compressor → EQ → room reverb → noise-reduction-as-unintentional-lowpass), the "wah on a synth, not just guitar" transposition trick borrowed from a different Jake One production (*Out of Time*), and the reminder that Southside's drum programming (layered snare+clap, reverb'd hats, rolls placed to complement other hits, a distorted 808) is doing real work underneath the sample layer.

**Confidence note:** this is a fast-cut, music-heavy breakdown and the auto-captions garble several plugin/synth names — "Bruce Free" (near-certainly **Brusfri**, the Klevgrand noise-reduction plugin, matching the described "removes noise... unintentionally acts as a low-pass filter" behavior exactly), "the CS-50 to Russ's Sheep" (unresolved — likely two synth/plugin names run together; not confidently identified, left qualitative rather than guessed), and "Spinz 808" (matches a name already flagged from a separate loner video's *Rodeo* breakdown — "Spin808"/"Spinz808" — same unresolved 808/synth-bass source, now cross-confirmed across two videos). Technique descriptions themselves (signal chain order, drum layering, automation move) are clear; it's specifically plugin/preset proper nouns that garble.

## Techniques

**Percocet and Stripper Joint (Future) — the vintage sample-flip chain:**
- **Dark chord voicing: all-minor chords, walking down the scale.** The entire dark, soft Rhodes vibe starts at the voicing level before any processing — minor chords only, moved stepwise down the scale rather than a major/minor-mixed progression. → [[Chord Progressions]]
- **The 4-stage vintage-sample processing chain, in order: valve compressor → EQ → room reverb → noise-reduction-as-unintentional-lowpass.** (1) A valve/tube compressor for the first pass of dynamic + harmonic coloring — owned equivalent: [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII]] (opto/tube compressor, "rides the whole phrase like a fader"). (2) An EQ — owned: [[EQ Eight]]. (3) A room reverb, explicitly for a "played next door" effect — a small, contained, slightly-muffled space rather than a hall — owned equivalent: [[UAD RealVerb-Pro]] (room-modeling algorithmic reverb). (4) A denoiser (captioned "Bruce Free," near-certainly **Brusfri** by Klevgrand) run not for its intended job but for its side effect: it "unintentionally acts as a low-pass filter," rolling off top end and adding vintage vibe — closest owned trick for the same unintended-filter behavior: [[RX Guitar De-noise]] (denoise modules reliably darken a source's top end as a byproduct of noise removal; lean into that rather than fight it). *(general starting point on exact settings — none given on screen)* → [[Compression]], [[Reverb]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Dry, low-octave guitar accent, minimally processed** — played low, deliberately dry, then only a guitar amp + EQ added (no heavy effects chain) to keep it feeling live/room-recorded rather than sample-processed like the Rhodes. Owned amp-sim equivalent: [[Morgan Amps Suite]]. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A second, brighter guitar layered in for contrast, run through a fuller chain: preamp → guitar amp → spring reverb → EQ.** Two guitar parts doing different jobs — the dry low one for foundation, this one purely for high-end contrast — rather than one guitar part covering the whole register. Owned equivalents: preamp stage → [[UAD Century Tube Channel Strip]]; amp → [[Morgan Amps Suite]]; **spring reverb specifically** → [[Ghz DC19 3]] (has a built-in spring reverb + a dedicated "Spring Hold" performance macro); EQ → [[EQ Eight]]. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A synth accent layered under the guitars** on the main sample, ahead of the B-section synth work covered below. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **B-section arrangement: high synth lead (attention-grabber) + synth brass underneath + a bass guitar tying it together, with extra melodic notes added at the end for texture.** A clear per-role stack for the second half of the sample rather than one element carrying the whole section — lead grabs the ear, brass fills the mid/low-mid, bass guitar (not a synth bass) ties the harmony and adds late melodic movement. → [[Bass Design]], [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Melody Writing]]

**Southside's drums (on the Percocet-style beat):**
- **Snare + clap layered together as one hit**, not chosen between — standard Southside/trap-drum layering discipline. → [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Reverb specifically on the hi-hats** as a recurring Southside signature — named example: *Married to the Game*. Not a send-only move necessarily; treat as either the hats' own insert or a dedicated hat-reverb send depending on the session. → [[2 Drums Hats Group]], [[Reverb]]

**A Man (Travis Scott) — Jake One's signature synth sound:**
- **Oberheim SEM as the named source synth for Jake One's "bright, unorthodox" sound that's meant to stand out from the mix**, not blend in — a deliberate ear-candy/lead-character choice, not a pad/bed choice. No dedicated SEM emulation owned; closest owned Oberheim-brand synth character: [[Oberheim OB-Xa]] (best-match, not a true SEM substitute — the SEM is a simpler duophonic module, the OB-Xa a full polysynth — added to [[Vintage Gear Worklist]] as its own line since the SEM isn't yet tracked). → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Automate the attack time per-chord for stab-length variety** — rather than one fixed attack setting for the whole chord progression, automating attack made some chords play as short stabs and others sustain longer, adding rhythmic/textural interest without changing notes or voicing. A reusable, tool-agnostic move: this is an automation-lane trick, not a plugin-specific one. → [[Automation]], [[Melody Writing]]
- **"Wah is not just a guitar effect" — reused from a different Jake One production (*Out of Time*, Future) where guitar chords are run through wah processing, transposed onto a synth part here.** loner explicitly calls out that there's no reason the same processing has to stay on the instrument it was invented for. Applied via a wah preset in [[Guitar Rig 7 FX]] (not owned — runs in demo; still the best-match tool for this exact "guitar-style wah on a synth" move) on the SEM-style chord part. → [[Modulation]]
- **A second, thicker layer of pulsating chords added underneath the wah'd lead** to fill out the frequency content the lead alone doesn't cover — source cited in captions as "the CS-50 to Russ's Sheep," not confidently identified (likely two synth/plugin names garbled together by auto-captions) — left as a qualitative technique (stack a second, more sustained/pulsating pad under a wah'd lead for fullness) rather than guessed at as a specific product. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Arturia CS-80 V4, initial stabby attack manually chopped out** for a second textural layer under the SEM-style lead — proof that a preset doesn't have to be used as-authored; editing out an unwanted transient turned a stabby patch into a smoother texture bed. Arturia CS-80 V4 already flagged unowned on [[Plugin Wishlist]] from an earlier Mike Dean/*Astroworld* breakdown — same plugin, second independent confirmation of real use. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A very soft, quiet background melody built from a plain sine wave** — the simplest possible synth source used deliberately for a low-key, barely-there layer rather than a designed patch; owned sine sources: [[Operator]] or [[Analog]]. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]

**Looking Exotic (Future):**
- **A short, stabby bass that opens/anchors the sample** — a percussive, non-sustained bass character chosen to "bring the sample to life" rather than a held sub tone. → [[Bass Design]]
- **A catchy, attention-grabbing lead** layered against the stabby bass — described explicitly as giving "the listener something to grab" onto, i.e. written as the sample's hook element. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **A melodic vocal, sourced from Splice and reversed, layered in deliberately to contrast the harsh synth elements** — a found-vocal-phrase texture move: pull a melodic vocal one-shot/phrase from [[Splice Sounds]], reverse it, and use the resulting swelled, backwards-attack shape as a softening counterweight to harsher synth layers rather than as a lead vocal. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Hi-hat rolls placed deliberately to complement the other drum hits, not just fill time** — plus low-pitched hat notes carried over from the *A Man* palette, reused here. The emphasis is on roll *placement* being intentional/arranged around the rest of the kit rather than a generic fill pattern. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **An open hat to finish the hat pattern**, then **a Spinz 808 with added distortion** as the closing drum element, deliberately distorted "to make it sound a little bit different" from a plain 808. Name matches an unresolved plugin/source already flagged from a separate *Rodeo* breakdown ("Spin808"/"Spinz808") — cross-confirmed as a real, recurring name across two different videos, still not confidently identified. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

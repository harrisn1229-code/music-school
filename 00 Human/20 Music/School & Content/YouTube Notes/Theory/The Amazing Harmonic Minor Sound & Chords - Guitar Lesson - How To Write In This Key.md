# The Amazing Harmonic Minor Sound & Chords - Guitar Lesson - How To Write In This Key

## Synopsis

A full walkthrough of harmonic minor's diatonic chords, compared side-by-side against natural minor's, plus two worked cadences for actually writing in the sound. Chord qualities match the vault's existing [[Harmonic Minor Modes]] table exactly (AmMaj7 - Bm7♭5 - Cmaj7♯5 - Dm7 - E7 - Fmaj7 - G♯dim7 for A harmonic minor) — this video's contribution is a practical "which chord signals you need this scale" tip and two ready-to-use cadences.

## What's taught

- **Chord-by-chord derivation, natural minor vs. harmonic minor, side by side** — stacking 3rds/5ths/7ths from each scale degree, comparing A natural minor's seven diatonic 7th chords against A harmonic minor's, showing exactly which chords change (i, ♭III, V, vii°) and which stay identical (ii, iv, ♭VI) since they don't contain the raised 7th.
- **The tonic minor-major-7 chord (AmMaj7) as the practical "you need harmonic minor here" signal.** Its major 7th (G♯) is the exact note that clashes if a soloist defaults to natural minor (playing the plain minor 7th, G, against it) — demonstrated as an audibly bad clash. A confirming, chord-first framing of the same characteristic-note-matching principle already logged elsewhere in this batch from other angles.
- **Two ready-to-use harmonic minor cadences, with specific voicings:**
  - **i - iv - V - i**: AmMaj7 → Dm7 → E7 → back to AmMaj7 (voiced from the 5th fret, low E string root).
  - **ii - V - i** (the harmonic-minor analog of the classic jazz turnaround): Bm7♭5 → E7 → AmMaj7.
- **Compositional framing: there is no independent "harmonic minor key"** — you write in the relative natural minor key and borrow specific chords from its harmonic minor collection where the color is wanted, rather than treating harmonic minor as a wholesale key replacement. (Matches [[Harmonic Minor Modes]]'s existing "no true key of harmonic minor, you borrow" framing.)

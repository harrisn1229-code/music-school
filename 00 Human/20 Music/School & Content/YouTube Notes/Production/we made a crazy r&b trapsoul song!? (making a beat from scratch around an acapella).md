# we made a crazy r&b trapsoul song!? (making a beat from scratch around an acapella)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=39aHjEJ5-FU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=39aHjEJ5-FU)

## Synopsis

A short, fast-moving loner build (FL Studio) that flips the usual sample-flip workflow: instead of chopping a found sample, he works around a full a cappella vocal (from singer Cali Claire, posted to Instagram/Dropbox specifically for producers to use), writing an R&B/trapsoul chord bed and full arrangement underneath it from scratch. The throughline is his standing personal-key habit — pitch whatever he's working with up to C minor (the scale he knows best), write freely, then transpose the whole thing back down to the source's original pitch afterward — already documented elsewhere on this vault as a repeatable go-to. From there it's a fast layering pass: Rhodes/keys chords (with a diminished-chord ending flourish for extra R&B color) run through a J37-style tape → filter/pad-ifying ShaperBox stage → spring reverb chain; a CS80 one-shot bass from his own "Nami's kit" one-shot bank, pulsed with a ShaperBox volume preset; a glittery Analog Lab Impulse-bank (Louis of Loophole's bank) texture layer for high-end sparkle, closed out with heavy reverb + a low cut + a high-cut filter; a filtered/half-timed Only Ones (Onlyones) perc loop; a doubling Teletone Audio "Postcard Piano" layer carrying the same chords as the Rhodes, thickened with a Reels "Excited Tape" preset and a Portal instance for extra character, plus a few original piano licks; and drums closed out with a low, dark open hat modeled on Bryson Tiller's "Next to You," a kick, and an 808 that reuses the earlier bass's note choices with a varied pattern. Nothing here is deeply novel processing-wise (most of the individual moves are already tracked elsewhere from this same channel), but it's a clean, compact worked example of the whole "build a beat around someone else's a cappella" workflow start to finish in under 9 minutes.

## Techniques

- **Work around a found a cappella instead of chopping a sample.** Source: singer Cali Claire's a cappella, posted publicly (Instagram + a Dropbox link) specifically so producers could build around it — a legitimate free-vocal source distinct from an uncleared sample. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Personal-key habit: pitch the source up to C minor (the key he knows best on piano), write freely over it, then transpose the whole thing back down to the source's original pitch when done.** Reused, standing workflow — matches the "learn one scale really well" habit already tracked on [[Melody Writing]] from an earlier video, here applied to a vocal source rather than a sample. Practical steps: drop the a cappella into an 8-bar pattern, drag it across the full loop, then freestyle chords on the keys against it.
- **Diminished-chord ending flourish for extra R&B flavor.** After landing on a chord idea that "sounds R&B-ish," the loop's final chord is swapped for a diminished voicing — **B, D, F, with B doubled as the bass note** — specifically to push the ending toward a more R&B-specific color rather than resolving plainly. → [[Diminished Chords]], [[Chord Progressions]]
- **Rhodes/keys chain: J37-style tape ("my favorite preset," described as a "metallic drum loop" character, said to work especially well on Rhodes) → ShaperBox filter preset (rolls off the high end, turns the part into more of a pad) → spring reverb, for an airy/ambient/vintage character.** Owned-lane mapping: J37 → [[Kramer Tape]]; ShaperBox filter → [[Shaper]]; spring reverb → [[Ghz DC19 3]]. → [[BED Slot]]
- **Bass: a CS80 one-shot from his own "Nami's kit" one-shot bank — a repeat go-to bass source — pitched up (brought "up by two"), the underlying chord's root note (C) dropped since the sample tracked too high otherwise.** Then a ShaperBox **volume preset** added so the bass "pulses a bit more" instead of sitting flat/present — the closing chord's position nudged slightly afterward because the pulse made the ending feel too jolty/in-your-face. → [[BASS Slot]], [[Shaper]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Analog Lab Impulse bank (credited to "Louis and XY" — Louis of Loophole — "definitely my favorite Analog Lab bank right now") for a glittery high-end texture layer.** Kept extremely minimal — just the last three notes of the progression, played once, purely for a textural high-end element rather than a full melodic part. Chain: heavy reverb (cut the low end out of the reverb return) → a filtering plug-in described as "Good Hertz Low High" to cut some of the remaining high end. Owned-lane mapping: Goodhertz Low/Hi filter → [[Ghz Loh1 3]]. → [[AIR Slot]], [[TEXTURE Slot]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Perc loop from "Ones" kits ("obviously they're the best perc loops")** — filtered and **half-timed**, plus an EQ **band-pass, narrow** to carve it into its own slot in the mix. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[EQ]]
- **Doubling piano layer: the exact same chords copied from the Rhodes part onto a Teletone Audio "Postcard Piano" instrument (third confirmed use of this VST on the vault), "to give it a bit more body."** Processed with a Reels-style tape device on its **"Excited Tape" preset** (harshness pushed up, flutter pushed up) — the same named preset already tracked from other loner videos on [[Plugin Wishlist]] — then a few original piano licks/riffs layered in for interest, and finally **Portal** added to the piano specifically "to make it sound super interesting, unique." *(No owned equivalent tracked for Postcard Piano — see [[Plugin Wishlist]].)* → [[Kramer Tape]], [[Portal]]
- **Hi-hat sound reference: "a really low one, like Next to You by Bryson Tiller"** — reaches for a specific song as the target hi-hat character (low/dark, open) rather than describing settings; paired with a plain kick afterward. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **808: reuses the bass part's note choices from earlier in the build, with the rhythmic pattern varied** rather than composing a fresh 808 line from scratch.

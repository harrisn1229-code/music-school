# Kate Bush's unique chord progression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcWMDoIr1F4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcWMDoIr1F4)

## Synopsis

Analysis of "Wuthering Heights"' unusual verse progression — A–F–E–C♯, a loop of four chromatic-mediant relationships in a row — plus how a fourth-relationship pivot chord (A♭) manages to sound resolved despite not belonging to the song's key.

## Techniques

- **A full loop built entirely from chromatic mediants**: A→F (major 3rd apart, chromatic mediant), E→C♯ (minor 3rd apart, chromatic mediant), and the loop-closing C♯→A is itself also a third apart — every single chord change in the progression is a chromatic mediant relationship, not just an occasional color move. The shared-note-but-not-functionally-related quality of chromatic mediants throughout is what gives the progression its "uncanny, spooky" vibe — described as a device more common in film scoring and classical music than pop.
- **How an out-of-key chord can still sound resolved: the 4th/5th-motion override.** After several loops, the progression adds an A♭ chord — not in the key of A major at all. It works because the preceding chord (D♭, i.e. C♯ respelled) moves to A♭ by a **perfect 4th**, and 4th/5th root motion is such a strong, familiar relationship that it can make an otherwise out-of-key chord land as resolved regardless of whether it's diatonic — the interval relationship overrides the key context.
- **A key change into the pre-chorus that avoids stating its own tonic**: the pre-chorus shifts to B♭ minor but never actually plays a B♭ minor chord — instead cycling through E♭m7 (iv), G♭ (♭VI), Fsus4 (V) — establishing the new key entirely through surrounding chords rather than the tonic itself (a variant of the tonic-avoidance technique covered elsewhere in this batch).
- **Metrically ambiguous depending on how you count tempo**: the song can be read as either alternating 5/4 and 7/4 bars (slower tempo count) or a 4/4-with-occasional-2/4 pattern (faster tempo count) — the same underlying rhythm, two valid time-signature readings depending on which pulse you anchor to.

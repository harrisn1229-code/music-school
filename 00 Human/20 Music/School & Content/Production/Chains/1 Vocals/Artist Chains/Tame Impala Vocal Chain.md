# Tame Impala Vocal Chain

**Reference/vibe:** soft, dreamy, dissolved into the track — a falsetto-leaning voice **layered until Parker "can't recognize the sound of his own voice,"** phased and echoed, sitting 2–4 dB lower than a pop vocal ("the instruments are brutal, the voice is really soft"). Another instrument, not the focus.
**Signal source:** relaxed, breathy delivery — simple melodies, no runs. Parker tracks on a dynamic (MD-421); your [[Shure SM57]] is the closer match, [[Neumann TLM 102]] for the airier lane.

## Step 1 — Record (UAD Console)
1. **UAD UA 610-B (Unison)** — soft tube, gentle level (the round choice over the 1073 here)
2. **UAD Teletronix LA-2A** — 2–3 dB smooth
3. **UAD Pultec EQP-1A** — bypass
- **The real step 1 is takes:** 4–8 unison layers of the same line, soft delivery — the blur between them IS the sound. Pitch-shifted doubles (octave/5th at low level) for thickness without "chipmunk."

## Chain overview
Thin it → level it → color it → smear it. His voice reads *thin on purpose* — don't fatten it; the lushness comes from layers + effects, not body.

## Step 2 — Process (Ableton)

### 1. [[Pro-Q 4]] — Thin and seat
- **HPF up to ~150 Hz** (yes, that high) · scoop low mids −2 to −3 dB @ ~350 Hz · gentle presence, no aggressive air
- **Why:** the vocal cuts by being *narrow*, floating above the brutal instruments rather than competing.

### 2. [[Pro-C 2]] — Subtle level
- 3:1 · 2–4 dB — even, unremarkable, invisible.

### 3. [[Saturn 2]] — Warm blur
- Tape, low drive, mix ~25% — softens consonants into the wash.

### 4. [[PhaserFlanger]] or [[ValhallaSpaceModulator]] — The psych movement
- Slow phaser, low depth, ~20–30% mix — constant gentle sweep. Automate deeper per section/line ("Mind Mischief" moments).

## Step 3 — Effects (sends — this is most of the sound)
- **Tape echo:** [[EchoBoy]] Space Echo/Tape mode, 1/8 or dotted-1/8, feedback 25–40%, wobble/saturation ON, send generous — echoes louder relative to the dry than pop would allow.
- **Long modulated verb:** [[ValhallaVintageVerb]] (70s mode, 2.5–4 s, modulation high) — the dissolve. Send hot; duck it under the dry only if lyrics vanish entirely.
- [[Width Send]] on the layered stack; slap for the drier RnB-leaning cuts ("The Less I Know the Better" verses).
- **Automate the delay time knob** for detune-warble transitions (the hardware move).

## Vocal arrangement

| Section | Lead | Behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Verse | **no lead** — 4–8 unison layers blended equal | the layers are the vocal |
| Chorus | the same stack, phaser deepened | pitched-shifted octave/5th layers at low level |
| Transition | delay-time automation, detune-warble | — |

**The move — there is no lead take.** Parker layers until he can't recognize his own voice, blended so no single take is "the one." That's a structural choice, not a mixing one: it's why the vocal can sit 2–4 dB under a pop vocal and still read.

**Automate the effects per line, not per section.** Phaser depth and delay feedback move on individual words ("Mind Mischief"), which gives sectional contrast without touching the arrangement — the same timbre-over-density habit that runs through [[Psych-Pop (Tame Impala)]] § Arrangement.

**Stack count: 4–8, flat across the song.**

## Notes / variations
- **Mix placement is the trick most people miss:** tuck the vocal down until it *just* reads, glue it to the band with the shared verb — resist the pop instinct to feature it.
- **Currents/Slow Rush (cleaner):** less phaser, more chorus width, verb slightly shorter.
- **Lonerism (rawer):** more Saturn drive, echo feedback higher, everything wetter.
- **Deadbeat (2025) — Parker's own account, first-party, contrasts with the default recipe above.** Tracking: [[Shure SM7]], held in hand while performing (chose it over a U47 clone specifically because he could hold and move with it while singing); tracked through a **dbx 165** compressor live. Post: "just compression and EQ… quite stock standard" — the character lives in the effects bus, not the tracking chain. **Doubling is a per-song call, not the 4–8-layer default:** on "My Old Ways" the main hook is deliberately left **single/undoubled** (to read as a sampled vocal), verses are the usual double-track. **Hard-panned whisper layer:** an extra whispery pass, panned hard L/R, heavily filtered (little bass or treble, midrange only) and heavily compressed — a near-inaudible "ASMR-y" width element distinct from the doubles (4 vocal elements total: 2 center doubles + 2 hard-panned whispers). Effects: [[EchoBoy]] (switched to from stock Ableton delay specifically for its stereo image, replacing *Currents*/*Slow Rush*-era stock echo), [[ValhallaRoom]] + Ableton's stock convolution Spring reverb on most tracks, [[Decapitator]] for light grit on some songs (e.g. "Dracula"). → [[TN 188 Tame Impala - Full Video Episode (Part 1)]] (Tape Notes)
- **Justice collab (Neverender, Hyperdrama, 2024) — the opposite of this chain's default: simple, live, single-take, minimally processed.** "The vocals are very simple. We recorded them in Conway in Los Angeles. And as soon as we were all together back in the room, and he was in the couch holding the mic like this, he sounded like an angel — he was singing everything perfectly." No 4–8 unison layers, no described phaser/echo/verb wash — Justice frame the whole session around being in one room and "rebounding on each other's musical proposition," not around the usual dissolve-into-the-track treatment. *(No mic, chain, or effects details given — logged as a contrasting reference point, not a settings recipe.)* → [[Vocal Mic Technique]] — Justice x Kevin Parker (Mix with the Masters), [Behind the Track｜'Neverender' by Justice & Tame Impala](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=emP9EWcqss4)

# 8 Awesome types of 3 note Chord Voicings and How to use them

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lG0TDGOvDho)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lG0TDGOvDho)

## Synopsis

Jens Larsen demonstrates 8 categories of 3-note jazz voicings by playing one full chorus of comping over "Solar," explaining each voicing type as it occurs. Most of the underlying theory (shell voicings, upper-structure triads, quartal harmony, drop-voicing-style extension swaps) is already deeply covered in this vault via other sources — the value here is mainly the worked "Solar" example threading them together and two specific mechanics not previously documented: an incomplete **1-5-7 shell voicing** (no 3rd) used strictly as an upper structure, and **triad inversions as free extra voicings** once one triad-substitution works for a chord.

## What's taught

- **8 voicing types demonstrated over Solar's changes:** basic shell voicings (also usable as upper structures, not just low-register comping); upper-structure triads (a 7th chord with its root omitted becomes the triad on its 3rd — G minor 7 minus G = B♭ major triad); quartal harmony (stacked 4ths, moved stepwise through a scale, deliberately incomplete/ambiguous voicings that still read clearly in context); a **1-5-7 shell voicing without a 3rd** (e.g. A♭-E♭-G, root/5th/7th only) — doesn't work as a standalone voicing since it can't spell major/minor, but works well as an upper structure over a different root (played over F, it reads as F minor 9 without stating F); sus4 triads substituted for a chord's usual minor-triad upper structure for added color (a G sus4 triad over E♭ major 7 instead of the expected G minor triad, yielding a 13-7-3 color); and triad-derived add9 voicings built the same way a drop-2 voicing gets an extension — take a known triad, swap the root for the 9th a whole step above.
- **Triad inversions multiply usable voicings for free.** Once one triad substitution is found to work over a chord (e.g. a B♭ diminished triad over C7♭9), its inversions are automatically usable too — three related voicings from finding one relationship, directly chainable into 2-5 reharmonizations.
- **Practice method, repeated for every type:** take the voicing type through the relevant scale (the tune's own scale at that point, e.g. C melodic minor, F major, D♭ major) to build a full set of usable shapes rather than memorizing one instance.
- **A "which root am I not playing" mental discipline for incomplete/upper-structure voicings** — when practicing a rootless or 1-5-7-type shape through a scale, deliberately think the unplayed root name at each step, not just the shape, so the voicing stays tied to real harmonic function rather than becoming an abstract shape.

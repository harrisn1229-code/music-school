## Synopsis

A joke-framed but genuinely useful minute on **Em11**. The description carries the actual theory and is worth reading before watching: a full E minor 11 is 1–♭3–5–♭7–9–11, six notes; the guitar has six strings and jazz voicings are typically four notes or fewer, so the 9 gets dropped and the chord is *still* Em11 — not, he argues at length, "Em7(add11)". The point underneath the joke is that extended-chord names describe the highest extension reached, not a requirement to voice every degree below it.

## What to watch for

- **Which degrees he keeps.** With the 9 dropped you are hearing 1–♭3–5–♭7–11; note which string carries the 11 and whether the root is even present.
- **Why it is 'the easiest chord ever'** — Em11 has an open-position voicing that is close to no fingers at all. Watch for that shape specifically, since it is the payoff of the joke.
- **The naming argument itself**, which is real theory: [[Chord Naming by Context]] and [[Extended Chords]] both cover why an extension name does not obligate every lower degree.
- **Then move it.** The open shape is only free in E — see what it costs to take it up the neck.

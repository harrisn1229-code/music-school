# Songs that only use one chord

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xrLZ1F69rsM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xrLZ1F69rsM)

## Synopsis

A genre survey of songs (and one genre, hip-hop, pushed to a "zero-chord" extreme) built on a single static harmony — the case that sidelining chord progressions entirely can make room for melody, rhythm, texture, and tone to carry a song instead. Closes with a general songwriting prompt: deliberately try a drone/one-chord approach as a break from always defaulting to chord-progression-first writing.

## Techniques

- **Early-2000s pop vamps**: "Get the Party Started" (Pink) — B minor throughout. "Music" (Madonna) — G minor throughout. "Slow" (Kylie Minogue) — B♭minor throughout.
- **Funk (rhythm/groove-focused, harmony deliberately backgrounded)**: "Chain of Fools" (Aretha Franklin) — Cm7 for virtually its whole length after a brief C7 intro. "Papa Was a Rollin' Stone" (The Temptations) — B♭m7 for its entire 12-minute duration.
- **Indian classical music (the concept of "chord progression" doesn't really apply)**: harmony is a drone (typically a tambura) under an improvised raga-based melody (vocal or sitar) — described as a style where analyzing it in Western chord terms is "missing the point." **George Harrison's Indian-inspired Beatles songs**: "Within You Without You" — drones on C, vocal melody explores C Mixolydian above it. "Tomorrow Never Knows" — drones on C major, though a B♭ major chord is occasionally superimposed via tape manipulation, arguably introducing a debatable "chord change." "Love You To" — drones on C♯ minor.
- **Blues (originally an a cappella tradition — literally no chords to begin with)**: early blues often stays on one chord, elaborated with guitar riffs rather than chord changes. Later blues-descended one-chord songs: "Run Through the Jungle" (CCR) — a D minor blues riff. "Loser" (Beck) — built almost entirely on one blues riff.
- **Bob Dylan, cited as a repeat user of this style**: "Political World" (F minor), "Tin Angel" (B♭ minor), "It's All Good" (G minor) — at least three one-chord Dylan songs named directly. Also: "Coconut" (Harry Nilsson) — built on a repeating C7.
- **Hip-hop, pushed to a "zero-chord song" extreme**: rap's focus on rhythm/flow means the loop underneath is sometimes reduced to almost no harmonic content at all. "WAP" (Cardi B & Megan Thee Stallion) — built on a bare 3-note chromatic bass line with no other harmonic/melodic instrument — described as not really describable as a chord progression at all.
- **Songwriting takeaway, stated directly**: even a songwriter who always defaults to chord-progression-driven writing should periodically try a drone/single-chord approach instead, specifically to make more room for melody, rhythm, texture, and tone.

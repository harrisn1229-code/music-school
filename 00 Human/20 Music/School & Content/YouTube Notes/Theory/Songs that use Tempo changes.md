# Songs that use Tempo changes

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MlRRV2zQqPA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MlRRV2zQqPA)

## Synopsis

David Bennett argues tempo is an underused expressive dimension — most songs stay at one fixed tempo throughout, which isn't wrong but forecloses a whole toolkit. He surveys roughly 14 songs across every shift shape (speed-up, slow-down, there-and-back, bookending contrasting sections), a metric-modulation trick that makes a big tempo drop feel seamless, rubato as a continuous (non-stepped) alternative, and a case that grid/click-track recording quietly kills the ability to do any of this.

## Techniques

Full worked breakdown of every shape (permanent speed-up, the metric-modulation halving trick, fast-intro/slow-body, verse/chorus alternation, bookending episodic sections, subtle few-BPM dips, rubato, click-track tradeoffs) written into the new **[[Tempo Changes and Rubato]]** technique note — see that page for the complete song-by-song detail (Baba O'Riley, Come On Eileen, Proud Mary, Take Me Out, We Are Young, Prayer of the Refugee, Welcome to the Black Parade, Good Vibrations, Paranoid Android, Bohemian Rhapsody, Scenes from an Italian Restaurant, Tiny Dancer, Dawn Chorus, You Can't Always Get What You Want).

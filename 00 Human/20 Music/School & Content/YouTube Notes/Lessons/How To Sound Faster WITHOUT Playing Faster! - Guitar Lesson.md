# How To Sound Faster WITHOUT Playing Faster! - Guitar Lesson

## Synopsis

An argument that perceived speed isn't primarily about BPM numbers — demonstrated with a neoclassical sweep-and-alternate-picking composition that sounds much faster than its actual tempo (160 BPM 16th notes) suggests, and a breakdown of the two factors that actually drive that perception.

## Techniques

- **The reframe:** raw BPM goals aren't the most important factor in how fast a passage *sounds* to a listener — perception and BPM number can diverge significantly.
- **Factor 1 — clean execution.** Every shape connects fluidly into the next, every single note stays audible, and string transitions are clean — described as more important to the "fast" perception than the actual tempo. Accuracy is valued over raw speed generally.
- **Factor 2 — tight timing/rhythm lock.** Recorded against a programmed drum track (including double bass) specifically to nail sixteenth notes without dragging or rushing; checking picking strokes against the DAW grid (shown in the Cubase project) is offered as a concrete way to self-diagnose dragging/rushing without obsessing over millisecond-level precision.
- **Practical takeaway:** a well-executed lick at a slower tempo reliably sounds and reads as faster/more impressive than a rushed, sloppy lick at a genuinely higher tempo — prioritize the two factors above before chasing a higher BPM number.

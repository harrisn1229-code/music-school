# API-2500

![[Images/API-2500.png]]

**Category:** Compressor/Limiter
**Vendor:** Waves

## What it is

Waves' emulation of the API 2500 bus compressor, known for its punchy, thick VCA-style character and unique THRUST control.

## What it's used for

Bus/glue compression with an aggressive, punchy analog character — a classic drum-bus and mix-bus compressor choice.

## FX chain notes

Placed at the end of a group/bus chain for punchy glue compression.

## Techniques

- **On a drum bus, for snap/punch as the first stage of a multi-compressor bus chain** — not the sole glue compressor, just the "snap" stage before a second Vari-Mu-style compressor and a Maag EQ4 low-pass trim. Overmono don't always reach for it, but when they do it's specifically for that transient snap character. → [[Compression Staging]] — Overmono (Tape Notes), [[TN 187 Overmono - Full Video Episode]]
- **Owned-lane stand-in for a hardware fast VCA compressor (dbx 160) driven hard on a secondary snare mic purely for snap, blended under the primary top mic rather than replacing it.** Sylvia Massy's three-mic snare technique: SM57 primary on top, SM81 secondary top mic through the dbx 160 for attack, then blended in under the SM57. Full multi-mic drum-kit context on [[Drum Mic Technique]]. → [[Compression Staging]], [[Drum Mic Technique]] — Sylvia Massy (Mix with the Masters), [[Analog Recording workflow - Sylvia Massy]]
- **API-flavored stereo-bus character, direct A/B against Neve and against digital/tube summing: "very focused, really nice in the mid-range."** Live comparison on a stereo bus — an API-flavored analog path reads focused and midrange-forward, distinct from a Neve stage's warm/thick low end ("glue"/"fuzzy") and from an all-tube path's greater low-level resolution/longer decay tail on reverb/compression tails. A tonal choice, not a right/wrong call — "it's just choosing different things, do I want the Neve, what kind of stereo bus do I want." → [[UAD Neve 1073]], [[Critical Listening for Mastering]], [[Saturation and Distortion]] — Jack Joseph Puig (Mix with the Masters), [Listening to the details with Jack Joseph Puig](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=20CbTBdY8sk)
- **Master-bus glue via "API summing" on general default settings, subtly** — one leg of a small home-studio master-bus chain: an API-style summing stage feeding an API bus compressor, "just kind of use some general default settings and set it in a subtle way to sound nice," specifically to keep the mix legible on small computer/phone speakers rather than to hit a loudness number. Paired with a Waves SSL bus comp and a Waves 1073 in M/S. → [[Master Chain]] — Jack Ruch, [My Home Recording Setup for Guitar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KYTGFneEJ2A)
- **Drive the circuit hard, tame with Mix (not threshold/ratio)** — on a drum bus, push the 2500 hard enough to be audibly slamming the transients on its own, then use the plugin's own dry/wet mix knob to dial the amount back to taste rather than backing off the compression settings — keeps the aggressive character while controlling how much of it comes through — Disclosure, [First Twitch Stream Ever (Production Tutorial)](https://youtu.be/IObzp3OugtY)

## Manual

- 📄 [[00 Human/20 Music/School & Content/Production/Manuals/API-2500.pdf|Local PDF]] · [Official Waves manual](https://images.equipboard.com/uploads/item/manual/60871/waves-api-2500-plugin-manual.pdf)
- 📄 Hardware original: [[00 Human/20 Music/School & Content/Production/Manuals/API-2500 (hardware - API 2500 Bus Compressor).pdf|API 2500 Bus Compressor manual]] · [source](https://apiaudio.com/docs/manuals/2500_user_18-06-18.pdf)

**High-level points:**
- Models the real API 2500 hardware bus compressor, known for an aggressive, punchy VCA character distinct from smoother FET/opto-style compressors.
- **THRUST is the signature control** unique to this unit — it delays the onset of compression on low frequencies so punchy transients (kick, snare) pass through before gain reduction engages, preserving punch even under heavy compression.
- Two selectable **Compression Type curves (Old/New)** model two different topologies used across hardware revisions — "Old" reads smoother/more vintage, "New" is more modern and aggressive.
- **Link** is variable (not just on/off) — full link keeps the stereo image locked together under compression; less link lets each side compress more independently for a wider result.
- Attack/Release times are **stepped, not continuous** — this mirrors the original hardware's rotary switch design deliberately, not a plugin limitation.
- **Knee is adjustable in real time** (unusual for a bus compressor) — hard knee for obvious pumping, soft knee for transparent glue.

## Used in my chains

- **[[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]]** — *slot 1, glue compression*: the drum-bus workhorse. Ratio 4:1, Attack 30 ms (slowest), variable/Auto release, 2.5 dB GR, Thrust Loud, Knee Hard, Type New, Link 100%, Mix 100% (→ ~75% for the parallel transient-slam move). Thrust is why the kick stops pumping the whole bus.

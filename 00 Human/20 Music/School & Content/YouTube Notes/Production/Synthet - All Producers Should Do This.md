## Synopsis

A non-singer-friendly backing-vocal recipe: record (or reuse) a single sustained "oo"/"ah" tone with minimal pitch variation, center it, and duplicate it up and down in pitch to build a pad-like harmony backdrop — the pitch is doing the harmonic work, not vocal skill, so it doesn't require an actual singing performance to sound intentional. Framed explicitly around producers who don't consider themselves singers.

## Techniques

- **Backing "oohs" don't need to sound human or skillful — only sustained and centered.** Record a long, held tone with variation minimized (or take an existing plain hummed/sung tone), pan it to center, and use that as the raw material rather than a full melodic performance. → [[1 Harmony Vocal Track]]
- **Build harmonic content by pitching duplicates up and down, not by singing different notes** — duplicate the centered tone and pitch-shift copies to different scale degrees to construct a chord/backdrop, rather than recording separate takes at each pitch.
- **A rough demo-vocal tone is enough raw material** — if all that exists is a scratch/demo take with the right sustained tone, record proper vocals over an Edison instance on the same track (or drag/drop a suitable sample) rather than needing a clean dedicated take from the start.
- **Explicitly pitched at non-singers** — the whole recipe is framed as something a "non-singing producer" can execute without needing real vocal-arrangement chops, positioning it as a low-bar entry into having backing vocals at all.

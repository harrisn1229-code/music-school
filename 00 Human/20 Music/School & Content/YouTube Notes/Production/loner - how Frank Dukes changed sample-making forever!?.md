# how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mKA2Ldh-19U)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mKA2Ldh-19U)

## Synopsis

loner traces Frank Dukes' evolution from a "flip real samples" sample-maker (early work for G-Unit/Wu-Tang) through his own Kingsway Music Library — self-made, distorted, "dark" original samples designed to be flipped exactly like real ones — into his ambient/melodic work on The Weeknd's *House of Balloons* era and, in his current "ging" alias, acoustic indie-pop production. The throughline is a method more than a single sound: build a longer chord progression than you'll actually need (so whoever flips it has room to chop), then push distortion and grit onto otherwise-clean synth/sample sources so a *made-up* sample reads as a genuinely old, worn one. Four representative flips are recreated: a Kingsway-style piano+perc+bass sample inspired by Untitled 07's detuned piano, a darker synth-only Kingsway flip inspired by an ASAP Mob cut, an ambient pad/vocal/synth flip in the *House of Balloons* "wasted times" vein (plus a note on Frank's separately-credited garage drum programming on that same song, done alongside Skrillex), and a current "ging"-style acoustic guitar/vocal/piano/pop-drums build. Like the channel's other breakdowns, this is a recreation using the sponsor sample pack ("L(ayers) Volume 3" one-shots) rather than Frank's actual source material, so treat sound *sources* as illustrative and the processing/structuring *moves* (longer progressions, per-note pitch/attack variation, distortion-then-reverb-and-filter contrast, off-grid percussion) as the transferable content.

## Techniques

**Kingsway-era method (the throughline):**
- **Sample longer than you'll flip — leave the whole progression in, not just the hook.** Frank's own samples run a full chord progression rather than the single short phrase most flippers (cited: Boy Wonder) would grab — the extra length is deliberate, giving whoever chops it later more options than a tight loop would, the same as a real vintage sample would offer. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Per-note pitch and attack automation on a sampled/played piano part, for a "super detuned," inconsistent character.** Inspired by Kendrick Lamar's "Untitled 07" (its Frank Dukes-adjacent piano is described as "super detuned"): rather than a single global detune, pitch and attack are automated note-to-note so different notes carry different textures across the same phrase — a more granular move than one static detune amount on the whole part. *(general starting point — no cent/ms values given)* → [[BED Slot]], [[Layering and Stacking]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Distortion + a "crunch"-style compressor as the constant across every Frank Dukes sample, regardless of source.** Explicitly called out as common to all of Frank's samples: a compressor driven for its distortion/crunch character (not gentle glue), owned-lane options [[Devil-Loc]] / [[Devil-Loc Deluxe]] / [[Decapitator]]. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Compression]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Automate a pitch-shifting plugin's timbre control to push in extra high-end at specific moments, rather than a static EQ boost.** Applied to the piano layer alongside the crunch-compressor pass — riding a pitch/formant-style plugin's timbre parameter over time adds brightness at chosen points instead of a fixed top-end lift. *(general starting point — plugin not confidently identified; owned-lane candidates: [[Auto-Tune Pro]], [[BitterSweet]])* → [[Pitch and Formant]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Frank's "minimal but effective" instrumentation rule — one part per role, chosen well, rather than stacking filler layers.** Stated as a general trait: most of his samples use few sounds, but each one is used effectively (worked example: the piano-led sample was finished off with just a perc layer and a bass guitar — no extra padding). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Two-part signature = distortion/grit contrasted against reverb+filter softness, plus real sample-style section structure.** loner's closing summary of what makes a Frank Dukes sample recognizable: (1) he "washes" distorted/gritty sounds with reverb and filtering for contrast rather than leaving the distortion raw and dry, and (2) his samples are built with distinct sections the way a real vintage sample would have, specifically so another producer can chop and flip different parts — not a single static loop. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Reverb]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"

**Kingsway synth-only flip (ASAP Mob-inspired):**
- **Push a synth one-shot past its comfortable range — heavy detune/warp as the whole point, not a side effect.** Recreating an ASAP Mob-style Kingsway cut: a synth one-shot pushed and warped harder than a normal patch tolerance, then run through heavy compression and a chorus specifically because that combination "brings out the dirty textures" in an already-warped source. → [[Compression]], [[Modulation]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **A distorted counter-melody plus a separate gliding lead layer, both from one-shots — glide notes standing in for a real synth's pitch wheel.** One-shot playback with **Glide** between notes emulates the expressive pitch-bend of a real hardware synth, without needing an actual pitch-wheel-capable instrument — used here for the gliding lead layer specifically. → [[Sound Selection Guide]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Bounce → chop slightly → reverse → automate pitch, as the finishing move on a synth flip.** After the layered/distorted synth elements were in place, the combined result was bounced to audio, chopped very slightly, reversed, and had its pitch automated, then loaded into a slicer (Fruity Slicer in-app) with a bass layer added underneath. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"

**House of Balloons-era "wasted times"-style ambient flip:**
- **Two layered pad one-shots as the harmonic bed, then a chopped/filtered vocal loop through a spring reverb + EQ to fill space.** Ambient/melodic Weeknd-collaboration-era Frank Dukes sound (post-Kingsway, described as more ambient and melodic while keeping his signature textures): stack two pad one-shots first, then add a vocal loop, chop it, and filter it through a spring reverb and EQ specifically to fill out the arrangement's empty space. → [[Reverb]], [[EQ]], [[Sound Selection Guide]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **A washed-out distorted synth counter-melody, drowned in reverb and delay rather than sitting dry.** The counter-melody on "wasted times" is described as very washed-out and distorted; recreated by distorting a one-shot and then heavily saturating it in reverb and delay (not a subtle send level — "drown"). → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Reverb]], [[Delay]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **A pitch-bending lead as a signature move, referenced from "Astro Thunder."** Called out explicitly as "such a Frank Dukes sound" — a lead line built around continuous pitch-bend movement rather than static notes, recreated for the R&B-style flip here. → [[Pitch and Formant]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Background texture as a near-constant layer across Frank Dukes' work, not an occasional accent.** Stated as present in "basically all of Frank's production" — a low-level texture bed under the melodic layers, here pulled from the sample pack's texture bank. → [[Sound Selection Guide]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Garage drums, credited alongside Skrillex — build from two hi-hats + a rim first, then percussion shifted well off the grid, then kick and bass last.** Frank is credited with the garage-style drum programming on "wasted times" (co-produced with Skrillex): start with two hi-hats and a rim, layer in a bunch of percussion, and the key move is shifting that percussion **"really far off grid"** rather than a light humanize — a deliberately loose, unquantized percussion layer under a comparatively tighter hat/rim foundation, finished with kick and bass. → [[Rhythm and Groove]], [[Genre Drum Patterns]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"

**Current "ging" alias — acoustic indie-pop build:**
- **Distorted electric guitar chain: guitar amp for distortion → EQ → delay → reverb, under melodic chords.** Frank's current acoustic-indie-pop material (as ging, previously producing similar material for Post Malone) opens with melodic chords and a distorted electric guitar run through a guitar amp (for the distortion itself) plus EQ, delay, and reverb to fill it out. *(general starting point — no specific amp model, EQ curve, or delay/reverb settings given)* → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Saturation and Distortion]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Ambient vocal-loop treatment: two reverbs + a compressor placed AFTER the reverbs, plus panning, for a huge soundscape.** Inspired by Frank Ocean's "Ivy": a vocal loop run through two stacked reverbs, with the compressor placed *after* both reverb stages specifically because compressing post-reverb "really brings out the texture" of the reverb tails (compressing before would just control the dry input, not the wash) — finished with a pan effect ([[PanMan]] is the owned Soundtoys lane for this move). A drone-like layer was also added for extra dimension. → [[Reverb]], [[Compression]], [[PanMan]], [[Stereo Field]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Percussion layered in for texture ahead of the main drum pattern, plus piano purely as a guitar-backing layer (not a lead part).** Inspired by "fake love": added percussion specifically for the textural lift it gives once fuller drums land on top later; a piano part was added purely to back up the guitar, not to carry melody on its own. → [[Sound Selection Guide]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"
- **Simple, punchy pop drums: kick + snare first, then hi-hat, then a bass guitar — deliberately plain, not a chopped/layered kit.** Inspired by Post Malone's "Circles": a straightforward pop-drum build (kick+snare → hat → bass guitar) as the explicit contrast to the earlier Kingsway-era chopped/layered sample drums — proof the same producer dials complexity up or down by genre. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]] — loner, "how Frank Dukes changed sample-making forever!?"

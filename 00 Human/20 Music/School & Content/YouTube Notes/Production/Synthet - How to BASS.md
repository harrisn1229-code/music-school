# Synthet - How to BASS

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FVoDtyZzO8s)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FVoDtyZzO8s)

## Synopsis

A bassline-writing checklist: starting from the simplest possible line and adding groove through note-length shortening, octave jumps, and a dedicated slap-preset mindset (a slapped bass isn't just an octave-up hit, it's a different articulation entirely, worth patching separately); pulling ghost-note ideas directly out of the chord track; imitating the groove of other instruments already in the arrangement; and finishing with velocity/timing humanization. Closes with a reminder that a bassline (or any part) can inspire other parts in return, not just the other way around, citing Childish Gambino's "Redbone" bass slap as a specific example.

## Techniques

- **Start simple, then add groove via note length and octave motion** — a plain, evenly-spaced bassline is the starting point; shortening note lengths adds groove immediately, and jumping to octaves of the root tone (up or down) adds motion without leaving the chord. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Slap is its own articulation, not just "octave up"** — a slapped bass sounds meaningfully different from a regular bass note even at the same pitch; worth building/using a dedicated slap preset (Flex was cited as having specific slap-only presets) rather than approximating slap character from a normal bass patch.
- **Extract ghost notes from the chord track (Alt+V)** — reveal the ghost notes implied by the underlying chord progression and pull ideas for the bassline directly from them, rather than inventing a line from scratch. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **It's usually best to land on the root tone at the start of each bar**, then use the rest of the bar more freely once that anchor is established.
- **Steal groove from whatever instrument is playing it best** — a bassline's rhythm doesn't have to originate with the bass itself; borrowing the piano's (or another instrument's) rhythmic feel for the bassline is a legitimate writing shortcut, and the reverse direction works too — a lead melody can double as a great bassline starting point.
- **A song's parts can inspire each other in either direction** — cites Childish Gambino's "Redbone" as an example of a bass part with a notable slap moment worth studying as a reference.
- **Finish with humanization: velocity and timing variation** — slight, deliberate imperfection in velocity and timing on the final bassline reads as groovier than a perfectly even performance. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]

# JAZ-C18 — One melody note, many chords

**Q.** A vocal is holding a C. Name several chords that can sit under it, and say what each one turns the C into.

> [!question]- Answer
> **Cmaj7** — C is the root, neutral and home · **Am7** — the ♭3, softer, relative minor · **Fmaj7** — the 5th, plagal and warm · **Dm7** — the ♭7, suspended and unresolved · **A♭maj7** — the 3rd, borrowed and cinematic · **F♯m7♭5** — the ♭5, tense and needing to move.
>
> Same melody note, six different meanings. It is the cheapest reharmonization move there is. The systematic version is **delayed resolution**: hold the melody on its expected tonic note but harmonise it with anything except the tonic chord, and arrive home late.

## Why it matters

Cycling chords under a sustained vocal is most of the neo-soul trick, and it costs nothing but the chord choice — the topline does not change, so nothing has to be re-sung or re-recorded.

It is also the cheapest way to get a second section: run the same held note through a different set of chords on the repeat and the passage reads as development rather than as a loop going round again.

## Drill

Record or program a single sustained note, then move six different chords underneath it without changing the note. Name what the note is in each chord as it goes past.

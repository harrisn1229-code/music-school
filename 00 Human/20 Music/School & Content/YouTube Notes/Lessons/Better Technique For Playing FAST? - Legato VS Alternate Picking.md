# Better Technique For Playing FAST? - Legato VS Alternate Picking

## Synopsis

A back-to-back comparison of legato and alternate-picking playing styles over the same backing track, plus a diagnostic exercise for legato evenness and a right-hand muting technique that makes legato-only playing sound clean.

## Techniques

- **The core tonal difference:** legato sounds more fluid/smooth/connected; alternate picking sounds more aggressive and articulated — same lick, audibly different character, demoed side by side.
- **Diagnostic evenness exercise:** play a semi-chromatic scale from the 5th to 8th fret, one string at a time — **hammer-ons only** ascending, **pull-offs only** descending. Every hammered note must sound as loud/articulated as a picked note (a weak hammer-on that gets swallowed by a noise gate is the failure mode to listen for); pull-offs specifically need a real "snap" motion pulling the string, not just lifting the finger off, since the snap is what carries volume at speed later.
- **Studying Tom Quayle's approach:** using hybrid picking within legato phrases so the first note of a phrase isn't accented differently from the legato notes that follow — an accented first note is a timing/sound tell that breaks the "all notes equal" goal of clean legato.
- **Right-hand blocking/muting for legato:** with no picking happening, the picking hand's role shifts to blocking/muting unplayed strings so string noise doesn't leak through during fast legato runs — string transitions should be inaudible to the listener, not just the fretted notes themselves.
- **When to reach for legato over alternate picking:** whenever alternate picking's pick noise/attack reads as "too aggressive" for the context — clean tones, crunch tones, and acoustic guitar specifically called out as contexts where legato's smoother character fits better.
- **On the common claim that alternate picking is "harder":** attributed mainly to string-transition difficulty at speed — legato sidesteps much of that since fewer notes require a fresh pick stroke.

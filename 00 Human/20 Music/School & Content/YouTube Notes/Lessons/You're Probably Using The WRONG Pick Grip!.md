# You're Probably Using The WRONG Pick Grip!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=igJSTWbcEiM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=igJSTWbcEiM)

## Synopsis

Two connected topics: BERNTH's pick/grip setup in detail (the FAQ he gets most), then a 4-level exercise progression on **pick-escape angle** — the specific wrist/pick angle needed to clear the strings after an upstroke vs. after a downstroke, framed as the actual bottleneck once raw picking speed is already there but certain string-transition-heavy licks still won't come out clean.

## Techniques

- **Pick choice and grip, in detail:** small Jazz III-size pick for maximum control. Grip point is the index finger's side pad with the thumb resting on top; very little pick tip exposed (only the tip touches the strings) — reduces unwanted noise from the pick grazing neighboring strings. Grip should be **light**, not clenched — practice drill: hold the pick loosely enough that it *almost* falls out of your hand, to calibrate how light "light" actually is (most players over-grip out of fear of dropping the pick). Grippy-surface picks (not smooth/slippery ones) recommended for sweaty-hands stage use.
- **Pick-escape angle is the real bottleneck for tricky string-transition licks, not raw picking speed.** Once a player has hit the target BPM technically, licks that transition strings in an "unusual" way often still won't come out clean — the missing piece is escape angle, not more speed work.
- **Two escape angles, chosen by which stroke crosses the string:** the **upstroke escape** angle (pick clears upward and away after an upstroke — used e.g. for 2-notes-per-string lines) and the **downstroke escape** angle (pick clears after a downstroke). Using the wrong angle for a given transition either gets the pick stuck between strings or forces it to graze a neighboring string.
- **4-level exercise progression** (built over a song excerpt, "Séance," for musicality) isolating then combining the two escape angles: Level 1 drills upstroke-escape only; Level 2 drills downstroke-escape only (both via deliberately constructed one-directional licks); Level 3 combines both within a single line with a full beat to switch angles (still relatively slow-transition); a final challenge piece is a 7/8 arpeggio étude requiring rapid, small, non-systematic angle changes note to note (irregular notes-per-string), described as where escape-angle mastery finally becomes subtle rather than a big visible motion change.
- **Goal stated directly: don't become an "upstroke-escape player" or a "downstroke-escape player"** — master both and the ability to switch between them fast, since real licks mix transition types unpredictably.

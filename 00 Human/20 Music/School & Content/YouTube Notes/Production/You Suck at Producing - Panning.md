# You Suck at Producing: Panning

## Synopsis
An early, first-principles explainer of panning — what the pan knob actually does to a signal, and a simple arrangement-level habit for using it well. Short and basic by the channel's later standards, but the two real points it makes (panning is a hard crossfade with zero bleed to the opposite channel, and foundational elements should stay centered while the surrounding detail spreads) are genuinely foundational groundwork for every later width/stereo video in the channel's catalog.

## Techniques
- **Panning is a hard L/R crossfade — turning fully to one side sends literally zero signal to the other channel**, with no bleed or crossover; this is the base mechanic every fancier stereo trick (Haas offsets, M/S processing, etc.) is layered on top of.
- **Keep an element's foundational/rhythmic core dead center, and only spread the surrounding detail layers outward** — on a composite/layered sound, its foundational parts (the kick-and-snare-equivalent of that element) stay centered so the mix has an anchor, while the secondary/ancillary layers around it pan outward for width and personality.
- **Audition a pan move by soloing the track first**, sweeping the knob from center to a hard side and back, before judging it against the full mix.

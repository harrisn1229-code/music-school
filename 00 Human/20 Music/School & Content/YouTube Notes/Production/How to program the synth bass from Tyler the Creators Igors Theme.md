# How to program the synth bass from Tyler, the Creator's "Igor's Theme"

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pL22AIDy7r8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pL22AIDy7r8)

## Synopsis

A modern reference sound (Tyler, the Creator's "Igor's Theme" opening bass, 2019 Grammy winner) approached the same ear-first way as the Weeknd breakdown — with a genuinely important practical finding: two "obvious" synth candidates (Jupiter-8, Oberheim OB-X) physically CANNOT play the part's low notes from the keyboard, ruling them out regardless of tone match.

## Techniques

- **Check an instrument's physical keyboard RANGE before committing to it as a candidate for a target patch — tone match doesn't matter if the keyboard can't reach the notes.** Both a Jupiter-8 and an Oberheim OB-X could get close to the target tone but their keyboards don't go low enough to play the actual bassline from the keys (a MIDI controller could reach lower, but playing live from the instrument's own keys cannot); the Prophet-5 was the only vintage synth on hand whose keyboard reaches low enough — decided the final instrument choice as much as tone did.
- **Intentional oscillator detuning is a deliberate EXCEPTION for a featured, exposed bass sound — not the default.** Two sawtooth oscillators an octave apart, unison mode, THEN a deliberate fine-tune detune between them for "shredding"/chorus character — explicitly framed against this channel's usual bass rule (favor one oscillator's level, keep things in tune, because other parts play against the bass) as a case where breaking that rule is the right call specifically because the bass is exposed and featured, not buried under other parts.
- **Arrangement-level use of an exposed bass tone: let the bass play completely alone before the beat enters, then have it drop OUT for a stretch once the groove is established, so it can re-enter later for a second "fresh" impact.** Not a synthesis technique, but the stated reason the patch works dramatically — space (silence from other elements at the intro, then bass silence mid-track) is what makes each entrance of a strong, exposed tone land, rather than the bass playing continuously throughout.

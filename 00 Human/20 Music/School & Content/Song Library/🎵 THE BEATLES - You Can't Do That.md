# You Can't Do That — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4 bars of I, ostinato running, bass and percussion held back until bar 3) · Verse (a 12-bar blues frame) · Verse · Bridge (8) · Verse · Verse (guitar solo) · Bridge · Verse · Outro (2 bars, abrupt, ostinato only, ending on a teasing sustained F♯). **The last bar of the verse is the tell:** verses leading into another verse move to V, verses leading into a bridge just sustain I.

A song with a split stylistic personality. The verse is a twelve-bar frame on I, IV and V ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]) whose tune is composed straight through with almost no parallelism between phrases. The bridge breaks that diet and stages a **fake modulation to E minor** — a tonicisation that never commits ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]) — and then turns home by **ii → iii → I** where the textbook would put V.

The verse works ♭7 with blues consistency, which puts it in direct collision with the major third inside the IV chord, and **both thirds of the tonic are in play, so the mode reads bi-modal** ([[BLU-C06 — Blue notes and the blues scale]]). The bridge tune drops every blue note in favour of a strict major third and major seventh.

The unifying hook is an **ostinato oscillating between the major and minor third**, played wherever the tonic sits still. The rhythmic hook is a hard syncopation on the eighth before the downbeat ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]) — and Ringo spells out "and-four-and-one" in even eighths after the last verse line so the ear can locate the displaced beat.

## Techniques

- **Modulator-mode cleanup/de-washing** — on a slightly washed-out sound, a light compression pass plus pushing the bands for brightness, adjusting Depth for a wetter/drier result, and tweaking Release and Range can clean the sound up rather than obviously vocoding it — an overlooked, subtle use of the device. → [[Vocoder]]
- **Pitch-tracking Carrier oscillator as a hidden color layer** — enabling the built-in pitch-tracking oscillator adds a synthesized tone that follows whatever's playing (no separate doubling instrument needed); adjust its Range for the amount of extra flavor. → [[Vocoder]]
- **Noise/cymbal texture design + per-band snare "polarizing"** — turn on Vocoder on a noise/hat source, raise Formant, tune Release toward a ride-cymbal-like character, adjust Depth/Brightness, and use the built-in XY pad to control the grain-i-ness — useful generally for texturing sounds. On a snare specifically, split with a high/low splitter first so only one band gets vocoded. → [[Vocoder]], [[Snare Design]]
- **External-carrier chord-driven "watery rhythm" — band count = harmonic smoothness** — External mode fed from anything playing chords (voice, dirty bass, a synth), Release tuned for a watery result; the number of active bands directly shapes how harmonic/smooth vs. narrow/notchy it reads. → [[Vocoder]]
- **Narrow-Bandwidth Modulator-mode "bloopy swoop" design, found through open play** — turning Bandwidth down makes each internal band-pass filter read as a narrow notch; more bands = more harmonic; modulate Formant for bloopy/swoopy sweeps; his own method is unstructured 5-minute play sessions rather than analytical dialing-in. → [[Vocoder]]

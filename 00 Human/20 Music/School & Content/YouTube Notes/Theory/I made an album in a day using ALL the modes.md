# I made an album in a day using ALL the modes

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UO9Ua3lLhWM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UO9Ua3lLhWM)

## Synopsis

A real-time composition challenge: seven solo-piano tracks recorded in one studio session, each one written entirely in a different mode of the major scale (all seven starting notes of the white keys, C through B), with the host talking through the character and compositional approach for each mode as he writes it.

## Techniques

- **C Ionian (major)**: the baseline/reference track — standard bright major-scale sound.
- **D Dorian**: reached for "the Dorian vamp" — i to IV, with IV as a MAJOR chord (the note that distinguishes Dorian from natural minor) — described as upbeat and sprightly; melody emphasizes the 2nd scale degree to foreground the mode.
- **E Phrygian**: described as exotic, unusual, slightly intimidating — common in metal, Middle Eastern music, and hip-hop; approached by pedaling the characteristic ♭2 note on the offbeat.
- **F Lydian**: described as magical/cinematic (a common film-score mode) but "temperamental" — easy to accidentally slip out of. Written in 3/4 (deliberately different from the other 4/4 tracks, to keep the seven pieces from blurring together) and opens directly on the raised 4th degree — Lydian's one character note vs. the major scale — to establish the sound immediately.
- **G Mixolydian**: written in 12/8 (shuffle/swing feel) specifically because Mixolydian's flattened 7th already gives it "a bluesy edge" that pairs naturally with a swung groove — described as all of major's brightness with "a slightly cooler edge," less driven toward resolution than the major scale.
- **A Aeolian (natural minor)**: deliberately steered AWAY from a stereotypically "sad" reading — built from A minor 7 and D minor 7 (both minor-7th, not plain triads) for a "jazzy, lush" Latin-inspired feel instead, description preferred as "solemn/mournful/serious" over flatly "sad."
- **B Locrian**: framed as the one mode the other six's compositional approach can't be applied to — genuinely unstable, with only the bare tonic note offering any sense of "home," so melodic phrases that would normally resolve have nowhere satisfying to land. The only way described to make it work compositionally is to fully commit to its unsettled character rather than fight it — "go full Halloween." Cites "Dust to Dust" (about grave-digging) as the reference example of Locrian used deliberately for its eerie quality.
- **General cross-mode composing note**: on any single day a composer's own habitual licks/motifs tend to bleed across pieces, so a genuine test of "does this really sound like the mode" is deliberately varying surface elements (time signature, tempo feel) between tracks to keep each one distinct rather than relying on the mode alone to differentiate them.

# Help! — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Pollack's central claim is that **the song is unusually static and closed, and that this is the point** — slow unvarying harmonic rhythm, one key throughout with no excursion anywhere, and a flat folk-ballad verse/refrain alternation with no story line to carry it. The closure underscores the single-mindedness of the lyric. The other headline: **♭VII doing two completely different jobs in the same song**.

**Form.** The **intro is an eight-bar compressed version of the refrain**, and the refrain is the same design **stretched to sixteen bars by doubling every chord's length** ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]) — which reveals a slow build the fast intro version conceals entirely. Write the section once, make the intro from it at half the harmonic rhythm, and the compressed version is a hook while the stretched version is a build.

**Harmony.** Six diatonic chords plus ♭VII, which **plays two roles** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). In the verse it makes an Aeolian cadence, standing in for V to close onto I. In the intro and refrain it isn't a dominant surrogate at all but a **filler chord pleated between ii and V**, and what makes it work is purely contrapuntal — the bass walks down while the top voice climbs chromatically. That intro progression also **starts the song in the wrong key on purpose**: heard one chord at a time it supports three different readings, and you aren't certain until the verse begins. The outro's payoff is a **deceptive cadence** ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]) landing on vi before the following phrase puts it right.

**Melody.** Confined to a **fifth**, built mostly from triadic figures and short descending scale fragments — which is exactly what makes the one exception land: the upward leap of a sixth in screaming falsetto at each refrain's climax ([[MEL-C05 — Register and intensity]]).

**Arrangement.** Parts are assigned by section ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]) — the lead guitar has no part in the verses at all. The best detail: near the end of the verse's first line the backing singers take **the lead's own five-note figure, trailing a few beats, jumping above him in range, and accenting a different note of it**. Not a harmony, not a response — a shadow.

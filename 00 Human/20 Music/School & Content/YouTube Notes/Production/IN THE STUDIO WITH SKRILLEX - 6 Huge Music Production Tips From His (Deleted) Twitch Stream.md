# IN THE STUDIO WITH SKRILLEX - 6 Huge Music Production Tips From His (Deleted) Twitch Stream

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmPblJB9LzY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmPblJB9LzY)

## Synopsis

Raw footage from Skrillex's own (since-deleted) Twitch stream, narrated over him working live in Ableton: building a beat from a simple melody/counter-melody/bassline combo, deciding what to quantize versus what to leave loose for groove, keeping sound design deliberately dumb-simple, bouncing skeleton ideas to audio and developing sound later, arranging a sub-only bass moment with automated filter and reverb movement, giving a section a "baby beat" narrative arc, getting creative with Ableton's stock Erosion device, and closing with his famously minimal plugin list. This is the same deleted stream the "mute test" clip in [[This ONE Question Will Improve Every Song You Make]] was pulled from — that specific core-identification technique ("if you can mute everything else and it's still there, that's the song") is already captured in full on that page and is not repeated here.

## Techniques

- **Layer a simple main idea with a counter-melody and an octave-doubled bassline to get "instant mood" fast.** His described starting point: a simple first melodic idea, then a second "counter" part against it, then a bassline that's simple and pitched an octave higher than expected on the low end — the combination reads as a mood almost immediately, before any sound design happens. → [[Melody Writing]] — Skrillex (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH SKRILLEX - 6 Huge Music Production Tips From His (Deleted) Twitch Stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmPblJB9LzY)
- **Quantize a groove for a deliberately "computer perfect" feel, then manually nudge specific hits earlier or later by ear to add swing back in — both directions are intentional choices, not defaults.** Working order: full quantize first ("making it more computer perfect, I guess, like Daft Punk — half human half robot"), then by-ear micro-adjustment of individual note positions (a hair earlier here, a hair later there) until the pattern feels "groovier," rather than applying one fixed swing percentage across the board. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]] — Skrillex (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH SKRILLEX - 6 Huge Music Production Tips From His (Deleted) Twitch Stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmPblJB9LzY)
- **Two very simple, individually unremarkable sounds combined can read as one rich, "dreamy" texture — the complexity lives in the combination, not either source sound.** His own description of a signature-sounding lead: "the stupidest simple of sound" — a Vengeance sample, a little glide, a little reverb — paired with a second equally plain element; neither sound is interesting alone, but the pair together is what he calls "the idea." → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Skrillex (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH SKRILLEX - 6 Huge Music Production Tips From His (Deleted) Twitch Stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmPblJB9LzY)
- **Cap a fresh idea at about an hour, save it, and move to a different idea rather than perfecting one in the moment — then revisit the pile later with "a fresh brain and a fresh ear" to decide what's actually good.** Pairs with building only a skeleton version first (patterns and structure with rough, uninteresting placeholder sounds — "the sounds aren't even that interesting but the idea is there"), bouncing that skeleton to audio, and deliberately deferring real sound-design work and quality judgment to a later session rather than doing either while still inside the idea. → [[Committing and Printing]] — Skrillex (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH SKRILLEX - 6 Huge Music Production Tips From His (Deleted) Twitch Stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmPblJB9LzY)
- **Strip a bass to pure sub (zero midrange) for a high-impact section, then automate filter brightness and reverb Size for movement — automating Size specifically throws off small pitch-like artifacts near the end of bars.** Going into a section, he cuts all midrange off the bass so only the sub remains, tunes it down "off the floor," then automates a filter from darker to brighter (or the reverse) across the section, and separately automates reverb Size — noting Size automation "does some weird stuff" especially near the end of measures, producing small pitch-bend-like blips worth exploiting rather than avoiding. → [[Bass Design]], [[Automation]] — Skrillex (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH SKRILLEX - 6 Huge Music Production Tips From His (Deleted) Twitch Stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmPblJB9LzY)
- **Give an arrangement section a narrative character — a "baby beat" that's deliberately thin and tentative at first, then gains "confidence" as it develops.** He frames a small early pattern as "just learning how to walk for the first time... a little shy, hasn't gained its full confidence yet" — a metaphor used to decide how sparse or hesitant a section should sound at its first appearance versus later restatements, immediately followed by his own caveat that not every detail needs this kind of meaning. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Skrillex (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH SKRILLEX - 6 Huge Music Production Tips From His (Deleted) Twitch Stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmPblJB9LzY)
- **Ableton's stock Erosion device can inject actual synthesized sine-wave content into a signal, not just noise/grit — and stacking two instances back to back compounds into a distinct, more digital texture.** On Erosion: "you can add an actual sine wave, like an actual synthesis coming out of here, which is crazy" — then duplicates the device for a second pass, comparing the result to an old-school "Doctor P"-style character. → [[Erosion]] — Skrillex (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH SKRILLEX - 6 Huge Music Production Tips From His (Deleted) Twitch Stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmPblJB9LzY)
- **Stated minimalist plugin philosophy: mostly stock Ableton devices, and if limited to exactly two third-party plugins for life, they'd be an EQ and a limiter — "you can make anything out of anything."** Direct quote: "I don't really use that many plugins to be honest... if I really needed one, there was only one plugin in the whole world — actually two plugins, all I need — Pro-L and Pro-Q3, EQ and limiter." Also claims to have built a full song using only vocal-derived sounds as the sole source material. → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Pro-L 2]] — Skrillex (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH SKRILLEX - 6 Huge Music Production Tips From His (Deleted) Twitch Stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmPblJB9LzY)

# Songs that use 19/8 time

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BVvSjkVgBW4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BVvSjkVgBW4)

## Synopsis

One of the rarest meters in the series — 19/8 — anchored by Chicago's "Introduction" and a direct lineage back to jazz-fusion bandleader Don Ellis, plus a performance-mindset quote from Chicago's drummer worth keeping alongside the purely rhythmic content.

## Techniques

- **A named subdivision pattern, traceable to a specific source**: Chicago's "Introduction" has a 19/8 interlude subdivided as 3+3+2+1+2+2+2 (three, three, two, one, two, two, two) — drummer Danny Seraphine had the subdivision written directly on his snare drum to keep track of it live. This exact grouping traces to Don Ellis's track "33221222" — its numerical title literally spells out the same subdivision (two groups of 3, three groups of 2, a single beat, then three more groups of 2), and Seraphine confirms Ellis's influence directly.
- **"Always try to make odd time feel like four" — a stated performance philosophy, not just a counting method.** Seraphine's own goal on Chicago's odd-meter material was to make it groove with rock feel despite the unusual subdivision, rather than let the odd count read as a purely academic exercise — worth keeping as a mindset alongside any specific subdivision scheme.
- **Don Ellis as the genre's odd-meter pioneer**: his track "Whiplash" (7/4, and the title track to the 2014 film of the same name) is offered as comparatively simple next to his own 19/8 material — Ellis built a career specifically around unusual meters in jazz.
- **A composite-meter 19/4 example, continuing the 14/4 pattern from this series**: the middle section of Soundgarden's "Rusty Cage" is in 19/4, best subdivided (per the video, as a performer-friendly transcription choice) into three bars of 3/4 plus two bars of 5/4.
- **Other confirmed 19/8 songs**: Bill Bruford's (of Yes/King Crimson/Genesis) "Hell's Bells"; Animals As Leaders' "The Problem of Other Minds"; Herbie Hancock's "Hidden Shadows."
- **An unused grouping, offered as a compositional idea**: 4+4+4+4+3 (still summing to 19) — described as feeling almost like fast 4/4 that skips a beat on every fifth rotation; the video's own closing composition uses this grouping as a demonstration since Bennett hadn't found a real song using it.

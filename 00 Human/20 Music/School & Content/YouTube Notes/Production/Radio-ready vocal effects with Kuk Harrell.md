# Radio-ready vocal effects with Kuk Harrell

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uo9E2elXAlI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uo9E2elXAlI)

## Synopsis

A short Mix with the Masters breakdown of Grammy-winning vocal producer/engineer Kuk Harrell's (Justin Bieber, Beyoncé, Rihanna, Chris Brown) go-to tracking-vocal effects chain, demoed live on a real session. The throughline is deliberate simplicity: the same short signal chain — EQ, a touch of compression, RVox, then reverb/delay/doubler sends — on every singer, dialed in by ear and feel rather than technical frequency knowledge ("I'm not going on tech, I'm going on emotion"). Harrell frames the whole chain as something he builds live in the artist's headphones *while* they're singing, not a mix-stage add-on — the goal is for the singer to hear something that already sounds like a finished record, which helps them find the right pocket and deliver a more confident, in-the-pocket take. He closes on a mix-handoff philosophy: keep routing dead simple (three sends only — reverb, delay, doubler/"chorus" — nothing daisy-chained through "a thousand aux"), so the session that reaches the mix engineer needs no cleanup and leaves them room to do more with it. Short runtime, but a tight, complete, and reusable philosophy plus a fully named chain.

## Techniques

- **Mic choice for warmth, not brightness — pick the source mic to compensate for/build on the singer's natural tone.** Go-to mics: **Sony C800** or **Elam 251**. On this particular record he chose the **251 specifically for its warmth**, because the singers were already excellent and he wanted to capture the full richness/warmth of their voices rather than adding brightness at the mic stage — he separately notes the demo singer's voice already "sounds really thin... doesn't sound like he used a great mic" yet still carries character-adding brightness, so the mic pick is about complementing what's already there, not fixing it. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Philosophy: mix by feel/emotion, not frequency knowledge — deliberately non-technical, and states it as a strength, not a gap.** "I'm not a really technical person, I don't know all the technical terminology, but for me it works because I'm not going on tech, I'm going on emotion." Reasoning: listeners (he singles out kids specifically) have zero frequency vocabulary but are "experts" at feeling — so tuning by ear against that felt response is the more direct signal than naming the frequency being moved.
- **Track the song's runtime like an arc, and let where you are in that arc set how much processing/energy to add at any given moment.** He often isn't consciously tracking lyrics while working — he's following the melody, and specifically whether "the emotion of that melody is fitting the time frame of the record." His example: a 3:52 record has a felt intensity target at the one-minute mark, and that target is what tells him whether he's adding too much or too little processing *right now*, not a fixed setting applied uniformly across the whole song.
- **A single fixed 3-band EQ recipe, applied identically to every singer, built almost entirely around adding top end.** Waves Renaissance EQ (regular): push up "the red" (he estimates ~10 kHz — a high shelf/band for air/brightness), bring "the purple" (a mid band) up a little, bring "the blue" up just a little bit too. Deliberately the *same move* on every voice — "all I need is just high-end" — rather than a per-singer diagnostic EQ pass. Owned equivalent (Renaissance EQ not owned): **[[Pro-Q 4]]** — see that note's new Techniques entry below. *(qualitative — no exact gain amounts given beyond "just a little")* → [[EQ]] (effect)
- **Light compression purely to control peaks, immediately after the EQ stage, before RVox.** Waves Renaissance Compressor, "compressing just a little bit to kind of keep the peaks out" — a gentle peak-catch, not a leveling or character move. Owned equivalent (not owned): **[[Pro-C 2]]**. → [[Compression]] (effect), [[Compression Staging]]
- **RVox used to push the vocal forward in the mix/monitor so it sits in front of the track, immediately after EQ+comp.** "I'll put the RVox on there to push it out a little bit because I want it to stand out in front of the track." RVox is owned directly — see the new Techniques entry on [[RVox]] below.
- **Build the entire finished-sounding chain live, in the singer's headphones, WHILE they're tracking — not as a later mix pass.** "When I'm cutting this, he's actually singing to this — so what I'm doing is creating it in his headphone to sound like a finished product, like a mix." The stated payoff: hearing it "sounding punchy, nice and crisp" in the cans lets the vocal "sit on top of the track," which "helps me to dictate the right pocket and everything" — i.e. the processed monitor mix actively shapes the performance's timing/feel, not just its comfort. → [[Latency-Free Tracking]]
- **A fixed reverb send preset used on every session — Waves R-Verb, "medium hall," always.** Called "creating the dreamscape, the imaging of it" — used for space/imaging rather than character. Owned equivalent (not owned): **[[Pro-R 2]]**. → [[Reverb]] (effect)
- **A fixed delay send preset used on every session — Waves H-Delay, quarter-note ping-pong, always.** Same "it's the same thing all the time" philosophy as the reverb pick. H-Delay is owned directly — see the new Techniques entry below. → [[Delay]] (effect)
- **A simple/basic doubler, used sparingly just to spread the voice's width a little — not a heavy multi-voice widener.** "The simple, the basic doubler, just to kind of spread his voice out just a little bit." Owned equivalent: **[[Doubler2]]** (2-voice, matches "basic/simple" better than the 4-voice Doubler4). → [[Modulation]] (effect), [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Deliberately minimal routing — only three sends total on the whole session (reverb, delay, doubler/"chorus") — as a mix-handoff courtesy, not just a workflow preference.** "I don't have anything routed, I don't have any of my effects or delays routed through a thousand aux... my goal is to make sure that I'm handing off an amazing session simplified, so when the mix engineer gets it he doesn't have to go through clean-up, crossfades, turn down S's, [or] figure out how I have everything routed... so now when I send this to the mix engineer he has room to do his job and create something even more special with what we've given him." He names this as something he knows some engineers *don't* do and that it "drives mix engineers crazy" when they don't. → [[Mix Handoff]]
- **Full named chain, in order:** Mic (Sony C800 or Elam 251, picked for warmth) → Renaissance EQ (fixed 3-band boost recipe) → Renaissance Compressor (light peak control) → RVox (push forward) → sends: R-Verb (medium hall) + H-Delay (quarter-note ping-pong) + basic Doubler (subtle width) — built live in the artist's headphones while tracking.

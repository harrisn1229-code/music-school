# Shure KSM 141

**Category:** Microphone (small-diaphragm condenser)
**Vendor:** Shure

## What it is

A small-diaphragm condenser designed to combine the character of two mics engineer Eddie Kramer names as favorites — the brightness of an AKG 451 and the vintage-preferred voicing of a Neumann KM 84 (the KM 184's predecessor) — in one affordable modern package. Switchable omni/cardioid, with a 20 dB pad. Not currently owned — logged as a direct, named recommendation, not a general mention.

## What it's used for

Close-mic'ing acoustic guitar and percussion — the two roles Kramer calls out specifically — as a budget-conscious stand-in for the vintage tube condensers (AKG/Neumann-class, now costing thousands) historically used on the same job.

## Signal chain notes

🎬 *From video:* "There's a mic that Shure makes called the KSM 141, and the idea there with this mic was to combine the best of two microphones that I happen to like very much: the AKG 451 — very bright, great for acoustic guitar, great for percussion — and the Neumann KM 84, the more vintage one, slightly better I feel. So what they did was combine the sounds of those two. There's also a pad, a 20 dB pad, and it's omni and cardioid. It's only 600 bucks versus a big-time difference — with a 67, which is probably about 8 grand now on the new one. Does a very lovely job on recording acoustic guitars, percussion, any of that stuff... I particularly like that KSM 141." Recommended specifically as the modern equivalent for a close-to-the-last-fret acoustic-guitar mic position (see [[1 Acoustic Guitar Track|Acoustic Guitar Track]]) — the position Kramer used historically (with vintage tube mics he can no longer confidently identify) on the 12-string/6-string blend that opens "All Along The Watchtower." — Eddie Kramer, [Eddie Kramer's story behind "All Along The Watchtower" by Jimi Hendrix](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N6m019pawms)

# The Modes of the HARMONIC MINOR

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CHUMiL-8K0E)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CHUMiL-8K0E)

## Synopsis

A companion to the major-scale-modes video: the same "start on a different degree" logic applied to harmonic minor instead of major, going mode-by-mode from the most obscure/rarely-used to the genuinely useful. Introduces the **chord scale** idea explicitly — a scale chosen per individual chord during improvisation, rather than one scale held over a whole progression — and uses several of these modes as worked chord-scale examples.

## What's taught

- **Mode 1 — harmonic minor itself.** Natural minor with a raised 7th degree, giving a stronger pull back to the tonic than plain natural minor.
- **Mode 2 — Locrian ♮6.** Locrian with the flat-6 raised back to natural. Framed as even rarer/more awkward than plain Locrian itself — no song example could be found for it.
- **Mode 3 — Ionian ♯5 ("Ionian augmented").** Major scale with a raised 5th, which makes the tonic triad an augmented triad. Rare as a whole-song scale, but useful as a **chord scale for a maj7♯5 chord** — noted as one of *two* valid chord-scale choices for that chord (the other being Lydian augmented, the 3rd mode of melodic minor, covered in a companion video); the two differ by only the 4th degree, giving each a slightly different color over the same chord.
- **Mode 4 — Dorian ♯4**, aka **Ukrainian Dorian** / **Romanian Dorian** / (in Jewish klezmer music) **Mi Sheberakh**. Regular Dorian with a raised 4th. Rooted in Ukrainian Duma epic-poem folk performance (kobzar musicians) and Eastern European folk music broadly; example cited: a performance by Marko Farion.
- **Mode 5 — Phrygian dominant**, aka **Spanish Phrygian**. Phrygian (flat 2) with a *major* 3rd instead of minor — described as the most genuinely widely-used mode of the set. Song examples: *Karma Police* by Radiohead (E Phrygian dominant); *Dream Brother* by Jeff Buckley (G Phrygian dominant); *Naughty Girl* and *Beautiful Liar* by Beyoncé. Common in Arabic and Middle Eastern music, where it's called **Maqam Hijaz** — Western composers reach for it as a quick way to evoke a Middle Eastern sound (example: Muse's instrumental interlude in *United States of Eurasia*).
- **Mode 6 — Lydian ♯9.** Lydian with a raised 2nd/9th degree, which in practice functions as a minor 3rd sitting alongside the scale's own major 3rd — an unusual "both a minor and major third present" color. Example: The Beatles' *Blue Jay Way*, in C, readable as C Lydian ♯9 if the added color tone is spelled D♯ — though in practice it's more usefully notated as E♭ (minor 3rd) since that's what a player's ear will actually track.
- **Mode 7 — Ultra Locrian**, aka **Super Locrian 𝄫7**. Super Locrian (the melodic minor's 7th mode) with the 7th degree flattened a second time. Rare/dark-sounding, but useful as a **chord scale over a diminished 7th chord** — reframed for easier reading as having a natural 6th (rather than double-flat-7), a major 3rd (rather than flat-4), and both a flat 6 and natural 6, both a minor 3rd and major 3rd present at once.
- **Chord-scale system, stated plainly**: rather than applying one scale across an entire progression, apply a different scale to each individual chord to bring out that specific chord's character during improvisation — a given chord can have more than one valid chord-scale choice (as shown with maj7♯5 above), each giving a subtly different flavor.
- Overall framing: harmonic minor's modes are "hit and miss" in real-world usefulness compared to the major-scale modes — several (Locrian ♮6, Ionian ♯5 as a whole-song scale) have essentially no song-length examples and exist mainly as chord-scale tools, while Phrygian dominant in particular is genuinely widely used across Western pop, Middle Eastern, and Jewish klezmer music.

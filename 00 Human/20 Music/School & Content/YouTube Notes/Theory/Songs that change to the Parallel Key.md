# Songs that change to the Parallel Key

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cf5iPS3-Oug)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cf5iPS3-Oug)

## Synopsis

David Bennett catalogs songs that shift between parallel major and minor (same tonic, different tonality) — in both directions, at different structural placements, and at different scales from a full-section shift down to a single Picardy-third chord — then generalizes the same device to any two adjacent modes on the brightness spectrum.

## Techniques

- **Minor→major shift opens a song up (brighter)**: "While My Guitar Gently Weeps" (A minor→A major chorus), "Happy Together" (The Turtles), "Runaway" (Del Shannon).
- **Major→minor shift closes a song in (darker/more intense)**: "I'm Still Standing" (Elton John, B♭ major verse→B♭ minor chorus).
- **Mid-verse shift, not tied to a section boundary**: "Penny Lane" — B major verse slides to B minor partway through as a transitional color, before modulating to an entirely different key (A major) for the chorus.
- **A shift where the melody doesn't need to change at all**: "My Favorite Things" — E♭ minor (verses 1–2) → E♭ major (verse 3); the melody uses only scale degrees 1, 2, 5, identical in both tonalities, so it works unchanged under either harmony.
- **A single-chord flicker (Picardy third), not a sustained shift**: "Crazy" (Gnarls Barkley) — one bar of C major inside an otherwise all-minor loop; "Exit Music (For a Film)" (Radiohead) resolves its final chord to B major after a whole song in B minor. Classical precedent: Mozart's *Rondo alla Turca* (A minor→A major for its second theme).
- **Technically not a modulation** — the tonic note never moves, only the tonality around it — better classified as a mode shift/interchange than a key change.
- **Generalizes to any two adjacent modes, not just major/minor**: "Norwegian Wood" shifts E Mixolydian (verse) → E Dorian (middle eight) — only one step apart on the brightness spectrum, so it reads as a much subtler shift than a full parallel major↔minor swap, despite being mechanically the same device.

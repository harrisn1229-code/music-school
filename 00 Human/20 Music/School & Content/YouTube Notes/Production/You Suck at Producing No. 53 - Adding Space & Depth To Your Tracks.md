## Synopsis
A short, arrangement-focused take on "space" that's distinct from the usual reverb/depth-cue framing — here space comes from where things sit in the stereo field and how many things are sounding at once, not from wet/dry ratio. Three moves in sequence on a cluttered demo: spread simultaneous elements across different points in the stereo field so they don't clump into one spot, thin out how many elements sound at the same instant by staggering entrances/exits, and — if it still feels cluttered after both — simply slow the tempo down so the ear has room to register what's playing. Basic by the channel's later standards but a clean, complementary angle on "depth and space" that's worth having alongside the wet/dry/pre-delay framing already documented.

## Techniques
- **Spread simultaneous elements to different positions across the stereo field** rather than leaving them all centered — pan overlapping parts to distinct "rooms" in the field so they read as occupying separate space instead of piling into one spot.
- **Thin out how many elements sound at the same instant** by staggering entrances and exits so parts hand off to each other rather than all sustaining together continuously — fewer things sounding at once reads as more spacious even with the same total arrangement.
- **Slow the tempo down if a section still feels cluttered** after panning and thinning — a lower BPM gives the ear room to actually register the detail of what's still playing, when spatial and density fixes alone aren't enough.

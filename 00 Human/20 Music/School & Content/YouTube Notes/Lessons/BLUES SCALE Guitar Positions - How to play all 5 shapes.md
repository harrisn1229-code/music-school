The headline device is the **[[Root-to-Root Scale-Form Mapping Method]]**: a 5-rule exercise (start on root, play the whole shape, double-hit every root you land on, never repeat any other note, end on the same root) built to solve a guitar-specific problem — a fixed-position scale shape almost never has the root as its lowest or highest note, so playing lowest-to-highest (the default) never actually plants the root as the ear's home base. ## What's taught

- **~3:36** — There are five movable blues-scale shapes, all written with root C (transposable to any root/key). "Blues scale positions/patterns/scale forms/scale shapes" are used interchangeably by the presenter.
- **~2:59** — Why the other 4 shapes (beyond the one popular "box 1"-type shape most players default to) get neglected: the added blue note (the ♭5) sometimes falls **outside** the main scale-form box, requiring a shift/stretch/reach that's confusing to place — this friction, not lack of value, is why 4 of the 5 positions are underused.
- **Blue-note placement rule:** when the blue note sits outside the shape to the left or right, play it **on the same string as the note you're resolving to**, and **slide/slur into it** rather than picking it separately. **Ascending**, play the outside note on the **left** side of the shape; **descending**, play the same pitch on a **different string** on the right side. Both spots are equally valid/necessary — know the note in both places, not just one.
- **~6:37–9:00** — **The [[Root-to-Root Scale-Form Mapping Method]]** (full 5-rule exercise captured in detail on its own page): start on root → play the whole shape → double-hit every root you land on → never repeat any other note → end on the same root you started on. Framed as the fix for guitar's "positions" problem (a fixed shape rarely has the root as its lowest/highest note, unlike root-to-root instruments like piano), and as the mechanism that lets the *same* physical shape read as minor blues scale or major blues scale depending only on which note is treated as home base.
- **Practice progression once the base method is solid:**
  1. Every root, every key — work all 5 shapes root-to-root off every root note (starting from the C-rooted shapes shown), tracked with a checklist; expect it to take multiple sessions, possibly weeks/months, to cover every shape in every reachable neck location and every key.
  2. **~13:11 — Melodic thirds ("skip up, come down one") pattern** — go up a scale step (skipping one), then back down one note, repeated through the shape; called a test of whether you're navigating the shape as a "map" (can jump around) vs. a "maze" (can only walk next-note-to-next-note); also breaks up the linear run and sounds more melodic.
  3. **~14:19 — "Every other note" pattern** — referenced as a second melodic-pattern drill; not worked out for the blues scale specifically in this video (the presenter has a free PDF with it worked for the plain pentatonic scale — "top 3 pentatonic scale guitar patterns," linked in the video description/soundguitarlessons.com).
  4. Root-to-root **with a metronome**, locked to an even pace — used as a smoothness/hesitation test, not a speed drill.
  5. **Improvise** with the shape while deliberately targeting/returning to the root as home base — the step that actually makes the minor/major distinction audible in real playing, not just in the drill.
- **~16:08 — Three supporting playing-quality habits** (secondary, general — not blues-scale-specific): alternate picking (pick down/up, i-m fingerstyle alternation, or thumb alternation); tone/tension awareness (watch for buzz/rattle from excess pressure, notice warm vs. bright tone); work toward legato/connected phrasing, expecting gaps to shrink with technique over time.

# Memorize the Bass Fretboard in 9 Minutes

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pQoWKra9LdQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pQoWKra9LdQ)

## Synopsis

BassBuzz lays out a five-step system for memorizing every note name on the bass fretboard, built around a running "lost in the forest" metaphor (money notes = the safe home base; the other tools = ways to navigate out from it and find your way back). Rather than brute-forcing all ~84 notes on the neck at once, the method front-loads a small high-value anchor set — the "money notes," the plain letter-name notes in the low, most-played first four frets of each string — then teaches three ways to *derive* any other note from the nearest anchor instead of memorizing it directly: the Say and Play chromatic-alphabet walk, the octave and unison relocation shapes, and a mnemonic-driven map of the dot-marker frets. A fifth step, Bass GPS, is a look-away/fret/recall drill that tests whether the map actually holds up under speed. It's a pure instrument-fundamentals lesson — no production/mixing/gear content — pitched at total beginners but genuinely systematic: every step builds directly on the one before it, and the video closes by explicitly warning against treating memorization drills as the whole practice diet rather than a means to playing real songs.

## What's taught

- **Money notes (the anchor set).** The plain letter-name notes within the first four frets of each string: open E, F (fret 1), G (fret 3) on the low E string; open A, B (fret 2), C (fret 3) on the A string; open D, E (fret 2), F (fret 3) on the D string; open G, A (fret 2), B (fret 4) on the G string. Read across the strings they form one unbroken alphabetical run with no gaps: E F G A B C D E F G A B. Learn one string at a time, a couple of minutes per pickup, rather than trying to absorb all four strings at once.
- **Say and Play exercise.** Play every fret up a string in turn, saying each note name aloud while looking at that fret: sharp names ascending (E, F, F♯, G, G♯, A, A♯, B, C, C♯, D, D♯, E — 12th fret = the octave), flat names descending on the way back (E, E♭, D, D♭, C, B, B♭, A, A♭, G, G♭, F, E). Explicitly framed as a *navigation* drill, not a memorization drill — it's what lets you step off a memorized money note and reach a nearby unfamiliar fret (e.g. the 4th fret of the A string has no money-note anchor of its own, so find the nearest one — 3rd fret A string, C — and walk one Say-and-Play step to C♯).
- **Octave shape.** Same note name, one octave up or down: **up two strings, up two frets** (or the mirror going down). Worked examples: 8th fret E string (C) → 10th fret D string (also C); 7th fret D string (A) → 5th fret E string (also A).
- **Unison shape.** The identical pitch on a neighboring string: **up one string, down five frets** (or the mirror: down one string, up five frets). Worked examples: 6th fret E string (B♭) → 1st fret A string (also B♭, since 6 − 5 = 1); 3rd fret D string (F) → 8th fret A string (also F, since 3 + 5 = 8).
- **Dot-marker mnemonics.** The inlay dots form a "skeleton" of known landmarks so you're never more than one fret away from a memorized note: open strings E A D G = "eat and drink grapes"; 3rd fret G C F B♭ = "guitarists can frequently be flat"; 5th fret A D G C = "and drink grapes, captain" (or "all dogs get cats"); 7th fret B E A D = "bead" (spells the word); 9th fret C♯ F♯ B E = "if you C♯, you'll F♯, and then be" (BassBuzz's own admittedly weak mnemonic). The 12th fret repeats the open strings (the octave point); frets past the 12th mirror the lower dots (15th = same shape as 3rd, etc.). Take one dot fret at a time — expect this to take weeks to fully internalize.
- **Bass GPS drill.** The recall test: look away from the bass, fret a random note without looking, then look back and say the note name as fast as possible. Start restricted to the money-note zone before expanding to the whole neck. Gamify with a metronome — 4 beats to look away and fret a note, 4 beats to look back and say the name — and increase tempo over time as accuracy holds.
- **Practice-diet warning.** Explicitly cautions against getting stuck doing only fretboard memorization exercises — once the map is workable, apply it while playing actual songs (calling out note names as you play basslines), which the video frames as the real payoff of the whole system.

# SYN-C10 — Wavetable moves the timbre at the source

**Q.** Where does a wavetable synth's timbral movement come from, compared with a subtractive one's?

> [!question]- Answer
> **From the source — a scannable table of single-cycle waveforms whose position is modulatable.** Subtractive imposes its movement downstream by filtering harmonics away; wavetable generates it at the oscillator, so the harmonic content itself changes while the pitch stays put. Set position statically and it's just an ordinary oscillator on an unusual waveform — **modulating position is the entire reason the method exists.** Slow LFO gives an evolving pad, an envelope gives a bright attack into a mellow body, velocity makes harder playing select a more aggressive waveform.

## Why it matters

It tells you which control to reach for when a sound needs to move. Position modulation changes what harmonics are there rather than filtering existing ones, which is why it usually beats a cutoff sweep for a pad. Two cautions: most tables hold a few useful waveforms and a lot of transitional material, so modulate between the good positions rather than sweeping the whole table — and on bass, sweeping position shifts the fundamental's level and reads as inconsistent volume.

## Drill

Hold one note on [[Wavetable]] with the amp envelope at full sustain and drag the position marker across the table by hand. No filter movement at all.

## Devices found in this tune (by location)

| Location | Device | What's happening |
|---|---|---|
| Measures 6–9 | Two 5–1 modulations | 5–1 in B, then 5–1 in E — signals a key change each time |
| Home key (ii chord) | Dominant two chord | D7 in place of the expected Dm7/Dm7♭5 — a static lydian-dominant/tonic-dominant color, not a functioning V |
| End of tune | Passing diminished chords | C°7 (read as rootless A7, resolving to Dm) and E♭° (resolving downward to D, read as triads from the diminished scale) |
| Measure 26 | Backdoor dominant | B♭7 → Cmaj7 — the ♭VII7→I backdoor cadence, treated with the same lydian-dominant/tonic-dominant sound as the dominant-two chord |
| Last 3 bars | Chord walkup progression | ii chord walks up to the V chord instead of sitting static |
| Closing turnaround | Tadd Dameron turnaround | A dense chromatic turnaround, simplified via C minor pentatonic or the "Coltrane pattern" (1-2-3-5 chord tones per chord) |

## Steal this

- **Pick one standard that happens to be harmonically dense and drill it as a survey of the whole vocabulary, rather than sampling one device per tune across many tunes.** → [[Song Practice]]
- **A diminished chord's resolution DIRECTION decides how to play it** — resolving up a half step into a dominant target → read it as a rootless dominant a half step below; resolving DOWN → read it as triads out of the diminished scale instead. A clean, generalizable rule, not tune-specific. → [[Diminished Chords]]
- **"Dominant two chord" and "backdoor dominant" are the same underlying sound wearing two different chord-symbol hats** — both get the lydian-dominant/tonic-dominant treatment (melodic minor off the 5th), so learning one grip vocabulary covers both. → [[Modal Jazz]] § *Tonic dominant*, [[Turnarounds]] § *Backdoor*
- **When a passage looks harmonically overwhelming, there's almost always a cheaper legal read available** — outline the key center instead of chasing every chord (walkup progressions), or lean on a single pentatonic scale across a dense turnaround (Tadd Dameron) — reserve the literal chord-by-chord read for when you actually want that level of detail. → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Turnarounds]]
- **Some chord changes exist to harmonize the melody, not to move through function — check whether the "chord progression" is actually just voicing a static or repeating melody note before analyzing it as real harmonic motion.** → [[Reharmonization]]

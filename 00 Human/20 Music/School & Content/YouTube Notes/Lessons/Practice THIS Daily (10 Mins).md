## What's taught

- **The five-fret-position insight (context before level 1).** Bassists default to thinking in four-fret chunks (one chunk per fretting finger), but the bass's strings are tuned in perfect fourths — **five** frets apart (E, F, F♯, G♯, A — five notes before the next string's open pitch repeats). Worked example of the trap: walking G to C by four-fret logic (G, G♯, A, A♯) skips **B**, the note directly before C. **A full position on bass is actually five frets, not four** — the fix for not getting lost or missing notes on the neck. The exercise deliberately spans frets 2–6 to build this awareness; it doesn't require a big physical stretch, just **microshifting** within the five-fret window as needed.
- **Level 1 — the basic riff and tempo target.** One-finger-per-fret, frets 2–6, starting middle finger: middle–index–index–ring–pinky, index–middle–ring–pinky–middle–index, [small shift], pinky–ring–middle–index. BPM targets *(general starting point)*: 100 beginner, 150 intermediate, 200+ advanced, played as smooth quarter notes.
- **Level 2 — fixing flying fingers, two distinct fixes for two directions.** Ascending fingerings (index→middle→ring→pinky): **add** each finger without lifting the previous one — the hand closes onto the position progressively rather than each finger acting alone. Descending fingerings (pinky→ring→middle→index): the reverse shape — start with all four down at the pinky, then **subtract** fingers one at a time going back rather than lifting the whole hand at once.
- **Two overwhelm-avoidance tips for level 2.** (1) Take it super slow first — no tempo/rhythm at all, one note at a time, focused purely on fretting-hand coordination; add tempo only once smooth. (2) Chunk it — work a small handful of notes (e.g. the first five) in slow motion until solid, then add the next chunk, then the next, then piece them together (the additive/chunking method).
- **Level 2 BPM target:** same 100/150/200 ladder as level 1, now with the flying-fingers fix applied.
- **Level 3 — fixing the plucking hand.** Slow tempos let a beginner cheat by plucking everything with one finger, which caps how fast the line can ever go. Two named options: **strict alternate** (index-middle-index-middle throughout — simple, scalable, the default), or **incorporate raking** (reuse a plucking finger across a string crossing instead of alternating through it) at two specific spots in this riff: coming down from the octave, **G string → D string**, and again **D string → A string**. Advice for bringing the fix in: drop the tempo back down to whatever speed lets the plucking land clean, then rebuild toward the level-1 BPM target.
- **Level 3 BPM target:** unchanged — the same ladder, now plucking-clean.
- **Level 4 — root, fifth, octave theory + three walking patterns.** The riff's only three "core" notes are **root, fifth, octave**; every other note is a walk-up or walk-down connecting them. Bar shape: root → walk to fifth → walk to octave → walk to fifth → root.
  - **Pattern 1 — chromatic walk up to the fifth** (three half steps leading in), and the **same chromatic walk up into the octave**.
  - **Pattern 2 — chromatic walk down** — same chromatic logic (three half steps, ignoring the scale), played back down across the strings; visually a "weird looking shape" but mechanically the same device in reverse.
  - **Pattern 3 — scale walk down** — uses the C major scale (diatonic, not chromatic) to walk back down to the root.
  - This specific bassline sits in a bluesy context in the video, but the three patterns are named as portable to jazz walking bass, gospel, and even the intro of "Smoke on the Water."
- **Level 4 BPM target:** unchanged from levels 1–3 — level 4 is a theory-framing layer on the same notes already being played, not a new tempo goal.
- **Level 5 — moving root notes (chord changes), intermediate and advanced.** Most songs change chords, so a static root-fifth-octave isn't the whole job.
  - **Intermediate:** add one more root — move the exercise up a string to **F**, starting the pattern in **C on the E string** (instead of the A string) so F is reachable without a full position change. The first three bars stay identical; bar four changes because the target root has moved — a **scale walk-up** leads into F on the next downbeat, the riff repeats from F on the A string, and bar four changes again with a scale-walk-down variation to resolve back to C, looping the exercise. *(Transcript note: this segment's exact note names are auto-caption-garbled — captured qualitatively above rather than guessed precisely.)*
  - **Advanced:** a free companion PDF (linked in the video description, not accessible from the transcript) walks the same exercise through **all 12 notes** as root notes.
  - Same fretting/plucking/tempo checks from levels 1–3 apply throughout level 5.
- **General framing, stated directly:** going too fast defeats the whole point — you lose the flying-fingers fix, the plucking pattern, *and* the theory layer simultaneously, not just raw speed. Go only as fast as each level actually holds up clean.

## What's taught
Another "Prance Connors" lecture, this one walking through all seven modes of the major scale by building each one on the white keys and holding a root-note drone underneath for comparison. Rather than deriving each mode from a formula, it demonstrates them "by ear" — same seven white keys, different starting note each time, checked against Ableton's Scale device — while assigning a plain-language mood word to each. The most useful takeaway is the mood-to-mode shortcut: pick the emotional target first, then choose the mode that matches, rather than defaulting to major/minor.

## Techniques
- **Build every mode from the same 7 white keys, just starting on a different note, and hold the root as a drone to isolate its color** — C to C (Ionian), D to D (Dorian), E to E (Phrygian), F to F (Lydian), G to G (Mixolydian), A to A (Aeolian), B to B (Locrian); doubling the root down an octave as a sustained drone underneath makes each mode's distinct flavor audible against a fixed reference point. → [[Diatonic Modes]]
- **Mood-word shortcut for picking a mode:** Ionian = happy, Dorian = melancholy-but-hopeful, Phrygian = dark/evil, Lydian = joyous/fantastical, Mixolydian = dreamy/romantic, Aeolian = sad (the most commonly used), Locrian = messed-up/scary (rarely used as a real home key). → [[Diatonic Modes]]
- **Verify/build any mode via Ableton's Scale feature** — click the root note, pick the scale/mode from the dropdown, and the piano roll highlights every note that belongs to it — a faster sanity-check than counting whole/half steps by hand each time. → [[Scale]]

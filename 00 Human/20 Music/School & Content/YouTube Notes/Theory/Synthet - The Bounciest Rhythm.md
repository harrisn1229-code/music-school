# Synthet - The Bounciest Rhythm

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TdovF-zHdUA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TdovF-zHdUA)

## Synopsis

A swing-feel explainer: swing is the offbeat 8th note of each beat pushed later in time, applied first to a melody, then to a whole song. Synthet draws a precise line between swing and triplets — remove the middle note of a triplet and the remaining long-short pattern *is* a swung 8th, but a true triplet always keeps that exact 2:1 spacing while swing is more fluid (light swing vs. heavy swing, anywhere in between) — and separates both from a straight offbeat placement. Ends with the practical side: swing is natural when clapped by hand, but in a DAW needs an explicit swing/groove quantize setting.

## What's taught

- **Swing = the offbeat 8th note delayed** — normally a bar's 8th notes sit at even spacing; swing pushes every *second* 8th note later, turning straight "1 and 2 and" into a long-short "1-and 2-and" (dotted-feel) pattern. Demonstrated on a melody first, then applied to a whole arrangement for a "punk-dunk" long-short feel.
- **Swing vs. triplets — related but distinct** — remove the middle note of a triplet and what's left *is* mathematically a swung 8th note. The difference: a triplet's spacing is always the exact 2:1 ratio; swing is more fluid — the delayed note can sit anywhere between straight and full-triplet placement, light swing to heavy swing, "a matter of feel."
- **Swing vs. plain offbeat isn't the same thing** — offbeat emphasis alone (see [[Synthet - The Undeniably Best Rhythm]]) is a different device; swing specifically means the *timing* of alternating notes is uneven, not just which notes are accented. When swing is the main rhythmic driver, a straight beat layered on top reads as "off."
- **Genre note:** jazz leans on swing constantly — removing it from a jazz-associated line makes it read as noticeably "less jazzy" even when nothing else changes. Swing also reshapes how a melody's held notes feel, not just the rhythm section.
- **In a DAW: quantize to a swing preset, then dial the amount up from there** rather than trying to manually offset every note — the straight MIDI grid needs an explicit swing setting to produce this by default.

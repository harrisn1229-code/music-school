# The SECRET to using CHROMATIC notes

## Synopsis
Jack Ruch builds eight worked guitar licks in the key of E♭ major to demonstrate one repeatable device: a **chromatic enclosure used as a rhythmic pickup**, always timed to land exactly on a chord tone at the moment a chord change happens. The first four licks work through a **major 251** (Fm7 → B♭7 → E♭maj7), targeting the root, minor 3rd, 5th, and ♭7 of the Fm7 chord in turn — one enclosure per arpeggio note, moving up through the chord. The last four licks switch to a **minor 251** built on E♭'s relative minor (Dm7♭5 → G7 → Cm7), targeting the same four scale degrees again but this time on the D half-diminished chord. The pedagogical hook tying both halves together: Ruch explicitly points out that **Dm7♭5 and Fm7 share three of their four notes** (F, A♭, C), because Dm7♭5 is really "an Fm6 voicing with a D in the bass instead of an E♭" — so three of the four chromatic-enclosure shapes from the major-251 half get reused verbatim over the minor-251 half, just resolving to a different V chord (G7 instead of B♭7) and a different I (Cm7 instead of E♭maj7). Every enclosure follows the same underlying grammar he names once and reuses throughout: **two chromatic notes above the target, one chromatic note below**, or the reverse (one above, two below) — always a rhythmic device to fill the beats leading into a downbeat chord-tone landing, not a decorative flourish for its own sake. The video closes by generalizing past the eight bebop-flavored examples: the same device works over blues changes, works "in a whole range of styles," and the core idea — pick a target chord tone you already know how to find, then use chromatic notes to fill however much time you have before the chord change lands — is framed as a rhythmic phrasing tool first and a note-choice trick second.

## What's taught

- **The core device, named once and reused throughout: an enclosure is 2–3 chromatic notes used as a rhythmic pickup into a target chord tone, timed to land exactly as the chord changes underneath.** Two variants recur: "two notes above, one note below" and "one note above, two notes below" — both converge chromatically onto the target from both sides. Landing precisely on the downbeat of the chord change is treated as more important than the specific note content of the enclosure itself. → [[Chord-Tone Soloing]] (Shape 3 — the same "note above the chord tone, note below" device, worked here as a rhythmic-pickup variant rather than a static shape) · [[Enclosures Through the Scale]] (the chord-tone-independent cousin of this exact device) · [[Bebop Vocabulary]] §2 *Enclosures*
- **Major 251 licks 1–4, all over Fm7 (ii) → B♭7 (V) → E♭maj7 (I), one lick per arpeggio note of Fm7, moving root → ♭3 → 5 → ♭7:**
  - **Lick 1 — targets the root, F.** Enclosure: **E♭ → G → G♭ → E → F** (two notes above, one chromatic note below, landing on the root). From there: up part of an F minor scale, chromatic walk into the **3rd of B♭7** (D), up a **D diminished arpeggio**, then a second enclosure — one note above, two chromatic notes below — into the **3rd of E♭maj7** (G). → [[Diminished Chords]] (the D° arpeggio riding under a dominant chord), [[Guide Tones]]
  - **Lick 2 — targets the minor 3rd, A♭.** Enclosure: **G → B♭ → A → G → A♭** (the same "two above, one below" shape as lick 1, moved to a new target). Continues with part of an A♭maj7 arpeggio, the 3rd of B♭7 again, another D diminished arpeggio, then the same enclosure into E♭maj7's 3rd — but this time resolves onto the **major 7th (D)** instead of the 5th, a deliberate variation on an otherwise near-identical line.
  - **Lick 3 — targets the 5th, C.** Enclosure: **B♭ → D♭ → B♭ → C** (down, up a half step above the target, back down, then chromatic walk up to land — a rhythmic pickup shape distinct from licks 1–2's "two above/one below"). Continues down an F minor scale, another enclosure into the **3rd of B♭7** (D), up a D diminished arpeggio, then the enclosure into **E♭'s 3rd**, moved up one string from lick 1's version.
  - **Lick 4 — targets the ♭7, E♭.** Enclosure: **D → F → E → D → E♭** (a half step below the target, up a whole step, back down a half step, down again, then land — the most angular of the four pickups). Same F-minor-scale-down → B♭7-3rd enclosure → D diminished arpeggio → E♭-3rd enclosure tail as licks 1 and 3, moved up the fretboard onto a lower string set — explicitly framed as working out the same vocabulary in different fretboard positions.
- **The pivot insight bridging both halves of the video: Dm7♭5 and Fm7 share three notes (F, A♭, C), so Dm7♭5 reads as "an Fm6 voicing with D in the bass instead of E♭."** Because of that overlap, three of the four chromatic-enclosure targets from licks 1–4 (root F, ♭3 A♭, ♭7 C) recur verbatim as enclosure targets in licks 5–8 over the half-diminished chord — only the destination chords change (G7/Cm7 instead of B♭7/E♭maj7). → [[The ii-V-I]] § *The minor version*, [[Modal Jazz]] (Dorian vs. Aeolian ♭6/natural-6 distinction on the note *above* a chord's 5th, the same kind of function-dependent decision)
- **Minor 251 licks 5–8, all over Dm7♭5 (ii°) → G7 (V) → Cm7 (i), targeting root → ♭3 → ♭5 → ♭7 of Dm7♭5 in turn:**
  - **Lick 5 — targets the root, D.** Enclosure: **C → E♭ → C → D** (down, up, chromatic walk up to land — mirrors lick 3's pickup shape reused on a new target). Continues through the Dm7♭5 arpeggio, a second enclosure into the **root of G7**, then straight up a G7 arpeggio (root–3rd–5th–♭7), closing on **G, the 5th of Cm7**.
  - **Lick 6 — targets F, the minor 3rd of Dm7♭5 (the same F targeted as Fm7's root in lick 1) — reuses lick 1's enclosure verbatim: E♭ → G → G♭ → E → F.** Plays out of the Dm7♭5 arpeggio (a "big stretch" fingering), moves to G7, then plays an **A♭ diminished arpeggio** over the G7 — giving a **G7♭9** sound that resolves smoothly into Cm7 — before closing the phrase. → [[Diminished Chords]], [[Altered Chords]]
  - **Lick 7 — targets A♭, the ♭5 of Dm7♭5 (same A♭ targeted as Fm7's minor 3rd in lick 2) — reuses lick 2's enclosure verbatim: G → B♭ → A → G → A♭.** Continues with a piece of the Dm7♭5 arpeggio, then a chromatic enclosure into the **3rd of G7** (B), landing finally on **E♭, the 3rd of Cm7**.
  - **Lick 8 — targets C, the ♭7 of Dm7♭5 (same C targeted as Fm7's 5th in lick 3) — reuses lick 3's enclosure verbatim: B♭ → D♭ → B♭ → C.** Works down an F minor scale, targets the **3rd of G7**, then plays a **B diminished arpeggio** for a **G7♭9** color, and closes with a deliberately "colorful" (not chord-tone-strict) move: slide up to Cm7's minor 3rd, then grab the **9th** on top for a **Cm9** sound — flagged explicitly as a departure from strict root/3rd/5th targeting, chosen because it "sounds good in this instance." → [[Altered Chords]]
- **Explicit generalization at the close: this is bebop-style enclosure vocabulary, but Ruch states he uses it "in straight ahead blues all the time," and frames it as a rhythmic phrasing device first** — useful whenever you know a chord's basic chord tones and want a way to fill the beats leading into the downbeat of a chord change, independent of genre. Works especially well over swing/shuffle rhythms. → [[Blues Form and Harmony]], [[Chord-Tone Soloing]] (the "learn it as a mechanical drill, then use it selectively" practice framing)

# Jacob Collier Q&A: Key Change Hack, Advanced Triplets, Breaking Rules, Ear Training, Music School

## Synopsis
A rapid-fire Q&A with Jacob Collier covering a genuinely useful harmonic trick alongside a lot of mindset material. The concrete content clusters into three areas: a "secret weapon" modulation hack built on the symmetry of the fully-diminished 7th chord (lower any one of its four notes a half step and it resolves into a different key each time), a practical ear-training habit of singing the notes of the chords you play to feel voice-leading motion in real time, and a challenge to the idea that triplet-based rhythms need to sit on a rigid grid, citing Gnawa music from Morocco and older British folk music as examples of loose, "lilting" triplet feel. The rest is philosophy — freedom over knowledge, fearlessness about "wrong" notes, skepticism of formal music education and arbitrary rules, and active/obsessive listening as the real engine of ear training — valuable as creative-mindset color but not routable to a specific theory concept.

## Techniques
- **Diminished-chord key-change hack — the "four-way crossroads."** A fully-diminished 7th chord (e.g. C°7 = C, E♭, F♯/G♭, A) is built entirely from stacked minor 3rds, so it's maximally symmetric. Lower any ONE of its four notes by a half step (a semitone) and the chord resolves cleanly into a different key each time: C→B lands you in E, E♭→D lands you in G, F♯→F lands you in B♭, A→A♭ lands you in D♭ — four destinations spaced a minor 3rd apart, mirroring the chord's own symmetry. Practical use: land on any diminished chord mid-progression, then simply pick which note to drop a half step to choose your destination key — a one-chord modulation pivot with a built-in menu of options. → [[Diminished Chords]] — Sol State, [Jacob Collier Q&A: Key Change Hack, Advanced Triplets, Breaking Rules, Ear Training, Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sa3A72qnFgI)
- **Every dominant chord carries the same diminished "crossroads" power.** Collier's framing: a dominant chord is essentially a triad with a dominant note added on top of a diminished shape, so every triad is, in a sense, "every other chord" — meaning the four-way resolution trick above isn't limited to spelled-out diminished chords; it's latent in dominant chords too. (Corroborates the existing vault note's reverse framing that a 7♭9 chord is itself a diminished chord with an added root.) → [[Diminished Chords]] — Sol State, [Jacob Collier Q&A: Key Change Hack, Advanced Triplets, Breaking Rules, Ear Training, Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sa3A72qnFgI)
- **Sing the chords you play to feel voice-leading motion.** Singing back the individual notes of a chord as you play it creates an immediate, instantaneous emotional connection ("if you sing, you emote"), and deliberately experimenting with a felt vocal line while playing builds real-time understanding of each note's role. Over time this sharpens your ear for the specific *motion* of each note between chords — and the more clearly you can feel that inter-chord motion, the more nuanced and "high resolution" your harmonic storytelling becomes. → [[Transcribing and Playing by Ear]] — Sol State, [Jacob Collier Q&A: Key Change Hack, Advanced Triplets, Breaking Rules, Ear Training, Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sa3A72qnFgI)
- **Train your ear through obsessive, active listening to musicians you love.** Collier says his own ear training came almost entirely from actively/consciously listening to musicians he liked and working out what they were doing — not formal drilling — until their musical vocabulary was internalized deeply enough to become reflexive (he jokes he often doesn't register song lyrics because he's too locked into the musical elements). → [[Scale Degree Ear Training]] — Sol State, [Jacob Collier Q&A: Key Change Hack, Advanced Triplets, Breaking Rules, Ear Training, Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sa3A72qnFgI)
- **Free triplets from the grid for a livelier feel.** Rhythms built on 3 don't have to divide time perfectly evenly — Collier points to Gnawa music from Morocco and older British folk music, both of which give triplet subdivisions a loose, "lilting" flexibility rather than snapping to a strict grid. He frames "the grid is correct" as a myth worth deliberately breaking in triplet-heavy parts. Worth testing against, not adopting outright: this cuts against the vault's peer-reviewed groove research, where quantized timing rated *higher* for swing feel than natural deviation in at least one controlled study — treat grid-freedom as a stylistic choice to A/B per part, not a rule. → [[Groove and Microtiming]] — Sol State, [Jacob Collier Q&A: Key Change Hack, Advanced Triplets, Breaking Rules, Ear Training, Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sa3A72qnFgI)
- **Freedom matters more than knowledge for creative risk-taking.** Compounding musical knowledge with genuine creative freedom — not worrying about whether a note is "wrong," whether something "sounds good," or whether there's a single right way to do it — is what actually produces interesting music; fearlessness about experimentation is treated as more important than any accumulated theory. (Mindset, not routed to a specific theory note.) — Sol State, [Jacob Collier Q&A: Key Change Hack, Advanced Triplets, Breaking Rules, Ear Training, Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sa3A72qnFgI)
- **Rules in music are arbitrary — formal education can make you afraid of "wrong" notes.** Collier says he doesn't think music school is necessary: learning is something you do for yourself, education is something other people do to you, and both have a place at different points in a musical life, but anyone who claims to know the definitively "right way" has simply stopped learning. His own best teachers were often just musicians and albums he listened to closely, not formal instructors. (Mindset, not routed to a specific theory note.) — Sol State, [Jacob Collier Q&A: Key Change Hack, Advanced Triplets, Breaking Rules, Ear Training, Music School](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sa3A72qnFgI)

# P.S. I Love You — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro · Verse (**10 bars**, phrased 3 + 2 + 2½ + 2½) · Verse · Bridge (a strict 8) · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Outro with a complete ending. Two bridges, one verse between, no instrumental verse — and the **intro is a disguised variant of the bridge**.

D major with Aeolian colouring: beyond the diatonic set it borrows **♭VI and ♭VII from parallel D minor** ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The oddest chord is the **C♯7 in the intro standing in for V** — the diatonic chord there is a diminished seventh, already a rootless V⁷, and turning it into a dominant seventh sets up a resolution to F♯ that instead lands on I by pure stepwise voice leading in every part.

Two progression types run against normal directed root motion. **Chord streams** with stepwise roots (I–ii–I, ♭VI–♭VII–I) read as languid, out of Impressionism and jazz. And **deceptive resolution used repeatedly** — V to vi, later V to ♭VI, and even the eventual arrival at I reached sideways through ♭VII ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]). The verse's phrasing is articulated almost entirely by where those evasions land ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]); the bridge is the contrast, I–IV–V only, square 4+4.

Not a rock record — tempo and percussion put it nearer lounge-pop or Latin dance. The best arrangement detail: **in every verse but the last the backing voices sing only on isolated words**, functioning like italics, and in the final verse they abandon that and sing straight through.

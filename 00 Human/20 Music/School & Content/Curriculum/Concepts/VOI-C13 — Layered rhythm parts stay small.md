# VOI-C13 — Layered rhythm parts stay small

**Q.** You are stacking three rhythm guitar parts on one progression. What keeps them from turning into mud, and what actually separates them?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Closed triads — three notes, no doublings, no six-string chords.** They are lean on purpose, which is what lets three of them sit on top of each other. What separates the layers is **string group, neck position and tone**, not writing different material for each: one triad vocabulary, relocated. A bed part low and arpeggiated, a backbeat part on the top three strings, a melodic double-stop part above that.

## Why it matters

It is the same "which notes do I leave out" economy as a shell voicing, applied for a production reason instead of a voice-leading one — you are buying headroom to layer. It is also the arranging version of the two-compers problem: the division is by register and role either way, whether the other player is in the room or on another track.

## Drill

15 min: record three passes of one four-chord loop — closed triads on the D-G-B strings arpeggiated, the same chords on the top three strings on the backbeat, and a double-stop line above. Change the tone between passes, not the chords.

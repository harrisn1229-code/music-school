# Cosmic Synth Slide | "Everybody Loves the Sunshine" by Roy Ayers

## Synopsis

A deep, nuance-focused patch recreation of Roy Ayers' "Everybody Loves the Sunshine" lead — a 3-oscillator ARP 2600 sliding melody sound, unpacked in three layers: the raw patch, the mix balance between its layered oscillators, and the resonance/release/reverb shaping that gives it its falling-pitch tail. The recurring theme is that small, deliberate imbalances (a quieter harmony voice, a detuned octave) are what make a patch sound alive rather than mechanical.

## Techniques

- **Voice a harmony interval (a fourth below the lead) at LOWER volume than the main note, the way an orchestrator would double a melody with extra strings/brass rather than balance them equally.** Oscillator 1 (sawtooth, the "main" pitch) carries the melody; oscillator 2 (pulse wave, 10% pulse width, tuned a fourth below) is mixed quieter in the filter's built-in mini-mixer — audibly thinner/weaker than the main note rather than matched. Same logic as bringing out a piano melody over the left hand, or doubling a lead trumpet/violin line to make it stand out without literally playing it louder solo.
- **"Wyoming tuning" — when deliberately detuning oscillators for dissonance/beating, spread the detune asymmetrically around the true pitch (one voice sharp, another flat) rather than detuning a single voice in one direction, so the perceived center frequency stays anchored.** Applied here to a high-octave third oscillator, tuned deliberately sharp and mixed in at low volume for a subtle beating/dissonance — "almost like a jazz element," not too perfect. Framed as a deliberate exception: Anthony's general rule is to keep oscillators IN tune (synths drift out on their own without help) — this is a case where controlled mistuning is the point.
- **High resonance set just short of self-oscillation, with the RELEASE time (not the resonance amount) controlling the audible "tail."** Resonance pushed to the edge of oscillating; on note release, the filter envelope's release stage produces an audible falling pitch — because modulating filter cutoff frequency IS modulating a pitch when resonance is high enough to ring, so the filter itself becomes a secondary pitched sound source during the decay.
- **VCA envelope as a simple gate (no attack, long release), used specifically to let the filter envelope's fall-off tail be heard rather than cut it off.** Same "VCA = gate, filter envelope = the actual shape" division seen elsewhere in this channel's patches — here the long VCA release is the deliberate choice that lets the resonant filter tail ring out audibly instead of being choked by the amp stage.
- **Send the raw dry patch to a spring reverb DIRECTLY (not through a send/return at a controlled level) for a wetter-than-usual reverb blend, then mix the reverb return back against the straight signal at the output stage.** A different reverb-routing choice than a typical mix-bus send — driving the reverb input hot on purpose rather than treating it as a subtle room tail.

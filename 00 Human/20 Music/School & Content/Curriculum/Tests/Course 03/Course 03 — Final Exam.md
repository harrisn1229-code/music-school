> **Q1.** A natural minor: A B C D E F G
> From the parallel side: major with **♭3, ♭6, ♭7**.
>
> **Q2.** A harmonic minor: A B C D E F G♯
> A melodic minor: A B C D E F♯ G♯
> Harmonic raises the 7th; melodic raises the 6th and 7th ascending.
>
> **Q3.** **To supply a leading tone and a functioning V — it creates an augmented 2nd between ♭6 and ♮7**
> The gap between F and G♯ is a step and a half, which is awkward melodically.
>
> **Q4.** **It raises the 6th as well, smoothing out that augmented 2nd**
> That gap is the entire reason the third minor scale exists.
>
> **Q5.** C major pentatonic: C D E G A · A minor pentatonic: A C D E G
> Same five notes from different roots — the relative major/minor relationship, which is why one shape covers both.
>
> **Q6.** **The 4th and the 7th — they're the notes that create half-step tension against a major triad**
> Remove them and nothing in the scale sits a semitone from a chord tone.
>
> **Q7.** **Derivative rotates a parent scale; parallel alters from the same root. Parallel is the one**
> Parallel keeps the mode's own tonic as home and puts the characteristic note in front of you.
>
> **Q8.** **Lydian ♯4 · Mixolydian ♭7 · Dorian ♮6 · Phrygian ♭2 · Locrian ♭5**
> The first two against major, the last three against minor. Locrian is the exception to one note per mode — against natural minor it has **both a ♭2 and a ♭5** — and the ♭5 is the one that names it.
>
> **Q9.** D Dorian: D E F G A B C · F Lydian: F G A B C D E
> D minor with a raised 6th; F major with a raised 4th.
>
> **Q10.** E Phrygian: E F G A B C D · G Mixolydian: G A B C D E F
> E minor with a ♭2; G major with a ♭7.
>
> **Q11.** **Its tonic triad is diminished — there's nothing stable to come home to**
> The ♭5 destabilises the one chord a key has to be able to rest on.
>
> **Q12.** **D natural minor**
> Every note played is also in D minor, so nothing distinguishes them. The characteristic note has to sound.
>
> **Q13.** **Keep the tonic, change the collection — those four are the triads of C natural minor**
> *Also accepted:* the parallel minor
>
> **Q14.** **Cmaj7♯11 takes Lydian, decided by the ♯4 · Cm7♭5 takes Locrian, decided by the ♭5**
> In both cases the chord's defining alteration is the scale's characteristic note.
>
> **Q15.** **The chord names the notes that must be there; the scale supplies the rest**
> The blues case is friction on purpose rather than an exception to the rule.
> *Rubric (5):* states the rule correctly (2) · says the ♭3 against the chord's major 3rd is the intended sound rather than an error (2) · notes that three of minor pentatonic's five notes are the dominant's own root, 5th and ♭7, so the ♭3 is the only deliberate clash in it (1)
> The rule is that the chord dictates its non-negotiable notes and you choose a scale whose characteristic note matches. Minor pentatonic over a dominant looks like a violation because its ♭3 sits a semitone from the chord's major 3rd. It isn't one, for two reasons: that grind is the blues sound, so the friction is the point rather than a side effect, and three of its five notes are the chord's own root, 5th and ♭7, so the ♭3 is the only note in it that deliberately argues with the harmony. (The natural 4th — the note that genuinely clashes with a major 3rd — is *in* minor pentatonic rather than omitted from it; blues passes through it instead of resting on it.) It's a deliberate exception with a mechanism, not a counterexample.
>
> **Q16.** **Spelling from the root — fix it by altering the parallel major or minor by one characteristic note**
> *Rubric (5):* names spelling-from-an-arbitrary-root as the missing skill (2) · says the fix is parallel thinking rather than derivative (2) · works E Phrygian as E minor with a ♭2 (1)
> Reciting the modes in order is a derivative-thinking skill: it tells you the sequence but always routes you back through a parent scale, so an unfamiliar root means hunting. What's missing is spelling from the root itself. The fix is to work in parallel — take the major or minor scale on that root, which you already know, and apply the single characteristic alteration. E Phrygian is E natural minor with the 2nd flattened, giving E F G A B C D, and you never had to think about C major to get there.

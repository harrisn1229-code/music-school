# Chapman Welch - Chicken Pickin

![](https://youtube.com/shorts/SgqXOru9DT4)

Nate's capture note: *"Chicken pickin technique."*

Hybrid picking, taught in 90 seconds. Passed down from an unnamed retired Nashville session player in Muscle Shoals — the source took one lesson, taped it, and never got the name.

## The technique

**Pick plus middle finger only.** Other fingers come later; starting with two is the point.

The pattern:

```
down – pluck – up – down – pluck – up – down – pluck …
```

- **down** = pick, downstroke
- **pluck** = middle finger, snapping the string
- **up** = pick, upstroke

The snap is what makes the sound. The middle finger pulls the string slightly away from the fretboard and releases, so it slaps back — that percussive click is the "chicken."

## The one thing that makes it work

> *"That first one is like a skater step pushing you off."*

The opening downstroke isn't part of the repeating cell — it's the push that launches it. Counted `1 and 2 and 3 AND 4`, with the **first downstroke and the final pluck each being a quarter note** and the middle of the pattern running faster between them.

Getting that asymmetry right is the difference between the technique grooving and sounding like an exercise. It's an accent-and-rhythm idea, not a hand-mechanics one.

## Beyond the cliché

The source's own preference, and the reason this is worth keeping: *"you can also play single lines with it, which I like to do."*

Chicken pickin' is filed mentally under country double-stops and Don Rich licks, but hybrid picking as a *single-line* technique is broadly useful — it gives you string-skipping and wide intervals that alternate picking can't reach cleanly, and the plucked notes come out with a different attack, which is free articulation contrast within one line.

Relevant well outside country: funk, neo-soul comping, and anywhere a line needs two distinct attack timbres without switching tone.

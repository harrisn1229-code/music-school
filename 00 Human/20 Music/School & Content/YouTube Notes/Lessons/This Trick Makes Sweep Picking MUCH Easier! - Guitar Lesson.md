## Synopsis

A picking-approach fix for a specific hard diminished sweep-picking lick that resisted the standard "accent every group-of-4 with a downstroke" approach — solved by switching to an **upstroke-only** pattern instead, plus a picking-angle detail that made the switch actually work.

## Techniques

- **The standard (harder) approach:** accent the start of each group of four notes with a downstroke — gives a clear downstroke on the heavy accents (easier to feel in time) but forces a lot of right-hand travel switching between strings, which gets messy at speed.
- **The fix: upstrokes only for this lick.** Took hours to get used to the unfamiliar motion, but the right hand ends up doing far less physical work once internalized — directly reduced string-transition mess versus the downstroke-accented version.
- **The detail that actually made it work: picking angle.** A poorly-angled pick made the upstroke-only approach *harder* to learn at first, not easier — once the pick's angle of attack was adjusted (pointed in a specific direction relative to the strings), string transitions in the upstroke pattern became noticeably smoother and the lick sped up cleanly.
- **General takeaway:** when a sweep lick fights a "standard" picking approach, trying the opposite stroke-direction bias (all-up instead of accent-locked) is a real alternative worth hours of dedicated practice, not just a curiosity.

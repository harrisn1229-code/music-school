# Can AI compose good music?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XEQ9mqTL8Kk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XEQ9mqTL8Kk)

## Synopsis

An opinion piece (era: AIVA, a mid-2010s AI composer trained to predict the next note from a corpus of classical music) rather than a technique video. Argues AI-generated music is genuinely good at reproducing an existing style's conventions but structurally can't innovate beyond its training data, so it's most useful for "underscore" music — the intentionally-unobtrusive background bed under dialogue/gameplay, where the goal is competent and cheap rather than original. Also covers a real copyright ambiguity: AIVA's license assigns copyright to the paying user (free tier: AIVA keeps it), a company unilaterally deciding a question the video argues should be a legislative one, drawing a parallel to the "monkey selfie" copyright case (two court rulings: non-human authorship isn't copyrightable).

## Techniques

None in the technical/musical sense — this is a discussion piece, not instructional. The one durable concept: **"underscore" as a distinct compositional target** — music explicitly written to stay in the background (TV scene beds, video game loops, radio ads) rather than to be listened to on its own terms, which is a genuinely different brief than a song or a film theme and tolerates formulaic/predictable writing that would read as a flaw anywhere else.

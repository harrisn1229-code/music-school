## Synopsis

The dedicated demo video for the **Dank Sauces Rack Pack** (Gumroad, $10 at the time, stock-Ableton-only) that batch 6's [[dnksaus - Instrument Racks + Dank Sauces Updates]] note deliberately deferred to — dnksaus builds a rough track from scratch using nothing but racks from the pack, narrating each one as it goes on: sends (master, compression bus, wide bus ×2, sidechain switcher), drum sound design (Artifactory, Background Laser, Snare Drum of Fire, Dynamic EQ, sidechain Gate, Ride Maker), bass (Sub Bass + Bass Enhancer, Tilt EQ), lead/laser sound design (Big Growler, Automate Me, Freeze, Home Base, OTT Multiband Compression with presets, RC22, Syncopation Webs, Squishy Virus, Good Time, Search and Reverb, Record Buddy, Multi-Band Splitter, Mid/Side Width, Random LFOs), and mix utilities (Digital Clipper, High/Low Splitter, Right/Left Splitter). It's a whistle-stop tour — most racks get 5–15 seconds of screen time — so per-rack technique depth varies a lot; the ones with an actual stated setting, build order, or use-case are pulled out below and into [[Plugin Wishlist]], the rest are name-drops. He explicitly does not finish the track ("that song is not my favorite and I will not finish it") — the point is the rack tour, not the song.

## Techniques

- **Sends: Compression Bus + two Wide Bus variants + a Sidechain Switcher, all fed through Sidechain Send 0** — his standard send-track setup: a glue-compression return, two different "wide bus" width racks (stereo-widen an individual track without falling apart in mono), and a rack for A/B'ing different sidechain methods against each other; every send (not only drums) is routed through a shared Sidechain Send 0 so anything can duck against the same trigger. → [[Sidechaining]], [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Artifactory: staccato-note "artifact" generator, extend the note tail** — a rack built to generate glitchy artifacts from a source: play staccato notes and extend the note-off tail past the note length, which reads especially well when time-based devices (delay/echo-type effects) are already in the chain; a second version of the rack has those time-based devices built in already rather than added separately. Random variations via the rack's randomize control; results are "kind of crazy" enough that resampling a good pass is the standard next step. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Randomized Rack Design]]
- **Background Laser rack as a set-and-forget background SFX layer** — a laser/riser-style texture rack he defaults to for filling out a background layer, distinct from a lead-focused laser sound. → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Snare Drum of Fire: a metallic-snare recording rack, used as a capture tool not a finished sound** — build the metallic-snare rack, record with a scratch MIDI pattern, then delete that MIDI, drop in his own favorite snare sample, align it to the grid/transient, and adjust from there — the rack's job is shaping the metallic character on top of whichever snare sample gets fed into it, not generating the snare itself. Ties directly to the dedicated metallic-snare technique on [[Grain Delay]]/[[Snare Design]] documented from a later video in this same batch. → [[Snare Design]], [[Grain Delay]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Dynamic EQ rack: knob 1 = compression, knob 2 = expansion, plus an envelope control, for drum shaping** — a two-stage dynamics rack (compress one knob, expand with the other) with a separate envelope-shape control, favored specifically for reshaping individual drum hits rather than as a bus-glue tool. → [[Multiband Dynamics]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **A dedicated Gate sidechain rack suits short drum hits, not longer ones** — when auditioning his sidechain-method racks against a snare that rings out a bit, the Gate-style option is flagged as better suited to short, quickly-decaying hits — a longer snare doesn't gate cleanly the same way. → [[Sidechaining]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Ride Maker: turns hi-hats into rides, now rebuilt from pure stock plugins** — a rack that reshapes a hi-hat sample into ride-cymbal-like character; he'd previously released an earlier version of the same idea as a free download, and this Rack Pack version rebuilds the same effect using only stock Ableton devices. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Sub Bass + a tunable Bass Enhancer, tuned to the patch's note** — his sub-bass starter rack paired with the same wishlisted "Bass Enhancer" utility seen elsewhere in this batch's notes, tuned to whatever note the bass patch is playing. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[BASS Slot]], [[SUB Slot]]
- **Tilt EQ "high-end B": a frequency split where only the top distorts** — a tilt-EQ-flavored rack where content under a set frequency passes through clean and everything above it gets distorted — a controlled way to add harmonic aggression to only the top end of a sound while the fundamental/body stays clean. → [[EQ]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Big Growler on Operator, best with a square-wave oscillator, plus a one-knob "Automate Me" macro** — a growl/distortion-flavored macro rack applied to an Operator patch, stated to work best over a square-wave oscillator; ships with an "Automate Me" macro that's exactly what it sounds like — a single knob meant to be automated/performed live rather than set-and-forget. Used twice in the build (once on the initial laser patch, once later on a G Jones-style acid lead). → [[Operator]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **A noisy Freeze/laser rack voiced for already-noisy source material** — one of the pack's laser-texture racks (as opposed to the cleaner "Splicey Bloopy" laser option) is specifically suited to noisy, non-tonal samples, described as landing somewhere near a Space Laces-style noise texture; the instruction is simply to automate whatever parameter sounds cool once it's dialed in. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Home Base: a "you went too far" reset/safety rack** — kept on hand specifically because it's easy to over-process while chaining these racks together; framed as a recovery tool rather than a sound-design rack in its own right. → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **OTT Multiband Compression rack: macro'd crossovers + time-scaling, plus a "multiply up to 3×" control and two baked-in presets** — a rack wrapping Ableton's stock multiband/OTT territory with macro control over band crossover points and per-band time-scaling amounts, plus a **multiply** knob that stacks progressively more OTT instances in series as it's turned up (an "OTT ×10" preset exists purely to show off how far the multiply knob can push it) instead of manually duplicating OTT by hand. Ships with a bass-focused multiband-compression preset and a general-purpose one, plus an OTT preset nicknamed "MSG." → [[OTT]], [[Multiband Dynamics]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **RC22: the sequel to his RC21 rack from an earlier Flume-style video, "more goodies"** — built on a Wavetable patch; described only as an expanded version of a previously-released rack, no specific new parameters named on camera. → [[Wavetable]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Syncopation Webs: turns any patch "wubby" with syncopation** — a rhythmic-modulation rack for adding a syncopated wobble-bass-style movement to whatever it's applied to. → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Squishy Virus: an all-purpose "make it squishy" character rack** — applied twice in the build (once flagged as possibly better suited to a different sound entirely, once later as a texture layer alongside the High/Low Splitter) — a broad compression/distortion-flavored squash rather than a targeted tool. → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Good Time: a lead rack built on Live 11's per-rack random buttons** — praised specifically because Live 11 added a randomize control to every rack, which this preset leans into for fast variation-hunting on a lead patch. → [[Randomized Rack Design]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Search and Reverb: cycle reverb presets by number while keeping the dry signal up front** — lets you audition different reverb-preset numbers on a send while keeping the original (dry) sound cutting through, rather than the reverb reading as too wet/washed. → [[Reverb]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Record Buddy: a general-purpose "seasoning" utility** — described only as a seasoning-style rack (a light character/finishing touch), no specific mechanism stated on camera. → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Multi-Band Splitter: split frequencies apart without phasing artifacts, then process one band in isolation** — used here to isolate a pitch-shift effect to the mid band only, leaving low/high untouched and avoiding the comb-filtering that a naive parallel-filter split can introduce. → [[Multiband Dynamics]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Mid/Side Width: narrow a band after splitting it, rather than only widening** — reached for immediately after the Multi-Band Splitter above when the isolated mid-band effect read "a little too wide" — a reminder that a width rack is just as useful for narrowing/focusing a band as for widening one. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Random LFOs: map several parameters to independent randomized LFOs at once, then resample the best pass** — a utility rack for mapping multiple destinations (here, several Big Growler parameters on a second lead patch) to independently-random LFO modulation in one step, with the explicit expectation that it takes a few tries — treat it as a resample-and-pick-the-best-take tool, not a one-shot result. → [[Randomized Rack Design]], [[LFO]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Digital Clipper: Drive + ceiling for perceived loudness with headroom, plus DC/Color toggles** — a clipping-style loudness rack: push **Drive** up and pull the ceiling down to make a sound read bigger/louder while actually preserving headroom in the mix (the inverse-feeling but standard clip relationship), with separate on/off toggles for a DC-offset stage and a "Color" (saturation-flavored) stage. → [[Clipping and Loudness]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **High/Low Splitter: switch between a clean high/low split or a "dry + added high" blend — the "sub beefer" foundation** — the same splitter rack can either divide the signal into independent high and low bands, or instead keep the whole dry signal intact and layer an extra high-frequency band on top of it; the dry-plus-high configuration is his base recipe for a "sub beefer"-style low-end reinforcement patch. → [[BASS Slot]], [[SUB Slot]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Right/Left Splitter: run entirely different effects per channel** — an asymmetric-processing rack that lets the left and right channels each run a completely different effect (demoed with a bloopy delay on the right channel only and the Squishy Virus rack on the left) rather than the same effect panned differently — a fast route to Haas-adjacent width through genuinely different processing rather than just a time offset. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

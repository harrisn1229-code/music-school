# It's Boring, But It'll Make You Improvise PERFECT Solos

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UffwAqWVbQc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UffwAqWVbQc)

## Synopsis

Four deliberately unglamorous drills Brandon D'Eon says pros actually practice and intermediate players skip: whole-note chord-tone targeting on thirds (with a scalar-resolve extension), a consistent-subdivision rhythm drill, working out chord voicings across every fretboard position, and a legato-vs-staccato phrasing fix. He's explicit that each one deserves weeks of dedicated practice, not a single pass.

## Techniques

- **Drill 1 — Whole-note thirds targeting, then scalar-with-resolve.** Over a chord progression (worked example: Am–C–Em–D), play only the **3rd of each chord** as a sustained whole note through that chord's full bar — thirds specifically chosen over roots because most beginner improvisers already default to roots, so thirds forces new territory. Once that's solid without fail, extend it: hold the chord-tone (now for 2 beats instead of 4) then fill the remaining 2 beats with scalar movement (e.g. A Dorian or A minor pentatonic over Am) — **but the scalar material must still resolve back to a chord tone**, not just wander. The whole-note stage is a hard prerequisite gate — don't add the scalar half until the whole-note version is reliable.
- **Drill 2 — Consistent-subdivision rhythm drill.** Pick a rhythmic subdivision (8ths, triplets, 16ths) and play continuously in that subdivision through the whole progression, allowed scale (A Dorian or A minor pentatonic in the worked example) freely chosen but the *rhythm* locked. Demonstrated at 86 BPM. Any drift, rush, or drag invalidates the rep — if 16ths at the target tempo aren't clean, drop to triplets rather than fake it. *(This is the same drill as Routine 3 in [[The Five-Category Practice Method]] and Daniel Seriff's Consistent Rhythms drill in [[The Five Essentials of Guitar Practice]] — a third independent teacher converging on the identical backing-track/fixed-subdivision mechanism.)*
- **Drill 3 — Work out the progression's chord voicings in every position, not just one.** Same chord progression, chord tones physically mapped in the 5th, 7th, and 12th positions (restricted to the D/G/B strings for consistency), demonstrated with a solo built using *only* those mapped chord tones. **Named as an assigned follow-up exercise for the viewer**: repeat the mapping in the 3rd position independently. *(Same underlying skill as [[Positional CAGED Soloing with Matched Modes and Arpeggios]]'s five-position chord-tone map, applied here to a different progression with fewer positions worked.)*
- **Drill 4 — Legato vs. staccato awareness.** A personal-history framing: a guitar-degree professor assigned this to fix an overly staccato (short, detached) default in his own lines. The drill itself is simple — play any material (a plain major scale works) once fully staccato and once fully legato, back to back, specifically to hear the difference and build conscious control over which one is the current default. The point isn't *what* you play, it's *how*.
- **Realistic timeline, stated directly:** each of the four drills, done properly (all positions, all string sets, all chord tones — not just the single worked example shown), is estimated at roughly a month of dedicated practice on its own; doing all four seriously is estimated at closer to three months.

# I Wanna Be Your Man — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A one-chord jam with a refrain bolted on. Pollack's verdict is blunt — style carrying almost no content, a song that could plausibly have been written in an afternoon (it was handed to the Stones). What's formally worth noting is the **bridge-shaped refrain** and the improvisatory instrumental break.

**Form.** The verse is **17 bars**: two 8-bar couplets plus one extra. That bar isn't required and it makes the meter lopsided, but drop it and the long pickup into the title phrase gets crammed into bar 16 — asymmetry was the lesser evil, and an odd bar count is a cheaper problem than a garbled hook. The refrain is 8 bars in four 2-bar phrases, each declaiming the title; the break is 12 bars, again over a single chord. Live and BBC versions add four bars of vamping before the vocal, turn the break into an explicit 12-bar blues frame, and take the song to a real ending instead of a fade.

**Harmony.** The verse is **one chord and nothing else**, with a brief hint of V late in each couplet. The refrain never leaves the home key either, but packs in functional changes whose roots move by fifths, so it sounds like it's hunting for something ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). That's the transferable idea: hold one chord for a whole section, then answer it with dense circle-of-fifths motion that never actually modulates — it reads as a key change and costs nothing.

**Melody.** The verse tune supplies the harmonic motion the chords don't, colouring the static tonic from above with heavy emphasis on the sixth and the **♭7** ([[BLU-C06 — Blue notes and the blues scale]]). The refrain's contrast is stylistic as much as harmonic — its cornball chromatic-scale guitar riffs are pointedly *not* bluesy.

**Arrangement.** Ringo on lead, John and Paul joining for the refrain. Texture is overstuffed with double tracking, overdubbed organ and a lot of screaming; the guitar break is a string of disconnected licks with none of the shape of the "I Saw Her Standing There" solo. The live arrangements drop the organ and are better for it.

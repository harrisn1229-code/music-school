# Learn To SHRED With Just 6 Patterns! (Guitar Lesson + Tabs)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HEnGiWwNQCQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HEnGiWwNQCQ)

## Synopsis

Six short 2-string picking "building block" patterns in A minor, explicitly framed as combinable Lego pieces rather than finished licks — the real payoff demonstrated is mixing and matching them on the fly, capped with a combined guitar-solo exercise at 120 BPM that strings several patterns together.

## What's taught

- **Pattern 1** — triplets, ascending a 3-note-per-string scale across two strings (extendable to more strings). Foundational building block.
- **Pattern 2** — ascend, play one note on the next string, return — introduces the **flying pinky** problem directly: letting the pinky lift far off the fretboard between notes kills speed; keep it (and all fretting fingers) close to the strings between notes.
- **Pattern 3** — 16th notes cycling between two strings with a deliberately **odd note count per string** (3-5-5-3, repeating) rather than an even split — creates a "progressive" asymmetric feel from a simple back-and-forth motion.
- **Pattern 4** — circles a single target note (D) with lower neighbor notes on the adjacent lower string; can start on a downstroke (for a deliberate accent on the target) or an upstroke (functionally the same pattern entered on a different beat) — recommends practicing both entry points.
- **Pattern 5** — starts from the HIGHEST note of each 3-note-per-string position rather than the lowest/root, combined with odd note grouping in 16ths — directly derived from and cross-referenced to one of BERNTH's other well-known alternate-picking workouts.
- **Pattern 6** — combines two different sub-ideas across its two strings (not the same idea repeated per string like the earlier patterns) — the most "advanced-sounding" building block specifically because it isn't internally symmetric.
- **The combined guitar solo exercise (120 BPM)** strings several of the six patterns together into one continuous phrase, deliberately mixing plain 16th notes and 16th-note triplets in the same pass — a real-world test of switching pulse subdivision without breaking the picking motion, matching the general "switch subdivisions without tensing" principle logged elsewhere this batch.
- **Core stated philosophy:** learn each pattern as an interchangeable building block first, in isolation, then get creative combining/reordering them — the patterns themselves are deliberately simple; the skill is in the combination.

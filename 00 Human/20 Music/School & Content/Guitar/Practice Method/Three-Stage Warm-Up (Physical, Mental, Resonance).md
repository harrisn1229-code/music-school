# Three-Stage Warm-Up (Physical, Mental, Resonance)

Part of [[Song Practice]]'s practice-methodology material — a warm-up framework built on three named principles rather than a fixed exercise list, each principle mapped to one of the three underlying components of music itself: **physical mechanism → rhythm**, **mental faculty → harmony**, **resonance principle → melody**. The exercises below are the worked examples, but the framework is meant to be regenerated with new exercises once the three principles are understood — "I can just make up exercises" once the categories are internalized.

## Stage 1 — Physical: right-hand/left-hand coordination
Warms the physical mechanism that produces rhythm. Worked exercises:
- **Chromatic scale the full length of the neck**, ascending and descending, shifting position by moving up a fret with the first finger each time (one-finger-per-fret, moving the whole hand up the neck rather than staying in one box).
- **A full major scale, same up-the-neck approach**, shifting through positions the same way as the chromatic run above.
- **Major pentatonic through the cycle of fifths**, ascending and descending — start on one key's pentatonic box, move to the next key up a fifth, and so on around the cycle.

## Stage 2 — Mental: speed up the thinking process
Same drills as Stage 1, but with a deliberate twist that forces faster real-time decision-making rather than rote repetition: **instead of always running a full pattern all the way up then all the way down in one key, change key partway through** — e.g. start the pentatonic-cycle-of-fifths exercise on G, ascend, then switch to the next key mid-pattern on the way back down rather than completing the full descent in the original key. The mental faculty being trained is the same one [[Continuous Scale Exercise]] drills for tune-specific key changes — find the closest correct note in the new key and keep moving — but here it's practiced as a warm-up in the abstract, not tied to a specific tune's chord-scale map. → [[Continuous Scale Exercise]]

## Stage 3 — Resonance: singing as its own intelligence
Activated by singing while playing — described as a principle with **"its own intelligence,"** independent of the mental/harmonic faculty. The strongest evidence for treating it as genuinely separate: after deliberately scrambling all conscious harmonic knowledge (see the retuning experiment below), singing every note stayed intact and accurate even though naming or predicting those same notes intellectually was no longer possible — the resonance principle kept working when the mental faculty had nothing to contribute. → [[Vocal Harmony]]

## Practice discipline: cycle all three, all the time — not just at the start of a session
The three stages aren't a fixed warm-up sequence to run once and then abandon; the discipline is to keep **jumping between all three principles throughout playing**, not only during warm-up: always singing while playing (resonance), always attending to touch and tone, and always keeping a mental overview a step ahead of the current moment (thinking ahead, tracking harmonic integrity) rather than only reacting in real time. Framed explicitly as a defense of the "mental"/intellectual faculty in music — a commonly distrusted faculty, but only a liability when it dominates the other two rather than working alongside them (see the retuning story below, where the mental faculty had become *too* dominant and needed deliberate suppression, not elimination).

## Retuning as a mental-pattern reset (an extreme Stage-2/Stage-3 rebalancing move)
A concrete story of what happens when the **mental faculty gets too dominant relative to the other two**: reached a point where seeing a chord symbol (e.g. G7) meant already knowing exactly what it would sound like before playing it — no surprise left, described as feeling "dry" and boring, a sign the mental/pattern-recognition layer had crowded out the physical and resonance layers. The fix was to **retune the guitar to an unfamiliar tuning**, which "obliterates" all of the accumulated mental/pattern knowledge at once (a known chord shape no longer produces the known sound) while leaving the physical connection to the instrument intact. The genuinely surprising result: singing (Stage 3, resonance) remained completely unaffected — every note could still be sung accurately even with zero conscious knowledge of what the retuned strings would produce, which is offered as direct evidence that the resonance principle really is independent of, not downstream of, the mental/harmonic faculty. After a period of relying on retuned exploration, the mental faculty was deliberately rebuilt *for the new tuning* (learning the shapes in the new tuning explicitly) before eventually returning to standard tuning — the point of the exercise wasn't the alternate tuning itself, but the deliberate, temporary rebalancing of which of the three principles was allowed to lead.

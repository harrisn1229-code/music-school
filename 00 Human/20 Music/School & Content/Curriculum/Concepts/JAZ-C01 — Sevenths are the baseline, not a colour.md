# JAZ-C01 — Sevenths are the baseline, not a colour

**Q.** In a jazz context, what does a bare chord symbol like "C" actually mean, and why does that assumption have to be settled before any other rule?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Cmaj7 or C6 — a four-note chord.** In common-practice harmony the triad is the default and the 7th is a colour added on top. In jazz the 7th *is* the default, and a triad is the special case. Every rule that follows — substitution, voice leading, guide tones — assumes four notes.

## Why it matters

First of the four ideas [[Jazz Harmony]] says the whole subject runs on, and the one that silently breaks the other three if it is missed. Guide tones cannot exist without a 7th; tritone substitution has nothing to preserve without one. Reading a jazz chart as triads is not a simpler reading, it is a wrong one.

It also transfers straight into pop and R&B writing: swapping the triads of a loop for sevenths is the single cheapest way to move a progression from bright-and-generic toward the neo-soul end of the dial.

## Drill

Take a four-chord loop you have already written and re-spell every chord as a seventh — maj7 on I and IV, m7 on ii, iii and vi, dominant 7 on V. Play both versions back to back and name what changed.

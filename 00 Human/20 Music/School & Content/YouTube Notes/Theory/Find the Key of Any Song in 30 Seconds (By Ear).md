# Find the Key of Any Song in 30 Seconds (By Ear)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lYtZxy-bqNY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lYtZxy-bqNY)

## Synopsis
Max Konyi demos his free web app **Keyseeker** (listen to a loop, guess the key, check the answer) as a framing device, then walks through the actual technique for finding a song's key by ear — both halves of it (tonic + tonality) — with worked examples pulled live from the app's song loops. The video is the clearest single explainer in the batch of the specific "find the tonic, then test major vs. minor" workflow that [[Transcribing and Playing by Ear]] builds its "Finding the key of a song by ear" section around, including the two most common mistakes people make doing this in practice.

## Techniques
- **A key has two independent aspects to solve: the tonic (which note is home) and the tonality (major or minor).** Solve them as two separate questions rather than one — most of the technique content here is really two parallel toolkits.
- **Finding the tonic, primary method:** listen to enough of the song for real context (10–15 seconds, or one loop), then pause playback and imagine the music resolving into a single note — the note that would feel like coming home, the way the last note of a scale run feels. Sing that note or find it on an instrument.
- **Finding the tonic, backup 1:** check the **last note of the melody** — a common place for a tonic to land, demonstrated live in the video (found B♭ this way) — but explicitly **not a guarantee**, just a place worth checking first.
- **Finding the tonic, backup 2:** identify the **tonic chord** (the most stable-feeling chord — the one the song could plausibly end on) by ear, then listen specifically for its **bass note** and sing/find that pitch — on a root-position tonic chord the bass note IS the tonic. Demoed live: heard the first chord of a 3-chord progression as "home," isolated its bass note, and confirmed.
- **Finding the tonic, advanced shortcut (for scale-degree-trained ears):** you don't need the tonic directly at all — play any note, judge what scale degree it feels like relative to the music (Konyi demos landing on "that's five"), then back-calculate the tonic from that relationship (if the note played is scale-degree 5, the tonic is a 4th below it). This is the technique [[Scale Degree Ear Training]] and the real-time transcription episodes build toward.
- **Finding the tonality, ideal method — pure vibe.** Once the tonic is identified, isolate and listen to just that note in isolation and ask whether IT feels bright/warm/major or dark/melancholy/minor — with practice, the whole song's emotional color becomes audible inside that single held pitch, because the tonic note becomes "imbued" with the song's overall major/minor coloring, not just its stability. This is explicitly the skill being trained, not a shortcut around training.
- **Finding the tonality, backup 1:** find the tonic chord and read its quality (major/minor) directly by ear.
- **Finding the tonality, backup 2 (concrete test):** with a candidate tonic pitch in mind, play the **minor 3rd and the major 3rd above it** against the track and listen for which one actually fits/sounds "correct" against what's playing — whichever third resolves the ambiguity tells you major vs. minor. Demoed live on a D-tonic track: F (minor 3rd) confirmed correct against F♯ (major 3rd), settling D minor.
- **Common mistake 1 — confusing relative major and minor.** Many progressions genuinely support being heard either way (the video's own worked example: could be heard as D minor OR its relative F major, and Keyseeker's own answer-checker accepts both in ambiguous cases) — know [[Keys|relative major/minor]] cold specifically so this is recognized as a real perceptual ambiguity, not treated as simply getting the wrong answer.
- **Common mistake 2 — assuming the first or last chord of a song is the tonic chord.** Sometimes true, but often not, especially in modern production — treat both positions as places to *check* by ear, never as a rule to apply blindly.
- **The full workflow chains into ~30 seconds with practice:** get context (10–15s) → resolve/sing the tonic → test tonality by vibe (with the two backups in reserve) → done. Repetition on many songs is what compresses this from a multi-minute deliberate process into a near-instant read.

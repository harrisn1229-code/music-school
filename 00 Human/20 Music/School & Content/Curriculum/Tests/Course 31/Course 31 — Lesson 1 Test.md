> **Q1.** **Cmaj7 or C6 — a four-note chord**
> *Also accepted:* a seventh chord · Cmaj7 · C6
> The seventh is assumed unless something says otherwise.
>
> **Q2.** **Common practice: the triad is the default and the 7th is a colour. Jazz: the 7th is the default**
> In jazz the triad is the special case, not the starting point.
>
> **Q3.** **Guide tones and tritone substitution — both are built out of the 7th**
> *Rubric (5):* names guide tones and says the 3rd/7th pair cannot exist without a 7th (2) · names tritone substitution and says the shared tritone only exists in a dominant 7th (2) · says a triad reading is wrong rather than simplified (1)
> Guide tones are the 3rd and the 7th; drop the 7th and half the pair is gone, along with the voice leading that runs on it. Tritone substitution works because two dominants a tritone apart share the same tritone, and a triad has no tritone to share. Sevenths are the baseline the rest of the subject assumes, so reading a chart as triads does not give you a rough version of jazz harmony — it gives you a different harmony that the rules do not describe.
>
> **Q4.** **Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7**
> *Also accepted:* Dm7 G7 Cmaj7 · Dmin7 G7 Cmaj7
>
> **Q5.** **Dm7♭5 – G7♭9 – Cm7 (or Cm6, or CmMaj7)**
> The ii goes half-diminished, the V keeps its major 3rd but gains a ♭9, and the tonic is deliberately ambiguous.
>
> **Q6.** **The roots fall a fifth twice — the circle of fifths in miniature**
> Root motion down a fifth is the strongest progression in tonal music, and the cell stacks two of them back to back.
>
> **Q7.** **Because the raised leading tone is what makes it a functioning dominant at all**
> It is borrowed from harmonic minor; natural minor would give a minor v with no pull.
>
> **Q8.** **Any m7 chord followed by a dominant 7th a fourth above it**
> Whatever it points at — the chord a fourth above the dominant — is the intended I, even when the I never actually arrives.
>
> **Q9.** **The deceptive move, and the unresolved ii–V**
> Deceptive: the ii–V lands on vi or ♭VI instead. Unresolved: it moves straight to the next ii–V without resolving at all.
>
> **Q10.** **Unresolved ii–Vs — a chain of them implies keys it never confirms**
> *Rubric (5):* identifies unresolved ii–Vs as the likely cause (2) · says the implied I never arrives, so no new key is confirmed (2) · names a check — whether a tonic is actually reached and cadenced on (1)
> A ii–V points at a I whether or not the I shows up, so a chart full of ii–Vs looks like a chart full of key centres. What makes a key change real is arriving at a tonic and confirming it, and a bebop head typically does neither — each ii–V hands straight over to the next. The check is to mark every implied I and see which ones actually sound and get cadenced on. Usually one does, at the end, and everything before it was motion rather than modulation.

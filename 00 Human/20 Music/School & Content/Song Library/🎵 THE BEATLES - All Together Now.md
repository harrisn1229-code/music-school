# All Together Now — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

A children's song with adult joinery inside it: I–IV–V harmony, a melody spanning about four notes, and a form that quietly refuses to settle. The improvised feel is faked — the execution is far too clean.

**Form.** The sophistication is structural, and it matches neither the pop nor the folk template ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]). It opens orthodoxly (verse, verse, bridge, refrain), then runs a verse, two refrains with no bridge between them, a bridge with no verse in front of it, and very nearly three refrains back to back. The verse and refrain are both harmonically closed 8-bar shapes on two chords; the bridge is an asymmetrical **10 bars** because its last phrase is rhetorically stretched.

**Harmony.** The bridge is the only section open at both ends — it starts on IV and finishes on V, which is also where the melodic climax lands, and it's the only place all three chords appear. That single difference does all the sectional contrast work. The outro turns an unexpected third refrain into a full V–I ending, and while the tempo pushes forward the **harmonic rhythm abruptly slows to one chord every four bars**; that slowdown against an accelerating backbeat is what tells you the record is landing ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]).

**Melody and arrangement.** Syllabic, patter-like, and still describing a real arch on four notes; the verse's syncopation is placed to coincide with the tune's apex rather than scattered ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]). The build is strictly choreographed — one element added per section, acoustic guitar alone up to a full chorus with horns and handclaps — and the sharpest move is **dropping the lead vocal** at the first bridge instead of adding to it.

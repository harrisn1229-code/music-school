## Synopsis

Guy walks through the drum bus on a Jesse Glick remix. This reinforces, more than it expands, the drum-bus chain already extensively documented in [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]] from several other Disclosure sources (API-2500 → tape/saturation → Soothe → limiter) — most of what's here confirms the same order and philosophy already captured there. The genuinely new specifics (a lighter 2:1-ratio glue variant, an explicit -8 dB peak target he calls "my go-to drum bus level," and a concrete Soothe-on-tambourine-resonance move) have been folded directly into the chain note rather than duplicated on a separate page.

## Techniques

- **The chain, in order, framed explicitly as "think like a mastering engineer with the drum stem":** bus compression (API-2500, slow attack, starting at 2:1 ratio) → saturation (Inflator, then Saturn in tube mode) → Soothe (resonance control) → limiter (Oxford Limiter preferred; Ozone 9 Maximizer or a vintage-style limiter as alternatives), targeting **-8 dB peak** as his standard drum bus level — a number he uses to know roughly where every other bus should sit relative to it. → [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]]
- **Soothe dialed to a 50/50 mix specifically to tame a resonant tambourine**, informed by years of hearing the same song loud in a club — a targeted, ear-informed resonance fix rather than a default setting. → [[Soothe2]]
- **Bypassed-vs-processed A/B: the processed bus isn't louder in peak terms, but reads with more body and weight.** Reinforces the recurring theme across Disclosure's drum-bus sources: the chain's job is glue and perceived size, not raw peak loudness.

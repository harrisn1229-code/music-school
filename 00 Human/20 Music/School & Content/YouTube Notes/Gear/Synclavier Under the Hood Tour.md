# Synclavier Under the Hood Tour

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TsLzIsbsexs)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TsLzIsbsexs)

## Synopsis

A hardware/history walkthrough of an original Synclavier II chassis with Kevin Maloney (a Synclavier product specialist since 1984): 32 FM voices, 24-harmonic additive synthesis, no analog filters by deliberate design choice, floppy-disk boot/storage, optional Winchester hard drives, an early MIDI implementation bolted on after the fact, and the built-in sequencer that functioned as an early DAW.

## What's taught

- **8-bit aliasing as a deliberately-preserved, now-prized character**: the Synclavier's 8-bit resolution produces real digital aliasing/"hashy" high-frequency content that reads as buzzy/gritty by modern (16-bit+) standards — a texture some producers now specifically seek out, since it's not reproducible on a clean modern digital synth without deliberately re-adding bit-crushing/aliasing.
- **No analog filters, by design philosophy, not cost**: New England Digital deliberately declined to add analog filters despite customer requests, offering only simple digital high-cut and high/mid-roll-off switches instead — sound shaping was largely limited to these two switches plus outboard VCF/EQ if more was needed. A minimal-filtering design choice that left the instrument's raw digital character mostly untouched at the source.
- **32-voice FM/additive engine**: each voice built from 24-harmonic sine-wave stacks (carrier) modulated by a sine-wave modulator — any harmonic grouping could be independently modulated.
- **Storage/workflow constraints of the era**: 128 KB floppy disks stored both the FM sound library AND sequences (hard drives, when available at all, were reserved for sample data, not FM patches); optional Winchester hard drives started at 5 MB and scaled to 20 MB before switching to SCSI.
- **The sequencer stored a track's exact sounds along with its notes** — recalling a saved sequence automatically recalled every sound used on every track, no manual re-loading of individual patches required (same "named file recalls the whole design" behavior noted on other Synclavier videos in this batch).
- **MIDI was bolted on after the fact, generously**: 1-in/4-out 5-pin DIN hardwired MIDI (expandable to 8 outputs) — implemented once MIDI existed, on a system whose built-in sequencer had already been doing DAW-like sequencing work without it.
- **SMPTE timecode support**: could generate SMPTE for printing to tape and later synchronize/read SMPTE for sync purposes.

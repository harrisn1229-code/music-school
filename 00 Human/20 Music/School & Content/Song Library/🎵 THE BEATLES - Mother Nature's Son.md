# Mother Nature's Son — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (an ad-lib figure, then 8 bars vamping on I) · Verse (16, as 4+4+8) · spacer · Verse · Bridge (15, with one bar elided) · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Outro. The verse's **final phrase is repeated alone as a spacer** between the two opening verses, and the same gesture returns at the end as the outro.

A folk-hymn built on almost no root motion at all. Verse and bridge both sit on a D pedal, and what the ear takes for chord changes is two stepwise inner voices — Pollack declines to name the resulting 6/4 sonorities as chords at all, calling them by-products of neighbour motion ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]], [[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]). Only the V-of-V in the verse and the V-of-IV in the bridge are genuine chromatic root events ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]).

Two effects worth stealing. The neighbour figures **double in speed** in the verse's third phrase, faking an acceleration of harmonic rhythm that a pedal makes impossible ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). And the bridge holds its C♮ an extra bar, pushing the phrasing off centre; the song then ends on a **D7 rather than a triad**, which is why the close reads as unsettled rather than resolved.

The arrangement schedules its entrances tightly: acoustic guitar alone, then brass arriving *during the spacer* rather than on the downbeat of the verse it belongs to, with percussive tapping and bass drum coming and going section by section — and two stray drum beats planted in the intro that mean nothing yet.

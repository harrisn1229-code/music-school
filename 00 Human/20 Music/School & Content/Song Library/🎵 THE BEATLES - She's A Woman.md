# She's A Woman — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (8) · Verse (**24 bars**) · Verse · Bridge (a scant **4 bars**) · Verse · Break (guitar solo) · Bridge · Verse · fade. The bridge is so short that the following verse is heard as a continuation of it.

The most blues-like thing in the catalogue to this point, as writing and as performance. The structural trick is that the **verse is a twelve-bar blues stretched to 24 bars** ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]), disguised further by IV chords jabbing into bars that would otherwise be plain tonic — and then the solo break and the outro drop the disguise entirely and play a straight twelve. I, IV and V and nothing else, apart from the bridges.

The bridge (iii – vi – iii – IV – V) is a neat counterpoint to "I Feel Fine", where iii went to IV: here the same chord is answered by **both** of its likely targets inside four bars, and it doubles as a compact reharmonisation demonstration — one identical two-bar melodic phrase stated twice under different chords ([[POP-C05 — Reharmonizing a loop]]).

The hook is the lick opening the verse: a leap up of a minor sixth followed by a craggy descent, carrying its own implied cross-relation between an early C♯ and a later C♮ ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]). It sits at the very top of Paul's comfortable range, and the audible strain is part of the record — he transposed the song down a fourth to sing it decades later ([[MEL-C05 — Register and intensity]]). Overdubbed piano sits out the entire first verse before staying in for the rest.

# Fox Stevenson: Advanced Sidechain for MORE BASS (Rare Tips)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkTUVnJKsB0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkTUVnJKsB0)

## Synopsis
Fox Stevenson walks through his two-band multiband sidechain approach for bass, breaking down why the low end and high end need fundamentally different duck treatment, then demonstrates how per-drum-element sidechaining (kick vs. snare) and a mastering-stage trigger consolidation trick ("adaptive sidechaining") recover extra low-end sustain without sacrificing punch. This is a different video from the other Fox Stevenson sidechain video already in the vault ("New Sidechain Trick (to Avoid Bow Tie...)," https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJwF61mjWuQ) — that one covers the bow-tie envelope problem on a single bass/sub and a separate kick+sub bus-clipping technique; this one is about the low/high split philosophy, matching sidechain depth to each drum element, and a shared-trigger consolidation move for mastering. No overlap in content — both are additive to the vault's Sidechaining note.

## Techniques
- **Low end is binary, high end is to taste — a philosophy for splitting the multiband sidechain.** Below the low-end crossover, duck essentially all-or-nothing (fully ducked out or full volume) so only one element ever occupies that register at a time — mute everything else on the low band and the kick alone fills the gap on its own; above the crossover, dial duck depth to taste since more of the mix can coexist there without masking. "You don't really want layers in your low end, you want just one thing." → [[Multiband Compression]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Advanced Sidechain for MORE BASS (Rare Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkTUVnJKsB0)
- **A spectrum of high-end pump styles, not just one setting.** On the top end he demos a genuinely subtle duck-plus-tiny-pump that clears space exactly where needed and leaves a little room for the hi-hat transient on the next eighth, a more aggressive and audibly pumped version, and the fully obvious "Daft Punk"-style pump that a lot of producers still reach for — he's personally not a fan of the most obvious pump but says it can absolutely work for the right aesthetic/genre. → [[Kickstart 2]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Advanced Sidechain for MORE BASS (Rare Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkTUVnJKsB0)
- **Give the snare its own, lighter low-end sidechain than the kick's.** A snare is shorter and pitched higher than a kick, so it doesn't need nearly as much low end taken out — run a separate, gentler duck on the snare's low end rather than reusing the kick's aggressive binary settings. — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Advanced Sidechain for MORE BASS (Rare Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkTUVnJKsB0)
- **"Adaptive sidechaining" — at mixdown/mastering, consolidate every instrument's low-end duck onto one shared trigger instead of a bespoke sidechain per instrument.** Once you're flipping your master or mastering the track, you don't want to be managing a different low-end sidechain per element. Keep the top-end ducks separate/per-instrument (they're subtle and to-taste anyway), but swap all the low-end ducking over to a single existing shared low-end trigger. The payoff isn't just simpler routing — it recovers low-end sustain: swapping the snare off its own dedicated low-end duck and onto the shared trigger let several more oscillations of low end survive under the snare's tail instead of getting cut off. → [[Multiband Compression]] — Fox Stevenson (Sol State), [Advanced Sidechain for MORE BASS (Rare Tips)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hkTUVnJKsB0)

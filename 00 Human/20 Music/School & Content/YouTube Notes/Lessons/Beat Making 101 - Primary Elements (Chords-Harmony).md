# Beat Making 101 - Primary Elements (Chords/Harmony)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wmfe2px4vmw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wmfe2px4vmw)

## Synopsis

Beginner-level overview of what a chord is and what chords do (emotion, tension/release, filling out the frequency spectrum), part of the [[Beat Making 101 - Elements of a Beat|Beat Making 101]] series. Mostly conceptual/theory-101 content with one concrete, reusable production move worth pulling out: doubling a chord part across two different instruments (piano + synth) at different volumes for texture, rather than using either alone.

## Techniques

- **Which instruments can play chords:** anything with a keyboard (piano, synths, electric piano) or enough independent strings to sound multiple notes at once (guitar, harp — violin/viola can manage two-note double-stops but not full chords). Monophonic instruments (sax, flute, voice) can't play a chord alone but CAN form one when several of them play different notes simultaneously in a section (e.g. three trumpets, each holding one note of the chord).
- **Chord doubling for texture: layer two different instruments playing the EXACT same chord part, with one set quieter than the other, to thicken the sound rather than using either instrument alone.** Worked example: a piano track and a synth track both play the identical chord progression, with the synth mixed noticeably quieter under the piano — the combination reads as one richer, more textured chord sound than either source alone. A deliberate, purposeful doubling choice (not a mistake or redundancy) — the two instruments could instead each play a different voicing/inversion of the same chord for a different flavor of thickening, rather than a literal unison double. → [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Chords fill out the frequency spectrum between the bass and the melody** — without a chord layer, a melody + bass + drums combination has an audible gap; chords occupy the middle of the spectrum and provide harmonic/emotional color the other elements can't supply on their own.
- **Ear-training exercise: listen for WHEN chords change, before trying to identify WHAT the chords are** — chord identification by ear is a much harder skill than noticing the moment the underlying harmony shifts (a chord repeated twice in a row for rhythmic effect vs. genuinely changing to a new chord); train the simpler skill first.

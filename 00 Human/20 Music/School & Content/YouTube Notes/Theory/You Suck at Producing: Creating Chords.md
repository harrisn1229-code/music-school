## What's taught
An early, bare-bones intro to building a triad by half-step counting rather than scale degrees: stack a root, a note 3 half-steps up, and a note a further 4 half-steps up to get a minor triad; raise only the middle note a half-step to flip it to major. The back half applies this directly inside a beat — finds the existing bass part's root note, builds a matching chord off that root, and alternates the same chord between minor and major across a loop for a mood shift.

## Techniques
- **Build a minor triad by half-step counting**: root, up 3 half-steps for the 2nd note, up 4 more half-steps from there for the 3rd note (e.g. F minor: F, A♭, C). → [[Chords]]
- **Flip minor to major by raising only the middle note a half-step** — the fastest way to toggle a chord's quality without rebuilding it from scratch (F minor → F major just moves the middle note up one半-step). → [[Chords]]
- **Find the existing bassline's root note before building a chord over it** — solo/inspect the bass clip, identify its repeated root pitch, then build the triad starting from that exact note so the chord and bass agree. → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Alternate the same root between its minor and major triad across a loop for a mood swing** — first half of a 2-bar loop plays the minor version, second half the major version (or vice versa), giving a mood shift without changing the root or the rhythm. → [[Chord Progressions]]

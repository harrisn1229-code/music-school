# Coldplay's go-to chord progression

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qd_qpdrl2i4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qd_qpdrl2i4)

## Synopsis

A single-artist case study (parallel to the Taylor Swift video earlier in this batch) tracking Coldplay's repeated use of the IV–I–V progression across their catalog, with particular attention to their favorite voicing tricks (sus4-resolving-to-plain-chord, first-inversion tonic, pedal points, hidden/disguised five chords) rather than just the bare Roman numerals.

## Techniques

- **The core shape**: IV–I–V, used across many Coldplay songs in different keys, frequently dressed with the same small set of voicing tricks rather than played as plain triads.
- **"Paradise"** — key of F: B♭–F–C, with the V chord (C) introduced as a **sus4 first, then resolving down** to the plain triad — a recurring Coldplay voicing signature per this video.
- **"Fix You"** — key of E♭: A♭–E♭–B♭sus4→B♭, same sus4-into-V trick, PLUS the tonic (E♭) is voiced in **first inversion** (G in the bass rather than E♭) rather than root position.
- **"Clocks"** — bridge section modulates to a different key (D♭) than the rest of the song (E♭) specifically to deploy this same IV–I–V shape there: G♭maj7–D♭–A♭6, all three chords sharing a common top note (F) that functions as an **inverted pedal point** — gluing the three otherwise-changing chords together via one held/repeated note on top rather than in the bass. Modulates back to E♭ for the remainder of the song. *(Also independently referenced in a mashup video earlier in this batch, where "Clocks" and "Speed of Sound" — same progression, keys a tritone apart — were RipX-mashed together.)*
- **"The Scientist"** — a stretched-out variant of the same shape: chorus goes IV (B♭) → I (F) → then a *disguised* V chord: notated on the page as "Fsus2" but functionally a C-family chord (Csus4/F, i.e., the V chord voiced with the tonic note underneath) — Bennett's read: "it is kind of like a five chord, but sort of hidden away." A second pass through the same phrase makes the disguised-V even more overt (an Fmaj9-no-3rd voicing that's really a C-over-F), followed by an F6-ish passing chord before finally landing on a plain, undisguised V — the same IV-I-V shape, "dragged out and developed with all these different suspensions."
- **"Yellow"** — a substitution variant: the usual I chord is swapped for **vi** (both tonic-function, near-identical notes — they differ by only one note, the top note, whether it's the 5th of I or the 3rd of vi) — giving "a slightly darker take on the same chord progression" without disrupting the underlying IV-[tonic]-V logic.
- **"Up in Flames"** — key of E♭, same shape (A♭–E♭–B♭), with the V chord again introduced as a suspension before resolving — reinforcing that the sus4-into-V move (documented in "Paradise" and "Fix You" above) is a genuine recurring Coldplay fingerprint, not a one-off.

# While My Guitar Gently Weeps — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** two-bridge model with verses between, one of them the guitar solo, but with the **usual opening double verse deliberately missing** — the verse is already an internal double couplet, so a second one back to back would be a repeat of a repeat. Intro 8 bars (the first half of the verse, instrumental, ending on **V** so the vocal entry is motivated) · Verse 16 · Bridge 16 (one 8-bar phrase stated twice, and unusually the two bridges carry **different lyrics**) · Outro, nearly two full verse cycles into a fade.

The sadness lives in the notes, not the production: the heavy official take and the acoustic *Anthology 3* outtake share melody, chords and phrasing. The verse holds one A-minor chord for three bars while the bass walks down chromatically — on the fretboard that looks like a new chord every bar, analytically it is **one harmonic event** and the odd sonorities are byproducts of the bass ([[POP-C04 — Four chords with a moving bass]], [[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]).

The bridge answers the verse's closing E with **A major, not A minor** ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]), then leans toward C♯ minor and never establishes it — it is reached through its **IV**, not by I–V–I. That is the pattern for the whole song: three keys touched and abandoned before any takes hold ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]).

**The solo is the structural peak.** Clapton ignores the couplet and plays two eight-bar phrases across the sixteen, and the high A at its top is the only place an upward gesture wins.

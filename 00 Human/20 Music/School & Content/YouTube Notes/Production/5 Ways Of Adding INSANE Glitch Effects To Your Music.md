# 5 Ways Of Adding INSANE Glitch Effects To Your Music

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Q3nWNOFiXc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8Q3nWNOFiXc)

## Synopsis

Underbelly (a recurring You Suck at Producing collaborator, working on a real track with Scroll called "I Guess I'm Alone") runs through five ways to add glitch effects, in escalating order of hands-on effort: starting from a free glitch sample pack and taming it with Ableton's Beats-mode warp settings; glitching your own material with the same warp tools plus Tones/Texture warp modes and automated grain size; a paid spectral bin-editor plugin (Spec Ops) for surgical, drawable glitch patterns; two free lossy-MP3-emulation plugins (Codec, and an unconfirmed LAME-based one) for a "downloaded off Limewire" digital-decay character; and finally a live-resample-and-splice workflow to capture a hands-on glitch performance and drop it back into the arrangement. Every technique is demoed on real material (a drum break, Scroll's guitar) rather than described abstractly.

## Techniques

- **Free glitch break sample packs (Glitch With Friends) as raw material, tamed with Beats-mode warp settings** — a chaotic pack-sourced glitch drum break gets tightened into something usable by setting the clip's Warp to **Beats** mode, **Preserve Transients** to a fixed value (16th notes), the loop-direction arrow forward-only, and pulling down the transient-loop slider. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Micro-edit a snippet: split + requantize for a pitched blip, split + reverse for a quick reverse hit** — select a tiny piece of a clip, **Cmd/Ctrl+E** to split it off, then either requantize it to a much finer grid (32nd notes) for a short pitched "blip," or split off a piece and hit **R** to reverse just that fragment — both fast, targeted micro-edits rather than whole-clip processing. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Glitch your own audio (not just packs) using Ableton's warp modes** — Beats-mode ×2 stretch repeated for a stutter effect, or switch to **Tones**/**Texture** warp mode (Texture preferred — wider Grain Size range) and automate Grain Size via the clip envelope (start 100%, ramp down) for an evolving glitch texture, plus pitching the result up an octave for extra intensity. Layer manual chop/duplicate/fade micro-edits on top to keep an underlying groove intact rather than pure noise. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Spec Ops: spectral bin editing as a drawable, surgical glitch tool** — splits incoming audio into narrow frequency-domain "bins" that can be individually rearranged, muted, volume-adjusted, and relocated; drawing directly onto the bin display creates entirely new rhythmic glitch patterns from any source material — functions like an extremely granular, drawable EQ rather than a fixed effect. Paid (~$100). → [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Free lossy-codec emulation for a "downloaded off Limewire" digital-decay texture** — **Polyverse Codec** (free) simulates badly-encoded MP3 artifacts via a Loss control, with a Repeat mode that auto-grabs and stutters glitchy segments (more aggressive with the bit-rate pushed); a second free LAME-encoder-based plugin ("Mame" as heard) gives a similarly crushed, "old Zune"-style character via a bit-rate control plus a Turbo mode. Stack both on the master for a denser lossy-decay effect. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Live-resample a glitch-plugin performance to the master, then splice the print back into the arrangement** — create a new "Master Resample" audio track set to External In/resampling, record while riding the glitch plugins' controls live (a performance, not a static setting), then splice the print back in: select the target range, **Cmd/Ctrl+L** to set loop-brace markers, delete the original content across all tracks in that range, and fill the gap with the resampled recording. → [[Committing and Printing]]

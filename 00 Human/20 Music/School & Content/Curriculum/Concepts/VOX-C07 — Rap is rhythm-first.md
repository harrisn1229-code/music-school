# VOX-C07 — Rap is rhythm-first

**Q.** What's different about recording and producing a rap vocal versus a sung one?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Rap is a rhythmic performance — you're producing pocket, not pitch.** Workflow follows: **punch-ins line by line**, timing edits at the **syllable** level, **doubles on bar-ends and key phrases** rather than the whole verse, and a **dedicated ad-lib pass**. Processing leans on **compression and presence for intelligibility**; pitch correction is absent or an obvious effect. **Keep the breaths** — in a dense verse they're punctuation.

## Why it matters

It's your core lane. The syllable-level timing rule is deliberately the opposite of the phrase-level comping rule ([[VOX-C02 — Comp by phrase, not by syllable]]): comp *performance* by phrase, fix *timing* by syllable. Doubling a whole verse flattens it — the double is an accent, not a texture.

## Drill

Take a rap verse you've recorded. Nudge it 15 ms late, then 15 ms early. Pick one and say why.

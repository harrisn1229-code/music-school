# Guitar Genius Behind Michael Jackson's Thriller | David Williams

## Synopsis

A tribute to David Williams' guitar part on Thriller with session guitarist Taro Zone recreating it — mostly reminiscence about Williams' physical, "slap-bass-like" playing style, with one genuinely useful production detail: a mid-song pickup-voicing change that marks the verse/chorus boundary.

## Techniques

- **Pickup voicing switched mid-song to mark an arrangement section**: a clean, single-coil-leaning "Spanky" tone for the verse becomes a darker, thicker, mid-forward, top-end-reduced tone for the chorus, on the same guitar part/pattern — achieved on a modern instrument via coil-split (verse) vs. full humbucker (chorus). The pickup change itself is what differentiates the section, independent of any separate processing automation.
- **A small, idiosyncratic phrase ornament (an open G string ring at a specific point) is part of what makes a repeated part "lay into the track"** — described as easy to miss but load-bearing; Williams reportedly found these details through repetition, playing a phrase "over and over" until one take had the right feel.
- **A guitar tone/pattern subtly shifted partway through a track can be an honest artifact of a real multi-session recording** (a break between takes, tape machines resyncing) rather than a deliberate choice — worth knowing this kind of inconsistency is normal in real multitrack production, not something to eliminate when recreating a vintage-feeling part.

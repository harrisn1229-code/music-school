# This Is Why You CAN'T Play FAST! (and 2 exercises to FIX it)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CWlqfMpSuDQ)

## Synopsis

Economy of motion as the real speed bottleneck (not raw effort), demonstrated on a minor-add9 arpeggio exercise deliberately played with strict alternate picking rather than an easier picking technique.

## Techniques

- **Economy of motion, both hands: reducing motion size is the actual speed unlock, not more force/effort.** A big, uncontrolled full-arm picking motion or wide fretting-hand finger travel is what caps top speed — "push harder" doesn't work and risks injury; the fix is deliberately shrinking the motion.
- **Minor-add9 arpeggio shape**, usable directly as improvisation/solo vocabulary over a minor chord — not just an exercise shape.
- **Deliberately picking with strict alternate picking (not sweep or economy picking) even though the exercise would be easier with those techniques** — the point of the exercise is training the wrist-driven alternate-picking motion through difficult string transitions specifically; substituting an easier technique would defeat the training goal. A stiff full-arm motion gets the pick stuck in the dead zone between strings on these transitions — a wrist motion is described as the only way through.
- **Picking-hand anchor reiterated**: palm rests on the bridge (not a "floating hand"), citing more reliable results and easier open/palm-muted switching — same anchor principle as [[Directional Alternate Picking System]]'s existing bullet from this batch.

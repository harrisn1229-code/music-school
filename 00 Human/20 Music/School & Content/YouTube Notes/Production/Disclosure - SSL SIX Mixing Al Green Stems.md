# Disclosure - SSL SIX Mixing Al Green Stems

A 4-hour **hybrid analog mixing masterclass** — Guy mixes the original 8-track stems of Al Green's "Let's Stay Together" through his **SSL SIX** desk and masters through the **SSL Fusion**. Not a Disclosure-track breakdown; it's the single best **mixing + mastering + gain-staging + "what is glue"** reference in this batch. Covers routing a 2-out interface into 6 analog channels, the SSL flow (comp → EQ → pan), the G-bus one-knob compressor, stem-grouping for glue, taste-level panning/reverb on a vintage soul tune, and every stage of the Fusion master bus. He deliberately *over-pushes* settings to demonstrate. Whisper transcript (no captions) — high confidence.

## Techniques

### The hybrid-analog concept
- **Analog summing is an end-of-process "flavor/glue" pass, not a from-scratch mix.** Take the digital mix → convert to analog → run through the desk (EQ/comp/glue) → record back to digital → hand to mastering. **Do everything except limiting.** It's the last 5–10% (some say 1%) — "analog doesn't write you a good song." → [[Master Chain]].
- **Conversion loss only matters where there's deep sub.** Al Green is all ~100 Hz-and-up, so A→D→A round-tripping doesn't hurt; for clean techno/dubstep with huge full-range sub, minimize conversions and stay in the box. Converters matter — UAD/Apollo use the same converters across the Twin and the big interfaces. → [[Referencing and Translation]].
- **Get 6 analog channels out of a 2-output interface** — three stereo outs from one Apollo Twin: main 1/2, line 3/4, and the **headphone out split via a stereo→dual-mono cable** into channels 5/6 (route each Logic track to the matching output). → gear routing.

### Routing & grouping for glue
- **Group by importance and by what should glue.** Only 2 SSL channels have EQ+comp; the rest are gain-only clean-in — put your *best* parts through the processed channels. Here: **drums+bass through one compressed channel** (glue them), **vocal mono-center** through the other comp channel, all **instruments (guitar/organ/strings/horns) through a stereo pair** (pan in the box first), **BVs down 5/6** hard-ish panned. → [[Compression Staging]].
- **SSL channel signal order is comp → EQ → pan → send → volume** (top-to-bottom). Note EQ comes *after* compression on the desk. → [[Compression Staging]].

### Gain staging (the discipline)
- **Aim for every desk fader near 0** — if you're riding faders way up/down, your gain staging is wrong. Set levels in the box + on the desk's trim knobs so faders only need tiny moves. Back off the interface output and gain *up* on the desk to avoid soft distortion on the way into the monitors. → [[Gain Staging]].

### Mix moves on the stems
- **G-bus (one-knob) comp on drums+bass, then on the whole mix** — punch + glue; keep mix-bus GR ~3 dB max (into the yellow = "radio compression," flattens everything = bad). Match makeup gain so the bypass is level-matched — judge *character*, not loudness. → [[Compression]].
- **Prep in the box so you can push the analog harder** — de-ess the drums (split-band) + [[Soothe2]] the harsh hat resonances *before* the desk, so you can then boost the SSL highs without it getting brittle. → [[De-essing]], [[EQ]].
- **Shelf EQ on drums/bass:** boost lows (sub of kick+bass — 60s records were cut light on lows) + a little top for hat sparkle. → [[Kick Design]].
- **Dynamic EQ a spiking instrument frequency** (the organ's resonant note ducks only over threshold) and **high-cut where there's only tape hiss** (no musical info up top). → [[Masking and Carving]].
- **Widen a mono instrument bus with a chorus** (TAL/Juno-style, low mix) — anachronistic but opens a mono 60s recording up; add [[Oxford Inflator]] (run stereo) for harmonics + a touch of width. → [[Width and Stereo]].
- **Glue a bongo into the instruments with API-2500 (wet/dry)** — slow attack (let transients through), low ratio, quick release. **General comp rule: fade the compression up from zero until you *just* hear it working, then back off** — that's where to leave it. → [[Parallel Processing]], [[Compression]].
- **[[Soothe2]] on strings (~75%), EQ *before* Soothe** so any brightening gets caught; keep the kick spill (it's a vibe, and the track has little real bass). → [[Masking and Carving]].
- **Even out a dynamic part with a compressor instead of volume automation** — an 1176 (slow release) on the loud string section so it doesn't spike, avoiding a ton of fader rides. → [[Compression]].
- **Bass tone:** R-Bass to add sub ("more like today"), a **vintage/Pultec-style EQ for tone** (not surgical FabFilter — "for a bass guitar you want killer musical EQ"), and **[[Decapitator]] with its filter to distort *only the top end*** for the missing "bass-amp" fizz (very low wet/dry — "psychological"). → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[EQ]].
- **Just gain up parts the original under-mixed** — the BVs were riding low; bring them up because they deserve it. → mixing judgment.

### Panning & reverb on a vintage tune
- **Pan like you're looking at a stage** (or from the drummer's POV) — not a fan of super-wide/full hard-pans ("fingernails on a blackboard"); keep things "where you can see them." Motown/early-stereo hard L/R (strings right, horns left) suits a 60s tune. → [[Width and Stereo]].
- **Record four of everything to fill the field** — 2 hard-panned + 2 medium-panned (~45%). With only 2 BVs, pan them ~45% each rather than full. → [[Width and Stereo]].
- **Be sparing with reverb/delay on a vintage tune** — "don't make it sound *different*, just pumped up." When used: **Capitol echo-chamber IRs** on the horns (Al Schmitt mic preset), short, with pre-delay, narrowed (the horns are mono); gated-verb as an option. → [[Reverb]].
- **BV bus:** Tube-Tech comp per side + a bus comp; **The Drama** multiband ("vocal enhance" + air band) dipping mids transparently — "use your ears, not your eyes." → [[2 Main Vocal Group|Main Vocal Group]].
- **Al's lead:** desk-channel comp (tame the belts, don't flatten the huge dynamics), EQ for character/highs, [[Soothe2]] (~90%) + a ~3 dB EQ notch on a nasty resonance, [[Oxford Inflator]] (mono), [[Gem Dopamine]] (highs + gentle de-ess), let it into the red a touch for saturation. → [[LEAD Slot]].

### Soothe timing
- **Soothe *before* distortion if the source is already bad; *after* if the distortion creates the junk** — no fixed rule, decide by what's causing the problem. → [[Soothe2]].

### Mastering through the SSL Fusion (stage by stage)
- Signal order L→R: **HPF** (30/40/50 — skip on a full mix, useful on vocals/guitars) → **Vintage Drive** (fullness/density; *trim output as you drive*; the density knob = harmonics on the left, compression on the right) → **Violet EQ** (a *non-linear* EQ — boost highs + lows hard, then tame with the next stage) → **HF Compressor** (crossover + threshold; a **sidechained high-frequency** comp that controls the vocal/snare top without the kick/bass triggering it; hold the button 5 s for **LMC mode** — the 80s "talkback trash mic" crush, killer on drums, *not* for mastering) → **Stereo Image (Space + Width)** (space = lows, width = top; incredible on BVs — run BVs through it full-whack as an effect) → **Transistor** (very subtle SSL vibe). The chain "sounds finished/mastered." → [[Master Chain]], [[Plugin Wishlist]].
- **Ideally insert the master box on the desk** (needs a D-sub cable); printing then re-converting through it works but double-converts. → gear routing.

### What "glue" actually is (the definitive explainer)
- **Glue = separate signals *affecting each other* tastefully** — the drums nudging/squashing the bass, the vocal sucked by the kick, shared harmonics doubling up. It reads as "finished/natural," and it's subtle. **Not only compression** — distortion, saturation, and especially **tape** all glue; *anytime you combine parts through one piece of hardware* (transistors/circuits, an MPC, an SSL G-bus) they interact. DAWs lose it because you have total control and no limitations — and limitations are sometimes good (being forced to bus organ+strings+horns down one channel glued them). → [[Compression Staging]], [[Saturation and Distortion]].

# Learn These 2 Chord Grips To IMPROVE Your JAZZ and BLUES Comping!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=svudT_4PBKs)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=svudT_4PBKs)

## Synopsis
A tight, single-idea lesson from Jack Ruch: two movable diminished-7th chord grips used as substitutions for a functioning dominant chord inside a 2-5-1. Ruch's derivation method is the standard one (build a dim7 off the **3rd** of the dominant chord to get a rootless dominant-♭9 sound) but the value here is entirely practical — two named, fretted guitar shapes (a 4-lower-string grip and a top-4-string grip), each with four usable positions thanks to the chord's minor-third symmetry, worked live through a full major 2-5-1 (Fm7-B♭7-E♭maj7) and a full minor 2-5-1 (Dm7♭5-G7-Cm7), then combined as the opening changes of the standard "I Fall in Love Too Easily." The closing point is the most useful framing in the video: because the same D-F-A♭-B dim7 shape substitutes over both B♭7 (V7 of E♭ major) and G7 (V7 of C minor) — the two dominants share the identical set of altered upper-structure notes off their 3rds — one small vocabulary of four physical grips covers the V chord of *any* major or minor 2-5-1 you land on, and the close, minor-third-apart voicings naturally avoid the "jump down to a low bar chord" habit Ruch calls out as the thing this approach breaks you away from.

## What's taught
- **Derivation: build a dim7 off the dominant chord's own 3rd to get a rootless dominant-♭9 substitution.** Worked on B♭7 (the V of a 2-5-1 in E♭ major, chords Fm7-B♭7-E♭maj7): B♭7's 3rd is D, so stack minor 3rds from D (D→F→A♭→B) to build **D°7**. Against B♭7 that D°7 voices the major 3rd, ♭7, ♭9, and 5th on top — i.e. it sounds like a B♭7♭9, a standard altered voicing for a 2-5-1's V chord. This is the same "7♭9 = rootless dim7 + root a major 3rd below" relationship already documented on [[Diminished Chords]] (Joe Pass framing) and [[Chord Voicings]], confirmed again here from the V-chord-derivation side rather than restated abstractly. - **Grip 1 — 4-lower-string D°7 voicing, fret numbers given directly:** A string 5th fret, D string 6th fret, G string 4th fret, B string 6th fret (bottom to top: D-A♭-B-F, spelling D°7). *(General fret arithmetic, not directly stated: A5=D, D6=A♭, G4=B, B6=F.)* [~0:50] → [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Grip 2 — top-4-string D°7 voicing:** open D string, 1st fret of the G string (A♭), open B string, 1st fret of the high E string (F) — two fretted notes (both 1st fret), two open strings. [~4:20] → [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Both grips are movable in minor thirds (3 frets) for 4 free inversions — the same chord-symmetry logic already documented for dim7 shapes on [[Chord Voicings]] and [[Diminished Chords]].** Sliding either shape up 3 frets produces the next inversion of the same 4-note set with zero new fingering, giving 4 usable neck positions per grip. Worked through all 4 positions of the major 2-5-1 (Fm7 → [D°7 / B°7 / F°7 / A♭°7, each a rotation of the same grip] → E♭maj7) and again for the minor 2-5-1 below. [~1:50]–[~3:30] → [[Diminished Chords]] (symmetry rule), [[Chord Voicings]]
- **The same exact dim7 shape substitutes over the V chord of BOTH the parallel major and minor 2-5-1 a minor 3rd apart — the reason one small vocabulary covers two different key centers.** Minor 2-5-1 in C minor: Dm7♭5 → G7 → Cm7. G7's 3rd is B, and stacking minor 3rds from B gives B-D-F-A♭ — **the identical four notes as the D°7 built off B♭7's 3rd above** (B♭7 and G7 are a minor 3rd apart, and their dim7-from-the-3rd constructions land on the same symmetric chord family). Practical payoff: the same 4 physical grips (in whichever of their 4 rotations is closest to hand) work as the altered-dominant substitution over *either* B♭7 or G7, with no new shapes to learn. [~9:00] → [[Diminished Chords]], [[The ii-V-I]]
- **Combined application: link a major 2-5-1 (E♭ major) directly to a minor 2-5-1 (C minor) using the shared dim7 vocabulary, played as the opening changes of the jazz standard "I Fall in Love Too Easily."** Full progression: Fm7 → B♭7(subbed with a D°7-family grip) → E♭maj7 → Dm7♭5 → G7(subbed with a B°7-family grip, this time voiced with G in the bass) → Cm7/Cm9. Demonstrated live over a backing track, freely choosing which of the 4 rotations to use at each dominant based on hand position. [~9:30] → [[The ii-V-I]]
- **Voice-leading payoff, stated directly: close voicings a minor 3rd apart keep you near the next chord and break the habit of jumping to a low bar-chord root every change.** "Wherever you wind up on the fretboard... you always have the next chord nearby" — because the dim7 substitution grips share so many common tones with their neighbors, comping this way reads as smoother/more melodic than resetting to a root-position bar chord on every change. Matches [[Chord Voicings]]'s existing "move the least / hold common tones" voice-leading rule, demonstrated here specifically through the dim7 family. [~4:45] → [[Chord Voicings]] § Voice leading is the actual point
- **Homework note: the top-4-string grip (Grip 2) wasn't drilled through the minor 2-5-1 on camera** — Ruch explicitly leaves working Grip 2 through the C minor 2-5-1 (and the combined progression) as a self-directed exercise, "the same way we just did" with the lower voicing. Worth practicing both grips through both 2-5-1s as a completeness pass. [~12:15]
- **Generalizes beyond both worked examples: this substitution works over ANY functioning dominant chord** — any secondary dominant, or the V7 of whatever key you're in, not just the two 2-5-1s shown. [~8:40] → [[Secondary Dominants]], [[Diminished Chords]]

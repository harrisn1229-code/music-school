# Tiny Details Most Producers Miss (Bring Your Song To LIFE & Nail Your Kick) [Jauz]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hv9r2rU-2qk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hv9r2rU-2qk)

## Synopsis
A livestream clip of Jauz narrating small, easy-to-miss fixes while finishing an actual in-progress track — folding in a collaborator's synth-chord stems by matching their processing to his existing bass instead of just EQing them to fit, taming a stock Korg piano patch with a velocity-shaping MIDI effect so it reads as played rather than programmed, deliberately NOT over-brightening toms/timpani because their perceived size in the build-up actually comes from everything else thinning out around them, catching an EQ Eight linear-phase artifact that was quietly spiking the kick's low end, and closing with a multi-step kick refinement pass — shortening it, layering in an acoustic kick sample purely for roundness with an all-but-inaudible reverb on that layer, then pushing it forward in the mix with a 4-band parallel multiband plugin. The throughline is his own stated philosophy: most producers hurt their mix by trying to make individual elements stand out in isolation, when the actual fix is almost always about how an element sits relative to everything around it.

## Techniques
- **Match a new layered element's processing behavior to what it's being layered against, not just its tone.** Incorporating a collaborator's super-saw chord stems that didn't fit his own bass-lead sound, he replicated the bass's own processing — a volume/amp shaper plus a filter that opens on the repeat — onto the chords, so they swell and decay the same way the bass already does; played with stock processing the chords were "way too much," but once shaped to move like the bass they "complement" it instead of competing. The lesson generalizes past this one case: a new part often clashes less by copying HOW an existing part *moves* (its envelope/filter behavior) than by only matching its tone via EQ. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Jauz (Sol State), [Tiny Details Most Producers Miss (Bring Your Song To LIFE & Nail Your Kick)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hv9r2rU-2qk)
- **A velocity-shaping MIDI effect turns a stock piano patch from "aggressive" into "actually played."** No sample swap — just Korg's stock M1 Grand Piano with Ableton's Velocity MIDI effect placed ahead of it, reshaping the incoming velocity curve; that one device is the entire difference between a piano part that reads as performed and the same MIDI played raw, which he describes as "super aggressive" without it. → [[Velocity]] — Jauz (Sol State), [Tiny Details Most Producers Miss (Bring Your Song To LIFE & Nail Your Kick)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hv9r2rU-2qk)
- **A drum layer reading as huge in a build-up is usually the arrangement clearing space around it, not the drum itself being hyped.** His toms/timpani would sound bright, punchy, and "kind of bad" if pushed to stand out on their own — doing that "probably would have swallowed up everything else" — so instead they're left duller and sit properly in the mix; the actual reason they read as massive during the build is that everything else is filtering/thinning out around that point in the arrangement, giving them more relative room, not more absolute processing. Direct statement of his core philosophy: chasing individual elements that "stand out" in isolation is usually the wrong move — elements that "sit" properly are what makes a mix work. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]] — Jauz (Sol State), [Tiny Details Most Producers Miss (Bring Your Song To LIFE & Nail Your Kick)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hv9r2rU-2qk)
- **EQ Eight's default (non-linear) phase mode creates its own artifact bump right at a low rolloff's cutoff frequency — Linear Phase mode removes it.** A rolloff set at 25 Hz "shouldn't be doing anything" to a kick sitting well above that frequency, but soloing the kick against the filter revealed a real spike in the low end caused purely by the filter's own phase response at the rolloff point; switching EQ Eight to Linear Phase eliminates the spike entirely. A concrete demonstration that a non-linear-phase filter can color a signal even where the gain number says it shouldn't. → [[EQ Eight]] — Jauz (Sol State), [Tiny Details Most Producers Miss (Bring Your Song To LIFE & Nail Your Kick)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hv9r2rU-2qk)
- **Layer a real/acoustic kick sample under a synth kick purely for roundness, then reverb JUST that layer at a level where the reverb itself is inaudible.** Starting from his usual mid-scoop-plus-click-control synth kick, he blends in a natural-sounding acoustic kick underneath specifically for the roundness the synth layer lacks on its own, then sends an "EDM kick ambiance" reverb onto that acoustic layer alone; in the finished mix the reverb itself isn't consciously audible, but it's what lets the kick "pop through" rather than sitting flat. Distinct from reverbing a kick for perceived scale — here the reverb's job is presence/cut-through on a background blend layer, at a level deliberately kept under the threshold of being heard as reverb. → [[Kick Design]], [[Hybrid Reverb]] — Jauz (Sol State), [Tiny Details Most Producers Miss (Bring Your Song To LIFE & Nail Your Kick)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hv9r2rU-2qk)
- **A 4-band parallel multiband plugin can push a kick forward in the mix — but its output needs pulling back down hard afterward.** Picked up from an Andrew Huang "3 plugins I use most" video, Shep's Parallel Particles splits the signal into 4 independently-controlled frequency bands processed in parallel with the dry source; applied to the kick it brought it forward aggressively enough that he had to turn the output down significantly and group the chain so he could A/B it on/off, rather than trust the plugin's own default blend. → [[Parallel Processing]] — Jauz (Sol State), [Tiny Details Most Producers Miss (Bring Your Song To LIFE & Nail Your Kick)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hv9r2rU-2qk)

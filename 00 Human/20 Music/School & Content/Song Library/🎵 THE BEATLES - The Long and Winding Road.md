## Production & Arrangement
McCartney's piano ballad, famously **drowned in Phil Spector's lush orchestra + choir overdub** (added without McCartney's blessing — a big reason for the 'Let It Be… Naked' redo). The lesson is a cautionary one: over-production vs. the bare demo.

## Steal this
- **Know when NOT to add:** compare a sparse piano/vocal against a lush orchestral version — sometimes bare wins (the Naked lesson).
- **Lush orchestral pad** if you *do* go big: strings + choir under a piano ballad ([[Reverb Send Long]]).
- Descending, resolving changes for a 'closing' feel → [[Chord Progressions]].

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — The Long And Winding Road]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #175.*

**Form.** A torch song whose surface sentimentality is bought back by genuinely sophisticated structure. The engine is a **major/minor tug of war**: the relative minor, C, spends the entire verse trying to unseat E♭ and never quite manages it, which is the harmonic image of the lyric's thwarted-but-still-hopeful want. A second, subtler device: the title phrase turns up in positions that make its formal job ambiguous — intro, verse opening, or bridge tail, depending where you are standing. The verse is a leisurely **24 bars in six even four-bar phrases**, but the lyric groups them **AB / CD / C′D′**, which is why it can be that long without sagging. The song opens mid-stream with no intro. The bridge is a short 8, phrased AA, harmonically open, and **elides straight back into the verse**.

**Harmony.** `Verse (24): vi (2) – V9/11 (2) · I7 (2) – IV (2) · IV – iii – vi (2) · ii7 – V – I7 (2) · IV – iii – vi (2) · ii – V – I (2)` · `Bridge (8): [I – IV – I6/3 – ii–V] ×2` · `Outro: ii – V – I (2) · V7/11 (2) – I`. The key never actually changes — C minor is **tonicised repeatedly without ever modulating** ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]) — and his reason why is the sharpest thing in the essay: **C minor is only ever served with a minor v, never a major V**, so it is denied the leading tone it would need to establish itself ([[SCL-C01 — Natural minor]]). Watch the A♭ chord pivoting: IV in the major reading, VI in the minor ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]). At the bridge's elision back into the verse, **V → vi is a textbook deceptive cadence** — one more chance for C minor to intercept the resolution ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]).

**Notable.** The whole verse is a **fight to land structurally on the high E♭**: it appears on the very first downbeat over a C minor chord, and only at the end of phrase 6 does it arrive as a goal, by way of a plain 6 – 7 – 8. Each four-bar phrase delivers its text in a burst landing on the **downbeat of bar 3** and leaves the rest empty. Spector removed Paul's vocal from the second bridge and replaced it with the arching violin line — and trimmed a stage-whisper piano coda that would have reopened the minor question after an apparently conclusive ending.

**Against this page.** Agrees on E♭ major (this page's capo-1 D shapes).

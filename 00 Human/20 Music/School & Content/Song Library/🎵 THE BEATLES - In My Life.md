# In My Life — The Beatles

> George Martin recorded the baroque piano solo at half speed, giving it a harpsichord-like timbre when played back at normal speed. Lennon's most personal early lyric, evoking specific Liverpool memories.

## Chords
**Key:** A major

**Verse:** A – E – F♯m – A7/G – D – Dm – A
→ **I – V – vi – I7/♭7 – IV – iv – I** (the **D → Dm**, IV sliding to minor-iv, is the song's signature heart-tug; the A7/G is a descending bass)

**Bridge ("All these places had their moments"):** F♯m – D – G – A – F♯m – B – Dm – A
→ **vi – IV – ♭VII – I – vi – II7 – iv – I** (the **B** is a secondary dominant, the **Dm** the borrowed iv again)

## Production & Arrangement
Restrained, reflective band arrangement whose one famous flourish is **George Martin's baroque piano solo — recorded at half speed**, so on normal-speed playback it sounds double-tempo, bright, and harpsichord-like (and physically un-playable by hand). The rest is understated: clean guitars, gentle rhythm section, double-tracked vocal.

**Newly discovered during the 2026 archival remix: before landing on the piano solo, George Martin's FIRST attempt at filling the 4th-track "hole" was an organ part** — probably a Hammond or Lowrey (unconfirmed from the recording itself), played in a rock-and-roll style. Giles Martin (his son) says this take had never surfaced before this project; his father would have written/scored the counterpoint mentally first, realized it wasn't playable live at tempo by hand, and only then moved to the half-speed piano solution. The 4th track was routinely left as a deliberate "hole" for a late solo overdub on Beatles 4-tracks, since the record was being made for mono and didn't need the solo isolated from the rest of the band.

**Tracking detail:** guitar, bass and a "ghosted" guide vocal from an early take share one track; the released lead vocal is a **replacement** — John sang live to the existing backing track and the earlier guide vocal was wiped and re-sung on take 3, standard practice at this session, *before* Ken Townsend's Artificial Double Tracking existed at Abbey Road. The doubled harmony vocals on the bridge are two genuinely separate live takes of the same part, not an ADT double.

- The **IV → iv (D → Dm) borrowed-minor slide** is the harmonic heart-tug ([[Borrowed Chords]]); the production stays out of its way.
- Warm, close, intimate — a storytelling mix, nothing loud.

## Steal this
- **Half-speed the solo:** record a keyboard/guitar solo with the session slowed, so playback is faster/brighter than you could play — a harpsichord-ish sparkle ([[Melodyne]]/warp or literally track at half tempo).
- **IV → iv** under a held vocal for instant nostalgia → [[Borrowed Chords]].
- **Understated arrangement:** let one flourish be the moment; keep everything else restrained.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — In My Life]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #86.*

**Form.** The warmth is real, but his reading is that it sits on top of a persistent **unease built into the harmony and the melody's relationship to it** — the tune is loaded with free dissonance and rides roughshod over the chords, and the harmony systematically dodges clear V–I closure. Rote alternation of verse and bridge, folk-ballad style, but with **a bridge instead of a refrain** and with the intro motif reused inconsistently as a spacer between sections, which blurs the folk association. Intro 4 bars, a 2-bar cell stated twice; verse 8, an AA couplet on one 4-bar phrase; bridge 8, a **pseudo**-AA′ couplet whose two phrases share a melody but are harmonised very differently; outro is spacer, then a short reprise of the bridge's last phrase, then the spacer again modified into a full ending.

**Harmony.** `Intro / spacer: I – V (×2)` · `Verse: I – V – vi – V-of-IV · IV – iv – I (×2)` · `Bridge: vi – IV – ♭VII – I · vi – V-of-V – iv – I`. Simple chords, indirect syntax. **V is scarce and never resolves normally** — it appears once in the verse and is answered deceptively by vi ([[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]]), and in the bridge it does not appear at all. That avoidance is the source of the song's reticence. The verse's **minor iv** is John's signature, driven by a chromatic descent in an implied inner voice ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]), and the bridge's second phrase stacks three surprises: **V-of-V that is never followed by V** ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]), the F♯/F♮ cross-relation from the minor iv, and the deadpan fact that the melody over all of it is unchanged from the first phrase.

**Notable.** A **rising sixth is the song's motto**, opening the intro and closing both verse and bridge. The tune stays rigidly pentatonic until the bridge, where degree 4 enters for the first time on "lovers"; degree 7 never appears in the sung melody at all ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]). Against that, an unusual load of appoggiaturas, escape tones and unprepared sevenths and ninths ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]) whose cumulative effect reads as weariness. The keyboard solo was **played an octave down at half speed and sped up** — two payoffs, the impossible velocity and the mangled attack that makes an electric piano read as harpsichord.

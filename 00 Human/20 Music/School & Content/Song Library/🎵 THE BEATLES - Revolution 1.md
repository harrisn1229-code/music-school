## Production & Arrangement
The **slow, bluesy acoustic** version (White Album) — distinct from the fast, distorted single. Lennon reportedly sang lying on his back for a relaxed tone; doo-wop 'shoo-bee-doo-wah' backing vocals and driven, distorted acoustic guitars.

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — Revolution and Revolution 1]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #129.*

**Form.** One essay covering both cuts, and his verdict is that **the single is the real song** and the slower, brassy album version reads as a parody of its own remake — the ranting delivery of the single fits the words in a way the nightclub treatment does not. Musically this is heavily stylized blues: nothing obeys a twelve-bar, but the chord choices and bent notes carry the genre anyway. Intro 4 bars of manic guitar triplets; verse phrased **ABAB′CC′** with unequal lengths *and* interpolated **half-bars** ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]) — lopsided enough that it can be parsed as a verse and bridge fused into one section; refrain 8 bars, even AAA-plus-rest phrasing, **deliberately square as a counterweight**; break an abridged verse reprise then four bars piling up on V; outro grows straight out of the last refrain.

**Harmony.** Almost entirely `I – IV – V`. `Intro: I (3 bars) – IV V` · `Verse: I (2½) · IV – I · I (2½) · IV – V · ii6/4 – V · ii6/4 – ♭VII/VI – V` · `Refrain: I – IV – I – IV · I – IV – V`. That final verse phrase is the one exception and lands on **the metrically strangest moment in the track** — the only half-bar falling mid-phrase instead of at a phrase end, with the chords syncopated hard against the beat. Read locally in the relative-of-ii minor it runs `i – ♭VII – V`, and he calls it an **unconsummated modulation to ii**, a pivot set up and never completed ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]), which suits the lyric's mood. Underneath the verse there is a recurring **5–6–5 figure in the inner voice just above the bass** — readable as I alternating with IV in second inversion, but structurally a neighbour tone with expensive furniture ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]).

**Notable.** About as close as the Beatles came to a grunge aesthetic. The odder treatment is the **lead vocal dubbing**, alternating plain single-track with ADT and punctuated by bursts of triple-tracking whose edits are too clumsy to be anything but deliberate.

**Against this page.** His Roman numerals are written for **the single, in B major**; "Revolution 1" is the same song **down a tone in A major** — the key this page uses — cleanly recorded, with brass, "shoo-be-doo-wop" backing vocals, **no break section**, a fade instead of a full ending, and the extra affirmative in the first verse.

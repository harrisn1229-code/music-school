# Why You Make NO Guitar Progress - Change That Today!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hUtuZgCZ0w8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hUtuZgCZ0w8)

## Synopsis

A mindset bonus episode, drawn from BERNTH's experience coaching ~1,000 students, addressing the most common frustration he hears: "I'm stuck in a rut / not making progress anymore." Core reassurance: a flattening progress curve after the beginner phase is a normal, expected shape, not evidence of doing something wrong — the fix is converting practice into a genuine daily habit, not finding a smarter exercise. Pure methodology/mindset, no exercise attached — stays unwired.

## What's taught

- **The guitar progress curve is naturally steep early (new chord every day as a beginner) and flattens over years — expected, not a sign of failure.** Reframes "I haven't improved in months" as a normal plateau shape rather than a personal shortcoming; the actual lever available at the plateau stage is consistency of habit, not a different practice technique.
- **Distinguish practicing time from playing time, strictly.** BERNTH's own example: gigs, studio sessions, and rehearsals don't count as practice time to him even though they're hours on the instrument — practicing specifically means focused, deliberate work on an exercise or theory concept being actively worked toward mastery. (Independent confirmation of [[Practice Mindset — Hard Truths and Realistic Expectations]]'s existing "most practice isn't practice — it's just playing" point, from the reverse framing.)
- **Common failure mode: starting with a 3-4 hour practice plan.** Motivating for a few days/weeks, then attendance erodes (5x/week → 4x → 3x...) until the whole plan gets abandoned out of frustration. Fix given: a routine sized to actually repeat daily — BERNTH's own is 1 hour split into four 15-minute blocks, rotated weekly with new material; start smaller (30 min = two 15-minute blocks) if needed and grow once the daily habit itself is solid.
- **Treat the practice routine like an existing daily habit (brushing teeth), not a random event that might happen.** Same start time, same end point, every day — named as the actual mechanism that makes it durable, more than the exercise content itself. BERNTH practices at 5am specifically for peak energy and zero interruption risk, but states evenings work equally well if that's the available slot.
- **Track the routine in a journal for the first few weeks** (start/stop time, what was worked on) — both to cement the habit and to enable a monthly review of what got covered vs. neglected. Book references given: *Atomic Habits* (James Clear) and *The Compound Effect* (Darren Hardy), both credited as directly translatable to guitar practice.

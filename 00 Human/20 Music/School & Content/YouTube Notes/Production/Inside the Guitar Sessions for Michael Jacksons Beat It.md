# Inside the Guitar Sessions for Michael Jackson's "Beat It"

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4qUF4R3DkA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w4qUF4R3DkA)

## Synopsis

How "Beat It"'s guitar parts actually came together: multiple session guitarists were recorded on the track before the final personnel (Steve Lukather on rhythm guitar and Fender Precision bass, Eddie Van Halen on the solo) were settled on, and Van Halen's famous solo was comped from several takes with Van Halen and his own engineer, Don Landee, choosing which sections made the final edit.

## Techniques

- **Record multiple players on the same part before committing to one — the "right sound" is a casting decision made by comparison, not assumed in advance.** Several different session guitarists were tracked on "Beat It" before the production settled on Steve Lukather for rhythm/bass; a full track was recorded and reviewed for combination and personality fit, not just individual tone.
- **A featured soloist can own their own comp**: Eddie Van Halen recorded his "Beat It" solo, then came back later with his own engineer (Don Landee) specifically to comp the final performance together from the original takes — the artist and their trusted engineer chose which sections of which takes made the final edit, rather than the producing team assembling it independently.
- **A track has to "gel" as a whole ensemble decision, made after the featured performance is already locked** — live drums (Jeff Porcaro) were recorded at a separate session, after the guitar solo was already "in the bag," once the production's overall vision for the song had solidified — arrangement personnel decisions continued sequentially rather than being finalized all at once up front.

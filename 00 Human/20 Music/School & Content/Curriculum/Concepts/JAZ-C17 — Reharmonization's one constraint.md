# JAZ-C17 — Reharmonization's one constraint

**Q.** What is the only rule reharmonization has to obey, and what does it imply about the order you work in?

> [!question]- Answer
> **The melody note must be a usable tone of the new chord** — a chord tone (1, 3, 5, 7) or a functioning extension (9, 11, 13, or an altered version of those). What it must not be is a note the new chord actively contradicts: a natural 9 over a ♭9 chord, or a natural 4 sitting on the 3rd.
>
> Everything else — function, bass motion, density — is negotiable. So you work **melody-first**: write out the melody, decide what each note could be relative to a candidate chord, and choose from there. Choosing chords first and hoping the tune survives is how you get the clashes.

## Why it matters

Reharmonization is the practical payoff of the whole strand — it is what turns a plain loop into something that sounds composed, and it is the fastest way to get a second section out of material you already have.

The constraint is also what makes it checkable. Every technique in the note — interpolating ii chords, tritone-subbing the dominants, adding secondary dominants, changing only the bass, a pedal point, modal interchange, or reharmonising the *rhythm* instead of the chords — is legal exactly when the melody still fits.

## Drill

Take a four-bar melody of yours, write each melody note above a blank bar, and list three chords that make each note a legal chord tone or extension. Pick a path through them and play it against the original harmony.

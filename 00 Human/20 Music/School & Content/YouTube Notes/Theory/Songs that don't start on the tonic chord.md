# Songs that don't start on the tonic chord

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jfxFcToSnhQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jfxFcToSnhQ)

## Synopsis

Distinct from Bennett's "never resolves to the tonic" video (same batch): this one covers songs that DO resolve to the tonic eventually, but deliberately open on a different chord — a graded-tension technique where the choice of opening chord (IV, ii, or V) sets how much forward momentum the song starts with.

## Techniques

- **Opening on IV**: mild tension, not urgent — "attention without needing immediate resolution." Examples: "Time After Time" (Cyndi Lauper, C major, opens on F), "Bad Romance" (Lady Gaga, C major, opens on F), "Walking in Memphis" (Marc Cohn, C major, opens on F), "Viva la Vida" (Coldplay, A♭ major, opens on D♭), "Bette Davis Eyes" (F major, opens on B♭), "Anti-Hero" (Taylor Swift, E major, opens on A).
- **Opening on ii**: similar mild-tension effect to opening on IV. Examples: "All My Loving" (Beatles, E major, opens on F♯m), "Dancing in the Moonlight," "Love Song" (Sara Bareilles).
- **Opening on V — the strongest-tension opening**: the dominant is the chord with the most pull back toward the tonic, so starting there creates immediate "we can't stay here" urgency. "This Love" (Maroon 5, C minor) opens on a **first-inversion G7** — the added 7th plus the inversion intensifies the instability even further, making the "we have to move" feeling as strong as possible right at the downbeat.
- **General principle**: opening away from the tonic is a graded tool, not a single trick — IV/ii for a gentle push, V for maximum urgency — and the technique works precisely because the song still resolves to the tonic eventually; the delay is what creates the momentum, distinct from a song (covered separately) that avoids the tonic entirely.

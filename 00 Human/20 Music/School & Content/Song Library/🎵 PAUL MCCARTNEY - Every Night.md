# Every Night — Paul Mccartney

> Simple acoustic ballad from Paul McCartney's 1970 solo debut — about wanting to stay in with a loved one instead of going out.

## Chords
**Key:** G major (chord charts commonly tab it in open E-shapes with a capo at the 3rd fret — E-position chords there sound as G) · 100 BPM

The verses sit on a single droning chord — E7 alternating with E7sus2, sounding as **I7** — a static vamp under the "every night / every day" lines, all rhythm and no harmonic motion.

The pre-chorus is where it finally moves: A – F#m – Bm – F#7 – B – E, sounding as C – Am – Dm – A7 – D – G → **IV – ii – v – V7/V – V – I**. The Bm/Dm is a borrowed minor v (not the diatonic major V), used as a passing chord into a proper secondary dominant (F#7/A7, "V7 of V") that resolves correctly to B/D (V) and on home to E/G (I).

The chorus is the tightest cadence in the song: F#m – B7 – E, sounding as Am – D7 – G → **ii – V7 – I** — a clean, textbook two-five-one right under "But tonight I just wanna stay in / And be with you."

progressions: [ii-V-I]

## Production & Arrangement
From **McCartney (1970)** — his first solo album, recorded mostly **at home on a Studer 4-track** (plus some Abbey Road time) with **McCartney playing every instrument himself**. A deliberate reaction to Beatles polish and the breakup: loose, intimate, lo-fi, unfinished-on-purpose.

- Gentle acoustic-led multitrack, warm and close; the charm is the homemade intimacy, not perfection.
- One man overdubbing guitar, bass, harmony vocals — a whole arrangement from a single player.

## Steal this
- **DIY home multitracking:** build a full arrangement yourself, part by part ([[Drum Rack]]/[[Simpler]] + one-take instruments), and keep the homemade looseness.
- **Intimacy over polish:** minimal processing, close and warm — imperfections are the sound.
- **One-person arrangement:** layer guitar/bass/harmony from a single performer → double your own harmonies ([[Artificial Double Tracking (ADT)]]).

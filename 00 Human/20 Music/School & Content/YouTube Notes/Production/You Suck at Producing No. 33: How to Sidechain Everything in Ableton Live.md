# You Suck at Producing No. 33: How to Sidechain Everything in Ableton Live

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gej-sIZDTVU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gej-sIZDTVU)

## Synopsis
A short, joke-heavy but technically dense tour of sidechaining beyond the classic kick-into-bass compressor duck. Underbelly builds the same trigger routing (kick → sidechain input) into three different Ableton devices in turn — a Compressor for the classic pump, an Auto Filter for an envelope-driven filter sweep, and a Gate for rhythmic chopping — showing that "sidechain" is really just "let one signal's envelope control another device's behavior," not something limited to compressors. It closes by pointing the sidechain source at a different pad in the same drum rack (hi-hats instead of kick), which is the most useful buried detail in the video: any element of a kit can be a trigger, not just the kick.

## Techniques
- **Classic compressor sidechain** — Compressor on the target, Sidechain enabled and keyed from the kick, Ratio pushed all the way up and Threshold pulled all the way down for an extreme, obvious pump/duck. → [[Sidechaining]]
- **Auto Filter envelope-sidechain for a triggered filter sweep** — enable Sidechain on an Auto Filter, key it from the kick, then use the Envelope amount to decide the direction: a negative amount (e.g. −127) sweeps the filter DOWN every kick hit for a "whoosh"; a positive amount sweeps it UP on every hit instead. Freq and Res shape the range/character of the sweep. → [[Sidechaining]], [[Auto Filter]]
- **Gate sidechain for rhythmic chopping** — enable Sidechain on a Gate placed on a sustained synth, key it from the kick, so the synth is only audible during kick hits and silent everywhere else — effectively chopping a held pad/synth into the kick's rhythm. Pushing Floor to −∞ makes the closed state fully silent for a more extreme, hard-gated effect. → [[Sidechaining]], [[Gate]]
- **Per-pad sidechain source inside a Drum Rack** — the sidechain source dropdown isn't limited to "the drum track" as a whole; a second menu lets you pick a specific pad within the rack (e.g. the closed hi-hat instead of the kick) as the actual trigger, with pre/post/post-mixer signal-point options. Any element of a kit can drive a sidechain, not just the kick. → [[Sidechaining]]

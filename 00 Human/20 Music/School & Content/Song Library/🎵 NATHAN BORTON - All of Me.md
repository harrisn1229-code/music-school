# All of Me — arr. Nathan Borton

> A 1931 Gerald Marks/Seymour Simons standard, featured in the 1932 film *Careless Lady*, used as the full worked example for a step-by-step solo-guitar chord-melody build process: melody → in-time-with-metronome → chord roots in the gaps → full chords → chromatic passing chords → melody harmonized by top-note voicing lookup → connective lines. Full process breakdown: [[THIS Simple Process Creates Successful Solo Guitar Playing]].

## Chords
**Key:** C major. The 1931 Gerald Marks/Seymour Simons standard's commonly published changes (Real Book):

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cmaj7 | E7 | A7 | Dm7 |
| E7 | Am7 | D7 | Dm7 – G7 |
| Cmaj7 | E7 | A7 | Dm7 |
| F | Fm | C – Em7 | A7 |
| Dm7 | G7 | C6 | |

→ **I – VI7 – II7 – ii** (a **1-6-2 "turnaround" shape** opening the tune, the dominant VI7/II7 pair working as secondary dominants that keep circling back to the ii chord rather than resolving straight home) **– VI7 – vi – II7 – ii-V** (repeats, then closes) **– IV – iv (borrowed) – I/iii – V7 – ii – V7 – I**, a genuine **ii-V-I** (Dm7-G7-C6) closing out the final phrase. Both the opening 1-6-2 shape and the closing ii-V-I are why this page is tagged `progressions: [ii-V-I, turnaround]`.

The **opening phrase's harmonization below (Borton's actual arrangement)** voices bar 4 as **F major13** rather than the Real Book's plain Dm7 — not a different chord, just a different bass/root choice: **Dm7 and F6 share every pitch** (D-F-A-C = the same four notes read from either root), the identical relative-major/relative-minor overlap already logged on this vault's [[🎵 ANTÔNIO CARLOS JOBIM - The Girl from Ipanema]] page. → [[Chord Naming by Context]]

## Learning notes

- **Learned from a period source, not a lead sheet** — Borton traces the tune back to its 1932 film appearance (*Careless Lady*) and learns the melody from a recording of that era rather than reading it off a chart, deliberately keeping it "in the ears and hands," not "in the eyes."
- **Metronome discipline used while building the arrangement:** accent beat 1 only (not 2-and-4) until the downbeat feel is solid on its own — see [[THIS Simple Process Creates Successful Solo Guitar Playing]] for the full reasoning.
- **Chromatic passing chords worked over the C major voicing** in this tune's opening — slide the same shape up or down a half step (or two) as connective color. → [[Passing Chords]] § *2. Chromatic passing/approach chords*
- **Connective line between phrases:** an enclosure to the 3rd of the current chord, then down to the 5th — worked over both E7 and A7 in this tune's opening progression, used interchangeably with the chord-in-the-gap option from the same slot.

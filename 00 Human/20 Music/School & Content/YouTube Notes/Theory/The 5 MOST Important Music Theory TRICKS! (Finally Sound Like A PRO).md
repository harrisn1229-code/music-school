# The 5 MOST Important Music Theory TRICKS! (Finally Sound Like A PRO)

## Synopsis

BERNTH gives a five-topic theory roadmap for guitarists who never formally studied theory: note location/fretboard visualization, chord construction (intervals → triads), composing in a key (diatonic harmony), scale theory (visualize intervals, not shapes), and ear training. It's a beginner-facing overview rather than a deep-dive on any one topic — most of the individual ideas are already covered in more depth elsewhere in the vault — but the **octave triangle** note-location drill and the framing of "exotic scales are just the major/minor scale with one note changed" are concrete, reusable takeaways.

## What's taught

- **Octave triangle — fretboard note-memorization drill (~0:53–3:24).** Learn the natural notes (E F G A B C D) on one string first (low E), then use the octave interval's shape across the strings (visually a triangle) to find the same note an octave up/down on a neighboring string, filling in sharps/flats between naturals as needed. Recommended as a 5–10 min daily warm-up before practice until fretboard note ID is instant. Extension: once comfortable finding single notes, use the same octave-shape movement to relocate scale fragments and arpeggios across the neck, not just single notes.
- **Chord construction via intervals, not shape-memorization (~3:24–6:42).** Instead of memorizing finger shapes for major/minor chords, name the actual notes and their interval relationships: major triad = root + major 3rd + perfect 5th; minor triad = root + minor 3rd + perfect 5th — the two differ by a single note (major 3rd vs. minor 3rd). Recommends drilling all 12 intervals against a root, naming each one, before moving to triads → 7th chords → extensions in that order.
- **Composing in a key — diatonic triads by stacking thirds (~6:42–9:33).** Write out a scale (e.g. C major: C D E F G A B), then build a chord on each degree by counting up 3 notes for the 3rd and 5 notes for the 5th, using only scale notes (e.g. degree 1 in C major: 1-C, 3rd=E, 5th=G → C major chord). Framed as the fix for guitarists who stack riffs without knowing what key they're in and can't find a matching melody/solo over their own riffs. Homework given: derive the diatonic triads for F major and G minor by hand.
- **Scale theory — visualize intervals, not scale-diagram dot patterns (~9:33–12:02).** States his own past mistake: memorizing scale-diagram shapes/root notes without knowing which actual notes/intervals he was playing, which made every new scale (modes, exotic scales) feel like a from-scratch memorization task. Reframe: master the natural major and minor scale as intervals first — most "exotic" scales (his shred-scale examples) are the major or minor scale with one or two notes altered, not a wholly new pattern.
- **Ear training — practical payoff framing, not a method (~12:02–end).** Argues ear training pays off directly in transcription/gig speed: hearing intervals/cadences in your head before finding them on the instrument saves real time on studio/live/transcription work. Recommends drilling the same intervals from the chord-construction section (since they recur constantly in rock/metal riffs) plus ear-training apps that quiz interval and cadence ID (harmonic and melodic), naming V–I and ii–V–I as the most-encountered cadences. Also name-drops sight-reading, tabbing your own writing in notation software, chord substitution, and jazz-standard cadence analysis as adjacent skills worth developing later.

No production/gear content — pure theory overview.

# JAZ-C26 — Keep the concept, not the lick

**Q.** Give the steps that turn a transcribed solo into your own vocabulary, and say which one gets skipped.

> [!question]- Answer
> Learn the solo by ear in full · **chunk it** into small sections, a phrase or a chord at a time · **analyse each chunk against the chord underneath it** for the scale, arpeggio or device being used · **reduce each chunk to one named concept** — a scale choice, an arpeggio substitution, a target note · then **improvise your own solo over the same changes applying the concepts rather than the licks**.
>
> The skipped step is the fourth. Learning a solo by ear is not enough on its own — **the concept, not the lick, is the thing worth keeping.**

## Why it matters

What you actually need from a great solo is a nudge toward somewhere, not a part to reproduce. A set of per-chord concepts is a template you can play through repeatedly, which turns one transcription into a whole practice session's worth of new lines rather than one memorised chorus.

The same logic generalises past soloing: it is the melodic twin of [[JAZ-C15 — Standard forms put devices in predictable slots]], where a chart is chunked into named functional units instead of chord symbols.

## Drill

Take eight bars of a solo you already know by ear. Write one named concept per chord, put the original away, and improvise three different choruses from the concept list alone.

# Alesis HR-16

## What it is
Alesis' $449 shot at the drum-machine market — 16-bit samples at a quarter of the going rate, in a plastic box with rubber pads. It flooded bedrooms and small studios at exactly the moment house, techno, and industrial were being invented in bedrooms and small studios.

## What makes the sound
- **16-bit / 47 kHz samples** — startlingly clean for the price; big roomy toms, crisp hats, and a notoriously polite kick people learned to EQ/saturate into shape.
- **Budget converters + zero-glamour voicing** give it a flat, slightly plasticky honesty — the "found sound" of countless late-80s underground records.
- 49 voices with tune/mix per pad, decent swing, and MIDI everything — the spec sheet of machines triple its price.
- The HR-16:B variant (darker, more electronic kit) is the cult one for industrial/EBM textures.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Impulse]] with HR-16/HR-16B samples; [[Saturator]]/[[Decapitator]] to give the kick the weight the hardware lacked.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2; vintage archives.
- **Free:** full HR-16 and HR-16B sample dumps are freeware staples.

## Used on
- Genre/era: late-80s/early-90s bedroom house and techno, industrial/EBM (the :B kit), and indie demos everywhere — the classic "everyone had one, nobody credited it" box; genre-level per the rule.

# The PERFECT Bass Practice Morning Routine For Beginners

## Synopsis

A BassBuzz beginner routine built from three short, named exercises meant to fit inside a **10-minute daily session** — the "morning" framing is explicitly a consistency hack (getting practice in before the day's distractions show up), not a claim that the exercises only work in the morning. The video's real content is the three exercises themselves, each targeting a distinct beginner weak point: **Finger Fixer** (fretting-hand strength/coordination, including the pinky, via a two-bar B-minor-scale-based pattern), **String Crossing Crusher** (plucking-hand pattern vocabulary — four ways to pluck any string-crossing line, plus a pinky finger-roll technique for root-fifth-octave shapes), and **Mute Master** (fretting-hand vs. plucking-hand muting, long mutes vs. short/staccato mutes, over an F-major-scale-shaped line that's really about the muted silences, not the notes). BassBuzz frames the routine as cumulative — early sessions might spend all 10 minutes on one exercise, later sessions rotate through all three — and closes by pointing to a separate play-along workout video and a "5 beginner bass riffs" video for applying the exercises to real songs. Coaching value is in the *how*, not just the tab: each exercise's practice notes (slow-it-down, isolate a bar, watch this specific finger) are what turn the drill into an actual habit-fixer rather than "mindlessly wiggling your fingers," which the video explicitly calls out as the wrong way to use it.

## What's taught

**Exercise 1 — Finger Fixer (fretting-hand strength/coordination):**
- Two-bar pattern in the **B minor scale** shape. Bar 1: ring–pinky–index–ring–pinky–ring–middle–pinky. Bar 2 (the "weird one"): ring–pinky–index–ring–pinky–index–ring — deliberately less rote than bar 1.
- **Keep fingers low to the frets** — named as the actual source of the strength/accuracy payoff.
- Bar 2's ring finger has to **curl back** rather than reach straight down — flagged as the hardest single move in the exercise for most beginners.
- Practice approach: slow the tempo to **50–75%**, or isolate **one bar at a time**, before attempting full speed/both bars together.
- Full extraction and cross-links: [[Fretting-Hand Finger Independence Drill]].

**Exercise 2 — String Crossing Crusher (plucking-hand pattern vocabulary + finger roll):**
- Worked on a string-only pattern (A-D-G-D-A), separated from the fretting-hand notes so the plucking-hand choice can be isolated on its own.
- **Four plucking approaches**, presented as reusable vocabulary for *any* bassline, not just this drill: alternate starting on index (index-middle-index-middle-index — the default on bass), alternate starting on middle, **rake** starting on index (index-middle-index-index-index — repeating a finger across the crossing-back-down move for efficiency), and rake starting on middle. The advice: get comfortable with all four so the right one becomes an intuitive per-bassline choice rather than a fixed habit.
- For the fretted notes: default is index-ring-pinky per group; the alternative is the **finger roll** (index-pinky-pinky) — useful for common **root-fifth-octave** shapes. Mechanics: pinky fingertip frets the first pinky note (e.g. the 5th), then for the second pinky note (e.g. the octave) straighten the pinky's end joint and/or rock the arm back, fretting nearer the first knuckle instead of the tip. Flagged as genuinely hard at first and hand-geometry-dependent — expect to experiment.
- Full extraction and cross-links (including how this maps onto the existing rake-vs-alternate drill on the same note): [[String Crossing and Plucking Patterns]].

**Exercise 3 — Mute Master (fretting-hand vs. plucking-hand muting):**
- Line is shaped like a plain **F major scale**, but the actual content is the **muted spaces between the notes**, not the notes themselves — mutes have to cover open strings *and* fretted notes, some mutes are a full rest ("long mute") and some are quick/staccato ("short mute"), and the player chooses fretting-hand or plucking-hand muting per note.
- **Long mute:** play the note, release fretting pressure, touch the string (either hand) for the full rest.
- **Short mute (staccato/dotted note):** lightly release fretting pressure while staying in contact with the string, then quickly press back down for the next note — a smaller, faster motion than the long mute.
- **Open-string mute:** no fretting finger is already on the string, so mute by reaching the fretting hand over to touch the open string (or use the plucking hand).
- Practice approach: work bar by bar (each bar swaps which fretting finger is muting, which resets the difficulty), slow the tempo down or take **one to two bars at a time** — called the single biggest difficulty-reducer of the three exercises.
- Full extraction and cross-links: [[Bass Note Muting Technique]].

**Routine structure and framing:**
- **10 minutes/day** is the target dose; **morning** is a consistency recommendation (practice before the day's distractions arrive), not a claim the exercises work differently at other times.
- Early on, one exercise may fill the whole session; as each becomes comfortable, rotate through all three in one session for a rounded routine.
- Points to a separate **full 10-minute play-along workout video** (linked in the original video's description, not captured here) for a guided version of all three exercises together, and to a **"5 beginner bass riffs"** video for applying the built technique to real songs — signals the routine is meant as a stepping stone to song-learning, not an end in itself.
- Claimed 30-day outcomes from BassBuzz's own student testers: stronger fingers (including the pinky), more accurate note-hitting, less "flailing and flying fingers," fewer wrong-string hits, and better muting with less unwanted ringing/rumbling.

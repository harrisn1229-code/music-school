# All You Need Is Love — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

The verses breathe unevenly — 4/4 with 3/4 bars folded in on a pattern that's regular without being predictable — and that is exactly what keeps an anthem-march from sounding like one. Pollack's structural claim is that the song opens with **three consecutive verses of which the first is really the intro**: it carries no tune, it installs the "love, love, love" backing figure everything later rides on.

**Form.** Three bars of deliberately misquoted "Marseillaise" to open, trilled to read as vaguely Baroque. Verses phrase four-square even though their bar *lengths* don't. The refrain is a near-square 8 bars with only its last bar dropping to 3/4. The final refrain and outro take roughly 1:40 of a 3:52 record — an unusual amount of structural weight on an ending — and the outro settles fully into 4/4, giving the fade a different underlying pattern from everything before it.

**Harmony.** The verse's real motion is only I ↔ V; the chords between are voice leading, outer parts in parallel tenths over a bass stepping G–F♯–E–D and back, which is why so many of them are inversions rather than root positions ([[POP-C04 — Four chords with a moving bass]]). The song keeps sagging out of G major toward E minor. In the refrain the B major is a secondary dominant to vi ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]), but the section's fingerprint is the backing vocals sounding A and G a major 2nd apart while the tune holds D above them.

**Melody.** The strongest unifier isn't in the tune: it's the **dotted bass figure D–E–G rising at the end of every phrase**, the same shape as the misquoted intro, and it persists straight through the fade.

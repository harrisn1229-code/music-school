# dez wright's synth secrets will elevate your melodies!?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_8vOz54DTiY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_8vOz54DTiY)

## Synopsis

Another entry in loner's "reverse-engineer a hit-maker's process" series, this one built around producer Dez Wright's sample-flip approach across four reference records: Drake's "Wants and Needs," A Boogie's "Numbers," Dez's own "Flocky Flocky," and "7 a.m. on Bridal Path." The throughline across all four is that Dez's chords and melodies are deliberately simple — the actual craft is in **sound selection and layering**: which synth patch, which processing chain, and how many small texture/accent layers get stacked to create depth. The video also doubles as a promo for loner's own "Synth Diaries Volume 1" one-shot kit (300 one-shots, 100 accents/phrases, 32 perc loops/textures, a bonus Portal preset kit), which supplies most of the pluck/brass/accent/texture sounds demonstrated — the sponsor angle doesn't undercut the techniques, which are concrete and reusable regardless of the specific kit. Two other producers get name-checked mid-breakdown: Cardo (drums on "Wants and Needs") and Baby Keem's "Patience" interlude (as the direct inspiration for a synth flute layer on "7 a.m."). Auto-captions are moderately garbled throughout ("synth" → "syn," "one-shots" → "onh shots," "A Boogie" → "boogie," "half-time" spelled inconsistently) — settings/terms below are read through that lens and flagged where uncertain.

## Techniques

### Wants and Needs (Drake) — pluck/brass/accent layering
- **Sound selection over complexity — chords and melodies stay simple; the sample's identity comes from processing and layering choices, not harmonic sophistication.** Stated as the organizing principle for the whole video, demonstrated across all four reference tracks below. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Counter-melody voiced a semitone apart for deliberate dissonance/tension**, rather than consonant intervals — the "dark vibe" starting point for the sample, built from a pluck patch (sourced from "Synth Diaries," loner's own one-shot kit — see Plugin Wishlist entry below). → [[Melody Writing]], [[BED Slot]]
- **Wash-out chain on the pluck: spring reverb → Portal (glitch/texture plugin, loaded with one of the kit's own presets) → half-time → EQ**, in that order — spring reverb first for the "washed out" foundation, Portal next for glitch/granular texture on top of the reverb tail, then a half-time device, finished with an EQ pass. Owned-equivalent chain: [[Ghz DC19 3]] (built-in spring reverb) → [[Portal]] → half-time (unowned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) → [[EQ Eight]]. → [[Reverb]]
- **Low synth brass layer built from ONE chord repeating with slightly different inversions**, rather than a moving progression — another "Synth Diaries" source patch, used specifically for harmonic bed rather than melodic interest. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Synth accent one-shots layered in purely for perceived depth** — a handful of accent hits from the kit added on top of the pluck+brass bed; called out explicitly as adding "so much depth" despite being a small addition. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **Automate the half-time device to engage only in the second half of the loop**, rather than running it constantly — an arrangement-level automation move so the texture shift itself becomes a build/contrast device across the loop. → [[Automation]]
- **"Elastic pitch" used to change the sample's key AND add grit/character**, not just to retune it — pushing a pitch/time-stretch algorithm (the élastique family, also present as Pro Tools' Elastic Audio) hard enough that the transposition artifacts become part of the sound rather than something to avoid. Ableton's closest owned equivalent is the **Complex/Complex Pro** warp algorithm, already documented doing the same "lean into the artifact" move elsewhere. → [[Pitch and Formant]]

### Wants and Needs — Cardo's drums + the beat switch
- **Cardo's drum pattern kept deliberately minimal: a rim, a two-step hi-hat pattern, and one hard-hitting 808** — no dense hat rolls or extra percussion, just three elements doing distinct jobs. → [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **A melodic beat-switch built by reusing the counter-melody and adding new chords underneath on a soft pad**, timed to lead directly into the next section of the song (where Drake's vocal enters) — copy the existing counter-melody line over, then write a fresh chord layer under it on a different, softer patch, rather than composing the switch-up from scratch. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Glide on one-shots as a stand-in for a real synth's pitch wheel** — playing one-shot samples with Glide/portamento between notes reproduces the expressive pitch-bend character of a hardware synth's pitch wheel without one. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A "crying," distorted lead built from a cassette/tape-style distortion stage plus a washing reverb** — distortion for the harsh "crying" edge, reverb afterward specifically to wash/soften it rather than leave it dry and harsh. Owned chain: [[Cassette]] → [[Reverb]]. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]

### Numbers (A Boogie) — guitar sample chain
- **A simple 5-chord-family progression clicked in with a placeholder sound first, THEN matched to a real guitar recording via a chord-finder website** — write the harmony blind with whatever patch is handy, identify the resulting chords, look them up on a chord/tab site, then record a real guitar (a C minor and a G minor voicing specifically) playing those same chords into the DAW — described as "the biggest cheat code for making guitar samples." Website name not given in captions — flagged as an unidentified tool, not a plugin (no wishlist entry warranted). → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Guitar processing chain: preamp → guitar amp → cassette → flanger → spring reverb → EQ, with a perc loop + texture layered underneath for ambience.** A full six-stage ordered chain — preamp and amp for tone, cassette for tape-style saturation, flanger for movement/color, spring reverb for space, EQ to finish — plus a separate rhythmic percussion loop and a texture layer mixed in underneath the processed guitar rather than left dry. Owned chain: [[UAD Century Tube Channel Strip]] (preamp) → [[Morgan Amps Suite]] (amp) → [[Cassette]] → [[Phaser-Flanger]] → [[Ghz DC19 3]] (spring) → [[EQ Eight]]. New chain note created: [[Dez Wright Guitar Sample Chain]]. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]

### Flocky Flocky (Dez Wright) — accent-driven texture
- **Two accent one-shots placed right "on the one" (the downbeat) for instant impact**, rather than scattered through the bar — landing both accents together on beat 1 is what gives the flip its immediate punch. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **A pad layered underneath the accents specifically to fill out low-end depth** the accent-only version was lacking — accents alone read thin without a sustained low layer under them. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[BASS Slot]]
- **A couple more synth textures added at the middle and end of the loop, framed explicitly as "ear candy"** — small textural additions placed at specific structural points (not evenly throughout) rather than a constant texture bed. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]

### 7 a.m. on Bridal Path — melodic/emotional layering
- **A thick synth pad carrying simple, emotional chords as the sample's main element** — the emotional weight comes from voicing/tone choice on a simple progression, not harmonic complexity, consistent with the video's overall thesis. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A second layer from the same sample source (Synth Diaries), voiced in INVERTED chords rather than root-position doubling** — stacking an inversion of the same chords as a second layer adds thickness without just doubling the same voicing. → [[Melody Writing]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Textures and accents deliberately changed every four bars while the chord loop itself repeats** — keeps a repeating 4-bar chord loop from feeling static by rotating what's layered on top of it on a 4-bar cycle, rather than looping the exact same texture alongside the chords. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **A synth string-type layer (captioned "synth wory," likely "synth swell/string" — garbled, unconfirmed) added after the inverted-chord layer**, followed by a synth flute layer directly inspired by the flute on Baby Keem's "Patience" interlude. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Put layered elements in mono and pan them individually, rather than keeping every layer stereo, to fit more layers without crowding the mix.** A mono-plus-pan approach lets many simultaneous texture layers coexist because each occupies a narrower, more separated position instead of every layer washing across the full stereo field. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **A synth bass layered alongside a kick and a rim to round out the drum foundation** for this beat specifically (distinct from the Cardo drum pattern on the Drake reference). → [[Genre Drum Patterns]]
- **A closing switch-up: same chord progression, swapped to a harsher-sounding patch, plus one new accent that "fit perfectly."** Reusing the exact chords under a different, more aggressive timbre is a fast way to signal a section change without rewriting the harmony. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

# Jaycen Joshua on creative drum mixing

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BELkgU5j2ww)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BELkgU5j2ww)

## Synopsis

Jaycen Joshua breaks down his hip-hop/trap drum-loop processing on a tambora/percussion loop, then zooms out to the drum bus's four parallel aux sends — the real substance of the video, and directly relevant to Nate's hip-hop/trap production. The loop chain is a straight subtractive-then-additive move: a CLA-76-style FET compressor for control, a Lo-Fi/bitcrush stage for weight, an EQ cut that deliberately cedes low-end territory to the 808 while boosting upper-high-mids for cut-through, and — the most unusual move — a noise suppressor run backwards, as a deliberate fidelity-destroying lo-fi effect rather than a cleanup tool. He states his overarching mix philosophy directly: strip a sound down to what it actually needs, then rebuild only what heavy bus compression/limiting took away via dedicated parallel aux sends, each doing one specific job (transient "spark," a front-to-back attention effect, and a Fruity-Loops-style blown-out kick character) — so nothing has to sound "perfect" in isolation, only correct in its frequency pocket next to everything else.

## Techniques

- **CLA-76-style FET compressor as the entry point on a tambora/percussion loop, run to genuinely shape the sound rather than just control peaks.** "It was given to me with the CLA-76, I guess that's his go-to compressor... he is truly using it and giving it a sound through the plug-in — if I took this plug-in off, you could see how much it's taking away, it distinctively changes the sound. So I commit." Owned best-match: [[UAD UA 1176LN]] (the hardware the CLA-76 emulates). → [[UAD UA 1176LN]], [[Compression]]
- **A Lo-Fi/bitcrush-style distortion-saturation stage added specifically to give a thin, "ticky, poofy" loop more weight — not brightness or clarity.** "So I just dirty it up a little bit with the Lo-Fi — I love this plug-in. You're adding the distortion and saturation that gives it the weight that it needs." Positioned after the compressor. Owned best-match: [[Decimort 2]] (bit/sample-rate reduction for vintage-sampler-style grit and weight). → [[Decimort 2]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Cut the low end on a percussion loop specifically because the 808 already owns that frequency territory, then boost upper-high-mids for cut-through — a Pro-Q-2-style EQ move.** "The Pro-Q 2, you're taking a little bit of the low end off because obviously the 808 is taking, or occupying, all of that. And you're adding a little bit of what you need — that upper high-mid that just lets that cut through." A direct example of frequency-pocket assignment between two competing low-end sources (the loop and the 808). → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Masking and Carving]], [[SUB Slot]], [[BASS Slot]]
- **A noise suppressor used CREATIVELY, not correctively — deliberately destroying the loop's fidelity for a lo-fi/degraded character, run last in the chain.** "And then a noise suppressor, as you can see, which changes everything. You're like, why would you use the noise suppressor? ... I'm taking away the fidelity, I'm f***ing this loop up... it's a totally different sound... sometimes wrong is right, sometimes you got to make something not beautiful to understand how beautiful it is. I do it all the time." Owned best-match: [[RX Voice De-noise]] (a vocal-tuned de-noiser here repurposed destructively on a drum loop). → [[RX Voice De-noise]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Governing mix philosophy stated directly: strip elements down and give every sound its own frequency pocket rather than trying to make each one sound "perfect" in isolation.** "I always try to remove stuff so things can be on its own and separated — the bass has its frequency region, the loop has its frequency region, and so on and so forth, and when they all work together it absolutely sounds beautiful." Confirmed by ear on the full mix: "the claps are the claps, the loop is the loop, you hear everything in its position and no one's fighting against each other, but yet it doesn't sound too perfect." → [[Masking and Carving]]
- **A dedicated compression stage on a small conga/percussion detail: attack pulled back off the top, and high-mids trimmed further to leave room for the vocal, with a touch of attack restored after.** A smaller, secondary move separate from the main loop chain above — cuts more high-mid content specifically "so that vocal can sit properly," then adds a little attack back rather than leaving the element fully dulled. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[Compression]]
- **Subtractive-then-additive drum-bus philosophy: after crushing the whole drum bus hard through compression, a limiter, and a "Black Box"-style unit, four dedicated parallel aux sends rebuild ONLY the specific qualities that heavy bus processing took away, rather than backing off the bus processing itself.** "So with these four, I'm making up what I'm losing by hitting the compression and the limiter and the Black Box super, super hard." The whole system is framed as deliberate: crush confidently on the bus, then surgically restore transient life, tonal attention, and low-end character via separate, purpose-built auxes rather than one heavy insert chain. → [[Parallel Processing]], [[3 Drums Bus]], [[Committing and Printing]]
  - **Aux 1 — "Transformer": rebuild transient life lost to bus compression, low end filtered out so only top-end "spark" returns.** Chain: a Decapitator-style saturator ("Transformer... a decapitator with the drive mid-aggressive, the tone is a little bit dark because obviously we don't want to overhype this record") → an SSL-style compressor on a personal "Dr. Dre preset" nicknamed **"Cross-Eyed"**: **Attack 30, Release 1, Ratio 2:1**, drawing about 8 dB of gain reduction, with the fast attack ("30") specifically chosen to let the transient "sneak through" before the compressor grabs it, adding harmonic distortion on top → the whole aux has its low end removed entirely, leaving only "a very subtle top end of that spark" to blend back in. Owned best-match for the saturator: [[Decapitator]] (Style T, warm even-harmonic tube punch — dark tone, mid-aggressive drive); owned best-match for the compressor: [[Glue Compressor]] or [[Solid Bus Comp]] (SSL-bus-style character). → [[Decapitator]], [[Glue Compressor]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[Parallel Comp Send]]
  - **Aux 2 — an MV2-style low-level enhancer run as a "front-to-back panner" attention effect, not a volume trick.** "I wanted to parallel the loop... basically I just wanted to add weight to it. So here you have an MV2, low-level enhancer — this one brings up your floor, this one takes down the top... it's basically a front-to-back panner as well, it just gives me a little bit — without it [vs.] with it — it gives it attention, like 'oh, there it is.'" No owned direct equivalent for the specific up-the-floor/down-the-top MV2 mechanism — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Parallel Processing]]
  - **Aux 3 — "Transformer 2," on the KICK specifically: the same Decapitator-style saturator driven harder, with a Chandler-style distortion character deliberately emulating a blown-out FL Studio kick sound common in hip-hop — subtle here, but explicitly pushable to full aggression for harder hip-hop records.** "My attempt of emulating Fruity Loops and the distortion of Fruity Loops that that kick has. And I call that 'Transformer 2,' which is Decapitator, same thing, the SSL compressor same thing, the only thing that changes is I'm adding distortion from the Chandler, and I'm opening it up a little bit more... you can get real aggressive with it like most hip-hop tracks that are done on Fruity Loops and go really, really, really aggressive... but here, obviously, you want to go subtle because the track is a very seductive record, so I just wanted to add a little personality to the kick." Owned best-match for the "Chandler" distortion character: [[Decapitator]]'s own **Style E** (modeled on the Chandler/EMI TG Channel) — no separate wishlist entry needed since the owned plugin already covers this specific character. → [[Decapitator]], [[Kick Design]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]

# What Makes The PERFECT Home Studio? (Jauz & Laidback Luke Debate)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)

## Synopsis
A short debate clip (Sol State channel) between Jauz and Laidback Luke about what actually makes a home studio "perfect" — and their answer is explicitly not "the gear." Laidback Luke describes starting at 15 with zero equipment in a bedroom, and says his ideal setup today is still a simple, casual corner he can step into on impulse rather than an office-like professional studio — the writers'-camp/studio-session environment feels sterile to him by comparison. Jauz counters with a story about his teenage son, who wanted to spend studio money on inbuilt LED lights instead of monitor speakers and plugins — Jauz's instinct was that the technical tools should come first — but then admits he's doing a version of the same thing himself: he's spending a chunk of his own studio budget on color-controllable Philips Hue lighting rather than outboard gear, because he's a highly visual person (he suspects he has synesthesia) and wants the room's color to match the emotional target of what he's making. Laidback Luke, more old-school, agrees that studios have always had "character" even without smart lighting — citing dark, leather-and-goat-horns rock/metal studios, Justin Martin's room full of keyboards, and Kenny Larkin's wall of outboard gear as different flavors of the same idea. Both land on Steve Aoki's all-white "spaceship" studio and the phenomenon of producers who black out their rooms entirely (so they lose track of day vs. night) as the extreme end of "environment over gear." The throughline: once you have the basic tools, the deciding factor in a home studio is whether the room gets you into a creative headspace — that's a legitimate design goal, not a distraction from real work, and it shouldn't be gatekept by how much outboard gear or acoustic treatment you can afford.

## Techniques
- **Invest in sound tools before decoration when budget is tight** — plugins, monitor speakers, and actual sound-shaping gear should come before things like inbuilt LED strips if you're starting out and money is limited (Jauz's advice to his teenage son, who wanted lights instead of gear/plugins). — Sol State, [What Makes The PERFECT Home Studio? (Jauz & Laidback Luke Debate)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)
- **Build a low-barrier "corner" setup, not a formal studio** — a small, casual space you can step into on a whim ("do I feel like making music? yes, okay, let's go") lowers the activation energy to start working, versus a big, precious, office-like studio that has to be "stepped into" formally. — Sol State, [What Makes The PERFECT Home Studio? (Jauz & Laidback Luke Debate)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)
- **Use color-controllable smart lighting as a creative/emotional tool, not just decor** — Jauz runs his whole studio on Philips Hue so he can shift the room's color to match the mood of what he's making: blue/white for something uplifting, dark green/red for something darker like dubstep. — Sol State, [What Makes The PERFECT Home Studio? (Jauz & Laidback Luke Debate)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)
- **Lean into color-sound association if you have it (synesthesia or otherwise)** — Jauz describes seeing colors when he hears sounds and uses that directly to drive his lighting choices while writing, treating it as a legitimate part of his creative process rather than a gimmick. — Sol State, [What Makes The PERFECT Home Studio? (Jauz & Laidback Luke Debate)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)
- **Black out the room to kill time-of-day cues and protect flow state** — some producers (referenced via friends' LA studio spaces) work in near-total darkness except for a single small light, so a session can run from "2pm" straight through to "2am" without the outside world breaking the trance. — Sol State, [What Makes The PERFECT Home Studio? (Jauz & Laidback Luke Debate)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)
- **Don't let the "you need a pro studio to be a real producer" narrative gatekeep beginners** — Laidback Luke started at 15 with no equipment at all in his bedroom, and both hosts push back on the idea that kids need to see a beautiful studio to believe they can make music. — Sol State, [What Makes The PERFECT Home Studio? (Jauz & Laidback Luke Debate)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)
- **Brief a studio designer on the feeling you want, not just the acoustic spec** — Laidback Luke told his studio designer he didn't care if the room "sounded great" or had top-tier outboard gear — the requirement was that it feel like a creative space, prioritizing emotional response over technical perfection. — Sol State, [What Makes The PERFECT Home Studio? (Jauz & Laidback Luke Debate)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)
- **Give the room a visual identity that primes you to work** — cited examples: Justin Martin's studio is essentially a giant room of keyboards, Kenny Larkin's has outboard gear covering the walls, old-school rock/metal studios lean into dark, moody decor (maroon walls, black leather), and Steve Aoki's studio is a stark all-white "spaceship" — each is a deliberate visual/energetic identity rather than a neutral acoustic box. — Sol State, [What Makes The PERFECT Home Studio? (Jauz & Laidback Luke Debate)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)
- **Treat "gear vs. environment" as a real, ongoing budget tradeoff, not a false choice** — once the basic tools are covered, the video frames it as a genuine open question worth revisiting for your own setup: put the next dollar toward better gear, or toward a room that actually gets you into a creative zone. — Sol State, [What Makes The PERFECT Home Studio? (Jauz & Laidback Luke Debate)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pSjwlrfp5B8)

# 🔥 IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER - "Deadmau5 chords", drum bus, mixing, mastering, kicks (Ableton)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)

## Synopsis
A wide-ranging in-the-studio walkthrough with Oliver (Sol State), moving fast across nearly every stage of a track: preset/loop organization habits, a screenshot-worthy FabFilter sidechain setting, bassline writing by jamming and editing down, drum bus processing (mostly just Glue Compressor), parallel vs. straight OTT, why parallel compression matters for drums, the "deadmau5 chords" trick of holding a static root note under a moving progression, atmospheric "track bed" layers for filling mix space, the BIP (Bounce In Place) workflow shortcut, his philosophy on keeping the master bus empty except for deliberate full-mix moves, a full walkthrough of his personal mastering chain (down to mastering on headphones), his kick-drum philosophy (pick the right sample instead of stacking layers), and a closing rapid-fire of go-to plugins for bass, delay, tape stop, and guitar phasing. It's framed as a tour through his Splice "Power Tools Volume 2" sample pack, but the actual content is a broad grab-bag of concrete production opinions and settings rather than a pack walkthrough.

## Techniques
- **Keep a running "Collections" folder of favorite presets, culled from every new pack as it comes in** — the moment a new preset pack lands, quickly audition it and save only the keepers into a personal Collections folder, rather than leaving good sounds buried inside pack-specific browser folders. → [[Sample Browser and Clips Workflow]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Prefer stripped-down, simple loops over ones with already-complicated drums baked in** — a lot of commercial loop packs come with busy, dense drum content; the simpler, barer loops end up more useful because there's more room to build on top of them. → [[Splice Sounds]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **A FabFilter Pro-C 2 setting dialed specifically for sidechain duty, called "the perfect setting" and worth screenshotting** — the sidechain's own input-gain control doubles as a pump-depth knob: crank the incoming sidechain signal and the duck gets noticeably deeper and more aggressive; back it off for something subtler. → [[Pro-C 2]], [[Sidechaining]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Write basslines by playing them in live and jamming, not programming note-by-note** — play over a section for a few minutes, keep the best bits from the take, then clean up and edit the result into the final part. → [[Bassline Writing]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Keep drum bus processing minimal — Glue Compressor alone is "so good it's kind of all you need."** Sometimes he'll add another EQ or a saturator on top, but the Glue Compressor by itself usually covers the glue work a drum bus needs. → [[Glue Compressor]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Run OTT in parallel rather than straight/serial on drums** — going straight OTT tends to strip out low end and gets hard to manage; a parallel OTT return keeps the dry signal's punch while adding OTT's density on top. → [[OTT]], [[Parallel Processing]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Drum processing is mostly just EQ and compression — and parallel compression specifically is what makes drums "smack."** Not every drum needs it, but when a drum bus needs to hit harder, parallel compression is the tool he reaches for over squeezing the bus straight. → [[Compression Staging]], [[Parallel Processing]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **F9 Audio's Ableton racks are a recommended go-to** — flagged as genuinely great racks for Ableton, worth grabbing if you don't already have them. — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **The "deadmau5 chords" trick: hold a single static root/bass note under a full moving chord progression.** A lot of what reads as distinctively deadmau5-flavored harmony comes from writing the chord progression against one held root note rather than letting the bass follow each chord's own root — described as "a good trick for writing chords" that works fairly universally, not just in that style. → [[Chord Progressions]], [[Non-Harmonic Tones]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **"Track beds" — one- or two-note atmospheric, semi-rhythmic layers for tucking into gaps in the mix.** Not meant to be a full musical part; they're there purely to fill in space and add texture once nudged in under the rest of the arrangement. — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **BIP (Bounce In Place) as the fast way to commit and free up a track in Ableton** — a quick key command prints the selected clip's audio, faster than manually creating a new track and resampling into it. → [[BIP for L9]], [[Committing and Printing]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Nothing belongs on the master by default — the one exception is a deliberate, musical full-mix move.** His baseline rule: there's no reason to put anything on the master. The exception: if you want a specific compressor character doing something intentional to the whole mix, that's a legitimate use — just not a "just in case" processor sitting there by habit. → [[Master Chain]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Mixing is the real work; mastering should be comparatively easy if the mix is already right.** People ask a lot about mastering, but it's framed as "not really the most important thing" — spend your development time obsessing over mixing (he spent years on it and still treats it as bottomless), and a genuinely finished mix only needs a limiter and maybe a touch of EQ to master. Don't use mastering as a crutch for an unfinished mix. → [[Master Chain]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **His personal splice-pack mastering chain, walked through stage by stage, used selectively rather than all at once.** Every stage sits in the chain by default but most of the time only a couple actually get engaged per track — he starts with everything off and switches on only what a given master needs. → [[Master Chain]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Soothe used on a master specifically to mellow harsh, resonant frequencies** — not something he'd normally reach for on a master bus, but useful when a drum loop or other source has weird resonant content that needs smoothing without a static EQ cut. → [[Soothe2]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **A post-master waveform-display plugin as a pure visual sanity check, no processing** — it doesn't do anything to the sound, it just shows the waveform after the master chain, which he finds genuinely useful for confirming the shape/consistency of the final wave at a glance. — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Mono the sub/low end on the master with an imager (or Utility) rather than leaving lows stereo** — he runs an imager for this but notes Utility's mono function is "probably the better way to do it" for the same result. → [[Utility]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **A Utility gain trim as the "something's peaking a little hot" fix late in the master chain** — rather than re-balancing upstream, drop a Utility in to turn the level down a touch wherever a stage is running hotter than intended. → [[Utility]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **OTT on the master too, used sparingly and tastefully** — not scared to reach for it on the master bus specifically; the caveat is restraint — used lightly it "kind of goes with anything," used heavily it's the thing people (rightly) frown on. → [[OTT]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Masters on headphones, and says so up front as not a recommended practice** — an honest admission rather than an endorsement; flagged explicitly as something he wouldn't tell others to do. — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Just pick the right kick for the job instead of stacking five or six layered kicks.** Years spent layering kick samples was, in hindsight, mostly wasted time — start from a single sample that already sounds great out of the box (Splice has near-endless options for any job) and only invest real per-kick design time when the track is kick-forward by genre, like techno where the whole record is effectively built around the kick. → [[Kick Design]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Sylenth1 and u-he Diva as go-to synths specifically for bass sounds** — named as his regular pair when a track needs bass-duty synthesis. — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Valhalla Delay as an unqualified favorite — versatile, and only $40** — framed as an easy recommendation: you can do a huge amount with it and "you can't go wrong" at that price. → [[ValhallaDelay]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **Kilohearts' Tape Stop snap-in as a "super basic" but still-loved go-to for tape-stop effects** — nothing fancy about it, just a reliable, simple tool he keeps reaching for. → [[kHs Tape Stop]] — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)
- **A specific, "super slept on" phaser plugin as the go-to for muted funky guitar parts** — after trying most of the well-known phasers, this is the one he keeps coming back to for a deep, distinctive phaser sound; used throughout his and his collaborators' records anytime a guitar phaser shows up. — Oliver (Sol State), [IN THE STUDIO WITH OLIVER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOJ-q8k8has)

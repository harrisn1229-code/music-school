# Songs that use the Mixolydian mode

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8hE3FAM9rVk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8hE3FAM9rVk)

## Synopsis

David Bennett defines Mixolydian as major with a lowered 7th degree, explains the harmonic mechanism behind its mellow/patient sound (a weakened leading tone), and argues it's probably more common than plain major in rock — with worked examples spanning fully-committed songs, chord-only usage, blues-adjacency, and mid-song shifts to a darker mode.

## Techniques

- **The lowered 7th is the whole story.** Mixolydian = major with scale degree 7 lowered a half-step — "the major scale's cooler younger brother." Fully committed: "Clocks" (Coldplay), "Bittersweet Symphony" (The Verve), "Royals" (Lorde). Chord-only: "Sweet Child O' Mine" (a ♭VII chord carries the color even though the riff/vocal never touch the ♭7 note itself).
- **Why it sounds patient, not resolved**: the major scale's 7th is a leading tone, a half-step below the tonic, pulling strongly upward (the tension behind a perfect cadence). Mixolydian's ♭7 is a whole step from the tonic — the pull weakens dramatically, so tension/release feels laid-back rather than urgent. This is why a Mixolydian riff can loop forever without feeling unfinished — "Cars" (Gary Numan): the ♭7 riff never completes a cadence, so it "can just continue rolling forward forever."
- **The i–♭VII–IV–i vamp** — tonic establishes the key, ♭VII confirms the lowered-7th color, a perfect-4th descent to IV and back home. Heard in "Royals," "Sweet Child O' Mine," "It's a Long Way to the Top" (AC/DC).
- **Halfway house between major and blues** — Mixolydian shares the blues scale's ♭7 but keeps major's natural 3rd and 5th (no minor 3rd, no ♭5), giving a brighter edge than full minor-blues. "All Blues" (Miles Davis, *Kind of Blue*) is a modal-jazz example on G Mixolydian.
- **Key-signature confusion**: Mixolydian shares a key signature with the major scale a 4th below it. "All Night Long" (Lionel Richie, fully A♭ Mixolydian) is frequently mislabeled D♭ major on sheet-music sites for exactly this reason — the signature fixes the notes, never the tonic.
- **Mid-song mode shift as a light→dark device**: "Norwegian Wood" — E Mixolydian verse, shifts one degree darker to E Dorian for the middle eight (only the 3rd flattens). "Burn the Witch" (Radiohead) — F♯ Mixolydian verse (i–♭VII vamp) shifts two degrees darker to F♯ Aeolian at the chorus (3rd and 6th both flatten) — a much more pronounced contrast than the Dorian shift above.

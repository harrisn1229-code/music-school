# 27 Beatles songs that George Martin played on

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YDYedISQ2JU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YDYedISQ2JU)

## Synopsis

A survey of George Martin's own instrumental and arranging contributions to Beatles records — not just his production role. Covers his piano/harpsichord/harmonium playing, the varispeed trick behind the "In My Life" solo, the tape-collage circus sound on "Being for the Benefit of Mr. Kite," his string/horn orchestration on Yesterday/Eleanor Rigby/Strawberry Fields Forever/I Am the Walrus, and his own account of the producer-as-galvanizer role and the pay he never got for songwriting-level contributions.

## Techniques

- **Varispeed piano solo ("In My Life")**: Martin composed a piano solo too intricate to play at the intended tempo, so he recorded it at half speed, then played the tape back at double speed — the notes land correctly, but an octave up and with a harpsichord-like timbre, which is why the solo doesn't sound like a real piano. He'd used the same "wound-up piano" trick previously with Billy J. Kramer. "Rocky Raccoon"'s piano solo is reportedly the same technique.
- **Real harpsichord**: Martin played actual (and electric) harpsichord on "Fixing a Hole" — distinct from the varispeed-piano solos that only sound like harpsichord.
- **Harmonium**: Martin's harmonium playing appears on "Cry Baby Cry," "A Day in the Life," and "Being for the Benefit of Mr. Kite!"
- **Piano performances**: intricate/sophisticated piano parts beyond John/Paul's own playing — "Lovely Rita," "Good Day Sunshine," "In My Life."
- **Early sampling via tape collage ("Being for the Benefit of Mr. Kite!")**: John Lennon asked for a circus/fairground atmosphere. Martin originally wanted a real steam/circus organ but it would have taken ~3 years to program, so he instead cut up pre-existing recordings of steam organs and circus sounds and collaged the fragments into the instrumental break — in his own words, a direct forerunner of the digital sampler: "taking existing recorded sounds and chopping them up and using them for my own purpose."
- **Orchestration**: classically trained (oboe, Guildhall), Martin wrote and conducted every string/horn part the Beatles themselves couldn't have written — "Yesterday," "Eleanor Rigby," "I Am the Walrus." His own favorite: the cello + trumpet arrangement on the last three minutes of "Strawberry Fields Forever," where Harrison and McCartney drop out almost entirely and the harmony is carried by a triplet-based cello line and a four-part trumpet harmony.
- **Producer as "galvanizer," not just engineer**: Martin describes the role shifting over the decade from "master and pupils" early on to himself as "pupil" by the *Pepper* era — extracting what the Beatles wanted and translating it into technique. Paul was articulate and knew what he wanted; John had strong ideas but no technical vocabulary ("couldn't change a fuse"), so Martin had to interpret a described feeling into an arrangement.
- **No songwriting credit or royalty**: despite contributions that would be credited as co-writing today, Martin was paid only his standard EMI production fee — a period-accurate contrast with how thoroughly-credited modern songwriting splits work.

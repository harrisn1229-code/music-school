## What's covered

- Format and voting: a mix of professional jury and public vote, changed structure over the contest's ~70-year history; the "Big Five" (France, Germany, UK, Spain, Italy) auto-qualify for the final due to their funding contribution, and are sometimes suspected of deliberately entering weak songs to avoid the expense of hosting the following year (the winning country hosts).
- Notable trivia: rock songs rarely win (Lordi 2006, Måneskin 2021 are the only two); the shift from native-language to English-language entries began with ABBA's "Waterloo" (1974); performers don't have to be from the country they represent (Flo Rida for San Marino, Céline Dion — Canadian — for Switzerland); Ireland has won the most (7), including three in a row (1992-94), with Johnny Logan winning twice as a performer and once as a songwriter.
- **The one item with real cross-link value**: Netta's "Toy" (Israel's 2018 winner) was noted for its resemblance to The White Stripes' "Seven Nation Army" — after Universal Records/Jack White raised the issue, Jack White was added as a credited co-writer on "Toy" rather than the matter going to a lawsuit.

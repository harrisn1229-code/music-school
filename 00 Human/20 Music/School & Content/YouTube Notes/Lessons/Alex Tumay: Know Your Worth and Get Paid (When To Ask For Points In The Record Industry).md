# Alex Tumay: Know Your Worth and Get Paid (When To Ask For Points In The Record Industry)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COgdiOtxmLk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COgdiOtxmLk)

## Synopsis

Mix engineer Alex Tumay makes the case for why engineers should ask for royalty points ("points") rather than accepting flat-rate-only pay for sessions where they're doing real creative work. Industry/business content rather than production technique — the throughline is that engineers today are often functioning as uncredited producers (directing harmony choices, vocal comping decisions, arrangement calls) while the industry structurally keeps them at a "work for hire" pay level. His core distinctions: retroactively asking for more money on a job you already agreed to a rate for is not okay, but proactively asking for a point on the next session once you can point to producer-level creative input is fair; a rough equitable split when an engineer is doing that level of work is artist 45% / producer 45% / engineer 10%; and the fear that holds engineers back from asking — that they're expendable — doesn't hold up, since the engineer who actually knows the artist, producer, and A&R and keeps a session running smoothly is not easily replaced by "any old engineer." He closes on a related point about how work actually comes in: almost none of it is cold outreach via social media, nearly all of it is word-of-mouth referral from whoever he worked with last.

## Techniques

- **Ask for points going forward, never retroactively.** Once you've agreed to a rate for a session, that's the deal — you can't go back and ask for more after the fact ("you agreed to do the job at a certain rate, now if you have a conversation with the artist..."). If you're doing vocal-producing-level work, raise it ahead of the *next* session instead, especially if the artist has already shown they value what you bring by calling you back. — Alex Tumay (Sol State), [Alex Tumay: Know Your Worth and Get Paid (When To Ask For Points In The Record Industry)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COgdiOtxmLk)
- **Directing a vocal take — picking the harmony note, telling the artist what to sing — is songwriting, not engineering, and should be compensated as such.** If an engineer picks the fifth and has the artist sing it, that's the same creative act a traditional producer does in the room; when a producer directs a session that way, they get 10% of the song 100% of the time, but engineers doing identical work rarely see equivalent credit. — Alex Tumay (Sol State), [Alex Tumay: Know Your Worth and Get Paid (When To Ask For Points In The Record Industry)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COgdiOtxmLk)
- **A workable baseline split when an engineer is genuinely doing producer-level creative work: artist 45% / producer 45% / engineer 10%.** Framed as fair rather than excessive — even a single percentage point of a platinum record is still meaningful money, so asking for 10% isn't "too crazy" when the work justifies it. — Alex Tumay (Sol State), [Alex Tumay: Know Your Worth and Get Paid (When To Ask For Points In The Record Industry)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COgdiOtxmLk)
- **Don't let fear of being "expendable" stop you from asking for what you're worth.** Engineers hold back because they've historically been treated as easily replaceable; his counter-test is to notice what happens on the session you don't show up to — if you're the one who knows the producer, the artist, and the A&R and keeps things running well, a random substitute engineer won't get the same result, and that gap is the actual leverage behind the ask. — Alex Tumay (Sol State), [Alex Tumay: Know Your Worth and Get Paid (When To Ask For Points In The Record Industry)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COgdiOtxmLk)
- **Extra creative effort (harmony suggestions, comping calls, arrangement input) is currently given away for free out of genuine care, not for the money — but that's exactly why it's worth converting into a real ask once it's a repeated pattern rather than a one-off favor.** He's explicit that engineers already do this work because they care about the record, not as a transaction — the ask-for-points step is about recognizing when that care has become a standing, uncredited role. — Alex Tumay (Sol State), [Alex Tumay: Know Your Worth and Get Paid (When To Ask For Points In The Record Industry)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COgdiOtxmLk)
- **Referral-driven word of mouth, not social media outreach, is what actually books paid work.** Only around 2% of his work comes from people reaching out via Instagram or Twitter; the overwhelming majority comes from whoever he mixed for last recommending him to the next person (cited example: an artist booking him because "Kenny Beats said you're the guy") — reputation built through delivered work is the real leverage behind being able to ask for points at all. — Alex Tumay (Sol State), [Alex Tumay: Know Your Worth and Get Paid (When To Ask For Points In The Record Industry)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=COgdiOtxmLk)

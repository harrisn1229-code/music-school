## Synopsis

A second BERNTH mindset/methodology video, overlapping partly with [[5 Habits That RADICALLY Improved My Guitar Playing!]] (daily routine, early mornings) but adding real new content: specific metronome-tool reasoning, "cultivate your personal strengths" (a real conservatory anecdote), three concrete technique+theory-combining exercise examples, and find-a-mentor.

## What's taught

- **Always practice with a metronome, and why specifically:** framed as the most neglected fundamental — it's how you find technique-related weak spots at specific tempos (his own example: discovering alternate-picking breakdowns at tempos he wasn't practicing consistently) and how you track speed progress objectively. Tool specifics: uses metronomeonline.com daily (no affiliation) for three concrete reasons — a click tone tolerable over long sessions (vs. a DAW's harsher click, compared directly against Cubase's), no built-in accent so it works for any note grouping, and a built-in practice-time tracker that keeps counting across tempo changes on the same exercise (no separate timer needed).
- **Daily practice routine — duplicate of [[5 Habits That RADICALLY Improved My Guitar Playing!]]'s already-routed content** (1-hour routine, four 15-minute blocks, daily > occasional-long-session); this video adds one new habit-formation tip not in the other: **write down the four topics worked each day for the first few weeks**, to turn the routine into an automatic daily habit the way tracking helps any new habit stick.
- **Getting up early — duplicate of the same prior video's content**, with one new detail: the schedule shift to 4:30am was gradual (7am → 6am → 5am → 4:30am over time), not an abrupt change, plus an explicit caveat to protect sleep and not push the schedule past what your health allows.
- **Find and cultivate your personal strengths, rather than trying to fit a mold.** Conservatory anecdote: studied jazz guitar (chosen for the theory grounding and to play electric, not out of love for the genre), spent early years trying to "fit in" playing standard jazz repertoire and got mediocre grades doing it; the turning point came from choosing fast, technical fusion pieces for exams instead — leaning into an actual strength rather than matching the room. Takeaway stated directly: real excellence comes from investing practice time in what you're genuinely drawn to and good at, not from forcing conformity to a genre/style.
- **Combine technique and theory in the same exercise whenever possible**, so one practice block trains both at once. Three worked examples: (1) practicing legato patterns while also targeting harmonic-minor-scale fretboard blind spots; (2) an alternate/economy-picking lick that switches between three minor modes on each repetition — Aeolian, Phrygian, Dorian — forcing a fretting-hand adjustment of the one characteristic scale degree each time (Phrygian: lower the 2nd; Dorian: raise the 6th to major); (3) sweep-picking arpeggio shapes in triplets cycled through minor, major, augmented and diminished qualities on the same shape, with deliberate memorization of where the 3rd and 5th sit in each.
- **Find a mentor.** Advocates a real teacher relationship (in person if possible) over pure self-study — cites his own conservatory teachers and continuing to study lesson packs from other players as ongoing mentorship. States a good teacher both corrects mistakes and supplies motivation a solo learner can't generate alone.

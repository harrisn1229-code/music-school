# MJ's Billie Jean Bass - It's 4 Instruments!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zKzcR0sUvV0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zKzcR0sUvV0)

## Synopsis

A full breakdown of one of Thriller's most complex sounds — the "Billie Jean" bass, which is actually four separate instruments layered together, with bassist Ernest Tibbs (a Louis Johnson devotee) joining to demonstrate the real electric-bass part.

## Techniques

- **Four-layer bass stack, each layer with a distinct role**:
  1. **Electric bass** (Louis Johnson's actual part, reconstructed with Ernest Tibbs and confirmed against Greg Phillinganes' memory) — the harmonic/rhythmic foundation.
  2. **Minimoog**, doubling the electric bass an octave or in unison — a waveform slightly brighter than a triangle, reads as a subtly distorted bass, syncopated on the roots and giving the bass "a little push" specifically going into the B-section.
  3. **Synclavier FM patch (additive, not subtractive synthesis)** — four internal components layered into one digital voice: a base component, a brighter/shorter "punchier" component, an attack transient, and a signature **"digital hash"** — a fixed, pitch-independent noise/texture layer that sounds nearly identical regardless of what note is played, functioning almost like a percussion element rather than a pitched voice. Deliberately chosen for its unusual, unfamiliar-at-the-time digital character.
  4. **Analog synth (ARP 2600) "funky" component** — medium decay, a little sustain, moderate release (avoiding a clipped-off tail), filter modulated for a bit of resonant "speaking" character, and a specific patching choice: **the filter runs directly to the mixer with NO VCA stage at all** — the note is triggered purely via keyboard control-voltage gate and trigger routed straight to the oscillator and filter, so the filter envelope itself shapes the amplitude rather than a separate VCA envelope.
- **Layer function, isolated and demonstrated in combination**: the digital (Synclavier) layer alone reads thin and mostly audible in the upper register; adding the analog (ARP 2600) layer fills in body/low-mid weight; the full 4-layer stack together is what reads as one iconic, complex bass tone.
- **Historical framing**: described as a rare Thriller example where several genuinely complicated elements combine to produce what still reads as a "fairly simple" bass sound — the opposite of Thriller's more common pattern of simple elements combining into something that sounds complex.

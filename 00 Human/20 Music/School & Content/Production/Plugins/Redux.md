# Redux

**Category:** Drive/Color
**Vendor:** Ableton

## What it is

Ableton's stock bit-depth and sample-rate reducer — a simple lo-fi/bit-crush device that steps down bit resolution and sample rate, with a jitter control for irregular, less-mechanical digital degradation.

## What it's used for

Cheap, fast lo-fi/retro-digital character on drums, SFX, and sound-design layers — the classic "old video game/sampler" sound, or a harsher digital-crunch texture at more extreme settings.

## FX chain notes

Insert late in a sound-design chain (after the main tone/pitch shaping is dialed in) so the crunch reads as a finishing character pass rather than the core of the sound.

## Techniques

- **Per-element sample-rate reduction across an entire drum kit (kick/808, snare, hats, vinyl samples), not a bus-wide crush — the darkening tool behind a whole album's "not in your face" drum aesthetic.** Owned best-match for the "Avid Lo-Fi" plugin instanced separately on nearly every drum channel, each one just pulling the sample rate down (not the distortion stage, on most instances). On the 808/kick specifically, dropping the sample rate turned a thin, clicky hit into something "really dark and gritty" — push the plugin's own drive/distortion stage in when a source needs that extra grit, leave it off for a subtler cut. On hi-hats it shaves the "breathy" top end for consistency with the rest of a filtered kit; on layered vinyl/sample elements it removes an "annoying high-end ring" and reads as harder/darker. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[3 Drums Bus]] — Mix with the Masters (Kieron Menzies), [[Drum programing with Kieron Menzies]]
- **Downsample to ~2000 Hz as the first stage of a reverse/pitch-up vocal-chop texture chain.** Pull Redux's sample rate down to roughly 2000 Hz for a heavily crunched, lo-fi vocal accent, then reverse the result, pitch it up an octave, drown it in reverb, and finish with a shaper for sidechain-style ducking — the extreme downsample stage is what gives the rest of the chain its gritty, degraded starting character rather than a clean source. → [[Shaper]], [[Reverb]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — loner ft. London Rayne, [this producers unique tricks will tranform your samples!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=INQDFQLR-h4)
- **Bits pulled near-silent, then DC Shift flipped on, reveals hidden grit instead of muting the sound** — dragging Redux's bit-depth control all the way down reads as an almost-complete mute, but toggling **DC Shift** on at that same extreme setting reveals a crunchy, gritty character underneath rather than silence — a counterintuitive setting worth checking instead of assuming "bits at zero = off." Also used as a deliberate on/off A/B on a bass layer: with Redux removed the bass reads cleaner, with it reinserted it adds a layer of white-noise grit — a genuine tone choice, not a bug to fix. Pair with a **Gate** afterward to clean up any noisy tail Redux adds to the end of each note. → [[Gate]], [[Bass Design]] — You Suck at Producing (Underbelly), [How To Make EDM (Emo Dance Music)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=skrcNjB4NIc)
- **Sample-rate reduction + jitter for a "video game" character on an SFX layer** — turn the sample rate down and add some jitter for an irregular, less-clean digital-degradation feel — used as the finishing pass on a resampled laser/riser element to push it toward a retro video-game character, paired with a circling [[Auto Pan]] for added depth. → [[Operator]] (Laser SFX recipe), [[Auto Pan]] — dnksaus, [How to Sound Design G JONES Style](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d6d7VA0jAW0)

# The Dorian Pentatonic Scale - Combining Modes With Pentatonics Part 2

## Synopsis

Part 2 of a two-part series (part 1 combined Lydian with major pentatonic — not yet in the vault). This time: combine the minor pentatonic scale with Dorian by adding exactly one note, the major 6th. Same "just change one note" logic BERNTH uses across the whole modes series — Aeolian → Dorian is F → F♯ in every position of the A natural minor scale.

## What's taught

- **Dorian = natural minor (Aeolian) + one raised note, the major 6th.** Demonstrated in A: A natural minor has F natural; A Dorian has F♯. Framed explicitly as "don't learn Dorian as a separate scale — learn where the one changed note falls in every position of the scale you already know."
- **Applying that to the pentatonic box:** starting from the classic A minor pentatonic (2 notes per string, no 2nd, no 6th), add the major 6th (F♯) at specific spots to get the "Dorian pentatonic" — a 6-note hybrid, not the full 7-note mode.
- **C Lydian and A Dorian share the same key signature/notes** — both are built from raising the same scale degree (the 4th in C major → C Lydian; the 6th in A natural minor → A Dorian) and F♯ is the characteristic note in both. Stated as a practice shortcut with a caveat: you *can* substitute "play C Lydian" for "play A Dorian" since the notes match, but you then lose track of exactly where the characteristic color note falls relative to the A root, so you can't deliberately accent it.
- **Why Dorian over Phrygian for this pairing:** Phrygian (♭2) is "the metal scale" and BERNTH's own preferred fingering for it is the 3-note-per-string pattern, not this 2-note-per-string pentatonic-hybrid approach — Dorian's added major 6th fits the pentatonic-hybrid framing better and shows up naturally in blues/jazz/fusion riffs and chords (e.g. alternating perfect 5th/major 6th vamps).
- **Practice suggestion:** loop a backing track, switch between plain minor pentatonic and the Dorian-hybrid (added 6th) without leaving the 2-note-per-string fingering system, so the ear learns to place the color note as a deliberate accent rather than incidentally.

# Nowhere Man — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Verse (8, three phrases where the third equals the first two combined) · Verse · Bridge (8) · Verse (guitar solo) · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Outro. **Three bridges** — no other song in the series to this point repeats a bridge a third time.

A major-mode surface with a **flat sixth pushing up through it**. The C natural sits prominently in the backing vocals and effectively forces a **minor iv into a major key**, one of Lennon's favourite chords ([[SCL-C05 — Characteristic notes]], [[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The other oddity is the bridge's iii → IV, last seen in "I Feel Fine".

Sustaining the A chord through bridge bar 7 gives the harmonic rhythm a subtly slow syncopation, and the bridge tune keeps dwelling on notes belonging to the chord before or after the current one. Those two lags combine to produce the **illusion of a 4–3 suspension closing the section when no suspension is there at all**.

The verse tune traverses a descending octave from the tonic and is deliberately plainer — melodically and rhythmically — than the octave descent in "Norwegian Wood", which runs from the fifth ([[MEL-C01 — Melodic contour]]). That blandness is what frees attention for the **little guitar riff trailing every verse**, the song's real hook, which descends an octave from the fifth with fanfare-like arpeggiation. The vocals are the event: one of their most ambitious three-part outings, opening **a cappella**, chorale-style in the verses and switching to lead-plus-la-las in the bridges as the lyric moves from description to direct address.

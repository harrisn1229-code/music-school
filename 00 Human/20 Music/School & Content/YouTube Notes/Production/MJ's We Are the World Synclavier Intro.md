# MJ's "We Are the World" Synclavier Intro

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QdTiJsFHmPo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QdTiJsFHmPo)

## Synopsis

The origin story of the bell intro on "We Are the World" (1984-85, one of Marinelli's first professional gigs): Quincy Jones asked for FM bells "to call all the people of the world" to the already-fully-recorded song, then rejected the first pass with the memorably vague direction "those are silver bells, I need golden" — solved entirely by ear-tweaking the FM ratio in real time.

## Techniques

- **Translating a synesthetic/color-based creative note into an FM parameter change**: given the feedback "silver bells, need golden," the fix was iteratively adjusting the FM ratio (not volume, not envelope) until the timbre read as "warmer"/"gold" rather than "cold"/"silver" — a concrete example of mapping vague color language onto FM ratio as the actual lever, arrived at in 5-10 minutes of by-ear tweaking.
- **Letting a bell/pad's natural decay ring past the cut point rather than chopping it**: the recording was stopped while the FM bell sound was still audibly ringing out, deliberately preserving the tail rather than cutting cleanly — natural to a Synclavier bell patch's long decay, and part of why the intro reads as atmospheric rather than a hard-edited sting.
- **Building an arrangement live and collaboratively in session, in real time, without hearing the finished song first**: the intro was constructed element-by-element on the spot (bells → "golden" bells → an added church-bell layer → a swelling stereo "swirl"/bloom element), with a second musician (Omar Hakim) brought in mid-session to add a melodic phrase after the bells — none of it pre-composed against the actual finished vocal track, which the team hadn't yet heard.
- **Technology-era constraint, for historical context**: this was FM-only (Synclavier, pre-polyphonic-sampling era — only mono sampling existed at the time), so the intro had to be physically recorded to tape and spliced/synced onto the front of the already-mixed multitrack song rather than dropped in digitally.

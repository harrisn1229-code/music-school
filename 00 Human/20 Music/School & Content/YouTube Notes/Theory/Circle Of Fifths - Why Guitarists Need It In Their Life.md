# Circle Of Fifths | Why Guitarists Need It In Their Life

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXqIvYafSLs)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXqIvYafSLs)

## Synopsis

BERNTH turns the circle of fifths into a practical fretboard-learning system: play the SAME fretboard fingering pattern (built on the C major scale shape) at every step around the circle, changing only the one note that the key signature actually adds — so learning all 12 major keys across the neck becomes one continuous chain of single-note edits to a shape already known, instead of 12 separate scale boxes to memorize or a single box relocated to a different (possibly inconvenient) part of the neck.

## What's taught

- **The exercise setup.** Base every key's scale on the C major fingering pattern at a fixed neck position, keeping the fingering identical across all 12 keys and only raising/lowering individual notes by a semitone as each key's accidentals require — deliberately NOT relocating the shape to start on each new root (the common guitarist shortcut of "just slide the box"), so the shared fingering makes the note-by-note differences directly audible and visible. Only the C major scale in the exercise starts from its own root; every other key's root sits somewhere else within the same fixed fingering.
- **The core technique: each step around the circle of fifths changes exactly one note.** C major → G major requires raising only F to F♯ (matches [[Keys]]'s existing key-signature-order content) — so a player who already knows C major across the neck can get G major across the neck for free, without moving position or learning a new shape, by changing that one note everywhere it occurs in the known fingering. G major → D major is the same one-note edit repeated (raise C to C♯), and so on around the circle — each new key is "the previous key's fingering, one note edited," chaining indefinitely rather than resetting to a fresh memorization task at every key.
- **Fretboard framing for the fifth interval itself.** Visualizes the fifth as a fixed fretboard shape (root on the A string, 3rd fret = C, moving through the circle in one direction; reversing direction covers the other half of the circle back to C) rather than as an abstract theory diagram — ties the circle directly to shapes already under the fretting hand.
- **Stated payoff beyond fretboard fluency:** this connected, one-note-at-a-time way of learning keys pays off specifically for sight-reading, notating your own compositions, and general composition work — framed as a deeper foundation than the box-sliding shortcut, even though the shortcut technically works.

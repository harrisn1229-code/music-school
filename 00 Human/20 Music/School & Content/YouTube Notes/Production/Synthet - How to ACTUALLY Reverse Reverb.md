## Synopsis

A deeper pass on the bounce-and-reverse reverb technique than the quick Edison version in [[Synthet - Reverse ANYTHING]]: bouncing each reverb tail out as its own audio clip (100% wet, no dry signal) instead of leaving the reverb live solves an overlapping-chords problem where a big, desired-sounding reverb was interfering with the next chord — bounced tails can be laid out and edited individually like samples. The video also uses the visualized, bounced tail as a diagnostic tool for tuning pre-delay, and closes with pitching a bounced tail for extra variation.

## Techniques

- **Bounce each reverb tail as its own 100%-wet clip instead of leaving reverb live** — when a big reverb's tail overlaps and clashes with the next chord change, bounce-in-place (Ctrl+C bounce, or the fuller re-render) each chord's reverb as a separate clip and lay them side-by-side manually — full control over exactly where each tail sits, none of the clash of a live send bleeding into the next chord. Bounce with **no dry signal** so the clip mixes cleanly back in alongside the original. → [[Reverb]]
- **A bounced/visualized reverb tail is a pre-delay diagnostic tool** — looking at the waveform of a snare's bounced reverb tail shows exactly when the reverb starts relative to the hit; adding a small gap (the pre-delay setting) before the tail starts keeps the transient of the source hit clear before the wash arrives.
- **Chop and reuse a bounced tail like an instrument** — once it's a standalone audio clip, it can be chopped, rearranged, and treated like any other sample rather than staying tied to its original trigger.
- **Pitch a bounced tail for extra variation and tension** — a light bonus move: a bounced reverb clip, lightly automated up in level, builds tension into a section; pitching the bounced clip itself is a further variation.

# How I Use The Modes of The Major Scale

## Synopsis
A short, plainspoken intro to the seven modes of the major scale, from Jack Ruch (the same channel behind [[How I Approach Practicing]] and [[Think Different with Chord Substitutions]]). The first half derives all seven modes from a single C major scale by walking the root note up through each scale degree (C Ionian → D Dorian → E Phrygian → F Lydian → G Mixolydian → A Aeolian → B Locrian) and ties each mode to the diatonic chord it fits over in a I–ii–V–I–vi–IV–I progression in C, with the corresponding mode name displayed on-screen as the chord changes underneath an improvised solo — a direct demonstration of the same "relative approach" already documented on [[Diatonic Modes]]. The real value of the video is the second half: three quick vamp-based **practical applications** — specific two-chord contexts where reaching for Dorian, Lydian, or Mixolydian (rather than the plain parallel major/minor scale) is the deliberately better choice, each demonstrated by improvising over a short vamp. This is a light, beginner-leaning lesson (11:48, no tab/notation on screen) — thin compared to the full mode/chord-quality tables already on [[Diatonic Modes]], but the three vamp-context rules are new, concrete, and worth capturing as reusable soloing heuristics.

## What's taught
- **All seven modes derived from one major scale by walking the root note through each degree** — C Ionian (C major, degree 1), D Dorian (minor scale with a raised 6th, degree 2), E Phrygian (minor scale with a flat 2, degree 3), F Lydian (major scale with a sharp 4, degree 4), G Mixolydian (major scale with a flat 7, degree 5), A Aeolian (natural minor, degree 6), B Locrian (minor scale with a flat 2 and flat 5, degree 7) — same seven notes throughout, only the starting/ending note changes. Matches [[Diatonic Modes]]'s table exactly; no new formulas here. (~0:45–2:15)
- **Each mode maps to the diatonic chord built on that same scale degree** — Ionian↔I, Dorian↔ii, Lydian↔IV (explicitly worked example: over an F major IV chord in the key of C, reach for F Lydian) — demonstrated live over a diatonic vamp (C–Dm–G–C–Am–F–C) with the matching mode name captioned on-screen as each chord arrives, framed as a way to "start wrapping your head around how these modes... fit into the context of a diatonic chord progression." (~2:15–3:45)
- **Dorian over a minor-key vamp moving to its major IV chord** — in a minor key (demoed as C minor → F major, i.e. a major IV chord borrowed from the parallel major), reach for the **Dorian mode of the tonic** (C Dorian here) instead of the plain natural minor: the F major IV chord contains the note A, which is Dorian's raised 6th degree, so Dorian "works really well" specifically because that raised-6 note is already sitting inside the chord you're soloing over — natural minor's ♭6 (A♭) would clash. → **[[BASS Slot]]/[[LEAD Slot]]** soloing context; ties directly into [[Diatonic Modes]]'s Dorian entry (minor + raised 6th) and the i–IV(major) borrowed-chord move on [[Borrowed Chords]]. (~4:00–4:55)
- **Lydian over two major chords a whole step apart** — demoed as a C major → D major vamp: D major's own 3rd (F♯) is Lydian's signature raised 4th relative to C, so soloing in **C Lydian** over the pair lets that color note land as a real chord tone on the second chord rather than a passing dissonance against C. General rule: when two major chords sit a whole step apart, treat the lower one's Lydian scale as the shared soloing scale. (~5:00–5:55)
- **Mixolydian over dominant/bluesy two-chord vamps** — demoed as a C major → G minor vamp: G minor contains B♭, which is Mixolydian's flat 7 relative to C, so **C Mixolydian** covers both chords cleanly; framed generally as the mode to reach for "over dominant chords and bluesy" contexts. (~6:00–6:45)
- **Practical framing throughout: pick the vamp's "odd note out" (the chord tone that doesn't belong to the plain parallel major/minor scale), then choose whichever mode already contains that note as one of its own scale degrees** — the same underlying logic across all three application examples (Dorian's raised 6, Lydian's raised 4, Mixolydian's flat 7), stated implicitly rather than named as a rule by Ruch, but consistent enough across all three demos to be worth stating as the actual takeaway skill. *(Synthesized from the three worked examples — not a direct quote.)*

# Hofner B-Bass H-Series

**Category:** Electric Bass
**Vendor:** Höfner
**Ballpark value (used, 2026):** $250–375

## What it is

Höfner's affordable H-series take on the 500/1 violin bass — hollow body, short scale, staple-style pickups. The silhouette and thump of *the* McCartney bass.

## What it's used for

The Beatles lane, literally: warm, round, short-sustain bass that sits like an upright; melodic basslines that read as hooks ([[BASS Slot]] hook case).

## Signal chain notes

DI or mic'd through the [[Fender Blues Junior]]; flatwound-style thump loves light compression and no HPF below its ~100 Hz fundamental (house rule).

## Manual

- 📄 [[00 Human/20 Music/School & Content/Production/Manuals/Hofner B-Bass H-Series.pdf|Local PDF — Höfner Vintage Control Panel instructions]] · [Official source](https://www.hofner.com/web/content/41995/Violin%20Bass%20Panel%20Instruction.pdf)

**High-level points** (the mental model, not a feature list):
- No dedicated owner's manual exists for the H-Series specifically — Höfner publishes the control-panel wiring/function sheet (used here) rather than a full instrument manual; this is the same real control layout across the vintage-style 500/1-derived violin/club basses (Ignition/Icon/H-Series lines all share it).
- Two independent volume knobs, one per pickup, not a master + blend — Volume 1 (nearest the bridge) controls the treble/bridge pickup, Volume 2 (nearest the neck) controls the bass/neck pickup.
- **Three toggle switches, not two — and they do two unrelated jobs.** *Bass On* and *Treble On* are **pickup on/off + tonal-character selectors**, not blend pots: Bass On kills the bridge pickup and adds bass boost; Treble On kills the neck pickup and adds treble boost. *Rhythm/Solo* is a **pure level switch**, unrelated to pickup selection — the sheet's only description is "boosts the overall volume of the bass when in the solo position."
- **There is no tone knob on this bass.** Volumes and switches are the entire control set, so any instruction to "roll the tone down" has to be executed as **Bass On** (neck pickup only + bass boost) — that switch is the tone control. Worth stating because chain notes written for normal basses assume a tone pot that isn't here.
- **Rhythm/Solo practical note** (beyond the sheet): the Rhythm position is a passive attenuator, so it also reads slightly *darker* — the network loads the pickup. For DI recording prefer **Solo** and set level at the preamp; padding the signal before a Hi-Z input costs signal-to-noise for nothing. Rhythm/Solo is **not** part of the both-switches-silent quirk below.
- **Bass On + Treble On both in the "On" position simultaneously = silence** (Rhythm/Solo is not involved) — a genuinely confusing quirk of the vintage wiring that reads as "broken bass" to anyone used to a normal 2-pickup blend circuit. To hear both pickups blended, both switches must be Off/full-on and both volumes turned up.
- Hollow semi-acoustic body (violin-shaped) + short 30" scale + staple-style single-coil pickups is what gives the thump/quick decay McCartney-style tone — it's structurally different from a solid-body bass, not just a cosmetic choice, which is why it doesn't sustain or fight for low-end space the way a solid-body P-Bass/J-Bass does.
- Flatwound strings (stock) are core to the tone — round-wounds would fight the intended warm/dead-thump character this bass is built around.

## Techniques

- **A real 1960s Höfner used to play actual chord voicings instead of root notes, then distorted along with the rest of the mix specifically to shrink its low-end footprint.** Myles Smith / Peter Fenn's *Dying Day* bridge: Peter plays chords (not a root-note bassline) on a genuine 60s Höfner, distorts it alongside the electric guitars — confirmed by A/B mute/solo that the distortion audibly thins the low end, freeing up room for the vocal to sit on top rather than fight the bass for space. → [[Compression]], [[EQ]] — Myles Smith / Peter Fenn (Tape Notes), [[TN 184 Myles Smith & Peter Fenn - Full Video Episode]]
- **This is the exact bass silhouette demonstrated in a from-scratch 60s/70s psych-rock sample-flip build** (the video used a plugin replica since a hardware Höfner wasn't tracked live for that session — the real instrument, owned here, is a direct substitute). Recipe: finger-picking play style with the part **muted 100%** — the damped/muted setting specifically read as period-correct (short, thumpy McCartney decay rather than a ringing sustain) — through a Fender Bassman amp replica, then heavily compressed (Waves CLA-76 in the source; owned equivalents [[UAD UA 1176LN]] / [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII]]), with a ~100 Hz high-pass applied to match the natural roll-off of period microphones — singled out as giving the bass "so much more of a natural feel." Full chain note: [[Beatles Psych-Rock Sample Chain]]. → [[Compression]], [[EQ]], [[Vintage Gear Worklist]] — loner, [these 1960's production secrets will change the way you make samples!?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ngz_fZraTP4)

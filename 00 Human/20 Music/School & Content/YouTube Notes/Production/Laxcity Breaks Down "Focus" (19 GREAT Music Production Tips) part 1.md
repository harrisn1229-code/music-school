# Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)

## Synopsis
Laxcity (Sol State) walks through the FL Studio project for his song "Focus," narrating tips as he solos and A/Bs individual chains. This is part 1 of a 2-part breakdown split across 19 total tips — this half covers the intro pluck/vocal chop, the vocal bus (mid-side widening, compression, OTT, multiband-as-de-esser), a philosophy segment on sticking to stock plugins and deliberately "breaking" devices for new sounds, string layering with Kontakt one-shots, his "intelligent guessing" approach to layering by timbre, reference-listening for arrangement ideas rather than copying, the pre-drop buildup (train field-recording texture, reverb-fading vocal, automated master pitch detune), and a full sound-design tear-down of the drop's lead patch (dual sine + FM, mid/sub layer split, subtractive EQ carving, an envelope/transient shaper for glue, and reused vocal one-shots from his own Splice pack).

## Techniques
- **Turn a drop's own signature sound into a melodic pluck by running it through a convolution reverb, then chopping the result into a new melody.** Laxcity took the sound already used in the drop, processed it through FL's convolution reverb (Fruity Convolver), and chopped the resulting texture into a chopped melodic line for the verse — reusing one sound's character to generate a second, related sound rather than designing from scratch. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Build a vocal's mono-to-stereo drop transition on a dedicated mid-side EQ bus, not the main vocal bus.** The vocal routes through a bus that forces the signal mono for the verse, then into a second bus doing mid-side EQ + stereo widening for the drop — EQ'd to remove whatever sounds bad widened, plus an enhancer — so the vocal is intentionally narrow/centered in the verse and only goes stereo for extra presence when the drop hits. → [[Width and Stereo]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Use a multiband compressor as a manual de-esser on the vocal bus, narrowed to the S/T sibilant band.** After a plain compressor for level control and OTT for presence, Laxcity runs Fruity Multiband Compressor purely to catch and flatten every S/T/harsh consonant so it doesn't spike, rather than reaching for a dedicated de-esser plugin. → [[De-essing]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **OTT for vocal presence, then EQ specifically to clean up the artifacts OTT itself introduces.** OTT is used on the vocal chain purely to add presence/brightness; because it also brings up unwanted low-mid buildup and general "muck," an EQ immediately after OTT is used to cut those newly-exposed frequencies rather than to shape the vocal's original tone. → [[OTT]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Learn your DAW's stock plugins deeply instead of chasing third-party ones — "everything that's in FL is all you'll ever need."** Laxcity's entire "Focus" session is built almost entirely from stock FL Studio plugins; his stated philosophy is that the time investment in mastering the stock toolkit (rather than collecting new plugins) is what actually moves production forward, and it's also free. — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Deliberately misuse or "break" a plugin/DAW parameter that isn't meant to be touched — the accidents are often the best sounds.** Named example: automating the *master pitch* knob (not meant to be automated) to create sweeping detune effects; because everything in a DAW can be recorded/automated, tweaking a parameter outside its intended use is a real source of new sounds, not just a gimmick. Related named example: Gross Beat's built-in scratching effect used far outside its typical purpose. → [[Automation]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Layer individual one-shot string samples (Kontakt Philharmonik violins) under a string-section patch for realism.** A stock string-section sound gets noticeably more "heavenly" and realistic once individual violin one-shot samples are layered underneath/alongside it — the patch alone sounds thinner and more synthetic without the one-shots. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Layer by "intelligent guessing" — pick a second sound whose real-world character or timbre plausibly matches the first, not a random texture.** Named examples: a pluck layered with the sound of a glass bottle being hit, because both read as a percussive "hitting something" transient; a choir layered with strings because they share similar timbral characteristics. The test is whether the two sounds make sense being heard together, not whether they're from the same instrument family. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **When a sound loses power or interest, the default fix is layering, not more processing on the same sound.** Named recipe: a supersaw layered with white noise on top specifically to push it toward feeling more intense — white noise chosen because it reinforces the "intensity" quality the supersaw is already going for. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Fight the "this idea is terrible" voice by referencing another artist's song for arrangement ideas, not by copying their sounds.** When self-doubt creeps in mid-song, Laxcity listens to another artist's track specifically for how they structure the arrangement — how they build drops, build-ups, verses, and melodies — then adapts that structural idea into his own thing rather than lifting sounds directly. Framed explicitly as "not necessarily stealing." → [[Referencing and Translation]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Hide a barely-audible field-recording texture (a passing train) under the pre-drop section for extra depth.** A recording of a train going by is mixed in low enough that it's nearly inaudible on its own, sitting under the winding-down pre-drop section purely to add subconscious texture/depth rather than to be consciously heard. — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Fade a vocal out with reverb so it feels like it's leaving and staying at the same time, right before the drop.** A pre-drop vocal moment is set up so the dry signal fades out while its reverb tail keeps ringing — creating the sensation the vocal is both fading away and still present simultaneously, used as a transitional device into the drop. → [[Reverb]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Automate the master pitch downward through a buildup so every instrument detunes together, creating a rising-tension "wind up" before the drop.** Rather than a filter sweep or riser sample, the entire mix's master pitch is automated to detune progressively through the buildup section; A/B'd against the buildup with the automation removed, the plain version noticeably fails to build the same anticipation for the drop. → [[Automation]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Build a drop lead from two sine waves with FM modulation plus a low-pass filter, then OTT + EQ to intensify and clean it.** The A-side drop patch is described part by part: two sine oscillators with FM modulation turned on, run through a low-pass filter for the base organ-like tone, then OTT for extra intensity/hit, followed by an EQ specifically to remove the low-end and high-end artifacts OTT introduces (OTT is called out as causing "a little bit crushing effect in the high end"). → [[OTT]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Duplicate the same synth patch into a mid layer and a sub layer, swapping the sub layer's oscillator from sine to square wave for extra harmonic content.** The drop lead is actually two instances of one patch at different octaves — a "mid" layer keeping the original sine wave, and a "sub" layer using a square wave instead of a sine at the lower octave — each then separately processed (saturation on the sub layer, an EQ removing unnecessary mid frequencies) before being glued back together. → [[Layering and Stacking]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Subtractively EQ two layered patches so each one owns its own frequency pocket — that's why they glue.** The mid layer and sub layer of the drop lead are explicitly carved apart by ear (rather than each being tuned in isolation) so the sub-layer patch "has its own space to sit in" low, and the mid/top patch "has its own like mid-rangey space to sit in" — credited as the actual reason the two layers sound like one glued sound rather than two competing patches. → [[Masking and Carving]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Use an envelope/transient-shaping device to tighten a patch's attack for extra glue, even when the difference is subtle.** A dedicated envelope controller shortens the drop lead's amplitude envelope; A/B'd, the difference is nearly inaudible on its own but becomes clearer when isolated — described as a small but real contributor to how "glued together" the final patch reads. Reverb baked into the original patch is also manually trimmed out of the rendered audio clip so it doesn't linger unwanted. → [[Transient Shaper]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)
- **Reuse vocal one-shots from your own previously released sample pack instead of re-recording or re-chopping from scratch.** The vocal ad-lib shots in "Focus" (specifically "Vocal One Shot 3") are pulled directly from Laxcity's own "Medicine" sample pack, released earlier on Splice — recommended as a straightforward time-saver: reuse assets you've already made and cleared rather than rebuilding equivalent sounds per song. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Laxcity (Sol State), [Laxcity Breaks Down "Focus" (19 GREAT Music Production Tips) [part 1]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NseRLW5SN3I)

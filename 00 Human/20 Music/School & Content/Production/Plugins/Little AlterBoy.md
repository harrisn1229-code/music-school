# Little AlterBoy

![[Images/Little AlterBoy.png]]

**Category:** Pitch/Vocal
**Vendor:** Soundtoys

## What it is

Soundtoys' pitch and formant-shifting effect, offering real-time pitch correction, harmony, and robotic/creature-voice effects.

## What it's used for

Creative vocal pitch/formant effects — robotic voices, gender-bending formant shifts, simple harmony.

## FX chain notes

Used as a creative vocal effect insert, separate from a transparent pitch-correction tool like Auto-Tune.

## Techniques

- **Robot/Quantize mode on nonsense-syllable backing vocals for flat, static "not real harmony" textures.** Rearrange backing vocal lyrics into nonsense syllables, then run through AlterBoy's robot mode — the result reads as static, one-note texture rather than a sung harmony part, stacked with a second even-more-processed layer through [[Bitspeek]]. → [[Bitspeek]], [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Charli XCX Vocal Chain]] — Charli XCX / A.G. Cook / Finn Keane (Tape Notes), [[TN 185 Charli XCX, A. G. Cook, Finn Keane - Full Video Episode]]
- **Crunched, hard-panned backing vocals promoted to lead at a song's end, artifacts left in deliberately.** Heavily distorted BVs (processed through **Waves Butch Vig Vocals**) sit hard-panned through most of a song, then take over as the loud lead at the very end; extra digital grit AlterBoy introduces at that setting is kept rather than cleaned up, with only light compression for dynamic range — "we're leaning into all of that sort of extra dirt... doesn't really matter [if it's] digital or analog." → **Waves Butch Vig Vocals**, [[Width and Stereo]], [[Charli XCX Vocal Chain]] — Charli XCX / A.G. Cook / Finn Keane (Tape Notes), [[TN 185 Charli XCX, A. G. Cook, Finn Keane - Full Video Episode]]
- **Pitch AND formant both shifted up together on a resampled ad-lib layer** — a slightly different move from this note's usual per-layer-formant-depth pattern: here both pitch and formant are raised in the same direction (formants specifically called out as raised "a lot," not just repitched) to change the character of a baby/ad-lib layer before it heads into MicroShift and a touch of Auto-Tune. → [[MicroShift]], [[Auto-Tune Pro]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]] — Overmono (Tape Notes), [[TN 187 Overmono - Full Video Episode]]
- **Used on a NON-vocal source — a string patch, not a voice — as the closing stage of a five-effect "dress it up until unrecognizable" chain.** Output Analog Strings → sample delay → clip distortion → [[ValhallaRoom]] → a channel EQ → AlterBoy last, turning a plain patch into "crazy kind of messed up old sounding" cinematic strings ("some Hans Zimmer type things"). Auto-captioned as the recurring "little altar boy" homophone. A reminder AlterBoy's formant/pitch/drive stage is useful as a character-aging tool on melodic/instrumental sources, not just vocals. *(qualitative only — no Pitch/Formant/Drive/Mix given)* → [[ValhallaRoom]], [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Finneas Drum Sampling Chain]] — Finneas (Mix with the Masters), [[Creative drum sampling with Finneas]]
- **A 3-take vocal stack (main/high/low) recorded completely raw first, each earning a DIFFERENT AlterBoy pitch/formant setting once processing starts — the earliest (2020) documented instance of the per-layer-formant-depth pattern this channel repeats for years afterward.** Building a from-scratch '90s R&B chord-bed sample: main, high, and low vocal takes are all recorded first with zero effects ("you don't have to be able to sing to do this"), THEN processed individually. Main: EQ (cut lows) → AlterBoy at the SAME pitch, Formant down 5 → reverb + a very slight delay. High: EQ (cut lows) → AlterBoy pitched ALL THE WAY UP, Formant down only a little → reverb pushed noticeably heavier than the main take ("I want this to be more in the [mix]"). Low: EQ (cut some lows) → AlterBoy also pitched all the way up but Formant brought down MORE than the high take → average reverb → a Fruity Delay (owned-lane [[Delay]]). All three are then bounced to one stem and filtered (Arturia high-pass sweep) specifically to leave "room" for a future artist to rap/sing on the eventual flip. → [[EQ]], [[Reverb]] (effect), [[Delay]] (effect), [[Auto Filter]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[90s R&B Sample Chain]] — loner, [how to make your own 90's rnb samples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PQjIBDgJehQ)
- **"Autotune boy" — a new auto-caption homophone joining this channel's long list — on a wordless 4-bar vocal chop used as texture, not a lead vocal.** Building a from-scratch '90s R&B chord bed with no real sampling: a deliberately nonsense-lyric 4-bar vocal run gets pitch shifted **all the way up**, Formant brought **down a little**, then EQ (cut lows AND highs) → reverb specifically "to kind of drown it out, space it out." A second "harmony" layer of vocal chops behind the main run runs "pretty much exactly the same" chain (AlterBoy → reverb → delay). → [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[LEAD Slot]], [[Bryson Tiller Fake-Sample Chord Bed Chain]] — loner, [how to make smooth rnb beats for bryson tiller from scratch (no samples)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZMB_sqF_Y3Q)
- **Two self-recorded melodic vocal takes for a sample flip, both sharing a base AlterBoy → EQ → reverb chain used defensively to mask an untrained performance, one take distinguished by an added tremolo/auto-pan + light delay.** "They both have AlterBoy, they both have EQ — like, quite a lot — trying to drown out... because obviously I can't sing, so it's drowning out the bad frequencies and the stuff you don't want, and a lot of reverb as well." Take 2's one distinguishing move is a tremolo control panning the vocal side-to-side (see [[Ghz Trem Control 3]]) plus a bit of delay — the same "shared base chain, one differentiating move per take" pattern already logged elsewhere on this page. Both takes are then bounced to one stem, double-timed, chopped to fit, with the last two small chopped parts reversed for a variation. A separate pass reverses and pitches the chop's companion mallet part down an octave (and pitches the vocals down to match) specifically to signal the verse section. - **A self-recorded 3-layer vocal stack differentiated mainly by formant depth: main vocal at Formant −5, first harmony at only −2.5 — the SMALLER shift on the harmony is what keeps it reading as a distinct, less-processed voice next to the main.** Full chain, main vocal: EQ (cut ~200 Hz and below, small high boost) → AlterBoy, Formant down ~5 (pitch untouched, so the key doesn't change — only the perceived pitch/timbre drops) → the recurring **"vocal distance sci-fi"** reverb preset (already logged twice elsewhere on this channel), pulled to ~50% mix, size nudged up — kept present, not buried. First harmony: same EQ/AlterBoy structure but Formant only down ~2.5, paired with a vintage-style reverb pushed further back/wetter than the main vocal's. A third layer skips AlterBoy's formant move differentiation and instead differentiates via reverb type (spring, big) + a single (not ping-pong) 8th-note delay with drive pushed up. The lesson generalizes past this specific build: varying the FORMANT AMOUNT itself (not just reverb/delay) is a valid way to make stacked layers of one voice read as different voices. → [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Reverb]], [[Delay]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[Vocal Comping and Takes]] — loner, [how to add your own vocals to your r&b samples (in depth tutorial)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NP27-3YFlDk)
- **Octave-up pitch, then AlterBoy, as the first two stages of a 6-stage 2000s R&B lead-vocal chain (repeated on the harmonies).** Chain in order: pitch up an octave → AlterBoy (no specific preset named) → [[Pro-Q 4|Pro-Q 3]] (cut highs AND lows) → [[Decapitator]] (**"mild tube vox"** preset, reached for specifically when the vocal "isn't cutting through the mix enough") → an unconfirmed "Intensity" reverb (**"vocal distance sci-fi"** preset, almost all the way wet — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]) → a super light delay. Harmonies underneath, hit at the end of each 4 bars, run "pretty much exactly the same" chain. → [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Decapitator]], [[Pro-Q 4]], [[Reverb]], [[Delay]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]], [[2000s R&B Piano Sample Chain]] — loner, [how to make a 2000's r&b sample & flip it into a beat for tory lanez/bryson tiller](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sPqBmZLaDM4)
- **A full 4-layer "choir from one voice" recipe — main/low/high/mid-tone takes, each earning a DIFFERENT AlterBoy setting rather than one shared preset.** Building a from-scratch gospel-choir sample: record four takes (main, low, high, and a fourth in the middle range but with a different vocal tone/approach), then process each differently — **main**: Formant down "very slightly" + very short ambient reverb (the lightest touch, closest to natural); **second (high) layer**: Pitch up a full octave + Formant down slightly, same reverb preset as the main layer, deliberately paired with it so the two read as one fused voice; **third layer**: same octave-up/formant-down combination, but since it reads "a lot more open and high pitched," it gets a longer, heavier reverb than the first two (reverb scaled to how exposed the layer sits, not uniform); **fourth (low) layer**: no AlterBoy at all — EQ cut on the low end, then [[Decapitator]] for crunch, then a heavy reverb. All four bounced to one stem once balanced. - **Another confirmed use of the pitch-up/formant-down move, this time closing with a bandpass EQ → heavy "drown it out" reverb → phaser chain.** On a consolidated vocal chop repurposed as a textural accent (not a lead vocal): AlterBoy pitched all the way up with Formant brought down a little, then a narrow bandpass EQ carve, a heavy wet reverb specifically to bury/obscure the source, and a phaser for movement.

# Chromeo Gives Chord Tips

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYgam1P6QtQ)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYgam1P6QtQ)

## Synopsis
Sol State sits down with Chromeo's Dave 1, who walks through the chord structure of one of their songs chord by chord, starting from a deliberately basic minor 7th and building it up into a full funk progression through two static-bass/moving-triad tricks, a plain-triad "home" resolution, and a jazzy 11th-chord tag at the end; he closes by naming the single practical trick he actually uses to find and communicate a chord in the first place — tracking the leading tone, which is usually just the top note of the voicing.

## Techniques
- **Build a whole progression around "change on one hand, hold still on the other"** — the funk-harmony principle driving every move below: keep some element (usually the bass note) locked in place while another element (the upper voicing) moves, and keep the moving part's intervals smooth and inside a tight range rather than leaping around. → [[Upper Structure Chords]], [[Chord Progressions]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [Chromeo Gives Chord Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYgam1P6QtQ)
- **Start from a deliberately generic, over-familiar progression on purpose, not as a fallback** — the underlying chord loop is described as "a progression you've heard 100 million times," basically what a beginner writes; the sophistication in the arrangement comes entirely from the voicing moves layered on top of it, not from picking an unusual progression. → [[Chord Progressions]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [Chromeo Gives Chord Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYgam1P6QtQ)
- **Chord 1 is deliberately the most basic voicing available: a plain root-position minor 7th (root, minor 3rd, 5th, minor 7th)** — stated outright as "very basic," the low bar the rest of the progression escalates from. → [[Seventh Chords]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [Chromeo Gives Chord Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYgam1P6QtQ)
- **Chord 2: hold the bass note from chord 1 and drop an unrelated major triad on top** — the triad by itself is called "the cheesiest chord on earth," but stacked over a bass note that isn't one of its own chord tones and left static, it stops sounding cheesy and starts sounding "fancy" purely because of the bass/triad mismatch. → [[Upper Structure Chords]], [[Chord Inversions]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [Chromeo Gives Chord Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYgam1P6QtQ)
- **Chord 3: keep that exact same top triad and switch only the bass note underneath it** — the identical three notes on top now get reinterpreted as belonging to a completely different chord (an m7/11-type chord built on the new bass), and the move is described as landing "a beautiful resolution." → [[Upper Structure Chords]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [Chromeo Gives Chord Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYgam1P6QtQ)
- **The final "home" chord is a plain major triad that would sound "boring" and "horrendous" in isolation, but reads as satisfying arrival because of everything before it** — proof that a resolution chord doesn't need its own interesting color; the buildup does the emotional work, and a maximally plain tonic triad is often the correct choice for "you're back home." → [[Chord Progressions]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [Chromeo Gives Chord Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYgam1P6QtQ)
- **Add short rhythmic "stabs" voicing an 11th chord right after the resolution for extra jazz flavor — the "Isaac Hayes touch"** — a quick way to keep a simple resolved progression from sitting flat once it lands, without altering the progression's actual harmonic path. → [[Extended Chords]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [Chromeo Gives Chord Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYgam1P6QtQ)
- **Find and voice-lead a progression by tracking a single note — the leading tone, usually the top note of the voicing — rather than the whole chord** — described as how the songwriting pair actually works together: one of them can hum or sing just that one top note, and the other builds the correct full chord underneath it, using the leading tone as the anchor that tells you which voicing is right. → [[Guide Tones]] — Chromeo (Sol State), [Chromeo Gives Chord Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZYgam1P6QtQ)

## What's taught

- **Why app-based ear training doesn't transfer to real music**: most apps are non-contextual — a single note ringing in free space, or over a static drone/chord, not the constantly shifting harmonic context of real music where the underlying chord root keeps moving and recontextualizing how a melody note "sounds." A given melody note takes on a totally different feeling depending on which chord it's currently sounding against (a C over a C chord sounds like "1," the same C over an F chord sounds like "5," over a Dm chord like "♭7").
- **Two separable skills, both trainable**: hearing a melody note's feeling relative to the CURRENT chord underneath it ("chord-relative," useful for voicings/comping) vs. hearing it relative to the overall KEY CENTER regardless of which chord is under it at the moment ("key-relative," the scale-degree skill, and the one that transfers to playing back what's heard). It's possible to hear both simultaneously with enough training — "that's the seven of the current chord, but the three of the key" — but this starts as two separate, sequential judgments.
- **The bridging drill from drone-isolated training to real music**: take a song already familiar, listen for the melody's scale-degree feelings as they go by, pause and sing/play the melody to check accuracy, relisten with the confirmed answer in mind, repeat on new material. Doing this consistently trains the mind to parse real music the same way it parses the isolated drone exercises — the ear starts to "get the idea" of what's being asked of it.
- **Improvising/composing mixes melodic-distance thinking and scale-degree-feeling thinking simultaneously**, not sequentially — wanting to hear a note that's "a half step above where I am" AND "the 6 of the key, the 9 of the chord" happens at once, with no conscious separation of technique; when the feeling is unclear in the moment there's a fallback to calculation ("I'm targeting the 9 of the two chord") that becomes felt experience the instant the note actually rings.
- **Hearing two simultaneous melodic lines at once is a separate, rarely-needed skill** not much trained even by an experienced ear — usually handled by hearing one line, then the other, sequentially, rather than truly simultaneously; harmony-plus-melody (as opposed to two melodic lines) is a different, more commonly needed skill built the normal way (voicings + scale degrees mixing together).
- **Q: should melodic-interval training be dropped in favor of scale degrees only?** No — not a waste of time, but scale degrees rank higher in priority. Both matter in practice: leaping a 5th feels distinctly different from leaping a 3rd or a 7th when improvising, and that awareness comes naturally through enough playing with conscious attention on it. The recommendation is periodic, not constant, attention — name things, know the key you're in, check in now and then — rather than being a stickler about labeling everything all the time.

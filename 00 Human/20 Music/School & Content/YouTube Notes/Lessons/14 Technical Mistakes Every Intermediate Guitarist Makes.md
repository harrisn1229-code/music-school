# 14 Technical Mistakes Every Intermediate Guitarist Makes

## Synopsis

A rapid-fire 14-point checklist of small physical-technique errors covering both hands — thumb placement, finger angle and grip choice (Hendrix vs. Italian), fret-wire avoidance, bend direction and mechanics, grip pressure, economy of motion for both hands, and hammer-on/pull-off execution. Ordered roughly common-to-rare. Full extraction routed to a dedicated technique note; see [[Fretting and Picking Hand Mistakes Checklist]].

## Techniques

Full 14-point list extracted and organized in [[Fretting and Picking Hand Mistakes Checklist]] — highlights:

- **Two named fretting grips, used contextually, not one "correct" grip.** "Hendrix" (baseball) grip — thumb hooked over the neck — for open chords and bending; "Italian" grip — thumb behind the neck, fingers not angled — for single-note lines and lead playing higher up the neck, since the Hendrix grip's over-the-top thumb limits reach there.
- **Minimum-pressure test:** slowly increase fretting/pick pressure only until the note first rings clean, then stop — that's the real pressure needed, no more.
- **Economy of motion applies to both hands** — minimize how far fingers lift off the fretboard/strings between notes, with the caveat that it's a fix for passages that *aren't* working, not a rule to police once something already sounds right.
- **"Double notes" are a hand-sync problem** — a note sounding twice (once from a hammer-on/pull-off, again when picked) means the fretting and picking hands aren't landing on the note at the same instant.
- **Weak pull-offs come from a passive lift instead of an active flick** — the fretting finger needs to actively flick/pluck the string sideways as it releases to set the string in motion.

# You Suck at DJing Ep. 2: Changing Tempo on the Fly

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JNOxvlc96wo)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JNOxvlc96wo)

## Synopsis

Covers the DJ-mixing problem of blending two tracks whose BPMs don't match closely enough to force one onto the other — instead, ride the master tempo gradually between the two BPMs during the transition itself, timed to a bar-matched mix window.

## Techniques

- **Map a controller fader to Ableton's master BPM, with a narrowed range.** The mapped-parameter range defaults to the full 999 BPM Live supports; in MIDI-map mode, narrow it to a musically useful window (e.g. ~80–165) so the fader's full travel actually corresponds to usable tempo changes instead of being mostly dead range.
- **Ride the BPM fader gradually across a transition to bridge a tempo gap too large to just snap to.** For a ~7 BPM gap between two songs, sweep the master BPM smoothly from the outgoing song's tempo to the incoming song's tempo *during* the mix window, rather than jump-cutting the tempo — the audible tempo drift reads as a natural DJ tempo-ride instead of a mismatch.
- **Scout the mix-out point's actual bar length before starting the transition.** Find where the outgoing song's outro naturally sits (e.g. an 8-bar outro starting at measure 41) and plan the transition length around that, rather than mixing in at an arbitrary point.
- **Double a too-short intro by re-triggering it mid-mix, to match the outgoing song's mix-out length.** If the incoming song's intro is only 4 bars but the outgoing song needs 8 bars to mix out cleanly, re-trigger the incoming intro clip partway through so it effectively plays twice — stretching it to match the outgoing section's length for a smoother, unhurried blend.

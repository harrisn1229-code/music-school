# The hard truth about learning to improvise (Bebop Scale Guitar Lesson - Level 2)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pIk3A3lmt4Y)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pIk3A3lmt4Y)

## Synopsis
Jared Borkowski's second video in a series on improvising melodies over harmony using the bebop scale and controlled chromaticism. Where [[Bebop Scale Guitar Lesson (Level 1) - Master the Art of Intentional Note Placement|Level 1]] establishes the bebop scale itself (major 6, dominant 7, and minor 7 versions), this video is entirely a practice-methodology lesson: a single graduated drill system, worked over one static chord (B♭7, shell voicing, 5th/6th position), for training the hands to land chord tones on strong beats — the mechanical payoff of the bebop scale's added passing tone. He names the drill "treading water" (looping back and forth between a chord tone and its neighbor tone instead of stopping or defaulting to running the scale straight), builds it from 2-note cells up through 5-note cells, and runs each cell through the same 4-step progression (open-ended repeats → 2 reps → 1 rep → free improvisation) so the targeting skill transfers into real playing rather than staying a pattern. The video is candid about the cost — Jared calls the drilling itself "monotonous" and "arduous" and frames the whole lesson's title around that honesty — and explicit that the goal is generating your own original melodic vocabulary rather than memorizing licks. No new gear, plugins, or production content; this is pure instrument/theory pedagogy.

## What's taught

- **Prerequisites, stated explicitly as gate-checks, not assumptions:** (1) chord tones of the target chord (root/3rd/5th/♭7) known well enough to improvise with *only* those four notes over a loop; (2) the matching scale (B♭ Mixolydian, noted as enharmonically the same shape as E♭ major — a [[Diatonic Modes]] relative-mode relationship) known well enough to improvise with just the scale; (3) the **bebop dominant scale** itself, up and down, in time (1‑2‑3‑4‑5‑6‑♭7‑passing-tone‑1) — this is the deliverable of [[Bebop Scale Guitar Lesson (Level 1) - Master the Art of Intentional Note Placement|"Level 1"]] in the series. *(~1:00–5:30)* — Jared frames failing any of these gate-checks as a genuine win ("you hit a gold mine for practicing"), not a reason to feel behind — go drill the missing prerequisite instead of forcing the main exercise.
- **Backing setup:** loop a simple shell voicing (root‑3rd‑7th or root‑7th‑3rd) of the target chord to practice against for the whole lesson, so there's always a harmonic reference. → [[Guide Tones]] shell voicings.
- **"Treading water" defined:** oscillating between a chord tone and an adjacent note instead of stopping to think or defaulting to running the scale top to bottom — described as staying afloat/surviving versus drowning. The core rule underneath it: chord tones land **on the beat**, the connecting scale/neighbor tone lands on the **offbeat**, played as straight eighth notes (not swung — swinging an unmusical drill "feels cheesy"; swing is saved for real playing). *(~9:00–12:00)*
- **The 4-step progression, run at every note-count level:** (1) tread water as long as needed on a cell, untimed rep count, until it's solid; (2) restrict to exactly **2 reps** before switching cells; (3) restrict to exactly **1 rep** — the hardest step, and the one where the drilled cell stops sounding like an exercise and starts sounding like a usable lick, because playing every chord tone once each with its neighbor *is* a chord-tone-on-the-beat melodic line; (4) **let go and freely improvise**, watching for the drilled cell to surface on its own rather than forcing it every time — the actual transfer step. *(~10:30–14:00, repeated at each level through ~32:00)*
- **2-note cells:** chord tone + one neighbor tone, each side of every chord tone drilled separately — root↔♭7-below and root↔2nd-above; 3rd↔2nd and 3rd↔4th; 5th↔4th and 5th↔6th; ♭7↔6th and ♭7↔major-7th (the passing tone). Worked live over B♭7 with the backing loop, both ascending through the chord tones and back down. *(~12:00–19:00)*
- **3-note cells:** chord tone with a neighbor on *both* sides, three notes per cell, same beat-anchoring rule, same set of chord tones walked through in the same order. *(~19:00–24:00)*
- **4-note cells, including a deliberate "wrong" note:** chord tone plus two more notes extending one direction. One of the four-note cells introduces the **major 7th over the dominant chord** — a note in neither the chord nor the Mixolydian scale, normally an outright bad note over a dominant 7 — and Jared calls it out explicitly as working *only* because it's placed strictly as a chromatic passing tone on an offbeat, describing the resulting rhythmic feel as "almost polymeter." Direct applied example of [[Bebop Vocabulary]]'s core rule (the extra chromatic tone exists to keep chord tones on strong beats, not for its own color) and a real-world [[Non-Harmonic Tones|passing-tone]] case. *(~24:00–29:00)*
- **5-note cells:** same targeting idea extended further; noted to actually line up *more* evenly against the beat than the 4-note version, making it feel easier despite the longer cell. *(~29:00–32:00)*
- **Stated purpose of the whole system:** builds personal melodic "vocabulary" — original licks generated from your own hands because the chord-tone-targeting reflex is automatic, as opposed to (a) running the scale straight, which is accurate but "sounds like a scale, not music," or (b) leaning on memorized licks from other players, workable but stifling if you can't manipulate them. Jared explicitly ties the same targeting ability to hitting a **chord change** accurately when harmony moves — this single-chord version is meant to isolate the targeting skill before adding chord-change timing on top. *(~32:00–34:30)*
- **Practice-mindset framing (the video's own "hard truth"):** the drilling is meant to feel monotonous and arduous — that's presented as the honest cost, not a sign something's wrong; expects real practice time across many sessions over weeks/months, not one pass. *(throughout, stated most directly ~0:40–2:00 and ~34:00–35:00)*
- **Series/resource pointers (lower priority, not concrete technique):** points to a free printable scale-reference PDF (seven scales covering all modes) and a paid chord-tone/arpeggio vocabulary pack (12 chord types × 5 positions) at soundguitarlessons.com; mentions a future video will go deeper on the "keep playing while you think ahead" ability this drill builds. *(~35:00–35:42)*

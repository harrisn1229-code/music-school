# I'll Be Back — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

The song is built on one device: **constant alternation between the major and parallel minor tonic** ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The two-bar intro exists almost entirely so its major chord sets a trap for a verse that starts minor; the fade-out then loops the same alternation so the song ends unresolved by design rather than by convention.

**Form.** Three bridge sections, not two — bridge 1 appears twice and a different bridge 2 sits in the middle, though the two are cousins: **the last two and a half bars of bridge 1 are repeated verbatim in bridge 2**. Two deliberate irregularities ([[ARR-C06 — Section-length conventions, and breaking them on purpose]]): **bridge 1 is 6½ bars**, with a half-measure in the middle, which in a world of 4-, 6- and 8-bar phrases is what makes the section stick; and **the final verse is cut to half length**, because with three verses and three bridges already banked a full one would overstay.

**Harmony.** The verse is a descending stream drawn from **natural minor** ([[SCL-C01 — Natural minor]]), one chord per bar over a bass walking down and back. Despite that pull the section is harmonically **closed**, and Pollack refuses to notate the passing second-inversion sonority as a chord change because the bass never moves under it. Bridge 1 leans toward the relative minor and immediately bails back to V. Bridge 2 is more open still, on the prowl for a key for five or six bars without ever actually leaving home, and carries a **descending chromatic inner line** plus a cross relation between adjacent chords.

**Melody.** Narrow range, thick with appoggiaturas ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]). The verses hinge on the major/minor third switch. Bridge 1 stacks **three appoggiaturas in close order on the same two pitches, each under different harmony**.

**Arrangement.** Percussive acoustic rhythm guitars with light maraca-like drumming. Relief comes from the vocals: parallel thirds in the verses with **John on the bottom part and Paul on top, yet John's is the voice you hear as the melody** — a recurring illusion in their catalogue. Surviving takes document the arrangement being found: an early one is in 3/4, with electric rhythm guitar and no intro at all.

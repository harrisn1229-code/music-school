# Uncommon Advice from Benny Blanco, Disclosure, Kenny Beats, Chris Lake, Jon Sine

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4aDSXwm-QI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4aDSXwm-QI)

## Synopsis

A five-artist rapid-fire compilation, mostly mindset and career rather than technical: Disclosure open on the "you don't need expensive plugins" framing and then pivot into a preference for imperfection and character over a polished mix; Kenny Beats relays a specific piece of career advice Benny Blanco gave him directly; Chris Lake makes the case that differentiation, not fitting in, is the actual job of an artist; Benny Blanco (in his own segment) explains how he revived a stalled stretch of his career by changing his approach rather than waiting it out; and Jon Sine tells the story of the worst professional decision of his career — a bad management deal — and how he engineered his way out of it early. Note: Disclosure's opening "don't buy expensive plugins, our biggest hit was all stock Logic" bit and their closing "mix on budget speakers, cheap Tannoys from my dad's auction house" bit are the exact same interview footage already fully captured in [[Wish I Knew This Before Spending $3k on Plugins]] — both are skipped here as verbatim repeats; only the genuinely new back half of Disclosure's segment (their imperfection/character preference) is included below.

## Techniques

- **Chases imperfection and character over a polished mix, deliberately.** With age, prefers old, crackly, character-heavy mixes over clean ones — polish is easy to achieve now, but natural character is the harder skill: "I'm never looking for a super clean professional mix now, I'm looking for character, I'm looking for vibe, I'm looking for a surprise, I'm looking for something in there I don't quite understand how it was done." → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]] — Disclosure (Sol State), [Uncommon Advice from Benny Blanco, Disclosure, Kenny Beats, Chris Lake, Jon Sine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4aDSXwm-QI)
- **Benny Blanco's one-line career advice: make yourself hot first.** Despite offers to sign, manage, or start a publishing arm around him — the standard move once you're established, and what every other successful person in his position had told him to do — Benny told him the opposite: focus fully on your own music and your own goals, you can always start signing/managing people later. The only advice-giver in that position who ever made him feel like he was doing the right thing, backed by the fact Benny himself now releases his own music as an artist rather than only working behind the scenes. → [[Kenny Beats - Motivation, Overworking and Building Reputation]] — Kenny Beats, relaying advice from Benny Blanco (Sol State), [Uncommon Advice from Benny Blanco, Disclosure, Kenny Beats, Chris Lake, Jon Sine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4aDSXwm-QI)
- **Differentiation is the actual job — copy nothing, build a body of work other people want to copy instead.** Being told his beats were "trash" for not following a trend didn't change his approach; the point of having a career as an artist is a body of work you can look back on as influential, which means deliberately trying to create something other people will want to copy, not the reverse — that's what moves a scene forward. Your signature sound is like handwriting: recognizable and personal, and something you have to consciously find and learn to express, not something that shows up by accident. — Chris Lake (Sol State), [Uncommon Advice from Benny Blanco, Disclosure, Kenny Beats, Chris Lake, Jon Sine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4aDSXwm-QI)
- **A stalled career gets revived by changing your approach, not by waiting it out.** After a stretch where "no one could give a [damn] about my music," the only thing that changed was how connected he became to what he was actually making — he felt it more himself, other people started feeling it too, and renewed interest built from there; explicitly frames the music industry as neither simple nor quick, so a comeback is a deliberate shift in how you work, not a matter of time passing. — Benny Blanco (Sol State), [Uncommon Advice from Benny Blanco, Disclosure, Kenny Beats, Chris Lake, Jon Sine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4aDSXwm-QI)
- **Background-check any multi-year deal before signing — a bad song deal costs one song, a bad management deal costs years.** His worst career moment: signing to a management company on a reputation that turned out to be a decade stale. Three months in they'd done nothing for him but were still contractually entitled to a cut of everything he made. His lawyer's advice for exiting a deal early (standard lock-in was three years): make yourself annoying enough — pointed daily questions about what they weren't doing for him — that they offer to void the contract themselves; it worked within a couple of months. Turned the lost time into more dedication rather than less: started his own label and fully self-managed afterward. — Jon Sine (Sol State), [Uncommon Advice from Benny Blanco, Disclosure, Kenny Beats, Chris Lake, Jon Sine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j4aDSXwm-QI)

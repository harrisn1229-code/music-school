# Vocal and synth production with Stelios

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=agOGoEeJbvw)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=agOGoEeJbvw)

## Synopsis

Stelios (producer for the artist Lolo) walks through a vocal- and synth-focused breakdown centered on writing melody that literally mirrors its lyric, keeping a lead vocal deliberately bare (unpanned, barely doubled) to preserve intimacy, and using small, fast, mostly-instinctive ear-candy flourishes to keep repeated sections alive. The second half is a short synth-design digression: a degraded, pitched "falling apart" synth built for chorus 2's variation, colored with UAD tape emulation (Ampex + Studer) with hum and hiss deliberately left on — described honestly as a beginner's accident with the plugins that he grew to like on purpose. Runs alongside a candid songwriting note about the featured artist's lyric being drawn directly from her own real life at the time (working as a waitress, wearing thrifted/fake-gold jewelry), framed as the reason the song reads as true rather than performed.

## Techniques

- **"Music onomatopoeia" — write the melody's contour to physically enact the lyric's meaning, not just to sit nicely over the chords.** On the word "low," the melody dips down in pitch exactly as the singer says "low" — a device the producer says he and the artist don't have a formal name for, so they've coined their own term. The payoff is described as subconscious: a listener may not consciously register it on a first listen, but on a second pass the connection between the sung word and the melodic motion reads as a small, "holistic," world-building detail. → [[Melody Writing]]
- **iZotope VocalSynth's formant-shift-down used to build a lower "double" that's really a thickening layer, not a genuine second take.** "Again, at the time that was done using iZotope VocalSynth, and you just lower the formant down a little bit — that's just the common strategy to take the lead vocal and change the formant to help it beef up the actual lead." The result reads as a distinct low vocal sitting under the high lead rather than a true unison double. Unowned plugin — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Pitch and Formant]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **No left-right panning at all on the lead vocal, kept dead center on purpose to preserve a "lonely" feeling.** "There's actually nothing panned left to right. I wouldn't normally think to do that, but there was something that just felt nice about the simplicity — just having that one lonely vocal with these weird little clips of vocals kind of sputtering around it." Paired with minimal doubling throughout ("kind of naked in a way") as a deliberate simplicity choice for the chorus specifically. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[Masking and Carving]]
- **Ableton's pitch/transpose tool used to generate quick ad-lib/ear-candy flourishes from existing vocal material rather than recording new takes.** "Some of those were created by using Ableton transpose" — a fast, low-effort way to multiply the amount of usable ear candy scattered through a verse without more studio time with the singer. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **Background "ahs" layered under a verse purely to add a hypnotic quality and break up the loneliness of a bare lead vocal — an atmospheric texture role, distinct from the ad-lib flourishes above.** Positioned specifically in verse 1, where the lead is otherwise deliberately unaccompanied. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **Ear-candy placement is instinctive and fast, not deliberated — a stated workflow habit: "it's good to just kind of think really fast, or not think at all, and just see what happens."** Presented as the actual process behind the scattered ad-libs/flourishes rather than a planned map of where they'd land. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]
- **A fixed 8-bar verse + 4-bar pre-chorus structure, with a stated rule that the pre-chorus needs a line that "really bites" right before the chorus lands.** The lyric example given (the artist's real trajectory of being flown to L.A. by excited industry people, then hearing nothing after a round of writing sessions back in New York) is framed as the kind of specific, true detail that makes a pre-chorus line land instead of reading generic. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **On chorus 2 specifically, add one or two small flourishes so a structurally repeated section doesn't read as a static loop, even though musically it may literally be one.** Two concrete moves used here: (1) a second synth, described below, introduced only at this point in the arrangement; (2) other small variation elements "scattered throughout." The explicit reasoning: some listeners are waiting specifically for chorus 2 to hear a little something new. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **A chorus-2-only synth built by pitching a single sound into chords until it sounds like it's "falling apart" — a deliberately degraded texture rather than a clean pad, described as landing somewhere between accordion and a lo-fi phone recording without that being the intent.** Built the same way as an earlier synth in the song (one core sound, pitched to form chords) but pushed further into degradation for a distinct chorus-2 identity when blended against the first synth. → [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]]
- **UAD Ampex (Warmth preset) and UAD Studer A800 (Vintage preset) stacked on a synth, with the units' hum and hiss controls deliberately left ON — a beginner accident kept on purpose because it added useful "fuzz and buzz."** "Around this time I was still getting to know the UAD plug-ins, so I still have all the hum and the hiss turned on. I didn't really quite know how to use them that great — maybe now I kind of almost always turn off the hum and the hiss, but seeing the noise turned on for this synth, to me makes a lot of sense, and I'm happy I didn't turn it off." A candid, self-aware case for keeping "wrong" settings when the result serves the sound. Unowned units — see [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Vintage Gear Worklist]]
- **A song's lyric drawn directly and specifically from the artist's real circumstances at the time of writing (working as a waitress, fake-gold hoop earrings, thrifted ripped jeans with a friend's patch detail) — offered as the actual reason a "struggle" song resonates, versus fictional flexing.** "If you really believe the words you are saying, and they are really true to your life, I think the listener is always very smart — knows when something is coming from a real place." A songwriting-process note, not a mix technique, but the stated root of why the vocal delivery reads as convincing. → [[Melody Writing]]

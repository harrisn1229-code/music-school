# Hands-On FM Synthesis w/ TimSund & Stephan Schmitt

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uoiqy5mkUKE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uoiqy5mkUKE)

## Synopsis

A companion piece to a separate "Demystifying FM" video, at nonlinear labs (Berlin) — performer Tim Sund walks through the FM-based C15 hardware synth's patches with designer Stephan Schmidt. Light on numeric FM settings (this instrument's sound design is macro/patch-based, not raw operator ratios), but rich in performance-oriented sound-design ideas: split-channel effects, a chord-tuned sympathetic-resonance patch, and a long internal recorder used as a performance tool.

## Techniques

- **Two different MONO effects, one per channel, instead of one stereo effect, for width.** On a split-note patch, a phase shifter runs on the left channel only, a flanger on the right only, with some dry signal brought up the center — the deliberate mismatch between channels is what builds width and complexity, more than a matched stereo effect on both sides would.
- **Sympathetic-resonance patch: a short percussive trigger on one side feeding a resonator bank tuned to specific chord tones on the other.** Playing the percussive trigger note alone produces silence from the resonator side (nothing to "kick it off"); playing it together with notes that match the resonator's tuned pitches makes those specific tones ring out, while non-matching notes stay comparatively quiet — the same acoustic principle as a piano's una corda pedal or a snare ringing sympathetically when a nearby drum is hit. Effectively lets a player "harmonize" whatever chord they tune the resonator to, live.
- **A comb-filtered signal fed back into a very slow (~1 Hz) FM operator as its carrier** produces an "otherworldly" slow undulation distinct from ordinary tremolo — the modulator is the source guitar-like sound itself, not a separate LFO; described by the demonstrator as "kind of tremolo but not."
- **Keep expressive parameters continuous, mapped to a ribbon or pressure controller, rather than switched on/off** — sustain-style and tremolo-depth parameters were both driven as continuous, gradually-turned values via the instrument's ribbon controller, explicitly contrasted against a simple on/off switch, for a more natural performance feel.
- **A long internal audio recorder (hours of buffer) that can recall and loop back ANY earlier moment of a performance, including changed settings** — used live as a "duet with yourself": the performer plays a phrase, the recorded phrase gets triggered back at a musically-timed moment, and the performer plays against their own earlier material in real time. Framed as effectively an extremely long delay line when used loosely, or a precise call-and-response tool when timed carefully. Worth knowing this class of feature exists (a buffer well beyond a normal looper's length, with settings-recall) when evaluating performance-oriented hardware.

# Good Day Sunshine — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Pollack's headline finding is that **the refrain's famous meter change isn't there** — the song is solid 4/4 until the very end, and what feels like a metric shift is a very slow **3 + 3 + 2 syncopation** spread across eight beats ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]). Take a familiar fast accent cell and stretch it over two slow bars and it stops sounding like syncopation and starts sounding like a meter change, for free.

**Form.** Folk-ballad alternation of refrain and verse, pulled toward pop by an intro, outro and semi-instrumental middle verse. The **4-bar intro is really just an open fifth tapped mechanically four to the bar** — its job is to lock the listener into the pulse before the refrain destabilises it, because disorientation only reads if there's a grid to lose. The refrain is 6 bars, open at *both* ends yet still converging on home; the verse is 8 squared-off bars, closed. The second verse's back half modulates and hands those four bars to a piano solo — a guitar solo in that slot is normal, a modulation plus a half-length solo is not. The one genuine metric break is a single **three-beat bar** near the end.

**Harmony.** Four chords total, every one major and adjacent on the cycle of fifths, so two are chromatic at home; the chain is stacked secondary dominants ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). Note the direction — from V-of-V it moves *backward* to that chord's own dominant rather than forward toward the home V, which is approach avoidance, and until the verse arrives the key reads as the wrong one. The break is a textbook pivot ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]), and the outro bumps the harmony **up a half step** while the vocals refract into cascading echoes ([[POP-C11 — Chromatic mediants and pop modulation]]) — a golden-age cliché the Beatles use only three times, all McCartney songs.

**Arrangement.** Piano, drums and handclaps carry the track with almost no guitar. A snare triplet punctuates two bars of every refrain — **but only from the second refrain onward** — and handclaps arrive only in the third verse. Introducing a background figure on the second pass registers as development instead of furniture.

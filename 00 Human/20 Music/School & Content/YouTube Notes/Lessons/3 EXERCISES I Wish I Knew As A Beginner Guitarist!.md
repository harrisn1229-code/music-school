# 3 EXERCISES I Wish I Knew As A Beginner Guitarist!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I3zfG0Zd3l8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I3zfG0Zd3l8)

## Synopsis

Three exercises framed as a "fix my own early mistakes" retrospective: a mid-tempo wrist-driven warmup, a harder string-skipping arpeggio riff, and a funk/rhythm strumming pattern practiced with the picking-hand wrist floating off the bridge rather than anchored on it — each targeting a fundamental the presenter says he skipped in favor of flashier shred material early on.

## Techniques

1. **Mid-tempo (~120 BPM) wrist-driven warmup combining alternate picking, string skipping, and fretting-hand muting.** Deliberately impossible to play with a stiff full-arm/elbow motion, which is the point — it forces wrist looseness by design. Named as the most commonly neglected tempo zone: many players have shred/sweep basics down but struggle to play simple material tight and controlled around 120 BPM, because practice time skews toward either very slow (learning) or very fast (pushing limits), leaving the middle unpracticed despite it being the range most real playing actually happens in.
2. **A harder arpeggio riff adding more aggressive string skipping/transitioning.** Same "can't be played with an elbow/full-arm motion" design principle, one level up in difficulty from exercise 1. Fretting-hand note: economy of motion (fingers close to the fretboard) is maintained, but fingers are deliberately curled *less* than usual specifically to let them lightly touch and mute adjacent strings — a targeted exception to the general "curl for clean separation" rule, used here because string-skipping arpeggios need active muting of strings being skipped over, not just clean articulation of the ones being played.
3. **Funk/rhythm strumming pattern practiced with the picking-hand wrist floating off the bridge, not resting on it.** Contrasts directly with the bridge-anchored resting position used for standard rock/metal picking (palm-muting access) — for chordal rhythm/strumming patterns specifically, an un-anchored, floating wrist unlocks a more natural motion. Personal anecdote: despite years of shred-focused practice, basic funk patterns and steady acoustic strumming were surprisingly difficult until deliberately practicing with this un-anchored hand position. Framed as a general wrist-unlocking exercise useful regardless of interest in funk specifically, not a funk-only skill.

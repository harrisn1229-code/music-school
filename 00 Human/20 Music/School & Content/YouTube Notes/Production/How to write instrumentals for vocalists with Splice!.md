# How to write instrumentals for vocalists with Splice!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NnIgCEn3f08)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NnIgCEn3f08)

## Synopsis

A remix-contest tutorial that's really a masterclass in Ableton's audio-to-MIDI and stem-arrangement workflow. Underbelly imports a full stem pack, uses a Splice acapella as an arrangement placeholder (warping it to tempo and — genuinely useful — re-keying it via the relative major/minor relationship rather than guessing an interval), builds a verse/pre-chorus/chorus structure by ear from the placeholder vocal's own phrasing, then constructs an 808 bassline almost entirely from Ableton's **Convert Drums to New MIDI Track** and **Convert Harmony to New MIDI Track** features — extracting the kick's rhythm and the chords' root notes as starting MIDI rather than hand-programming from scratch. Closes with a filter "pump" keyed from the kick instead of the chords it's filtering, and a reversed-chord pickup for a smoother section transition.

## Techniques

- **Stem-import checklist: Auto-Warp Long Samples off first, Cmd/Ctrl-drag for separate tracks, headroom trim** — before dragging a stem folder in, disable **Auto-Warp Long Samples** in Live's Record/Warp/Launch preferences; select all stem files and drag them in holding Cmd/Ctrl so each lands on its own track; match the session's master BPM to the stems; then select all and trim down (~−12 dB) for headroom before building anything on top.
- **Re-key a found acapella via its relative major/minor, then Complex Pro for the shift** — a vocal in A major dropped into a C-minor song: A major's relative minor is F# minor (major key → down 3 semitones); since a key and its relative share every note, the vocal already works over F# minor, and now it's simple semitone counting up to the actual target (F# → C = 6 semitones). Switch the warp algorithm to Complex Pro for a cleaner result at that large a shift. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Build a verse/pre-chorus/chorus structure by ear from your own placeholder vocal, not an external reference** — listen for where the vocal sits low/conversational (verse), climbs toward its range's top (pre-chorus), and peaks (chorus), placing Arrangement View locators at each shift — the same locator-blueprint method used with a reference track, but driven by a developing vocal of your own. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Trim an odd 10-bar chord loop down to a standard 8 bars** before building a section around it. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Convert Drums to New MIDI Track, to give an 808 the kick's exact rhythm** — right-click a drum stem, extract its rhythm as MIDI, strip unneeded hi-hat/snare lanes, max out kick velocities, then paste that pattern onto a Simpler-loaded 808 track instead of hand-programming the bass rhythm from scratch. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Legato to close gaps + octave-shift out of "wet fart" territory + envelope shaping for 808 punch** — extend short converted-MIDI notes edge-to-edge with Legato, shift the whole pattern up an octave or two if it lands inaudibly low, then pull Sustain down / push Decay and Attack up slightly for a punchier hit that leaves room for the kick. → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Kick Design]]
- **Convert Harmony to New MIDI Track purely to read off chord ROOT notes, then hand-match the 808 to them** — the harmony-extraction feature doesn't need to be accurate as a full chord transcription; only the lowest note of each detected chord (the root) matters — paste the extracted MIDI as a deactivated reference layer on the 808 track and nudge the actual bass notes to follow its contour by eye/ear. → [[Bassline Writing]], [[Chord]], [[Scale]]
- **Filter "pump" keyed from a DIFFERENT element than the one being filtered** — Auto Filter's envelope on a chord stab, sidechained from the KICK's MIDI/audio rather than the chords' own transients, so the filter opens/closes in sync with the kick instead of the chord hits it's actually processing — a "wah pedal" mental model applied cross-element. → [[Auto Filter]], [[Sidechaining]]
- **A reversed single chord as a pickup/lead-in into the next section** — isolate just the first chord of the upcoming section, reverse it, stretch/fade it into the downbeat; temporarily disable sidechain ducking and open the filter's high end for that one transition moment so the reversed swell reads clearly. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]]

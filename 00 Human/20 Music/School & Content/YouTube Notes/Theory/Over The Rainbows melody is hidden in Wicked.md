# Over The Rainbow's melody is hidden in Wicked!

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rnPBkMCHxSI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rnPBkMCHxSI)

## Synopsis

*Wicked*'s recurring "Unlimited" theme secretly quotes the first seven notes of "Somewhere Over the Rainbow" — a deliberate homage the composer has confirmed publicly, deliberately disguised via rhythm and harmony changes and deliberately capped at seven notes for a stated legal reason.

## Techniques

- **The "eighth note" copyright rule, stated directly by the composer as his own reasoning.** Steven Schwartz has explained he used exactly the first SEVEN notes of "Over the Rainbow" and no more, specifically because "when you get to the eighth note in a melody, people can come and say you stole our tune" — a concrete, composer-stated practical threshold for how much of an existing melody can be quoted before it crosses into infringement risk, distinct from the general "melody is the load-bearing feature" guidance elsewhere.
- **Disguising a direct quote via rhythm AND harmony change simultaneously.** The same seven pitches are re-rhythmized entirely differently from the original, AND moved from "Over the Rainbow"'s conventional I-vi-... progression onto a single static suspended chord instead of a moving progression — specifically an A major9(♯11) (the IV chord in the key of E, voiced as a Lydian chord) rather than any tonic-functioning harmony, for a magical/ethereal/slightly unsettled Lydian color that reads as completely different in feel from the source even though the pitches are identical.

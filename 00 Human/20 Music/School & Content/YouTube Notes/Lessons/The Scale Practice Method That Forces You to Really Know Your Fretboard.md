## What's taught

- **The core drill: circle-of-fourths, one-position, all-12-keys scale mastery check.** Pick one scale or mode, stay in roughly one fretboard position/area, and play it root-to-root or low-to-high-to-low through **all 12 keys back to back in the circle of fourths**, in one unbroken take, with **zero mistakes** — that single clean pass is what lets him check the scale off in his tracking spreadsheet. (~1:30–3:00, ~7:30–10:30)
- **Two organizing axes for scale practice, named explicitly:** (1) all five scale-forms/positions off the *same root* (stay on one key, move the shape around the neck), or (2) all 12 *roots* inside the *same position* (stay in one area, cycle the key) — this video drills the second axis; he alternates between both approaches across his overall practice but demos only the same-position/all-keys version here. (~1:45–2:20)
- **Parent scale vs. mode changes the phrasing rule.** For a **parent scale** (e.g. harmonic minor), just play all the notes available in the position, any starting point. For a **mode** (e.g. Lydian, derived from a parent scale), play strictly **root-to-root** — starting and ending the run on the mode's own root note — because that's the only way the modal color is actually audible; playing the same notes from an arbitrary starting point just sounds like the parent scale. (~2:35–2:55, ~8:45–9:00)
- **Full rule set for the drill, stated explicitly before demoing (his own words, "the rules"):**
  1. Subdivision: **16th notes** for a parent-scale pass; **8th notes** for a root-to-root mode pass (doubled, because the root-to-root method needs more pauses/restarts between keys, so the faster subdivision keeps the overall pace reasonable).
  2. **Metronome click = quarter notes, placed on the offbeat** (not the downbeat).
  3. **Start each new key on the next available beat** — not a fixed wait; the gap before the next key starts depends on exactly where the previous key's scale run ended.
  4. **Range: lowest note to highest note and back**, within the position, using his recommended **"inchworm" fingering** (the fingering scheme from his Scales PDF pack, see below).
  5. **Sequence: the full circle of fourths**, all 12 keys, staying in the same position/area throughout (small shifts allowed as the shape moves, same spirit as [[One-Position Fretboard Overview Drill]]'s "don't be rigid about a strict box").
  6. **Pass/fail gate: one full clean take through all 12 keys, zero mistakes, counts** — he doesn't require multiple clean reps, reasoning that getting it right once *after* many failed attempts already means each individual scale form got repeated 2–3+ times along the way, which is real practice in itself. (~7:45–8:10)
- **Tempo-logs-as-progress, never redo at the same tempo.** He records the BPM he cleared the drill at directly in the tracking spreadsheet. Once a scale/mode is checked off at a tempo, the rule is: **don't repeat it at that same tempo** — next time revisit it, it has to be at a higher tempo (or move on to a new scale/mode entirely). Explicitly framed as what separates "real practicing" from comfort-zone repetition: "I'm not going to redo... I'm going to move on... or make it more challenging next time and not repeat myself." (~6:50–7:10)
- **Live worked example 1 — harmonic minor (parent scale).** 90 BPM, 16th notes, one position. Took **over 20 attempts** to land one clean pass through all 12 keys. Confirms the pass/fail gate above in practice — a clean take does not mean an easy or fast process. (~7:10–7:50)
- **Live worked example 2 — Lydian (mode), on D♭ specifically named as his starting point.** Root-to-root phrasing, **8th notes at 180 BPM** (doubled from a 90 BPM quarter-note click, per rule 1 above) because of the extra pause/restart overhead between key changes in root-to-root mode. (~1:00–1:20 setup, ~8:30–10:30 execution)
- **The spreadsheet system itself.** A "practice home base" screen with a **Vocabulary category** listing every parent scale and every mode as individually checkable items (major scale and major pentatonic already checked off at the point of filming) — the drill above is literally what earns a checkmark. Not a generic to-do list; each item's checkmark is gated by the one-clean-take-through-all-12-keys rule.
- **Why bother with pure scale drilling at all (his own framing, stated directly to camera):** this vocabulary work isn't the end goal — it's what lets him "be ready to use those scales improvisationally" and "understand the fretboard really well" for real improvisation, songwriting, and composing, and — just as important to him — it tests actual technical execution under time pressure, not just conceptual familiarity. He states his next practice-log entry will apply this same focused, milestone-gated method to an actual piece of repertoire rather than pure scales. (~9:00–9:40)
- **Everything is built on his 3-fret-position major-scale system.** He bases every other scale off major-scale fingerings playable inside **any 3-fret position**, and points to his separate "Simplifying Jazz Guitar" series (not yet in the vault) for the full walkthrough of that underlying system.
- **Companion resource — Scales PDF pack (recently updated).** soundguitarlessons.com/scales (link in his video description). Contains: fingerings for every parent-scale position (including the "inchworm" fingering used in this drill), a version with chord-tone scale-degree numbers marked, and a bonus page listing every mode derivable from each parent scale.

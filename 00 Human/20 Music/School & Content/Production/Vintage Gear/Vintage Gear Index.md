# Vintage Gear Index

Reference library on the iconic vintage/analog (and early-digital-classic) **sound sources behind the sounds** — what each unit is, *what makes the sound* (method + circuitry + why), the digital versions to grab (cross-linked to owned [[Plugin Index|plugins]]), and what music it defined. Distinct from `Production/Gear/` (owned hardware). Reverse lookup ("what drum machines were on 80s MJ records") lives in [[Gear by Era & Genre]]; the build queue is [[Vintage Gear Worklist]].

**Progress: 40 / 149 documented** (batch 3, 2026-07-15 — **drum machines complete** (27/27); Roland + Sequential synths done, DX7/CS-80/Oberheim/ARP/Korg/Moog next). ## Drum Machines
- [[Roland TR-808]] — analog voices, self-oscillating sine kick; the bass instrument of hip hop, trap, and modern pop
- [[Roland TR-909]] — hybrid analog voices + 6-bit sampled hats; the kick/clap/open-hat of house and techno
- [[LinnDrum]] — 8-bit real-drum samples + Linn swing; the mid-80s pop kit and synthwave's snare
- [[Linn LM-1]] — first sampled drum machine, invented quantize/swing; the Prince drum sound
- [[E-mu SP-1200]] — 12-bit + analog SSM filters + the 33/45 trick; golden-era boom-bap texture
- [[Akai MPC60]] — 12-bit pads-and-swing sampler-sequencer; the groove instrument of 90s hip hop
- [[Oberheim DMX]] — 8-bit slab-hitting sampled kit; early hip hop and "Blue Monday"
- [[Roland CR-78]] — wood-panel analog preset box; the intimate tick of "In the Air Tonight" and "Heart of Glass"
- [[Roland TR-707]] — companded PCM kit + the best TR sequencer; drove Phuture's "Acid Tracks" beside the 303
- [[Roland TR-606]] — the 303's pocket-analog companion; thin clicky drums, cult sizzle hats
- [[Roland TR-505]] — the $300 bedroom TR; papery PCM kit + latin voices, lo-fi indie cult box
- [[Roland TR-626]] — last classic TR; tunable cleaner PCM, dancehall/digital-reggae workhorse
- [[Oberheim DX]] — the DMX's cheap hackable sibling; swappable EPROMs = proto-user-sampling
- [[E-mu SP-12]] — 12-bit user sampling in a drum machine; the bridge to the SP-1200
- [[E-mu Drumulator]] — the $995 people's Linn; 8-bit ROM kit that funded E-mu's future
- [[Akai MPC3000]] — 16-bit MPC perfected; J Dilla's machine (literally, it's in the Smithsonian)
- [[Akai MPC2000]] — the democratized MPC; the 2000XL time-stretch crunch and 2000s beat culture
- [[Simmons SDS-V]] — analog drum *synthesizer* on hex pads; the 80s electronic tom incarnate
- [[Roland R-8]] — 16-bit "Human Rhythm Composer"; feel engine + the Dance card's 808/909 reprints
- [[Yamaha RX5]] — deep-editing 12-bit flagship; the pitch-mangled tom/kick machine
- [[Yamaha RY30]] — AWM2 sample-synthesis workhorse of early-90s demos
- [[Korg Mini Pops]] — 60s preset charm; Jarre's *Oxygène* rhythm and Disclosure's delicate percussion
- [[Maestro Rhythm King]] — Sly's "Funk Box"; the first #1 hit with a drum machine
- [[Alesis HR-16]] — $449 16-bit box that fed bedroom house/techno/industrial
- [[Alesis SR-16]] — best-selling drum machine ever, still in production; ambience-baked kits
- [[Sequential Circuits DrumTraks]] — per-step tune/level programming from the Prophet company
- [[Casio RZ-1]] — toy-store sampling (0.8 s, 8-bit); the lo-fi crunch democratizer

## Synths
- [[Minimoog Model D]] — 3 drifting VCOs into the ladder filter; the definition of synth bass and lead
- [[Roland Juno-106]] — one DCO + *that* BBD chorus; house stabs, synthwave beds, bedroom-pop pads
- [[Roland Juno-6 - Juno-60]] — the wilder parents: sweeter filter, snappier envelopes, the arpeggiator
- [[Roland Jupiter-8]] — Roland's 2-VCO flagship; the "Hungry Like the Wolf" luxury arp
- [[Roland Jupiter-4]] — Roland's drifty first poly; the cult ensemble button
- [[Roland SH-101]] — one fat VCO + sequencer; the bassline machine of 90s dance
- [[Roland TB-303]] — diode-ladder squelch + accent/slide; acid house incarnate
- [[Roland JX-3P - JX-8P]] — hidden 2-DCO lushness; mid-80s ballad beds, synthwave sleeper
- [[Roland D-50]] — LA synthesis: sampled attacks + synth sustains; the late-80s digital sheen
- [[Roland JD-800]] — 59 sliders of digital; the rave/trance piano ("Children")
- [[Sequential Prophet-5]] — the first programmable poly; Poly-Mod growls, mix-ready warmth
- [[Sequential Prophet-600]] — the P5's hazy sibling; received the first MIDI message ever
- [[Sequential Prophet VS]] — vector joystick morphing into Curtis filters; Wavestation's parent

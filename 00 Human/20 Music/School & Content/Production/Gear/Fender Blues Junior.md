# Fender Blues Junior

**Category:** Tube Amp (15W, 1×12)
**Vendor:** Fender
**Ballpark value (used, 2026):** $400–600 (mods may add — document them)

> [!todo] Modified — document what the mods are (speaker swap? bias/cap job? tube change?) so the manual/parameters section can account for them.

## What it is

Fender's 15-watt EL84 tube combo — spring reverb, "Fat" switch, the small-venue/home-studio classic that breaks up at recordable volumes.

## What it's used for

Clean-to-edge-of-breakup Fender tone for the [[Fender Stratocaster]] and [[Hofner B-Bass H-Series]]; the pedal platform for the board.

## Signal chain notes

Mic'd with the [[Shure SM57]] (dust-cap edge, ~1" off grille); Master low + Volume high = earlier tube grit at bedroom levels.

🎬 *From video:* a third source confirms the small-clean-Fender-as-pedal-platform lane this amp occupies — "all these old Fender amps have a great balanced, clean tone and are a really nice starting place for adding pedals," demoed on a vintage Princeton Reverb (volume ≈3–4) for studio work and a bigger Pro Reverb for more headroom live with a louder drummer. New, reusable dial-in tip for this amp's own pedal platform: **amp volume changes the whole downstream pedal calculation** — at a quiet studio volume, boost/overdrive pedal settings can be more assertive; pushed toward the live end of this amp's own Volume 5–7 "edge of breakup" range (see ① below), the amp's own tubes/speaker are already compressing on their own, so pedal-side volume/boost settings need to come back down from wherever they sat at bedroom volume. Re-tune the pedalboard any time this amp's own Volume/Master setting changes, rather than assuming one pedal dial-in transfers across a quiet mix session and a loud live set. → [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track|Electric Guitar Amp Track]] — Jack Ruch, [Dial In Your Amps and Pedals for Great Clean and Overdriven TONES!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=45s77xBLZBU)

## Manual

- 📄 [[00 Human/20 Music/School & Content/Production/Manuals/Fender Blues Junior.pdf|Local PDF — Blues Junior IV Owner's Manual]] · [Official manual](https://www.fmicassets.com/Damroot/Original/10001/2231500_gamp_manual.pdf)

**High-level points** (the mental model, not a feature list):
- VOLUME is preamp gain, not output level — raising it pushes the 12AX7 preamp tubes into distortion/sustain; MASTER is the true output-level control, set *after* VOLUME has dialed in the desired amount of preamp breakup. This VOLUME→grit / MASTER→loudness split is the whole "recordable tube crunch at bedroom volume" trick this amp is known for (matches the note's own house convention: Master low + Volume high = earlier grit at low SPL).
- FAT is a preamp gain boost switch (extra distortion/sustain), footswitchable — plugging in a footswitch disables the panel FAT button and hands control to the pedal instead, they don't work simultaneously.
- TREBLE/BASS/MIDDLE are simple passive tone-stack controls shaping overall character, not surgical EQ — think broad tonal tilt, not per-band correction.
- REVERB is a real spring tank (physical spring reverb), not digital — expect the characteristic "boing"/splash on hard transients (footswitch taps, palm-mute hits) that solid-state or plugin spring emulations often miss.
- Output stage is a genuine EL84 tube pair (class AB, ~15W RMS) into a single 12" Celestion A-Type speaker — the breakup character comes from real tube compression, which is why this amp mic's well even at modest volumes for recording (per the note's own convention: SM57 at the dust-cap edge, ~1" off the grille).
- An 8Ω load must always be connected to the speaker jack whenever the amp is powered on — running it with no speaker/cab attached risks output-transformer damage.

## Alternatives / Comparisons

- **[[Mara S10V]] ($799, single 6L6)** — a US-built boutique amp pitched directly at Blues Junior buyers: 6L6 (American clean-to-breakup) vs. the Blues Junior's EL84 pair (British voice, earlier breakup); Canadian Hammond transformers vs. stock imports; solid pine cab vs. the Blues Junior's particle board; hand-wired USA build at the same street price — sales-pitch framing (affiliate link, no real A/B), worth a look if ever shopping a Blues Junior replacement/second amp, but not a verified tone upgrade. — Daniel Seriff, [Don't Buy A Blues Junior Before Watching This Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VXbj448lntQ)

## Key Parameters

| # | Parameter | What it is / does | Technique | Artist / record example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ① | Volume | Preamp gain — the primary distortion/sustain control | Push toward 5-7 for edge-of-breakup rhythm tone that still cleans up with the guitar's own volume knob; this is the [[Saturation and Distortion]] source in the [[2 Guitar Group|Guitars]] chain, feeding [[MIDS Slot]] / [[LEAD Slot]] | Classic "amp on the edge, guitar volume rides the rest" blues/rock technique |
| ② | FAT switch | Boosts preamp gain further for extra distortion/sustain; footswitchable, panel button disabled when a footswitch is connected | Use for a lead-tone gain bump without touching Volume/Master — quick A/B rhythm-to-lead switch | — |
| ③ | Master | Overall output level, set after Volume has dialed in the desired grit | Low Master + high Volume = full tube compression/breakup at low real-world SPL — the standard home-studio move for this amp (already the note's house rule) | — |
| ④ | Treble / Bass / Middle | Passive tone stack — broad tonal shaping | Middle scoop for a more "scooped" modern rock voicing; Middle boosted keeps the vintage Fender midrange bark that cuts through a mix without much EQ later | — |
| ⑤ | Reverb | Real spring-tank reverb depth | Light amounts add natural room air to a dry DI'd/mic'd guitar; heavy amounts get surf/spring "boing" — a texture the [[Reverb]] plugin page's spring-tank section references as the analog reference point | Surf-rock spring reverb (Dick Dale-style) at high settings |

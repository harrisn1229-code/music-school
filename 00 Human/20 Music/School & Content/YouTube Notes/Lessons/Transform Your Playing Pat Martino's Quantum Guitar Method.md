# Transform Your Playing: Pat Martino's Quantum Guitar Method

## Synopsis
Nathan Borton distills one method from Pat Martino's instructional video *Quantum Guitar*: a system for stacking multiple substitute sounds over a single static chord. The video is built around two layers — a **"dimensional plane"** counting framework (one sound over a chord, up through four-or-more sounds over the same chord) and a pair of **"parental forms"** (symmetric source shapes — a diminished chord and an augmented triad) that each generate a whole family of substitute chords by altering one note of the shape at a time. Martino explicitly ties the diminished form to Barry Harris's method (lowering a note of a diminished chord to reveal an inverted dominant 7th), and the video's real payoff is showing that the diminished and augmented forms can be **combined into one hybrid shape** — worked from a G-diminished-derived voicing that simultaneously reads as an A7♯5 chord and an A-augmented triad, which means a single memorized line inherits both the diminished form's minor-third transposability (working over four different dominant-♯5 chords) *and* the augmented form's major-third-related triad family (an extra color layer at each of those four targets). Most valuable single idea: pick one substitute from each form, get those two sounds solid in small fragments before trying to stack more, and use the guitar-shape mnemonic (the chord's top note = the augmented-form triad's top note) to find the second sound fast rather than deriving it from scratch. The video is explicitly a condensed teaser for a longer etude/PDF/Guitar Pro breakdown gated on the creator's Patreon — the transcript itself carries the full conceptual framework but not tab-level fingerings for the closing 12-bar-blues etude.

## What's taught
- **The dimensional-plane framework: counting how many distinct substitute sounds are stacked over one static chord**, worked entirely over A7. 1D = just the A7 sound (arpeggio or A7 language). 2D = add the **ii-over-V** substitution, **E minor 7 over A7** (arpeggio or E-minor language layered against the A7). 3D = add the **tritone substitute, E♭7 over A7** (arpeggio or E♭ language), plus optionally that E♭7's own ii chord, **B♭ minor**. 4D+ = four or more distinct sounds stacked at once, generated systematically via the parental forms rather than picked ad hoc. → [[Pat Martino's Dimensional Planes and Parental Forms]], [[Chord Substitution]]
- **The diminished parental form — Barry-Harris-adjacent: lower one note of a diminished 7th chord at a time to reveal four different dominant 7th chords.** Worked from **G diminished** (G-B♭-D♭-E): lowering the top note (E) a half step gives an **E♭ dominant 7 chord, inverted with the ♭7 in the bass**. Repeating on each of the four notes in turn yields **four different dominant 7 chords**, each a minor third apart, all usable as substitutes over the same target. **Each of those four can also be reinterpreted as a minor chord instead of dominant**, doubling the family to eight usable substitute sounds from one diminished shape. → [[Diminished Chords]], [[Pat Martino's Dimensional Planes and Parental Forms]]
- **The augmented parental form: raise or lower one note of an augmented triad at a time to generate minor or major triad families respectively.** Lowering each note of an augmented triad in turn produces a family of **major** triads; raising each note in turn produces a family of **minor** triads; playing them together creates the composite "augmented sound." Named as less elaborated in the source video than the diminished form, but the mechanism mirrors it directly (one-note-at-a-time alteration of a symmetric shape). → [[Augmented Chords]], [[Pat Martino's Dimensional Planes and Parental Forms]]
- **Combining both forms into one hybrid shape: lower the top note (diminished-form move) AND raise the next note down (augmented-form move) on the same G-diminished-derived voicing, producing an A triad over a G bass — the same sound as an A7♯5 chord.** Because the shape descends from the diminished form, it transposes in minor thirds across **A7♯5, C7♯5, E♭7♯5, and G♭7♯5** (plus their minor-chord reinterpretations); because it's *also* an augmented triad with a bass note, each of those four targets additionally carries its own augmented-derived triad for extra color — **F major** at A7♯5, **A♭ major** (or D♭ minor) at C7♯5, **D triad** (or B♭ minor) at G♭7♯5. → [[Pat Martino's Dimensional Planes and Parental Forms]]
- **Worked bebop line demonstrating the hybrid shape's payoff: a line outlining an E minor 7 sound, played over A7♯5, reused verbatim (diminished-form transposition) over G minor, B♭ minor, and D♭ minor, then layered with an F major triad (the augmented-form color at that same A7♯5 target) for a combined sound.** One memorized phrase, four harmonically-related targets, plus an extra color layer — the concrete demonstration of the "4-dimensional-plane-and-beyond" payoff. → [[Pat Martino's Dimensional Planes and Parental Forms]]
- **Practice recommendation: start narrow — pick just one sound from the diminished form and one from the augmented form and get comfortable with only those two before stacking further.** Concrete suggested pairing: **B♭ minor 7 over A7** (diminished form, "easy to think about and sounds great") plus **F major triad over A7** (augmented form). → [[Pat Martino's Dimensional Planes and Parental Forms]]
- **Guitar-shape mnemonic for finding the augmented-form triad fast: on an A7♯5 chord grip, the top note is the chord's ♯5 (F) — the corresponding augmented-form major triad is simply built with that same note (F) as its top voice**, letting the substitute be located directly from a familiar chord shape rather than re-derived from theory each time. → [[Pat Martino's Dimensional Planes and Parental Forms]]
- **Practice method stated directly: work the stacked sounds in small fragments, then return for more, rather than trying to run the full combined stack at once.** → [[Pat Martino's Dimensional Planes and Parental Forms]]
- **The method is stated to generalize past dominant chords — "not only over dominant chords but any type of chord"** — the video's worked examples are all dominant/♯5 because that's what Martino's *Quantum Guitar* source material covers, but the generative diminished/augmented process is framed as portable to any chord quality. → [[Pat Martino's Dimensional Planes and Parental Forms]]

# Bass and Guitar sound with Tom Lord-Alge

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4fmeR2Yg5sc)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4fmeR2Yg5sc)

## Synopsis

A short Mix with the Masters screen-share of Tom Lord-Alge walking through the bass and guitar tracks of a full analog-recorded band mix, comparing a "modern" revisit against his own original mix of the same song. The throughline is **restraint as the headline skill** — he repeats "know when to EQ and when to leave it alone" multiple times, and several guitar tracks get little or no processing at all because the source (a real amp, mic'd, on an analog recording) already sounds right. Where he does dig in, the moves are small and purposeful: a top-end shelf on a slightly hollow bass DI, a deliberate deviation from the original mix by adding parallel amp-sim distortion to the bass for a "nasty and gritty" modern take, a tight midrange bark-boost on the main guitars, and a specific high-pass-plus-top-end move to make the intro guitar sound thinner because that's what the original mix did. There's also a genuinely reusable session-organization habit (his "extra guitar" always lives on channel 16, always panned center, always more distorted than the main guitars — the song's "anchor") and a clean articulation of *why* arrangements build guitar layers in over the song's course: so the band has "somewhere to go" when it finally comes in. A small but concrete workflow habit threads the whole video: doing gain trims with the plugin's own input/output stage rather than the fader, specifically so the fader stays put as a visual reference. Transcript is real YouTube captions (not auto-generated ASR), reasonably clean, though speech is very filler-heavy ("you know," "again," "okay") and the caption pull cuts off mid-sentence at the very end of the video (bridge guitar section) — the last ~30–60 seconds of content are not captured.

## Techniques

**Bass:**
- **Distinguish DI vs. amp regions by waveform shape alone, before even soloing the tracks** — a DI take reads visually "pointier" than a mic'd amp take on the same instrument. A fast eyeballing habit for navigating a session with parallel DI/amp bass or guitar tracks. *(~0:00)*
- **Top-end shelf on a bass DI that reads slightly hollow** — described only as "some top end, some actually 450 shelf" (the number is filler-adjacent and not fully legible in the transcript — read with caution, likely a shelf somewhere in the low-mid-to-presence range rather than literally 450 Hz as a shelf corner). A/B'd bypassed vs. engaged to confirm it was doing real work before committing. → [[EQ]], [[BASS Slot]], [[1 Electric Bass DI Track]]
- **Deliberately deviate from the original mix: blend a parallel amp-sim distortion in with the clean bass DI for a "nasty and gritty" modern re-mix, using a personal saved preset as the starting point rather than dialing from scratch.** "So this is IK Multimedia's AmpliTube and I love it... I have a couple of my own presets that I have in my rig that I use, and one of them is for bass distortion — I use that preset as my starting point and then I dialed in the sound that I was looking for. So when you play the two sounds together, it's just nasty and gritty." Explicitly **not** on the original mix — a case of a remix/revisit taking a creative liberty the first pass didn't. → AmpliTube (unowned, see [[Plugin Wishlist]]), [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[Parallel Processing]], [[1 Electric Bass DI Track]]

**Guitars — overall philosophy:**
- **"Know when to EQ and when to leave it alone" — stated as the organizing principle for the entire guitar pass, repeated on three separate tracks.** Because the guitars were tracked to an analog recording, he says up front "there's no gouging of EQ" — most tracks get only a small top-end add or nothing at all; he specifically calls out the handful of tracks that DO carry real EQ as the exception, not the rule. → [[EQ]], [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track]]
- **Session-organization convention: the "extra guitar" always lives on channel 16, always panned dead center, and is always more distorted than the main guitars — his self-described favorite habit, functioning as the song's "anchor."** On this song it plays the intro and a chunking part in the bridge, and it's also the source of a small whistle-noise texture at the start. Demonstrated with an in/out A/B against the two main guitars: without it the intro sounds thin, with it the three guitars together sound "pretty awesome." → [[2 Guitar Group|Guitar Group]], [[Layering and Stacking]]
- **Gain trims done with the plugin's own input/output control, not the fader — specifically so the fader position stays a reliable visual reference ("in case I bump it, I know where it is").** A small but repeatable session-hygiene habit distinct from the usual "unity gain in/out of every device" rule — this is about *which control* handles a trim, not the level itself. → [[Gain Staging]]

**Guitars — specific tracks:**
- **Derek's opening (intro) guitar: roll off boxiness/bottom, add a touch of top end as the baseline move — then, specifically for the very start of the song, high-pass filter + a boost around 6–7 kHz to make it deliberately thinner.** He references the *original* mix to justify this: "I did reference the original mix, I wanted to see what I did there... I felt it was just a little too chubby, so I made it a little bit thinner." Framed as an arrangement decision, not a taste fix: "because we're building as this track goes along, so that when the band does come in we have somewhere to go" — deliberately holding the intro guitar back so there's room to add fullness later. → [[EQ]], [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Main guitars: a tight, narrow +6 dB bump around 700 Hz specifically to make the tone "bark," plus a high-pass filter and a little top end — nothing more.** "I'm giving a 6 dB bump at 700, a little mid-range, knock it — a very tight curve, because I want it to kind of bark, I want that mid-range to bark." A/B'd the center "extra guitar" in and out against these two main guitars to show how the three read as one wall of sound together vs. thin without the center layer. → [[EQ]], [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track]], [[2 Guitar Group|Guitar Group]]
- **Intro lead/octave guitar: almost no EQ — "nothing crazy," described as mostly balancing/level cuts plus "nice compression over the whole thing."** Reiterates the "know when to leave it alone" line. → [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track]]
- **Octave guitars (enter at the chorus, doubling the main guitar an octave up): hard/"brick wall" compression plus deliberate bottom-end removal, because the layer read "a little too chubby" and needed "its own sound" distinct from the main guitars.** Bypassed A/B confirms the move is doing real, audible work (not a placebo tweak). States plainly that the compressor here is doing double duty — glue/character AND overall level placement: "the compression just leaves it at the one, and it also acts to reduce the level, put it where I want it." Also notes he *does* do real EQ work while soloed on this layer, but the final call is always made in-context: "we're not going to be listening to this in solo... we're going to balance it in the track so when I play it in the track it sounds exactly like it should." → [[Compression]], [[EQ]], [[2 Guitar Group|Guitar Group]], [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track]]
- **Chorus guitar: a straight double of the main guitar, only a touch of top end added.** Another "know when to leave it alone" beat. → [[1 Electric Guitar Amp Track]]
- **Bridge guitar (first part): zero EQ.** "This guitar just sounds great, so I didn't need to do anything to it other than gain it." The clearest single statement in the video that a good source needs no processing at all — the fader/gain-only move is treated as a legitimate finished chain, not a placeholder. → [[EQ]], [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Bridge guitar (second part, an arpeggiation guitar entering halfway through the bridge): filter only — a high-pass, nothing else.** *(Note: the source caption pull cuts off mid-sentence right after this — no further detail on this track or anything past it is available.)*
- **Arrangement shape confirmed directly: the song runs on essentially two guitars for most of its length, with a third (second main) guitar entering at a later section, then a doubled pair plus a set of octave guitars added specifically at the choruses.** Matches the "somewhere to go" philosophy above — the guitar count itself is one of the build's main tools, not just processing intensity. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Layering and Stacking]]

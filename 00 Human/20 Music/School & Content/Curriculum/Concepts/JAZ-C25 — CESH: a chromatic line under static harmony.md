# JAZ-C25 — CESH: a chromatic line under static harmony

**Q.** What is CESH, and how does it differ from an approach note or an enclosure?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Chromatic embellishment of static harmony** — take one note that matters (a chord root, a guide tone) and walk it by half step across several beats or bars, while the harmony underneath stays still or moves more slowly.
>
> The difference is scale. An approach note or an enclosure targets **a single arrival on a single chord tone**. CESH is a **sustained chromatic line**, closer to a bass-line device borrowed into the melody than to a one-note ornament. The term names the chromatic line, not the chord.

## Why it matters

It is the direct answer to a vamp or a held chord going static — forward motion generated without changing the chord itself, which is exactly the standing problem in loop-based writing.

It pairs with the modal material from Lesson 6: when the harmony is deliberately refusing to move, this is one of the few devices that supplies motion without breaking that decision.

## Drill

Hold one chord for four bars and walk a single inner voice down chromatically underneath it. Then do the same over a two-bar vamp you already have and decide whether it needs any other change at all.

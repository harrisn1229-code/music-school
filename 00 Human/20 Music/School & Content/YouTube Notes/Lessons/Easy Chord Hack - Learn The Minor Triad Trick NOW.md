## Synopsis

Part 2 of a major/minor triad series (part 1, major triads, is not in the vault). Maps all A minor triads across every string group and all three inversions, then applies the knowledge in a real studio-arrangement scenario: rewriting a chord riff to sit higher on the neck for spacing reasons. BERNTH frames this explicitly as his #1 working-musician skill (three guitarists in his touring band means constant frequency-range conflicts).

## What's taught

- **Minor triad = major triad with one note changed.** Once you know the major triad shapes across the neck (part 1, not logged), minor triads are the same shapes with the major 3rd lowered to a minor 3rd — "you don't have to study major and minor triads as a divided thing."
- **Three inversions per string group, systematically:** root-3rd-5th (root position), 3rd-5th-root (1st inversion), 5th-root-3rd (2nd inversion) — mapped across all four string groups (low E-A-D, A-D-G, D-G-B, G-B-E), with the G-B string pair's voicings shaped differently due to the guitar's non-uniform (major 3rd, not perfect 4th) tuning interval there.
- **Practice habit: don't always start voicings with the root on the bottom.** Deliberately using inversions instead of only root-position triads is what makes chord riffs sound "suspenseful" and interesting rather than like stock open chords.
- **Real studio-arrangement use case, demonstrated end to end:** given a basic chord riff (Am-F-Dm-C-G-Am) that requires large fretboard jumps and clashes with other instruments in a low register, use "nearest triad, nearest inversion" logic — for each new chord, find its closest voicing to the previous chord's fretboard position (not a fixed shape) to minimize movement and reposition the whole riff higher on the neck. This is the direct practical payoff of memorizing triads by string group/inversion rather than by fixed chord name.
- **Diminished/augmented triads as a one-note extension of the same system** (lower the 5th for diminished, raise it for augmented) — named but not worked in this lesson; flagged as a follow-up topic.
- **Daily practice recommendation:** 10-15 minutes/day on triad shapes across string groups as the foundation for later chord extensions (7ths, 9ths, 11ths, 13ths) and arpeggios.

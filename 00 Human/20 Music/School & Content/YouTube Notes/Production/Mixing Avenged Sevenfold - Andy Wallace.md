# Mixing Avenged Sevenfold - Andy Wallace

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tVC8y-Sd8_M)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tVC8y-Sd8_M)

## Synopsis

A Mix with the Masters session walkthrough with Andy Wallace (Nirvana's *Nevermind*, Slayer, Rage Against the Machine) working an Avenged Sevenfold rock mix. Two threads run through the video. First, a kick-drum EQ diagnosis and fix: a kick that reads as "too loud and too quiet at the same time" is a symptom of a missing beater/attack transient rather than a low-end problem, fixed by adding presence in the 1–3 kHz range instead of touching the low end further. Second, and more central, is Wallace's overall mix philosophy for a dense rock arrangement, which he describes visually as an old-fashioned steam engine — powerful and heavy, but streamlined, with nothing sticking out. Applied concretely, that means matching the low-end weight of the kick, bass, snare, and guitars so none of them dominates, and matching their high-end "clank"/"whack" character the same way — achieved partly by trimming a dirty, un-musical low end (specifically named as ~40 Hz) off the guitars so the kick and bass can be heard clearly. The video closes with two quick, unrelated production tricks mentioned in passing: removing the note just before a section change to make the next section land harder by contrast, and cross-wiring an Echo Boy's stereo feedback path (left feeding right and vice versa) for a bouncing rather than static stereo delay.

## Techniques

- **Diagnose a kick that reads "too loud and too quiet at the same time" as a missing beater/attack transient, not a low-end problem — fix with a boost around 1–3 kHz rather than more low end.** "That kind of almost like what you're hearing with my hand here... I'm looking probably to be exploring the 1 to 3k range, 3,000 cycles." Confirmed by an on/off A/B with zero upper-range EQ otherwise applied — only low-end shaping had been done on the kick mic up to that point. → [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[EQ]] (effect), [[Kick Design]], [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]]
- **The "freight train" mix philosophy: match the low-end WEIGHT of the kick, bass, snare, and guitars so none of them carries noticeably more low end than the others — dense and powerful, but streamlined, nothing sticking out.** Explicit goal: "I don't want one to sound like it's much louder low-end... I want all that low end to be about the same impact on you." The snare specifically doesn't need real sub-40 Hz content to satisfy this — it needs to *feel* thick in the bottom relative to the other elements, which is a perceptual/balance goal, not a literal frequency-matching one. → [[SUB Slot]], [[BASS Slot]], [[Masking and Carving]]
- **Trim "dirty" un-musical low end off guitars (named: ~40 Hz) specifically to clear mud and let the kick/bass/guitars "speak" in a more open low-end space.** Explicit cause-and-effect: "when I trimmed some of the super low bottom, and I say super low, dirty 40 cycles, when I trimmed some of that off the guitars, that was one of the things I chose to do to kind of get rid of that mud." Same balancing logic then repeats at the high end — matching the guitars' top-end "clank," the bass's "clank," and the snare/kick's "whack" so nothing pokes out there either. → [[SUB Slot]], [[EQ]] (effect), [[Masking and Carving]], [[2 Guitar Group|Guitar Group]]
- **Old transition trick: delete/silence the note immediately before the section you want to sound louder — the contrast, not extra level, is what sells the pop.** Mentioned in passing as a standing habit ("old tricks to make next sections pop a little bit — get rid of the note prior to what you want to sound louder"). → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Cross-wire an Echo Boy's stereo feedback path — left channel's repeats feed back into the right side and vice versa — for a genuinely bouncing stereo delay instead of two independent per-channel repeat trails.** Direct owned-plugin match: EchoBoy's Ping-Pong/multi-tap routing is the modern equivalent of manually cross-patching feedback L↔R on a stereo delay unit. → [[EchoBoy]], [[Stereo Field]], [[Delay]] (effect)

# Guitar Exercises Ranked By Importance

## Synopsis

A tier-list-format ranking of the channel's own recurring exercise categories by how often they actually deserve a spot in a practice routine (daily / often / occasionally), reflecting on 200+ episodes of material — the real value is the prioritization judgment itself, since most individual exercises named are already covered elsewhere on the channel.

## Techniques

- **Finger-independence/economy-of-motion exercises → daily.** Cited as one of the exercises with the most measurable multi-year personal impact (a direct "night and day" comparison against 2019 footage); explicitly *not* a waste of time even at just 5-10 minutes, because finger control underlies everything else attempted on the instrument.
- **One-note-per-string alternate-picked arpeggio shapes** — a real string-transition workout, but has a practical ceiling around 120 BPM before sweep picking becomes the faster real-world choice for the same shapes; still a good warmup/challenge, ranked as worth regular (not necessarily daily) practice.
- **Tied groups-of-three rhythm exercise** (four odd 3-note groups across a 4/4 bar, the third tied across the barline) — useful for internalizing the specific subdivision concept, but the concept is grasped within days, so it doesn't earn a permanent daily slot the way a physical-technique exercise does.
- **Classical pieces (e.g. Paganini) as picking-transition workouts** — recommended as a genuinely valuable, near-daily-worthy source specifically because classical composers wrote naturally demanding, non-uniform note-per-string picking patterns without "thinking like a guitarist" (unlike most guitar-native pentatonic/modal material, which tends toward uniform note counts per string). Personal example: arranging and recording the entire Paganini Caprice No. 24 for guitar was named one of the most technique-advancing projects of the last several years.
- **CAGED system practice (chord → arpeggio → pentatonic → full scale, per shape) → occasional, not daily.** Valuable for understanding how chords/arpeggios/scales relate to each other across the neck, but once internalized it's something to revisit every few months rather than drill constantly — the value is periodic reinforcement, not repetition.
- **4-notes-per-string stretch exercises → occasional.** Good for breaking a habitual 2- or 3-notes-per-string mental default and opening new phrasing possibilities, but not something needed in every session.
- **String-skipping (wide low-to-high-to-low interval jumps) → occasional, mostly practiced through improvising rather than isolated drilling.** Described as giving playing a "piano-like" quality from the big interval leaps.
- **A 7-notes-per-string odd-grouping exercise across all six strings → daily**, a personal long-running favorite warmup: endurance for the picking hand, string-transition practice, and timing discipline (groups of 7 get noticeably harder to keep in time at speed) all in one pattern.
- **A direction-changing sweep-picking arpeggio pattern (8th-note triplets) → often, genre-specific.** Valuable specifically because it breaks the common habit of only sweeping straight up-then-down through an arpeggio — switching sweep direction mid-arpeggio is named as the real skill that makes a sweep-picked line sound distinctive, and is deliberately trained here rather than left to chance.
- **A legato workout with a timing twist (four straight 16th notes into a 16th-note-triplet burst, single pick-attack per string) → daily.** Named as one of the most personally transformative exercise categories of the last two years, worked 20-30 minutes daily; the twist specifically drills not rushing or dragging across the subdivision change from straight 16ths into the triplet burst.

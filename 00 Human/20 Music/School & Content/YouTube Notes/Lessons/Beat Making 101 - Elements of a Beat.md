## Techniques

- **Terminology note:** "beat," "track," "song," "record," "composition," and "piece" are used interchangeably in casual producer speech — "making a beat" doesn't mean just programming drums, it means the whole track.
- **Six-element checklist for a song's fullest/loudest section:** melody, chords, bass, drums, percussion, effects/ear candy. Not every element is required everywhere (thinner sections are fine, even desirable), but the main/climactic part of a track will usually have all six present in some form. Useful as a quick self-audit ("do I have chords? bass? drums? a melody? any ear candy?") when a section feels incomplete.
- **"Busyness" named explicitly as the failure mode opposite of emptiness** — too many simultaneous elements (extra percussion layers, doubled melodies, extra chord material) makes a section unclear/distracting rather than rich. Already the subject of [[Arrangement and Song Form]]'s "simplicity referee" section (Kenny Beats/Lil Baby's 5–7 sound cap) — no new information here, just the same principle from a beginner's-eye framing.

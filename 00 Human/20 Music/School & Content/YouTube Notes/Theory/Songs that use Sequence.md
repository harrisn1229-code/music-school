Per the description, a 6-minute survey of melodic **sequence** — repeating a short melodic idea at a different pitch level — with examples spanning Nirvana, the *Game of Thrones* theme, ABBA, *The Greatest Showman*, Daft Punk, Coldplay, Beethoven, and Louis Armstrong, framed by Adam Neely's "Repetition Legitimises" idea (a phrase that would sound arbitrary once reads as intentional once it's been repeated/sequenced) and a Bernstein "What is a Melody?" reference.

## What to watch for

- Each example is presumably a single melodic cell restated at a new pitch level (up or down a step, third, etc.) rather than a literal repeat — listen for whether the *interval shape* of the phrase stays constant while its starting pitch moves, which is the defining test for sequence vs. plain repetition.
- Watch specifically for whether any example sequences **diatonically** (staying in the home key, so the exact interval sizes shift slightly to stay in-scale) vs. **exactly/chromatically** (transposing the precise interval pattern, which can pull briefly outside the key) — the vault's existing [[Motivic Development]] note already documents one deliberately-non-diatonic sequence used as a joke (Nate Smith's "Bounce"), so this video is a chance to find more ordinary, non-joke examples of both types across very different genres (classical, jazz, film score, pop).
- The Beethoven and Louis Armstrong examples are worth extra attention since they'd be the oldest/most "textbook" cases — useful if a canonical worked example is ever needed for teaching sequence from scratch.

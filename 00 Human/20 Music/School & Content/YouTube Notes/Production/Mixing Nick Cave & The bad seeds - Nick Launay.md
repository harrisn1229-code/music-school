# Mixing Nick Cave & The bad seeds - Nick Launay

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HT6xC9EdKHI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HT6xC9EdKHI)

## Synopsis

A Mix with the Masters session walkthrough with Nick Launay (Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds, Arcade Fire, PiL) mixing an extremely dynamic Nick Cave song — one that moves from around −7 on the meters at the quiet opening to fully pinned by the end. Launay states plainly that he doesn't want to fight that range: Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds aren't a "sounds louder than the last song on the radio" act, the song's dynamic arc IS the point, so the compressor gets set to match the loudest section and the quiet opening is simply left uncompressed. He describes his general mixing workflow as a "juggling" process — push the stereo bus level, ride faders up while balancing them against each other, routinely end up too hot overall, then select all faders on the automated desk and pull them down together rather than fighting individual levels. He typically starts a mix by soloing just drums, bass, and guitar as the "basic backing track" to get that trio feeling right before building outward. The back half of the video is a dedicated kick-drum demonstration: the raw, well-tuned mic'd kick is already decent, but for extra "boom" he runs it in parallel through a SansAmp guitar-distortion pedal, deliberately tuning the resulting low-end note to the song's key using a very narrow, sweepable EQ (functioning almost like an inverse notch filter), then gates that distorted layer with a Drawmer DS201 to control exactly how long the note rings before blending it back under the clean, clear-topped close mic.

## Techniques

- **Set the compressor to match the LOUDEST section of a song with an extreme dynamic range, and deliberately leave the quiet sections uncompressed — an explicit artist-identity call, not a technical default.** "I tend to not want to mess too much with Dynamics when it comes to Nick Cave and the Bad Seeds, because that's what they're all about — they're not about having a song that comes in loud on the radio and sounds louder than the song before." Practical result: the beginning of the song is essentially uncompressed and the end is "pretty damn squashed" purely because of how extreme the song's own arrangement dynamics are — the compressor threshold/ratio decision is made by finding the loudest moment first, then leaving everything quieter alone. → [[Compression]] (effect)
- **Mix-bus fader "juggling": push all channel faders up together to find the balance, routinely end up collectively too hot, then select every fader at once on an automated desk and pull them down together rather than re-balancing individually.** A named, repeatable workflow habit for arriving at a rough balance fast without losing the relative levels once they're right. → [[Gain Staging]]
- **Start a mix by soloing just drums + bass + guitar — the "basic backing track" — and get that trio feeling right before building everything else around it.** A deliberate minimal-first mixing order rather than bringing every element in at once. → [[Session Templates]]
- **SansAmp (Tech 21 guitar-distortion pedal) run as a parallel duplicate on a kick, with the resulting low end TUNED to the song's key using a very narrow, sweepable EQ — described as functioning almost like an inverse/reverse notch filter — then blended back under the clean close mic for extra "boom" without losing top-end clarity.** "I usually distort the low end in particular so it actually has a note... I usually tune that note to the song because I like to keep things musical." The close mic alone was already a good, well-tuned kick sound — this is a deliberate character addition, not a fix. Same SansAmp-on-kick trick already logged from a different Launay/BRMC session ([[Parallel distortion by Nick Launay]]); this instance adds the tuned-to-key detail that one didn't have. → [[Saturation and Distortion]] (effect), [[SUB Slot]], [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Gate the distorted parallel layer (Drawmer DS201) specifically to control the LENGTH of the tuned note, not for noise-floor cleanup.** Without a gate the sustained distorted low end would be "a ridiculous sound"; with the DS201 in the chain the note's length is adjustable from long to short by ear, giving a controllable, musical decay on what is otherwise a raw distortion signal. → [[Gate]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]]

# 6 common chord progressions and why they work

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v3YbEL-_eoI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v3YbEL-_eoI)

## Synopsis

A follow-up survey covering the "Wonderwall"/Plagal Cascade progression again (same as a separate video already enriched from this batch), the RHCP "Can't Stop" progression, two more Axis Progression relatives beyond the four standard rotations, and a reframe of the 12-bar blues as structurally unusual despite its familiarity.

## Techniques

- **"Wonder Wall" / Plagal Cascade — i–♭III–♭VII–IV** — repeat topic, already fully covered on the dedicated video page [[Songs that use the Wonderwall chord progression]] and on [[Chord Progressions]].
- **"Can't Stop" / Red Hot Chili Peppers progression — i–♭VII–v–♭VI** — entirely diatonic to Aeolian/natural minor, no borrowed chords, giving it a smooth, moody character without the strong resolve-toward-tonic pull that harmonic-minor-flavored progressions have. Song: "Can't Stop" (Red Hot Chili Peppers).
- **I–V–ii–IV** — the standard Axis Progression with vi swapped for ii; small functional change (both carry subdominant-adjacent function and share notes) but the ii→IV motion reads as a small ascending climb. Songs: "Hot n Cold" (Katy Perry), "Moves Like Jagger" (Maroon 5).
- **IV–I–V–vi** — already documented as one of the four core Axis rotations on [[The Axis Progression]] — no new content here.
- **I–IV–vi–V** — a genuinely new Axis relative: the standard four chords with IV and V swapped, so V lands at the END of the loop instead of early — the loop point becomes a real V→I perfect cadence (a stronger resolution than the plagal IV→I cadence the standard rotations lean on), giving it a brighter, more climbing character. Song: "We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together" (Taylor Swift).
- **12-bar blues, reframed as structurally unusual**: a 12-bar phrase length is atypical against Western music's default 4/8/16-bar phrasing, and putting a dominant 7th on the tonic chord itself (not just IV/V) is unusual outside blues — elsewhere in Western harmony a dominant-quality "tonic" reads as unstable, but blues simply treats it as home. Songs: "Johnny B. Goode" (Chuck Berry), "Stuck in the Middle with You" (Stealers Wheel).

# I'll Cry Instead — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

Standard two-bridge model with no solo, no double verse and no outro — it simply stops at the end of the last verse. The analytical interest is the split between sections: **the verses are bluesy in both melody and chords (I, IV, V only), while the bridge is a short-lived but real modulation to V** with none of the blues in it.

**Form.** Two bars of intro vamping on I, a 16-bar verse in four equal phrases, an 8-bar bridge in two, and a complete halt at the end. (For the film's running-and-jumping scene this was originally meant to run instead of "Can't Buy Me Love"; to fix the timing an alternate version was spliced with the first verse repeated at the end, which is what appears on the United Artists album.)

**Harmony.** Harmonic rhythm is close to plodding for most of the verse, and the **speed-up in the final phrase** is what makes the section feel closed ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). The intro's guitar hints at a chord on the offbeat, but those are neighbour tones filling between two tonics rather than a change of root. The bridge's pivot is deliberately clumsy ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]): the first move signals nothing on its own, the next tells you something is happening without telling you what, and you don't get a clear key again until the new tonic is nailed down by its own ii–V–I near the end — at which point the song scampers straight home in the final bar, the fastest harmonic rhythm anywhere in it.

**Melody.** Bluesy in the verses only, and not consistently even there: the tune keeps **alternating the natural and flat third**, and that wobble is most of its character ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]). The phrase over the IV chord is the specimen, carrying both the flat seventh and flat third while riding roughshod over the chord underneath.

**Arrangement.** John's is the only voice, double-tracked and unusually tightly synced. The overall sound is countryish — strummed rhythm guitar, a chordal obbligato lead part, prominent tambourine, and a recurring habit of zooming up into chords from the note below. In the two verses that follow a bridge, the penultimate phrase strips out to **walking bass plus tambourine only**, which links the sections without needing a transition.

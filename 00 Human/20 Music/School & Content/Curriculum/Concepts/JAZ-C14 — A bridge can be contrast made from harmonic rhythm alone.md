# JAZ-C14 — A bridge can be contrast made from harmonic rhythm alone

**Q.** Give the Rhythm Changes bridge, and say what makes it feel like a different section given that it introduces no new material.

> [!question]- Answer
> **`| D7 | D7 | G7 | G7 | C7 | C7 | F7 | F7 |`** in B♭ — III7–VI7–II7–V7, four dominants two bars each, each resolving down a fifth into the next, and only the last of them — F7, the key's own V7 — diatonic.
>
> What makes it feel new is not a key change, a tempo change or new instrumentation. It is a switch from diatonic and busy to chromatic and slow: **harmonic rhythm drops from two chords per bar to one chord per two bars — a 4× slowdown — and that alone reads as a different section.**

## Why it matters

This is the cheapest bridge available in production. Contrast does not require new material: halve the harmonic rhythm, leave everything else alone, and the section changes.

The bridge is also a self-contained lesson in dominant motion by fifths — playing the eight-bar loop teaches it faster than any explanation. Its off-the-shelf cousin is [[The Montgomery-Ward Bridge]], the stock I7–IV7–II7–V7 Tin Pan Alley writers reached for when they had two A sections and needed eight contrasting bars; three of its four root moves are fifths and the fourth, IV → II, is the seam.

## Drill

Take a busy eight-bar section of your own and rewrite it with the harmonic rhythm halved and then halved again, changing nothing else. Decide at which point it stops sounding like the same section.

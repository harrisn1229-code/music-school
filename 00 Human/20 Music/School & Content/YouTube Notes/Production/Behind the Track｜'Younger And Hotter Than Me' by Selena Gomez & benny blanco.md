# Behind the Track｜'Younger And Hotter Than Me' by Selena Gomez & benny blanco

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rkJ-LaoArUY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rkJ-LaoArUY)

## Synopsis

A **Mix with the Masters** "Behind the Track" piece with Benny Blanco and Selena Gomez walking through "Younger and Hotter Than Me." Like the channel's "Elephant" episode, it's mostly a process/philosophy piece rather than a plugin-by-plugin walkthrough, but it's denser on concrete production moves than that one — there's a real vocal-chain decision (deliberately un-tuning the first verse), a real vocal-arrangement device (octave placement per section), a genuinely reusable mixdown workflow trick (muted alternate "passes" as ear candy), and a short but specific effects chain for building a dense bridge (distortion → CLA/Altiverb/Lair reverbs → delay → vocoder). The song's origin is a nice bit of songwriting lore: the chord progression came from a piano tag Benny improvised on a cover years earlier, which went viral on TikTok on its own — Selena recognized the sound and asked to build a song around it, and the two of them re-derived the progression together (6/8 waltz time, starting around B-flat major). The lyric theme (the passing of time, self-consciousness) grew out of Benny simply asking Selena how she was feeling that day, tied to her real biography as a child star (working since age 7 on *Barney*). Selena is candid that she isn't a technical engineer — "I'm not a perfect wizard of Pro Tools" — and frames the whole video around a permission-giving message: know what sounds good to your own ear and don't let technical gaps stop you from finishing an idea.

## Techniques

- **Deliberately remove pitch correction from the first verse for a raw, spoken quality, contrasted against a bigger, more processed chorus.** They had tuned the vocal, then Benny said "no — take the tune off of the first verse... it should just feel so raw it sounds like she's just speaking." Every breath and small vocal detail was deliberately kept in rather than edited out, because it reinforces the intimate, whispered-in-your-ear character they wanted for the verse before the chorus opens up big. → [[Vocal Tuning]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]]
- **Octave-shifted vocal arrangement, one register per verse, as a structural build device.** Once Selena's take was in, they placed it "down the octave in the first verse" and "up the octave in the second verse" — a deliberate register contrast across the song's two verses rather than one fixed vocal register throughout. *(no plugin/pitch-shift tool named — reads as either a second take or a pitch-shifted layer, not specified)* → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]]
- **Background vocal layering from casual collaborators, not a hired stack.** Backgrounds on the song include "a little bit of Phineas in the background" plus choir-style harmonies contributed by a friend (Lucy) — vocal stacking sourced from whoever's around and willing, not a planned session. *(no processing chain given — logged as a workflow/culture note, not a technique with settings)*
- **Invite friends over to overdub live instruments organically, rather than booking session players.** "I always have people coming in and out of my house, and if a friend comes in I say, 'Hey man, you want to play guitar on this?'" — the guitar part from friend Dylan Day is singled out for its expressive, almost vocal-like ("sounds like a person crying") quality, specifically because Dylan's own playing style, not a pedal/processing chain, is what makes it distinctive. *(a player-quality mention, not a settable technique — logged here only)*
- **Build several alternate "passes" of a section, mute them, then unmute for a moment whenever the mix needs a hit of ear candy — an explicit fix for listener fatigue.** "One of the things that unifies us is we'll do several passes of something — often weird passes, like a slightly distorted synth, a slightly distorted bass, vocal throws. You do a full pass of the song, then you mute it, and you just listen to the song, and every time your brain wants a little bit of candy, you unmute a little moment of it — because if something's always present in a production, you forget about it a minute in." The mechanism: build the candy as a full-length muted layer first, then bring in only brief unmuted moments by ear during playback, rather than composing exact automation points in advance. → [[Ear Candy and Hiding Synths]], [[Automation]]
- **Deliberately dense, distorted bridge built by degrading and stacking effects on the vocal, as a structural contrast to a simple song.** "Because the song was so simple, I wanted to have the bridge be very dense" — starting from a raw vocal fragment ("someone else's" part, i.e. a background/borrowed vocal piece), the chain runs: pull the level down → distort it → [[Saturn 2|Saturn]] for a "cool," distorted character → delay and reverb layered on top → a [[Vocoder|vocoder]] pass — building toward "pretty crazy" vocal texture for the bridge specifically. A second, related pass takes a plain vocal and stacks reverbs and delays on it including "some CLA, some Altiverb [unconfirmed, garbled in captions], and [[Lair]]" (described directly as "it's like a Reverb") for "an extended feeling," then everything except the vocal is cut away to make it "really cut" through. → [[Saturn 2]], [[Lair]], [[Vocoder]], [[Reverb]] (effect), [[Delay]] (effect), [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]]
- **The song's chord progression and 6/8 waltz feel originated from an old, unrelated project — a piano tag improvised on a cover years earlier that went viral on TikTok independently, which then became this song's starting point.** Benny had done a cover of another song years before and improvised a short piano tag over its instrumental; that tag later blew up on TikTok on its own. Selena recognized the sound, asked Benny about it, and the two rebuilt/rediscovered the progression together by noodling in his studio — 6/8 (or 3/4) time, starting around B-flat major, moving through G minor, and (per the transcript, imperfectly captured) D and E-flat. Song-specific chord/lyric content — stays on this page only, no reusable technique to extract beyond the "TikTok validated the tag before the song existed" origin story.
- **Start a co-write by simply asking the artist how they're feeling that day.** Benny: "a lot of these songs started because I just asked her — I said, 'how are you feeling today?'" — used directly as the lyric-writing spark for this song's theme (the passing of time, self-consciousness), tied to Selena's own life story (working since age 7 on *Barney*, growing up as "this shiny toy" in the industry). A simple, repeatable session-opener for lyric direction rather than a music-production technique.
- **The fashion-designer framing for building a vocal to an artist, not a generic beat-first workflow.** "Imagine you're a fashion designer... here's your design, and if they go 'I like that one,' you take measurements, cut it all out, and make it fit the person's essence in the perfect way" — a metaphor for tailoring a demo/production specifically to the artist's voice and delivery once a vocal idea has landed, rather than building a beat and hoping a vocal fits it.
- **Permission-giving creative philosophy: technical mastery isn't the bar, taste is.** "I'm not a typically great player, I'm not a perfect wizard of Pro Tools and knowing exactly what to do — I just know what I think sounds good. Nothing should stop you from getting your creativity out... just do it, and keep doing it till it sounds good to you." Framed as encouragement to record and release ideas despite technical gaps, rather than a production technique.

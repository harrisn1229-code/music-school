# 1 Chord Progression, 5 Levels of Complexity

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C_i2axaWzaE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C_i2axaWzaE)

## Synopsis

Takes one simple, extremely common progression (the "50s"/I-vi-IV-V progression, in C: C-Am-F-G) and elaborates it through five increasingly sophisticated levels, each a genuinely different reharmonization technique rather than a "better" version of the last.

## What's taught

- **Level 1 — plain triads.** C – Am – F – G, the bare I-vi-IV-V "50s progression."
- **Level 2 — color tones/extensions.** Add a non-essential extra note to each chord: C → **Cadd9**; Am → **Am7**; F → **Fadd9**; G → **Gsus2** (replacing the 3rd with the 2nd rather than adding to it).
- **Level 3 — passing chords.** Insert a chord built on the scale degree *between* two main chords to bridge the gap: C → **G/B** (a first-inversion chord built on the note between C and Am) → Am; Am → **C/G** → F; F → **F♯ diminished** (bridging F to G, since F♯/G♭ sits between them) → G. The loop can close G → C directly with no passing chord needed.
- **Level 4 — secondary dominants.** A secondary dominant = the dominant (V) chord of a *different* chord in the key, not just the home key's own V. Precede each chord with its own dominant: C → **E7** (V/vi) → Am → **C7** (V/IV) → F → **D7** (V/V) → G → **G7** (dominant 7th on the home V, to pull firmly back) → C.
- **Level 5 — full ii-V-I substitutions.** Precede each destination chord with both its 2 and its 5 (a full ii-V), all with 7ths added for extra pull: C → **Bm7 – E7** (ii-V of Am) → Am → (ii-V of F) → F → (ii-V of G) → G7 → C.
- **Bonus level 6 (mentioned, not fully demonstrated)**: take level 5's ii-V-I chain and further color each chord with upper extensions and altered tensions (9ths, ♭9ths, etc.).
- **Framing note**: these levels are explicitly *not* a quality ladder — they're different flavors/options for the same underlying harmonic movement, chosen for the effect wanted, not because a higher level is objectively better. Even when playing the most elaborate version, the presenter says he's still thinking in the simple I-vi-IV-V terms underneath.

# Magical Mystery Tour — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (6) · Verse (16, a vamp) · Refrain (8) · Verse · Refrain · instrumental Bridge (10) · Verse · Refrain · Refrain · fade. The whole back half of the record runs at roughly **half the tempo** of the front half.

An overture whose one real trick stays visible rather than buried: the song begins and ends in D, but most of it *sounds* like E. The intro's D–A–E reads either as I–V–V/V in D or as ♭VII–IV–I in E ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]], [[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]), and the verse then argues for E by sheer repetition while pointedly **withholding the V chord** that would settle the question. The refrain finally closes it with a complete V–I in D, set up by a stepwise descending bass that gives every step its own chord ([[POP-C04 — Four chords with a moving bass]]), including a minor iv borrowed from the parallel minor. The next verse starts on E and the ambiguity resets.

The bridge is where the withheld E-major V finally arrives, and it runs a slow/fast harmonic-rhythm shift ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]) that fakes an acceleration — right before the real tempo halves. Rhythm carries the form throughout: dotted and on 1-and-3 in the intro, hard four with 2-and-4 accents in the verse, undotted again in the refrain. The arrangement is a deliberate collision of folk-rock strumming, pomposo trumpets and cocktail-lounge piano, with block vocals cut slow so they play back high and strident.

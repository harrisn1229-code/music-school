# One After 909 — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (4) · Verse (16, AABA′) · Verse · Bridge (16, a single 8-bar unit played twice) · Verse · Verse (guitar solo) · Bridge · Verse · Outro with a complete ending. As boxy as it gets — every section is an even number of four-bar phrases and the solo simply runs over a verse.

Written in the late fifties, taped by the Quarrymen around 1960, cut at EMI in 1963, shelved for six years, and finally released from the rooftop performance. It is **compositionally the simplest thing they ever put out**: four chords — I, IV, V and V-of-V — made to look worse than they are by a five-sharp key signature. That C♯ is the only chromatic chord in the song and it is the bridge's entire engine ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]).

The formal work is done by one asymmetry: **the verse is closed at both ends** (starts and finishes on I) **while the bridge is open at both ends**, beginning on IV and stranded on V. Inside the verse the harmonic rhythm goes from completely frozen for eight bars to a chord every two. The bluesiness arrives without a blues form — ten bars of stationary I plus a melody that keeps laying minor inflections over major chords ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]).

Come-as-you-are rooftop playing, closer to Dixieland collective improvisation than to an arrangement. **John leads with Paul in parallel thirds above him** on the verses, Everly Brothers style, while the bridges are John alone. The *Let It Be* and *Get Back* mixes of the same take differ sharply in panning, balance and edits.

# Tomorrow Never Knows — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (6 bars, built as 2 bars each of three entering elements — tamboura drone, hard-rock rhythm track, seagull loop) · Verse (a straightforward 8, repeated mantra-like **seven times**) · Instrumental (16 bars, divided **6 + 10** instead of 8 + 8) · Verse × several · Outro, ending in disintegration rather than a true fade.

A kitchen-sink collage — Indian drone, modal tune, tape loops, ADT, vocal through revolving Leslie speakers, distorted close miking — that somehow does not scatter. The structural reason it can afford all that is that **the harmony essentially never moves**: the whole track sits on a C pedal ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]), with an implied vacillation toward **♭VII** in the second half of virtually every verse ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). That is the entire harmonic content of the song.

The tune is equal parts triadic bugle call and bluesy lick leaning on ♭7, and its first half is deliberately simplistic — the archetypal case of keeping one parameter plain while the rest go to extremes. The instrumental's two halves split the modal question between them: the first emphasises the **major third**, the second the **bent minor third**, both over the flat seventh ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]).

Despite the thickness, the fabric is made of discrete elements each with its own timbre *and* its own rhythmic identity, which is what keeps it parseable — and mapping which element appears where shows the orchestration is **carefully scheduled**, not free-wheeling ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]).

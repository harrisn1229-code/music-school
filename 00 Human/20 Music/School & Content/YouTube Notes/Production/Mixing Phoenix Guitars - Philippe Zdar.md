# Mixing Phoenix Guitars - Philippe Zdar

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ie_Xn9qaA3U)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ie_Xn9qaA3U)

## Synopsis

Philippe Zdar (Cassius, Phoenix producer) walks through his guitar-bus approach on a Phoenix session, working on a real Trident console with an SSL-style bus compressor and a Helios-style EQ. The through-line is restraint and feel over precision: EQ chosen for its character (a slight Helios distortion at higher gain, loved on purpose), pan used deliberately to create air rather than pushed to the extremes, one guitar kept wet with reverb and its double kept bone dry and centered on the same pan as the wet one for warmth without width, and — the most distinctive Phoenix-specific trick — sub-audible "drone" layers mixed in and never meant to be consciously heard, there purely to add tension the way a jump-cut adds tension in film editing. He closes on the psychology of mixing guitars: give yourself permission to just be assembling pieces rather than "finishing" in the moment.

## Techniques

- **Pan as the primary air-creation tool, not extreme hard-panning.** "Pan is the key also to mixing, I always remember this: if you pan everything on the extreme you will be stacking millions of stuff on the side — if you pan a little bit there, you're helping a lot to get some air." → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **One guitar wet, its double bone dry, same pan position as the wet one.** Two guitar layers panned together — one carries the reverb return, the other is kept completely dry and set to the *exact same pan* as the wet guitar (not spread wide) specifically to avoid making the pair sound "large" — the goal was warmth, not width. → [[Width and Stereo]], [[6 Guitars/2 Guitar Group|Guitar Group]]
- **Helios-style channel EQ chosen for its mid-range character and its distortion-on-push behavior.** "I go to the Helios EQ because I love its mediums on the guitars, and also its cranking up a little bit — if you crank it there's a slight distortion that I love." A musical EQ used partly as a saturation source when pushed hard, not just for tonal correction. Unowned — see [[Plugin Wishlist]] (Waves HLS already tracked there for the Helios sound). → [[EQ]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **SSL-style bus compressor on the guitar return, described as a workhorse you could mix an entire record with alone.** "The compressors of the SSL... they're really cool, if you have only this you can still mix lots of songs with only this, you don't need all of this, of course it's just a luxury." → [[Compression]], [[6 Guitars/3 Guitar Bus|Guitar Bus]]
- **A phaser (heard in captions as "the only [garbled name] is fantastic") used on a guitar layer, noted as unfortunately mono.** Low-confidence transcript on the exact plugin name — logged qualitatively rather than guessed. → [[Modulation]]
- **Sub-audible "drone" layers as a Phoenix signature — mixed to never be consciously heard, purely for tension.** "Nobody knows, nobody hears drones — you put a lot of drones behind, always. We don't hear them, but they add a lot of stress/tension. You should never hear this kind of stuff, but if you take it out, a lot of the tension goes away." Compared explicitly to a jump-cut in film editing — the technique is felt, not heard. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Depth and Space]]
- **A budget hardware splurge over more in-the-box plugins — one distinctive analog reverb (an Oak spring reverb, ~€700) rather than a top-tier EMT/AKG unit (~€10,000).** Zdar's stated philosophy for anyone working entirely in the box: "I would urge you to buy one or two little things that are really different... this will add something a lot... you can buy them, record them, and have the sound that nobody else has, only your sound." → [[Reverb]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Trident console character named directly: strong at gluing extreme top-end detail with heavy low end in the same signal path** — used as the reasoning for routing the drone/tension layer through it. → [[Vintage Gear Worklist]] (Trident console not yet documented — logged there)
- **Mixing-psychology tip: consciously separate "assembling" from "finishing."** When a mix session feels frustrating and slow, Zdar's fix is to mentally reframe: "I am not tripping right now, I'm just putting all the stuff together" — working the floor and the desk at one monitoring level before judging the result. → [[Client Communication and Revisions]]

A single alternate-picking pattern gets re-run with one note manually altered each time to switch parallel scales (natural minor → Phrygian → harmonic minor), turning a speed drill into an active ear/theory exercise. ## What's taught

- **The core practice philosophy: combine technique and theory in one exercise rather than two separate practice blocks.** Named benefits: technique work stops feeling purely mechanical (there's a musical/theoretical payoff attached), and theory stops feeling like textbook study (it's tied to something audible and physical). BERNTH names this as his single most effective practice discovery, crediting it with years of saved time.
- **The base exercise: 3-note-per-string alternate picking, ascending horizontally across two adjacent strings (D and G strings in the demo), not boxed in one position.** Strict alternate picking throughout (down-up-down-up, no economy picking) — chosen specifically to drill the string-crossing skill needed for fast alternate-picking shred lines.
- **Picking-angle fix for clean string transitions:** angle the pick noticeably away from the guitar body (not into it) when crossing strings — the wrong angle traps the pick between the two strings mid-transition; the corrected angle clears both directions of the crossing.
- **The theory layer — parallel mode switching by altering one note, with no diagram lookup allowed:** run the identical fingering pattern on E natural minor, then manually lower the 2nd (F♯→F) at every occurrence to get E Phrygian, then instead raise the 7th (D→D♯) to get E harmonic minor — forcing the player to consciously track and alter a single scale degree live rather than recalling a second memorized shape. Explicitly framed as [[Diatonic Modes|parallel]], not derivative, mode thinking.
- **Practical sequencing recommendation:** master the natural-minor pass (technique + actively thinking the note names, not just finger positions) before adding scale-switching; start the switching work with Phrygian and harmonic minor specifically since they're the most-needed colors for metal/shred soloing. Rotate the target scale pairing weekly (e.g. major↔Lydian, natural minor↔Dorian/melodic minor) and rotate which string pair is used once the D/G pair feels comfortable, to generalize the horizontal-visualization skill across the whole neck. Run 10-15 minutes daily.

# What makes Oasis sound like The Beatles?

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sIEsyIPUSUU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sIEsyIPUSUU)

## Synopsis

A survey of exactly how Oasis's Beatles influence shows up — direct musical lifts (confirmed by Noel Gallagher himself), melodic/structural parallels, shared instrumental choices, and lyric-level references — framed explicitly as influence and homage, not plagiarism, closing on Noel Gallagher's own quote: "it's nobody's sound to own."

## Techniques

- **A "top-bottom" broken-chord piano pattern lifted wholesale, confirmed by the songwriter**: "Don't Look Back in Anger"'s piano intro (C, then F/second-inversion, same top-bottom voicing pattern both times) is admitted by Noel Gallagher as directly lifted from the two-chord top-bottom pattern in Lennon's "Imagine."
- **A melodic contour (not exact notes) reused as a recognizable homage**: "She's Electric"'s chorus melody echoes "While My Guitar Gently Weeps" via the same jump-up-then-stepwise-descent shape (up to a raised scale degree, then down by step, down by step again) in both songs, plus a separate outro that mirrors "With a Little Help from My Friends"'s outro structure.
- **Reusing an iconic drum loop as a direct genre nod — legitimized by having the same drummer**: "Falling Down" (Oasis) reuses a drum pattern extremely close to "Tomorrow Never Knows" (Beatles); notable because Zak Starkey — Ringo Starr's son — is the drummer playing it, making the citation a literal generational handoff rather than just a sonic copy.
- **Signature-instrument borrowing as a genre-association shortcut**: using a **Mellotron** (an instrument closely identified with the Beatles as early adopters) automatically reads as "Beatle-esque" — Oasis used one on "Go Let It Out." Related move: brass/chamber-orchestra instrumentation layered into a rock-band arrangement, another Beatles-signature choice Oasis reuses repeatedly.
- **The reversed guitar solo as a Beatles-popularized production technique, reused as a direct genre citation**: Beatles used it on "Tomorrow Never Knows"; Oasis uses a similar reversed guitar solo on "The Masters Plan." - **Drummer-specific fill vocabulary as a genre signature independent of the song itself**: Ringo Starr's characteristic slow, tom-heavy fills are echoed across the Oasis catalog, most notably in "Don't Look Back in Anger" — a drumming *style* citation, distinct from the drum-loop-reuse example above.

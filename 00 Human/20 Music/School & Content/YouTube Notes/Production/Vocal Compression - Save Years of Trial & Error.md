# Vocal Compression: Save Years of Trial & Error

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2RWijLWuW7I)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2RWijLWuW7I)

## Synopsis

Sage Audio gives a concrete, ready-to-dial recipe for an upfront modern vocal compression sound, explains what attack/release actually do moment-to-moment (not the simplified "wait then snap" model most people carry), settles the "EQ before or after compression" debate as a false dichotomy (use both, for different jobs), and offers a wave-shaping fallback for when compression alone leaves a vocal feeling weak or lifeless.

## Techniques

- **Attack/release don't work like a switch — attenuation ramps continuously to its maximum over the attack time, and continues ramping back down over the release time even after the signal drops below threshold.** A 20 ms attack doesn't mean "wait 20 ms then snap to full gain reduction" — the compressor starts attenuating the instant the signal crosses threshold and reaches maximum attenuation at the 20 ms mark; the ramp curve (linear vs. non-linear) varies by compressor/algorithm, so "10 ms into a 20 ms attack" could mean anywhere from a few dB to nearly the full attenuation depending on the unit. Same logic in reverse for release: falling below threshold *starts* the release countdown, but the signal is still being compressed throughout that release window, not instantly restored. → [[Compression]]
- **A concrete 5-part recipe for an upfront, detailed modern vocal sound.** **Soft knee + 4:1 ratio** (more compression on lower-amplitude content while staying transparent) + **quick attack with 1–4 ms of look-ahead** (attenuation lands right at the vocal's onset because the compressor measures ahead of the signal) + **5–8 dB of gain reduction** (enough to tame aggressive peaks without a "compressed" sound when combined with the above) + **automatic makeup gain** (brings quiet details forward since the initial transient survives the smooth compression curve). This combination is stated as reliably producing an "upfront" vocal that still reads detailed and nuanced rather than squashed. → [[Compression]], [[Compression Staging]], [[1 Main Vocal Track]]
- **EQ-before-or-after-compression is a false dichotomy on a vocal — use both, for different jobs, in this order.** EQ #1 (pre-compression): attenuate problem content — plosives, aggressive sibilance, nasal tones, unpleasant resonances, unneeded low-mids — to give the compressor a clean, "safe" signal to work on. Compress with the 5-part recipe above. EQ #2 (post-compression): amplify what you now want *more* of — a boost somewhere in **2–5 kHz** for clarity/intelligibility, air via EQ or an exciter, and (if placed even later in the chain, after reverb/delay) side-image warmth around **~300 Hz**. → [[EQ]], [[Compression]]
- **When compression alone leaves a vocal weak, far-back, or lifeless — don't reach for more compression (including serial compression); reach for wave shaping instead, applied conservatively.** More/serial compression tends to warp tone in unpleasant ways past a point. A wave shaper (Melda's free wave shaper demoed, and the owned [[Oxford Inflator]] compared) lets you amplify only the quiet-detail amplitude range while keeping a linear (1:1) input/output relationship above it — peaks stay exactly where they are, but quiet detail gets pulled forward, achieving the "upfront" effect from a different angle than gain-reduction-based makeup gain. Melda's is more flexible/adjustable; the Oxford Inflator is less flexible but has a distinct, pleasant character. → [[Oxford Inflator]], [[Compression]]

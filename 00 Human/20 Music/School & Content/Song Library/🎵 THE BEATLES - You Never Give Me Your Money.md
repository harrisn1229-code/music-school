## Production & Arrangement
The opening of the **Abbey Road medley** and a suite in miniature — several unrelated fragments (a piano ballad → a boogie "out of college" section → a dreamy "one sweet dream" → the "1-2-3-4-5-6-7" nursery coda) segued into one, then flowing straight into the rest of the medley. A masterclass in **transitions**.

- Each section has its own key/feel/tempo; the **segues** (tape edits, key pivots, sound-effect crossfades — crickets into "Sun King") are the art.
- Rich [[Seventh Chords|7th]]-chord piano harmony.

## Steal this
- **Medley/suite transitions:** write fragments and focus on the *joins* — pivot chords, tempo ramps, sound-effect crossfades → [[Modulation (Key Change)]] · /setlist flow thinking.
- **Segue with a sound effect** (crickets, noise) to bridge two unrelated sections.
- **7th-chord piano** for a bittersweet, sophisticated bed → [[Seventh Chords]].

## Pollack's analysis

*Extracted from [[AWP — You Never Give Me Your Money]] — Alan W. Pollack, "Notes On…" #13.*

**Form.** The clearest Beatles example of a **teleological medley** rather than a cyclical song form — three sections (X, Y, Z) that never return, each in its own key, each fragmentary enough to sound like the opening of a different song. What holds it together, he argues, is two things: a single ordinary syncopation recurring across unrelated material, and a key plan tracing an arch — **A minor → C major → A major**. In place of a return, every section repeats one short phrase several times immediately; that substitution is the song's whole formal argument. X is three passes of an 8-bar phrase, the first an unusually generous instrumental intro that gives away the entire verse. Y splits in two — YA is two passes of a 4-bar phrase, YB is **five** passes of a 3-bar phrase, a prime number of repeats of an asymmetrical unit. Z also splits: ZA is an 8-bar instrumental intro plus a **seven-and-a-half-bar** verse, ZB a 2-bar phrase run fourteen times to the fade.

**Harmony.** **X** (A minor) is a complete diatonic circle of fifths, `i – iv – ♭VII – ♭III – ♭VI – ii° – V – i`, every chord in root position and none converted into a secondary dominant — placid and propulsive at once, because even harmonic rhythm holds it still while fifth motion pushes it along ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). **YA** (C major) is `I – V-of-vi – vi – V-of-IV – IV – V – I`, the same fifth-driven motion but harder, because the fifths are now secondary dominants ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). **YB** flips C major to C Mixolydian for a double plagal `♭VII – IV – I` ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]). **ZA** cycles in **minor thirds**, bass and upper voice each outlining diminished sevenths, which gives clanging chromatic cross relations plus a few bars of genuine keylessness — the "Rhapsody in Blue" device, unique in their work. **ZB** is `♭III – ♭VII – I` in A, the song's entire key arch compressed into two bars.

**Notable.** The connective tissue is an accented eighth falling between beats 2 and 3 — ordinary for the genre, which is exactly why it works as glue rather than as a motif.

**Against this page.** Agrees: A minor into a C major boogie section, and then out into A major.

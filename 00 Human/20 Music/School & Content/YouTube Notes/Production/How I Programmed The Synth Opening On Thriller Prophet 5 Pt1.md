# How I Programmed The Synth Opening On Michael Jackson's Thriller: Prophet 5 Pt.1

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y4jHC3ktbUk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y4jHC3ktbUk)

## Synopsis

Part 1 of a 4-part breakdown of Thriller's opening "brass" stack — the Prophet-5 layer specifically. The real content is the composite-orchestration concept stated up front: four different sources (Prophet-5, Jupiter-8, Synclavier "pipe organ," and real live brass — see companion [[How I Programmed The Synth Opening On Michael Jackson's Thriller The Synclavier Pt.3]]) each contribute a DIFFERENT quality to one perceived sound, and none of them would read as "the sound" heard alone.

## Techniques

- **A composite "instrument" built from 4 unrelated sources, each contributing a distinct quality no other layer supplies: the Prophet-5 gives body, the Jupiter-8 gives resonance, the Synclavier gives a "digital bottom," and real live brass gives punch/human personality/brassiness that only actual brass tubes produce.** Explicitly stated: individually, none of the layers "sound like it" — the composite only reads as the target sound once combined. A concrete worked example of building one perceived instrument from genuinely different sound sources rather than layering near-identical patches.
- **Use BOTH sawtooth AND pulse wave from EACH oscillator simultaneously (not choosing one per oscillator) to maximize overtone/harmonic content**, then double that whole waveform pair an octave up on a second oscillator — every available waveform except sine, deliberately, to get maximum brightness/harmonic density for a brass-substitute texture.
- **Attack time trades brassy "hit" character against playability — slow the attack only as far as the part can still be played in time.** A slower attack gives the front of the note a brass-like bloom/swell instead of an instant hit, but pushing it too far makes the part impossible to play accurately — the same tradeoff logic already seen on other patches from this channel, restated here specifically for a slow-swelling brass-substitute sound.
- **Release time set specifically to match a mix-level fade-out, not to the patch's "natural" feel** — the Prophet layer's release time exists because the recorded track physically fades out at that point; without adequate release, the sound would cut off abruptly rather than tailing into the fade. A reminder to check what the ARRANGEMENT/mix needs from an envelope stage, not just what sounds good on a held note in isolation.

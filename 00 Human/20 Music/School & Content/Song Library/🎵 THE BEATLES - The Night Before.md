# The Night Before — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** the long two-bridge model — Intro (a single **8-bar phrase**) · Verse (16, four equal phrases, AABC) · Verse · Bridge (8) · Verse · Verse (half solo) · Bridge · Verse · Outro (a *petit reprise* of the verse's last phrase plus the returning guitar fanfare lick).

A bluesy foreground over a pop chassis. **Half of the eight chords are foreign to the key** — ♭III, minor iv, ♭VII and V-of-V alongside I, IV, V and vi ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]) — and this may be the first time they put **♭VII between I and IV** ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). The narrative is harmonic: the first two phrases open wide to V, the third refuses to resolve and introduces the ominous minor iv, and only the fourth restores I. In verses that lead to another verse, the ending is **forced away from I at the last moment** so the next verse's downbeat has something to arrive from.

The chromatic ♭3/♮3 pair runs at three scales at once ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]): melodically, with the blue third clashing against the major chords beneath; harmonically, in the vi/iv alternation; and ornamentally, in the bridge's noodling. Range is tightly managed for one payoff — the verse repeatedly caps at G and **the song's only high A arrives at the very end of the bridge** ([[MEL-C05 — Register and intensity]]).

The vocal arrangement is the standout: what sounds like call and response is really **one melodic line split hocket-style** between double-tracked lead and backing voices, with the backers dropping out entirely for the final phrase.

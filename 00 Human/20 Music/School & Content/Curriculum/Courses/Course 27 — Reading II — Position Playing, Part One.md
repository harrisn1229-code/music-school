# Course 27 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part One

The first half of Section Two of Leavitt's *A Modern Method for Guitar, Volume 1* — pages 60 to 99, where the open strings drop out and the hand moves up the neck: the position system itself, four of the five movable major-scale fingerings, four of the five Reading Studies, and the derivative chord sequence of Rhythm Accompaniment Part Two.

**What it assumes**

- [[Course 26 — Reading I — First Position, Part Two]] — Section One finished, the first position fluent in seven major keys and three minors. Section Two is the same reading skill relocated, and it does not re-teach the notation.
- A guitar, [[A Modern Method for Guitar - Volume 1.pdf]] open on a stand, and a metronome.
- No theory. This course teaches no ideas and tests none — it is the place you play the page.

| Lesson | Title | Pages | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fingering Types 1 and 1A in Second Position | 60–79 | performance check |
| 2 | Types 2 and 3, Harmonics and the Latin Beat | 80–99 | performance check |

**What this unlocks**

- [[Course 28 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part Two]] — fingering type 4, the review that plays all five types back to back, and the same shapes moved to positions III, I and IV.
- [[Guitar Positions]] stops being inferred and starts being sourced. The position system it is built on is defined on p. 60.
- Reading in C, F, G and D in the second position with no open strings — fingering types 1, 1A, 2 and 3, four of the five the volume teaches.
- The first half of the derivative-fingering chord sequence of Rhythm Accompaniment Part Two, plus natural harmonics, the muffled effect and the Latin beat.

## The one rule that matters most

Leavitt's own instruction for the **Reading Studies**, printed at the top of p. 64. It is a method rather than a suggestion, and it governs pp. 64–65, 74–75, 84–85 and 94–95 here — and pp. 102–103 in [[Course 28 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part Two]], which is the fifth and last of them.

> **DO NOT PRACTICE these two pages** — just READ them, but not more than twice through in any single practice session. Do not play them on two consecutive days. Do not go back over any particular section because of a wrong note. **DO KEEP AN EVEN TEMPO** and play the proper time values.

A Reading Study you have memorised is no longer a reading study. This is the one thing in the book destroyed by ordinary practice, and it is exactly the material this course exists to serve. Page 95 extends it: play the tonic chord first to set your ear, then try to *sing* the music to yourself as you play it.

Everything else in the course is practice material and should be practised. The Reading Studies are not.

## How to run it

- **Position is defined on p. 60**, and it is the most load-bearing page in the book. Position is the fret the **first finger** plays, written as a Roman numeral, and a position occupies four adjacent frets. Notes outside that span are reached by **finger stretch (F.S.)** — stretching the 1st or 4th finger *without shifting the hand*.
- **F.S. has a rule, not a preference.** For a scale tone the stretch follows the fingering type — type I stretches the 1st finger, type IV the 4th. For a non-scale tone, moving upward use F.S. 1 and moving downward F.S. 4, regardless of type. Page 61 adds the exception: always move *back into* a position from a stretch, never away from it.
- **No open strings from here on**, which is what makes every scale fingering movable — the same principle p. 45 applied to chords.
- **The chord track is half the book.** Rhythm Accompaniment Part Two starts on p. 67 and its forms are deliberately ordered so each new shape is a preparation for the next: *"Memorize the fingering for all chord structures in the order of their appearance… Do not skip around."*
- **The duets need a second part** — a recording, a teacher, or DAW playback of the other line. Both guitar parts are written to be studied.
- **Section One stays in rotation.** The first position does not maintain itself while the hand is at the second, and the triplet picking types from p. 37 are used again on p. 77.

## The lessons

### Lesson 1 — Fingering Types 1 and 1A in Second Position

**The material**

- **Pages 60–79.** Section Two opens. C major as fingering type 1 and F major as type 1A, both in the second position, each followed by its own eighth-note study, arpeggio study, chord etude and speed study.
- **The first two Reading Studies** arrive on pp. 64–65 and 74–75, under the rule above. The book's own safety net, p. 65: *"If unusual difficulty is encountered reading these pages, go back to page 60 and start again."*
- **Rhythm Accompaniment Part Two** begins on p. 67 with three basic forms, then runs derivatives off each of them across pp. 68, 71 and 77.
- **Right-hand technique for rhythm playing, p. 69** — a combined rotary forearm and loose wrist motion producing an *"explosive"* attack, with pressure release points and accents determining the beat.

**Homework**

- [ ] **Play** — the *C Major* scale, fingering type 1, 2nd position, pp. 60–61. Watch the F.S. marks; they are the point of the page.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Eighth Note Study* in C, p. 61.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Arpeggio Study (Broken Chords)* in C, p. 61, as picked and again with strict alternate picking.
- [ ] **Play** — *Chord Etude No. 1*, p. 62. Slowly and evenly, connecting the chords so they flow with no silence between them.
- [ ] **Play** — *Etude No. 5* (duet), p. 63. Both parts.
- [ ] **Read** — the *Reading Studies* in C major, fingering type 1, pp. 64–65. Twice through at most, never two days running, no going back for wrong notes.
- [ ] **Play** — *Ballad* (duet), pp. 66–67. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — *Movable Chord Forms (Rhythm Accompaniment, Part Two)*, p. 67 — basic forms #1, #2 and #3, chromatically up and down the neck, naming the roots.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Chord Forms* on p. 68, basic form #1 plus its seven derivatives, and the exercise under them.
- [ ] **Play** — *Rhythm Accompaniment: Right Hand Technique*, p. 69.
- [ ] **Play** — *Picking Etude No. 4*, p. 69. Third finger held down throughout.
- [ ] **Play** — the *F Major* scale, fingering type 1A, 2nd position, p. 70.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Eighth Note Study* and *Arpeggio Study* in F, p. 71, and the *Chord Forms* under them.
- [ ] **Play** — *Chord Etude No. 2*, p. 72. Rubato, as freely as you like.
- [ ] **Play** — *Another Duet in F*, p. 73. Both parts.
- [ ] **Read** — the *Reading Studies* in F major, fingering type 1A, pp. 74–75. Same rule.
- [ ] **Play** — *Play It Pretty* (duet), p. 76. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Chord Forms* on p. 77 and the *Triplet Study* below them, using both picking types from p. 37.
- [ ] **Play** — *Speed Study – fingering type 1*, p. 78, as written and with a dotted-eighth feel, with and without repeats.
- [ ] **Play** — *Speed Study – fingering type 1A*, p. 79, the same way.
- [ ] **Check** — a cold read of an unseen page at or below p. 79, once, at tempo, with no practice run.

### Lesson 2 — Types 2 and 3, Harmonics and the Latin Beat

**The material**

- **Pages 80–99.** G major as fingering type 2 and D major as type 3, both in the second position, plus the two guitar effects the volume teaches and its syncopation study.
- **Natural harmonics, pp. 82–83** — third finger laid lightly over the 12th fret, strings struck sharply, finger removed almost at the same instant. The 7th and 5th frets are the next most practical.
- **The muffled effect, p. 86** — right hand laid lightly along the top of the bridge, all sounding strings covered. It inhibits picking, so the part gets practised open first.
- **Syncopation, p. 92** — pick down on the beat and up on the "and" while it is still being counted; vary the picking for phrasing only once the off-beats are no longer a problem.

**Homework**

- [ ] **Play** — the *G Major* scale, fingering type 2, 2nd position, p. 80, and the *Eighth Note Study* under it.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Arpeggio Study* in G, p. 81, and the *Dotted 8th and 16th Study* below it, both as written and as a triplet figure.
- [ ] **Play** — *Waltz for Two* (duet), pp. 82–83. Both parts, ending on the harmonic.
- [ ] **Play** — *Presenting Natural Harmonics*, pp. 82–83, and find the same harmonics at the 7th and 5th frets.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Chord Forms* on p. 83 — diminished 7th, major 7th, 6th, 13th, 9th — and both exercises under them.
- [ ] **Read** — the *Reading Studies* in G major, fingering type 2, pp. 84–85. Twice through at most, never two days running. The second study continues without stopping in waltz time.
- [ ] **Play** — *Blues in G* (duet), p. 86. Practise the 1st guitar part open first, then with the muffled effect.
- [ ] **Play** — *Chord Etude No. 3*, p. 87, observing the position marks, which are what make it playable.
- [ ] **Play** — *Rhythm Accompaniment: Right Hand Technique*, p. 88, and its basic Latin beat, both written versions.
- [ ] **Play** — *Picking Etude No. 5*, p. 88. Fourth finger held down throughout.
- [ ] **Play** — *Short and Sweet* (duet), p. 89. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — the *D Major* scale, fingering type 3, 2nd position, p. 90, and the *Eighth Note Study* under it.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Arpeggio Study* in D, p. 91, and the *Chord Forms* below it — sus 4, minor 7th, minor 7th♭5 — with the exercise.
- [ ] **Play** — *Melodic Rhythm Study No. 1* (syncopation), pp. 92–93, as the optional duet with rhythm guitar on a Latin beat. Count it until you can feel it.
- [ ] **Play** — *Chord Etude No. 4*, p. 93, holding every note its full value.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Staccato – Legato* exercise, p. 93.
- [ ] **Read** — the *Reading Studies* in D major, fingering type 3, pp. 94–95. Same rule, plus p. 95's addition: play the tonic chord first, then sing the line to yourself as you play it.
- [ ] **Play** — *Dee - Oo - Ett* (duet), pp. 96–97. Both parts, and play the dynamics.

## Course final

A **performance check**, not a test page. There are no `type: school-test` pages in this course and nothing here enters the spaced-repetition drip — sight reading is a motor skill, and the drip is for facts.

The check is a rep. Open the book at an unseen page at or below p. 99, play it **once, at tempo, with no practice run**, and keep an even tempo through the wrong notes rather than stopping to fix them. Pass is your own honest read of whether it held together. Nothing in the system can hear the guitar, so nothing here pretends to grade it.

Reading decays the way ear training does. The course closing is not a reason to stop reading — it is the point at which [[Course 28 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part Two]] takes over, finishes the volume and hands the habit over to outside material.

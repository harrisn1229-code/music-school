# Sequential Prophet-600

## What it is
The [[Sequential Prophet-5]]'s affordable six-voice sibling — and a genuine piece of history: on stage at NAMM in January 1983, a Prophet-600 and a Roland JP-6 exchanged the **first-ever MIDI messages**. The instrument the connected studio was born on.

## What makes the sound
- **Same broad architecture as the P5** — two oscillators per voice into Curtis filters — but with **microprocessor-scanned (slower) envelopes** and simplified voicing: a slightly softer, hazier version of the Prophet sound; "P5 with the edges sanded" reads as *dreamy* rather than *punchy*.
- Poly-Mod survives (filter env/osc B to freq/PW) plus **unison mode** — six voices stacked into one enormous mono lead/bass the P5 can't match.
- Onboard polyphonic sequencer and arpeggiator — jam-machine features the flagship skipped.
- Modern retrofit kits (GliGli) replace the slow envelopes, but the classic records carry the original haze.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drift]] (soft-edged analog poly is exactly its register), [[Omnisphere]] Prophet-family sources, [[DUNE 3]] two-osc pads with slow envelopes.
- **Paid:** u-he Repro-5 or Arturia Prophet-5 V dialed softer covers it; no major dedicated 600 emulation.
- **Free:** Surge XT.

## Used on
- Genre/era: mid-80s synthpop/new wave B-lists and studio workhorse duty everywhere MIDI arrived — historically monumental, sonically under-credited; genre-level per the rule (its NAMM MIDI handshake is the documented claim to fame).

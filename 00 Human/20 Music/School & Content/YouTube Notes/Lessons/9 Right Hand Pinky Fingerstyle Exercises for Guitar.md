# 9 Right Hand Pinky Fingerstyle Exercises for Guitar

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C6q9U4Z-Mw0)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C6q9U4Z-Mw0)

## Synopsis

Jared (Sound Guitar Lessons With Jared) walks through nine drills he built for himself to bring the picking-hand **pinky finger (classical letter *c*, "chiquito")** into fingerstyle/classical playing — a finger almost nobody uses for plucking. He's explicit this is an exploration, not a "you must do this" technique: some players get great results using only p-i-m or p-i-m-a, and adding *c* is purely optional color. The nine exercises build in a deliberate order — calibration → warm-up integration → arpeggios → planting → block chords → tremolo → alternation → free improvisation — each one adding the pinky into a picking-hand skill he already had with the other four fingers. He closes by showing exactly where he used the pinky in his own "Georgia on My Mind" solo-guitar arrangement (available free in his arrangement pack), which is the practical payoff: passages that need all five right-hand digits firing on one chord to avoid a strum or a skipped string. Video carries no numbers/settings in a production sense — it's pure right-hand mechanics — but the drill sequence itself is very concrete and directly practice-able.

## What's taught — the 9 exercises (drill sequence)

Right-hand (picking-hand) finger labels used throughout (Spanish/classical convention): **p** = thumb, **i** = index, **m** = middle, **a** = ring, **c** = pinky ("chiquito").

1. **Right-hand calibration (all fingers).** Rotate up/down the open strings one finger at a time: **plant** (rest the fingertip lightly on the string, securing it) → **pluck** → move to the next string and repeat. Goal: consistent tone/volume/attack point per finger, not missed strings or flailing. Works with or without nails, any guitar. *(00:48–01:31)*
2. **a/c alternating calibration.** Same plant-then-pluck idea, but alternating ring (**a**) and pinky (**c**): pattern **c-a-c-a-c**, then reversed **a-c-a-c**. Isolates the a↔c coordination before adding more fingers. *(01:36–02:16)*
3. **Pinky/ring warm-up (grafted onto the standard left-hand permutation warm-up).** Jared's daily warm-up is left-hand finger permutations (1-2-3-4 chromatic patterns and all their orderings); to build pinky comfort he picked the **entire** warm-up with only **c-a alternation** (pinky-ring-pinky-ring…) instead of his usual picking-hand fingers, for an extended stretch. Free downloadable PDF of the base warm-up is linked in the video description. No prescribed nail angle/shape — it's genuinely idiosyncratic; if the pinky's tone doesn't match the other fingers, experiment with picking angle and nail shape (or play pinky nail-less) until it does. *(02:25–03:30)*
4. **p-i-m-a-c arpeggios across string groups.** Standard classical right-hand arpeggio pattern, now run through all five fingers: **p-i-m-a-c** ascending on strings **6-5-4-3-2**. Then rotate the string groups since there are 5 fingers but 6 strings: strings **6-4-3-2-1** (thumb skips the 5th/a-string), then hop the thumb up and do strings **5-4-3-2-1**. Cycle bottom-five-skip-a-string → top-five-skip-a-string. Add chord color: **Em** under the first grouping, **B7** under the second, to turn it into a real étude. Then reverse direction — same string groupings, **descending** — and finally chain **ascending→descending** (up-down-up-down) through both string groups with the chords under it. *(03:30–05:44)*
5. **Full-plant arpeggio version.** Same p-i-m-a-c arpeggio, but with the **full plant** technique: after the thumb plucks, **plant all four fingers (i-m-a-c) simultaneously** onto their strings before playing them individually, going **up**. Coming back **down**, plant **sequentially** (one finger at a time, as in ex. 4) — so the pattern is sequential-plant descending / full-plant ascending. Described as very hard but "quite secure feeling" once it clicks. Add an **Am** chord under it for the full musical version. *(05:44–06:51)*
6. **Five-note block chords.** Simply pluck all **five strings simultaneously** (p-i-m-a-c together) — any five-string combination, aiming for every string ringing at equal volume. Apply the same bottom-five/top-five-skip-a-string string groupings from ex. 4, two plucks each. Directly useful for arrangement/comping chord stabs and jazz voicings. *(06:51–07:25)*
7. **Tremolo with the pinky (six variations).** Tremolo = rapid repeated plucking on one string to fake a sustained note; Jared calls it one of the best plucking-hand conditioning drills, period, independent of whether you'll ever use it musically. Variations, all built around a repeating thumb pluck on a bass string:
   - Standard tremolo for reference: **p-a-m-i** (thumb, then ring-middle-index).
   - **Pinky tremolo: p-c-a-m** (thumb, then pinky-ring-middle) — the direct swap-in.
   - Same pattern **slow** and even (don't chase speed) while letting the top note ring, optionally arpeggiating a chord underneath or walking the thumb through other bass notes, or playing a moving bass-line melody (e.g. an A minor scale on the 3rd string) under the held tremolo note.
   - **4-finger tremolo, no thumb** — just i-m-a-c repeated, slow.
   - **4-note tremolo with thumb+pinky struck together** on the same beat (thumb and pinky fire simultaneously, then the tremolo continues) — his words: "a really fun one."
   - **5-note tremolo** (flamenco-style, not normally played with the pinky): **1-2-3-4-5** repeated across all five fingers.
   *(07:25–10:22)*
8. **Thumb + pinky alternating single notes.** Where players commonly alternate thumb+index or thumb+middle for single-note lines, alternate **thumb-pinky-thumb-pinky…** instead. Apply it to a scale (he demos a Sovia/Spanish-sounding minor scale) or blues-scale improvisation — the constraint is: only thumb and pinky, no other fingers touch a note. *(10:22–11:12)*
9. **Thumb pedal-tone + four-finger chord-shape improvisation (free-play, not a drilled exercise).** Thumb holds a repeating open bass note (low **E** or **A** string) while **i-m-a-c together** voice a chord/inversion on the top four strings. Concretely: play the open **Em7** shape (low E + open top 4 strings), then walk through its **inversions** up the neck with the four fingers while the thumb keeps pulsing the open E — add melody on top of the inversions for a chord-melody feel. Repeat the same idea on **A7** (open A string + top-four-string inversions). The "keep the thumb pedal going the whole time while the fingers move freely on top" version is flagged as the most musically useful of all nine drills. *(11:12–14:26)*

## Where it landed in a real arrangement

In Jared's own "Georgia on My Mind" solo-guitar arrangement, several early passages need all five right-hand fingers on one chord (an open **Em7** stab, an **A7** stab, and a **G13** voicing) — without the pinky he'd have had to strum or drop a note. He states plainly it's optional: you can play the arrangement without the pinky (skip/strum those spots) or use it — his free arrangement pack lets you choose. This is the concrete "why bother" payoff for exercises 4–9.

## What's taught

- **The 12 notes**, octave equivalence (why notes an octave apart share a name — the higher one's frequency is exactly double), and sharp/flat naming convention (sharp = up in pitch, flat = down).
- **Intervals** measured in semitones as a common "currency" (minor 3rd = 3 semitones, major 3rd = 4).
- **Triads from stacked 3rds**: major = major 3rd + minor 3rd; minor = minor 3rd + major 3rd (the reverse); diminished = minor 3rd + minor 3rd; augmented = major 3rd + major 3rd. "Tertiary harmony" named for this 3rd-stacking convention.
- **7th chords, symmetric grid**: dominant 7 (major triad + minor 3rd on top); major 7 (major triad + major 3rd on top); minor 7 (minor triad + minor 3rd on top); minor-major 7 (minor triad + major 3rd on top).
- **Major scale formula**: tone-tone-semitone-tone-tone-tone-semitone, from any starting note.
- **Natural minor**: major scale with ♭3, ♭6, ♭7. **Harmonic minor**: natural minor with the 7th raised back to natural. **Melodic minor** (ascending/jazz form): harmonic minor with the 6th also raised back to natural (so effectively "major with just a ♭3").
- **Pentatonic scales**: major pentatonic = major scale minus the 4th and 7th degrees; minor pentatonic = natural minor minus the 2nd and 6th degrees — in both cases, the removed degrees are specifically the ones that would otherwise create a semitone clash somewhere in the scale, making the 5-note result "foolproof" against dissonant note choices.
- **Blues scale** = minor pentatonic + an added ♭5.
- **Roman-numeral chord-progression analysis**: the same chord progression sounds identical regardless of key, so progressions are described by scale-degree Roman numerals (uppercase = major chord, lowercase = minor chord) rather than by absolute chord names — e.g. I-V-vi-IV describes the same shape whether played as C-G-Am-F or D-A-Bm-G.

# House Music Tutorial in Studio One in 15 Minutes | Weekly Challenge No. 04

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CIO3Z_a0gaA)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CIO3Z_a0gaA)

## Synopsis

Weekly Challenge #4: minimal house at 128 BPM, "mix as I go," speed-built in **Studio One** (a different DAW than Nate's Ableton Live — techniques below are DAW-agnostic). This is the most mix-craft-dense of the four videos in this batch — Max narrates concrete engineering decisions in real time: a velocity-discipline habit (always start maxed, only ever reduce), stereo-informed clap/snare layering, attack-vs-body kick compression for punch, 16th-note vs 8th-note swing, drum-bus glue compression rationale, a bass philosophy split between "childish simple" and "syncopated funky" house baselines, heavy sidechaining, a chord-extensions-vs-triads aggression tradeoff, a 4-bar "state then tail" phrase structure, percussion routed through a serial small→large reverb chain (echoing the tiered-reverb-for-realism logic already documented from the jazz-fusion breakdown), and an organ-derived synth-pad tone built by stripping harmonics from an organ patch.

## Techniques

**Workflow discipline**
- **Always program new MIDI at maximum velocity, then only ever reduce it — never worry about matching previous velocities.** A simple but effective habit: since the kick (and by extension every new part) will only ever get quieter relative to its max, starting everything at 127 removes an entire category of relative-loudness bookkeeping across a session. → [[Gain Staging]]
- **"Mix as I go" as a stated philosophy** — makes small mix decisions (filtering, compression, panning) immediately as each part is written rather than deferring all of it to a separate mixing pass.

**Drums**
- **Pick clap/snare layers with inherently good stereo information**, and filter out each layer's low end so only the wide, textural top content remains — stereo width comes from the *source material's* stereo image, reinforced by cutting mono-ish lows that would otherwise clutter the center. → [[Width and Stereo]]
- **Humanize every secondary snare/clap layer EXCEPT the main hit** — apply humanize/randomization to the non-primary layers so repeated hits get subtly different "click/clock" character each time, while the anchor hit stays consistent. → [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Panning convention: hats center, kick center, snare/clap wide** — a simple, explicit width map for a basic house kit.
- **Compress the KICK'S BODY while leaving the attack untouched, to widen the gap between transient and body for extra punch** — rather than compressing the whole kick (which reduces the attack/body contrast), target the sustain/body portion specifically so the attack reads comparatively louder and sharper; demonstrated live by progressively bringing the attack up and confirming the punchier result by ear. → [[Kick Design]], [[Compression]]
- **16th-note swing is a distinct, coarser feel from 8th-note swing — and it's silent unless something is actually programmed on that subdivision.** Studio One's (and Ableton's) swing amount shifts every second note of whichever grid resolution is selected; picking 16ths shifts 16th notes specifically for a tighter shuffle than 8th-note swing gives — but if nothing in the pattern lands on a 16th note, the swing setting has nothing to act on and will sound like it's "not working." → [[Rhythm and Groove]]
- **Drum-bus glue compression: the goal is simultaneous movement, not gain reduction per se** — compressing a full kit/bus together so "when one thing happens, it happens to all of them simultaneously" is the actual mechanism behind a glued, cohesive-sounding drum group — framed explicitly as the same underlying idea as sidechaining (one element's dynamics shaping others), just applied within the kit instead of across the whole mix. → [[Compression]], [[Sidechaining]]
- **Send percussion loops to a large, prominent shared reverb** — "a lot of the darker EDM genres" lean on big reverb tails ringing out specifically on percussion elements, more than on other parts of the kit.
- **Serial reverb routing on a texture layer: small room reverb feeding into a large hall reverb** — a nylon-guitar sample is sent to a small room reverb, which then also feeds into a larger reverb, rather than picking one size — a scaled-down version of the tiered small-room→large-hall reverb-layering system already documented from the jazz-fusion breakdown, applied here to a single texture element instead of a whole ensemble. → [[Reverb]]

**Bass**
- **Two house-bassline archetypes: "stupidly simple" (almost childish, functions as the melody, reads as aggressive/heavy — a metal-adjacent straight-ahead feeling) vs. syncopated/funky (16th-note patterns with swing, more complex)** — a useful two-bucket mental model for picking a bassline character to fit the track's intended energy. → [[Bassline Writing]]
- **State the bass idea fully in bar 1 of a 4-bar phrase, then only "tail" it (a shorter response figure) in the following bars** rather than restating the full idea — keeps a simple 4-bar loop from feeling flatly repetitive. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Filter envelope modulating a second sound-shaping parameter, not just cutoff** — map Envelope 2 to a patch's "character"/sound knob (in addition to whatever's driving the filter itself) so the bass's timbre shifts over the note's duration on two axes at once; mod wheel also mapped to filter for live control. → [[Bass Design]]
- **Chorus on a bass, despite the common "don't chorus your bass" advice** — a deliberate house-genre exception: house basslines take chorus well even though it's frequently advised against for bass in general (chorus typically destabilizes a mono low end). → [[Modulation]]
- **Chord extensions reduce a bassline's aggression; straight triads keep it hitting hard** — adding harmonic extensions on top of an aggressive bass pattern noticeably softens its intensity, while sticking to plain triads (or no chords at all) preserves the harder feel — a genuine tradeoff to make consciously depending on whether the goal is aggressive or "sophisticated." → [[House - EDM]]
- **Heavy sidechaining reaffirmed as essential to "the pumping sound"** — explicitly named as the missing ingredient when a bass pattern isn't sitting right yet.
- **A short vocal/synth phrase "dup and loop"-ed as a rhythmic hook element** — duplicate and loop a short recorded phrase directly into the arrangement as a repeating hook/texture, the same quick-and-dirty technique used for buildup material elsewhere in this channel's videos.

**Harmony**
- **Writing chord voicings by ear on paper without listening first is explicitly flagged as a bad idea** — tried live as a self-aware anti-pattern ("the worst thing... I would not recommend anybody does this"); the result was initially unusable ("garbage") until the voicings were simplified back down to straight triads — a cautionary data point that composing harmony fully blind, even for an experienced writer, doesn't reliably work.
- **Advanced slash-chord voicings over a non-root bass note** — worked examples: an E triad over an F bass note, a major7♯5 voiced over a different chord tone (not the root), and an F♯13(♯11)-type extended dominant — a non-root bass note doesn't have to be a simple inversion; it can support a genuinely different chord quality above it for a more "sophisticated" house harmonic color. → [[Chord Voicings]]
- **Repeat a chord hit ("this chord should hit twice") and revoice it on the second hit** rather than sustaining or repeating identically — a small voice-leading move that keeps a static harmonic moment from feeling flat.

## Sound/tone selection

- **An organ patch with its harmonics stripped out, reading as a soft, dark synth-like pad** — inspired by classical pipe-organ "tremors"/registration stops that remove most harmonic content, leaving a very soft, almost synth-like tone; applied here as a deliberate sound-design move (strip harmonics from an organ source) rather than starting from a synth patch directly. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Nylon guitar sample as a textural percussion-adjacent layer**, routed through a small-room-then-large-hall serial reverb chain rather than treated as a lead instrument.
- **Clap/snare layers chosen specifically for good inherent stereo information**, then low-filtered to keep only the wide top content.

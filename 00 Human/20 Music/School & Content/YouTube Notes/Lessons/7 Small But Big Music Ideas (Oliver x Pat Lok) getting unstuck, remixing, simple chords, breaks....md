# 7 Small But Big Music Ideas (Oliver x Pat Lok) [getting unstuck, remixing, simple chords, breaks...]

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjGd4m5E1y4)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjGd4m5E1y4)

## Synopsis
A short conversational interview between producers Oliver and Pat Lok (Sol State) that moves quickly through seven compact but high-leverage ideas about the mental and workflow side of making music, rather than any specific plugin or mix technique: where to find inspiration when stuck, how a remix actually gets started, why simpler chord voicings serve a song better than dense ones, how to hold a huge long-term goal alongside small doable daily actions, why both results-driven and pure-process sessions matter, why stepping away from a track resets your ears and turns you back into a listener, and why constantly bouncing out even throwaway sketches (plus being willing to release music you're not 100% sure about) is what actually builds a body of work and a recognizable sound over time.

## Techniques
- **Dig for inspiration when stuck** — when you hit a wall, go down the rabbit hole of listening to weird, interesting, out-of-your-lane music (Oliver name-checks a Frank Zappa binge); something in it — a sound, a chord, a texture — will spark, and that spark becomes the seed you carry back into your own track. — Oliver / Pat Lok (Sol State), [7 Small But Big Music Ideas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjGd4m5E1y4)
- **Start a remix with chords and a concept, not a stem-by-stem copy** — Pat Lok's remix process almost always begins by playing chords over the original and deciding on a direction first (fun dance record? nerdy/glitchy/weird flip?); if the original's production is already excellent, don't try to out-mix it — go somewhere completely different instead, and if it isn't, aim to just make the version you'd actually want to hear. — Oliver / Pat Lok (Sol State), [7 Small But Big Music Ideas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjGd4m5E1y4)
- **Simplify chord voicings so there's room for a vocal** — thick, fat 10-note chord voicings (the kind chased from gospel-piano tutorials) leave literally no frequency space for anyone to write a song on top; using just a few of the "three gospel moves" instead of the whole stack keeps the chord recognizable while opening up the room a vocal actually needs to sit in the mix. → [[Chords]], [[Masking and Carving]] — Oliver / Pat Lok (Sol State), [7 Small But Big Music Ideas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjGd4m5E1y4)
- **Pair one outrageous long-term dream with small daily-doable actions** — hold the most ridiculous, ambitious version of where your career/life could end up as the endgame, then break it into pieces small enough that each one individually feels achievable day to day; the accumulation is what makes the big dream start to feel real and attainable rather than just aspirational. — Oliver / Pat Lok (Sol State), [7 Small But Big Music Ideas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjGd4m5E1y4)
- **Deliberately toggle between results-oriented and process-oriented sessions** — some sessions are explicitly "I want a finished result from this," others are just messing around, experimenting, and learning with zero outcome pressure; both modes matter and neither should fully replace the other. — Oliver / Pat Lok (Sol State), [7 Small But Big Music Ideas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjGd4m5E1y4)
- **Walk away for a day or two to become the listener instead of the creator** — step back from a track entirely, come back with fresh ears, and you'll immediately hear what needs to happen because you've gained distance from it; working ears-fatigued just makes it easier to lose the plot. → [[Referencing and Translation]] — Oliver / Pat Lok (Sol State), [7 Small But Big Music Ideas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjGd4m5E1y4)
- **Bounce and file away everything, even 5-minute sketches, then go put yourself out there** — always bounce out ideas, even ones you spent almost no time on, and throw them in a folder; months later you'll rediscover one, not even remember making it, and be able to develop it into something ("that sounds like an Oliver thing"). Combine that habit with a willingness to release music while still unsure about it — a body of work and a recognizable sound only come from making (and putting out) a lot of music, including some that isn't great. → [[Sample Browser and Clips Workflow]] — Oliver / Pat Lok (Sol State), [7 Small But Big Music Ideas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wjGd4m5E1y4)

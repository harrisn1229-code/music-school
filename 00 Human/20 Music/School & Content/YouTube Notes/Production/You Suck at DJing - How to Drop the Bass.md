# You Suck at DJing - How to Drop the Bass

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZXdbYG-vo5g)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZXdbYG-vo5g)

## Synopsis

A short, technique-dense DJ-mixing lesson on the single biggest problem with blending two songs in Ableton: two basslines fighting for the same low-frequency real estate. The fix is a mapped EQ Three low-cut on each deck's return track, manually swapped in/out during the blend so only one song's bass is ever audible below ~250 Hz at a time.

## Techniques

- **Bass wants a monopoly below ~250 Hz — never let two songs' basslines overlap during a blend.** When mixing song A into song B, song A's bassline competing with song B's kick/bass creates a muddy low end; the fix is to remove one song's bass entirely for the overlap window rather than trying to EQ both down together. → [[Masking and Carving]], [[BASS Slot]]
- **Map a knob to EQ Three's low-gain on each deck's return track, ranged 0 dB (off) to −6 dB (full cut), never boosting.** Insert an EQ Three on both return tracks (before the Utility), MIDI-map a controller knob to the low band's gain on each, and constrain the mapped range to only ever *remove* bass (max −6 dB down to 0), never add it. Twisting the knob live pulls that deck's bass out; twisting it back restores it.
- **Also map the EQ's on/off toggle, not just the gain — bypass it entirely when not in use.** An always-on EQ Three colors the sound even at 0 dB gain; mapping the device's power toggle to the same knob range (e.g. on above ~1/3 travel) means the EQ is fully out of the signal path except during the actual bass-swap moment.
- **Practical use: as song B's bass fades in, manually pull song A's bass out via its mapped knob.** The two knobs (one per return/deck) are ridden in opposition during the blend — cut deck A's low end right as deck B's kick/bass becomes the dominant low-frequency element, so there's never a moment with two competing basslines.

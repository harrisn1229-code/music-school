# One-Note Chord Embellishments

Small, single-finger changes to open/basic chord shapes that upgrade a plain triad into a more colorful voicing, plus a rule for finding a safe spot to use a maj7♯11 chord. The whole method: take a chord shape you already know, move or add exactly one note, and the chord gets more interesting without changing its basic function.

## The embellishments

- **E minor → E minor add9.** Move the note on the D string's 2nd fret up two frets (to the 4th fret). Safe substitute wherever E minor appears alongside C, G, or D — a low-risk swap-in since only one note changes from the plain triad.
- **D (C-shape, capo'd up two frets) → Dadd4.** A common, pleasant substitute for D specifically in the key of G or D — works with progressions like G–C–D, but isn't universally safe outside those keys.
- **C → Csus2.** Lift the middle finger off the standard open C shape.
- **A minor → A minor add9.** Instead of fretting the open A shape's standard 3rd-fret note, use the pinky one fret up to add the 9th.
- **F → Fadd9, or Fmaj9 with the open high E added.** Lift the middle finger off the standard F shape for add9; include the open high E string on top for the fuller maj9 color.
- **G → G6/9.** From the standard open G, replace the fretted note with the middle finger back down in a different spot, then add the open high E string.

## The "Hallelujah" progression reveal

The worked progression through the video — Em(add9), Dadd4, Csus2, C (repeated), then G7, resolving to Csus2 — is, note for note, the chord progression of "Hallelujah." Framed as proof the whole method works: take chords already known, embellish with one-note changes, and even a famous progression is really just a handful of familiar shapes lightly dressed up.

## Placing a maj7♯11 chord safely in a key

A **maj7♯11** chord (worked as Cmaj7♯11 in the key of G) is safely usable wherever the key's **IV chord** sits — find the IV by counting up a 4th from the key's root (trivial on guitar since the instrument is tuned in 4ths: same string-set shape, moved up). Worked progression in G: Esus2 (i.e. an E-rooted sus2 shape) → Cmaj7♯11 (the IV of G, root moved from E to C) → Bm → Em(add9) (or a plain Em) → F♯° — Cmaj7♯11 functions as the IV-chord color substitution in that context. Rule generalizes to any key: identify the IV chord, and a maj7♯11 built on that root will sit safely in the progression.

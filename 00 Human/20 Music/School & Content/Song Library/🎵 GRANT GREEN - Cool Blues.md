## Chords

**Key:** B♭ · **Form:** 12-bar blues. "Cool Blues" is a Charlie Parker composition (a "Bird blues" head) — Nathan Borton's breakdown of Green's *Born to Be Blue* solo works off three named chord targets rather than a bar-by-bar chart: **B♭7 (I) → E♭7 (IV) → F7 (V)**, i.e. the standard **I7 – IV7 – V7** blues skeleton. → [[Blues Form and Harmony]]

## Solo breakdown (Nathan Borton's concept breakdown)

- **5-to-1 lines at the I→IV move.** Single-note lines outlining a V→I pull, used at the B♭7→E♭ transition to create forward momentum rather than static chord-to-chord playing. → [[Guide Tones]], [[Blues Form and Harmony]]
- **Altered 5-to-1 lines (9, ♭9, ♯9) for a stronger pull.** Two further phrases lean on altered dominant tensions specifically because they pull harder to the target than the plain version. → [[Altered Chords]]
- **2-equals-5/5-equals-2 over F7: soloing C minor (F7's related ii) straight over the sounding F7.** Up the C Dorian scale, then a descending 5-3-2-1 pattern — no F7 arpeggio played at all, just C minor language, and it "sounds awesome" over the dominant. → [[Chord Substitution]] § 4, [[The ii-V-I]]
- **5-to-1 mixed into its related ii, recurring at bar 8 nearly every chorus.** A form-anchored combination move, not a one-off. → [[Turnarounds]]
- **Tonic dominant over a sitting E♭7 — the 9th and 13th as stable, resolved-sounding melodic content.** Treats the static dominant as "home," not tension. Two techniques: a **D♭ major 7 arpeggio** (the maj7 arpeggio starting on the ♭7 of E♭7, spelling its own ♭7-9-11-13), and the **dominant bebop scale** (landing the added chromatic passing tone into the 13th). → [[Bebop Vocabulary]] § 1, [[Extended Chords]], [[Blues Form and Harmony]] § *Why every chord is a dominant 7th*
- **Walk-ups/walk-downs — chromatic connectors between chord tones.** Anchored root, chromatic walk-down from the ♭7 to the 5th (reversible: 5th→♭7 ascending); also a 3rd→5th walk-up elsewhere in the solo. → [[Blues Lead Phrasing and Touch]]
- **Classic, movable blues-opening vocabulary.** The solo's own opening phrase, stated to work over any dominant or major chord in the form (B♭7, E♭7, or F7) unmodified.
- **The Kansas City blues scale.** A named five-note distillation of the blues scale, guitaristic and movable, with an emphasis on the 6th degree — used to riff and generate melodic ideas. Exact interval formula gated to the creator's paid lesson, not in the free captions. → [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]] § *Kansas City blues scale*

## Steal this

- **Alter a 5-to-1 line (9/♭9/♯9) any time you want the resolution to feel harder-earned** — the same device as a plain guide-tone line, just with more pull.
- **Solo a dominant chord's related ii minor straight over the dominant itself** — no ii chord has to actually sound; it's purely a soloing-vocabulary reframe of any V7.
- **On a static dominant that isn't resolving, reach for its 9th/13th (or the maj7-arpeggio-off-the-♭7 shortcut) instead of running its Mixolydian scale plain** — the extensions are what make an unmoving dominant sound settled rather than stuck.
- **Any classic blues opening phrase is worth testing over all three chords of the form**, not just the one it was first played over — dominant/major vocabulary transfers more freely than it seems like it should.

# Yesterday — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** the shorter two-bridge model at an uncharacteristically slow tempo. Intro (**2 bars** of guitar on an open fifth, **no third**, so major or minor is withheld from the start) · Verse (**7 bars**, three phrases grouped 3 + 2 + 2) · Verse · Bridge (8 on paper) · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Outro (one reprise of the final phrase, hummed). The bridge does not sound like its notation: each phrase is heard as starting on the notated *second* bar, with the first functioning as a wind-up tacked onto the previous phrase.

Under the over-familiarity is a genuinely asymmetrical song whose engine is a home key that keeps sliding toward its relative minor ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]). The music retreats to D minor **before F is ever properly established** — the vi chord in bar 3 is the first moment anything feels grounded. Secondary dominants carry that pull: V-of-vi at the top of both verse and bridge, V-of-V near the verse's end ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]) — and that V-of-V then hands off to **IV** rather than V, softened by a descending chromatic inner line.

The **only clean V–I cadences in the song are the bridge phrase endings** ([[HAR-C02 — Authentic cadence]]), and because the bridge starts from the relative minor's point of view they land more like a modulation arriving than a return home.

Layering discipline: **the bowed strings do not enter until verse 2**, and no section ever gets an identical quartet part twice. The cello's E♭ in the bridge is the only flat seventh in the entire song.

# Block Chord Melody Guitar Lesson - In the style of Wes Montgomery

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yW3bCKXmvgg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yW3bCKXmvgg)

## Synopsis

A guest lesson on Jared Borkowski's channel from Nathan Borton (already a heavily-cited source in this vault's jazz coverage — see [[Chord Melody Arranging]]), teaching **Wes Montgomery-style block-chord melody harmonization** using drop 2/drop 3 voicings, worked over the A section and bridge of "All The Things You Are." The method is a distinct, complementary device to the vault's existing chord-melody coverage: rather than the "which chord tone is the melody note, choose/break-up the voicing" approach already documented, this video's core mechanism is **cycling a single drop-voicing shape through its own chord-tone inversions** so the melody note always lands on top, plus a specific fallback (a diminished chord) for melody notes that fall outside the underlying chord's arpeggio. Recommended listening: Wes Montgomery's "Gone with the Wind" from *The Incredible Jazz Guitar of Wes Montgomery*.

## What's taught

- **Drop 2 and drop 3 voicings, identified by which string the root falls on.** Root on the A string → drop 2 voicing; root on the low E string → drop 3 voicing. Presented as prerequisite vocabulary, root-position shapes for each given directly.
- **Inversion-cycling method (the core new device): to move a drop voicing through its inversions, take each note of the block chord and move it up to the next note of that same chord's arpeggio, one note at a time, repeating the process until the shape returns to its starting form.** Worked example on Cmaj7 drop 2 (C-G-B-E, i.e. root-5th-7th-3rd rearranged for the guitar): first note (C) moves to E, next note (G) moves to B, next (B) moves to C, last (E) moves to G — yielding the next inversion. Do this for every chord quality, and for both drop 2 and drop 3 voicing families (video demonstrates drop 2 only "for time's sake").
- **The chord-melody harmonization method, step by step:**
  1. Play the melody note as the top note, with the chord's own root in the bass, for each melody note over its underlying chord.
  2. Check whether the melody note is a chord tone (part of that chord's arpeggio). If yes, fill in the middle notes with the matching inversion of the drop-2 (or drop-3) voicing found via the cycling method above — worked example: melody note A♭ over an Fm7 chord is the ♭3rd of Fm7, so it gets the root-position Fm7 drop-2 voicing.
  3. **If the melody note is NOT a chord tone of the underlying chord, harmonize it with a diminished chord instead** (voiced with roots available on the low E, A, and D strings — learn all three root positions). This is the video's explicit fallback rule for "orphan" melody notes.
  4. Not every note needs to be harmonized — if a melody note sits too low on the neck to voice comfortably under a full chord, it's fine to leave it un-harmonized (single note only).
  5. **Add musical flair with a call-and-response device**: after harmonizing a melody note, insert an additional voicing of the *same* underlying chord right after it (a second voicing "answering" the first), and/or approach the next chord with a chromatic passing chord — described explicitly as "the melody is the call and the other chords are the answer."
- **Worked over "All The Things You Are"** (A section first, then the bridge, which has more melody notes and more non-chord-tones requiring the diminished-chord fallback) — full harmonization walkthrough demonstrated in real time in the video, not reproduced note-by-note here (see the video itself or [[🎵 JEROME KERN - All The Things You Are]] for the tune's own chord changes).

# 90% Of Guitarists DON'T Practice This! (A HUGE Mistake...)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XmUBeU8vW80)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XmUBeU8vW80)

## Synopsis

A subdivision-switching timing exercise built over a real cadence (Emadd9 → G7 → Dsus2): one measure of 16th notes, one measure of 8th-note triplets, back to 16th notes, with the actual practice goal being locking into the new subdivision immediately at the switch rather than settling into it gradually.

## Techniques

- **The core timing skill: land immediately in time on the new note value right at the switch**, not drift/search for correct spacing over the first half of the new measure — named as the actual practice target, independent of the specific lick or tempo.
- **Worked cadence:** Emadd9 (16th notes, constant alternate picking, arpeggio spread out rather than a stock box shape, includes the 9th) → G7 (8th-note triplets, alternate picking into a sweep-picked tail) → Dsus2/D major (16th notes, alternate picking into legato and a touch of hybrid picking).
- **Chord-tone-outlining as an ear trick:** the lick's notes are chosen so a listener can hear the underlying chord progression from the guitar solo alone, with no other instruments — deliberately including each chord's characteristic tones (root/3rd/5th/color tones like the 9th) rather than a generic scale run.
- **Build-your-own version recommended**, using any short chord progression in a key of choice, applying the same one-measure-per-subdivision-switch structure (16ths → triplets → 16ths, or reversed, or a full quarter→eighth→16th "written pyramid" progression) — scale the difficulty to current level, since the note-value switch is the skill being drilled, not any particular tempo or chord set.

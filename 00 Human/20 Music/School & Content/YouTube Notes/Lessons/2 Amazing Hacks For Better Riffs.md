# 2 Amazing Hacks For Better Riffs! (Guitar Lesson)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-3RtvBk-8_8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-3RtvBk-8_8)

## Synopsis

A single riff in 7/8, used to teach two stackable rhythm skills: subdividing an odd meter into small groups instead of counting straight through, and using continuous alternate picking with dead notes to make down/up-stroke placement automatic in rhythmically dense, rest-filled passages.

## What's taught

- **7/8 explained via the 4/4 comparison:** a measure in 4/4 has 4 quarter notes' worth of total value to fill any way; 7/8 works identically but with 7 eighth notes — it sounds "unstable" specifically because the ear expects one more eighth note to resolve it into a familiar 4/4 or 8/8 grouping.
- **Hack 1 — never count an odd meter straight through (1-2-3-4-5-6-7); break it into small sub-groups (2s and 3s) instead.** Worked example: this riff's 7 eighth notes are grouped 2-2-3, mirrored to 3-2-2 in the second half of a two-measure phrase. Practical rule: start each small group with a downstroke, so the first note of every group gets a natural accent — this is what makes odd-meter playing feel controlled rather than counted-and-hoped-for.
- **Hack 2 — when a rhythmic pattern has a lot of rests, fill them with dead (muted) notes and keep continuous alternate-picking motion going, rather than trying to consciously plan every down/up stroke.** Reasoning: at speed, it's very hard to pre-plan pick-stroke direction around syncopated rests; keeping the picking hand in constant up/down motion (playing a dead note instead of a rest) lets the correct down/up placement emerge naturally from the continuous motion, and you memorize where the strokes land through repetition rather than by calculation.
- **Combined payoff:** once the wrist is moving continuously (Hack 2) and the phrase is felt as small counted groups with downstroke accents (Hack 1), a riff that looks intimidating in an odd meter becomes playable and — his stated point — a great source of creative, non-generic-sounding rhythmic ideas.

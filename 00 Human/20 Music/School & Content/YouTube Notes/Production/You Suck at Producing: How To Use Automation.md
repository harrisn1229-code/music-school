# You Suck at Producing: How To Use Automation

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZFXrs2-s_YI)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZFXrs2-s_YI)

## Synopsis
An intro-level Ableton automation lesson taught through one running example: gradually filtering in a low-passed background layer over a song's intro instead of letting it enter all at once. Underbelly first performs the fade by hand (turning the Auto Filter's Frequency knob live while the transport plays), which turns out to already have written an automation curve for that exact motion — the video then explains the visual grammar around that: Arrangement View (not Session View) is where automation lanes live, the pink line on a track mirrors a device parameter over time, hovering it highlights it solid, clicking creates a breakpoint, and dragging a second breakpoint up/down draws a ramp between them. The most genuinely useful mechanic covered is what happens when you touch an automated parameter outside of editing it deliberately — the current breakpoint dot goes gray to flag that you've deviated from the written automation, and a small orange "back to automation" arrow appears above the track/device to snap it back.

## Techniques
- **Automation only lives in Arrangement View** — switch to it (the icon showing horizontal lines, next to Session View's grid icon) to see or create automation lanes at all; there's no timeline for this in Session View. → [[Automation]]
- **Turning a knob while the transport plays records an automation curve for that motion** — riding a device's parameter (here, Auto Filter's Frequency) live while the song plays writes the exact movement as automation; no separate "record automation" step is needed beyond just moving the knob during playback. → [[Automation]], [[Auto Filter]]
- **The pink automation line + breakpoints are the whole editing language** — the pink line on an automated track mirrors the parameter's value over time; hovering it turns it solid/highlighted; clicking it drops a breakpoint at that time/value; a second click further along, dragged up or down, draws a ramp between the two points — this is how a hand-performed sweep gets manually redrawn or extended afterward. → [[Automation]]
- **The gray-dot override + orange "back to automation" button** — nudging an automated parameter outside of deliberately editing its automation makes the current breakpoint turn gray, Ableton's way of flagging that the live value has strayed from what's written; clicking the small orange arrow that appears above the device/track snaps the parameter back to the automated value without having to manually re-match it by ear. → [[Automation]]

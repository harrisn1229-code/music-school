# All My Loving — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

This is the essay where Pollack sets out his working distinction between **bridge and refrain** — hook inside the verse means the middle section is a bridge (contrast and respite); hook elsewhere means that elsewhere is a refrain (the payoff, with key-confirming harmony) ([[MEL-C07 — What makes a hook]]). The song is the awkward case that has both.

**Form.** No intro, no downbeat to give Paul his pitch, and a non-tonic first chord — a device they liked enough to reuse repeatedly. A 16-bar verse in two parallel 8-bar phrases (first open on V, second closed on I), an 8-bar refrain built from one 4-bar phrase twice, an 8-bar bridge for the guitar solo, and an outro extending the refrain.

**Harmony.** Chords change almost every bar and never sit longer than two — hyperactive by their 1963 standards ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). Verse bars 3–8 run a chain of descending thirds instead of a circle of fifths, and the ♭VII in bar 7 acts as a **connector between ii and V**, motivated by a rising inner line ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]). The refrain's best detail is a rootless augmented chord — a line cliché falling out of an inner voice descending C♯–C♮–B — held a full bar so you can't miss it. The outro repeats the refrain's vi–I but strips the augmented chord out, and that subtraction is what reads as an ending.

**Why both sections.** Pollack's thought experiment: delete the refrain and there's no hook; delete the bridge and the refrain is exposed as giving no harmonic confirmation, no textural contrast and no change of pace. The bridge supplies all three, above all a **slower harmonic rhythm** — which is why the solo needed its own changes rather than the verse's ([[ARR-C02 — How to actually write the B section]]).

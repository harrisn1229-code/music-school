# VOI-C04 — Octave shift, and the collision fix

**Q.** Give the method for turning one chord shape you already know into new voicings of the same chord, and the rule that keeps it from dead-ending.

> [!question]- Answer
> **Move one note of the shape up or down an octave.** If that lands two notes on the same string, **move the newly-colliding note to its own nearest octave** — and cascade that fix until every note has its own string. Repeat from the shape you land on. Some results are unplayable; discard those and move a different note.

## Why it matters

It is the mechanism behind close and open position rather than a name for them: a close voicing becomes an open one exactly by lifting an interior note an octave and leaving the gap. So instead of memorising a voicing chart you can generate the chart. Moving the root of a close C major up an octave gives another close inversion; moving the middle note up instead gives the open 1-5-3.

## Drill

15 min: take one chord shape you play automatically, move each note in turn up an octave, resolve every collision, and keep the two results you like. Name what family each one landed in.

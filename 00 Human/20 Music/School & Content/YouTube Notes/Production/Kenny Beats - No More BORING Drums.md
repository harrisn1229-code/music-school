# Kenny Beats: No More BORING Drums

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAUfaggMYzM)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAUfaggMYzM)

## Synopsis
A viewer asks why their drums feel "flat and generic" despite good sound selection, and Kenny narrows it to two culprits: lack of swing (the biggest one) and, less often, sound choice. He walks through the mechanics of manual swing — leave the kick quantized, nudge the snare a touch early, nudge the hat a touch late, the same move that turns a straight "1-2-3-4" into a Dilla/MPC-style triplet-leaning bounce — a sidechain trick for gluing a whole group of non-kick elements to the kick's hit for "vibe" rather than just masking control, and a timing rule for where the main melodic element (sample, synth, or MIDI part) should sit relative to the kick and 808. Short clip, dense with actionable detail. It overlaps in theme with [[Kenny Beats - Making Trap Drums More Interesting]] (which also touches "swing it, break the grid") but goes considerably deeper on the actual swing mechanics, and it complements the vault's existing swing/pocket coverage in [[Rhythm and Groove]] and [[Pocket and Microtiming]] — those pages are sourced from other creators (You Suck at Producing, Max Konyi); this is Kenny's own version of the same idea, worth having directly from him.

## Techniques
- **Lack of swing is the #1 cause of flat/generic drums** — Kenny's diagnosis for "good sound selection, drums still feel flat": almost always missing swing, occasionally sound choice; even house/techno wants a little swing on the hats, not just hip hop. → [[Rhythm and Groove]] — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [Kenny Beats: No More BORING Drums](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAUfaggMYzM)
- **Manual swing recipe: kick stays quantized, nudge the snare a touch AHEAD, nudge the hat a touch BEHIND** — a starting formula for finding a bounce rather than a single "correct" swing amount; move things off the grid by ear, either with a per-element millisecond nudge in Ableton or by literally dragging notes off-grid, and listen for the pocket. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Rhythm and Groove]] — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [Kenny Beats: No More BORING Drums](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAUfaggMYzM)
- **The Dilla/MPC bounce is swing pushed toward a triplet feel** — going from a straight "1-2-3-4" toward "1-2-3" and letting the fourth subdivision lean into triplet spacing is what produces that specific loose, rocking bounce; it's a destination on the swing dial, not a separate technique. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]] — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [Kenny Beats: No More BORING Drums](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAUfaggMYzM)
- **Group non-kick elements and sidechain the whole group to the kick for "swell," not just masking control** — on the beat he uses as the example, almost everything except the kick (and one FX element) sits inside one Group with a big sidechain keyed off the kick, so the whole group swells to every kick hit; framed as a vibe/glue move, not a problem-fix. A lighter version: sidechain just the hats/perc to the kick so the kick and 808 stay present without the percussion getting buried, even if that percussion is already swung "a little weird." → [[Sidechaining]] — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [Kenny Beats: No More BORING Drums](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAUfaggMYzM)
- **Line up your keys/samples/melody to your drums, not just your drums to each other** — once the drums are swung, look at where the first kick hits and where the snare lands, then place notes in the keys/sample/MIDI part deliberately ahead of or behind those drum hits instead of leaving them dead-on; treat the melodic material as another element to offset, the same way you're offsetting snare/hat. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Melody Writing]] — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [Kenny Beats: No More BORING Drums](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAUfaggMYzM)
- **Rule of thumb: put the main sound of the beat slightly BEHIND the kick/808 hit, not ahead of it** — when the kick and 808 land together, the main melodic element (sample, synth, or MIDI part) sitting a touch behind them reads as intentional and cohesive; the same element landing ahead of the kick/808 instead reads as clashed and off, even at the same offset amount. Direction matters more than the amount. → [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Bassline Writing]] — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [Kenny Beats: No More BORING Drums](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAUfaggMYzM)
- **Rotate sample packs/808s instead of running one kit into the ground** — even a genuinely good 808 (his example: Spinz's 808) goes stale after around 20 beats on it; keep loading in fresh sound sources rather than trusting sound selection alone to carry a beat. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]] — Kenny Beats (Sol State), [Kenny Beats: No More BORING Drums](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAUfaggMYzM)

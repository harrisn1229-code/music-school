## Synopsis

Builds on a prior "groups of 5" pentatonic lesson (not in the vault) by extending the odd-note-grouping idea to a 3-note-per-string scale: play groups of 7 over a regular 16th-note pulse in the classic A minor scale position, using economy picking (two consecutive downstrokes) rather than strict alternate picking where the phrase naturally calls for it.

## What's taught

- **Odd note grouping as a de-clicheing device.** Common licks fall into groups of 2, 3, or 4 (matching the underlying pulse); deliberately grouping notes in 5s or 7s instead — while still playing in time, still 16th notes — immediately sounds more creative even in a completely standard scale position, because the accent pattern no longer lines up with the expected subdivision.
- **Worked example: a 7-note pattern in the 3-note-per-string A minor scale**, starting on the 5th fret low E string (root A), moved identically through every scale position/string by sliding the same 7-note shape.
- **Economy picking emerges from the note count, not a separate decision.** Because the phrase naturally ends each 7-note group on a downstroke, the next group's first note is also a downstroke — rather than force strict alternation, let two downstrokes happen in a row (staying in a "sweep picking mindset" for that transition) to keep the motion smooth at speed.
- **Accent the first note of each group** to keep the timing legible to the listener (and to yourself) — without the accent, an odd grouping just sounds arbitrarily off-time rather than deliberately displaced.
- **Practice assignment given directly:** don't just drill this one lick — try odd-number groupings (not just 5 or 7) in scales/patterns you already know, as a general creativity tool.

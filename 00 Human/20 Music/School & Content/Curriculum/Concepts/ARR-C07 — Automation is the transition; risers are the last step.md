# ARR-C07 — Automation is the transition; risers are the last step

**Q.** A transition sounds stitched together. Why is reaching for a riser the wrong first move?

> [!question]- Answer
> Because it papers over a transition that doesn't work. An analysis of ~100 reference tracks found most weren't leaning on reverse cymbals, swooshes or snare rolls at all — those are icing once parts already connect. The real technique happens earlier: **automating existing devices** (filters, detune, sends, wet/dry) *across* the section so one part flows into the next. Three shapes: a **micro drop** (instant filter wet/dry snap plus a gradual percussion sweep), a **macro drop** (max-contrast section change via kick filtering, bass removal, slight detune, timbre shift), and a **wet/dry drop** (send automation contrasting wet-vs-dry and wide-vs-mono instead of tone).

## Why it matters

It relocates the transition from an inserted object to a property of the two sections. Also worth mining your own elements — a bassline repurposed as a sweep — before importing a sample.

## Drill

Take a transition that isn't working. Delete every riser and impact. Fix it with filter and send automation only.

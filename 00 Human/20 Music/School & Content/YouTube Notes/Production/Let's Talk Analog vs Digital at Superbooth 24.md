## Synopsis

A "vox pop" survey at Superbooth '24 (the big Berlin synth-industry trade show) — Anthony asks random attendees a two-word association game: "what do you think of when I say digital / analog?" Mostly personal free-association rather than technical content, but a couple of interviewees with real design credentials (a Haken engineer, working synth designers) contribute one genuinely substantive technical point, folded into the existing analog/digital philosophy note rather than repeated here as a standalone extraction.

## Techniques

Not really applicable — this is an opinion survey, not an instructional video. The one substantive point, from a Haken engineer discussing the Continuum: even a "digital" instrument is analog at both ends of its signal path — the hall-effect sensor data reading finger position is fundamentally analog, requiring careful conversion to digital and back to analog output without degrading fidelity either direction; the hardest engineering work in a "digital" instrument often lives at those conversion boundaries. Several other synth-hardware designers interviewed independently described analog circuit design as genuinely difficult to execute well, and described their own work as managing how much analog to use within an otherwise digital signal path rather than treating either domain as an automatic goal.

For reference, the range of personal associations gathered: digital → computers, fingers, glitch, resolution, perfection, clipping at 0dB, single-processing, "modern"; analog → organic, warm, history, physicality, Moog, vinyl, windage, "terribly detuned in certain temperatures," round, raw.

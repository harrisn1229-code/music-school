# Korg Mini Pops

## What it is
Korg's family of preset analog rhythm boxes — home-organ accompaniment machines (rhumba! bossa! swing!) from before "drum machine" meant anything cool. The Mini Pops 7 became legendary anyway: soft, charming analog ticks that one French synthesist rode to 18 million records.

## What makes the sound
- **Fully analog preset voices**: bridged-T resonant pings (bongo/conga/kick), filtered noise (snare/hats/cymbal "pshh"), all tiny, round, and lo-fi — the kit equivalent of a music box.
- **Preset patterns only** — but pressing multiple rhythm buttons at once combines them into hybrid grooves (the Jarre trick: "Oxygène" runs stacked presets), and the quirky pattern designs are half the charm.
- Delicate cowbell/ride/cabasa voices with no low end at all — sits *on top of* a mix as texture → [[TEXTURE Slot]], and already flagged in the [[Sound Selection Guide]] (Howard from Disclosure's favourite for delicate percussion).
- Tempo slider + a few voice level knobs; that's the whole interface. Commitment as a feature.

## Digital versions
- **Owned:** [[Drum Rack]] / [[Impulse]] with Mini Pops samples; [[Cassette]]/[[RC-20 Retro Color]] deepen the vintage haze; [[Echo]] for the Jarre space.
- **Paid:** UVI Beat Box Anthology 2; Samples From Mars-style archives; Korg's own iOS/plugin recreations appear in their Gadget/Collection line.
- **Free:** Mini Pops sample sets are classic freeware fare.

## Used on
- Jean-Michel Jarre — *Oxygène* (1976): the Mini Pops 7 carries the whole record's rhythm, stacked-preset trick and all.
- Disclosure — delicate cowbell/ride/cabasa layers (per the Sound Selection Guide's `/music-enrich` notes).
- Genre/era: 70s home-organ pop and chanson, then ambient/electronica's favorite "naive" rhythm texture.

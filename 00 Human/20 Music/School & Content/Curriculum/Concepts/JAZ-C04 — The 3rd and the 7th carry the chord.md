# JAZ-C04 — The 3rd and the 7th carry the chord

**Q.** Which two notes state a chord's quality, why those two rather than the root and the 5th, and what is the one exception?

> [!question]- Answer
> **The 3rd and the 7th.** The 3rd says major or minor; the 7th says major, dominant or minor. Together they name the chord.
>
> The root tells you nothing about quality and the 5th tells you almost nothing, which is why the 5th is the first note dropped from a voicing. **The exception is m7♭5** — Cm7♭5 and Cm7 share E♭ and B♭, so only the ♭5 separates them, and a voicing that drops it states the wrong chord.

## Why it matters

The highest-leverage idea in the strand. Two notes per chord are enough to state a whole progression, which is why a bass player and a horn can imply the harmony with no chordal instrument present at all.

It is also a mix tool. When the mids are crowded, cutting the chordal instrument down to guide tones frees space without losing any harmony — the 5th and the doubled root were the notes doing the crowding.

## Drill

Write out only the 3rds and 7ths of a tune's changes as two horizontal lines, before voicing anything. Then play just those two lines and check the progression is still recognisable.

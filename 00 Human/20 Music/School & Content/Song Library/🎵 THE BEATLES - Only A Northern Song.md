# Only A Northern Song — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro · Verse (**19 bars**) · Verse · Refrain (**15 bars**, 4+6+5) · Verse (instrumental) · Refrain · Verse · Refrain (instrumental) · Outro (fade). Pollack parses the whole thing in 2/4 just to make the uneven phrasing notatable; the refrain's third phrase opens with an interpolated bar of 3/4, the same trick as "Strawberry Fields Forever".

The lyric's complaint about chords going wrong is painted obviously by the noise layer on top, but the **erratic harmonic rhythm and constantly shifting phrase lengths do the same job underneath**, which is the more interesting half. The verse is glacial — eight bars on a single chord — and its only body English is the last line's chord change arriving **a full bar early** ([[POP-C01 — Harmonic rhythm]]). Shape-wise it opens toward V and then backs off to IV, a cadence set up and declined.

The refrain does the opposite, changing chords every bar or two and making a genuine, forced **modulation to ii (B minor)** rather than a passing tonicisation ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]), carried across by a functional pivot and by the note B held in common through all four chords of that phrase.

Over an organ/bass/drums base sits a stylised **noise track** — trumpets, metallic percussion, piano doodling — that probably never stops once it starts and is simply ridden on the fader. The rule is worth internalising: it is faded **down at the start of sections and up at their ends** ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]).

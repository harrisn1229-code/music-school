# 'Jazz Chords' In Metal? How To Spice Up Boring Riffs! (Chord Theory Lesson For Metal)

## Synopsis

A chord-theory lesson (BERNTH's own background is jazz guitar study layered onto a rock/metal foundation) on extending basic power chords with 7ths, 9ths and beyond to build "jazz chord"-flavored voicings for metal riffs, reharmonizing a plain chord progression as the practical example throughout.

## Techniques

- **Foundation:** metal riffs mostly use basic intervals — perfect 5th (power chord), perfect 4th, minor 3rd (minor color), major 3rd (major color), minor 6th, etc. — study these before building complex voicings.
- **Add the 7th first:** major 7th for major chords, minor 7th for minor chords — these are already what most players mean by "jazz chords," giving an immediate tension/color shift over a plain triad. Demoed directly in a II-V-I with basic triads vs. the same progression with maj7/min7/dom7 chords.
- **Worked E minor voicing (no root needed — bass/rhythm guitar covers it):** perfect 5th (B) + 9th (F♯) + minor 3rd (G) on top. The suspenseful color comes from the 9th (F♯) and minor 3rd (G) sitting a semitone apart and rubbing against each other — played on separate strings it reads much less dissonant than it looks on paper. A second inversion of the same three notes (G-B-F♯ ordering) works too.
- **Sus4 variant:** take an E minor 7 voicing and swap the minor 3rd (G) for the perfect 4th (A) — turns it into an Esus4(add7)-type sound, used as a passing color inside the same riff.
- **G major voicing:** major 7th (F♯) on top of the G power chord, plus a very dissonant diminished 5th (♭5, C♯) added — a harsher, more chromatic-sounding jazz-metal color than the minor voicings.
- **9th resolving into the 3rd:** a favorite trick — voice the 9th as a tension, then resolve it down into the minor 3rd (a less dissonant interval) within the same phrase, rather than just sitting on either note statically.
- **Bigger E minor voicing:** the same 9th+3rd-on-5th shape with the root (E) added back on the low A string for a fuller sound when the stretch is playable; the smaller rootless version stays available as the easier fallback.
- **G major "popular" voicing:** power chord (5th) + 9th + major 3rd all together — a fuller, more demanding shape than the 2-tension voicings above, but cited as a widely-used one worth the stretch.
- **General method:** the harmonic info for building these voicings from any scale degree is already in the scale itself — layer 3rd/7th/9th/etc. on top of the plain power chord rather than treating "jazz chords" as a separate vocabulary to memorize from scratch.

## Synopsis

Part 1 of a two-part series from the "asd" (Ableton Sound Design) channel breaking down Ableton's stock Grain Delay device architecture, parameter by parameter, before a follow-up video (not in this queue) demonstrates creative applications. Grain Delay's defining trait vs. a standard delay: it splits the incoming signal into many tiny grains and applies pitch/delay processing to each grain individually, before the standard delay/feedback stage.

## What's taught

- **Standard delay backbone.** Grain Delay retains a normal delay circuit's core controls — delay time, feedback, dry/wet — behaving like an ordinary delay when its grain-specific parameters are left neutral.
- **Frequency = grain size, not literal frequency.** The "Frequency" parameter actually sets grain length, ranging from 1 Hz (grains ~1 second long) to 150 Hz (grains ~1/150th of a second) — better mentally relabeled "grain size." Moving it alone (with Spray at zero) produces no audible difference.
- **Spray = per-grain randomized delay offset, and a free stereo-widening tool.** Spray delays each individual grain by a random amount between 0ms and the set Spray value (e.g. Spray=20ms → each grain gets a random 0–20ms extra delay). Calculated **independently for left and right channels** — meaning Spray alone widens the stereo image of any source. Small Spray amounts smear the input subtly; large amounts fully disintegrate its transient/time identity.
- **Frequency (grain size) and Spray interact.** Larger grains with high Spray retain more of their individual character even when scattered in time; smaller grains under the same Spray amount blur together and lose individual identity faster — grain size sets how much "material" Spray has to work with per grain.
- **Pitch modulation is per-grain, independent of the delay/feedback stage, and happens BEFORE it.** Each grain can be pitched up to 1 octave above or 3 octaves below the source — like a traditional sampler, pitching also changes each grain's playback speed. A dedicated random-pitch parameter randomizes pitch per grain for a texture/chaos effect. Signal order matters: grain pitch/size processing happens first, THEN the result is fed into the standard delay/feedback loop, so feedback recirculates the already-granulated signal.
- **The XY controller as the recommended entry point for learning the device** — map any two parameters to the X/Y pad and drag around; the pad instantly produces compelling results regardless of which two parameters are assigned, and visually demonstrates how parameters interact/are independent from each other, faster than adjusting knobs individually while learning.

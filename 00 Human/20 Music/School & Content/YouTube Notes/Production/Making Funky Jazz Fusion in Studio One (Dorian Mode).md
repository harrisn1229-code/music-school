## Synopsis

A fast, heavily time-lapsed "weekly challenge" speedrun (Max Konyi, made in Studio One, from his Discord community's challenge series) constrained to writing entirely diatonically in **Dorian mode** — no notes outside the key — aimed at an uptempo, "proggy," jazzy-fusion feel (name-checks Weather Report as a reference). Lower spoken-commentary density than the other three videos in this batch (it's largely a live improvised build with short narrated fragments), but it still yields real, extractable content: a practical ear-based method for identifying Dorian by ear, a couple of concrete sound choices (a piano+Rhodes layered patch, a pad playing the whole mode as a drone/bed), a chords-with-no-bass-notes voicing choice, and a dramatic full-stop arrangement device around the drum fills.

## Techniques

- **Ear-ID method for Dorian, independent of theory analysis:** "Do I hear this note?" — over what would otherwise read as a plain minor key, the presence of the natural 6th (the one note that distinguishes Dorian from Aeolian/natural minor) heard recurring "over and over" is the practical tell; there are lots of ways to build the Dorian sound, but hearing that one color-tone repeatedly is the fast, ear-first identification method, independent of working out scale formulas. → [[Diatonic Modes]]
- **Write chord voicings with no bass note first, add the bass/root separately afterward** — chords built and auditioned with only the upper voicing (no root in the bass) "sound kind of funny/hilarious" on their own but are meant to be paired with a separate bass part once the groove is in place; voicing and bass-note assignment are treated as two separate writing passes rather than one.
- **A dramatic full stop is a legitimate drum-fill alternative** — cutting the drums out completely for a beat (rather than playing a busy fill through it) reads as a deliberate "oh" moment; paired here with an unusually long, showy drum fill on either side of the stop for contrast.
- **Brass stabs from a synth-brass patch, pitched an octave higher and tightened rhythmically**, used as a disco-adjacent accent layer within an otherwise Dorian-fusion arrangement — a reminder that genre-defining harmonic constraints (diatonic-only Dorian here) don't have to lock the instrumentation/arrangement palette to one lane.

## Sound/tone selection

- **Piano layered with a Rhodes-type patch (Omnisphere) as a combined "key-bass" texture** — described as "a piano mixed with a Rhodes from Omnisphere," used as a compound comping/bass-adjacent sound rather than two separate parts. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **A pad (Serum) voiced to play the entire mode at once as a drone/harmonic bed**, duplicated and reverbed — a fast way to establish a mode's full color as a static wash under a moving progression, rather than voicing individual chords for the pad part. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]

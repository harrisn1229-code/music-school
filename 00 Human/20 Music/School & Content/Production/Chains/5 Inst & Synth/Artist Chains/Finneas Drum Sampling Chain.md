# Finneas Drum Sampling Chain

**Reference/vibe:** a from-Finneas-himself breakdown of one song's build, reverse-engineered from [Creative drum sampling with Finneas](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CPROY0-0T2U) (Mix with the Masters, 2021). Unlike the fuller multi-take vocal-chain breakdowns elsewhere in this vault, this one is short and the value is concentrated in **process and philosophy** rather than exact numeric settings: a solo drum-programming session as the song's literal starting point, a stated belief that any raw sound is only a starting place ("dress it up until it's kind of unrecognizable"), and a direct compositional lesson pulled from writing "bad guy" (make the bass line itself singable). Treat every device setting below as qualitative — the video shows the signal path and reasoning, not exact parameter values.

## Chain overview

Two chain-shaped elements plus a build-order lesson: (1) a drum kit built from sound-first layering (Battery → PanMan on one layer, a two-kick pattern, and a chorus-gated character tom), and (2) a five-effect "dress it up until unrecognizable" processing stack turning a plain Output Analog Strings patch into cinematic, aged-sounding strings. A separate 808 Studio bass stage rounds out the low end. The reusable core idea: **a raw starting sound (sampled, patched, or purchased) is a beginning, not a ceiling — the payoff is in how far you're willing to process it, and a drum kit's real job is how it locks with the bassline's pocket, not how it sounds solo.**

## 1. Drum foundation — the song's actual starting point

- **Source:** the very first piece of the song, written alone, became "the main percussion element" before anything else existed.
- **Kit build:** primary kick chosen for its own sound first, then how it layers with the rest — a sparse "one and one" (downbeat-only) kick plus a second kick pattern hitting 1-and-3. Claps layered directly onto the 2-and-4 backbeat snare as one composite hit; a second snare doubled with a deliberately "crappy"-toned tom, reserved for a specific section (named as chorus) — described as reading like "20th Century Fox or something."
- 🎬 *From video:* a stated minimalism preference governs the whole kit — "I love minimalism, I love simplicity, anything that's just a wonderful foundation for other things to develop is really exciting to me." → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Kick Design]], [[Snare Design]], [[Arrangement and Song Form]]

## 2. A picked drum layer — Battery → PanMan

- **Source:** Battery (Native Instruments), his primary sampler at the time this song was written — since replaced by Logic Pro's Drum Machine Designer + Quick Sampler as his main engine. Workflow was pure auditioning: browse the library, stop on whatever "felt really interesting."
- **Chain:** [[PanMan]], set to a continuously-active "hard bounce MIDI" pattern (not a one-off pan move) — "just takes things out of mono mania... added a real sort of depth."
- 🎬 *From video:* an instrument-engine choice can migrate over a producer's career without the earlier tool being wrong — Battery is still "adored," just superseded by a newer in-DAW option. → [[Plugin Wishlist]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Drum Rack]], [[Simpler]]

## 3. Strings — Output Analog Strings, dressed up until unrecognizable

- **Source:** Output's Analog Strings, praised specifically for its own onboard Dirt/Delay/Pitch faders ("so satisfying, and they add such a good image to the whole thing") before any outboard FX are even added.
- **Chain:** sample delay → clip distortion → [[ValhallaRoom]] → a channel EQ → [[Little AlterBoy]] (last stage, on a non-vocal source).
- **Result:** "crazy kind of messed up old sounding strings," compared directly to "some Hans Zimmer type things."
- 🎬 *From video:* the whole point is stated on camera — a plain starting patch "doesn't mean that you can't sort of change it into something kind of unrecognizable... when you dress it up, it takes on so much character and totally changes the way it actually is." → [[ValhallaRoom]], [[Little AlterBoy]], [[Sampling and Sound Selection]], [[Sound Selection Guide]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

## 4. Bass / 808 — 808 Studio, deep envelope tweaking

- **Source:** 808 Studio (started this song before 808 Studio 2 released), reached for specifically over "finding an 808 sample and throwing it on the keyboard."
- **Processing:** "tweaked all the envelopes on this, the sub box" — a synthesis-level approach rather than a sample-plus-pitch shortcut.
- 🎬 *From video:* the pocket comes from the bass sitting on the eighth note, locked with an eighth-note hi-hat pattern (his on-screen name: "zipper") — and the bass line itself should be genuinely singable, a lesson credited directly to writing "bad guy": "you can sort of sing it in your own right, as opposed to only thinking of the vocal line as the thing that you'd sing along to." → [[Roland TR-808]], [[Bass Design]], [[Bassline Writing]], [[Pocket and Microtiming]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

## Arrangement

**Reach for a layer only in the section that needs it.** The chorus-gated character tom in Stage 1 exists for one section and is absent everywhere else — the same principle as [[Sound Selection Guide]]'s Tom Elmhirst chorus-only reinforcement entry.

| Section | Kit |
|---|---|
| Verse | sparse downbeat-only kick + composite clap/snare on 2 and 4 |
| Chorus | + the second kick pattern (1-and-3), + the character tom |

**The move — minimalism as a foundation, not an endpoint.** Finneas: *"anything that's just a wonderful foundation for other things to develop."* The kit is deliberately under-built so the arrangement has somewhere to go.

**Density: 3 → 5 drum elements.** Note how small that swing is — the drums aren't carrying the section contrast, they're leaving room for it.

## Variations

- **A hand-programmed, non-sampled "cowbell-type" clacky percussive layer, rhythmically keyed to the kick rather than pulled from a cowbell sample.** A second Finneas source (a Billie Eilish vocal-production breakdown, not this video) describes a percussion layer explicitly NOT built from a cowbell sample — "not that complicated," but functioning rhythmically the way a cowbell would relative to the kick drum, adding what he calls "a little bit more neck." Thin on synthesis/programming specifics, logged qualitatively as a second confirmed instance of Finneas building small non-sampled percussive texture layers rather than always reaching for a sample. → [[Layering and Stacking]], [[Kick Design]] — Mix with the Masters (Finneas), [[Vocal production for Billie Eilish by Finneas]]
- **The "dress it up until unrecognizable" philosophy (Stage 3) generalizes past strings** — any plain patch/sample/keyboard recording is a candidate for a multi-effect transformation pass, not just an instrument that already sounds "cinematic."
- **The chorus-gated character-tom move (Stage 1) is the same principle as [[Sound Selection Guide]]'s Tom Elmhirst "chorus-only reinforcement" entry** — reach for an extra layer only in the section that needs it, not throughout.
- **The "singable bass line" rule (Stage 4) applies to any instrumental hook-writing situation**, not just 808 bass — write the part so someone could hum it back, not just the vocal melody.

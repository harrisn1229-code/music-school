# VOI-C05 — One triad, every string group

**Q.** How many places does one major triad live on the fretboard, how are they organised, and what is the practical payoff of knowing them all?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Four adjacent 3-string groups** — E-A-D, A-D-G, D-G-B, G-B-E — each holding **three inversions** (root, 3rd or 5th on the bottom): twelve locations for one chord. The payoff is that you pick each chord's voicing **by proximity to the previous chord** instead of defaulting to the root-position shape, which cuts hand travel, raises the playable tempo and sounds less generic.

## Why it matters

This is the fretboard half of voice leading: the rule says move the least, and this is the inventory that makes moving the least possible. The two groups that cross the G–B string pair reuse the same shapes with the notes **above** that pair shifted up one fret, to compensate for its major-3rd tuning: **D-G-B needs the top note a fret higher, G-B-E the top two** — so it is genuinely three shapes in three fingerings, not twelve unrelated grips.

## Drill

15 min: pick one major triad and find all three inversions on all four string groups. Then play a four-chord progression choosing, each time, the nearest voicing rather than the root-position one.

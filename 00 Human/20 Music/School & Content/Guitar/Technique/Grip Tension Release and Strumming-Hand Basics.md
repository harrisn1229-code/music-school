# Grip Tension Release and Strumming-Hand Basics

Two small, concrete physical-technique fixes from the same Q&A video, grouped together as fundamentals with no existing home in the vault: releasing fretting-hand tension under playing intensity, and which fingers do what when strumming.

## Fretting-hand tension release: arm weight, not grip

For a player whose fretting hand tenses up specifically when playing harder/more intensely — a problem Jared had himself in his rock-band years, including a "hitchhiker thumb" habit (thumb hooking hard over the neck to add grip pressure):

- **Use the weight of the fretting arm instead of squeezing.** A full barre chord can be held down with essentially no thumb pressure at all by letting gravity and the natural weight of the arm supply the clamping force against the neck, rather than gripping harder as playing intensity rises. The felt cue: a relaxed grip that gravity is doing at least part of the work for, not a hand actively pulling/squeezing to generate pressure.
- **Mindful/box breathing to catch tension in the moment.** While playing, take a deliberate breath, consciously scan the body for where tension is being held, and breathe it out. **Box breathing pattern:** inhale 4 seconds, hold 4 seconds, exhale 4 seconds, hold 4 seconds, repeat.
- Recommended combined: both together, used as a pair whenever grip tension is a recurring problem, not just one or the other.

## Strumming-hand finger technique

For "which fingers should I strum with": **strum downward with the backs of the fingernails**, and **strum upward with the back of the thumbnail only.** Any of the fingers can be used for the downstroke — Jared varies between just the middle finger alone, the middle two fingers, or three/four fingers together, depending on feel and the sound wanted. The up-down alternation is fingers-down / thumb-up throughout (down, up, down, up), not fingers doing both directions.

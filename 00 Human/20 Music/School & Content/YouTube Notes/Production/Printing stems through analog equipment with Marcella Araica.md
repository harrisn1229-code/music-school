# Printing stems through analog equipment with Marcella Araica

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q9O3x1l3wWg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q9O3x1l3wWg)

## Synopsis

A short, screen-recorded Pro Tools clip of Marcella Araica (Mix with the Masters) demonstrating her hybrid analog/ITB workflow: kick and snare have already been routed out to her analog board, EQ'd and shaped with an SPL transient designer, and this clip is the printing-back step — soloing each processed source and recording it back into Pro Tools as its own stem, one at a time, even though the kicks and snares each physically share a pair of output channels on the board. She explains the "why" behind reaching for hardware at all (a philosophy note as much as a technique), then continues the signal flow: all drums route to an "All Drums" bus, all non-drum musical elements (samples, transitional risers, guitars) route to a separate "All Music" bus, both feeding an empty premaster. The clip closes on one concrete bus move — a UA 33609 compressor added to BOTH buses, but deliberately dialed differently: pushed for obvious "extra sauce" and hit on the drums, kept light and breathing on the music bus so it adds presence without sounding squashed. Short and light on hard numbers (no ratios, thresholds, or GR figures are given on screen), but the printing discipline and the same-plugin-different-intensity bus philosophy are both concrete, transferable workflow moves.

## Techniques

- **Print individually-processed stems one by one even when the sources physically share output channels, for future mix flexibility.** Both kicks are routed out through one shared pair of channels on the board (same for the snares), but rather than printing that stereo pair back as one grouped stem, she solos and prints each drum separately, labeling each as it comes in ("kick B stem," etc.) — "even though I have both kicks and all the snares going out two channels, I'm going to print them back one by one, so that I have flexibility with them in the future." The print captures ALL the board processing (EQ + transient shaping) permanently onto that one stem, so keeping stems separate at print time is what preserves the ability to treat each drum independently later, even though the analog processing itself was shared/grouped going out. → [[Committing and Printing]], [[1 Drums Kick Track|Drums Kick Track]], [[1 Drums Snare-Clap Track|Drums Snare-Clap Track]]
- **Printing back off an analog board is just soloing the source and recording the stereo buss return — no special routing trick.** "Printing back is easy because you're literally just printing back off of the stereo buss." Confirm levels first, then print; repeat per source. A plain, low-friction workflow step worth remembering as the reason analog processing doesn't have to feel like a heavy production overhead. → [[Committing and Printing]]
- **On the board: EQ + an SPL transient designer on kick and snare, printed back with all of that processing baked in.** The specific chain named for this record's drum sources — corrective/tonal EQ plus hardware transient shaping — captured permanently once printed, not left as an editable insert. *(no EQ frequencies or transient-designer attack/sustain settings given — qualitative only)* → [[Transient Master]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]
- **Stated philosophy for reaching for analog/outboard gear at all: it's "another tool," offering texture/color/warmth she can't get otherwise — not a superiority claim over plugins.** "All the plugins are tools, you know, I have all this outboard gear behind me — those are my tools. And if I have the ability to do it, I have a studio, then why not. It's literally a different texture, a different color, different warmth that I'm not able to get." Framed as one tool among many rather than "analog is better" — pick the tool (in-the-box or outboard) for the specific color a source needs. → [[Committing and Printing]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Genre/era homage as the deciding factor for choosing the analog-heavy route on this particular record.** "I want it to have a more warmer sounding sound because of the homage that it's paying to the music from back then. For me it was a no-brainer that I would want to use the board heavily and use the outboard gear." A reminder that the choice of processing character (analog warmth vs. clean digital) is a genre/reference decision made up front, not a default — match the gear/processing style to what the record is paying homage to.
- **Explicit hybrid-mixing definition: analog board processing at the front (tracking/printing stage), the rest of the mix continued fully in the box.** "This allows me to continue mixing within the box, so it's a hybrid — like I'm basically mixing on the board, but I'm bringing it back in the box and I'm continuing the process." Not an either/or choice between analog and digital — the board does specific early-stage color/shaping work per source, then everything (bus glue, further processing, mix decisions) happens ITB from that point forward. → [[Committing and Printing]]
- **Bus routing after stems are printed: all drums → one "All Drums" bus; all non-drum musical elements (samples, transitional risers, guitars) → one "All Music" bus; both feed an empty premaster.** A simple two-bus split (rhythmic vs. everything-else-musical) rather than per-instrument-family busses at this stage — guitars, sample layers, and transitional risers/rises are all folded into the same music bus rather than getting their own separate bus. The premaster itself is left untouched at this point in the session ("nothing's on here") — bus-level shaping happens before anything is added at the master stage. → [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]], [[3 Inst-Synth Bus|Inst-Synth Bus]], [[3 Guitar Bus|Guitar Bus]], [[Master Chain]]
- **The SAME bus compressor (UA 33609) added to both buses, but deliberately dialed to two different intensities by role: pushed hard for obvious impact on drums, kept light and "breathing" on the music bus.** "On both of these buses I'm going to add a 33609 from UA — this really brings extra sauce on the drums that really make him hit... [on the music bus] I don't want it compressing too much, I want it to breathe. It's not about really over-compressing it, just allowing it to give it more presence without it feeling like it's being [squashed]." On the music bus she rides the gain by ear while listening against the rest of the mix, and pulls the guitars down slightly as part of that same pass. The reusable lesson: one compressor, two completely different amounts of drive/GR, chosen by what each bus's *role* needs (drums = hit/impact, music = presence without squashing). *(no ratio/threshold/GR figures given — qualitative)* → [[Compression Staging]], [[3 Drums Bus|Drums Bus]], [[3 Inst-Synth Bus|Inst-Synth Bus]], [[Plugin Wishlist]]

# How The Beatles used Middle 8s

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SkC_8wdzGn8)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SkC_8wdzGn8)

## Synopsis

Defines "middle eight" via 10 Beatles songs, tracing the term back to 32-bar AABA form (American popular-song/jazz-standard structure — *Over the Rainbow*, *I Got Rhythm*, *Blue Moon*) and showing how Lennon/McCartney (Paul in particular, via his father's jazz background) kept using and steadily distorting that form well past its 1930s-50s heyday.

## What's taught

- **Textbook AABA (32-bar form)**: *From Me to You* and *I'll Follow the Sun* — two 8-bar A sections, an 8-bar contrasting B section (the middle eight), then a return to A.
- **AABA with an irregular A length, middle eight still ~8 bars**: *Yesterday* (7-bar A, still called a "middle eight" despite the asymmetry); *I Feel Fine* and *And I Love Her* (10-bar A); *A Hard Day's Night* (12-bar A — explicitly the 12-bar-blues/rock-and-roll phrase length grafted onto a jazz-standard AABA skeleton, read as the song fusing both of its stated influences in its very form); *Lady Madonna* (4-bar A).
- **Middle eight as the place a song visits a new key**: common in old jazz standards/American Songbook writing, and used the same way in *Lady Madonna* (A major → C major for the middle eight, via a ii-V-I in the new key) and *From Me to You* (C major → briefly F major via a ii-V, cited as arguably the **earliest key change in a Beatles song**).
- **AABA missing a section**: *Norwegian Wood* is ABA (one A dropped) — its A section is in E Mixolydian, its B section (middle eight) shifts to the parallel mode E Dorian, a mode-only contrast rather than a full key change; *Nowhere Man* is AAB (final A dropped) — flagged as functionally closer to modern verse/chorus than AABA, since the B section here plays like an actual chorus (AABA songs generally have no chorus at all — the hook lives in the A section, as in *Over the Rainbow*).
- **AABA as a substructure inside a bigger form**: *Eight Days a Week* contains a complete AABA unit (with middle eight) but then adds a further distinct section — arguably better described as verse/chorus/bridge overlaid on the AABA skeleton than as pure AABA, illustrating that these labels are descriptive templates, not fixed rules.
- Overall framing: "middle eight" = the 8-bar B section of AABA form specifically, distinct from a general "bridge" or "pre-chorus," but as Beatles songs drift from pure AABA into verse/chorus territory the line between "B section" and "chorus" gets genuinely blurry — no single correct label applies to every case.

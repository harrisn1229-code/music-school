## Techniques

- **Restate the main theme in a new key on its later return** — the piece's main melodic theme comes back later transposed into the key of E (different from its first statement) rather than repeated verbatim — a simple, real arrangement device (a key-shifted restatement reads as development, not just repetition) even though no further detail (which original key, why E) is given. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]]
- **Build an orchestral texture by adding one instrumental color at a time and listening after each addition** — cello first, then harp, then auditioning pizzicato ("picado") strings, then high strings, then percussion accents (glockenspiel sparingly, "every once in a while," plus timpani and a cymbal roll) — an incremental, ear-led orchestration process rather than writing a full arrangement up front. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]
- **Deliberating lead-instrument choice by character, not just availability** — explicitly weighed trumpet vs. flute for the same melodic lead role at different points, choosing by ear for the section's character rather than defaulting to one instrument throughout.
- **A sample library's "strum" articulation as a fast way to humanize a string-ensemble texture** — used a "strum" feature (an articulation switch on the string patch that simulates a played, non-mechanical bowing/pluck gesture across the ensemble) for a hummed/full-string texture layer, noting he'd never reached for that specific articulation before. → [[Sound Selection Guide]]

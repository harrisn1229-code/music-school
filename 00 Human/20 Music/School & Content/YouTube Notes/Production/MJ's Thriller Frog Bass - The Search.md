# MJ's Thriller Frog Bass - The Search

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NEwzTpd8uRg)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NEwzTpd8uRg)

## Synopsis

Investigative history piece (with Stephen Ray) tracking down the source of Thriller's famous "frog" sound in the breakdown: a cheap, toy-like Casiotone CT-401 home keyboard — a preset one-shot instrument sound literally labeled "Frog" (next to a "Funny" button), likely from Quincy Jones' own house, believed played by Greg Phillinganes. No confirmed provenance beyond that; the investigation is honestly left open (there may have been more than one Casio involved).

## What's taught

- **Layering a novel/toy sound against an existing bass line as an orchestration technique**: the "frog" preset enters mid-breakdown while the song's real bass (ARP 2600) is already playing underneath — framed explicitly as the pop-music equivalent of adding a contrabassoon under a string bass in orchestral writing: a second, "edgier" low voice that pokes through the existing bass rather than replacing it, deliberately introduced later in the song ("the intro, or the first 8-32 bars, never has it") so the ear registers it as something genuinely new arriving.
- **Cheap/toy gear as a legitimate character source, not a compromise**: a home Casiotone with built-in one-button presets (not even a proper synthesizer section) was reportedly never seen on a professional session before or since — the explicit reasoning given was wanting something "odd," "almost like a toy," specifically BECAUSE it wasn't the kind of gear anyone else would reach for.
- **Restraint as part of the technique**: the frog sound was used completely plain — no harmonies, no layering effects, no processing chain — just the single raw preset, one hit at a time, played in the pocket. The simplicity is framed as part of why it works rather than something to "improve."

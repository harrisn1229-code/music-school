# Alternate Picking: How To Play FASTER! (Speed Burst Exercises)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BXwglURgQ-M)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BXwglURgQ-M)

## Synopsis

A short-burst alternate-picking speed exercise on the chromatic scale (4 notes per string, 2 strings), built around a specific tension/relaxation principle: compare how little picking-attack force is actually needed during a fast burst by contrasting it directly against the relaxed feel of the rest period between bursts, rather than just grinding the tempo up.

## What's taught

- **The core exercise: 4 notes on one string, 1 note (a downstroke) on the next, chromatic, two strings (starting on D).** Pattern: down-up-down-up (4 notes on string 1) then down (1 note on string 2) — trains the specific string-switch of ending on an upstroke and immediately needing a downstroke on the next string, an unfamiliar grouping for players used to 3-notes-per-string phrasing.
- **The tension/relaxation principle — the exercise's actual point.** Don't alternate between "super tense" bursts and "super relaxed" rests (a jagged tension graph); instead use the *comparison* between a fast burst and the following rest to discover how little attack force a fast burst actually needs — the target tension curve is described as "relatively flat," not a spike-and-drop pattern. Stated benefit beyond speed: also trains hand synchronization and the specific string-switching skill above.
- **Self-check built into the exercise:** because the pattern always ends the D-string group with an upstroke, the first note on the next string should always land as a downstroke — landing on anything else (an upstroke, or a stroke "in between") is immediate, audible proof the hands aren't synchronized, without needing outside feedback.
- **Progression: remove the rests entirely for a pure speed variant** — once the burst-then-rest version feels controlled, chain the fast bursts back to back with no gap, in both directions, as a harder speed-only version of the same string-switching pattern.
- **Two-hand relaxation checklist:** picking motion should not visibly grow in size as tempo increases (a growing motion signals excess tension and produces unwanted string noise/wasted energy); left hand — keep fingers close to the fretboard rather than letting them fly away, worst when the pattern reverses direction and starts from the pinky finger going down to the index finger.

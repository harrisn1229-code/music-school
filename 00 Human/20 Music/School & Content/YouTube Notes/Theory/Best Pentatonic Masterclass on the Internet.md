# Best Pentatonic Masterclass on the Internet

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lFFlVv4pmXk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lFFlVv4pmXk)

## Synopsis

Brandon D'Eon's long-form pentatonic curriculum: beyond the standard minor/major pentatonic shapes, there are three more pentatonic shapes hiding in every key — Dorian, Phrygian, and Mixolydian pentatonic, each a 5-note "skeleton" of the corresponding mode. He walks through a six-level progression (learn the shapes → drill all 12 keys while changing position → improvise musically → drill all 12 keys anchored to one fretboard position, starting from every string → chain exercises → daily habit) and closes with his reasoning for informally naming the shapes after their parent modes.

## Techniques

- **Five pentatonic shapes per key, not two.** Minor pentatonic (from the relative minor/Aeolian), major pentatonic (from the major root/Ionian), Dorian pentatonic (major 2nd above the major root), Phrygian pentatonic (major 3rd above), Mixolydian pentatonic (5th above). Lydian and Locrian pentatonic shapes aren't taught — treated as low-value/rarely used.
- **Picking-direction rule (reinforced from other material):** ascending 2-notes-per-string runs start on an upstroke (inside picking), descending runs start on a downstroke — see [[Inside-Outside Picking and Pick Slanting]] for the full existing writeup.
- **Level 2 drill — one key, all five shapes.** Use a random key generator, pick the shape giving the most trouble, derive its scale-degree offset from the major root, and drill it through all 12 keys at a comfortable tempo (demoed at 120 BPM), timing and logging each run.
- **Level 3 drill — all five shapes, all 12 keys, changing fretboard position.** Faster tempo (~125 BPM), full octave per key, deliberately starting each key on a shape without open strings, using a randomizer instead of the circle of fifths so key order doesn't become memorized.
- **Level 4 — improvise for real**, over backing tracks in 3 different keys, rather than just drilling shapes to a metronome; move on once confident playing in any drilled key anywhere on the neck.
- **Level 5 drill — position-anchored, all 12 keys, all six starting strings.** Stay in one fretboard zone (e.g. 7th–10th fret) and cycle all 12 keys, playing whichever pentatonic shape sits closest to that position; run the whole cycle six times, once starting from each string (low E through high E), since real improv requires starting lines from any string, not just the low E.
- **Level 6 — chain exercises.** Continuous unbroken stream of notes at a fixed subdivision (quarters/8ths/16ths/triplets), timed for endurance — report both tempo and subdivision together, since either alone doesn't define difficulty.
- **Cadence: one key a day, every day, for a month** is the stated minimum to see real gains.
- **Naming logic (meta, not a technique):** each modal pentatonic shape is "a skeleton" of its parent mode — take the mode's diatonic scale and drop the two notes that would form a tritone; what's left is that mode's pentatonic shape. He's explicit the names are informal — the shapes as commonly taught don't necessarily preserve each mode's defining color tones.

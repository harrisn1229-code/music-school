# String Skipping Approaches

No prior home in the vault for string-skipping as its own picking topic — created 2026-08-23 from a BERNTH lesson; registered here rather than in [[Directional Alternate Picking System]] since the picking-angle problem it creates is distinct enough to warrant its own page (linked both ways).

## What it is

Escaping the default habit of building shred licks from **adjacent string pairs** (ascending/descending between two neighboring strings) by deliberately **skipping a string** — e.g. picking the low E and D strings while leaving the A string untouched, instead of the more common E–A pairing. The payoff is lick variety: constant adjacent-pair phrasing is a common source of licks feeling repetitive.

## The picking-angle problem it creates

Skipping a string means the picking hand has to travel further between strokes than a normal adjacent transition, and the **switch direction matters**:
- Coming from a downstroke on a lower string into an upstroke on the skipped-to higher string is comparatively easy.
- The harder direction is switching back — from that upstroke on the higher string into a downstroke on the lower string — because the hand has to travel the same distance in reverse, and it's easy to let unwanted noise or a stray pull-off creep in during the crossing.
- **Practice implication:** slow the transition down with a metronome specifically at the direction-switch point, not just the lick as a whole — the failure mode is rushing/dragging exactly at the skip, not general sloppiness across the phrase.

## Worked example (E natural minor / Aeolian, with a Phrygian twist)

A descending run through E natural minor (same notes as A minor/C major, F raised to F♯), modulating to E Phrygian at the phrase's end (lowering the 2nd degree back to F — landing back on the C major/A minor note set, readable as C Ionian's 3rd mode or A Aeolian's 5th). The scale choice is secondary to the point — any scale works — but the phrase is moved through multiple positions on the neck as the actual workout.

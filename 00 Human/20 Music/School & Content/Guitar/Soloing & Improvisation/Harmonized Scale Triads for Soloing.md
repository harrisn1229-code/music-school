# Harmonized Scale Triads for Soloing

Part of [[Guitar Positions]] and [[Diatonic Modes]]. A third distinct triad-soloing device alongside the vault's other two: [[Triads as a Fretboard Map]] takes **one** triad (the tonic) and finds its three close-position zones across the neck; [[Upper Structure Triads from Seventh Chords]] extracts a triad from **inside** a single seventh chord's 3rd-5th-7th; this page instead **harmonizes the whole parent scale into seven triads** (one per scale degree, skip-a-note/tertian stacking) and uses each one, in sequence up the neck, as a self-contained lick-starting motif. Worked in **A Mixolydian over an A7 vamp**. See also [[Triad Pairs]] — a fourth device that takes this page's same harmonized-triad table but combines **two** adjacent triads at once rather than using them one at a time.

## Deriving the seven triads (skip-a-note method)
Build the parent scale first: **A7 scale = A major with a ♭7** = **A Mixolydian**: A B C♯ D E F♯ G. Stack a triad on each degree by playing every *other* note of the scale from that starting point (skip one, play one, skip one, play one): starting on A, skip B, play C♯, skip D, play E → **A C♯ E = A major**. Move the same skip-a-note pattern up one scale step at a time and the qualities cycle through the whole scale:

| Degree | Triad | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| I | A | major |
| ii | B | minor |
| iii | C♯ | diminished |
| IV | D | major |
| v | E | minor |
| vi | F♯ | minor |
| ♭VII | G | major |

*(general note, not stated as a formal rule in the source video but implied by the derivation: this is Mixolydian's own harmonized-triad sequence — I–ii–iii°–IV–v–vi–♭VII — and it differs from the parent **major** scale's harmonization in exactly one triad: Mixolydian's flattened 7th moves the diminished triad from the major scale's vii° down to Mixolydian's iii°, and turns the major scale's vii° into a plain **major** ♭VII instead. Cross-reference [[Diatonic Modes]]'s "only one degree in each key carries a dominant 7th, and it's the Mixolydian degree" note — same fact, seen from the chord-quality side.)*

## Two supporting shapes to have under the fingers first
- **All seven triads on one string set, moving up the neck.** Play the seven triads above (A major, B minor, C♯ diminished, D major, E minor, F♯ minor, G major) as close-position shapes on the **top three strings only**, each one physically higher up the neck than the last as the scale degree rises — a single continuous ladder of grips rather than seven unrelated shapes.
- **The scale laid out horizontally on one string.** The same A Mixolydian scale, played as a single-string ladder on the **high E string**: open E, F♯, G, A (root), B, C♯, D, back to E an octave up. This is the source of the "two scale notes" half of the motif below — a fretboard-local pool of scale tones sitting right next to wherever a given triad shape lands.

## The core motif: triad arpeggio + two scale notes, as a jumping-off point
Combine the two shapes above into one small phrase: **arpeggiate the triad, then add two notes from the adjacent scale-on-one-string ladder**, moving up through it. Play that same short motif shape-for-shape through **all seven triads**, ascending the neck one triad at a time — "any one of these little triad patterns can be a jumping off point for a lick... the catalyst to get you started playing a cool line or idea." The motif itself isn't the destination; it's a reliable two-second on-ramp into a longer improvised line, repeated at seven different points on the neck.

## Blend the triad-scale skeleton with blues vocabulary, not just the A7 scale
Once a motif lands, what follows is deliberately **not** restricted to the plain A7/Mixolydian scale — it's blues/pentatonic vocabulary layered on top, the same "sweet Mixolydian + gritty blues scale" blend documented on [[Blues Scales and Blue Notes]]: the ♭5 as a passing tone, and the minor 3rd played together with or sliding into the major 3rd (the ♭3/♮3 "friction is the sound" point on that page, here demonstrated as a literal in-lick bend/slide rather than just a scale choice).

## Seven worked licks (one per triad, in ascending order)
Played starting from the **iii° (C♯ diminished)** triad rather than the tonic — chosen deliberately because "this is really right out of this bar" — then moving straight up through **IV, v, vi, ♭VII, I**, and finishing on **ii**:
- **C♯ diminished (start):** arpeggio + two scale notes, resolving to A; from there, a blended minor-pentatonic/major-pentatonic phrase using the **flat 5th** — comes down from A to G, then a chromatic run, landing on the **minor 3rd** with the option to bend or slide it up into the **major 3rd**.
- **D major:** arpeggio + two scale notes, then descends through the A7 scale, deliberately playing the **minor 3rd instead of the major 3rd** for a bluesier read of the same line.
- **E minor:** arpeggio + two scale notes, then skips down to A, back up to B, down to G then F♯, into a chromatic run built on the **flat 5th** — down to E♭, jump down to B, up to D, then slides into the 3rd and root of an A chord.
- **F♯ minor** — worth calling out on its own: F♯ minor is A major's **relative minor**, so an F♯ minor triad and an A major triad differ by only one note, which is why this shape sits so naturally alongside straight A-major/A-pentatonic vocabulary. Lick: arpeggio + two scale notes, descend the A7 scale, again **accenting the minor 3rd over the major 3rd** for the bluesy flavor.
- **G major:** arpeggio + two scale notes, then largely straight out of the A7 scale using **three-note-per-string** picking through the position.
- **A major (I, the "home" triad):** essentially outlines the notes of A7 itself; decorated with **chromatic passing tones** — a slide from the minor 3rd up to the major 3rd, a hammer-on between the 2nd and the minor 3rd, and a **♭7 down to the 6th** passing move.
- **B minor (ii, final):** arpeggio + two scale notes, then slides down into a run of A7-outlining, "mixolydian stuff," landing back on chord-tone territory to close the pass.

## Building a full solo: seed licks dropped into a bluesier improvisation
The closing demonstration doesn't play all seven licks back to back — it plays a **freely improvised bluesy solo** over the A7 vamp (the same pentatonic/blues vocabulary as the individual licks above) and drops one of the seven triad-motif phrases in **wherever it feels right**, rather than on a fixed schedule. The triad ladder is a **vocabulary source to dip into**, not a set of exercises meant to be performed in sequence.

## Practice sequence
1. Derive the seven diatonic triads of the target Mixolydian scale by the skip-a-note method (or any mode — the derivation generalizes to any parent scale).
2. Drill the seven triads as one continuous ladder on a single string set (top three strings, moving up the neck).
3. Map the parent scale on a single string near each triad shape, for the "two scale notes" half of the motif.
4. Drill the triad-arpeggio-plus-two-scale-notes motif through all seven triads, ascending.
5. Use each motif as a jumping-off point for a full lick, freely blending in blues-scale/pentatonic vocabulary (♭5 passing tone, ♭3/♮3 blend) rather than staying inside the parent scale.
6. Fold individual licks into a freely improvised solo over the vamp, dropped in opportunistically rather than played start to finish.

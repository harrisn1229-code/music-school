# Michael Jackson Just Worked Harder Than Everyone Else

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xOE6kam4Xgk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xOE6kam4Xgk)

## Synopsis

On Michael Jackson's studio preparation and vocal-recording discipline for Thriller-era sessions — arriving with every ad-lib and harmony part already planned, real mic-technique skill, and how the era's real track-count limits shaped how many takes a vocal actually got.

## Techniques

- **Plan the stereo placement of doubled ad-libs mentally, before recording — not decided later in the mix.** A background vocal part is doubled (recorded twice: the "basic" take panned left, the "double" panned right); Michael reportedly knew, while singing, which specific ad-lib phrase was going to land on the left (basic) side versus the right (double) side, effectively pre-arranging the stereo picture as a performance decision rather than a mix decision.
- **Vary vocal texture by directing physical distance from the mic, not just processing.** The engineer would ask for a literal step back from the mic on certain parts specifically to reduce proximity effect and get a different (less boosted-low-end, more natural) texture — a performance-blocking move, applied deliberately rather than left to chance.
- **Manage hard plosive consonants (P, B) with a slight head turn, even with a pop filter/foam already in place.** Turning the head just enough that the loudest part of the breath blast doesn't hit the capsule dead-on reduces pop artifacts beyond what foam windscreen alone catches — a performer-side mic-technique skill, not purely an engineering fix.
- **Real analog track-count limits created a natural, disciplined take ceiling.** With a fixed number of open vocal tracks on a given reel (five, in the example given), a singer effectively had to land the part within that many takes; rather than reading as a limitation, this constraint is described as what produced a superior take — by the last available attempts, the singer was "locking in" emotionally in a way that read as the strongest performance. Retakes were coarse-grained ("back it up, take it from this point") rather than word-by-word punch-ins.

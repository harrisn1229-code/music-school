# The Key to Bringing JAZZ Influence to Your BLUES Soloing

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SgBUGT0C2qU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SgBUGT0C2qU)

## Synopsis
A direct companion to [[The Key To Understanding A Jazz Blues]] — same channel, same B♭ jazz-blues 1-6-2-5 turnaround (bar 7 of a 12-bar form: B♭maj7–G7–Cm7–F7), but where that earlier video built and dissected one worked line over the section, this one delivers **four complete, independently-fingered lick examples** over the identical four bars, each demonstrated first against a backing track and then dropped into a full 12-bar blues solo so it's heard in context. Ruch explicitly frames the sequence as borrowed from **rhythm changes** (the *I Got Rhythm* A-section skeleton, played here at one chord per bar instead of two beats per chord) rather than native blues vocabulary — reinforcing [[Rhythm Changes]]'s own note that its A section *is* this exact I–vi–ii–V loop. The four licks are a genuinely useful vocabulary set for the same slot: Lick 1 is a pure chord-tone-outlining pass (including a diminished-seventh-arpeggio-inside-a-♭9-dominant device); Lick 2 stacks all four chords' arpeggios into one tight neck position by leaning on their shared/adjacent shapes and thirds; Lick 3 is the most chromatic of the four, opening with a major-7th lean on the I chord and closing with a diminished-into-augmented arpeggio pair over the V chord; Lick 4 is pure bebop phrasing — enclosures, chromatic walks, and altered extensions (♯9/♭9) stacked back to back. A recurring, genuinely reusable device across three of the four licks: **playing a major 7 arpeggio built off the minor third of the ii chord** (E♭maj7 over Cm7) for a minor-9 color, rather than just running the C minor scale. No plugin, effect, mixing, or sound/tone-selection content — pure instrument/theory pedagogy (guitar improvisation vocabulary over a jazz-blues turnaround). Tabs/notation and the backing track are gated behind Ruch's Patreon, not in the free video.

## What's taught

- **The turnaround's rhythm-changes origin, made explicit.** The B♭maj7–G7–Cm7–F7 sequence (I–vi–ii–V) is named directly as "a major part of rhythm changes," here compressed from rhythm changes' native two-beats-per-chord pace to one full bar per chord, and relocated to bar 7 of a 12-bar blues rather than bars 1–4 of an AABA form. *(0:00–1:00)* → [[Rhythm Changes]] § *The A section — I–vi–ii–V, twice*, [[Turnarounds]], [[Blues Form and Harmony]]
- **Lick 1 — straight chord-tone outlining, chord by chord:** B♭maj7 = slide into the major 7 (A), come down through the 5th and major 3rd, drop and climb a full maj7 arpeggio — "nothing fancy, just chord tones." G7 = root–3rd–5th–♭7, then a reach up to the **♭9** (a deliberately "altered dominant" color) resolving down into Cm7's minor 3rd. Cm7 = plain C minor scale/chord tones. F7 = a **diminished-seventh arpeggio hidden inside the F7♭9 chord** (3rd–5th–♭7–♭9 of F7 traces an A° diminished-7 shape), then down to the root and a short chromatic bebop cliché resolving back to B♭. → [[Diminished Chords]] § *diminished-7 arpeggio off an altered dominant*, [[Bebop Vocabulary]], [[Guide Tones]]
- **Lick 2 — one neck position, arpeggios "stacked right on top of one another":** skips B♭maj7 entirely (left open/comped), starts on G7's 3rd–5th–♭7–♭9–root (the same diminished/♭9 device as Lick 1, reused), a short chromatic enclosure into Cm7's minor 3rd, then **an E♭ major 7 arpeggio built off Cm7's flat third** for the minor-9 color, into F7's 3rd, back to B♭'s major 3rd. Named payoff: because the CAGED-family shapes for G7, Cm7, F7 and B♭maj7 sit right next to each other on the neck, the whole turnaround plays "in one position" — a live worked instance of [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]]'s CAGED-adjacency method — and the line **leans heavily on the third of every chord**, a direct application of [[Guide Tones]]'s "aim at 3rds and 7ths" rule to all four chords in a row. *(~7:30–10:00)* → [[Arpeggio Shapes for Playing Over Changes]], [[Guide Tones]]
- **Lick 3 — chromatic and augmented-heavy:** opens by **leaning into B♭maj7's own major 7th** specifically to mark the sonic difference between this section's maj7-flavored I chord and the plain-blues dominant-7 I chord elsewhere in the form (same distinction [[The Key To Understanding A Jazz Blues]] already makes) — then a hammer-on/pull-off (A→B♭) and a chromatic walk-down (G-G♭-F-E♭-D-C) landing exactly on G7's 3rd (B) as the chord change lands. Over G7: another diminished-7-for-♭9 arpeggio (viewable as a B° shape over the G7 root), pull-off to the root, chromatic enclosure into Cm7's minor 3rd. Over Cm7: the same E♭maj7-off-the-♭3 device as Lick 2. Over F7: **a diminished arpeggio (A°) immediately followed by an F augmented arpeggio** (♭6/♯5–major 3rd–root) — Ruch names the augmented shape explicitly as "this classic augmented voicing you've probably heard in a ton of songs" — resolving to chord tones back at B♭. *(~10:30–14:00)* → [[Diminished Chords]], [[Augmented Chords]], [[Bebop Vocabulary]]
- **Lick 4 — pure bebop phrasing, breaking out of one fixed position:** over B♭maj7, a pivoting A↔G phrase into a hammer-on/pull-off/slide figure (F→G→D) leading to G7's 3rd landed exactly on the chord change, followed by a **classic enclosure to the root** (half step above, then chromatic walk up from F to the G7 root) — the ♭9-flavored enclosure vocabulary from [[Bebop Vocabulary]] § *2. Enclosures*, worked concretely. Over Cm7: the same E♭maj7-off-the-♭3 arpeggio a third time (making it 3-for-4 licks — the single most reused device in the lesson), read here as the **9th–♭3–5th–7th–9th of a Cm9 chord**. Over F7: a fully altered pass using **both the ♯9 and ♭9** before landing the root, then resolving to F7's 3rd back at B♭. *(~15:00–19:00)* → [[Bebop Vocabulary]], [[Altered Chords]]
- **The recurring "major 7 off the flat 3" device, isolated as a standalone reusable tool.** Named directly as "a really common thing to play over a minor chord" — build a major-7 arpeggio starting on the target minor chord's own ♭3 (E♭maj7 over Cm7 gives 9-♭3-5-7-9 of Cm9) rather than defaulting to the minor scale. Used in three of the four licks (2, 3, 4) over the identical Cm7 bar, each time landed and phrased slightly differently — the clearest evidence in the video that Ruch treats it as core vocabulary rather than an incidental choice. → [[Chord-Tone Soloing]] § *Three chromatic shapes for decorating a chord-tone arpeggio*, [[Extended Chords]]
- **Practice/demonstration structure, stated directly and followed consistently across all four licks:** play the lick against the backing track first, then break it down chord-by-chord with the reasoning named out loud, then show it dropped into a full improvised 12-bar B♭ blues solo so the isolated four-bar vocabulary is heard reintegrated into a real chorus — the same isolate-then-reintegrate teaching method [[The Key To Understanding A Jazz Blues]] uses for its one worked line, here run four times over.

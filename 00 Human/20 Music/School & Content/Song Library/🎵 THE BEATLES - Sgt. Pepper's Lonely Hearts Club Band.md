# Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** an **arch**, with both a bridge and a refrain in symmetry: Intro (4, intruding over ten seconds of hall ambience) · Verse (8, AAB) · instrumental Bridge (5, two uneven phrases) · Refrain (12, AA′B) · Bridge · Verse · Outro (2 bars on **IV** over a descending bass, segueing straight into the next track). Intros on a non-tonic chord are common in the catalogue; an outro on IV is not.

The harmonic engine is the archetypal Beatles progression **I – V-of-V – IV – I**, in use since "Eight Days A Week", whose entire character comes from a cross relation: the C♯ inside V-of-V immediately followed by the C♮ inside IV, which reads as deferred gratification ([[HAR-C07 — Secondary dominants]]). Because you assume the opening chord is I, the intro's shift from V-of-V to IV lands as genuinely strange.

The sharpest lesson is in the two bridges, which use the same four chords and **differ only by which one carries the seventh** ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]). C7–F–C–D reads in F, a relatively distant excursion; C–F7–C–D reads in C, with the seventh as bluesy colour rather than a structural dominant. Both are then re-heard as IV and V at home.

The refrain — the arch's keystone, and nothing but this section twice is the Side 2 "Reprise" — runs a **major/minor switch on I**, the minor version in first inversion ([[HAR-C08 — Borrowed chords and modal interchange]]). The tune is barely a notch above talking-blues patter, and that is the design: it leaves attention free for the production.

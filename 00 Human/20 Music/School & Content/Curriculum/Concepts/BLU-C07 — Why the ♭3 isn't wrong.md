# BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong

**Q.** The blues scale has a ♭3 but the chords have major 3rds. Why isn't that just wrong?

> [!question]- Answer
> **Three reasons.** 1) **The friction IS the sound** — the grind between ♭3 and ♮3 is what 'bluesy' means. 2) **Blues isn't functional major/minor** — the scale is imposed over the chords, not derived from them. 3) **The note is a region, not a pitch** — players bend the ♭3 up toward the ♮3 and sit between them. The blues has **microtonality built in**.

## Why it matters

Point 3 is what actually resolves the apparent contradiction — notation can only approximate what's played. It's also why pitch-correction software struggles with blues vocals ([[Vocal Tuning]]). And the underrated half: over a dominant, three of minor pentatonic's five notes are the chord's own tones (root, 5th, ♭7), so the ♭3 is the only note in it that deliberately fights the chord. It does **not** avoid the natural 4th — in A that's the D in A C D E G — which is the one note that genuinely clashes against a major 3rd; blues passes through it rather than resting on it, the same way it treats the ♭5. **Major** pentatonic is the scale that leaves the 4th out.

## Drill

Over an A7 vamp, play the ♭3 (C), then bend it toward C♯ and sit between them.

# Octopus's Garden — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (8) · Verse (a huge **32 bars**, four even phrases, AABA′) · Verse · instrumental break (16, transposed to IV) · Verse · Outro with a complete ending. The verse is effectively a whole verse/verse/bridge/verse module folded inside one section.

The entire song is **one four-chord cliché — I, vi, IV, V** — played at home and then transposed wholesale to IV for the break ([[POP-C02 — Why four chords is satisfying]]). Pollack calls it naive in the painterly sense and means it partly as praise: he flags barely-hidden parallel fifths and octaves between tune and bass, and is disappointed that the modulating section introduces no new material at all.

What keeps 32 bars of four chords standing up is that the **harmonic rhythm is staged across the section** — steady through the first two phrases, halved in the first half of phrase 3, then accelerated to close phrase 4. Phrase 3 begins on vi as the result of a [[HAR-C04 — Deceptive cadence]], and the outro parlays that same deception into a three-times-and-out ending. The break is a complete textbook pivot [[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]] to IV.

Tune and guitar riffs are almost purely pentatonic ([[SCL-C03 — Pentatonic scales]]) and mostly patter without much contour; the section gets an arch at all simply because **phrase 3 sits a register higher**. The verse layering changes exactly one variable per phrase — vocal doubling, piano entering, drum pattern — which is the whole arrangement plan.

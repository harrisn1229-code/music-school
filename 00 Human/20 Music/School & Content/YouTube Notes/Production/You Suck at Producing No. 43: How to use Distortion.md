# You Suck at Producing No. 43: How to use Distortion

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EolO6-evB2M)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EolO6-evB2M)

## Synopsis
A fast beginner tour of Ableton's built-in distortion options, framed around one demo sound (a clean 808) pushed through each device in turn. It opens with the fundamentals — hard clipping via a Utility gain overdrive (visualized on the oscilloscope as a sine wave's peaks getting sheared into a square wave, and on the spectrum analyzer as new harmonic overtones stacking above the fundamental) versus soft clipping via Saturator, which rounds the same waveform instead of shearing it. From there it runs through the rest of the stock "drive" menu — Saturator's curve types, Overdrive's built-in notch filter, a passing mention of Dynamic Tube, the Pedal stompbox emulation with its per-band gain and Fuzz mode, and finally Drum Bus pushed to Hard mode on a drum bus/master to distort a whole drum group at once. The most useful takeaway for someone new to distortion is the mental model: hard clip = square wave = harsh/aggressive, soft clip = rounded = warm/gentle, and every device on the list sits somewhere on that spectrum.

## Techniques
- **Hard clipping demonstrated via Utility's Gain knob** — pushing a Utility device's Gain knob past 0 dB on a sine-wave 808 shears the top and bottom of the waveform flat, turning it into a square wave (visible on the oscilloscope); the spectrum analyzer shows this as new harmonic overtones stacking above the clean fundamental — that's what makes a clipped signal sound "nasty"/aggressive rather than just louder. → [[Utility]], [[Clipping and Loudness]]
- **The same hard-clip square wave is reachable at the source, no distortion device needed** — swapping a synth oscillator's waveform directly to square produces essentially the same harsh, harmonic-rich result as hard-clipping a sine downstream. → [[Saturator]]
- **Saturator's Soft Clip curve rounds the waveform instead of shearing it** — a gentler, warmer alternative to hard clipping; turning up Drive pushes further into that rounded distortion without the harsh square-wave edges. → [[Saturator]]
- **Saturator's Digital Clip curve reproduces the harsh hard-clip sound inside Saturator itself** — switching Saturator's curve type to Digital Clip gets back to square-wave territory, showing Saturator covers the full hard↔soft spectrum in one device. → [[Saturator]]
- **Dynamic Tube named as an available option, not demonstrated with a setting** — mentioned only in passing as another distortion device in the stock arsenal ("I don't really use this one"), no concrete technique given. → [[Dynamic Tube]]
- **Overdrive's built-in notch filter adds crunch on top of drive** — Overdrive's resonant filter section is called out specifically for making an 808 "nice and crunchy" beyond what plain drive alone does. → [[Overdrive]]
- **Pedal (guitar-stompbox emulation) as a distortion option with per-band control** — turning up Pedal's Gain knob increases distortion same as any drive device, but it also exposes separate frequency-band controls so the low/mid/high content can be driven differently; switching on its Fuzz mode adds heavier, bass-emphasized distortion ("more base"). → [[Pedal]]
- **Drum Bus pushed to Hard mode with Drive maxed, distorts a whole drum bus/master at once** — rather than distorting one element at a time, routing Drum Bus onto a drum bus or the master and setting its Distortion mode to Hard with Drive all the way up distorts the entire drum group (not just the 808) in one move. → [[Drum Bus]]

# Hybrid Reverb

**Category:** Reverb/Delay
**Vendor:** Ableton

## What it is

Ableton's stock reverb that blends two engines in one device: a **Convolution** side (loads any sample as a real impulse response) and an **Algorithmic** side (five distinct synthetic algorithms), crossfaded or blended together.

## What it's used for

Everything from a straightforward vocal/pad space to using arbitrary samples (drum hits, whole loops, chord bounces) as impulse responses for tonal, rhythmic, or texture-driven reverb effects — well beyond a normal room/hall/plate reverb.

## FX chain notes

Used as a send for shared space, or as a direct **insert** for sound-design moves (freezing, convolution-as-resynthesis, drop-FX creation) — several of the techniques below specifically want it inserted rather than sent.

## Techniques

- **Multiple sources routed into SEPARATE instances loaded with the SAME impulse response, each with its own pre-convolution EQ and independent Decay, is the owned-lane way to build a shared "virtual room" for a whole ensemble — the mechanism behind Vienna MIR Pro's scoring-stage workflow.** A film-score mixer places every instrument group (violins, violas, harp, cello, bass, french horns) into its own convolution "shell"/insert feeding one identical impulse response — turning a pile of dry, separately-tracked instruments (same mic type, different rooms, only "continuity," not real shared space) into one consistent room. Two details worth stealing on this device specifically: (1) EQ the DRY signal **before** it hits the device rather than only shaping the wet return afterward — Meyerson calls this "the EQ feeding into the convolution," and it's why each instrument's tonal color inside the room differs even though they share one IR; (2) each instance's **Decay** can be set independently so every source gets its own tuned corner of the same space instead of one blanket tail length. Pan/width each dry source before the send too (see [[Width and Stereo]]) so position inside the shared room stays source-specific. → [[Reverb]], [[Depth and Space]], [[Width and Stereo]] — Alan Meyerson (Mix with the Masters), [[Using reverb to create space with Alan Meyerson]]
- **The foundational recipe underneath every convolution trick on this page: record a clap in the room, trim it, export, load it as the IR.** All you need is a clap or other sharp transient recorded in the room you want to emulate — a phone/portable recorder is enough — edited down to just the transient and its tail, exported as a WAV, then loaded into this device's **Convolution** side as the impulse response; the room becomes a permanent, reusable reverb setting from then on. The foley-as-IR, chord-as-IR, and "Dank Bank" personal-IR-library techniques below are all elaborations on this same basic move — real space instead of synthetic/creative source material. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]] — Sage Audio, [Reverb Masterclass](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9G1ME30K2ro)
- **Convolution side is the closest owned match to "the most realistic reverb possible" for a rare master-bus cohesion move.** For mastering-stage reverb (only when a whole mix reads disjointed — elements that sound recorded in different rooms — never when it's already blurred/masked), a video specifically calls for a realistic *convolution* reverb over a colored/algorithmic one, since it's touching 100% of the signal. Blend fully toward Convolution with a real-room IR (not a creative sample-as-IR) for that transparency. Chain position: after any upward compression on the master, before the peak limiter — dial the send in with the limiter engaged, since its makeup gain exposes tails that seemed subtle unlimited, and check both loud and sparse sections (automate the send if no single level covers both). Keep it very subtle — a cohesion nudge, not a wash; [[ValhallaRoom]] is the alternative if its Early/Late/Depth controls + EQ return suit the specific gap better. → [[Master Chain]], [[Reverb]] — Sage Audio, [Reverb on a Master: Everything You Need to Know](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vzJSKU7kGrU)
- **A barely-there send increase to "embed" an element into the space just ahead of a section change, rather than throwing it into a full-size reverb.** As part of a subtle "wet/dry drop" transition, a small Hybrid Reverb send increase on a cymbal/percussion element gives it just enough space to feel like it's opening up the room ahead of a new section — deliberately restrained rather than a dramatic wash, and paired with a [[Grain Delay]] return doing the same wet-increase move on the lead. → [[Grain Delay]], [[Depth and Space]] — Pick Yourself, [3 transition techniques pros use instead of risers](https://youtu.be/05A0Ez0IWAE)
- **Convolution-only mode turns any sample into a tonal "instrument" for a totally different source — Size doubles as a pitch control** — turn the **Blend** knob fully to the Convolution side (ignoring the Algorithmic side entirely) and drop in an arbitrary sample as the impulse response — a tonal one-shot (e.g. a grand piano hit) convolved with a rhythmic loop (a hi-hat pattern) makes the rhythm ring with that tonal character instead of just adding space to it. **Decay** trims tail length for a tighter result, **Attack** adds a soft fade-in, and **Size** doubles as a pitch control on the convolved result — smaller reads higher-pitched, bigger reads lower. → [[Randomized Rack Design]] — You Suck at Producing, [Turn Drum Loops into Melodies with Live 11's Hybrid Reverb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0l7PS7C1JNw) · [How To Morph One Sound Into Another (4 techniques)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jdXhjfQhk3Y)
- **Automate the un-automatable: group into a rack, duplicate the chain with a different IR, then LFO the Chain Selector to crossfade between two convolved "instruments"** — Attack, Decay, and Size can't be automated directly on the device itself, so group Hybrid Reverb into an Audio Effect Rack, duplicate the chain, and load a *different* impulse-response sample into the duplicate (ideally in the same key as the first) — the same source now yields two distinct convolved instruments in parallel. Drag each chain's small volume slider fully toward its own end (one fully left, one fully right) so the rack's **Chain Selector** becomes a literal blend control between them, then map an LFO to the Chain Selector for a hands-off, continuously drifting crossfade — **narrow the LFO's mapped range** so the sweep never fully loses either sound, keeping both textures present throughout instead of hard-cutting between them. → [[Randomized Rack Design]], [[Automation]] — You Suck at Producing, [Turn Drum Loops into Melodies with Live 11's Hybrid Reverb](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0l7PS7C1JNw)
- **Build a reusable "chord impulse" sample library, organized per key** — record fresh chord stabs for a progression, EQ/reverb them to taste, then export each one (Cmd+Shift+R) into a dedicated personal sample-collection folder for sound-design sessions (his own is nicknamed the "Dank Bank"), organized by key/chord (e.g. an "F# minor" subfolder). Drag those chord-impulse samples into Convolution-mode Hybrid Reverb instances (Size ~100–200, or down toward 50 for an octave-up character) the same way as the chord-following-convolution rack documented below, with a Scale MIDI device on the source track so playing stays in key. The payoff over a one-off convolution rack: a growing, reusable personal IR library instead of re-recording impulses from scratch on every new song. → [[Scale]] — dnksaus, [Color Bass and Future Sound Design in 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Izo8-oYXvOQ)
- **Reverse the source, foley-as-IR, then Beats-mode warp for a percussive texture layer** — reverse a whole granulated/textural sample, run it through Hybrid Reverb's **Convolution** side with a foley or other percussion one-shot dropped in as the impulse response (Convolution-only is fine), then consolidate the reversed clip, switch it to **Beats** mode with the **single-arrow** warp mode, and turn transient preservation down for a differently-shaped result; freeze and flatten once dialed in. Phaser/flanger and light envelope tweaks can go on top before freezing. → [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]], [[Granulator 3]] — dnksaus, [Texturing Your Tracks in Ableton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9Bab-gq4E00)
- **Freeze on the Algorithmic side for instant pads** — Algorithm mode (e.g. **Prism**), Dry/Wet 100%, then **Freeze**: pressing it after a sample starts playing freezes whatever's sounding at that instant; holding the small arrow continues freezing what was playing just before. Audition several slices/vowels of a vocal chop to find one that freezes cleanly — an instant vocal-pad source with no separate sustain-loop instrument needed. → [[Sound Palette]] — dnksaus, [reverb can be POWERFUL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gi_y81NmoGg)
- **Convolution mode: any sample as the room** — on the **Convolution** side, drop any sample in as the impulse response — the input takes on that sample's spectral/temporal shape. **Size** acts like pitch/length of the IR (50 = an octave up), **Decay** trims the tail. Dropping in a **drum loop at the song's BPM** as the IR creates a rhythmic, tonal delay-like effect; without a kick in the loop it reads like a timbre-rich vocoder without actually vocoding. → [[Vocoder]] — dnksaus, [reverb can be POWERFUL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gi_y81NmoGg)
- **Chord-following convolution texture** — bounce each chord of a progression to its own short audio clip, group a percussion loop's Convolution-mode Hybrid Reverb into a rack, duplicate the chain per chord, drop one bounced chord into each chain as its impulse response, then select all chains and **Distribute Ranges Equally** on the chain-select bar and map it to a macro — automating that macro makes the percussion loop's tonal color follow the chord changes. Shorten Decay to ~150 ms per chain for a cleaner result than convolving with a full loop; bump Size up an octave for extra color. → [[Randomized Rack Design]], [[Automation]] — dnksaus, [reverb can be POWERFUL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gi_y81NmoGg)
- **Pre-delay as a built-in slapback** — the Pre-Delay knob (present on both Convolution and Algorithmic sides) can be pushed to read as a slapback delay rather than a smeared tail; pair with a Note Length device on the source so short chops don't ring on long enough to obscure what the pre-delay is doing. → [[Delay]] — dnksaus, [reverb can be POWERFUL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gi_y81NmoGg)
- **Reverb + OTT + multiband compression = a drop "womp" one-shot** — take a plain synth zap/hit, drown it in Hybrid Reverb, run [[OTT]] after it, then a multiband compressor on top — the wash plus heavy compression turns a basic zap into a huge-impact drop one-shot. Works just as well on sub-bass as anything else — "don't put reverb on sub-bass" is gatekeeping. → [[OTT]], [[Multiband Dynamics]] — dnksaus, [reverb can be POWERFUL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gi_y81NmoGg)
- **Glitch-Atmos rack (Shimmer algorithm, no convolution)** — grouped with an auto-slicer Echo into a dry/wet chain-volume-fade rack for a self-mutating ambient/glitch generator. → [[Randomized Rack Design]] — dnksaus, [delay just became an AUTO SLICER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zAjmcohzIwc)
- **Sound-design tool, not just a mixing tool** — reached for on a chopped vocal phrase ahead of [[OTT]] specifically as creative sound design rather than glue/mix compression. → [[OTT]] — dnksaus, [the best knob in ableton's stock plugins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dcP02dHWgu4)

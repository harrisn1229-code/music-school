# Does Michael Jackson's Thriller Really Have 7 Basslines? Greg Phillinganes Explains

## Synopsis

A full-take walkthrough of all seven distinct bass parts layered across Thriller, performed live by Greg Phillinganes. Reconfirms single-vs-multi-trigger phrasing (already documented in depth from other videos in this series) and adds two genuinely new arrangement details: a cinematic-length sustained organ pedal bass, and orchestral-style "punctuation" bass layering.

## Techniques

- **Single-trigger mode preferred specifically for expressive bass phrasing** — reconfirms the woodwind-tonguing/legato-articulation framing from earlier videos in this series; Stevie Wonder's glide-based "growl" technique is cited as only achievable in single-trigger mode, since multi-trigger re-fires the filter on every note and kills the connected phrasing glide relies on.
- **A pipe-organ pedal bass held continuously through an ENTIRE pop-song outro** — unusually long sustain for pop, borrowed from cinematic/film-scoring convention rather than pop arrangement norms, described as giving the outro a "Gothic" power. Distinct from (and layered alongside) the separately-documented "monster growl bass" one-off sweep effect elsewhere in the song.
- **Occasional low-register "punctuation" bass hits layered against a sustained arco-style bass**, likened explicitly to adding pizzicato accents against a held orchestral bass line — used sparingly (every few bars) rather than continuously, for emphasis at structurally important moments (the opening fanfare's downbeats).
- **7 distinct bass layers total across the song** (synth bass, opening-fanfare-doubling bass, one-off orchestral-emulation intro bass, organ pedal bass, the "froggy" Korg MS-20-style bass, plus others), each with a specific structural role and window of use rather than all playing simultaneously — reinforces the layering-with-distinct-roles principle already documented from the "Billie Jean" bass stack.

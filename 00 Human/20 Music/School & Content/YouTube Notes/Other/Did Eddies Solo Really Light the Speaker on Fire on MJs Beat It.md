# Did Eddie's solo REALLY light the speaker on FIRE? On MJ's 'Beat It'

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6vH7Ot5XWWY)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6vH7Ot5XWWY)

## Synopsis

Matt Forger (recording engineer on Michael Jackson's sessions) confirms and corrects the studio-lore version of this story: during "Beat It" mix prep, an amplifier failure sent a DC power surge into a Westlake soffit-mounted monitor, overheating the woofer's voice coil until it glowed and smoked — not during Eddie Van Halen's actual guitar solo, and not a literal open flame (speaker cones are made fire-resistant, so it smolders rather than burns). Rod Temperton's dry-humor line about the song being "really hot" is what turned the incident into legend. No production or synthesis technique — studio history only.

## What's taught

- **Why a speaker "catches fire" is usually a DC-current fault, not the audio signal itself**: normal audio is AC (the cone actually moves back and forth); a power-supply/amplifier fault sending DC instead causes constant, non-oscillating current through the voice coil, overheating it like a toaster element rather than driving it musically.
- Speaker cones are deliberately made of fire-resistant/retardant paper specifically so a fault like this smolders (with an acrid burning smell) rather than becoming an open flame.

# Chord Progressions - Beginner's Guide - Part 3 (Minor Chord Formula)

## Synopsis

Part 3 mirrors Part 2 for minor chords: half-step formula (+3 +4), scale-degree equivalent (1-♭3-5), and the useful "one note apart" framing — a major chord flips to minor by lowering just the middle note (the 3rd) a half step, and vice versa. Also demonstrates building both formulas on a QWERTY-keyboard MIDI input and in Studio One's piano roll (Studio-One-specific, not relevant to Nate's Ableton workflow) and closes with a "practice building chords on random roots constantly" push that Part 5 turns into a formal task.

## What's taught

- **Minor chord formula: root, +3 half steps, +4 half steps** — the mirror of the major formula (+4 +3). Worked on C, F, A♭.
- **Major ↔ minor is a one-note move**: lower the *middle note* (the 3rd) of a major chord by one half step and it becomes minor; raise it back and it's major again. Fastest way to toggle a chord's quality without rebuilding it from scratch.
- **Scale-degree equivalent: a minor chord is 1-♭3-5 of its root's major scale** (vs. major's 1-3-5) — the ♭3 is the only altered scale degree.
- Two formula "languages" clarified: half-step counting (+4+3 / +3+4) for building chords fast, vs. scale-degree naming (1-3-5 / 1-♭3-5) for understanding bigger/extended chords later.
- QWERTY-keyboard MIDI input and Studio One piano-roll chord entry — DAW-specific, not applicable to Ableton.
- Closing practice push: build major and minor chords on random root notes repeatedly, by ear or by formula, until it's automatic.

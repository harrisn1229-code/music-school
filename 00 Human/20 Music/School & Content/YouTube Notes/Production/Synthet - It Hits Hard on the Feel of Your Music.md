## Synopsis

A sample-envelope/tail-control lesson framed around hi-hats: an open hi-hat sample that's allowed to ring past the next hat hit creates an impossible sound (a real drum kit's open hat physically cuts off when the closed hat strikes), so the sampler needs to be told to choke/cut itself off, or a zero-velocity note can be inserted to silence a ringing sample early. The broader point is that tail length — milliseconds of release — is a genuine feel decision, not just a technical cleanup, and applies to bass samples too where an overhanging low-end tail is especially ugly.

## Techniques

- **Choke/cut-itself groups fix impossible overlapping tails** — an open hi-hat sample overriding a following closed-hat hit is something a real drum kit physically can't do (the closed hat mutes the open one); in a sampler, enable the "cut itself" / choke option so a new hit on the same group truncates the previous sample's tail instead of letting it ring through. → [[1 Drums Hat Track]]
- **A zero-velocity note can silence a ringing sample early** — placing a note with zero velocity at the point you want a sound to stop cancels it out, an alternative to (or stack with) choke groups when you need a precise cutoff point.
- **Bass sample tails overriding each other are especially ugly** — the same overlapping-tail problem is worse in the low end; worth deliberate attention on bass sample tracks specifically. → [[Bass Design]]
- **Tail/release length is a feel decision, measured in milliseconds** — a "knob" controlling sample length/tail matters even at small changes; shorter/tighter vs. longer/looser tails read as genuinely different feels, not just louder/quieter. Author's own preference leans short/tight ("chuga-chuga" short release) over long overhanging tails.
- **Ctrl+L for a quick legato/tail shortening shortcut** — fast keyboard route to apply this adjustment without digging through menus each time.

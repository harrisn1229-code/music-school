# TN: 184 MYLES SMITH & PETER FENN - FULL VIDEO EPISODE!

![](https://www.patreon.com/posts/161939546)

## Synopsis

Artist Myles Smith and his co-writer/producer Peter Fenn walk through three songs from the album *My Mess, My Heart, My Life* — **Stargazing** (the Billie Eilish-conquering breakout single), **My Mess** (the title track, written on a mandolin Peter learned in ten minutes), and **Dying Day** (the album closer) — plus a brief unbroken-down clip of **Mary's Song**. This is a mobile-writing-camp workflow start to finish: almost everything is built on the road (Airbnbs, hotel rooms, a heat-wave-stricken house in Oslo) on a bare-bones rig (a desk, an Apollo, a Neumann U87, some guitars, a MIDI controller), mixed as they go rather than left for a mix engineer, and leans hard on UAD Spark's plugin-subscription suite because it needs nothing but the internet — no Apollo/DSP hardware required away from the "anchor" studio. Companion piece to [[TN 184 Myles Smith & Peter Fenn - Gear List]] — this episode is where nearly every item on that list gets real, song-specific technique behind it.

## Techniques

### Mobile writing/production rig (applies across the whole album)
- **Bare-bones road setup, deliberately:** a desk, a small [[Universal Audio Apollo Twin MkII]], a Neumann U87 (not owned) for Miles' vocal, "a bunch of guitars," a MIDI controller, some speakers — chosen specifically to keep the room feeling like a living room, not a studio, because pressure kills the writing. Peter: "90% of our songs are written in Airbnbs." → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]].
- **[[UAD Spark]] (unowned, added to Wishlist from the Gear List page) is the workhorse that makes this possible** — a native plugin-subscription suite that needs only an internet connection, no Apollo/DSP hardware, so the exact same chains travel from the anchor studio to a hotel room. Named favorites: Decapitator, the Lexicon Reverb, Capitol Chambers, the X 1176 compressor, Hitsville EQ, NS1.
- **Remote co-writing over FaceTime** is the default when apart (Peter in LA, Miles in London) — described as "we're always in the studio together somehow." For live-streamed audio specifically (not just video call), Peter uses **[[Audiomovers LISTENTO]] (unowned, added to Wishlist)**, "sound movers," to hear what's happening in the room in real time from anywhere, including guitar takes recorded on tour buses. → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]].
- **Mixing happens live, all the way through — no separate mix stage.** Peter keeps a running master-bus chain on as he produces (see Master bus below) specifically so what he hears while writing is close to the final master; final mix engineer Michael Freeman works from **a frozen session Peter sends**, not printed stems — a deliberate choice ("the mix is such a part of it as we go along... we don't need someone to recreate it"). Reference-checked by day-bouncing to his car. → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]].
- **Vocal mic choice is a raw-texture decision, not a fidelity one.** They tried "clean" mics early on and rejected them for polishing away the grit they want; a lot of ad-libs/BVs specifically go through the (unowned) [[Shure SM7]] "just because it sounds raw," and one full song ("Solo") was tracked on an iPhone for the same reason. On *Dying Day*, Miles' whole lead vocal ended up on the SM7 (not the usual U87) simply because the SM7 let him sit back and relax into the take. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]].
- **The guitar that starts 90% of their songs is a single, unmaintained Fender-style acoustic** Peter bought at a shop in Santa Cruz in 2011 and has never restrung — "someday kids are gonna be like that's the guitar."

### Stargazing — arrangement/motif
- **Motifs are built from repurposed post-choruses, not written fresh for the intro.** Their recurring trick, named explicitly as "our thing": take the song's post-chorus hook and move it to the front of the arrangement so the first thing a listener hears is the most memorable part — reasoned from both having live backgrounds (imagining "the front of Wembley" the moment the lights go off and the song starts). → [[Arrangement and Song Form]].
- **Every chorus repeat is deliberately different**, on this song and as a rule across the album: chorus 1 drops the kick out entirely and lets the piano/vocal breathe, chorus 2 is full production (all elements in), the final chorus strips back down again. Rationale: repeating an identical chorus reads as lazy to a listener even though the melody/lyrics have to repeat — vary the production instead. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]].
- **A doubled kick was removed from the pre-chorus on purpose** ("we wanted it to be felt more than heard") specifically so reintroducing the double in the chorus makes the song audibly widen — a deliberate held-back element as a arrangement lever, not just a mix choice.
- Reverse piano + reverse crash used as the transition device out of the intro into the first verse, for a "suction" pull into the next section. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]].
- A short "orchestral swoosh" transition sample, sourced from Splice years ago and kept in a personal curated sample folder ever since, used as a recurring pre-chorus signal ("here's the chorus, we're getting to it").

### Stargazing — intro instrumentation
- **Two live pianos layered** — a bass-octave-notes piano plus a separate chords piano, tracked on the same unbranded piano at the manager's studio "every song... I don't even know what kind of brand it is." Light compression via [[UAD UA 1176LN]] plus the **[[UAD Spark]] plugin suite**.
- **A recurring "piano drone" motif** (a sustained low piano hit) became the turning-point element that convinced Peter the song was special — added mid-write on a hunch, tested through playback speakers, and it "felt electric." Notable because there is no snare in this song at all; the drone plus a four-on-the-floor kick supplies all the rhythmic urgency.
- Acoustic guitar strums on the same never-restrung guitar, mic'd with a **Soyuz pencil mic (unowned, no note yet)** specifically because "the source sound is so important" to their whole workflow (source-first philosophy over fix-in-post). Chain: **[[Waves NS1]] (unowned)** for noise removal, then **[[UAD Lexicon Reverb]] (unowned)** — both via the [[UAD Spark]] suite.
- **[[Decapitator]]** run on the intro piano — called out by name as "a favorite plugin." → [[Decapitator]].

### Stargazing — bass
- **Two simultaneous basses, a recurring pattern in their arrangements:** a sustained sub-bass playing quarter notes (a Serum patch called "Base Subway"), plus a live bass playing eighth notes for movement/character. → [[Serum 2]], [[Layering and Stacking]].
- **The sub bass is sidechain-pulsed via [[Kickstart 2]] on essentially every song, even in a four-on-the-floor track like this one** — deliberately turning a sustained bass note into a rhythmic pulse "to bring movement from something that's usually so static." Peter's framing: the pumping is *felt*, not consciously heard, and is a major reason the track feels physically danceable live. → [[Sidechaining]].
- Live bass compression is light — a preset called **"Bass Squeeze" on the [[UAD Spark]] X 1176 compressor**, used specifically on the bass bus. → [[UAD UA 1176LN]].
- The signature transient-heavy one-shot bass used throughout the album is a Splice one-shot called "Dip Bass," chosen specifically for a transient character that "punches through" alongside their kick sound. → [[Sound Selection Guide]].

### Stargazing — drums/percussion
- **Different kick samples for the pre-chorus vs. the chorus** — a smaller, near-processing-free kick in the pre ("there's literally nothing on this kick drum"), swapped for a thicker "stadium"/"massive" kick in the chorus, layered with an extra snare on the four-on-the-floor pattern.
- **Tambourine layered directly under the kick specifically to add transient** the kick alone lacks — "the tambourine just gives the kick so much transient." Shaker and tambourine both tracked live (two different players, "every song") rather than programmed.
- **[[UAD Capitol Chambers]] (unowned)**, described as "a UAD Sparks plugin I absolutely love," run as a light reverb send on the shaker/tambourine bus for room feel — first of several sightings of this plugin as their go-to reverb across the whole session. → [[Reverb]].
- **8–13 live hand claps recorded per chorus, panned randomly and deliberately left at uneven, non-uniform loudness** — the unevenness is intentional, meant to preserve "the feeling of the room" rather than sounding like one clean, quantized clap sample. → [[Layering and Stacking]].

### Stargazing — vocals ("Milesauce")
- **A named, repeatable ad-lib layer they call "Milesauce":** on top of the standard lead + doubles + hard-panned-doubles stack, Miles physically steps back from the mic and shouts/moves around it for a fourth, more aggressive texture layer — distinct from a normal double, treated as its own instrument. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]].
- **A dedicated saved chain specifically for Milesauce: Decapitator (driven hard) → 1176 → Valhalla reverb (specific plugin not named in the transcript).** Demoed dry vs. wet — the Decapitator drive is pushed hard enough to audibly distort the vocal on purpose, described as making an already deep voice sound "thick." → [[Decapitator]], [[UAD UA 1176LN]].
- **[[Leapwing DynOne]] (unowned) — "a big time sauce" — run as a multiband compressor across the entire vocal bus** (every vocal element, not per-track), described as subtle but "brings out the transients"; Peter says he rarely sees other people using it. First sighting of this plugin in the session. → [[Multiband Dynamics]].
- Raw-texture mic philosophy reiterated here: even on the "biggest" song of the album, the goal was to keep vocals feeling "raw and natural" rather than polished, partly through mic choice (SM7 for some layers) and partly through the layering itself.

### Stargazing — master bus (Peter's live-mixing chain)
- **A standing master-bus chain kept on throughout production, not applied at the end:** [[UAD Hitsville EQ Collection]] (unowned) on a preset simply labeled "Mix," tweaked slightly (small adjustments to highs and mids), plus **[[God Particle]] (Cradle, unowned — already vetted-and-skipped for purchase, see Wishlist) with its own limiter dialed back**. Peter demoed it A/B: the Hitsville preset audibly brightens the mix. Framed explicitly as "printing it in the mix" — the mix bus processing bleeds into the final master rather than being a separate mastering pass. → [[Master Chain]].

### My Mess — writing & foundation
- **The whole song's foundation is a mandolin part Peter learned to play in roughly ten minutes**, bought and found in Oslo specifically for this session on a bandmate's suggestion, then written around within the hour. The mandolin take that made the final master is intentionally "a little sloppy but live and real" — no re-tracking. → [[Creative Process and Idea Capture]].
- **Six-string banjo layered in verse 2**, played by Peter, added later in his LA studio (not part of the original Oslo session).
- Verse drums are a hybrid: **live percussion (shaker, brush snare) layered against programmed "in the box" drum samples** deliberately spliced together — their stated philosophy is that live drums alone lose the "snappiness" the songs need, so even fully-live-sounding sections keep a programmed drum layer underneath for tightness. → [[Layering and Stacking]].
- **Live group stomps and claps recorded with 6–7 people around one mic**, direct reference to the lyric ("slamming doors"), intended to put the listener physically in the room. Light SSL-brand compression (specific plugin/hardware not named) applied to the stomp bus.

### My Mess — verse/chorus instrumentation
- **Mandolin doubled left/right**, both takes panned hard, run through roughly the same **1176 + [[UAD Capitol Chambers]]** chain as Stargazing's vocal reverb (Capitol Chambers again singled out as "the plug-in that I love"). Acoustic guitar strums layered under it through the same **Soyuz pencil mic**.
- **A live piano part recorded after the Oslo session**, back at Peter's LA studio, played by a friend ("Cajon" credited in the transcript, possibly a mis-transcribed name).
- The same **Splice "Dip Bass" one-shot** from Stargazing reused here, again for its punch-through transient.
- **Live "gang" background vocals recorded outdoors with 6–7 people around one mic**, built up starting small and layered with recurring "oh" vocal patterns — a technique Peter names explicitly as one he reaches for often because open vowel sounds "sing so well" as a supporting pattern under a lead melody. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]].

### My Mess — vocal bus
- **[[Leapwing DynOne]] again on the vocal bus, paired this time with [[Soothe2]] using a saved preset called "DS"** specifically to tame harshness/sibilance without a dedicated de-esser — second sighting of DynOne in the session, confirming it's a standing element of their vocal-bus chain rather than a one-off. → [[Soothe2]], [[Multiband Dynamics]].
- No Milesauce layer on this song at all — a deliberate restraint call, contrasted directly against Stargazing: "on an intimate song like this, you just gotta do what it calls for." Every hook is still doubled at minimum, described as a fixed rule across the whole album regardless of how intimate a song is.

### My Mess — bridge (EBow layer)
- **A stack of multiple EBow passes builds the entire bridge atmosphere**, inspired directly by Bon Iver's use of the instrument — described as wanting "atmospherical" more than any specific note content. Layered with tremolo-heavy live strings from a guest string player (credited as "Lily" in the transcript) for the same emotional/atmospheric build. → [[EBow]], [[Ambient Texture and Field Recording]].
- Vocals in the bridge are one continuous take from Miles, deliberately unpolished — the "rawest" vocal on the album by Peter's own description, made possible because it was recorded at the tail end of a multi-day writing camp when Miles' voice was genuinely worn out, which matched the song's emotional content. They tried re-cutting in a different key and reverted back to the original take both times specifically to preserve that vocal exhaustion. → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]].

### Dying Day — vocals & arrangement
- **Miles' entire lead vocal was tracked on the (unowned) [[Shure SM7]] instead of their usual U87**, purely because it let him sit back and relax into a more natural performance — a repeatable "performability beats fidelity" mic-choice rule, not a tonal preference. → [[Vocal Mic Technique]].
- **A doubled-octave vocal texture: upper-octave falsetto plus a lower-octave part layered together**, described as neither octave working alone (upper felt too thin, lower not delicate enough) — used together specifically because the blend reads differently from either part in isolation. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]].
- **Song written and demoed entirely on electric guitar, then rebuilt around acoustic** — Peter kept the original electric-guitar work tape and reintroduces the electric guitar later in the arrangement (faded in ahead of the bridge) rather than discarding it, effectively recycling the demo instrument as a deliberate late-song texture layer.
- **A full drop-out on the "one"** at the pre-hook — noted by Peter as the first time they'd used that specific arrangement device. Song is in 3/4, unusual for their catalog.
- **[[iZotope VocalSynth 2]] (unowned) used as a chordal vocoder** in the bridge — the vocoder plays actual chord voicings (root, third and fifth), not a single pitched line, layered under a Milesauce pass and the lead. Second sighting of this plugin in the session (also on the Gear List page), and the first with a real technique behind it. → [[iZotope VocalSynth 2]].
- **A ~20-vocal-part female backing/harmony stack, recorded remotely via voice memos** and assembled by Peter — the guest vocalist sang roughly seven parts an octave below the main harmony, doubled, sent back as individual voice-note files, then stacked into a ~4-second climax at the very end of the song. → [[Vocal Stacks and Ad-libs]].

### Dying Day — bass & guitars
- **A real 1960s [[Hofner B-Bass H-Series|Hofner bass]] used to play actual chord voicings rather than root notes** — a deliberate departure from a typical root-note bassline, distorted along with the rest of the bridge instrumentation to intentionally thin out the low end and free up room for the vocals. → [[Hofner B-Bass H-Series]].
- **DI'd electric guitars run through [[Neural DSP Archetype: Cory Wong X]] (unowned)** for a heavier, distorted crunch in the bridge — tracked dry in Oslo, the amp modeling applied afterward. Second sighting of this plugin (also on the Gear List page), first with real technique. → [[Neural DSP Archetype: Cory Wong X]].
- **Live drums layered over programmed drums specifically for extra "air"** on the crashes/hits — no sidechaining involved between drums and vocal; balance is handled purely by level/gain-staging, by Peter's own description, not automation or ducking.
- **The distorted bass is a deliberate low-end-management move**, not just a tone choice: distorting it audibly shrinks its perceived low-end footprint, opening space for the vocal to sit on top — confirmed by A/B soloing/muting.
- Bridge climax layers live horns, live strings (same guest player, "Lily"), the ~20-part female vocal stack, and a heavily distorted guitar all at once — deliberately "throwing a lot of paint at the wall" for the album's emotional peak.

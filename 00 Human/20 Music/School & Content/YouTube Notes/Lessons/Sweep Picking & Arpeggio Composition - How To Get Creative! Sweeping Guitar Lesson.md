## Synopsis

A composition-focused sweep-picking lesson: how a sweep-picked lead section is actually written against a chord progression, using a worked advanced example in F♯ minor as the walkthrough.

## Techniques

- **Emphasize the underlying chord progression in the solo/sweep line**, not just an arpeggio shape run independently — whenever the backing has a clear progression, the sweep should audibly track it.
- **Worked harmony:** in F♯ minor, the diatonic chords are F♯m, G♯dim, A, Bm, C♯m, D, E. The progression used: **i – VI – III – VII** (F♯m – D – A – E), then a **dominant substitution** — the tonic's minor 3rd is raised to a major 3rd to create a **C♯ dominant 7** chord, adding tension before resolving back to the tonic.
- **Backing-vs-lead relationship:** rhythm guitars play plain power chords under the progression, but the composer keeps the *full* harmonic concept (7ths, quality) in mind while writing the sweep-picked lead over it — the lead carries harmonic information the rhythm part doesn't spell out.
- **Practical takeaway:** treat a sweep-picking section as a composition exercise first (what chord, what quality, what tension/resolution) and a picking-technique exercise second — the shapes chosen should be driven by the harmony, not picked for technical display alone.

# The Inner Light — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (ad-lib drone plus a harpsichord pickup, then 10 bars in tempo) · Verse (an odd **13 bars**, short phrases as AA′A′A′BCC′) · Break · Verse · Break′, extended to a full ending. The two breaks are built on the intro's foundation and their backing tracks are close to identical takes of the same material.

The purest of Harrison's three Indian records — backing cut entirely in Bombay, lyric inward-facing, form alternating almost mechanically between instrumental and sung blocks. Harmonically it is **about as close to a drone as you can get while still using more than one chord**: Mixolydian in name, but the mode's calling card never shows up, because **there is no dominant function anywhere — no V, and not even ♭VII**. The vocabulary is I against ii or IV and nothing else, with everything else carried by the drone ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]). The one cadential gesture is the lead instrument's upward run to scale degree 5 at the end of every section, which sets the sense of home unusually high in the register.

The backing material is regimented into descending four-note scale fragments, each answered by a three-note ascent; the sung tune is deliberately the opposite, moving in thirds, fourths and one fifth.

Texture articulates the form as reliably as chords would ([[ARR-C04 — Arrangement interest is scheduled absence]]): the breaks carry a deep drone, percussion and oboe leads, while **the verses drop the low drone and the tambourine, add flutes, and hold the oboe back until the last phrase**. Pollack's aside is worth keeping: perceived length tracks formal complexity, not duration.

# What You're Doing — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (**8 bars in two layered halves** — four bars of **solo drums** playing a marching fanfare, a first for them, then the band entering with the ostinato twice) · Verse (8, or **9 when another verse follows**, with a two-bar ostinato restatement overlapping the last bar) · Verse · Bridge (8, parsed 2+2+4) · Verse · Verse (guitar solo) · Bridge · Verse · Outro (a triple *petit reprise*, then the intro replayed in reverse layering).

Standard form, five chords, and yet what the essay keeps returning to is **the sheer volume of melodic dissonance** — appoggiaturas, suspensions and free non-resolving sevenths and ninths, enough to turn most of those plain triads into added-sixth and extended sonorities ([[HAR-C09 — Non-harmonic tones]]). That is what makes the song read jazzy rather than hymn-like.

The home key is established **entirely by I and IV**. Apart from vi, no other chord appears at all until the bridge, and **V is withheld right to the end of it** — which then stops wide open on that dominant, set up by V-of-V, so the section's whole job is to motivate the verse's return ([[HAR-C05 — Half cadence]]). Both sections lean on alternating vi and IV, the device Paul would push further in "Drive My Car".

The tune leans hard on the bluesy ♭3, which cross-relates with the major third inside the tonic chords every time the hook lands ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]). One subtle distinction worth having: **the ostinato enters syncopated from the previous bar's "four-and" while the tune starts squarely on the downbeat** — same material, two identities.

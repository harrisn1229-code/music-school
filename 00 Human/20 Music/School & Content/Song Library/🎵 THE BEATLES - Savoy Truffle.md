# Savoy Truffle — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (a drum fill, then 2 bars vamping on E) · Verse (**12 bars**, three roughly parallel phrases) · Refrain (a single **4-bar phrase**) · Intro′ · Verse · Refrain · Bridge (8, AA) · Verse (instrumental) · Refrain · Bridge · Verse · Refrain · Outro with a complete ending. Every section is **harmonically open** — verse and bridge both end on V — which is why nothing settles.

The song is filed in G major only because that is where it stops. **It actually lives in the parallel pair E major / E minor**, and the swing to G at the end of the refrain is close to a non sequitur ([[POP-C08 — Relative-key switching]]). Under the exotic surface, I–IV–V is still the skeleton of both verse and bridge; the F♯ and G chords are helpers, neither native to E major, and each is **entered by whole-tone root motion** — up to F♯, down to G. The signature colour is root movement by a major or minor third between non-diatonic chords, with chromatic scale riffs as connective tissue ([[POP-C11 — Chromatic mediants and pop modulation]]).

The refrain is the pivot machinery: home shifts to the parallel minor, which makes the relative major an easy step away, and G is then confirmed only by a **plagal cadence** — deliberately weak, which is why the new key never feels earned ([[HAR-C03 — Plagal cadence]]). Blues content comes from the near-minor third sung against the major I ([[BLU-C07 — Why the ♭3 isn't wrong]]). Best detail: beat 4 of the last bar has carried a pickup all song, and this time it is left silent.

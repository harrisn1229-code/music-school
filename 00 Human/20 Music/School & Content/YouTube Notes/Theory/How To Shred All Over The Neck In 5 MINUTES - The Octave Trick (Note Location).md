# How To Shred All Over The Neck In 5 MINUTES - The Octave Trick (Note Location)

## Synopsis

A companion piece to [[Octave and Unison Shapes for Fretboard Navigation]] and this batch's own [[Easy Note Location Tricks - Guitar Fretboard Memorization Exercises]] — instead of one triangle repeated 3 times, this video walks a small symmetric 6-note scale fragment via octave jumps all the way from the 3rd fret (low E) to the 23rd fret (high E), covering the whole usable neck without ever referencing a scale-box diagram.

## What's taught

- **Derive the octave shape from a power chord you already know.** A power chord = root + perfect 5th; remove the 5th and add the same root note one octave higher instead, and you have the octave shape (played with the pinky above the root, same physical relationship as the omitted 5th). Framed as the fastest on-ramp for players who don't yet consciously know the octave shape but already play power chords.
- **A deliberately truncated 6-note scale fragment, chosen specifically for symmetry.** From G minor, only 6 of the 7 notes are used (worked: G-A-B♭ on the low E string, then the identical fret pattern C-D-E♭ on the A string) — dropping to 6 notes makes the two-string chunk perfectly repeatable, which is what makes the whole-neck version below work cleanly.
- **Copy-paste the fragment up the neck via octave jumps, covering the full fretboard from a single small shape.** Starting at G (3rd fret, low E), the same 6-note fragment is repeated at each successive octave position (found via the octave-shape relationship above) all the way to the 23rd fret on the high E string — one small, memorized shape stamped across the whole neck rather than a sequence of separate scale boxes.
- **Portable to any scale, not just this 6-note G minor fragment** — explicitly suggested as homework to apply the same octave-jump-and-repeat method to the 5-note pentatonic scale or the full 7-note natural minor scale. Caution given: think about the actual notes being played at each repetition, not just the moved shape, or the exercise degrades into blind pattern-shifting.

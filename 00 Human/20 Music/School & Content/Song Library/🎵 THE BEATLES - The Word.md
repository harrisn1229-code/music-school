# The Word — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro · Verse (a straight **12-bar blues frame**) · Bridge (**4 bars only**) · Verse · Bridge (instrumental) · Verse (partly instrumental) · Outro (a repeat of the instrumental bridge, rapid fade). Underneath a loud, inventive production sits a twelve-bar blues and a four-bar tag — that is the whole song, and the direct structural ancestor of "All You Need Is Love".

The labels break down in a useful way: what functions as the verse behaves like a refrain, because the same lyric closes every one of them, which starts to make the bridge sound like the real verse. The verse is I, IV and V only, with appoggiaturas and an arpeggiated bass decorating the V→IV move in bars 9–10 ([[BLU-C01 — The 12-bar form]]). The bridge runs I – ♭VII – ♭III – IV, where the ♭VII functions as **V-of-♭III** — odd only because home is major; in the parallel minor it would be ordinary ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]).

The best device is in that bridge: a four-note ostinato under fuzz guitar runs through every bar while the chords move beneath it, so **each note's consonance is rewritten chord by chord** — a free seventh over one, a 9–8 appoggiatura over the next, the most consonant support over the third, the most dissonant over the fourth ([[POP-C03 — Pedal point]]).

The outro hides its own downbeat: the harmonium sustains through bars 1–2 of the final verse so the vocal re-entry lands on bar 3 and reads as some new section. It isn't.

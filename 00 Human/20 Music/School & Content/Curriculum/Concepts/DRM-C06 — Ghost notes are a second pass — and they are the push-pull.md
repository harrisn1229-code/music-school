# DRM-C06 — Ghost notes are a second pass — and they are the push-pull

**Q.** When do you write ghost notes, and what do they actually do to a listener?

> [!question]- Answer
> **After** the main pattern is finished — kick, snare and basic hat complete, then a distinct second pass hunting for the gaps where nothing is hitting. Mechanically: the ear leans toward an expected strong beat, gets a syncopated ghost hit instead, and that **displaced anticipation is the pleasurable tension of groove**. Not decoration — the mechanism.

## Why it matters

Writing them simultaneously with the main pattern produces clutter, because you can't see the gaps until the anchors exist. And knowing *why* they work tells you where to put them: at the point of maximum expectation, not evenly.

## Drill

Finish a plain kick/snare/hat pattern. Second pass only: add very-low-velocity ghost snares in the gaps. Count how many you deleted again.

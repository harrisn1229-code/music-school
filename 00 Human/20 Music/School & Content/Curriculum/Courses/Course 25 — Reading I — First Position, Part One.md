# Course 25 — Reading I — First Position, Part One

The first half of Section One of Leavitt's *A Modern Method for Guitar, Volume 1* — pages 3 to 29, where the notation arrives and gets read in the open first position: the staff, the note values, the key of C in both registers, accidentals, eighth notes, rests, ties and dots, and the first two pieces the book calls solos.

**What it assumes**

- Nothing. Leavitt starts from zero, and page 3 is where it starts.
- A guitar, [[A Modern Method for Guitar - Volume 1.pdf]] open on a stand, and a metronome.
- No theory. This course teaches no ideas and tests none — it is the place you play the page.

| Lesson | Title | Pages | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | First Position in the Key of C | 3–14 | performance check |
| 2 | Accidentals, Eighths and the First Solos | 15–29 | performance check |

**What this unlocks**

- [[Course 26 — Reading I — First Position, Part Two]] — pages 30 to 59, the rest of Section One: six more major keys, the relative minor, sixteenths and triplets, and the eleven movable chord forms the section ends on.
- The complete first position in the key of C, upper register and lower, ledger lines included.
- Accidentals and their scope, eighth notes, rests, ties and dotted notes — read at tempo rather than defined.
- The rhythm-accompaniment track from its first chord symbols (p. 11) through bass notes and chords (p. 17) to its first chord diagrams (p. 24).

## How this course is different, on purpose

Every other course in the school teaches *ideas* and tests them in words. This one teaches nothing new — it is the place you **play the page**.

| | [[Course 04 — Reading & Position Play]] (Class 2) | This course (Class 8) |
|---|---|---|
| Teaches | Notation *as concepts* — dots, `D.S. al Coda`, clefs, why guitar reading is hard | Nothing new; it is reps |
| You do | Read a note, answer a question | Open the book and *play the page* |
| Uses the book as | One cited source among several | The spine — worked through in order |

Same pages can appear in both. Course 04's ledger-lines lesson may *teach from* p. 12; this course has you *reading* p. 12 at tempo. Leave both.

## How to run it

- **The book is four courses, two lessons each, one lesson a week.** Section One is this course and [[Course 26 — Reading I — First Position, Part Two]]; Section Two is [[Course 27 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part One]] and [[Course 28 — Reading II — Position Playing, Part Two]]. A lesson is twelve to twenty book pages and up to twenty pieces, which is a week of reading rather than an evening's.
- **It teaches reading through repertoire, not drills.** Concept pages are sparse — across pages 3 to 29 it is pp. 3, 4, 12, 15, 18 and 21, and that is the whole of it. The rest is melodies, duets and etudes you sight-read, and progress comes from playing them.
- **The duets need a second part.** Leavitt is explicit that there is no student/teacher division — both guitar parts are written to be studied, and almost all of them stand alone musically. A recording, a teacher, or DAW playback of the other line all work.
- **Review is not optional.** *"Regular review of all material is a must!"* is printed at p. 9, and again at the foot of p. 18 shortened to *"Review of all material is a must"*; the notices get more insistent later in the book — *"REVIEW ALL MATERIAL"* at pp. 37 and 52. Finished pages stay in rotation.
- **From the preface:** *"Do not skip or 'slight' anything, and also do not attempt to 'completely perfect' any one lesson before going on. Playing technique is an accumulative process."*
- **Page numbers below are the book's own printed numbers.** One of them is absent from the vault's scan — p. 15 (Sharps and Flats). It is named in the homework anyway, because it is part of the lesson; source it elsewhere or skip it knowingly.
- **Where the reading lane had reached, as of 2026-08-20: page 24** — mid-Lesson 2. Everything past it sits ahead of that on purpose; that is the point of having the whole book.

## The lessons

### Lesson 1 — First Position in the Key of C

**The material**

- **Pages 3–14.** The whole of the first position in C, upper register then lower, closing on a full review.
- **Concept pages** — p. 3 tuning, the staff, the clef sign and the common note values; p. 4 time signatures; p. 12 ledger lines. Chord symbols and the rhythm-accompaniment track open on p. 11.
- **The instruction that governs the fingering numbers**, printed on p. 5: *"'READ' the notes, NOT the fingering, as these numbers will eventually be omitted."*

**Homework**

- [ ] **Play** — Exercises 1–4, pp. 4–5. The first-position notes as the book introduces them, one string group at a time.
- [ ] **Play** — *Sea To Sea* (duet), pp. 6–7. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — Exercises 5–6, p. 8. The upper register, starting on C an octave above the 5th-string C.
- [ ] **Play** — Exercises 7–8, p. 9, the *Note And Chord Review*.
- [ ] **Play** — *One, Two, Three, Four* (duet), pp. 10–11. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — the *Rhythm Accompaniment* introduction, p. 11. The first chord symbols, strummed in time.
- [ ] **Play** — Exercise 9, p. 12. The lower register, which is where the ledger lines arrive.
- [ ] **Play** — Exercise 10, p. 13. The review that closes the complete first position in the key of C.
- [ ] **Play** — *Imitation Duet*, p. 14. Both parts.
- [ ] **Check** — a cold read of an unseen page at or below p. 14, once, at tempo, with no practice run.

### Lesson 2 — Accidentals, Eighths and the First Solos

**The material**

- **Pages 15–29.** Accidentals and their scope, dynamics, eighth notes, rests, ties and dots — and the first two pieces the book calls solos, where melody and accompaniment land in the same part.
- **Concept pages** — p. 15 sharps, flats and naturals; p. 18 eighth notes, counted and picked; p. 21 rests, tied notes and dotted notes. Rhythm accompaniment gains bass notes and chords on p. 17 and its first chord diagrams on p. 24.
- **What a solo means here**, from the heading on p. 23: a *"solo arrangement… with melody AND accompaniment"*, with the standing instruction to hold every note for its full time value.
- **Page 15 is missing from the vault's scan.** Sharps, flats and naturals are covered from another source or read in a physical copy — the pieces from p. 16 on assume them.

**Homework**

- [ ] **Play** — *Here We Go Again* (duet), pp. 16–17. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — *Rhythm Accompaniment: Bass Notes and Chords*, p. 17.
- [ ] **Play** — the eighth-note exercises 1–3, pp. 18–19, counted aloud and picked as marked.
- [ ] **Play** — *Etude No. 1* (duet), p. 20. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — the rests, ties and dotted notes exercises, p. 21.
- [ ] **Play** — *Etude No. 2* (duet), p. 22. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — *First Solo*, p. 23. Melody and accompaniment together, every note held its full value.
- [ ] **Play** — *Rhythm Accompaniment: Chord Diagrams*, p. 24, and the exercises under it.
- [ ] **Play** — *Second Solo*, p. 25.
- [ ] **Play** — *Etude No. 3* (duet), p. 26. Both parts.
- [ ] **Play** — *Picking Etude No. 1*, p. 27, and the *Etude* below it on the same page.
- [ ] **Play** — *Two, Two* (duet), pp. 28–29. Both parts, and mind the `D.S. al Coda`.
- [ ] **Check** — a cold read of an unseen page at or below p. 29, once, at tempo, with no practice run.

## Course final

A **performance check**, not a test page. There are no `type: school-test` pages in this course and nothing here enters the spaced-repetition drip — sight reading is a motor skill, and the drip is for facts.

The check is a rep. Open the book at an unseen page at or below p. 29, play it **once, at tempo, with no practice run**, and keep an even tempo through the wrong notes rather than stopping to fix them. Pass is your own honest read of whether it held together. Nothing in the system can hear the guitar, so nothing here pretends to grade it.

Reading decays the way ear training does. The course closing is not a reason to stop reading — it is the point at which [[Course 26 — Reading I — First Position, Part Two]] takes over and these pages become review material.

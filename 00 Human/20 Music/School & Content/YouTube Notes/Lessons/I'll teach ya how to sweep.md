# I'll teach ya how to sweep

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AXsjSYeXdKE)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AXsjSYeXdKE)

## Synopsis

Brandon D'Eon runs through the mechanical fundamentals of sweep picking — pick grip, right-hand placement, the sweeping motion itself, muting, and a couple of practical gear tricks — framed as a checklist of mistakes intermediate players commonly make. Lighter on musical content than his other lessons; this is a pure mechanics/troubleshooting video.

## Techniques

- **Pick grip changed for sweep-vs-alternate consistency.** He now holds the pick the same way for both alternate picking and sweep picking (rather than switching grips), because readjusting grip mid-line when switching between the two techniques costs speed; he deliberately sacrificed some sweep-picking speed to keep one consistent grip, since alternate picking is "more useful" overall.
- **Right-hand placement stays in one fixed zone** near the bridge/pickup area rather than roaming, for consistent tone and control.
- **The sweep is one continuous pick stroke through all the notes**, not individually picked notes — the same physical motion as strumming a chord, applied one note at a time.
- **Don't grip the pick too hard.** A visible "death grip" wastes energy and stiffens the whole picking motion; the fix for a pick slipping out of your hand is more reps, not a tighter grip.
- **Pick slant follows the sweep direction** — slanted into the direction being swept on one pass, closer to neutral (slight tilt or flat) on the return pass.
- **Left-hand: notes must be detached, not left ringing together like a chord** — a sweep that lets notes ring together might sound fine but isn't actually sweep-picking technique; practicing note-stopping in isolation fixes this.
- **Mute open strings during the sweep** using fret wraps (or socks as a cheaper substitute) plus left/right-hand muting, to prevent unplayed strings from ringing out as the sweep ascends/descends.
- **Prefers 7th-arpeggio shapes over triads for sweeping** — four notes instead of three means more hammer-ons/pull-offs woven into the sweep, which he finds more musically useful.
- **Never practice sweeps in total isolation** — always practice the notes immediately before and after the sweep too, or the shape won't transfer into real solo lines.
- **Sweep picking is a slow-build skill, not a quick win** — he ran a personal 30-day sweep-picking challenge, and later repeated it months afterward because he still wasn't satisfied.

# Vocal chain with Tony Maserati

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7R0nD4nCYVU)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7R0nD4nCYVU)

## Synopsis

A short, in-the-room clip of Tony Maserati (Mix with the Masters) working a near-finished vocal mix — parallel vocal, background vocals, lead-in-the-choruses, and an "answer" effect vocal, plus a quick detour into blending a synth/string moment. The throughline isn't one big technique, it's **restraint calibrated to each part's role**: the BVs and the choruses' lead are explicitly "meant to be part of the background," so he does "very little" to them, while the parallel vocal (an intelligibility device, not a density trick) gets automated up specifically in the bridge because the effects stacking there would otherwise bury the words. On the BVs he auditions two compressors (the Tube-Tech channel's CL 1B vs. an R Compressor) and ends up needing almost nothing beyond the CL 1B. On the lead-in-choruses he layers a tape-based de-ess/thinning stage (Studer A800, "Pop" setting), a dynamic EQ specifically dipping the *attacks* (not sibilance — an unusual dynamic-EQ target), another light compressor pass, and a parallel blend he's not sold on yet. A separate "answer" vocal effect gets pushed harder via a Decapitator send purely for punch. The most transferable moment is a troubleshooting one: an edit sounds "a little weird," he diagnoses it by ear as a missing consonant (not "b" or "m" — actually a missing "h" on "hey there"), grabs the "h" from elsewhere in the session, and pastes it onto both the BV tracks and the lead vocal to fix the entrance. He closes with a non-vocal aside — a guitar synth's already-left-heavy attack is fighting a string/cello blend, and Transient Designer-style attack reduction is the fix he reaches for. Almost nothing here comes with hard numbers (a raw session clip, not a settings tutorial) — the value is the *decision-making*: which parts earn processing, which don't, and how to chase down a specific missing sound in an edit by naming the actual phoneme.

## Techniques

- **Parallel vocal exists for intelligibility, not density — confirmed by a mute test.** Muting the parallel path audibly drops "the vocal intelligibility factor... it's that sort of intelligibility idea that you're able to hear every word." A distinct rationale from the usual "add density/aggression" parallel-comp framing (see [[Parallel Processing]]) — here the parallel path's whole job is making words easier to hear, not thicker. → [[Parallel Processing]], [[LEAD Slot]]
- **Automate the parallel vocal UP specifically in the bridge, ahead of other effects being added there.** "I'm EQing that a bit, I'm automating it in the bridge because I want that much higher, even though I'm adding some effects on the bridge" — the logic: a section that's about to get more FX stacked on it needs its intelligibility device boosted *first*, or the effects will bury the words the parallel path exists to protect. *(no exact dB given)* → [[Automation]], [[Parallel Processing]]
- **Deliberately near-nothing processing on a part whose ROLE is background, not lead — stated as the explicit reason, twice (hooks and lead-in-choruses).** On the hook vocal: "he's really meant to be part of the chorus in the background vocals there, so he's not meant to stick out too much." On the lead during the choruses: "he's meant to be part of the overall background sound, so I am doing very little." Worth generalizing: decide a part's *role in the arrangement* before deciding how much to process it — a lead singing a supporting/background part in a given section gets the light-touch chain, not the full lead chain, even though it's the same voice elsewhere in the song. → [[Arrangement and Song Form]], [[Mix Handoff]]
- **Auditioned two compressors on the background vocals through a Tube-Tech-style channel strip, and the more transparent one won by doing "not really anything."** "On the background vocals I'm choosing to use the Tube-Tech channel where I get a little bit more flexibility. I'm not doing anything really, it ends up all I'm doing is using the CL 1B. I'm also trying an R Compressor." Owned lane: [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII]] as the primary opto leveler; [[Pro-C 2]] (or [[LALA]]) stands in for the "R Compressor" (Waves Renaissance Compressor) audition — same best-match mapping already used for Kenny Beats' "R-Compressor slot." *(qualitative — no ratio/GR given, described as barely-there)* → [[UAD Tube-Tech CL 1B mkII]], [[Pro-C 2]], [[Compression]], [[Compression Staging]]
- **A tape-machine stage doing double duty as a de-esser AND a deliberate thinning move, via a named preset — not a dedicated de-esser plugin.** "A little de-essing, again I'm using the Studer A800 and trying to get that analog sound. In this case, I'm using the Pop setting because I wanted him to be a little thinner." The de-essing here is a side effect of tape saturation/EQ character (the "Pop" formula/preset), not a threshold-based de-esser — worth remembering tape emulation is a legitimate de-ess lane when the goal is character + gentle taming together, not surgical sibilance control. Owned best-match: [[Kramer Tape]] (no "Pop" preset equivalent, but the tape-thinning mechanism transfers — dial EQ/saturation toward thin/bright rather than warm/full). Studer A800 itself unowned → [[Plugin Wishlist]]. → [[Kramer Tape]], [[De-essing]], [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Dynamic EQ used to dip the vocal's ATTACKS specifically — an unusual target for a dynamic band, which is normally aimed at sibilance or a resonant frequency.** "Again, doing a bit of dynamic EQ with a Pro-Q 3 and I can show you where that's dipping out as well... it's just the attacks and we can solo that so you can hear that a little bit better." Reads as a dynamic band tuned to trigger on transient/attack energy rather than a sustained frequency problem — soften the front edge of syllables dynamically instead of a static EQ cut (which would dull the whole word, not just its onset). Owned equivalent: [[Pro-Q 4]] (current version of the Pro-Q 3 named on screen). *(qualitative — no frequency/range given)* → [[Pro-Q 4]], [[EQ]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Same "R Compressor" audition reused on the lead-in-choruses, framed here as a keep-him-high-in-the-mix move rather than character.** "A little bit of R Compressor as well. Again, trying to keep him high in the mix." Same owned-lane mapping as the BV instance above. → [[Pro-C 2]], [[Compression]]
- **Parallel on the lead-in-choruses, flagged as possibly not earning its place — a real-time "maybe cut this" moment, not a settled recipe.** "I've also got him going through the parallel. I'm not sure that's quite working at the moment, maybe we'll get rid of that." Worth keeping as a reminder that not every part needs the house parallel treatment just because it's available — audition it and be willing to pull it. → [[Parallel Processing]]
- **Push a send-based "answer" vocal effect harder via Decapitator purely for punch, judged with a without/with A-B.** "I'm liking this answer effect. I want to try to get a little bit more out of that. I'm sending it to a Decapitator because I really wanted it to kind of punch out a little bit... just brought up the level and brought up the overall mix of the Decapitator." A send (not an insert) feeding a saturation stage specifically to add punch to a secondary/"answer" vocal line (a call-and-response or ad-lib-style part), landed by direct without/with comparison rather than a target number. → [[Decapitator]], [[Saturation and Distortion]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]]
- **Diagnose a "weird" edit by naming the actual missing phoneme, not just "something's off" — false leads first, correct answer by process of elimination.** An edit "sounds pretty much like we're missing the 'b' of baby. Or maybe it's an 'm' of maybe... it's actually supposed to be 'hey there.' And so we're missing the 'h.'" Worth the general lesson: when an edit reads as subtly wrong, try naming candidate missing/doubled consonants against the actual lyric rather than re-editing blind — the fix is trivial once the specific sound is identified. → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]]
- **Fix a missing consonant by copying it from elsewhere in the session and pasting it onto the affected tracks — including BOTH the background vocals AND the lead, as two separate passes.** Once the missing "h" ("hey") was identified: "take that 'h' that's there and let's put it on these tracks here... we're going to need more... copy that, maybe we should get that on a beat" for the BVs, then explicitly repeated on the lead: "let's do the same thing for the lead vocal... let's get that 'hey' on the lead voice as well." A find-a-donor-take-and-paste edit, run per-track rather than assumed to propagate, with a beat-aligned placement check ("get that on a beat") before committing. → [[Vocal Comping and Takes]]
- **A guitar synth's attack fights a string/cello blend — reach for a Transient Designer-style tool to tame it, and expect the fix to work by contrast rather than by matching.** "The guitar synth part is kind of feeling already like it's a bit left-heavy. Its attack is already a bit left-heavy. I might be able to decrease that... maybe a bit of Transient Designer on there, that might help decrease that attack a bit... now what I'm doing is getting rid of that attack... perhaps let that be okay, because maybe it'll be the opposite and the three parts will blend." The blend goal for a string + guitar-synth + cello combination wasn't matching their attacks, it was *decreasing* one part's attack enough that the three read as one blended texture rather than three competing transients. Owned equivalent: [[Transient Master]] (or [[Transient Shaper]]) attack knob pulled down. *(no exact amount given)* → [[Transient Master]], [[TRANSIENT Slot]], [[Masking and Carving]]

## Techniques

### Dark synth trap beat (No BS-style)
- **Core melody = two layered synth elements, both from the Technicolor sample/preset pack** — a synth brass preset (source unclear — captions read "mine until September's new upcoming analog live," likely a synth plugin/preset browser name, too garbled to identify with confidence) layered with a textural synth pad from Technicolor; these two are the beat's melodic foundation before any chopping. *(low confidence on the synth source name — qualitative only, per transcript garbling)*
- **Reverse-and-chop the core melody for variety, the way Ozzy does on "pop star" and the "2C slide"** — reverse the brass layer and pitch it, then separately stretch out the pad and reverse just its second chord — a signature reversed-synth texture running through the beat rather than the melody playing straight. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Descending counter-melody for a darker vibe** — a counter line with the notes stepping down the scale, layered against the main melody specifically to deepen the dark trap mood.
- **A real-instrument texture layer breaks up an all-synth stack** — a piano arc texture (also from Technicolor) is the one non-synth element among otherwise synth-based layers, deliberately added for variety/contrast against everything else.
- **Distorted synth bass as an energy-change trick: pull the 808 out, drop in a distorted synth bass in its place** — swapping the low end from clean 808 to a distorted synth bass (temporarily removing the 808) is used purely to mark a shift in energy at that point in the arrangement. → [[Saturation and Distortion]]
- **Hi-hats: simple two-step pattern, gaps filled with rolls** — the core hat pattern stays basic (two-step), with rolls used specifically to fill the gaps rather than densifying the whole pattern.
- **Two 808s: a subby/long main 808 (Ozzy's regular go-to) plus a second, reversed 808 layered in purely for flavor** — the main 808 favors sustain/sub weight; the reversed second 808 is a texture add, not a second rhythmic voice. → [[Bassline Writing]]

### Building a soulful sample from scratch
- **Organ + Shaper Box + Room Reverb + high-cut EQ as the sample's harmonic bed** — start with a synth organ (Technicolor), run it through a Shaper Box (Cableguys ShaperBox — unowned, already tracked in [[Plugin Wishlist]]) for transient/volume shaping, add a room reverb, then an EQ pulling out a lot of the top end so the organ reads vintage/dark rather than bright and modern. → [[Reverb]], [[EQ]]
- **Electric piano (high octave) + a deliberately busier bass pattern from Kontakt, written specifically to create groove** — the bass part is more rhythmically complex than usual on purpose, because on this kind of soulful sample the groove has to come from the bass pattern itself rather than the drums. → [[Kontakt 7]], [[Bassline Writing]]
- **Piano "pitch-down-after-bounce" texture trick: play a part in a high octave, bounce it to audio, THEN pitch that bounced audio down an octave** — pitching down a high-register performance *after* it's committed to audio (rather than just playing/recording it an octave lower to start) gives a distinctly nicer texture than recording low directly; layer the original (un-pitched) piano part back in at a lower octave alongside it for body. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Fake "old sample" drums: simple boom-bap kick/snare pattern run through Edison and the sample rate lowered, for a washed-out/filtered vintage feel** — a plain kick+snare pattern is dropped into a sample-editor (Edison, FL Studio's tool — Nate's owned equivalent for this exact move is [[Decimort 2]] or [[kHs Bitcrush]]) and its sample rate reduced, mimicking the way real vintage samples always carry drums baked into the loop. → [[Decimort 2]]
- **Strings added specifically to fill a missing high-end gap** once the organ/piano/drums were assembled and the top end still felt empty — strings chosen as the answer to "what's missing up top," not as a planned layer from the start.
- **Vocals: source a Splice vocal pack, process (reverb/delay/EQ — one processing step name was garbled in captions, likely an Auto-Tune-family plugin, not confirmed), then stretch the whole sample to double speed and lower its sample rate + chop it up** — the double-speed stretch was needed to get the vocal's character right ("sounds so fire") before the final lo-fi/chopped treatment was applied on top. → [[Splice Sounds]]

### Flipping the finished sample two ways
- **Flip 1 (melodic up-tempo trap): isolate + repeat the last chord, drag to a new channel and consolidate, reverse it, stretch it (darker vibe), half-time it, add drums while flipping (audition drum choices against the chop rather than committing to the first one), then add an 808 pitched up a few semitones.** The reverse+stretch+half-time sequence, done in that order, is the core "make it darker" move before drums go back in. → [[Sampling and Sound Selection]]
- **Flip 2 (soulful boom-bap/emotional rap): filter the hi-hats heavier (darker/duller), put the hats on their own mixer track and add a delay, then finish with a synth bass patch ("trap soul," Technicolor).** Two flips built from the exact same source chop, differentiated entirely by drum choice + hat treatment + bass patch. → [[Delay]]

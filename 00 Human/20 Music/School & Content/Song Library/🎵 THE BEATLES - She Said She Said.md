# She Said She Said — The Beatles

## Pollack's analysis

**Form:** Intro (3 bars on I) · Verse (10 — an 8-bar verse plus a 2-bar instrumental reprise of its last two bars) · Verse · Bridge · Verse · Bridge · Verse · fade. In the bridge the meter goes fluid: beats per bar run **4 + 4 · 3 + 3 + 3 · 6 + 3 · 6 + 3**, and Pollack reads the long bars as single 6-beat bars because that is where the chord changes fall.

The famous feature is that collapsing meter, but the real lesson is a compositional rule: **when you push one parameter to the limit, hold the others still.** All that turbulence sits inside a textbook two-bridge form over four chords total, one of which does not arrive until the bridge's climax.

It is also his cleanest specimen of **pure Mixolydian** harmony with no leakage into ordinary major. The v is minor and cannot make a working dominant, so IV and ♭VII carry the key ([[POP-C09 — Mixolydian ♭VII]]), and the verse's ♭VII–IV–I tail is the double-plagal move. The bridge is a **real modulation** ([[HAR-C10 — Tonicization vs modulation]]): F minor, heard for the first time at the climax, is re-read as ii in E♭. The paradox worth holding onto is that in this mode the tonic makes a stronger V-of-IV than the minor v makes a V-of-I.

The tune is locked inside a fifth for nearly the whole song — the hard ceiling produces the trapped, circular quality. In the bridge, drums and bass **give up syncopation entirely** and mark even eighths so the shifting meter stays readable ([[RHY-C02 — Syncopation]]).

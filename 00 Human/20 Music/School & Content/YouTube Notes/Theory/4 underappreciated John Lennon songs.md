# 4 underappreciated John Lennon songs

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qm2Y36ABlIk)
📺 [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qm2Y36ABlIk)

## Synopsis

A closer analytical look at four John Lennon solo tracks, with real technique on two of them: "Happy Xmas (War Is Over)"'s chain of smooth closely-related-key modulations, and "Mother"'s climbing triad move (also reused on "I Am the Walrus" and "Imagine").

## Techniques

- **Chained modulations through closely-related keys, via single-note pivots**: "Happy Xmas (War Is Over)" moves A major → D major → G major → back to D → back to A, each pair of adjacent keys sharing all but one note. Each pivot happens on the single differing note (e.g. G♮ replacing G♯ turns the A chord into an A7, pivoting cleanly into D) — the whole chain works because every hop is only a one-note distance from the last key, so nothing feels like a hard modulation.
- **A public-domain melodic borrow, confirmed by melodic similarity**: "Happy Xmas" draws melodic inspiration from the traditional folk song "Stewball" (via Peter, Paul and Mary's 1963 recording) — since the underlying folk tune is public domain, this was legally unrestricted regardless of intent.
- **The 5→6→7 climbing triad move**: on "Mother," Lennon climbs from the 5th degree up through the 6th to the 7th as a melodic/harmonic device over otherwise simple I-IV-V piano chords — he reused this same climb in "I Am the Walrus" and later "Imagine," making it a recognizable personal songwriting fingerprint across his catalog.
- **Irregular bar-shortening as a rhythmic habit**: "Mother" cuts a bar short into 6/8 amid an otherwise 2/4-leaning pulse — flagged as "a very typical John Lennon move," i.e. a recurring idiosyncrasy rather than a one-off.
